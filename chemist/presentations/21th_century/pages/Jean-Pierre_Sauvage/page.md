**Jean-Pierre Sauvage** (French pronunciation: [[ʒɑ̃pjɛʁ sovaʒ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 21 October 1944) is a French [coordination chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_chemistry "Coordination chemistry") working at [Strasbourg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg "University of Strasbourg"). He graduated from the National School of Chemistry of Strasbourg (now known as [ECPM Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_européenne_de_chimie,_polymères_et_matériaux "École européenne de chimie, polymères et matériaux")), in 1967. He has specialized in [supramolecular chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supramolecular_chemistry "Supramolecular chemistry") for which he has been awarded the 2016 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") along with [Sir J. Fraser Stoddart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Fraser_Stoddart "J. Fraser Stoddart") and [Bernard L. Feringa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Feringa "Ben Feringa").

## Biography

Sauvage was born in Paris in 1944, and earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree from the [Université Louis-Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Louis-Pasteur "Université Louis-Pasteur") under the supervision of [Jean-Marie Lehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Lehn "Jean-Marie Lehn"), himself a 1987 laureate of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry. During his doctoral work, he contributed to the first syntheses of the [cryptand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptand "Cryptand") ligands. After postdoctoral research with [Malcolm L. H. Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._L._H._Green "M. L. H. Green"), he returned to [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg "Strasbourg"), where he is now emeritus professor.

Sauvage's scientific work has focused on creating molecules that mimic the functions of machines by changing their conformation in response to an external signal.

His Nobel Prize work was done in 1983, when he was the first to synthesize a [catenane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catenane "Catenane"), a complex of two [interlocking ring-shaped molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanically_interlocked_molecular_architectures "Mechanically interlocked molecular architectures"), which were [bonded mechanically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_bond "Mechanical bond") rather than chemically. Because these two rings can move relative to each other, the Nobel Prize cited this as a vital initial effort towards making [molecular machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_machine "Molecular machine"). The other two recipients of the prize followed up by later creating a [rotaxane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotaxane "Rotaxane") and a [molecular rotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_molecular_motor "Synthetic molecular motor").

Other research includes [electrochemical reduction of CO2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrochemical_reduction_of_carbon_dioxide "Electrochemical reduction of carbon dioxide") and models of the [photosynthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis "Photosynthesis") reaction center.

A large theme of his work is molecular [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topoisomer "Topoisomer"), specifically [mechanically-interlocked molecular architectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanically-interlocked_molecular_architectures "Mechanically-interlocked molecular architectures"). He has described syntheses of [catenanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catenane "Catenane") and [molecular knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_knot "Molecular knot") based on coordination complexes.

He was elected a correspondent member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences") on 26 March 1990, and became a member on 24 November 1997. He is currently emeritus professor at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg "University of Strasbourg") (Unistra).

He shared the 2016 Nobel Prize in Chemistry "for the design and synthesis of [molecular machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_machine "Molecular machine")" with [Sir J. Fraser Stoddart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Stoddart "Fraser Stoddart") and [Bernard L. Feringa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Feringa "Ben Feringa"). He was elected a foreign associate of the US [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in April 2019.

As of 2021, Sauvage has an [h-index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-index "H-index") of 109 according to [Google Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Scholar "Google Scholar") and of 100 according to [Scopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scopus "Scopus").
