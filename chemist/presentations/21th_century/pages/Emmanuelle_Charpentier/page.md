**Emmanuelle Marie Charpentier** (French pronunciation: [[emanɥɛl maʁi ʃaʁpɑ̃tje]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 11 December 1968) is a French academic, microbiologist, geneticist, and biochemist. She has served as a director at the [Max Planck Institute for Infection Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Infection_Biology "Max Planck Institute for Infection Biology") in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin") since 2015. Three years later, she founded an independent [research institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_institute "Research institute"), the Max Planck Unit for the Science of Pathogens. In 2020, Charpentier and American biochemist [Jennifer Doudna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Doudna "Jennifer Doudna") of the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), were awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") "for the development of a method for [genome editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome_editing "Genome editing")" (through [CRISPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRISPR_gene_editing "CRISPR gene editing")). This was the first science Nobel Prize ever won by two women only.

## Early life and education

Charpentier's paternal grandfather, surnamed Sinanian, was an [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians "Armenians") who escaped to France during the [Armenian Genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Genocide "Armenian Genocide") and met his wife in [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille "Marseille"). Charpentier was born in 1968 in [Juvisy-sur-Orge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juvisy-sur-Orge "Juvisy-sur-Orge") in France and studied biochemistry, microbiology, and genetics at the [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University "Pierre and Marie Curie University") (which became the Faculty of Science of [Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University "Sorbonne University")) in Paris. She was a graduate student at the [Institut Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Pasteur "Institut Pasteur") from 1992 to 1995 and was awarded a research doctorate. Charpentier's PhD work investigated molecular mechanisms involved in [antibiotic resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimicrobial_resistance "Antimicrobial resistance").

## Career and research

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/MPI_f%C3%BCr_Infektionsbiologie%2C_Berlin_%282009%29.jpg/250px-MPI_f%C3%BCr_Infektionsbiologie%2C_Berlin_%282009%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:MPI_für_Infektionsbiologie,_Berlin_(2009).jpg)

The [Max Planck Institute for Infection Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Infection_Biology "Max Planck Institute for Infection Biology") in Berlin, Germany

Charpentier worked as a university teaching assistant at Pierre and Marie Curie University from 1993 to 1995 and as a postdoctoral fellow at the [Institut Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasteur_Institute "Pasteur Institute") from 1995 to 1996. She moved to the US and worked as a postdoctoral fellow at [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University") in New York from 1996 to 1997. During this time, Charpentier worked in the lab of microbiologist [Elaine Tuomanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_Tuomanen "Elaine Tuomanen"). Tuomanen's lab investigated how the pathogen *[Streptococcus pneumoniae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streptococcus_pneumoniae "Streptococcus pneumoniae")* utilizes mobile genetic elements to alter its genome. Charpentier also helped to demonstrate how *S. pneumoniae* develops [vancomycin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancomycin "Vancomycin") resistance.

Charpentier was an assistant research scientist at the [New York University Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYU_Langone_Medical_Center "NYU Langone Medical Center") from 1997 to 1999. She worked in the lab of Pamela Cowin, a skin-cell biologist interested in mammalian gene manipulation. Charpentier published a paper exploring the regulation of hair growth in mice. She held the position of Research Associate at the [St. Jude Children's Research Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Jude_Children's_Research_Hospital "St. Jude Children's Research Hospital") and at the [Skirball Institute of Biomolecular Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skirball_Institute_of_Biomolecular_Medicine?action=edit&redlink=1 "Skirball Institute of Biomolecular Medicine") in New York from 1999 to 2002.

After five years in the United States, Charpentier returned to Europe and became the lab head and a guest professor at the Institute of Microbiology and Genetics, [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna "University of Vienna"), from 2002 to 2004. In 2004, Charpentier published her discovery of an RNA molecule involved in the regulation of virulence-factor synthesis in *[Streptococcus pyogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streptococcus_pyogenes "Streptococcus pyogenes")*. From 2004 to 2006, she was lab head and an assistant professor at the Department of Microbiology and Immunobiology. In 2006, she became a [privatdozentin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozentin "Privatdozentin") (Microbiology) and received her [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation "Habilitation") at the Centre of Molecular Biology. From 2006 to 2009 she worked as lab head and associate professor at the [Max F. Perutz Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_F._Perutz_Laboratories "Max F. Perutz Laboratories").

Charpentier moved to Sweden and became lab head and associate professor at the Laboratory for [Molecular Infection Medicine Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_Infection_Medicine_Sweden "Molecular Infection Medicine Sweden") (MIMS), at [Umeå University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umeå_University "Umeå University"). She held the position of group leader from 2008 to 2013 and was visiting professor from 2014 to 2017. She moved to Germany to act as department head and W3 Professor at the [Helmholtz Centre for Infection Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmholtz_Centre_for_Infection_Research_(HZI) "Helmholtz Centre for Infection Research (HZI)") in Braunschweig and the [Hannover Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannover_Medical_School "Hannover Medical School") from 2013 until 2015. In 2014, she became an [Alexander von Humboldt Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Professorship "Alexander von Humboldt Professorship").

In 2015, Charpentier accepted an offer from the German [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society "Max Planck Society") to become a scientific member of the society and a director at the [Max Planck Institute for Infection Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Infection_Biology "Max Planck Institute for Infection Biology") in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin"). Since 2016, she has been an Honorary Professor at [Humboldt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University "Humboldt University") in Berlin. Charpentier retained her position as visiting professor at Umeå University until the end of 2017 when a new donation from the Kempe Foundations and the [Knut and Alice Wallenberg Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knut_and_Alice_Wallenberg_Foundation "Knut and Alice Wallenberg Foundation") allowed her to offer more young researchers positions within research groups of the MIMS Laboratory. In 2018, she became the founding and acting director of the [Max Planck Unit for the Science of Pathogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Unit_for_the_Science_of_Pathogens?action=edit&redlink=1 "Max Planck Unit for the Science of Pathogens").

## CRISPR/Cas9

Charpentier is best known for her Nobel-winning work of deciphering the molecular mechanisms of a bacterial immune system, called [CRISPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRISPR "CRISPR")/[Cas9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cas9 "Cas9"), and repurposing it into a tool for [genome editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome_editing "Genome editing"). In particular, she uncovered a novel mechanism for the maturation of a non-coding RNA which is pivotal in the function of CRISPR/Cas9, demonstrating that a small RNA called [tracrRNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-activating_crRNA "Trans-activating crRNA") is essential for the maturation of crRNA.

In 2011, Charpentier met [Jennifer Doudna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Doudna "Jennifer Doudna") at a research conference in San Juan, Puerto Rico, and they began a collaboration. Working with Doudna's laboratory, Charpentier's laboratory showed that Cas9 could be used to make cuts in any [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") sequence desired. The method they developed involved the combination of Cas9 with easily created synthetic "guide RNA" molecules. Synthetic guide RNA is a chimera of crRNA and tracrRNA; therefore, this discovery demonstrated that the CRISPR-Cas9 technology could be used to edit the genome with relative ease. Researchers worldwide have employed this method successfully to edit the DNA sequences of plants, animals, and laboratory [cell lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_line "Cell line"). Since its discovery, CRISPR has revolutionized genetics by allowing scientists to edit genes to probe their role in health and disease and to develop genetic therapies with the hope that it will prove safer and more effective than the first generation of gene therapies.

In 2013, Charpentier co-founded [CRISPR Therapeutics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRISPR_Therapeutics "CRISPR Therapeutics") and ERS Genomics along with Shaun Foy and Rodger Novak.

## In popular culture

In 2019, Charpentier was a featured character in the play *STEM FEMMES* by Philadelphia theater company Applied Mechanics.

In 2021, [Walter Isaacson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Isaacson "Walter Isaacson") detailed the story of Jennifer Doudna and her collaboration with Charpentier leading to the discovery of CRISPR/CAS-9, in the biography *[The Code Breaker: Jennifer Doudna, Gene Editing, and the Future of the Human Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Code_Breaker:_Jennifer_Doudna,_Gene_Editing,_and_the_Future_of_the_Human_Race "The Code Breaker: Jennifer Doudna, Gene Editing, and the Future of the Human Race")*.
