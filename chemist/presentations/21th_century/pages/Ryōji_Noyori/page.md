**Ryōji Noyori** (野依 良治, *Noyori Ryōji*; born 3 September 1938) is a Japanese [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist"). He won the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 2001. Noyori shared a half of the prize with [William S. Knowles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Knowles "William S. Knowles") for the study of [chirally catalyzed hydrogenations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_hydrogenation "Asymmetric hydrogenation"); the second half of the prize went to [K. Barry Sharpless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Barry_Sharpless "K. Barry Sharpless") for his study in chirally catalyzed oxidation reactions ([Sharpless epoxidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpless_epoxidation "Sharpless epoxidation")).

## Education and career

Ryōji Noyori was born in [Kobe, Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe "Kobe"). Early in his school days Ryoji was interested in physics. His interest was kindled by the famous physicist [Hideki Yukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Yukawa "Hideki Yukawa") (1949 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") winner), a close friend of his father. Later, he became fascinated with chemistry, after hearing a presentation on [nylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nylon "Nylon") at an industrial exposition. He saw the power of chemistry as being the ability to "produce high value from almost nothing". He was a student at the School of Engineering (Department of Industrial Chemistry) of the [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University"), where he graduated in 1961. He subsequently obtained a [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree "Master's degree") in [Industrial Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Chemistry "Industrial Chemistry") from the Graduate School of Engineering of the [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University"). Between 1963 and 1967, he was a research associate at the School of Engineering of the [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University"), and an instructor in the research group of [Hitoshi Nozaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitoshi_Nozaki "Hitoshi Nozaki"). Noyori obtained a [Doctor of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Engineering "Doctor of Engineering") degree (DEng) from the [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University") in 1967. He became an associate professor at the same university in 1968. After postdoctoral work with [Elias J. Corey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elias_J._Corey "Elias J. Corey") at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") he returned to Nagoya, becoming a full professor in 1972. He is still based at Nagoya, and served as president of [RIKEN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIKEN "RIKEN"), a multi-site national research initiative with an annual budget of $800 million, from 2003 to 2015.

Noyori believes strongly in the power of [catalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis "Catalysis") and of [green chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_chemistry "Green chemistry"); in a 2005 article he argued for the pursuit of "practical elegance in synthesis". In this article he stated that "*our ability to devise straightforward and practical chemical syntheses is indispensable to the survival of our species.*" Elsewhere he has said that "*Research is for nations and mankind, not for researchers themselves.*" He encourages scientists to be politically active: "*Researchers must spur public opinions and government policies toward constructing the [sustainable society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development "Sustainable development") in the 21st century.*"

Noyori is currently a chairman of the Education Rebuilding Council, which was set up by Japan's PM [Shinzō Abe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinzō_Abe "Shinzō Abe") after he came to power in 2006.

Noyori is most famous for [asymmetric hydrogenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_hydrogenation "Asymmetric hydrogenation") using as [catalysts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalyst "Catalyst") [complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_(chemistry) "Complex (chemistry)") of [rhodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodium "Rhodium") and [ruthenium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenium "Ruthenium"), particularly those based on the [BINAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BINAP "BINAP") [ligand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligand "Ligand"). Asymmetric [hydrogenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogenation "Hydrogenation") of an [alkene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkene "Alkene") in the presence of ((*S*)-[BINAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BINAP "BINAP"))Ru([OAc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetate "Acetate"))2 is used for the commercial production of enantiomerically pure (97% [ee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomeric_excess "Enantiomeric excess")) [naproxen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naproxen "Naproxen"), a [nonsteriodal anti-inflammatory drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonsteroidal_anti-inflammatory_drug "Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug"). The [antibacterial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibacterial "Antibacterial") agent [levofloxacin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levofloxacin "Levofloxacin") is manufactured by asymmetric hydrogenation of [ketones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketone "Ketone") in the presence of a Ru(II) BINAP halide complex.

He has also worked on other [asymmetric processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_synthesis "Asymmetric synthesis"). Each year 3000 [tonnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonnes "Tonnes") (after new expansion) of [menthol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menthol "Menthol") are produced (in 94% [ee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomeric_excess "Enantiomeric excess")) by [Takasago International Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takasago_International_Corporation "Takasago International Corporation"), using Noyori's method for [isomerisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomerisation "Isomerisation") of [allylic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allyl "Allyl") [amines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amine "Amine").

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/59/Menthol_synthesis.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)

More recently with Philip G. Jessop, Noyori has developed an industrial process for the manufacture of *N,N*-[dimethylformamide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimethylformamide "Dimethylformamide") from [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen "Hydrogen"), [dimethylamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimethylamine "Dimethylamine") and [supercritical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercritical_fluid "Supercritical fluid") [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide "Carbon dioxide") in the presence of RuCl2(P(CH3)3)4 as catalyst.

## Recognition

[The Ryoji Noyori Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ryoji_Noyori_Prize "The Ryoji Noyori Prize") is named in his honour. In 2000 Noyori became Honorary Doctor at the [University of Rennes 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rennes_1 "University of Rennes 1"), where he taught in 1995, and in 2005, he became Honorary Doctor at [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich "Technical University of Munich") and [RWTH Aachen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWTH_Aachen_University "RWTH Aachen University"), Germany. Noyori was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2005 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 2005"). and an Honorary Doctorate degree from the [Institute of Chemical Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Chemical_Technology "Institute of Chemical Technology"), Mumbai (formerly known as UDCT) on the 23rd day of February 2018.

He has also been awarded:

- 1982 – [Chuniichi Culture Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chunichi_Shimbun "Chunichi Shimbun")
- 1985 – The Chemical Society of Japan Award

- 1992 – [Asahi Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahi_Prize "Asahi Prize")
