**Roderick MacKinnon** (born February 19, 1956) is an American biophysicist, neuroscientist, and businessman. He is a professor of molecular neurobiology and [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics") at [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University") who won the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") together with [Peter Agre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Agre "Peter Agre") in 2003 for his work on the structure and operation of [ion channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_channel "Ion channel").

## Biography

### Early life and education

MacKinnon was born in [Burlington, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington,_Massachusetts "Burlington, Massachusetts") and initially attended the [University of Massachusetts Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Boston "University of Massachusetts Boston"). MacKinnon then transferred to [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University "Brandeis University") after one year, and there he received a bachelor's degree in [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") in 1978, studying [calcium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium "Calcium") transport through the [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane") for his honors thesis in Christopher Miller's laboratory. It was also at Brandeis where MacKinnon met his future wife and working-colleague Alice Lee, who is an organic chemist.

After receiving his bachelor's degree from [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University "Brandeis University"), MacKinnon entered medical school at [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University "Tufts University"). He got his M.D. in 1982 and received training in Internal Medicine at [Beth Israel Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Israel_Deaconess_Medical_Center "Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center") in Boston. He did not feel satisfied enough with the medical profession, so in 1986 he returned to Christopher Miller's laboratory at Brandeis for postdoctoral studies.

### Career

In 1989 he was appointed assistant professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") where he studied the interaction of the [potassium channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_channel "Potassium channel") with a specific toxin derived from scorpion venom, acquainting himself with methods of protein purification and [X-ray crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography "X-ray crystallography"). In 1996 he moved to [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University") as a professor and head of the Laboratory of Molecular Neurobiology and Biophysics where he started to work on the structure of the potassium channel. These channels are of particular importance to the [nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous_system "Nervous system") and the heart and enable potassium ions to cross the [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane").

## Scientific contributions

Potassium channels demonstrate a seemingly counterintuitive activity: they permit the passage of potassium ions, whereas they do not allow the passage of the much smaller [sodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium "Sodium") ions. Before MacKinnon's work, the detailed molecular architecture of potassium channels and the means by which they conduct ions were only generally known and indirectly inferred.

In 1998, despite barriers to the [structural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_biology "Structural biology") study of [integral membrane proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_membrane_protein "Integral membrane protein") that had thwarted most attempts for decades, MacKinnon and colleagues determined the three-dimensional molecular structure of a potassium channel from an actinobacterium, *Streptomyces lividans*, utilizing [X-ray crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography "X-ray crystallography"). With this structure and other biochemical experiments, MacKinnon and colleagues were able to explain the exact mechanism by which [potassium channel selectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_channel#Selectivity_filter "Potassium channel") occurs.

His prize-winning research was conducted primarily at the [Cornell High Energy Synchrotron Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_High_Energy_Synchrotron_Source "Cornell High Energy Synchrotron Source") (CHESS) of [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"), and at the [National Synchrotron Light Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Synchrotron_Light_Source "National Synchrotron Light Source") (NSLS) of [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory").

MacKinnon was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 2005. In 2007 he became a foreign member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences").

## Business activities

MacKinnon is co-inventor with his friend and scientific collaborator, neurobiologist Bruce Bean of [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School "Harvard Medical School"), of a [dietary supplement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietary_supplement "Dietary supplement") for treating and preventing muscle cramps; they tested it in clinical trials and are co-founders a company to commercialize their invention, Flex Pharma. [Christoph Westphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Westphal "Christoph Westphal") and Jennifer Cermak were co-founders as well.

The company undertook [drug development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_development "Drug development") of a formulation of supplement as a drug candidate for neuromuscular disorders like [ALS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALS "ALS"), and raised a $40 million Series A round. The company had an $86 million [initial public offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_public_offering "Initial public offering") in 2015. In 2016, the company launched "HotShot" as a dietary supplement for endurance athletes. In June 2018 the company halted clinical development of the drug candidate due to tolerability issues, cut its workforce, and said it was considering its strategy. In July 2018 MacKinnon resigned from the board of directors.
