**Brian Kent Kobilka** (born May 30, 1955) is an American physiologist and a recipient of the 2012 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with [Robert Lefkowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lefkowitz "Robert Lefkowitz") for discoveries that reveal the workings of [G protein-coupled receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_protein-coupled_receptors "G protein-coupled receptors"). He is currently a professor in the department of Molecular and Cellular Physiology at [Stanford University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University_School_of_Medicine "Stanford University School of Medicine"). He is also a co-founder of ConfometRx, a [biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology "Biotechnology") company focusing on G protein-coupled receptors. He was named a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 2011.

## Early life

Kobilka attended St. Mary's Grade School in [Little Falls, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Falls,_Minnesota "Little Falls, Minnesota"), a part of the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Saint Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Saint_Cloud "Roman Catholic Diocese of Saint Cloud"). He then graduated from Little Falls High School. He received a [Bachelor’s Degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor’s_Degree "Bachelor’s Degree") in [Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology "Biology") and [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") from the [University of Minnesota Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_Duluth "University of Minnesota Duluth"), and earned his [M.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine "Doctor of Medicine"), *[cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cum_laude "Cum laude")*, from [Yale University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University_School_of_Medicine "Yale University School of Medicine"). Following the completion of his residency in [internal medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_medicine "Internal medicine") at [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis") [School of Medicine's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_School_of_Medicine "Washington University School of Medicine") [Barnes-Jewish Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnes-Jewish_Hospital "Barnes-Jewish Hospital"), Kobilka worked in research as a [postdoctoral fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_fellow "Postdoctoral fellow") under [Robert Lefkowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lefkowitz "Robert Lefkowitz") at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University"), where he started work on cloning the [β2-adrenergic receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta-2_adrenergic_receptor "Beta-2 adrenergic receptor"). Kobilka moved to Stanford in 1989. He was a [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute") (HHMI) investigator from 1987 to 2003.

## Research

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/Brian_Kobilka_1_2012.jpg/250px-Brian_Kobilka_1_2012.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Brian_Kobilka_1_2012.jpg)

Kobilka in Stockholm 2012

Kobilka is best known for his research on the structure and activity of [G protein-coupled receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_protein-coupled_receptors "G protein-coupled receptors") (GPCRs); in particular, work from Kobilka's laboratory determined the molecular structure of the [β2-adrenergic receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta-2_adrenergic_receptor "Beta-2 adrenergic receptor"). This work has been highly cited by other scientists because GPCRs are important targets for pharmaceutical therapeutics, but notoriously difficult to work with in [X-ray crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography "X-ray crystallography"). Before, [rhodopsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodopsin "Rhodopsin") was the only G-protein coupled receptor where the structure had been determined at high resolution. The β2-adrenergic receptor structure was soon followed by the determination of the molecular structure of several other G-protein coupled receptors.

Kobilka is the 1994 recipient of the [American Society for Pharmacology and Experimental Therapeutics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Pharmacology_and_Experimental_Therapeutics "American Society for Pharmacology and Experimental Therapeutics") John J. Abel Award in Pharmacology. His GPCR structure work was named "runner-up" for the 2007 "Breakthrough of the Year" award from *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_(journal) "Science (journal)")*. The work was, in part, supported by Kobilka's 2004 Javits Neuroscience Investigator Award from the [National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Neurological_Disorders_and_Stroke "National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke"). He received the 2012 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with [Robert Lefkowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lefkowitz "Robert Lefkowitz") for his work on G protein-coupled receptors. In 2017, Kobilka received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").

As part of [Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen "Shenzhen")’s 13th Five-Year Plan funding research in emerging technologies and opening "Nobel laureate research labs", in 2017 he opened the Kobilka Institute of Innovative Drug Discovery at the [Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_University_of_Hong_Kong,_Shenzhen "Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen") in Southern China.

## Personal life

Kobilka is from [Little Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Falls,_Minnesota "Little Falls, Minnesota") in [central Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Minnesota "Central Minnesota"). Both his grandfather Felix J. Kobilka (1893–1991) and his father Franklyn A. Kobilka (1921–2004) were bakers and natives of [Little Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Falls,_Minnesota "Little Falls, Minnesota"), Minnesota. Kobilka's grandmother, Isabelle Susan Kobilka (née Medved, 1891–1980), belonged to the Medved and Kiewel families of [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia "Prussia") immigrants, who from 1888 owned the historical Kiewel brewery in Little Falls. His mother is Betty L. Kobilka (née Faust, b. 1930).

Kobilka met his wife Tong Sun Thian, a Malaysian-Chinese woman, at the [University of Minnesota Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_Duluth "University of Minnesota Duluth"). They have two children, Jason and Megan Kobilka.
