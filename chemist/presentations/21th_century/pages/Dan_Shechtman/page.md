**Dan Shechtman** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): דן שכטמן; born January 24, 1941) is an Israeli chemist, the Philip Tobias Professor of Materials Science at the [Technion – Israel Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology "Technion – Israel Institute of Technology"), an Associate of the [US Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")'s [Ames National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ames_National_Laboratory "Ames National Laboratory"), and Professor of Materials Science at [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University "Iowa State University"). On April 8, 1982, while on sabbatical at the [U.S. National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), Shechtman discovered the [icosahedral phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_phase "Icosahedral phase"), which opened the new field of [quasiperiodic crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal "Quasicrystal"), also referred to as "[quasicrystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal "Quasicrystal")." For this discovery, he was awarded the 2011 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"), making him one of six Israelis who have won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

## Biography

### Early life

Dan Shechtman was born in 1941 in [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv "Tel Aviv"), in what was then [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine "Mandatory Palestine"); the city became part of the new state of [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel") in 1948. He grew up in [Petah Tikva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petah_Tikva "Petah Tikva") and [Ramat Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_Gan "Ramat Gan") in a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") family. His grandparents had immigrated to Palestine during the [Second Aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Aliyah "Second Aliyah") (1904–1914) and founded a printing house. As a child, Shechtman was fascinated by [Jules Verne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Verne "Jules Verne")'s *[The Mysterious Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mysterious_Island "The Mysterious Island")* (1874), which he read many times. His childhood dream was to become an engineer like the main protagonist, [Cyrus Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Smith "Cyrus Smith").

> I thought that was the best thing a person could do. The engineer in the book knows mechanics and physics, and he creates a whole way of life on the island out of nothing. I wanted to be like that.

— Dan Shechtman

### Spouse and children

Shechtman is married to Prof. Tzipora Shechtman, Head of the Department of Counseling and Human Development at [Haifa University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa_University "Haifa University"), and author of two books on [psychotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychotherapy "Psychotherapy"). They have a son [Yoav Shechtman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoav_Shechtman "Yoav Shechtman") (a former postdoctoral researcher in the lab of [W. E. Moerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_E._Moerner "William E. Moerner") and now professor at the [Technion – Israel Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology "Technion – Israel Institute of Technology")) and three daughters: Tamar Finkelstein (an organizational psychologist at the Israeli police leadership center), Ella Shechtman-Cory (a PhD in [clinical psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_psychology "Clinical psychology")), and Ruth Dougoud-Nevo (also a PhD in clinical psychology).

## Academic career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/Dan_Shechtman_in_1985.jpg/250px-Dan_Shechtman_in_1985.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dan_Shechtman_in_1985.jpg)

Meeting at NIST in 1985 where Shechtman (left) explains the atomic structure of quasicrystals

After receiving his Ph.D. in [Materials Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_Engineering "Materials Engineering") from the [Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion "Technion") in 1972, where he also obtained his B.Sc. in [Mechanical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_Engineering "Mechanical Engineering") in 1966 and M.Sc. in Materials Engineering in 1968, Shechtman was an [NRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council "United States National Research Council") fellow at the [Aerospace Research Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Research_Laboratory "Air Force Research Laboratory") at [Wright Patterson AFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Patterson_Air_Force_Base "Wright Patterson Air Force Base"), Ohio, where he studied for three years the microstructure and physical [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy "Metallurgy") of [titanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium "Titanium") aluminides. In 1975, he joined the department of materials engineering at Technion. In 1981–1983 he was on sabbatical at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University"), where he studied rapidly solidified [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminum "Aluminum") transition metal alloys, in a joint program with [NBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology"). During this study he discovered the [icosahedral phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_phase "Icosahedral phase") which opened the new field of [quasiperiodic crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal "Quasicrystal").

In 1992–1994 he was on sabbatical at [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology") (NIST), where he studied the effect of the defect structure of [CVD diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_vapor_deposition_of_diamond "Chemical vapor deposition of diamond") on its growth and properties. Shechtman's Technion research is conducted in the Louis Edelstein Center, and in the Wolfson Centre which is headed by him. He served on several Technion Senate Committees and headed one of them.

Shechtman joined the Iowa State faculty in 2004. He currently spends about five months a year in [Ames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ames,_Iowa "Ames, Iowa") on a part-time appointment.

Since 2014 he has been the head of the International Scientific Council of [Tomsk Polytechnic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomsk_Polytechnic_University "Tomsk Polytechnic University").

## Work on quasicrystals and controversy

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Quasicrystal1.jpg/250px-Quasicrystal1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Quasicrystal1.jpg)

Shechtman's Nobel Prize–winning work was in the area of [quasicrystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal "Quasicrystal"), ordered crystalline materials lacking repeating structures, such as this Al-Pd-Mn alloy.

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Nobel_Dan_Shechtman_2011-12-08.ogv/250px--Nobel_Dan_Shechtman_2011-12-08.ogv.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser)](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/48/Nobel_Dan_Shechtman_2011-12-08.ogv?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=original)

Interview with Dan Shechtman after his Nobel lecture

A [quasiperiodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiperiodic "Quasiperiodic") [crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal "Crystal"), or, in short, [quasicrystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal "Quasicrystal"), is a [structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure "Structure") that is [ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_and_disorder_(physics) "Order and disorder (physics)") but not [periodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bravais_lattice "Bravais lattice"). A quasicrystalline pattern can continuously fill all available space, but it lacks [translational symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translational_symmetry "Translational symmetry"). "[Aperiodic mosaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aperiodic_mosaic "Aperiodic mosaic"), such as those found in the medieval Islamic mosaics of the [Alhambra palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhambra_palace "Alhambra palace") in [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain") and the [Darb-i Imam shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darb-e_Imam "Darb-e Imam") in [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), have helped scientists understand what quasicrystals look like at the atomic level. In those mosaics, as in quasicrystals, the patterns are regular – they follow mathematical rules – but they never repeat themselves."

The Nobel Committee at the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences said that "his discovery was extremely controversial," but that his work "eventually forced scientists to reconsider their conception of the very nature of matter." Through Shechtman's discovery, several other groups were able to form similar quasicrystals by 1987, finding these materials to have low [thermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conductivity_and_resistivity "Thermal conductivity and resistivity") and [electrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistivity_and_conductivity "Electrical resistivity and conductivity") conductivity, while possessing high structural stability. Quasicrystals have also been found naturally.

From the day Shechtman published his findings on quasicrystals in 1984 to the day [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling "Linus Pauling") died in 1994, Shechtman experienced hostility from Pauling toward the non-periodic interpretation. "For a long time it was me against the world," Shechtman said. "I was a subject of ridicule and lectures about the basics of [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography "Crystallography"). The leader of the opposition to my findings was the two-time Nobel Laureate Linus Pauling, the idol of the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society") and one of the most famous scientists in the world. For years, 'til his last day, he fought against quasi-periodicity in crystals. He was wrong, and after a while, I enjoyed every moment of this scientific battle, knowing that he was wrong." Pauling is noted saying "There is no such thing as quasicrystals, only quasi-scientists." Pauling was apparently unaware of a paper in 1981 by [H. Kleinert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagen_Kleinert "Hagen Kleinert") and K. Maki which had pointed out the possibility of a non-periodic [Icosahedral Phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_Phase "Icosahedral Phase") in [quasicrystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal "Quasicrystal") (see the
[historical notes](https://web.archive.org/web/20230213224502/http://users.physik.fu-berlin.de/~kleinert/icosahedral/index.html)). The head of Shechtman's research group told him to "go back and read the textbook" and a couple of days later "asked him to leave for 'bringing disgrace' on the team." Shechtman felt rejected. On publication of his paper, other scientists began to confirm and accept empirical findings of the existence of quasicrystals.

Quasicrystalline materials could be used in a large number of applications, including the formation of durable steel used for fine instrumentation, and non-stick insulation for electrical wires and cooking equipment., but presently have no technological applications.

The Nobel prize was 10 million [Swedish krona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_krona "Swedish krona") (approximately US$1.5 million).

## Presidential bid

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/Reuven_Rivlin_hosted_the_winners_declaration_event_of_the_2018_Wolf_Prize%2C_February_2018_%286355%29.jpg/250px-Reuven_Rivlin_hosted_the_winners_declaration_event_of_the_2018_Wolf_Prize%2C_February_2018_%286355%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Reuven_Rivlin_hosted_the_winners_declaration_event_of_the_2018_Wolf_Prize,_February_2018_(6355).jpg)

Shechtman and President [Reuven Rivlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuven_Rivlin "Reuven Rivlin"), present the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize "Wolf Prize") award in 2018. Behind them is an [Israeli basalt ash relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts_in_Israel "Visual arts in Israel").

On January 17, 2014, in an interview with Israel's [Channel One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_1_(Israel) "Channel 1 (Israel)"), Shechtman announced his candidacy for [President of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Israel "President of Israel"). Shechtman received the endorsement of the ten Members of [Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knesset "Knesset") required to run. In the [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Israeli_presidential_election "2014 Israeli presidential election"), held on June 10, 2014, he was awarded only one vote. This led Israeli press and Israeli humorists to qualify Shechtman as "quasi-president" in reference to the "quasi-scientist" quote.
