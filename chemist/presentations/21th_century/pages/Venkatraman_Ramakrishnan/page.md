**Venkatraman Ramakrishnan**  (born 1952) is a British-American [structural biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_biology "Structural biology"). He shared the 2009 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with [Thomas A. Steitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Steitz "Thomas A. Steitz") and [Ada Yonath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Yonath "Ada Yonath") for research on the structure and function of [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome").

Since 1999, he has worked as a group leader at the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(United_Kingdom) "Medical Research Council (United Kingdom)") (MRC) [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") (LMB) on the [Cambridge Biomedical Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Biomedical_Campus "Cambridge Biomedical Campus"), UK and is a Fellow of [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"). He served as [President of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Royal_Society "President of the Royal Society") from 2015 to 2020.

## Education and early life

Ramakrishnan was born in 1952 in [Chidambaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chidambaram "Chidambaram") in [Cuddalore district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuddalore_district "Cuddalore district") of [Tamil Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Nadu "Tamil Nadu"), India.

His parents, Prof. C. V. Ramakrishnan and Prof. [Rajalakshmi Ramakrishnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Rajalakshmi_(scientist) "R. Rajalakshmi (scientist)") were both scientists, and his father was head of the department of biochemistry at the [Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_Sayajirao_University_of_Baroda "Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda"). At the time of his birth, Ramakrishnan's father was away from India doing [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") with [David E. Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_E._Green "David E. Green") at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison") in the US. Ramakrishnan's mother obtained a PhD in psychology from [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University "McGill University") in 1959, completing it in only 18 months, and was mentored, among others, by [Donald O. Hebb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_O._Hebb "Donald O. Hebb").

Ramakrishnan has one sibling, his younger sister [Lalita Ramakrishnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalita_Ramakrishnan "Lalita Ramakrishnan"), who is professor of immunology and infectious diseases at the department of medicine, [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), and a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences").

Ramakrishnan moved to [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara "Vadodara") (previously also known as Baroda) in [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat "Gujarat") at the age of three, where he had his entire schooling at the Convent of Jesus and Mary, except for a year and a half (1960–61) which he and his family spent in [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide "Adelaide"), Australia. Following his pre-science at the [Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_Sayajirao_University_of_Baroda "Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda"), he did his undergraduate studies in the same university on a [National Science Talent Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Talent_Search_Examination "National Talent Search Examination"), graduating with a Bachelor of Science degree in physics in 1971. At the time, the physics course at Baroda was new, and based in part on the *[Berkeley Physics Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Physics_Course "Berkeley Physics Course")* and *[The Feynman Lectures on Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Feynman_Lectures_on_Physics "The Feynman Lectures on Physics")*.

Immediately after graduation, he moved to the US, where he obtained his Doctor of Philosophy degree in physics from [Ohio University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_University "Ohio University") in 1976 for research into the [ferroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferroelectricity "Ferroelectricity") [phase transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_transition "Phase transition") of [potassium dihydrogen phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopotassium_phosphate "Monopotassium phosphate") (KDP) supervised by Tomoyasu Tanaka. Then he spent two years studying biology as a graduate student at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego") while making a transition from theoretical physics to biology.

## Career and research

Ramakrishnan began work on [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosomes "Ribosomes") as a [postdoctoral fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher "Postdoctoral researcher") with [Peter Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Moore_(chemist) "Peter Moore (chemist)") at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"). After his postdoctoral fellowship, he initially could not find a faculty position even though he had applied to about 50 universities in the United States.

He continued to work on ribosomes from 1983 to 1995 as a staff scientist at [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory").

In 1995, he moved to the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah "University of Utah") as a professor of biochemistry, and in 1999, he moved to the Medical Research Council [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge"), England, where he had been a sabbatical visitor during 1991–92 on a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship "Guggenheim Fellowship").

In 1999, Ramakrishnan's laboratory published a 5.5 [angstrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angstrom "Angstrom") resolution structure of the [30S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30S "30S") subunit. The following year, his laboratory determined the complete molecular structure of the 30S subunit of the ribosome and its complexes with several [antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotics "Antibiotics"). This was followed by studies that provided structural insights into the mechanism that ensures the fidelity of [protein biosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_biosynthesis "Protein biosynthesis"). In 2007, his laboratory determined the atomic structure of the whole ribosome in complex with its [tRNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRNA "TRNA") and [mRNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRNA "MRNA") ligands. Since 2013, he has used [Cryogenic electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryogenic_electron_microscopy "Cryogenic electron microscopy") to work primarily on eukaryotic and mitochondrial translation. Ramakrishnan is also known for his work on [histone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histone "Histone") and [chromatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatin "Chromatin") structure.

As of 2019 his most cited papers according to [Google Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Scholar "Google Scholar") have been published in *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")*, *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_(journal) "Science (journal)")*, and *[Cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(journal) "Cell (journal)")*.

### Presidency of the Royal Society

Ramakrishnan's term as president of the Royal Society from 2015–2020 was dominated by [Brexit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit "Brexit") and, in his final year, the COVID-19 pandemic and its response. In an interview in July 2018, he said that Britain's decision to leave the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") was hurting Britain's reputation as a good place to work in science, commenting "It's very hard for the science community to see any advantages in [Brexit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit "Brexit"). They are pretty blunt about that." He saw advantages to both the UK and the EU for Britain to continue to be engaged in [Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_(satellite_navigation) "Galileo (satellite navigation)") and [Euratom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euratom "Euratom"), which, unlike the [European Medicines Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Medicines_Agency "European Medicines Agency"), are not EU agencies.

Ramakrishnan argued that a no-deal Brexit would harm science. Ramakrishnan wrote, "A deal on science is in the best interests of Europe as a whole and should not be sacrificed as collateral damage over disagreements on other issues. If we are going to successfully tackle global problems like climate change, human disease and food security, we can't do so in isolation. There is no scenario where trashing our relationships with our closest scientific collaborators in the EU gets us closer to these goals."

### Awards and honours

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/The_President%2C_Smt._Pratibha_Devisingh_Patil_presenting_Padma_Vibhushan_Award_to_Dr._Venkatraman_Ramakrishnan%2C_at_the_Civil_Investiture_Ceremony-I%2C_at_Rashtrapati_Bhavan%2C_in_New_Delhi_on_March_31%2C_2010.jpg/250px-thumbnail.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_President,_Smt._Pratibha_Devisingh_Patil_presenting_Padma_Vibhushan_Award_to_Dr._Venkatraman_Ramakrishnan,_at_the_Civil_Investiture_Ceremony-I,_at_Rashtrapati_Bhavan,_in_New_Delhi_on_March_31,_2010.jpg)

The President, Smt. Pratibha Devisingh Patil presenting Padma Vibhushan Award to Dr. Venkatraman Ramakrishnan, at the Civil Investiture Ceremony-I, at Rashtrapati Bhavan, in New Delhi on March 31, 2010

Ramakrishnan was elected a Member of the [European Molecular Biology Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Molecular_Biology_Organization "European Molecular Biology Organization") in 2002, a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2003 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 2003"), and a Member of the U.S. [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") in 2004.

In 2007, Ramakrishnan was awarded the [Louis-Jeantet Prize for Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis-Jeantet_Prize_for_Medicine "Louis-Jeantet Prize for Medicine") and the [Datta Lectureship and Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_European_Biochemical_Societies#Datta_Lectureship_and_Medal "Federation of European Biochemical Societies") of the [Federation of European Biochemical Societies (FEBS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_European_Biochemical_Societies "Federation of European Biochemical Societies").

Ramakrishnan was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 2009, along with [Thomas A. Steitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Steitz "Thomas A. Steitz") and [Ada Yonath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Yonath "Ada Yonath"). He received India's second highest civilian honor, the [Padma Vibhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Vibhushan "Padma Vibhushan"), in 2010.

In 2008, Ramakrishnan won the Heatley Medal of the British [Biochemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemical_Society "Biochemical Society"), and became a Fellow of [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge") and a foreign Fellow of the [Indian National Science Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Science_Academy "Indian National Science Academy"). He has been a member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina") and an [Honorary Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Fellow_of_the_Academy_of_Medical_Sciences "Honorary Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences") (Hon FMedSci) since 2010.

He has received honorary degrees from the [Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_Sayajirao_University_of_Baroda "Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda"), [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah "University of Utah"), [Ohio University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_University "Ohio University") and [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"). He is also an Honorary Fellow of [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"), [Somerville College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerville_College,_Oxford "Somerville College, Oxford"), and [The Queen's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Queen's_College,_Oxford "The Queen's College, Oxford").

Ramakrishnan was made a [Knight Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in the [2012 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_New_Year_Honours "2012 New Year Honours") for services to [molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology"), but does not generally use the associated title "Sir". That same year, he was awarded the [Sir Hans Krebs Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Hans_Krebs_Medal "Sir Hans Krebs Medal") by the FEBS. In 2014, he was awarded the XLVI [Jiménez-Díaz Prize](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premio%20Lección%20Conmemorativa%20Jiménez%20Díaz "es:Premio Lección Conmemorativa Jiménez Díaz") by the Fundación Conchita Rábago (Spain).

In 2017, Ramakrishnan received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").

Ramakrishnan was included as one of 25 Greatest Global Living Indians by [NDTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDTV "NDTV") Channel, India on 14 December 2013.

His certificate of election to the Royal Society reads:

> Ramakrishnan is internationally recognised for determination of the atomic structure of the [30S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30S "30S") ribosomal subunit. Earlier he mapped the arrangement of proteins in the 30S subunit by [neutron diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_diffraction "Neutron diffraction") and solved [X-ray structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography "X-ray crystallography") of individual components and their RNA complexes. Fundamental insights came from his crystallographic studies of the complete 30S subunit. The atomic model included over 1500 bases of [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA") and 20 associated proteins. The RNA interactions representing the [P-site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-site "P-site") [tRNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_RNA "Transfer RNA") and the mRNA binding site were identified and the likely modes of action of many clinically important antibiotics determined. His most recent work goes to the heart of the decoding mechanism showing the 30S subunit complexed with poly-U mRNA and the [stem-loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem-loop "Stem-loop") of the cognate [phenylalanine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenylalanine "Phenylalanine") tRNA. [Anti-codon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_RNA#Anticodon "Transfer RNA") recognition leaves the "[wobble" base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wobble_base_pair "Wobble base pair") free to accommodate certain non-Watson/Crick [basepairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_pair "Base pair"), thus providing an atomic description of both codon:anti-codon recognition and "wobble". He has also made substantial contributions to understanding how [chromatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatin "Chromatin") is organised, particularly the structure of linker [histones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histone "Histone") and their role in [higher order folding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_folding "Protein folding").

In 2020, he was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") and became a board member of the [British Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Library "British Library").

Ramakrishnan was made a [Member of the Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_Merit "Member of the Order of Merit") (OM) in 2022.

## Personal life

In 1975, Ramakrishnan married Vera Rosenberry, an author and illustrator of children's books. He has a step-daughter, Tanya Kapka, a physician, specializing in public health and health-care delivery to under-served communities; and a son, Raman Ramakrishnan, a cellist specializing in chamber music and professor at [Bard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bard_College "Bard College") in [New York State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State "New York State").
