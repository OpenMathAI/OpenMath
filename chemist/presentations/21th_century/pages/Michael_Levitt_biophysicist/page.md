**Michael Levitt**, [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): מיכאל לויט; born 9 May 1947) is a South African-born [biophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysicist "Biophysicist") and a professor of [structural biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_biology "Structural biology") at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"), a position he has held since 1987. Levitt received the 2013 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"), together with [Martin Karplus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Karplus "Martin Karplus") and [Arieh Warshel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arieh_Warshel "Arieh Warshel"), for "the development of multiscale models for complex chemical systems". In 2018, Levitt was a founding co-editor of the *[Annual Review of Biomedical Data Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Review_of_Biomedical_Data_Science "Annual Review of Biomedical Data Science")*.

## Early life and education

Michael Levitt was born in [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria "Pretoria"), South Africa, to a Jewish family from [Plungė](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plungė "Plungė"), [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania "Lithuania"); his father was from [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Lithuania "History of the Jews in Lithuania") and his mother from the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_the_Czech_Republic "History of the Jews in the Czech Republic"). He attended Sunnyside Primary School and then [Pretoria Boys High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria_Boys_High_School "Pretoria Boys High School") between 1960 and 1962. The family moved to England when he was 15. Levitt spent 1963 studying [applied mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematics "Applied mathematics") at the [University of Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pretoria "University of Pretoria"). He attended [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London "King's College London"), graduating with a [first-class honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_undergraduate_degree_classification#First-class_honours "British undergraduate degree classification") degree in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") in 1967.

In 1967, he visited Israel for the first time. Together with his Israeli wife, Rina, a [multimedia artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia "Multimedia"), he left to study at [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), where their three children were born. Levitt was a PhD student in [Computational biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_biology "Computational biology") at [Peterhouse, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge "Peterhouse, Cambridge"), and was based at the [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") from 1968 to 1972, where he developed a computer program for studying the conformations of molecules that underpinned much of his later work.

## Career and research

In 1979, he returned to Israel and conducted research at the [Weizmann Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute_of_Science "Weizmann Institute of Science") in [Rehovot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehovot "Rehovot"), becoming an Israeli citizen in 1980. He served in the [Israel Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces "Israel Defense Forces") for six weeks in 1985. In 1986, he began teaching at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"), and since then has split his time between Israel and California. He went on to gain a research fellowship at [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge "Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge").

From 1980 to 1987, he was Professor of Chemical Physics at the Weizmann Institute of Science, Rehovot. Thereafter, he served as Professor of Structural biology, at Stanford University, California.

- [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") Exchange Fellow, Weizmann Institute, Israel, 1967–68
- Staff Scientist, [MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRC_Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology"), Cambridge, 1973–80
- Professor of Chemical Physics, Weizmann Institute, 1980–87 (dept. chair 1980–83)
- Professor of Structural Biology, Stanford University, 1987–present

Levitt was one of the first researchers to conduct [molecular dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_dynamics "Molecular dynamics") simulations of DNA and proteins and developed the first software for this purpose. He is currently well known for developing approaches to predict [macromolecular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macromolecule "Macromolecule") [structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_validation "Structure validation"), having participated in many [Critical Assessment of Techniques for Protein Structure Prediction (CASP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASP "CASP") competitions, where he criticised molecular dynamics for inability to refine [protein structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_structure "Protein structure"). He has also worked on simplified representations of protein structure for analysing folding and packing, as well as developing scoring systems for large-scale sequence-structure comparisons. He has mentored many successful scientists, including [Mark Gerstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Bender_Gerstein "Mark Bender Gerstein") and [Ram Samudrala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Samudrala "Ram Samudrala"). [Cyrus Chothia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Chothia "Cyrus Chothia") was one of his colleagues.

### Industrial collaboration

Levitt has served on the Scientific Advisory Boards of the following companies: Dupont Merck Pharmaceuticals, AMGEN, Protein Design Labs, Affymetrix, Molecular Applications Group, 3D Pharmaceuticals, Algodign, Oplon Ltd, Cocrystal Discovery, InterX, and [StemRad, Ltd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StemRad "StemRad"),.

### COVID-19

Levitt has been outspoken during the ongoing [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), and made a number of wrong predictions on the disease's spread based on his own modelling. On 18 March 2020, he predicted that Israel would see less than ten deaths from COVID-19, and on 25 July 2020, he incorrectly predicted that the outbreak in the U.S. would be over by the end of August 2020 with a total of fewer than 170,000 deaths. As of November 2021, the U.S. was recording COVID-19 deaths at the rate of about 1,000 per day, while Israel has reported over 8,000 COVID-19 deaths since the start of the pandemic.

Levitt has also raised concerns about potential damaging effects of [COVID-19 lockdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns "COVID-19 lockdowns") orders on economic activity as well in increasing suicide and abuse rates, and has signed the [Great Barrington Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Barrington_Declaration "Great Barrington Declaration"), a statement supported by a group of academics advocating for alternatives to lockdowns which has been criticized by the [WHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization") and other public health organizations as dangerous and lacking in sound scientific basis.

Critics have expressed concern regarding Levitt's incorrect or potentially misleading predictions as well as his anti-lockdown positions, in part due to his status as a Nobel laureate and his large following on Twitter. [Maia Majumder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maia_Majumder "Maia Majumder"), a computational epidemiologist at [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School "Harvard Medical School"), stated that "Michael Levitt has a huge, huge following, so this creates lots of problems when he’s tweeting something that may be misinformative." [Randy Schekman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Schekman "Randy Schekman"), a 2013 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") winner, wrote of Levitt's expressed positions that "in this instance, I believe he crossed a boundary from data to public policy where the impact of his word as a Nobel laureate has undue influence."

### Awards and honors

Levitt was elected an [EMBO Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMBO_Membership "EMBO Membership") in 1983, a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2001 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 2001"), and a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 2002, and received the 2013 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, together with Martin Karplus and Arieh Warshel, "for the development of [multiscale models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiscale_modeling "Multiscale modeling") for complex chemical systems". He received the [DeLano Award for Computational Biosciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeLano_Award_for_Computational_Biosciences "DeLano Award for Computational Biosciences") in 2014. He was elected an [ISCB Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISCB_Fellow "ISCB Fellow") by the [International Society for Computational Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Society_for_Computational_Biology "International Society for Computational Biology") in 2015.

## Personal life

Levitt holds South African, American, British and Israeli citizenship.

His wife Rina died on 23 January 2017.

He is the sixth Israeli to be awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in under a decade.
