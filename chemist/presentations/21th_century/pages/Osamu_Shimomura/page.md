**Osamu Shimomura** (下村 脩, *Shimomura Osamu*; August 27, 1928 – October 19, 2018) was a Japanese [organic chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemist "Organic chemist") and [marine biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biologist "Marine biologist"), and [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus "Professor emeritus") at [Marine Biological Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Biological_Laboratory "Marine Biological Laboratory") (MBL) in [Woods Hole, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woods_Hole,_Massachusetts "Woods Hole, Massachusetts") and [Boston University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University_School_of_Medicine "Boston University School of Medicine"). He was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 2008 for the discovery and development of [green fluorescent protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_fluorescent_protein "Green fluorescent protein") (GFP) with two American scientists: [Martin Chalfie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Chalfie "Martin Chalfie") of Columbia University and [Roger Tsien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Y._Tsien "Roger Y. Tsien") of the University of California-San Diego.

## Biography

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Krugman-Tsien-Chalfie-Shimomura-Kobayashi-Masukawa-press_conference_Dec_07th%2C_2008-1.jpg/250px-Krugman-Tsien-Chalfie-Shimomura-Kobayashi-Masukawa-press_conference_Dec_07th%2C_2008-1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Krugman-Tsien-Chalfie-Shimomura-Kobayashi-Masukawa-press_conference_Dec_07th,_2008-1.jpg)

[Paul Krugman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Krugman "Paul Krugman"), [Roger Tsien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Tsien "Roger Tsien"), [Martin Chalfie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Chalfie "Martin Chalfie"), Osamu Shimomura, [Makoto Kobayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makoto_Kobayashi_(physicist) "Makoto Kobayashi (physicist)") and [Toshihide Masukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshihide_Masukawa "Toshihide Masukawa"), Nobel Prize Laureates 2008, at a press conference at the [Swedish Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy_of_Science "Swedish Academy of Science") in Stockholm.

Born in [Fukuchiyama, Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuchiyama,_Kyoto "Fukuchiyama, Kyoto") in 1928, Shimomura was brought up in [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo "Manchukuo") ([Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria "Manchuria"), China) and [Osaka, Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka,_Japan "Osaka, Japan") while his father served as an officer in the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army "Imperial Japanese Army"). Later, his family moved to [Isahaya, Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isahaya,_Nagasaki "Isahaya, Nagasaki"), 25 km from the epicenter of the August 1945 [atomic bombing of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombing_of_Nagasaki "Atomic bombing of Nagasaki"). He recalled hearing, as a 16-year-old boy, the bomber plane *[Bockscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bockscar "Bockscar")* before the atom bomb exploded. The explosion flash blinded Shimomura for about thirty seconds, and he was later drenched by the "black rain" bomb fallout. He overcame great odds in the following 11 years to earn an education and achieve academic success.

Shimomura's education opportunities were starkly limited in devastated, post-war Japan. Although he later recalled having no interest in the subject, he enrolled in the College of Pharmaceutical Sciences of Nagasaki Medical College (now [Nagasaki University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki_University "Nagasaki University") School of Pharmaceutical Sciences). The Medical College campus had been entirely destroyed by the atomic bomb blast, forcing the pharmacy school to relocate to a temporary campus near Shimomura's home. This proximity was the fortuitous reason he embarked upon the studies and career which would ultimately lead to unanticipated rewards. Shimomura was awarded a [BS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree in pharmacy in 1951, and he stayed on as a lab assistant through 1955.

Shimomura's mentor at Nagasaki helped him find employment as an assistant to Professor [Yoshimasa Hirata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshimasa_Hirata "Yoshimasa Hirata") at [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University") in 1956. While working for Professor Hirata, he received a [MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") degree in organic chemistry in 1958 and, before leaving Japan for an appointment at Princeton University, a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in organic chemistry in 1960 at Nagoya University. At Nagoya, Hirata assigned Shimomura the challenging task of determining what made the crushed remains of a type of [crustacean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crustacean "Crustacean") (Jp. *umi-hotaru*, lit. "sea-firefly", *[Vargula hilgendorfii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vargula_hilgendorfii "Vargula hilgendorfii")*) glow when moistened with water. This assignment led Shimomura to the successful identification of the protein causing the phenomenon, and he published the preliminary findings in the *[Bulletin of the Chemical Society of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_the_Chemical_Society_of_Japan "Bulletin of the Chemical Society of Japan")* in a paper titled "Crystalline Cypridina luciferin." The article caught the attention of Professor Frank Johnson at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), and Johnson successfully recruited Shimomura to work with him in 1960.

## Studies

Shimomura worked in the department of biology at [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") for Professor Johnson to study the [bioluminescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioluminescence "Bioluminescence") jellyfish *[Aequorea victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aequorea_victoria "Aequorea victoria")*, which they collected during many summers at the [Friday Harbor Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_Harbor_Laboratories "Friday Harbor Laboratories") of the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington "University of Washington"). In 1962, their work culminated in the discovery of the proteins [aequorin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aequorin "Aequorin") and [green fluorescent protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_fluorescent_protein "Green fluorescent protein") (GFP) in *A. victoria*; for this work, he was awarded a third of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2008.

## Family

His wife, Akemi, whom Shimomura met at [Nagasaki University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki_University "Nagasaki University"), is also an organic chemist and was a partner in his research activities. Their son, [Tsutomu Shimomura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsutomu_Shimomura "Tsutomu Shimomura"), is a computer security expert who was involved in the arrest of [Kevin Mitnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Mitnick "Kevin Mitnick"). Their daughter, Sachi Shimomura, is director of Undergraduate Studies for the English Department at [Virginia Commonwealth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Commonwealth_University "Virginia Commonwealth University") and the author of *Odd Bodies and Visible Ends in Medieval Literature*.

## Death

Shimomura died on October 19, 2018, of [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer") in Nagasaki.
