**William Standish Knowles** (June 1, 1917 – June 13, 2012) was an American [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist"). He was born in [Taunton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunton,_Massachusetts "Taunton, Massachusetts"). Knowles was one of the recipients of the 2001 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"). He split half the prize with [Ryōji Noyori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryōji_Noyori "Ryōji Noyori") for their work in [asymmetric synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_synthesis "Asymmetric synthesis"), specifically for his work in [hydrogenation reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_hydrogenation "Asymmetric hydrogenation"). The other half was awarded to [K. Barry Sharpless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Barry_Sharpless "K. Barry Sharpless") for his work in [oxidation reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidation "Oxidation").

## Education

Knowles attended [Berkshire School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire_School "Berkshire School") in [Sheffield, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield,_Massachusetts "Sheffield, Massachusetts"). He led his class academically and upon graduation was admitted to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). Feeling that he was too young to go to college, Knowles spent a year at [Phillips Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Academy "Phillips Academy") in [Andover, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andover,_Massachusetts "Andover, Massachusetts"). At the end of the year, he captured his first award in chemistry, the school's $50 Boylston Prize.

After his year in [preparatory school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University-preparatory_school "University-preparatory school"), Knowles attended Harvard, where he majored in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"), focusing on [organic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry"). He received his undergraduate degree in 1939, and attended [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") for graduate school.

## Awards and honors

- 1983 [Chemical Pioneer Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Pioneer_Award "Chemical Pioneer Award") from the [American Institute of Chemists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Chemists "American Institute of Chemists")

### Nobel Prize

He shared half of the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 2001 with [Ryōji Noyori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryōji_Noyori "Ryōji Noyori") for "their work on chirally catalysed hydrogenation reactions". The other half of the prize was awarded to [K. Barry Sharpless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Barry_Sharpless "K. Barry Sharpless") for the development of a range of catalytic asymmetric oxidations. Knowles developed one of the first [asymmetric hydrogenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_hydrogenation "Asymmetric hydrogenation") catalysts by replacing the achiral [triphenylphosphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triphenylphosphine "Triphenylphosphine") ligands in [Wilkinson's catalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkinson's_catalyst "Wilkinson's catalyst") with chiral [phosphine ligands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphine_ligand "Phosphine ligand"). This experimental catalyst was effective for [enantioselective synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantioselective_synthesis "Enantioselective synthesis"), achieving a modest 15% [enantiomeric excess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomeric_excess "Enantiomeric excess").

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Hydrogenation-Knowles1968.png/500px-Hydrogenation-Knowles1968.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hydrogenation-Knowles1968.png)

Knowles was also the first to apply enantioselective metal catalysis to industrial-scale synthesis; while working for the [Monsanto Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsanto_Company "Monsanto Company") he developed an enantioselective hydrogenation step for the production of [L-DOPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-DOPA "L-DOPA"), utilising the [DIPAMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DIPAMP "DIPAMP") ligand.

## Personal life

Following his retirement in 1986, Knowles resided in Chesterfield, Missouri, a suburb of St. Louis. In retirement he restored native prairie grasses on a 100-acre farm that his wife had inherited. He was married to his wife, Nancy, for 66 years and had four children, Elizabeth, Peter, Sarah and Lesley. He also had four grandchildren. Knowles died in Chesterfield on June 13, 2012, at age 95. He and his wife had previously stated that their farm would be donated to be converted into a city park after their deaths.
