**Akira Suzuki** (鈴木 章, *Suzuki Akira*; born September 12, 1930) is a Japanese chemist and [Nobel Prize Laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_Laureate "Nobel Prize Laureate") (2010), who first published the [Suzuki reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki_reaction "Suzuki reaction"), the [organic reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_reaction "Organic reaction") of an [aryl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryl "Aryl")- or [vinyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinyl_group "Vinyl group")-[boronic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boronic_acid "Boronic acid") with an aryl- or vinyl-[halide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halide "Halide") catalyzed by a [palladium(0)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium "Palladium") [complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_(chemistry) "Complex (chemistry)"), in 1979.

## Early life and education

Suzuki was born on September 12, 1930, in [Mukawa, Hokkaidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukawa,_Hokkaidō "Mukawa, Hokkaidō"), his father died when he was in high school. He studied chemistry at [Hokkaido University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaido_University "Hokkaido University") (Hokudai) and after receiving his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") while he worked there as assistant professor. He initially wanted to major in [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"), as his favorite subject in childhood was [arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic "Arithmetic"). It was an encounter with two books that became an opportunity to advance to the path of [organic synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_synthesis "Organic synthesis"), one is *Textbook of Organic Chemistry* written by [Louis Fieser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Fieser "Louis Fieser") of [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), and another is *Hydroboration* written by [Herbert C. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_C._Brown "Herbert C. Brown") of [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University "Purdue University").

## Career

From 1963 until 1965, Suzuki worked as a postdoctoral student with [Herbert C. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_C._Brown "Herbert C. Brown") at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University "Purdue University") and after returning to the Hokudai he became a full professor there. The postdoctoral experience was utilized in the study of the [coupling reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling_reaction "Coupling reaction") with his assistant [Norio Miyaura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norio_Miyaura "Norio Miyaura") and led to the discovery of [Suzuki reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki_reaction "Suzuki reaction") announced in 1979. Its organic [boronic acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boronic_acids "Boronic acids") with [aryl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryl "Aryl") and [vinyl group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinyl_group "Vinyl group") are stable to water and air, easy to handle, and because the conditions required for use are also relatively mild, even among the several cross-coupling techniques, it is said to be easy to use. Suzuki Coupling Full Mechanism 2

With his retirement from Hokudai in 1994 he took several positions in other universities: 1994–1995 [Okayama University of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okayama_University_of_Science "Okayama University of Science") and 1995–2002 [Kurashiki University of Science and the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurashiki_University_of_Science_and_the_Arts "Kurashiki University of Science and the Arts"). In addition, he was an invited professor at Purdue University (2001), [Academic Sinica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Sinica "Academic Sinica") and the [National Taiwan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Taiwan_University "National Taiwan University") (2002).

In 2010, Suzuki was jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize for Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") together with [Richard F. Heck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_F._Heck "Richard F. Heck") and [Ei-ichi Negishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ei-ichi_Negishi "Ei-ichi Negishi").

To celebrate International Year of Chemistry (IYC 2011), Suzuki was interviewed by the *[UNESCO Courier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Courier "UNESCO Courier")* magazine, he said：

> Today some people see chemistry just as a polluting industry, but that is a mistake ... Without it, productivity would drop and we could not enjoy the life we know today. If there is pollution, it is because we are releasing harmful substances. Obviously, we have to adapt treatment and management regimes and work to develop chemical substances and manufacturing processes that respect the environment.

In 2014, a Canadian-Chinese student asked for Suzuki's advice: "how can I become a great chemist like you?", Suzuki answered him: "... above all else, you must learn to see through the appearance to perceive the essence."

## Invention without patent

Suzuki has not obtained a patent on [Suzuki reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki_reaction "Suzuki reaction") technology because he thinks that the research was supported by government funds, therefore coupling technology has become widespread, and many products using this technology have been put into practical use. To date, there are more than 6,000 papers and patents related to Suzuki reaction.
