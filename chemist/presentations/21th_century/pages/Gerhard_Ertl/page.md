**Gerhard Ertl** (German pronunciation: [[ˈɡeːɐ̯haʁt ˈʔɛʁtl̩]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Gerhard_Ertl.ogg "File:De-Gerhard Ertl.ogg"); born 10 October 1936) is a German [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and a Professor [emeritus](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/emeritus "wikt:emeritus") at the Department of Physical Chemistry, [Fritz-Haber-Institut der Max-Planck-Gesellschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Haber_Institute_of_the_MPG "Fritz Haber Institute of the MPG") in Berlin, Germany. Ertl's research laid the foundation of modern [surface chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_chemistry "Surface chemistry"), which has helped explain how fuel cells produce energy without pollution, how catalytic converters clean up car exhausts and even why iron rusts, the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences said.

His work has paved the way for development of cleaner energy sources and will guide the development of fuel cells, said Astrid Graslund, secretary of the Nobel Committee for Chemistry.

He was awarded the 2007 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his studies of chemical processes on solid surfaces. The Nobel academy said Ertl provided a detailed description of how chemical reactions take place on surfaces. His findings applied in both academic studies and industrial development, the academy said. “Surface chemistry can even explain the destruction of the ozone layer, as vital steps in the reaction actually take place on the surfaces of small crystals of ice in the stratosphere,” the award citation reads.

In 2015, Ertl signed the [Mainau Declaration 2015 on Climate Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainau_Declaration "Mainau Declaration") on the final day of the 65th [Lindau Nobel Laureate Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindau_Nobel_Laureate_Meetings "Lindau Nobel Laureate Meetings"). The declaration was signed by a total of 76 Nobel Laureates and handed to then-President of the French Republic, [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Hollande "François Hollande"), as part of the [COP21 climate summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Climate_Change_Conference "Paris Climate Change Conference") in Paris.

## Biography

Ertl was born in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart "Stuttgart"), Germany, where he studied physics from 1955 to 1957 at the [Technische Hochschule Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Stuttgart "University of Stuttgart") and then at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris") (1957–1958) and [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich "LMU Munich") (1958–1959). He completed his [Diplom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplom "Diplom") in Physics at the Technische Hochschule Stuttgart in 1961, followed his thesis advisor [Heinz Gerischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Gerischer "Heinz Gerischer") from the [Max Planck Institute for Metals Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Metals_Research "Max Planck Institute for Metals Research") in Stuttgart to Munich and received his PhD degree from the [Technische Hochschule München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule_München "Technische Hochschule München") in 1965.

### Academic career

After completing his PhD, he became an assistant and lecturer at [Technische Hochschule München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule_München "Technische Hochschule München") (1965–1968). From 1968 to 1973, he was professor and director at [Technische Hochschule Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_University_Hannover "Leibniz University Hannover"); then, he became a professor at Institute for Physical Chemistry, LMU Munich (1973–1986). During the 1970s and 80s, he was also a visiting professor at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") (1976–1977), the [University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Milwaukee "University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee") (1979) and the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") (1981–82).

He became the director at the [Fritz Haber Institute of the MPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Haber_Institute_of_the_MPG "Fritz Haber Institute of the MPG") from 1986 till his retirement in 2004. In 1986, as honors, he was named "Honorary Professor" at the [Free University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Berlin "Free University of Berlin") and at the [Technische Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Berlin "Technische Universität Berlin"), and in 1996 at the [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin").

From 2008 to 2016, Ertl served as a member of the university council of [Technische Universität Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Darmstadt "Technische Universität Darmstadt").

### Research

Gerhard Ertl is known for determining the detailed molecular mechanisms of the catalytic synthesis of [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia "Ammonia") over iron ([Haber Bosch process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haber_Bosch_process "Haber Bosch process")) and the catalytic oxidation of [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide "Carbon monoxide") over [platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum "Platinum") ([catalytic converter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalytic_converter "Catalytic converter")). During his research he discovered the important phenomenon of oscillatory reactions on platinum surfaces and, using photoelectron microscopy, was able to image for the first time, the oscillating changes in surface structure and coverage that occur during reaction.

He always used new observation techniques like [low-energy electron diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-energy_electron_diffraction "Low-energy electron diffraction") (LEED) at the beginning of his career, later [ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet_photoelectron_spectroscopy "Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy") (UPS) and [scanning tunneling microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scanning_tunneling_microscope "Scanning tunneling microscope") (STM) yielding ground breaking results.
He won the [Wolf Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Chemistry "Wolf Prize in Chemistry") in 1998 along with [Gabor A. Somorjai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabor_A._Somorjai "Gabor A. Somorjai") of the University of California, Berkeley for "their outstanding contributions to the field of the [surface science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_science "Surface science") in general and for their elucidation of fundamental mechanisms of heterogeneous catalytic reactions at single crystal surface in particular."

Gerhard Ertl was awarded the 2007 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his studies of chemical processes on solid surfaces. The award, worth [SEK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Krona "Swedish Krona") 10 million ([US$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar")1.7 million, [£](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_sterling "Pound sterling")1.15 million), was announced on Ertl's 71st birthday. "I am speechless", Ertl told Associated Press from his office in Berlin. "I was not counting on this."

As of November 2022, Ertl has an [h-index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-index "H-index") of 124 according to [Scopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scopus "Scopus").

### Personal life

Ertl and his wife Barbara have two children and several grandchildren. His hobbies include playing the piano and also playing with his cats when he is not doing experiments. He identifies as Christian.
