**Karl Barry Sharpless** (born April 28, 1941) is an American [stereochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereochemistry "Stereochemistry"). He is a two-time [Nobel laureate in chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Chemistry "List of Nobel laureates in Chemistry"), known for his work on [stereoselective reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereoselective_reactions "Stereoselective reactions") and [click chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Click_chemistry "Click chemistry").

Sharpless was awarded half of the 2001 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") "for his work on chirally catalysed [oxidation reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidation_reaction "Oxidation reaction")", and one third of the 2022 prize, jointly with [Carolyn R. Bertozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolyn_R._Bertozzi "Carolyn R. Bertozzi") and [Morten P. Meldal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morten_P._Meldal "Morten P. Meldal"), "for the development of click chemistry and [bioorthogonal chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioorthogonal_chemistry "Bioorthogonal chemistry")". Sharpless is the fifth person (in addition to two organizations) to have twice been awarded a Nobel prize, along with [Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie "Marie Curie"), [John Bardeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bardeen "John Bardeen"), [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling "Linus Pauling") and [Frederick Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Sanger "Frederick Sanger"), and the third to have been awarded two prizes in the same discipline (after Bardeen and Sanger).

## Early life and education

Sharpless was born April 28, 1941, in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia"), Pennsylvania. His childhood was filled with summers at his family cottage on the [Manasquan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manasquan_River "Manasquan River") in [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey "New Jersey"). This is where Sharpless developed a love for fishing that he would continue throughout his life, spending summers in college working on fishing boats. He graduated from [Friends' Central School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends'_Central_School "Friends' Central School") in 1959, and continued his studies at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College "Dartmouth College"), earning an [A.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree in 1963. Sharpless originally planned to attend medical school after his undergraduate degree, but his research professor convinced him to continue his education in chemistry. He earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in organic chemistry from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") in 1968 under [Eugene van Tamelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_van_Tamelen "Eugene van Tamelen"). He continued post-doctoral work at Stanford University (1968–1969) with [James P. Collman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_P._Collman "James P. Collman"), working on [organometallic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organometallic_chemistry "Organometallic chemistry"). Sharpless then moved to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") (1969–1970), studying enzymology in [Konrad E. Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_E._Bloch "Konrad E. Bloch")'s lab.

## Academic career

Sharpless was a professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (1970–1977, 1980–1990) and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") (1977–1980). While at Stanford, Sharpless discovered [Sharpless asymmetric epoxidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpless_asymmetric_epoxidation "Sharpless asymmetric epoxidation"), which was used to make (+)-[disparlure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disparlure "Disparlure"). As of 2023, Sharpless led a laboratory at [Scripps Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Research "Scripps Research").

## Research

Sharpless developed stereoselective oxidation reactions, and showed that the formation of an inhibitor with femtomolar potency can be catalyzed by the enzyme [acetylcholinesterase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetylcholinesterase "Acetylcholinesterase"), beginning with an azide and an alkyne. He discovered several chemical reactions which have transformed asymmetric synthesis from science fiction to the relatively routine, including aminohydroxylation, dihydroxylation, and the Sharpless asymmetric epoxidation.

In 2001 he was awarded a half-share of the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his work on chirally catalyzed [oxidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidation "Oxidation") reactions ([Sharpless epoxidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpless_epoxidation "Sharpless epoxidation"), [Sharpless asymmetric dihydroxylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpless_asymmetric_dihydroxylation "Sharpless asymmetric dihydroxylation"), [Sharpless oxyamination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpless_oxyamination "Sharpless oxyamination")). The other half of the year's Prize was shared between [William S. Knowles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Knowles "William S. Knowles") and [Ryōji Noyori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryōji_Noyori "Ryōji Noyori") (for their work on stereoselective [hydrogenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogenation "Hydrogenation")).

The term "[click chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Click_chemistry "Click chemistry")" was coined by Sharpless around the year 2000, and was first fully described by Sharpless, [Hartmuth Kolb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartmuth_C._Kolb "Hartmuth C. Kolb"), and [M.G. Finn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.G._Finn "M.G. Finn") at [The Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Research_Institute "Scripps Research Institute") in 2001. This involves a set of highly selective, [exothermic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exothermic_reaction "Exothermic reaction") reactions which occur under mild conditions; the most successful example is the [azide alkyne Huisgen cycloaddition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azide_alkyne_Huisgen_cycloaddition "Azide alkyne Huisgen cycloaddition") to form [1,2,3-triazoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1,2,3-Triazole "1,2,3-Triazole").

As of 2024, Sharpless has an [h-index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-index "H-index") of 130 according to [Scopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scopus "Scopus").

## Awards and honors

Finn") (left) and [Hartmuth C. Kolb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartmuth_C._Kolb "Hartmuth C. Kolb") (right) at the 2022 Nobel awards ceremony

Sharpless is a two-time Nobel laureate. He is a recipient of the 2001 and 2022 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his work on "chirally catalysed oxidation reactions", and "[click chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Click_chemistry "Click chemistry")", respectively.

He was awarded the 2001 [Wolf Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Chemistry "Wolf Prize in Chemistry") together with [Henri B. Kagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_B._Kagan "Henri B. Kagan") and [Ryoji Noyori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryoji_Noyori "Ryoji Noyori") “for their work in developing asymmetric catalysis for the synthesis of chiral molecules, which aid in the manufacturing of multiple useful products.

In 2019, Sharpless was awarded the [Priestley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priestley_Medal "Priestley Medal"), the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society")'s highest honor, for "the invention of catalytic, asymmetric oxidation methods, the concept of click chemistry and development of the copper-catalyzed version of the azide-acetylene cycloaddition reaction". He received the [Gold Medal of the American Institute of Chemists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Chemists_Gold_Medal "American Institute of Chemists Gold Medal") in 2023.

He is Distinguished University Professor at [Kyushu University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu_University "Kyushu University"). He holds honorary degrees from the [KTH Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTH_Royal_Institute_of_Technology "KTH Royal Institute of Technology") (1995), [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich "Technical University of Munich") (1995), [Catholic University of Louvain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_catholique_de_Louvain "Université catholique de Louvain") (1996) and [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University "Wesleyan University") (1999).

## Personal life

Sharpless married Jan Dueser in 1965 and they have three children. He was blinded in one eye during a lab accident in 1970 where an [NMR tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NMR_tube "NMR tube") exploded, shortly after he arrived at MIT as an assistant professor. After this accident, Sharpless stresses "there's simply never an adequate excuse for not wearing safety glasses in the laboratory *at all times*."
