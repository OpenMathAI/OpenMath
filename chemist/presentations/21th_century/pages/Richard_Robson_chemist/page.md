**Richard Robson** [FAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Australian_Academy_of_Science "Fellow of the Australian Academy of Science") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (born 4 June 1937) is an English and Australian chemist and Professor of Chemistry at the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne "University of Melbourne"). Robson specialises in [coordination polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_polymers "Coordination polymers"), particularly [metal-organic frameworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal-organic_framework "Metal-organic framework"). He has been described as "a pioneer in crystal engineering involving [transition metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_metals "Transition metals")". In 2025, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") jointly with [Susumu Kitagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susumu_Kitagawa "Susumu Kitagawa") and [Omar M. Yaghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_M._Yaghi "Omar M. Yaghi") for the development of metal-organic frameworks.

## Early life and education

Robson was born in [Glusburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glusburn "Glusburn"), [West Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire "West Yorkshire") (now [North Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Yorkshire "North Yorkshire")), England, on 4 June 1937. He read chemistry at [Brasenose College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brasenose_College,_Oxford "Brasenose College, Oxford"), earning a BA in 1959 and a DPhil in 1962. His doctoral research, supervised by John A. Barltrop at the [Dyson Perrins Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyson_Perrins_Laboratory "Dyson Perrins Laboratory"), focused on the [photochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photochemistry "Photochemistry") of organic molecules.

Robson conducted postdoctoral research at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") (1962–64) and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") (1964–65) under [Henry Taube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Taube "Henry Taube") before accepting a lectureship in chemistry at the University of Melbourne in 1966, where he remained for the rest of his career.

## Research

Richard Robson's groundbreaking research established foundational principles in the field of coordination polymers, particularly for infinite polymeric frameworks—later termed [metal–organic frameworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal–organic_framework "Metal–organic framework") (MOFs). His interest in the field was sparked in 1974 while constructing large wooden models of crystalline structures for first-year chemistry lectures.

In the 1990s, Robson created a new class of coordination polymers that underpinned an entire modern field of chemistry. His innovative approach used copper(I), which favours a tetrahedral geometry, in combination with a custom-designed tetranitrile organic linker. This method produced crystalline scaffolds with a diamond-like structure but with significant, engineered void space within the framework.

## Honors and awards

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/2025_Nobel_laureates_in_chemistry_at_Stockholm_University.jpg/250px-2025_Nobel_laureates_in_chemistry_at_Stockholm_University.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:2025_Nobel_laureates_in_chemistry_at_Stockholm_University.jpg)

Robson with [Susumu Kitagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susumu_Kitagawa "Susumu Kitagawa") and [Omar Yaghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Yaghi "Omar Yaghi")

Robson received the Burrows Award from the Inorganic Division of The [Royal Australian Chemical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Chemical_Institute "Royal Australian Chemical Institute") in 1998 and was elected a Fellow of the [Australian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Academy_of_Science "Australian Academy of Science") in 2000. He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") in 2022.

Robson shared the 2025 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his early contribution to the field of [metal–organic frameworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal–organic_framework "Metal–organic framework") (MOFs).

## Personal life

His daughter is former TV presenter [Naomi Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Robson "Naomi Robson").
