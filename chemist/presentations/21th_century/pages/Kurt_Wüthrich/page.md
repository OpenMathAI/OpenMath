**Kurt Wüthrich** (born 4 October 1938 in [Aarberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarberg "Aarberg"), [Canton of Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bern "Canton of Bern")) is a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland") [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist")/[biophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics") and [Nobel Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") [laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate "Nobel laureate"), known for developing [nuclear magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance") (NMR) methods for studying biological [macromolecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macromolecules "Macromolecules").

## Early life and education

Born in [Aarberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarberg "Aarberg"), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland"), Wüthrich was educated in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"), [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics"), and [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") at the [University of Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bern "University of Bern") before pursuing his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") supervised by Silvio Fallab at the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel "University of Basel"), awarded in 1964.

## Career

After his PhD, Wüthrich continued [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") with Fallab for a short time before leaving to work at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") for two years from 1965 with [Robert E. Connick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_E._Connick "Robert E. Connick"). That was followed by a stint working with [Robert G. Shulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_G._Shulman "Robert G. Shulman") at the [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs "Bell Labs") in [Murray Hill, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Hill,_New_Jersey "Murray Hill, New Jersey") from 1967 to 1969.

Wüthrich returned to Switzerland, to [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich "Zürich"), in 1969, where he began his career there at the [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich "ETH Zürich"), rising to Professor of Biophysics by 1980. He currently maintains a laboratory at the [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich "ETH Zürich"), at [The Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scripps_Research_Institute "The Scripps Research Institute"), in [La Jolla, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla,_California "La Jolla, California") and at the iHuman Institute of [ShanghaiTech University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ShanghaiTech_University "ShanghaiTech University"). He has also been a visiting professor at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh "University of Edinburgh") (1997–2000), the [Chinese University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_University_of_Hong_Kong "Chinese University of Hong Kong") (where he was an Honorary Professor) and [Yonsei University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonsei_University "Yonsei University").

During his graduate studies Wüthrich started out working with [electron paramagnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_paramagnetic_resonance "Electron paramagnetic resonance") spectroscopy, and the subject of his PhD thesis was "the [catalytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis "Catalysis") activity of [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper") compounds in autoxidation reactions". During his time as a [postdoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoc "Postdoc") in [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") he began working with the newly developed and related technique of [nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance_spectroscopy "Nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy") to study the hydration of [metal complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_complexes "Metal complexes"). When Wüthrich joined the Bell Labs, he was put in charge of one of the first [superconducting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting "Superconducting") NMR spectrometers, and started studying the structure and dynamics of proteins. He has pursued this line of research ever since.

After returning to Switzerland, Wüthrich collaborated with, among others, Nobel laureate [Richard R. Ernst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_R._Ernst "Richard R. Ernst") on developing the first two-dimensional NMR experiments, and established the [nuclear Overhauser effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Overhauser_effect "Nuclear Overhauser effect") as a convenient way of measuring distances within [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein"). This research later led to the complete assignment of resonances for among others the [bovine pancreatic trypsin inhibitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BPTI "BPTI") and [glucagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucagon "Glucagon").

In October 2010, Wüthrich participated in the [USA Science and Engineering Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Science_and_Engineering_Festival "USA Science and Engineering Festival")'s Lunch with a Laureate program where middle and high school students will get to engage in an informal conversation with a Nobel Prize–winning scientist over a brown-bag lunch. Wüthrich is also a member on the [USA Science and Engineering Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Science_and_Engineering_Festival "USA Science and Engineering Festival")'s Advisory Board and a supporter of the [Campaign for the Establishment of a United Nations Parliamentary Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_the_Establishment_of_a_United_Nations_Parliamentary_Assembly "Campaign for the Establishment of a United Nations Parliamentary Assembly"), an organisation which campaigns for democratic reform in the United Nations.

Wüthrich is a member of the Executive Advisory Board of the [World.Minds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World.minds "World.minds") Foundation, where he contributes to international dialogue on science, research, and innovation policy.

## Awards and honors

He was awarded the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in 1991, the [Louis-Jeantet Prize for Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis-Jeantet_Prize_for_Medicine "Louis-Jeantet Prize for Medicine") in 1993, the [Otto Warburg Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Warburg_Medal "Otto Warburg Medal") in 1999 and half of the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 2002 for "his development of [nuclear magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance") [spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy "Spectroscopy") for determining the three-dimensional structure of biological macromolecules in solution". He received the Bijvoet Medal of the [Bijvoet Center for Biomolecular Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijvoet_Center_for_Biomolecular_Research "Bijvoet Center for Biomolecular Research") of [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University "Utrecht University") in 2008. He was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2010 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 2010"). In 2017 he was awarded the Endel Lippmaa Memorial Medal by the Estonian Academy of Sciences. He was also awarded the 2018 Fray International Sustainability Award at SIPS 2018 by FLOGEN Star Outreach.

## Personal details

On 2 April 2018, Dr. Wüthrich established permanent residency in [Shanghai, China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai "Shanghai"), after obtaining a Chinese permanent residence card.
