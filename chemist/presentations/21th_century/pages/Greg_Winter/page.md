**Sir Gregory Paul Winter** (born 14 April 1951) is a [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize")–winning [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people "British people") [molecular biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_Biologist "Molecular Biologist") best known for his work on the therapeutic use of [monoclonal antibodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoclonal_antibodies "Monoclonal antibodies"). His research career has been based almost entirely at the MRC [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") and the MRC [Centre for Protein Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Protein_Engineering "Centre for Protein Engineering"), in Cambridge, England.

He is credited with the invention of techniques to both [humanize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanized_antibody "Humanized antibody") (1986) and, later, to fully humanize using [phage display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phage_display "Phage display"), antibodies for therapeutic uses. Previously, antibodies had been derived from mice, which made them difficult to use in human therapeutics because the human immune system had anti-mouse reactions to them. For these developments Winter was awarded the 2018 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") along with [George Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Smith_(chemist) "George Smith (chemist)") and [Frances Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Arnold "Frances Arnold").

He is a Fellow of [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge") and was appointed [Master of Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Masters_of_Trinity_College,_Cambridge "List of Masters of Trinity College, Cambridge") on 2 October 2012, remaining in office until 2019. From 2006 to 2011, he was Deputy Director of the [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology"), [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(UK) "Medical Research Council (UK)"), acting Director from 2007 to 2008 and Head of the Division of Protein and Nucleic Acids Chemistry from 1994 to 2006. He was also Deputy Director of the MRC Centre for Protein Engineering from 1990 to its closure in 2010.

## Education

Winter was educated at the [Royal Grammar School, Newcastle upon Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Grammar_School,_Newcastle_upon_Tyne "Royal Grammar School, Newcastle upon Tyne"). He went on to study [Natural Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Sciences_(Cambridge) "Natural Sciences (Cambridge)") at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"), graduating from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") in 1973. He was awarded a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree, from the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, for research on the [amino acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid "Amino acid") sequence of [tryptophanyl tRNA synthetase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WARS_(gene) "WARS (gene)") from the bacterium *[Bacillus stearothermophilus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacillus_stearothermophilus "Bacillus stearothermophilus")* in 1977 supervised by [Brian S. Hartley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_S._Hartley "Brian S. Hartley"). Later, Winter completed a term of post-doctoral fellowship at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London "Imperial College London"), and another at the Institute of genetics in the University of Cambridge.

## Career and research

Following his PhD, Winter completed [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") at the [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") in Cambridge. He continued to specialise in protein and nucleic acid sequencing and became a Group Leader at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology in 1981. He became interested in the idea that all antibodies have the same basic structure, with only small changes making them specific for one target. [Georges J. F. Köhler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_J._F._Köhler "Georges J. F. Köhler") and [César Milstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/César_Milstein "César Milstein") had won the 1984 Nobel Prize for their work at the Laboratory of Molecular Biology, in discovering a method to isolate and reproduce individual, or monoclonal, antibodies from among the multitude of different antibody proteins that the immune system makes to seek and destroy foreign invaders attacking the body. These monoclonal antibodies had limited application in human medicine, because mouse monoclonal antibodies are rapidly inactivated by the human immune response, which prevents them from providing long-term benefits.

Winter pioneered a technique to "humanise" mouse monoclonal antibodies; a technique used in the development of [Campath-1H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemtuzumab "Alemtuzumab")  by the Laboratory of Molecular Biology and University of Cambridge scientists. This antibody eventually obtained regulatory approval for the treatment of multiple sclerosis and [chronic lymphocytic leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_lymphocytic_leukemia "Chronic lymphocytic leukemia"). Humanized monoclonal antibodies form the majority of antibody-based drugs on the market today and include several blockbuster antibodies, such as [Keytruda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembrolizumab "Pembrolizumab").

Winter founded [Cambridge Antibody Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Antibody_Technology "Cambridge Antibody Technology") in 1989, and Bicycle Therapeutics.
He worked on the Scientific Advisory Board of *Covagen*, (now part of [Cilag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilag "Cilag")) and is also the chairman of the Scientific Advisory Board for Biosceptre International Limited.

In 1989, Winter was a founder of [Cambridge Antibody Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Antibody_Technology "Cambridge Antibody Technology"), one of the early commercial biotech companies involved in antibody engineering. One of the most successful antibody drugs developed was HUMIRA ([adalimumab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adalimumab "Adalimumab")), which was discovered by Cambridge Antibody Technology as D2E7, and developed and marketed by [Abbott Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbott_Laboratories "Abbott Laboratories"). HUMIRA, an antibody to [TNF alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNF_alpha "TNF alpha"), was the world's first fully human antibody, which went on to become the world's top selling pharmaceutical with sales of over $18 billion in 2017. Cambridge Antibody Technology was acquired by [AstraZeneca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AstraZeneca "AstraZeneca") in 2006 for £702m.

In 2000, Winter founded Domantis to pioneer the use of domain antibodies, which use only the active portion of a full-sized antibody. Domantis was acquired by the pharmaceutical [GlaxoSmithKline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GlaxoSmithKline "GlaxoSmithKline") in December 2006 for £230 million.

Winter subsequently founded another company, Bicycle Therapeutics Limited as a start up company which is developing very small protein mimics based on a covalently bonded [hydrophobic core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophobic_effect "Hydrophobic effect").

### Awards and honours

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Greg_Winter_EM1B5921_%2831295405977%29.jpg/250px-Greg_Winter_EM1B5921_%2831295405977%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Greg_Winter_EM1B5921_(31295405977).jpg)

Greg Winter during Nobel press conference in Stockholm, December 2018

Winter was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1990 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1990") and awarded the [Royal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Medal "Royal Medal") by the society in 2011 "for his pioneering work in protein engineering and therapeutic monoclonal antibodies, and his contributions as an inventor and entrepreneur". He was given the [Scheele Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheele_Award "Scheele Award") in 1994.

In 1995, Winter won several international awards including the [King Faisal International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_International_Prize "King Faisal International Prize") for Medicine (Molecular Immunology) and in 1999, the Cancer Research Institute [William B. Coley Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_B._Coley_Award "William B. Coley Award"). Winter was formerly the Joint Head of the Division of Protein and [Nucleic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleic_acid "Nucleic acid") Chemistry-Biotechnology, and was Deputy Director, at the [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology"), Cambridge, an institution funded by the UK [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(United_Kingdom) "Medical Research Council (United Kingdom)"). He was also Deputy Director of the MRC's [Centre for Protein Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Protein_Engineering "Centre for Protein Engineering") until its absorption into the Laboratory of Molecular Biology. He is a member of the Advisory Council for the [Campaign for Science and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_Science_and_Engineering "Campaign for Science and Engineering"). Winter was appointed [Commander of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire "Commander of the Order of the British Empire") (CBE) in 1997 and [Knight Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in 2004. He served as [Master of Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Masters_of_Trinity_College,_Cambridge "List of Masters of Trinity College, Cambridge") from 2012 to 2019. In 2015 he received the [Wilhelm Exner Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Exner_Medal "Wilhelm Exner Medal").

Along with [George Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_P._Smith_(chemist) "George P. Smith (chemist)"), Winter was awarded half of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry on 3 October 2018 for his work on phage displays for antibodies (while [Frances Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Arnold "Frances Arnold") received the other half of the prize that same year "for the [directed evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_evolution "Directed evolution") of enzymes"). In 2020 he was featured on *The Times'* 'Science Power List'. In 2024 he received the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal "Copley Medal") of the Royal Society. In 2025, Winter received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").
