**Richard Frederick Heck** (August 15, 1931 – October 9, 2015) was an American chemist noted for the discovery and development of the [Heck reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heck_reaction "Heck reaction"), which uses [palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium "Palladium") to catalyze [organic chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemical "Organic chemical") reactions that couple [aryl halides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryl_halide "Aryl halide") with [alkenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkene "Alkene"). The analgesic [naproxen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naproxen "Naproxen") is an example of a compound that is prepared industrially using the Heck reaction.

For his work in [palladium-catalyzed coupling reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium-catalyzed_coupling_reactions "Palladium-catalyzed coupling reactions") and [organic synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_synthesis "Organic synthesis"), Heck was awarded the 2010 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"), shared with the Japanese chemists [Ei-ichi Negishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ei-ichi_Negishi "Ei-ichi Negishi") and [Akira Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Suzuki_(chemist) "Akira Suzuki (chemist)").

## Early life and education

Heck was born in [Springfield, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Massachusetts "Springfield, Massachusetts"), in 1931. He moved to Los Angeles when eight years old and later attended the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles "University of California, Los Angeles") (UCLA), gaining a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") in 1952 and then a Ph.D. in 1954 working under the supervision of [Saul Winstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Winstein "Saul Winstein") on the chemistry of aryl sulfonates. After postdoctoral research at the [ETH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich") in [Zurich, Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich,_Switzerland "Zurich, Switzerland") with [Vladimir Prelog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Prelog "Vladimir Prelog"), and then back at UCLA, Heck took a position with the [Hercules Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules_Corporation "Hercules Corporation") in [Wilmington, Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_Delaware "Wilmington, Delaware") in 1956, working initially on polymer chemistry.

## Career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Nobel_Prize_2010-Press_Conference_KVA-DSC_8019.jpg/250px-Nobel_Prize_2010-Press_Conference_KVA-DSC_8019.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nobel_Prize_2010-Press_Conference_KVA-DSC_8019.jpg)

[Peter Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Diamond "Peter Diamond"), [Dale T. Mortensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_T._Mortensen "Dale T. Mortensen"), [Christopher A. Pissarides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_A._Pissarides "Christopher A. Pissarides"), [Konstantin Novoselov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Novoselov "Konstantin Novoselov"), [Andre Geim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Geim "Andre Geim"), [Akira Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Suzuki_(chemist) "Akira Suzuki (chemist)"), [Ei-ichi Negishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ei-ichi_Negishi "Ei-ichi Negishi"), and Richard Heck, Nobel Prize Laureates 2010, at a press conference at the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences") in Stockholm.

At Hercules, Heck soon became interested in [organometallic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organometallic_chemistry "Organometallic chemistry"), including work with [David S. Breslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_S._Breslow "David S. Breslow") on [organocobalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organocobalt "Organocobalt") reactions. This led to the development of the [Heck reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heck_reaction "Heck reaction"), which began with his investigation during the late 1960s of the coupling of [arylmercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organomercury "Organomercury") compounds with olefins using [palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium "Palladium") as a catalyst. This work was published in a series of seven consecutive articles in the *[Journal of the American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_American_Chemical_Society "Journal of the American Chemical Society")* for which Heck was the sole author.

During the early 1970s, Tsutomu Mizoroki independently reported the use of the less toxic aryl halides as the coupling partner in the reaction. Heck became a professor of chemistry at the [University of Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Delaware "University of Delaware")'s Department of Chemistry and Biochemistry in 1971, where he continued to improve the transformation, developing it into a powerful synthetic method for organic synthesis.

The importance of this reaction grew as it was taken up by others in the organic synthesis community. In 1982, Heck was able to write an *[Organic Reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_Reactions "Organic Reactions")* chapter that covered all the known instances in just 45 pages. By 2002, applications had grown to the extent that the *Organic Reactions* chapter published that year, limited to [intramolecular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramolecular_reaction "Intramolecular reaction") Heck reactions, covered 377 pages. These reactions, a small part of the total, couple two parts of the same molecule.
The reaction is now one of the most widely used methods for the creation of [carbon-carbon bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon-carbon_bond "Carbon-carbon bond") in the synthesis of [organic chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_compound "Organic compound"). It has been subject to numerous scientific review articles, including a monograph dedicated to this subject published in 2009.

Heck's contributions were not limited to the activation of halides by the oxidative addition of palladium. He was the first to fully characterize a π-allyl metal complex, and the first to elucidate the mechanism of alkene [hydroformylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroformylation "Hydroformylation").

### Palladium-catalyzed coupling reactions

Heck's work set the stage for a variety of other [palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium "Palladium")-catalyzed coupling reactions, including those of aryl halides with derivatives of [boronic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boronic_acid "Boronic acid") ([Suzuki–Miyaura coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki_reaction "Suzuki reaction")), [organotin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organotin "Organotin") reagents ([Stille coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stille_reaction "Stille reaction")), [organomagnesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organomagnesium "Organomagnesium") compounds ([Kumada-Corriu coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumada_coupling "Kumada coupling")), [silanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silane "Silane") ([Hiyama coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiyama_coupling "Hiyama coupling")), and [organozincs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organozinc "Organozinc") ([Negishi coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negishi_coupling "Negishi coupling")), as well as with amines ([Buchwald–Hartwig amination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchwald–Hartwig_amination "Buchwald–Hartwig amination")) and [alcohols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohols "Alcohols"). These palladium-catalyzed coupling reactions are now widely practiced in organic synthesis, including for the manufacture of [pharmaceutical drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical_drug "Pharmaceutical drug") such as [naproxen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naproxen "Naproxen").

Of the several reactions developed by Heck, the greatest societal impact has been from the palladium-catalyzed coupling of an alkyne with an aryl [halide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halide "Halide"). This is the reaction that was used to couple fluorescent dyes to DNA bases, allowing the automation of [DNA sequencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_sequencing "DNA sequencing") and the examination of the [human genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_genome "Human genome"); the reaction also allows biologically important proteins to be tracked. In Sonogashira's original report of what is now known as the [Sonogashira coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonogashira_coupling "Sonogashira coupling"), his group modified an alkyne coupling procedure previously reported by Heck, by adding a copper(I) salt.

## Later life and death

Heck retired from the University of Delaware in 1989, where he became the Willis F. Harrington Professor Emeritus in the Department of Chemistry and Biochemistry. Its annual lectureship was named in his honor in 2004. In 2005, he was awarded the [Wallace H. Carothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_H._Carothers "Wallace H. Carothers") Award, which recognizes creative applications of chemistry that have had substantial commercial impact. He was awarded the 2006 [Herbert C. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_C._Brown "Herbert C. Brown") Award for creative research in synthetic methods. On October 6, 2010, the Swedish Royal Academy of Sciences awarded Heck the Nobel Prize in Chemistry, which he shared with [Ei-ichi Negishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ei-ichi_Negishi "Ei-ichi Negishi") and [Akira Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Suzuki_(chemist) "Akira Suzuki (chemist)") "for palladium-catalyzed cross couplings in organic synthesis". In 2011, Heck was awarded the [Glenn T. Seaborg Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_T._Seaborg_Medal "Glenn T. Seaborg Medal") for this work. In 2012, he was appointed by [De La Salle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_La_Salle_University "De La Salle University") in [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila "Manila") as an [adjunct professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunct_professor "Adjunct professor") in its chemistry department. He had moved to [Quezon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon_City "Quezon City"), [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") after retirement, with his wife, Socorro Nardo-Heck. The couple had no children.

Heck died on October 9, 2015, in [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila "Manila") in a public hospital. His wife predeceased him by 2 years.

## Honorary degrees

Heck received honorary doctorates from the Faculty of Pharmacy at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University") in 2011 and De La Salle University in 2012.
