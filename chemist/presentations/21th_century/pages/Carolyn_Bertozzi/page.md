**Carolyn Ruth Bertozzi** (born October 10, 1966) is an American [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") and [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Laureate_in_Chemistry "Nobel Laureate in Chemistry"), known for her wide-ranging work spanning both chemistry and biology. She coined the term "[bioorthogonal chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioorthogonal_chemistry "Bioorthogonal chemistry")" for chemical reactions compatible with living systems, and is a major figure in the field of [glycobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycobiology "Glycobiology"), the study of structural carbohydates or [glycans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycans "Glycans"). Her work focuses on developing tools of [analytical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_chemistry "Analytical chemistry") to study glycans and their role in diseases such as cancer, inflammation, and viral infections like [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19"). At [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"), she holds the Anne T. and [Robert M. Bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bass_(businessman) "Robert Bass (businessman)") Professorship in the School of Humanities and Sciences. Bertozzi is also an Investigator at the [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute") (HHMI) and is the former director of the [Molecular Foundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_Foundry "Molecular Foundry"), a [nanoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoscience "Nanoscience") research center at [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Laboratory "Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory"). Since 2024, she has served as a scientific advisory board member of [Arc Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_Institute "Arc Institute").

She received the [MacArthur "genius" award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellowship "MacArthur Fellowship") at age 33. In 2010, she was the first woman to receive the [Lemelson–MIT Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemelson–MIT_Prize "Lemelson–MIT Prize") faculty award. She is a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") (2005), the [Institute of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Medicine "Institute of Medicine") (2011), and the [National Academy of Inventors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Inventors "National Academy of Inventors") (2013). In 2014, it was announced that Bertozzi would lead *[ACS Central Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACS_Central_Science "ACS Central Science")*, the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society")'s first peer-reviewed [open access journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_access_journal "Open access journal"), which offers all content free to the public. Since 2021 she has been a member of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei "Accademia dei Lincei").

Bertozzi was awarded the 2022 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"), jointly with [Morten P. Meldal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morten_P._Meldal "Morten P. Meldal") and [Karl Barry Sharpless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Barry_Sharpless "Karl Barry Sharpless"), "for the development of [click chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Click_chemistry "Click chemistry") and bioorthogonal chemistry".

## Education

Carolyn Bertozzi received her B.A., *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude "Summa cum laude")*, in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), where she worked with Professor Joe Grabowski on the design and construction of a photoacoustic calorimeter. Grabowski was impressed with her work and required Bertozzi to write a thesis on this project, which was submitted and Bertozzi won the [Thomas T. Hoopes Undergraduate Thesis Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_T._Hoopes_Prize "Thomas T. Hoopes Prize"). While an undergraduate, she played in several bands, notably Bored of Education with future [Rage Against the Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rage_Against_the_Machine "Rage Against the Machine") guitarist [Tom Morello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Morello "Tom Morello"). After graduating from Harvard in 1988, she worked at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs "Bell Labs") with Chris Chidsey.

Bertozzi completed her Ph.D. in chemistry at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1993 with Mark Bednarski, working on the [chemical synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_synthesis "Chemical synthesis") of [oligosaccharide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligosaccharide "Oligosaccharide") analogs. While at Berkeley, she discovered that viruses can bind to sugars in the body. The discovery led to her field of research, [glycobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycobiology "Glycobiology"). During Bertozzi's third year of graduate school, Bednarski was diagnosed with colon cancer, which resulted in him taking a leave of absence and changing his career path by enrolling in medical school. This left Bertozzi and the rest of the lab to complete their Ph.D. work with no direct supervision.

## Career and research

After graduating from Berkeley with a Ph.D., Bertozzi was a [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral "Postdoctoral") [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow "Fellow") at [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco "University of California, San Francisco") (UCSF) with Steven Rosen, where she studied the activity of [endothelial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endothelial "Endothelial") oligosaccharides in promoting cell adhesion at [inflammation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflammation "Inflammation") sites. While working with Rosen at UCSF, Bertozzi was able to modify the protein and sugar molecules in the walls of living cells so that the cells accept foreign materials such as implants.

In 1996, Bertozzi became a faculty member in the [UC Berkeley College of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Berkeley_College_of_Chemistry "UC Berkeley College of Chemistry") and a faculty scientist at [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Laboratory "Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory"), where she served as the director of the [Molecular Foundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_Foundry "Molecular Foundry"). She has been an investigator with [HHMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HHMI "HHMI") since 2000. In 1999, while working with HHMI and at Berkeley, she founded the field of [bioorthogonal chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioorthogonal_chemistry "Bioorthogonal chemistry") and coined the term in 2003. This new field and technique allows researchers to chemically modify molecules in living organisms and not interrupt the processes of the cell. In 2015, Bertozzi moved to Stanford University to join the ChEM-H Institute.

Bertozzi studies the [glycobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycobiology "Glycobiology") of underlying diseases such as [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer"), inflammatory disorders such as [arthritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthritis "Arthritis"), and [infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_diseases "Infectious diseases") such as [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis"). In particular, Bertozzi has advanced the understanding of cell surface [oligosaccharides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligosaccharides "Oligosaccharides") involved in cell recognition and inter-cellular communication. Bertozzi has applied the techniques of [bioorthogonal chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioorthogonal_chemistry "Bioorthogonal chemistry") to study [glycocalyx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycocalyx "Glycocalyx"), the sugars that surround the cell membrane. Her discoveries have advanced the field of biotherapeutics. Her lab has also developed tools for research. One such development is creating chemical tools for studying [glycans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycans "Glycans") in living systems. In 2018, her lab reported a solvatochromic trehalose probe for the rapid detection of *[Mycobacterium tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycobacterium_tuberculosis "Mycobacterium tuberculosis")* in sputum. In 2017, due to her lab's discovery of linking the sugars on the surface of cancer cells and their ability to avoid the immune system defenses, she was invited to speak at Stanford's [TED talk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TED_talk "TED talk"), giving a talk entitled "What the sugar coating on your cells is trying to tell you".

### Biotechnology startups

In 2001, Bertozzi and Steve Rosen co-founded **Thios Pharmaceuticals** in [Emeryville, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeryville,_California "Emeryville, California"), the first company to target [sulfation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfation "Sulfation") pathways. Thios Pharmaceuticals dissolved in 2005.

In 2008, Bertozzi co-founded **Redwood Bioscience**, also in [Emeryville, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeryville,_California "Emeryville, California"), with her former graduate student David Rabuka. Redwood Bioscience is a biotechnology company that uses [SMARTag, a site-specific protein modification technology](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1InC-VR1eNg) that allows small drugs to attach to sites on the proteins and can be used to help fight cancers. Redwood Bioscience was acquired by [Catalent Pharma Solutions](https://biologics.catalent.com/biologics/drug-substance/bioconjugate-development/precision-based-technology/) in 2014. Bertozzi remains a part of the advisory board for the biologics sector of the company.

In 2014, she co-founded **Enable Biosciences** of [South San Francisco, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_San_Francisco,_California "South San Francisco, California"). It focuses on biotechnologies for at-home diagnoses for [type 1 diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_1_diabetes "Type 1 diabetes"), [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV "HIV"), and other diseases.

Bertozzi became a co-founder of **Palleon Pharma** of [Waltham, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltham,_Massachusetts "Waltham, Massachusetts"), in 2015. Palleon Pharma focuses on investigating glycoimmune checkpoint inhibitors as a potential treatment for cancer.

In 2017, Bertozzi helped found **InterVenn Biosciences**, which uses [mass spectrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometry "Mass spectrometry") and [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") to enhance [glycoproteomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycoproteomics "Glycoproteomics") for target and [biomarker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomarker "Biomarker") discovery, [ovarian cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovarian_cancer "Ovarian cancer") diagnostics, and predicting the successes and failures of clinical trials.

She co-founded the Grace Science Foundation in 2018. The foundation focuses on curing NGLY1 deficiency through developing therapeutics that are efficient and inexpensive.

In 2019, she co-founded both OliLux Biosciences and Lycia Therapeutics. OliLux Biosciences develops new methods for tuberculosis detection. The founding of Lycia Therapeutics occurred when Bertozzi's group discovered lysosome-targeting chimeras (LYTACs). The new molecule class may be able to degrade some cardiovascular disease and cancer targets. Lycia Therapeutics focuses on developing technology which utilizes lysosome-targeting chimeras (LYTACs).

Bertozzi served on [Eli Lilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Lilly_and_Company "Eli Lilly and Company")'s board of directors from 2017 to 2021, including on its Science and Technology Committee, and resigned after Lilly entered a licensing agreement with [Lycia Therapeutics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycia_Therapeutics?action=edit&redlink=1 "Lycia Therapeutics"), which she co-founded. She has also served on the research advisory boards of pharmaceutical companies including [GlaxoSmithKline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GlaxoSmithKline "GlaxoSmithKline"), and on the scientific advisory board of [Colossal Biosciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossal_Biosciences "Colossal Biosciences") since 2021.

## Personal life

Bertozzi grew up in [Lexington, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Massachusetts "Lexington, Massachusetts"), the daughter of the late Norma Gloria (Berringer) and William Bertozzi. Her father was of [Italian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Americans "Italian Americans"). Her maternal grandparents were from [Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Scotia "Nova Scotia"), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada"). She has two sisters, one of whom, [Andrea Bertozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Bertozzi "Andrea Bertozzi"), is on the mathematics faculty at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles "University of California, Los Angeles"). Her father was a physics professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology").

She briefly considered a music major after her keyboard skills earned her places in several college rock bands, but felt she was "always centered on the sciences." She continued to make room for music, eventually learning bass guitar.

Bertozzi is a [lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian "Lesbian") and has been out since the late 1980s. She and her wife have three sons. As an open lesbian in academia and science, she has served as a role model for students and colleagues.
