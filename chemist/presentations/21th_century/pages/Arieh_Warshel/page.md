**Arieh Warshel** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): אריה ורשל; born November 20, 1940) is an Israeli-American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist") and [biophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysicist "Biophysicist"). He is a pioneer in computational studies on functional properties of [biological molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomolecule "Biomolecule"), Distinguished Professor of Chemistry and Biochemistry, and holds the Dana and David Dornsife Chair in Chemistry at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California "University of Southern California"). He received the 2013 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"), together with [Michael Levitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Levitt_(biophysicist) "Michael Levitt (biophysicist)") and [Martin Karplus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Karplus "Martin Karplus") for "the development of [multiscale models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiscale_modeling "Multiscale modeling") for complex chemical systems".

## Biography

Warshel was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") family in 1940 in [kibbutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibbutz "Kibbutz") [Sde Nahum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sde_Nahum "Sde Nahum"), [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine "Mandatory Palestine"). Warshel served in the [Israeli Armored Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_Corps_(Israel) "Armored Corps (Israel)"). After serving the Israeli Army (final rank [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_(armed_forces) "Captain (armed forces)")), Warshel attended the [Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion "Technion"), Haifa, where he received his [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree in chemistry, *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude "Summa cum laude")*, in 1966. Subsequently, he earned both [MSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") and [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degrees in [Chemical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_physics "Chemical physics") (in 1967 and 1969, respectively), with [Shneior Lifson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shneior_Lifson "Shneior Lifson") at [Weizmann Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute_of_Science "Weizmann Institute of Science"), Israel. After his PhD, he did postdoctoral work at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") until 1972, and from 1972 to 1976 he returned to the Weizmann Institute and worked for the [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology"), Cambridge, England. After being denied tenure by Weizmann Institute in 1976, he joined the faculty of the department of chemistry at [USC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California "University of Southern California"). He was awarded the 2013 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry").

As a soldier, he fought in both the 1967 [Six-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-Day_War "Six-Day War") and the [1973 Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Yom_Kippur_War "1973 Yom Kippur War"), attaining the rank of captain in the [IDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces "Israel Defense Forces").

## Current activities

As part of [Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen "Shenzhen")'s 13th Five-Year Plan funding research in emerging technologies and opening "Nobel laureate research labs", in April 2017 he opened the Warshel Institute for Computational Biology at the [Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_University_of_Hong_Kong,_Shenzhen "Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen") campus.

In 2025, Warshel was elected to the Serbian National Academy of Science (SASA), recognizing his continued contributions to scientific innovation and discovery.

## Honors

Warshel is known for his work on computational biochemistry and biophysics, in particular for pioneering [computer simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_simulation "Computer simulation") of the functions of biological systems, and for developing what is known today as Computational Enzymology.
He is a member of many scientific organisations, most importantly:

- Elected member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") (2009)
- Fellow of the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry") (2008)
- Fellow of the [Biophysical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysical_Society "Biophysical Society") (2000)
- Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science") (2012)
- Honorary fellow of the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry") (2014)
- Honorary doctorate at [Bar-Ilan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar-Ilan_University "Bar-Ilan University") (2014)
- Honorary doctorate of the Faculty of Science and Technology at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University") (2015)

## Major research achievements

Arieh Warshel made major contributions in introducing computational methods for structure–function correlation of biological molecules, pioneering and co-pioneering programs, methods and key concepts for detailed computational studies of functional properties of biological molecules using [Cartesian-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate_system "Cartesian coordinate system") [force field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_field_(chemistry) "Force field (chemistry)") programs, the combined [Quantum Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Chemistry "Quantum Chemistry")/[Molecular mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_mechanics "Molecular mechanics") (i.e., [QM/MM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QM/MM "QM/MM")) method for simulating enzymatic reactions, the first [molecular dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_dynamics "Molecular dynamics") simulation of a biological process, microscopic electrostatic models for proteins, [free energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbs_free_energy "Gibbs free energy") [perturbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perturbation_theory "Perturbation theory") in [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") and other key advances. It was for the development of these methods that Warshel shared the 2013 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry").
