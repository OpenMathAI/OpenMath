**Paul Lawrence Modrich** (born June 13, 1946) is an American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"), [James B. Duke Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_B._Duke_Professor "James B. Duke Professor") of [Biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University") and Investigator at the [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute"). He is known for his research on [DNA mismatch repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_mismatch_repair "DNA mismatch repair"). Modrich received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") 2015, jointly with [Aziz Sancar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aziz_Sancar "Aziz Sancar") and [Tomas Lindahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomas_Lindahl "Tomas Lindahl").

## Early life and education

Paul Modrich presenting himself

Modrich was born on June 13, 1946, in [Raton, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raton,_New_Mexico "Raton, New Mexico") to Laurence Modrich and Margaret McTurk. He has a younger brother Dave. His father was a biology teacher and coach for basketball, football and tennis at [Raton High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raton_High_School "Raton High School") where he graduated in 1964. Modrich is of [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Americans "Croatian Americans"), [Montenegrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrin_Americans "Montenegrin Americans"), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans "German Americans") and [Scottish (Gaelic)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch-Irish_Americans "Scotch-Irish Americans") origin. His paternal grandfather, of Croatian descent, is probably from the small village of [Modrići](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modrić_(surname) "Modrić (surname)") near [Zadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zadar "Zadar"), and grandmother of Montenegrin descent, both immigrated to the United States from coastal [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia "Croatia") in the late 19th century. His maternal family is of mixed German and Scotch-Irish descent. Modrich married fellow scientist Vickers Burdett in 1980.

Modrich obtained a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientiæ_Baccalaureus "Scientiæ Baccalaureus") degree from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") in 1968 and subsequently a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") in 1973. He continued his research as a postdoc in the lab of [Charles C. Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_C._Richardson "Charles C. Richardson") at [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School "Harvard Medical School") for a year (1973–1974).

## Research

Modrich became an assistant professor at the chemistry department of [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1974. He joined Duke University's faculty in 1976 and has been a Howard Hughes Investigator since 1995. He works primarily on strand-directed mismatch repair. His lab demonstrated how DNA mismatch repair serves as a copyeditor to prevent errors from [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") polymerase. [Matthew Meselson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Meselson "Matthew Meselson") previously proposed the existence of recognition of mismatches. Modrich performed biochemical experiments to study mismatch repair in *E. coli*. They later searched for proteins associated with mismatch repair in humans.

## Honors and awards

Honors and awards received by Modrich include:

- 1983: [Pfizer Award in Enzyme Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfizer_Award_in_Enzyme_Chemistry "Pfizer Award in Enzyme Chemistry")
- 1996: General Motors [Charles S. Mott Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_S._Mott_Prize "Charles S. Mott Prize") in Cancer Research
- 1998: [Robert J. and Claire Pasarow Foundation Medical Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_J._and_Claire_Pasarow_Foundation_Medical_Research_Award "Robert J. and Claire Pasarow Foundation Medical Research Award") for cancer research
- 2000 Feodor Lynen Medal
- 2005: [American Cancer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Cancer_Society "American Cancer Society") Medal of Honor
- 2015: Nobel Prize in Chemistry
- 2016: Arthur Kornberg and Paul Berg Lifetime Achievement Award in Biomedical Sciences

Modrich is a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") and a member of the [National Academy of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Medicine "National Academy of Medicine") and the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences").
