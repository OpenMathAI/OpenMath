**Morten Peter Meldal** (born 16 January 1954) is a Danish [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") and [Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") laureate. He is a professor of chemistry at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen") in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen "Copenhagen"), Denmark. He is best known for developing the [CuAAC-click reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azide-alkyne_Huisgen_cycloaddition "Azide-alkyne Huisgen cycloaddition"), concurrently with but independent of Valery V. Fokin and [K. Barry Sharpless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Barry_Sharpless "K. Barry Sharpless").

## Biography

Meldal received [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") and PhD degrees in [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Engineering "Chemical Engineering") from [Technical University of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Denmark "Technical University of Denmark") (DTU); his PhD work was supervised by Klaus Bock and focused on the synthetic chemistry of carbohydrates. From 1983 to 1988 he was a postdoctoral fellow in organic chemistry, first at the DTU, next at the [MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRC_Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology") at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University "Cambridge University") and then at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen"). In 1996 he was appointed assistant professor at DTU. Since 1998 he has led the synthesis group in the Department of Chemistry of the [Carlsberg Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Laboratory "Carlsberg Laboratory").

Meldal developed several technological techniques and instruments for peptide synthesis near the start of his career. He developed the multiple-column synthesis used in peptide and organic synthesis instruments, as well as for assembling large split-mix libraries. He first presented the cycloaddition of acetylenes and azides used in peptide and protein conjugations, in polymers and in material sciences. Meldal's group has then showed this reaction to be orthogonal to the majority of functional group chemistries.

More recently Meldal has developed an optical encoding technique and has focused on the merger of [organic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry") and peptide chemistry on solid support. He has devised a range of novel methods for the generation of N-acyl iminium ions in which combinatorial libraries of these compounds are generated and screened for substances with activity toward [G protein-coupled receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_protein-coupled_receptor "G protein-coupled receptor") in cell-based on-bead screening.

In 2019, Meldal co-founded the company Betamab Therapeutics ApS, based on the concept of beta-bodies, i.e. peptide mimics of [antibodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibodies "Antibodies"). The company closed in 2021.

## Honors and awards

Meldal was awarded the 2022 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"), jointly with [Carolyn R. Bertozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolyn_R._Bertozzi "Carolyn R. Bertozzi") and [Karl Barry Sharpless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Barry_Sharpless "Karl Barry Sharpless"), "for the development of click chemistry and [bioorthogonal chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioorthogonal_chemistry "Bioorthogonal chemistry")".
