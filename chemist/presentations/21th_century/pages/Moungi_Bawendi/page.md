**Moungi Gabriel Bawendi** (born 15 March 1961) is an American [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist"). He is currently the [Lester Wolfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Wolfe "Lester Wolfe") Professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"). Bawendi is known for his advances in the chemical production of high-quality [quantum dots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_dot "Quantum dot"). For this work, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 2023.

## Early life

Moungi Bawendi was born in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), France, the son of Tunisian mathematician [Mohammed Salah Baouendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Salah_Baouendi "M. Salah Baouendi"). After periods living in France and [Tunisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisia "Tunisia"), Bawendi and his family migrated to the United States when he was a child. They lived in [West Lafayette, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Lafayette,_Indiana "West Lafayette, Indiana"), as Salah worked in the math department at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University "Purdue University"). Bawendi graduated from [West Lafayette Junior-Senior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Lafayette_Junior-Senior_High_School "West Lafayette Junior-Senior High School") in 1978.

### Higher education and career

Bawendi received both an [A.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in 1982 and an [A.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") in 1983 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). He earned a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in chemistry in 1988 from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"), under the supervision of [Karl Freed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Freed "Karl Freed") and [Takeshi Oka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeshi_Oka "Takeshi Oka").

With Freed, Bawendi worked on theoretical [polymer physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer_physics "Polymer physics"), and with Oka, Bawendi worked on experiments on hot-bands of [H3+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trihydrogen_cation "Trihydrogen cation"), which played a role in deciphering the emission spectrum of [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_(planet) "Jupiter (planet)") observed in 1989.

During his graduate studies, Oka recommended Bawendi to a summer program in [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs "Bell Labs"), where [Louis E. Brus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_E._Brus "Louis E. Brus") introduced Bawendi to the research on quantum dots. Upon graduation, Bawendi went to work with Brus at Bell Labs as a [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher "Postdoctoral researcher").

Bawendi joined [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (MIT) in 1990 and became professor in 1996.

## Research

Bawendi was one of the most cited [chemists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") of the decade from 2000 to 2010. He is a leading figure in the research and development of [quantum dots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_dot "Quantum dot"). Quantum dots are tiny [semiconducting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor") [crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal "Crystal") whose nanoscale size gives them unique optical and [electronic properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_configuration "Electronic configuration").

A major challenge in quantum dot research was to find ways to create high quality quantum dots that are stable and uniform. Bawendi is recognized for his work in developing standardized methods for quantum dot synthesis. In 1993, Bawendi, and his PhD students David J. Norris and [Christopher B. Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_B._Murray "Christopher B. Murray"), reported on a hot-injection synthesis method for producing reproducible quantum dots with well-defined size and with high optical quality.
This breakthrough in chemical production methods made it possible to “tune” quantum dots according to size, and achieve predictable properties as a result. It gave scientists much greater control over the material, and made it possible to achieve precise and reproducible results.

The method opened the door to the development of large-scale technological applications of quantum dots in a wide range of areas. Quantum dots are now used in [light-emitting diodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-emitting_diode "Light-emitting diode") (LEDs), [photovoltaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaics "Photovoltaics") (solar cells), [photodetectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photodetector "Photodetector"), [photoconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoconductors "Photoconductors"), lasers, [biomedical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_imaging "Biomedical imaging"), [biosensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosensing "Biosensing") and other applications.

## Awards and honors

Bawendi was granted the [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship "Sloan Research Fellowship") in 1994. He won the 1997 Nobel Signature Award for Graduate Education in Chemistry of [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society") (ACS). In 2001, he received the [Sackler Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sackler_Prize "Sackler Prize") in Physical Chemistry of Advanced Materials. In 2006, he was awarded the [Ernest Orlando Lawrence Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Orlando_Lawrence_Award "Ernest Orlando Lawrence Award").

He was elected member of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science") in 2003, of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 2004, and of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 2007.

In 2010 during the National Meeting on March 23, 2010, Bawendi received the [ACS Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACS_Awards "ACS Awards") in [Colloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colloid "Colloid") and [Surface Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_science "Surface science"). He also received the 2011 [SEMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEMI "SEMI") Award for North America for quantum dot research.

Bawendi was selected as a [Clarivate Citation Laureate in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Clarivate_Citation_laureates_in_Chemistry "List of Clarivate Citation laureates in Chemistry") in 2020, jointly with [Christopher B. Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_B._Murray "Christopher B. Murray") and [Hyeon Taeghwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyeon_Taeghwan "Hyeon Taeghwan"), "for synthesis of [nanocrystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanocrystallites "Nanocrystallites") with precise attributes for a wide range of applications in physical, biological, and medical systems.".

In 2023, Bawendi was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") jointly with [Louis E. Brus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_E._Brus "Louis E. Brus") and [Alexey Ekimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Ekimov "Alexey Ekimov") "for the discovery and synthesis of [quantum dots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_dot "Quantum dot")".

He was elected to the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering "National Academy of Engineering") in 2026. He was also awarded the Medal of Honor by [Tunis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunis_University "Tunis University"). In 2025, he received a [Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Corporation_of_New_York "Carnegie Corporation of New York") [Great Immigrant Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Immigrants_Award "Great Immigrants Award")

## Personal life

Bawendi is married to [journalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist "Journalist") Rachel Zimmerman, widow of a computer science MIT professor, [Seth J. Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_J._Teller "Seth J. Teller").
