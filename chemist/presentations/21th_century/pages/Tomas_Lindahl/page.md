**Tomas Robert Lindahl** (born 28 January 1938) is a Swedish-British scientist specialising in [cancer research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_research "Cancer research"). In 2015, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") jointly with American chemist [Paul L. Modrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_L._Modrich "Paul L. Modrich") and [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey") chemist [Aziz Sancar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aziz_Sancar "Aziz Sancar") for mechanistic studies of [DNA repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_repair "DNA repair").

## Education

Lindahl was born in [Kungsholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kungsholmen "Kungsholmen"), [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm"), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden") to Folke Robert Lindahl and Ethel Hulda Hultberg. He received a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree in 1967, and an [MD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Medicinae_(Danish_and_Norwegian_degree) "Doctor Medicinae (Danish and Norwegian degree)") degree qualification in 1970, from the [Karolinska Institutet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karolinska_Institutet "Karolinska Institutet") in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm").

## Career and research

After obtaining his research doctorate, Lindahl did [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") and [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University").
He was professor of medical chemistry at the [University of Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Gothenburg "University of Gothenburg") 1978–82. After moving to the United Kingdom he joined the [Imperial Cancer Research Fund (now Cancer Research UK)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_Research_UK "Cancer Research UK") as a researcher in 1981. From 1986 to 2005 he was the first Director of Cancer Research UK's [Clare Hall Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Research_Institute#Clare_Hall_laboratories "London Research Institute") in [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire "Hertfordshire"), since 2015 part of the [Francis Crick Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Crick_Institute "Francis Crick Institute"). He continued to research there until 2009. He has contributed to many papers on [DNA repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_repair "DNA repair") and the genetics of cancer.

## Awards and honours

Lindahl was elected an [EMBO Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMBO_Member "EMBO Member") in 1974 and [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1988 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1988"), his certificate of election reads:

> Dr. Tomas Lindahl is noted for his contributions to the comprehension of [DNA repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_repair "DNA repair") at the molecular level in bacterial and mammalian cells. He was the first to isolate a mammalian [DNA ligase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_ligase "DNA ligase") and to describe a totally unanticipated novel group of [DNA glycosylases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_glycosylase "DNA glycosylase") as mediators of DNA excision repair. He has also discovered a unique class of enzymes in mammalian cells, namely the [methyltransferases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methyltransferase "Methyltransferase"), which mediate the adaptive response to [alkylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkylation "Alkylation") of DNA and has shown that the expression of these enzymes is regulated by the ada gene. More recently he has elucidated the molecular defect in [Blooms syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom_syndrome "Bloom syndrome") [*[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic "Sic")*] to be the lack of DNA ligase I. Apart from providing profound insights into the nature of the DNA repair process his very important contributions promise to facilitate the design of more selective chemotherapeutic drugs for the treatment of cancer. Lindahl has also made a number of significant contributions to understanding at the DNA level the mechanism of transformation of [B-lymphocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B_cell "B cell") by the [Epstein-Barr virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein–Barr_virus "Epstein–Barr virus"). The most notable of these was the first description of the occurrence in [lymphoid cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphopoiesis "Lymphopoiesis") of closed circular duplex viral DNA.

Lindahl received the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society")'s [Royal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Medal "Royal Medal") in 2007 "making fundamental contributions to our understanding of DNA repair. His achievements stand out for their great originality, breadth and lasting influence." He is a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters "Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters"). He was awarded the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal "Copley Medal") in 2010. He was elected a founding [Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Academy_of_Medical_Sciences "Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences") (FMedSci) in 1998. In 2018, he was elected a foreign associate of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences").

He shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2015. The Swedish Academy noted that "The Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2015 was awarded jointly to Tomas Lindahl, Paul Modrich and Aziz Sancar 'for mechanistic studies of DNA repair'."
