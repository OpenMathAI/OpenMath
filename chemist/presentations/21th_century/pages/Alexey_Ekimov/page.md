**Alexey Ekimov** or **Aleksey Yekimov** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Алексей Екимов; born 1945) is a Russian [solid state physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_physics "Solid-state physics") and a pioneer in [nanomaterials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanomaterials "Nanomaterials") research. He discovered the [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor") nanocrystals known as [quantum dots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_dot "Quantum dot") in 1981, while working at the [Vavilov State Optical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vavilov_State_Optical_Institute "Vavilov State Optical Institute"). In 2023, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for this discovery.

## Life

### Early years and education

Ekimov was born in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad "Leningrad"), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") in 1945. In 1967, he graduated from the Faculty of Physics, [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University "Saint Petersburg State University"). He went on to receive his PhD in physics at the [Ioffe Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioffe_Institute "Ioffe Institute") of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") in 1974.

### Research and career

After graduation, Ekimov moved to the [Vavilov State Optical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vavilov_State_Optical_Institute "Vavilov State Optical Institute") to conduct research. He began studying [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor")-activated glasses, known as [Schott glasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schott_AG "Schott AG"), and developing theories to explain their color. When the glasses were heated and then cooled, [copper chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_chloride "Copper chloride") crystals formed, as revealed by [X-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray "X-ray"), creating blue colors. Smaller crystals produced bluer glass.

In 1981, Ekimov, along with Alexei A. Onushchenko, reported the discovery of [quantum size effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_size_effects "Quantum size effects") in [copper chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper(I)_chloride "Copper(I) chloride") nanocrystals in glass, a phenomenon known now known as [quantum dots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_dots "Quantum dots"). During his time at the institute he further investigated these system and developed the theory of [quantum confinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_confinement "Quantum confinement") with [Alexander Efros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Efros "Alexander Efros").

Since 1999, Ekimov has been living and working in the United States as a scientist for Nanocrystals Technology, a company based in [New York State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State "New York State").

## Honors and awards

Ekimov was awarded the [1975 USSR State Prize in Science and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize#1975 "USSR State Prize") for work on [electron spin orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spintronics "Spintronics") in semiconductors. He is co-recipient of the 2006 [R. W. Wood Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._W._Wood_Prize "R. W. Wood Prize") of the [Optical Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_Society_of_America "Optical Society of America") for "discovery of nanocrystal quantum dots and pioneering studies of their electronic and optical properties" shared with [Alexander Efros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Efros "Alexander Efros") and [Louis E. Brus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_E._Brus "Louis E. Brus").

Ekimov, Brus and [Moungi Bawendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moungi_Bawendi "Moungi Bawendi") were the recipients of the 2023 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") "for the discovery and synthesis of quantum dots".
