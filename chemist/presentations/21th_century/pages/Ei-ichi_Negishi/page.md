**Ei-ichi Negishi** (根岸 英一, *Negishi Eiichi*; 14 July 1935 – 6 June 2021) was a Japanese [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") who was best known for his discovery of the [Negishi coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negishi_coupling "Negishi coupling"). He spent most of his career at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University "Purdue University") in the United States, where he was the Herbert C. Brown Distinguished Professor and the director of the Negishi-Brown Institute. He was awarded the 2010 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") "for [palladium catalyzed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium-catalyzed_coupling_reactions "Palladium-catalyzed coupling reactions") cross couplings in [organic synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_synthesis "Organic synthesis")" jointly with [Richard F. Heck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_F._Heck "Richard F. Heck") and [Akira Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Suzuki_(chemist) "Akira Suzuki (chemist)").

## Early life and education

Negishi was born in [Xinjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changchun "Changchun") (today known as Changchun), the capital of [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo "Manchukuo"), in July 1935. Following the transfer of his father who worked at the [South Manchuria Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Manchuria_Railway "South Manchuria Railway") in 1936, he moved to [Harbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin "Harbin"), and lived eight years there. In 1943, when he was nine, the Negishi family moved to [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon "Incheon"), and a year later to [Kyongsong Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeonggi_Province "Gyeonggi Province") (now [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul "Seoul")), both in [Japanese-occupied Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule "Korea under Japanese rule"). In November 1945, three months after [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") ended, they moved to Japan. Since he excelled as a student, a year ahead of what would have been his graduation from grammar school, he was admitted to an elite secondary school, Shonan High School. At the age of 17, he gained admission to the University of Tokyo. After graduation from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo "University of Tokyo") in 1958, Negishi did his internship at [Teijin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teijin "Teijin"), where he conducted research on polymer chemistry. Later, he continued his studies in the United States after having won a [Fulbright Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program "Fulbright Program") and obtained his Ph.D. from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") in 1963, under the supervision of professor [Allan R. Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_R._Day?action=edit&redlink=1 "Allan R. Day").

## Career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Nobel_Prize_2010-Press_Conference_KVA-DSC_8019.jpg/250px-Nobel_Prize_2010-Press_Conference_KVA-DSC_8019.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nobel_Prize_2010-Press_Conference_KVA-DSC_8019.jpg)

[Peter Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Diamond "Peter Diamond"), [Dale T. Mortensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_T._Mortensen "Dale T. Mortensen"), [Christopher A. Pissarides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_A._Pissarides "Christopher A. Pissarides"), [Konstantin Novoselov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Novoselov "Konstantin Novoselov"), [Andre Geim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Geim "Andre Geim"), [Akira Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Suzuki_(chemist) "Akira Suzuki (chemist)"), Ei-ichi Negishi, and [Richard Heck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Heck "Richard Heck"), Nobel Prize Laureates 2010, at a press conference at the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences") in Stockholm

After obtaining his Ph.D., Negishi decided to become an academic researcher. Although he was hoping to work at a Japanese university, he could not find a position. In 1966 he resigned from Teijin, and became a postdoctoral associate at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University "Purdue University"), working under future Nobel laureate [Herbert C. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_C._Brown "Herbert C. Brown"). From 1968 to 1972 he was an instructor at Purdue.

In 1972, he became an assistant professor at [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University "Syracuse University"), where began his lifelong study of transition metal–catalyzed reactions, and was promoted to associate professor in 1979. He returned to Purdue University as a full professor in the same year.

He discovered [Negishi coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negishi_coupling "Negishi coupling"), a process which condenses organic zinc compounds and organic halides under a [palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium "Palladium") or [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel "Nickel") catalyst to obtain a C–C bonded product. For this achievement, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 2010. Negishi also reported that [organoaluminum compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organoaluminum_compound "Organoaluminum compound") and organic zirconium compounds can be used for cross-coupling. He did not seek a patent for this coupling technology and explained his reasoning as follows: "If we did not obtain a patent, we thought that everyone could use our results easily."
In addition, Zr(C5H5)2 obtained by reducing [zirconocene dichloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zirconocene_dichloride "Zirconocene dichloride") is also called **Negishi reagent**, which can be used in oxidative cyclisation reactions. The technique he developed is estimated to be used in a quarter of all reactions in the pharmaceutical industry.

By the time Negishi retired in 2019, he had published more than 400 academic papers. He was committed to instilling rigorous practices in his lab, emphasizing the need of keeping organized and comprehensive records. Before any separations, he asked his student to evaluate crude reaction mixtures in order to minimize loss of any useful scientific information.

## Personal life and death

Negishi began dating Sumire Suzuki in his freshman year and they announced their engagement to their parents in March 1958. They had met at a choir of which they were both members at in university. They married the next year and together they had two daughters.

Negishi loved playing the piano and conducting. During the "Pacifichem" 2015 conference's closing ceremony, he conducted an orchestra.

### Disappearance

On the evening of March 12, 2018, both Negishi and his wife were reported missing by family members. Police determined that, based on a purchase made earlier in the day, the couple had left their home in [West Lafayette, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Lafayette,_Indiana "West Lafayette, Indiana"), and headed north. At about 5 a.m. the next day, officers in [Ogle County, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogle_County,_Illinois "Ogle County, Illinois"), received a call to check on the welfare of an elderly man who was walking on a rural road south of [Rockford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockford,_Illinois "Rockford, Illinois"). When he was taken to hospital, officers identified him as Negishi and found that police in Indiana were looking for him and his wife. A short time later, Suzuki's body was found at the Orchard Hills Landfill in [Davis Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Junction,_Illinois "Davis Junction, Illinois"), along with the couple's car.

According to a statement from the family, the couple was driving to [Rockford International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockford_International_Airport "Rockford International Airport") for a trip when their car became stuck in a ditch on a road near the landfill. Negishi went looking for help and was said to be suffering from an "acute state of confusion and shock". The Ogle County Sheriff Department said there was no suspicion of foul play in Suzuki's death, although the cause of her death was not immediately released. The family said Suzuki was near the end of her battle with [Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease").

In May 2018, an autopsy concluded that Suzuki died from [hypothermia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothermia "Hypothermia"), but Parkinson's disease and [hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension "Hypertension") were contributing factors.

### Death

Negishi died in Indianapolis, Indiana, on June 6, 2021. He was 85 years old. No funeral services took place in the United States, but his family planned to lay him to rest in Japan in 2022.
