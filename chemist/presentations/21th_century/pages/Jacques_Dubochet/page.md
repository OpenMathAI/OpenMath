**Jacques Dubochet** (born 8 June 1942) is a retired [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland") [biophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysicist "Biophysicist"). He is a former researcher at the [European Molecular Biology Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Molecular_Biology_Laboratory "European Molecular Biology Laboratory") in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany"), and an honorary professor of [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics") at the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne "University of Lausanne") in Switzerland.

In 2017, he received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") together with [Joachim Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Frank "Joachim Frank") and [Richard Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Henderson_(biologist) "Richard Henderson (biologist)") "for developing [cryo-electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryo-electron_microscopy "Cryo-electron microscopy") for the high-resolution structure determination of [biomolecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomolecular_structure "Biomolecular structure") in solution". He received the [Royal Photographic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Photographic_Society "Royal Photographic Society") Progress Medal, alongside his colleagues Professor Joachim Frank and Dr Richard Henderson, in 2018 for 'an important advance in the scientific or technological development of photography or imaging in the widest sense'.

## Career

Dubochet started to study physics at the *École polytechnique de l'[Université de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne "University of Lausanne")* (now [École polytechnique fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique_fédérale_de_Lausanne "École polytechnique fédérale de Lausanne")) in 1962 and obtained his degree in physical engineering in 1967. He obtained a Certificate of Molecular Biology at University of Geneva in 1969 and then began to study electron microscopy of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA"). In 1973, he completed his thesis in biophysics at [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva "University of Geneva") and [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel "University of Basel").

Dubochet at the Nobel Prize press conference in Stockholm (December 2017)

From 1978 to 1987, Dubochet was group leader at the [European Molecular Biology Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Molecular_Biology_Laboratory "European Molecular Biology Laboratory") in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg"), then part of [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany "West Germany"). From 1987 to 2007, he was professor at the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne "University of Lausanne"). In 2007, at 65 years old, he retired and became an honorary professor at the University of Lausanne.

During his career, Dubochet developed technologies in [cryo-electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryo-electron_microscopy "Cryo-electron microscopy"), [cryo-electron tomography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryo-electron_tomography "Cryo-electron tomography") and cryo-electron microscopy of [vitreous sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryo-electron_microscopy#Vitreous_sections "Cryo-electron microscopy"). These technologies are used to image individual biological structures such as [protein complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_complex "Protein complex") or virus particles. At Lausanne he took part in initiatives to make scientists more aware of social issues.

In 2014, Dubochet received [EMBL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Molecular_Biology_Laboratory "European Molecular Biology Laboratory")'s [Lennart Philipson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lennart_Philipson "Lennart Philipson") Award. Describing his career in 2015, Professor Gareth Griffiths, his colleague at EMBL explained: "Jacques had a vision. He found a way of freezing thin films of water [so fast that crystals had no time to form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitrification "Vitrification") [that could damage samples] [...] over time the technique has become increasingly important to [life science research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_life_sciences "List of life sciences"), and it is clear today it is Nobel Prize-worthy."

When asked by his university how he would like his Nobel Prize to be recognised by the institution he asked for a parking space for his bicycle which was duly given. He had cycled to his lab almost every day for 30 years.

At the end of November 2021, the Dubochet Center for Imaging (DCI), which bears his name, was launched by the [Swiss Federal Institute of Technology in Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne "École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne"), the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne "University of Lausanne") and the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva "University of Geneva"). Just a few weeks later, the DCI was able to make a significant contribution to deciphering the [Omicron variant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2_Omicron_variant "SARS-CoV-2 Omicron variant") of the [COVID-19 virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2 "SARS-CoV-2").

## Personal life

Dubochet is married with two children. He has [dyslexia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyslexia "Dyslexia").

In the 1970s, for the second meeting with his future wife, they went to [protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-nuclear_movement_in_Switzerland "Anti-nuclear movement in Switzerland") against the [Kaiseraugst nuclear power plant construction project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiseraugst#History "Kaiseraugst").

Dubochet is a member of the [Social Democratic Party of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland "Social Democratic Party of Switzerland"), and a member of the municipal parliament of [Morges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morges "Morges"), where he holds a seat on the supervisory committee. He is also part of the [climate movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_movement "Climate movement") as a member of the Grandparents for Future and emphasized the urgency of saving our societies.
