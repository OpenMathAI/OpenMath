**Susumu Kitagawa** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (北川 進, *Kitagawa Susumu*; born 4 July 1951) is a Japanese [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry")-winning [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") specializing in [coordination chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_chemistry "Coordination chemistry"), with a focus on organic–inorganic hybrid compounds and the chemical and physical properties of [porous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porous_medium "Porous medium") coordination polymers, particularly [metal-organic frameworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal-organic_framework "Metal-organic framework"). He is Distinguished Professor at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University")'s Institute for Integrated Cell-Material Sciences (iCeMS), which he co-founded. In 2025, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") jointly with [Richard Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Robson_(chemist) "Richard Robson (chemist)") and [Omar M. Yaghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_M._Yaghi "Omar M. Yaghi").

## Life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/20251009_Shigeru_Ishiba_and_Susumu_Kitagawa_1.jpg/250px-20251009_Shigeru_Ishiba_and_Susumu_Kitagawa_1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:20251009_Shigeru_Ishiba_and_Susumu_Kitagawa_1.jpg)

Kitagawa attended an online meeting with [Shigeru Ishiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeru_Ishiba "Shigeru Ishiba") (October 9, 2025)

Kitagawa was born on 4 July 1951 in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto "Kyoto").

He earned his PhD in hydrocarbon chemistry from [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University") in 1979, having completed his undergraduate studies at the same institution. That year, he was appointed assistant professor at [Kindai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindai_University "Kindai University"), where he was promoted to lecturer in 1983 and associate professor in 1988.

In 1992, he became professor of [inorganic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inorganic_chemistry "Inorganic chemistry") at [Tokyo Metropolitan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_University "Tokyo Metropolitan University"). He returned to Kyoto University in 1998 as a professor of inorganic functional chemistry in the Department of Synthetic Chemistry and [Biological Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry"). In 2007, he co-founded the [Institute for Integrated Cell-Material Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Integrated_Cell-Material_Sciences?action=edit&redlink=1 "Institute for Integrated Cell-Material Sciences") (iCeMS), serving as founding deputy director, then as director from 2013 to 2023. In 2024, he was appointed Executive Vice-President for Research Promotion at Kyoto University.

His overseas academic postings include a postdoctoral fellowship at [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University "Texas A&M University") with [F. Albert Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Albert_Cotton "F. Albert Cotton") (1986–1987) and a guest professorship at the [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York "City University of New York") (1996). He received an honorary doctorate from the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich "Technical University of Munich") in 2018.

Kitagawa served as a member and associate member of the [Science Council of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Council_of_Japan "Science Council of Japan") from 2011 to 2023.

In 2025, Kitagawa, along with [Richard Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Robson_(chemist) "Richard Robson (chemist)") and [Omar M. Yaghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_M._Yaghi "Omar M. Yaghi"), was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for their foundational work on [molecular building blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_block_(chemistry) "Building block (chemistry)") for metal-organic frameworks.

## Research

Following the discoveries of [Makoto Fujita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makoto_Fujita_(chemist) "Makoto Fujita (chemist)") (1994) and [Omar M. Yaghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_M._Yaghi "Omar M. Yaghi") (1995), Kitagawa demonstrated in 1997 that coordination polymer structures possess gas adsorption properties, a key finding for the development of functional porous materials.

His most influential works include:

- A 1997 seminal report on a porous coordination polymer (MOF) for small molecule adsorption.
- A 2004 early review of functional porous coordination polymers.
- A 2009 review of "soft porous crystals," which feature large-scale structural transformability upon chemical or physical stimulation.
