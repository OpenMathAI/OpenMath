**Sir David William Cross MacMillan** (born 16 March 1968) is a Scottish chemist and the James S. McDonnell Distinguished University Professor of Chemistry at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), where he was also the chair of the Department of Chemistry from 2010 to 2015. He is also a Distinguished Scholar at the Princeton Branch of the [Ludwig Institute for Cancer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Institute_for_Cancer_Research "Ludwig Institute for Cancer Research"). He shared the 2021 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with [Benjamin List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_List "Benjamin List") "for the development of asymmetric [organocatalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organocatalysis "Organocatalysis")". MacMillan used his share of the $1.14 million prize to establish the May and Billy MacMillan Foundation.

## Education and early life

MacMillan was born in [Bellshill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellshill "Bellshill"), [North Lanarkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Lanarkshire "North Lanarkshire"), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland "Scotland"), in 1968 and grew up in nearby [New Stevenston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Stevenston "New Stevenston"). His father was a steelworker, while his grandfather was a miner. He attended the local state-funded schools, New Stevenston Primary and Bellshill Academy, and credited his Scottish education and Scottish upbringing for his success.

He received his undergraduate degree in chemistry at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow "University of Glasgow"), where he worked with Ernie Colvin.

In 1990, he left the UK to begin his doctoral studies under the direction of Professor [Larry Overman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Overman "Larry Overman") at the [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine "University of California, Irvine"). During this time, he focused on the development of new reaction methodology directed toward the stereocontrolled formation of bicyclic [tetrahydrofurans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahydrofuran "Tetrahydrofuran"). MacMillan's graduate studies culminated in the total synthesis of 7-(−)-deacetoxyalcyonin acetate, a eunicellin [diterpenoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terpenoid "Terpenoid") isolated from the soft coral *Eunicella stricta*. He earned his Ph.D. in 1996.

## Career and research

Upon receiving his Ph.D., MacMillan accepted a postdoctoral position with Professor [David Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_A._Evans "David A. Evans") at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). His postdoctoral studies centered on enantioselective catalysis, in particular, the design and development of [Sn(II)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin "Tin")-derived [bisoxazoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisoxazoline_ligand "Bisoxazoline ligand") complexes (Sn(II)box).

MacMillan began his independent research career as a member of the chemistry faculty at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in July 1998. He joined the department of chemistry at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech "Caltech") in June 2000, where his group's research interests centered on new approaches to enantioselective catalysis. In 2004, he was appointed as the [Earle C. Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earle_C._Anthony "Earle C. Anthony") Professor of Chemistry. He became the James S. McDonnell Distinguished University Professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in September 2006.

First generation MacMillan catalyst

He is considered to be one of the founders of [organocatalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organocatalysis "Organocatalysis"). MacMillan's research group has made many advances in the field of asymmetric organocatalysis, and they have applied these new methods to the synthesis of a range of complex natural products. He developed chiral [imidazolidinone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imidazolidinone "Imidazolidinone") catalysts. [MacMillan catalysts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacMillan_catalysts?action=edit&redlink=1 "MacMillan catalysts") are used in various asymmetric syntheses. Examples include [Diels-Alder reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diels-Alder_reaction "Diels-Alder reaction"), [1,3-dipolar cycloadditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1,3-dipolar_cycloaddition "1,3-dipolar cycloaddition"), [Friedel-Crafts alkylations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedel-Crafts_alkylation "Friedel-Crafts alkylation") or [Michael additions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_addition "Michael addition").

MacMillan has also extensively developed photoredox catalysis for use in organic synthesis.

In the early 2020s, MacMillan expanded his research into chemical biology, including the development of µMap, a photocatalytic proximity-labeling platform that enables the mapping of molecular interactions at nanometer-scale resolution. The technology subsequently became a focus of his laboratory's research into biological systems, including cancer biology, and in 2025 he was appointed a Distinguished Scholar of the Princeton Branch of the Ludwig Institute for Cancer Research.

Between 2010 and 2014, MacMillan was the founding editor-in-chief of the journal *[Chemical Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Science_(journal) "Chemical Science (journal)")*, the flagship general chemistry journal published by the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry").

As of March 2024, MacMillan has an [h-index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-index "H-index") of 125 according to [Google Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Scholar "Google Scholar") and of 115 according to [Scopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scopus "Scopus").

## Honours and awards

MacMillan was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in the [2022 Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Birthday_Honours "2022 Birthday Honours") for services to chemistry and science.

- 2012 – Elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (FRS)
