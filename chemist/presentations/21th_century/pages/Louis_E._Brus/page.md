**Louis Eugene Brus** (August 10, 1943 – January 11, 2026) was an American chemist, and the Samuel Latham Mitchell Professor of [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"). He was the co-discoverer of the [colloidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colloid "Colloid") [semi-conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-conductor "Semi-conductor") nanocrystals known as [quantum dots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_dots "Quantum dots"). In 2023, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry").

## Early life and education

Louis Eugene Brus was born in [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland "Cleveland"), Ohio, on August 10, 1943. During high school in [Roeland Park, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roeland_Park,_Kansas "Roeland Park, Kansas"), he developed an interest for chemistry and [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics").

He entered [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University "Rice University") in 1961 with a [Naval Reserve Officers Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Reserve_Officers_Training_Corps "Naval Reserve Officers Training Corps") (NROTC) college scholarship, which required him to participate in NROTC activities at sea as a [midshipman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midshipman "Midshipman"). In 1965, he graduated at Rice with a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree in chemical physics, and then moved to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") for his doctoral research. For his [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis "Thesis"), he worked on the [photodissociation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photodissociation "Photodissociation") of [sodium iodide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_iodide "Sodium iodide") vapor, under the supervision of [Richard Bersohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bersohn "Richard Bersohn"). After obtaining his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree in chemical physics in 1969, Brus returned to the Navy as a lieutenant and served as a scientific staff officer in collaboration with [Lin Ming-chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin_Ming-chang "Lin Ming-chang"), at the [United States Naval Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Research_Laboratory "United States Naval Research Laboratory") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.")

Under the recommendation of Bersohn, Brus left the Navy permanently and joined [AT&T Bell Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs "Bell Labs") in 1973, where he did the work that led to the discovery of [quantum dots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_dot "Quantum dot"). In 1996, Brus left Bell Labs and joined the faculty in the Department of Chemistry at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University").

## Work on quantum dots

Brus was a foundational figure in the research and development of [quantum dots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_dot "Quantum dot"). Quantum dots are tiny semiconducting crystals whose nanoscale size gives them unique optical and electronic properties. He was independently the first to synthesize them in a solution in 1982. At the time, he was studying [organic photochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_photochemistry "Organic photochemistry") on [cadmium sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadmium_sulfide "Cadmium sulfide") particle surfaces using pump–probe [Raman spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raman_spectroscopy "Raman spectroscopy"), looking for possible applications for solar-energy. He noticed that the optical properties of the crystals changed after leaving them for 24 hours. He attributed this change in [band gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_gap "Band gap") energy to [Ostwald ripening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostwald_ripening "Ostwald ripening") when the crystal increased size. Brus provided the theoretical framework for understanding the behavior of quantum dots in terms of quantum size effects. He identified the connection between the particle size of semiconductors and the wavelength of the light they emit, now known as the [Brus equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brus_equation "Brus equation").

Brus tried to contact researchers in the Soviet Union. It was in 1990, that he finally met [Alexey Ekimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Ekimov "Alexey Ekimov") and [Alexander Efros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Efros "Alexander Efros"), who had first developed the semiconductor nanocrystals in glass in 1981 under more rudimentary conditions, however their research was not available in the United States.

At Bell Labs, Brus worked with postdoc researchers [Paul Alivisatos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Alivisatos "Paul Alivisatos") and [Moungi Bawendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moungi_Bawendi "Moungi Bawendi") in a research project with organometallic synthetic chemist Michael L. Steigerwald on reducing the size of the quantum dots.

## Death

Brus died of [myelodysplastic syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myelodysplastic_syndrome "Myelodysplastic syndrome") in [Hastings-on-Hudson, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings-on-Hudson,_New_York "Hastings-on-Hudson, New York") on January 11, 2026, at the age of 82.

## Awards and honors

Brus was elected a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow "Fellow") of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1998, a member of the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") in 2004, and was a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters "Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters").

He received the Distinguished Alumni Award from the Association of [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University "Rice University") Alumni in 2010. He was co-recipient of the 2006 [R. W. Wood Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._W._Wood_Prize "R. W. Wood Prize") of the [Optical Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_Society_of_America "Optical Society of America") "for the discovery of nanocrystal quantum dots and pioneering studies of their electronic and optical properties" shared with Alexander Efros and Alexey Ekimov. He also received the inaugural [Kavli Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavli_Prize "Kavli Prize") for [nanoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoscience "Nanoscience") along with [Sumio Iijima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumio_Iijima "Sumio Iijima") in 2008 for "their large impact in the development of the nanoscience field of the zero and one dimensional nanostructures in physics, chemistry and [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology "Biology")". In 2009 he was awarded the [Willard Gibbs Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard_Gibbs_Award "Willard Gibbs Award") "for his leading role in the creation of chemical quantum dots". Brus was chosen for the 2010 [NAS Award in Chemical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Award_in_Chemical_Sciences "NAS Award in Chemical Sciences"). In 2012 he received the [Franklin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Institute "Franklin Institute")'s [Bower Award and Prize for Achievement in Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bower_Award_and_Prize_for_Achievement_in_Science "Bower Award and Prize for Achievement in Science"), and was selected as a [Clarivate Citation laureate in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Clarivate_Citation_laureates_in_Chemistry "List of Clarivate Citation laureates in Chemistry") "for discovery of colloidal semiconductor nanocrystals (quantum dots)".

In 2023, Brus was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") jointly with Ekimov and Moungi Bawendi "for the discovery and synthesis of quantum dots". Bawendi had worked as a postdoc with Brus, when they were in Bell Labs.

He was inducted as a 2025 fellow of the [National Academy of Inventors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Inventors "National Academy of Inventors").
