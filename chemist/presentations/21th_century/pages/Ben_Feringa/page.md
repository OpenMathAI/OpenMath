**Bernard Lucas** "**Ben**" **Feringa** (Dutch pronunciation: [[ˈbɛrnɑrt ˈlykɑz bɛn ˈfeːrɪŋɣaː]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Dutch "Help:IPA/Dutch"); born 18 May 1951) is a Dutch [synthetic organic chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry") who specializes in [molecular nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_nanotechnology "Molecular nanotechnology") and [homogeneous catalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_catalysis "Homogeneous catalysis"). He is the [Jacobus van 't Hoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobus_Henricus_van_'t_Hoff "Jacobus Henricus van 't Hoff") [Distinguished Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Professor "Distinguished Professor") of [Molecular Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_Science "Molecular Science") at the [Stratingh Institute for Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratingh_Institute_for_Chemistry "Stratingh Institute for Chemistry"), [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen "University of Groningen"); and an Academy professor of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences"). Feringa's research on [molecular machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_machine "Molecular machine") was awarded the 2016 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") alongside [Sir J. Fraser Stoddart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Stoddart "Fraser Stoddart") and [Jean-Pierre Sauvage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Sauvage "Jean-Pierre Sauvage").

## Personal life

Feringa was born as the son of farmer Geert Feringa (1918–1993) and his wife Lies Feringa née Hake (1924–2013). Feringa was the second of ten siblings in a Catholic family. He spent his youth on the family's farm, which is directly on the border with Germany, in [Barger-Compascuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barger-Compascuum "Barger-Compascuum") in the [Bourtange moor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourtange_moor "Bourtange moor"). He is of Dutch and German descent. Among his ancestors is the settler [Johann Gerhard Bekel](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann%20Gerhard%20Bekel "de:Johann Gerhard Bekel"). Together with his wife Betty Feringa, he has three daughters. He lives in [Paterswolde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paterswolde "Paterswolde") near [Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groningen "Groningen").

## Career

Feringa received his [MSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") degree [with distinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honors "Latin honors") from the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen "University of Groningen") in 1974. He subsequently obtained a PhD degree at the same university in 1978, with the thesis titled "Asymmetric oxidation of phenols. [Atropisomerism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atropisomer "Atropisomer") and [optical activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_activity "Optical activity")". Following a short period at [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Shell "Royal Dutch Shell") in the Netherlands and the United Kingdom, he was appointed as lecturer at the University of Groningen in 1984 and [Full Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Professor "Full Professor"), succeeding Prof Wijnberg, in 1988. His early career was focused on [homogenous catalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogenous_catalysis "Homogenous catalysis") and [oxidation catalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidation_catalysis "Oxidation catalysis"), and especially on [stereochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereochemistry "Stereochemistry") with major contributions in the field of [enantioselective catalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantioselective_synthesis "Enantioselective synthesis"), including the monophos [ligand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligand "Ligand") used in [asymmetric hydrogenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_hydrogenation "Asymmetric hydrogenation"), asymmetric [conjugate additions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleophilic_conjugate_addition "Nucleophilic conjugate addition") of [organometallic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organometallic_chemistry "Organometallic chemistry") [reagents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagent "Reagent"), including the highly reactive [organolithium reagents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organolithium_reagent "Organolithium reagent") and organic [photochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photochemistry "Photochemistry") and [stereochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereochemistry "Stereochemistry"). In the 1990s, Feringa's work in [stereochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereochemistry "Stereochemistry") led to major contributions in [photochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photochemistry "Photochemistry"), resulting in the first monodirectional light driven molecular rotary motor and later a molecular car (a so-called [nanocar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanocar "Nanocar")) driven by electrical impulses.

Ben Feringa holds over 30 patents and has published over 650 [peer reviewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review "Peer review") [research papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_paper "Academic paper") to date, cited more than 30,000 times and has an [*h*-index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-index "H-index") in excess of 90. He has guided over 100 PhD students over his career.

## Contributions to research

Feringa found that the early introduction of chiroptical molecular switches, based on the design of the first chiral overcrowded alkenes and the demonstration of optically controlled molecular switching and amplification of chirality in mesoscopic systems, lead to molecular rotary motors in which chirality plays a critical role in achieving the same function achieved by nature, for example, the unidirectional rotation of [retinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retinal "Retinal") in [rhodopsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodopsin "Rhodopsin"). This work led to the discovery of the world's first unidirectional molecular rotary motor and this work has been laying the ground-work for a key component of future [molecular nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_nanotechnology "Molecular nanotechnology") i.e. [nanomachines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanomachines "Nanomachines") and [nanorobots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanorobots "Nanorobots") powered by [molecular motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_motor "Molecular motor"). Feringa's research has shown that molecular motors can be used to control and direct the functions of a chemical system, such as changing both the speed and [enantioselectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantioselectivity "Enantioselectivity") of a molecular catalyst.

Applications of molecular switches developed in his group include responsive materials and [surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_science "Surface science"), [liquid crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_crystal "Liquid crystal"), [electrochromic devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrochromic_device "Electrochromic device") for [optoelectronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optoelectronic "Optoelectronic"), photo-switchable [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") as a molecular memory stick, responsive [gels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gel "Gel"), [polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer "Polymer"), and light-switchable [protein channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_channel "Protein channel") for nanoscale [drug delivery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_delivery "Drug delivery") systems, [anion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anion "Anion") [sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensor "Sensor"), responsive [catalysts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalyst "Catalyst") and [photopharmacology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photopharmacology "Photopharmacology") as well as entirely novel approaches using responsive [drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical_drug "Pharmaceutical drug") toward [anticancer agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticancer_agent "Anticancer agent"), [antibiotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotic "Antibiotic") treatment and [antibiotic resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotic_resistance "Antibiotic resistance"), and [biofilm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofilm "Biofilm") formation. Interfacing [molecular motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_motor "Molecular motor") with the macroscopic world by surface assembly on gold nanoparticles and a macroscopic gold film, has shown that the motor functions while chemically bound to a surface, a key result for future nanomachines such as a molecular conveyor belt. Experiments that involve doping liquid crystals with molecular motors demonstrate that the motion of the motor can be harnessed to make macroscopic objects rotate on a liquid crystal film and drive molecular systems [out-of-equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_chemistry "Equilibrium chemistry"). Several of these discoveries were selected for the list of most important chemical discoveries of the year by [Chemical & Engineering News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_&_Engineering_News "Chemical & Engineering News").

Aside from molecular motors and switches, Feringa's work has crossed many disciplines and includes the use of phosphoramidites as ligands in asymmetric catalysis, an excellent stereocontrol was archived in copper-catalysed C–C bond formation, which led to a breakthrough in catalytic asymmetric conjugate addition. As phosphoramidites found use in industry, recently they utilised them as starting reagents for asymmetric C-P bond formation. Traditionally, an external chiral ligand is used for chiral induction in a C–P coupling reaction, but the competitive coordination of initial and final phosphorus compounds with the metal catalysts, together with an external chiral ligand, reduces the enantioselectivity. As BINOL-containing phosphoramidites have the properties of an intrinsic chiral ligand and simultaneously can serve as a substrate, they hypothesized that they would increase stereoselectivity in C–P coupling processes with aryl compounds, and were delighted when that data confirmed that they did.

Moreover, many other highlighted works are chiral electromagnetic radiation to generate enantioselectivity, low molecular weight gelators, imaging porphyrins with STM, drying induced self-assembly, organic synthesis, CD spectroscopy, asymmetric catalysis, exploring the origins of chirality including the possibility of an extraterrestrial source and various aspects of surface science including surface modification, surface energy control, and porphyrin allayers.

## Honours and awards

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Nobel_Laureates_0907_%2831341926832%29.jpg/250px-Nobel_Laureates_0907_%2831341926832%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nobel_Laureates_0907_(31341926832).jpg)

Feringa at Nobel press conference in Stockholm, Sweden, December 2016

Feringa is a member of many chemical and scientific related societies: In 1998, Feringa was elected as a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry") (FRSC). In 2004, he was elected International Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"). Feringa is an elected member, since 2006, and Academy Professor, since 2008, of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences") (KNAW). At the KNAW Feringa served as vice-president and Chair of Board of the Science Division. In addition, Feringa is a former president of the [Bürgenstock Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bürgenstock_Conference?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bürgenstock Conference") in 2009, Switzerland, and an elected Member of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea "Academia Europaea") since 2010. In 2013, he was appointed as Council Member of the Royal Society of Chemistry. On 13 October 2016, Feringa was elected an Honorary Member of the [Royal Netherlands Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Chemical_Society "Royal Netherlands Chemical Society").

In recognition of his contributions to synthetic methodologies and catalysis, Feringa was given the [Novartis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novartis "Novartis") Chemistry Lectureship Award 2000–2001. A large part of Feringa's research career has focused on molecular nanotechnology and especially molecular photochemistry and stereochemistry. His contributions in these areas have been recognized in research awards including [Körber European Science Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Körber_European_Science_Prize "Körber European Science Prize") in 2003, the [Spinoza Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinoza_Prize "Spinoza Prize") in 2004, and the Prelog Gold Medal in 2005 (ETH-Zürich), Switzerland, He won the [James Flack Norris Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Flack_Norris_Award "James Flack Norris Award") in Physical Organic Chemistry of the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society") in 2007, USA, the [European Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council "European Research Council") Advanced Grant in 2008, and the Paracelsus Award of the Swiss Chemical Society, in 2008.

Feringa furthermore was awarded the [Chirality Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirality_Medal "Chirality Medal") for distinguished contributions to all aspects of stereochemistry in 2010, the [Solvias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvias?action=edit&redlink=1 "Solvias") Ligand Contest Award (shared with John Hartwig, [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") (US), the Organic Stereochemistry Award in 2011 of the Royal Society of Chemistry, UK, and the Decennial Van‘t Hoff Medal in 2011 of the Genootschap ter Bevordering van de Natuur-, Genees-, en Heelkunde, in the Netherlands.

Feringa's contributions to the molecular sciences have been recognized with the [Arthur C. Cope Scholar Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_C._Cope_Scholar_Award "Arthur C. Cope Scholar Award"), the [Nagoya Medal of Organic Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_Medal_of_Organic_Chemistry?action=edit&redlink=1 "Nagoya Medal of Organic Chemistry"), the 2012 Grand Prix Scientifique Cino del Duca, and the Humboldt award of the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation in 2012, Germany.

In 2013, he won subsequently the Lily European Distinguished Science Award, the Nagoya Gold Medal in Nagoya, Japan, the Yamada-Koga Award in Tokyo, Japan, the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry") Award for distinguished service, and the [Marie Curie Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie_Medal "Marie Curie Medal") of the [Polish Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Chemical_Society "Polish Chemical Society").

He has written several invited review articles and book chapters for a number of journals and books including [Chemical Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Reviews "Chemical Reviews"), [Accounts of Chemical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accounts_of_Chemical_Research "Accounts of Chemical Research"), [Angewandte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angewandte_Chemie "Angewandte Chemie") and the main textbook in the field of [circular dichroism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_dichroism "Circular dichroism"), [Comprehensive Chiroptical Spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Chiroptical_Spectroscopy?action=edit&redlink=1 "Comprehensive Chiroptical Spectroscopy").

He won the Theodor Föster Award of the [German Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_German_Chemists "Society of German Chemists") (GDCh) & Bunsen-Society for Physical Chemistry in 2014, Germany, and the Arthur C. Cope Late Career Scholars Award of the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society") in 2015. In November 2015, he was the recipient of the "[Chemistry for the future Solvay prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Solvay_Prize "Ernest Solvay Prize")", which was awarded for "his work on groundbreaking research on molecular motors, a research field that paves the way to new therapeutic and technological applications with nanorobots."

On 20 December 2016, Feringa jointly received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"), together with [Sir J. Fraser Stoddart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Stoddart "Fraser Stoddart") and [Jean-Pierre Sauvage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Sauvage "Jean-Pierre Sauvage"), for their work on molecular machines. Feringa had been considered a candidate for the Nobel Prize for some time, with *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons")* including him in a list of candidates in 2010. In 2016, he also received the Hoffman Medal of the [German Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Chemical_Society "German Chemical Society") and the [Tetrahedron Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedron_Prize "Tetrahedron Prize") awarded by [Elsevier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elsevier "Elsevier"). In 2017, Feringa received the Centenary Prize of the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry"). In August 2018, Feringa was awarded the European Gold Medal presented by the [European Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Chemical_Society "European Chemical Society") (EuChemS) during the 7th EuChemS Chemistry Congress, held in [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool "Liverpool"), UK. In 2019, Feringa accepted the Raman Chair of the [Indian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Academy_of_Sciences "Indian Academy of Sciences"), an honorary position whereby eminent scientists are invited to lecture on their work and interact with the research community in India.

In 2008, he was appointed a [Knight of the Order of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_of_the_Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion "Knight of the Order of the Netherlands Lion") by [Queen Beatrix of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrix_of_the_Netherlands "Beatrix of the Netherlands"), and on 23 November 2016 he was promoted to Commander of the same Order by [King Willem-Alexander of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem-Alexander_of_the_Netherlands "Willem-Alexander of the Netherlands"). On 1 December 2016 Feringa was made an Honorary Citizen of Groningen. On 6 April 2017 a street in his birthplace Barger-Compascuum was named *Prof. Dr. B. L. Feringadam*.

In 1997, he completed the 200 km [Elfstedentocht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elfstedentocht "Elfstedentocht") in 12 hours.

He was elected a foreign associate of the US [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in April 2019.

On 2 April 2019, Ben Feringa was conferred an honorary doctoral degree by the [University of Johannesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Johannesburg "University of Johannesburg") in recognition of his contributions to the [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") field and [Scientific community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_community "Scientific community") as a whole.

In 2019 he became a member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina").

He was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") in 2020.

## Professional activities

Ben Feringa has served as an editorial board member for several journals published by the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry"), including *[Chemical Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Communications "Chemical Communications")* (until 2012), the *[Faraday Transactions of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraday_Transactions_of_the_Royal_Society "Faraday Transactions of the Royal Society")*, and as Chair of the [Editorial Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editorial_Board "Editorial Board") of *[Chemistry World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry_World "Chemistry World")*. He is the founding Scientific Editor (2002–2006) of the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry") journal *[Organic & Biomolecular Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_&_Biomolecular_Chemistry "Organic & Biomolecular Chemistry")*. In addition, he is an editorial (advisory) board member for the journals *[Advanced Synthesis and Catalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Synthesis_and_Catalysis "Advanced Synthesis and Catalysis")*, *Adv. Phys. Org. Chem.*, *Topics in Stereochemistry*, *[Chemistry: An Asian Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry:_An_Asian_Journal "Chemistry: An Asian Journal")* published by Wiley, and advisory board member for the *[Journal of Organic Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Organic_Chemistry "Journal of Organic Chemistry")*, *[Journal of the American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_American_Chemical_Society "Journal of the American Chemical Society")* published by the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society").

On 26 November 2017, Feringa, on a visit to [South China Normal University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Normal_University "South China Normal University") in [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou "Guangzhou"), was appointed honorary professor of South China Normal University. From December 2017, he holds a "[green card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_residence_(United_States) "Permanent residence (United States)")" in China, and will lead a team researching “[self-healing materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-healing_materials "Self-healing materials")” at Shanghai’s [East China University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_University_of_Science_and_Technology "East China University of Science and Technology").

Feringa is a co-founder of the contract research company Selact (now a part of Kiadis), which was originally established to provide services in the area of organic synthesis but later developed high throughput screening methods.
