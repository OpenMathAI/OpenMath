**David Baker** (born October 6, 1962) is an American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist") and [computational biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_biology "Computational biology") who has pioneered methods to [design proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_design "Protein design") and [predict their three-dimensional structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_structure_prediction "Protein structure prediction"). He is the Henrietta and Aubrey Davis Endowed Professor in Biochemistry, an investigator with the [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute"), and an adjunct professor of genome sciences, bioengineering, chemical engineering, computer science, and physics at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington "University of Washington"). He was awarded the shared 2024 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his work on computational [protein design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_design "Protein design").

Baker is a member of the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") and of the [United States National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Engineering "United States National Academy of Engineering"), and is the director of the University of Washington's Institute for Protein Design. He has co-founded more than a dozen biotechnology companies and was included in [*Time*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)") magazine's inaugural list of the 100 Most Influential People in health in 2024.

## Biography

### Early life and education

Baker was born into a Jewish family in [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle"), Washington on October 6, 1962, the son of physicist Marshall Baker and geophysicist Marcia (née Bourgin) Baker. He graduated from Seattle's [Garfield High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garfield_High_School_(Seattle) "Garfield High School (Seattle)").

Baker received a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree with a major in biology from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") in 1984. He then joined the laboratory of [Randy Schekman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Schekman "Randy Schekman"), where he worked primarily on protein transport and trafficking in yeast, and obtained a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in biochemistry from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1989. In 1993, he completed his postdoctoral training in biophysics with [David Agard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Agard "David Agard") at the [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco "University of California, San Francisco").

### Career

Baker joined the Department of Biochemistry at the [University of Washington School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington_School_of_Medicine "University of Washington School of Medicine") as a faculty member in 1993. He became a [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute") investigator in 2000. Baker was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 2009.

### Personal life

Baker is married to [Hannele Ruohola-Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannele_Ruohola-Baker "Hannele Ruohola-Baker"), another biochemist at the University of Washington. They have two children.

## Research

Although primarily known for the development of [computational methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_biology "Computational biology") for predicting and designing the [structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_structure "Protein structure") and [functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_function "Protein function") of proteins, Baker maintains an active experimental biochemistry group. He has authored over 600 scientific papers.

Baker's group developed the Rosetta algorithm for *[ab initio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ab_initio "Ab initio")* [protein structure prediction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_structure_prediction "Protein structure prediction"), which has been extended into a tool for protein design, a [distributed computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_computing "Distributed computing") project called [Rosetta@home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta@home "Rosetta@home"), and the computer game [Foldit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foldit "Foldit"). Baker served as the director of the Rosetta Commons, a consortium of labs and researchers that develop biomolecular structure prediction and design software. His group has regularly competed in the [CASP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASP "CASP") structure prediction competition, specializing in *ab initio* methods, including both manually assisted and automated variants of the Rosetta protocol. Using [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"), his group has developed later a newer version of the program known as RoseTTAFold.

Baker's group is also active in the field of [protein design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_design "Protein design"); they are noted for designing [Top7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top7 "Top7"), the first artificial protein with a novel fold.

In 2017, Baker's Institute for Protein Design received over $11 million from [Open Philanthropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Philanthropy "Open Philanthropy"), followed by an additional $3 million donation in 2021.

In April 2019, Baker gave a [TED talk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TED_talk "TED talk") titled "5 challenges we could solve by designing new proteins" at TED2019 in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver "Vancouver"), Canada.

Baker has co-founded several biotechnology companies, including Prospect Genomics which was acquired by an Eli Lilly subsidiary in 2001, Icosavax which was acquired by AstraZeneca in 2023, Sana Biotechnology, Lyell Immunotherapeutics, Xaira Therapeutics and GenBio AI.

## Awards

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/David_Baker%2C_2024_Nobel_Prize_Laureate_in_Chemistry.jpg/250px-David_Baker%2C_2024_Nobel_Prize_Laureate_in_Chemistry.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:David_Baker,_2024_Nobel_Prize_Laureate_in_Chemistry.jpg)

Baker delivering a lecture during 2024 Nobel Week

For his work on protein folding, Baker has received numerous awards, including the [Overton Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overton_Prize "Overton Prize") (2002), the [Sackler International Prize in Biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sackler_Prize#Sackler_Prize_in_Biophysics "Sackler Prize") (2008), the [Wiley Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiley_Prize "Wiley Prize") (2022) and the [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Award "BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award") in the category "Biology and Biomedicine" (2022).

For his work on protein design, Baker has received the [Newcomb Cleveland Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcomb_Cleveland_Prize "Newcomb Cleveland Prize") (2004),
the [Feynman Prize in Nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_Prize_in_Nanotechnology "Feynman Prize in Nanotechnology") (2004), and the [Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Life_Sciences "Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences") (2021).

In 2024, Baker was awarded half of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his work on protein design; the other half went to [John M. Jumper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_M._Jumper "John M. Jumper") and [Demis Hassabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demis_Hassabis "Demis Hassabis") for development of [AlphaFold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlphaFold "AlphaFold"), a program for [protein structure prediction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_structure_prediction "Protein structure prediction").

In 2025, Baker was elected to the National Academy of Inventors (NAI) 2025 class of NAI fellows.
