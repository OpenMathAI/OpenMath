**Roger Yonchien Tsien** (Chinese: 錢永健*;* February 1, 1952 – August 24, 2016) was an American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). He was a professor of chemistry and biochemistry at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego"), and was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 2008 for his discovery and development of the [green fluorescent protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_fluorescent_protein "Green fluorescent protein"), in collaboration with organic chemist [Osamu Shimomura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osamu_Shimomura "Osamu Shimomura") and neurobiologist [Martin Chalfie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Chalfie "Martin Chalfie"). Tsien was also a pioneer of [calcium imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_imaging "Calcium imaging").

## Early life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Krugman-Tsien-Chalfie-Shimomura-Kobayashi-Masukawa-press_conference_Dec_07th%2C_2008-1.jpg/330px-Krugman-Tsien-Chalfie-Shimomura-Kobayashi-Masukawa-press_conference_Dec_07th%2C_2008-1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Krugman-Tsien-Chalfie-Shimomura-Kobayashi-Masukawa-press_conference_Dec_07th,_2008-1.jpg)

[Paul Krugman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Krugman "Paul Krugman"), Roger Tsien, [Martin Chalfie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Chalfie "Martin Chalfie"), [Osamu Shimomura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osamu_Shimomura "Osamu Shimomura"), [Makoto Kobayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makoto_Kobayashi_(physicist) "Makoto Kobayashi (physicist)") and [Toshihide Masukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshihide_Masukawa "Toshihide Masukawa"), Nobel Prize Laureates (2008), at a press conference at the [Swedish Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy_of_Science "Swedish Academy of Science") in Stockholm.

Tsien was born to a Chinese American family in New York in 1952. He grew up in [Livingston, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livingston,_New_Jersey "Livingston, New Jersey"), and attended [Livingston High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livingston_High_School_(New_Jersey) "Livingston High School (New Jersey)"). Tsien traces his family ancestry to [Hangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou "Hangzhou"), China. His father [Hsue-Chu Tsien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsue-Chu_Tsien "Hsue-Chu Tsien"), an [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT "MIT") and [Shanghai Chiao Tung University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Chiao_Tung_University "Shanghai Chiao Tung University") alumnus, was a [mechanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering "Mechanical engineering") engineer and had excelled academically, graduating at the top of his university class.

Tsien suffered from asthma as a child, and as a result, he was often indoors. He spent hours conducting chemistry experiments in his basement laboratory. When he was 16, he won first prize in the nationwide [Westinghouse Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Science_Talent_Search "Intel Science Talent Search") with a project investigating how metals bind to [thiocyanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiocyanate "Thiocyanate").

### Education

Tsien attended [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College "Harvard College") on a [National Merit Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Merit_Scholarship "National Merit Scholarship") and was elected to [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa "Phi Beta Kappa") as a junior. He graduated *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude "Summa cum laude")* with a Bachelor of Arts in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") and physics in 1972. According to his freshman-year roommate, economist and Iowa politician [Herman Quirmbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Quirmbach "Herman Quirmbach"), "It's probably not an exaggeration to say he's the smartest person I ever met ... [a]nd I have met a lot of brilliant people."

After completing his bachelor's degree, Tsien joined the [Physiological Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Physiology,_Development_and_Neuroscience,_University_of_Cambridge "Department of Physiology, Development and Neuroscience, University of Cambridge") at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge"), England with the aid of a [Marshall Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Scholarship "Marshall Scholarship"), and resided at [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge "Churchill College, Cambridge"). He received his PhD in [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology") in 1977 for research on *The Design and Use of Organic Chemical Tools in Cellular Physiology* formally supervised by [Richard Adrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Adrian,_2nd_Baron_Adrian "Richard Adrian, 2nd Baron Adrian") in the department of physiology and assisted by Andy Holmes, Gerry Smith and [Jeremy Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Sanders "Jeremy Sanders") in the department of chemistry.

## Research and career

Following his Ph.D., Tsien was a research fellow at [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge "Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge"), from 1977 to 1981. He was appointed to the faculty at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), from 1982 to 1989. Beginning in 1989, he worked at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego"), as professor of pharmacology and professor of chemistry and biochemistry, and as an investigator of the [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute").

Tsien contributed to the fields of cell biology and neurobiology by discovering genetically programmable fluorescent tags, thereby allowing scientists to watch the behavior of molecules in living cells in real time. He also developed fluorescent indicators of [calcium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium "Calcium") ions and other ions important in biological processes.

In 2004, Tsien was awarded the [Wolf Prize in Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Medicine "Wolf Prize in Medicine") "for his seminal contribution to the design and biological application of novel fluorescent and photolabile molecules to analyze and perturb cell signal transduction."

In 2008, Tsien shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry with [Osamu Shimomura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osamu_Shimomura "Osamu Shimomura") and [Martin Chalfie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Chalfie "Martin Chalfie") for "the green fluorescent protein: discovery, expression and development."

### Fluorescent proteins

The multicolored [fluorescent proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_fluorescent_protein "Green fluorescent protein") developed in Tsien's lab are used by scientists to track where and when certain genes are expressed in cells or in whole organisms. Typically, the gene coding for a protein of interest is fused with the gene for a fluorescent protein, which causes the protein of interest to glow inside the cell when the cell is irradiated with a suitable wavelength of light and allows microscopists to track its location in real time. This is such a popular technique that it has added a new dimension to the fields of molecular biology, cell biology, and biochemistry.

Since the discovery of the [wild type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_type "Wild type") [GFP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_fluorescent_protein "Green fluorescent protein"), numerous different mutants of GFP have been engineered and tested. The first significant leap forward was a [single point mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_mutation "Point mutation") (S65T) reported by Tsien in 1995 in *Nature*. This mutation dramatically improved the fluorescent (both [intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intensity_(physics) "Intensity (physics)") and [photostability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photobleaching "Photobleaching")) and spectral characteristics of GFP. A shift of the major excitation peak to 488 nm with the emission peak staying at 509 nm thus can be clearly observed, which matched very well the spectral characteristics of commonly available [FITC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescein "Fluorescein") facilities. All these then largely amplified the practicality of using GFP by scientists in their research. Tsien mainly contributed to much of our understanding of how GFP works and for developing new techniques and mutants of GFP.

Former trainees of Roger Y. Tsien include Atsushi Miyawaki and [Alice Y. Ting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Y._Ting "Alice Y. Ting").

Timelines of GFP-development involved by Tsien:

- 1994: Tsien showed the mechanism that GFP [chromophore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromophore "Chromophore") is formed in a chemical reaction which requires [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen "Oxygen") but without help from the other proteins.
- 1994–1998: Tsien and collaborators made various GFP mutants by genetic modification and structural tweaking. Newly created variants of GFP can shine more brightly and show different colours, such as yellow, cyan, and blue.
- 2000–2002: Tsien produced monomeric variants of DsRED, which can glow in shades of red, pink, and orange. Remarkably, since then complicated marcromolecular networks of living organisms can be labelled or marked by using "all the colours of the rainbow".

Other detailed highlights involved by Tsien:

- 2002: The critical structural difference between GFP and DsRed was revealed. One extra double-bond in the chromophore of DsRed extends its conjugation thus causes the red-shift.
- 2002: Monomeric DsRed (mRFP) was first developed.
- 2004: New "fruit" FPs were generated (by *in vitro* and *in vivo* directed evolutions).

In 2009, a new kind of Infrared Fluorescent Protein (IFP) was developed by Tsien's group, and further reported and described by *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_(journal) "Science (journal)")*. The new IFPs are developed from [bacterial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterial "Bacterial") [phytochromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytochrome "Phytochrome") instead of from [multicellular organism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular_organism "Multicellular organism") like jellyfish. Under normal conditions, bacterial phytochromes absorb light for signaling instead of fluorescence, but they can be turned fluorescent after deleting some of the signaling parts by [genetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics") means such as [site-directed mutagenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site-directed_mutagenesis "Site-directed mutagenesis"). In order to fluoresce, IFPs require an exogenous [chromophore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromophore "Chromophore"), [biliverdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biliverdin "Biliverdin").

In 2016, a new class of [fluorescent protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescent_protein "Fluorescent protein") was evolved from a [cyanobacterial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanobacteria "Cyanobacteria") (*[Trichodesmium erythraeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trichodesmium_erythraeum "Trichodesmium erythraeum")*) [phycobiliprotein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phycobiliprotein "Phycobiliprotein"), α-[allophycocyanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allophycocyanin "Allophycocyanin"), and named small ultra red fluorescent protein ([smURFP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SmURFP "SmURFP")). [smURFP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SmURFP "SmURFP") [autocatalytically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis "Catalysis") self-incorporates the [chromophore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromophore "Chromophore") [biliverdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biliverdin "Biliverdin") without the need of an external [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein"), known as a [lyase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyase "Lyase"). Jellyfish- and [coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral "Coral")-derived fluorescent proteins require [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen "Oxygen") and produce a [stoichiometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoichiometry "Stoichiometry") amount of [hydrogen peroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_peroxide "Hydrogen peroxide") upon [chromophore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromophore "Chromophore") formation. [smURFP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SmURFP "SmURFP") does not require [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen "Oxygen") or produce [hydrogen peroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_peroxide "Hydrogen peroxide") and uses the [chromophore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromophore "Chromophore"), [biliverdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biliverdin "Biliverdin"). [smURFP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SmURFP "SmURFP") has a large [extinction coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molar_attenuation_coefficient "Molar attenuation coefficient") (180,000 M−1 cm−1) and has a modest [quantum yield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_yield "Quantum yield") (0.20), which makes it comparable biophysical brightness to [eGFP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EGFP "EGFP") and ~2-fold brighter than most red or far-red [fluorescent proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescent_protein "Fluorescent protein") derived from [coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral "Coral"). [smURFP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SmURFP "SmURFP") spectral properties are similar to the organic dye [Cy5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cy5 "Cy5").

### Next generation sequencing

Roger Tsien built the foundation of next generation sequencing technology that became widely used. On October 26, 1990, [Roger Tsien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Tsien "Roger Tsien") *et al.* filed a patent of stepwise ("base-by-base") sequencing with removable 3' blockers on DNA arrays. Illumina integrated this concept with DNA cloning for their next generation sequencer.

### Calcium imaging

Tsien was a pioneer of [calcium imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_imaging "Calcium imaging") and known for developing various dyes which become fluorescent in the presence of particular ions such as calcium. One such dye, [fura-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fura-2 "Fura-2"), is widely used to track changes of calcium concentration within cells. [indo-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-1 "Indo-1") and [fluo-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluo-3 "Fluo-3"), other popular calcium indicators, were also developed by Tsien's group in 1985 and 1989 respectively. He has also developed fluorescent indicators for other ions such as magnesium, zinc, copper, iron, lead, cadmium, aluminum, nickel, cobalt, and mercury.

[Aequorin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aequorin "Aequorin") is also a useful tool to indicate calcium level inside cells; however, it has some limitations, primarily is that its prosthetic group coelenterazine is consumed irreversibly when emits light, thus requires continuous addition of coelenterazine into the media. To overcome such issues, Tsien's group also developed the [calmodulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calmodulin "Calmodulin")-based sensor, named [Cameleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameleon_(protein) "Cameleon (protein)").

### FlAsH-EDT2

[FlAsH-EDT2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FlAsH-EDT2 "FlAsH-EDT2") is a bioanalytical research method developed by Roger Tsien and colleagues in 1998, in which the abbreviation stands for ***F**luorescin **A**r**S**enical **H**airpin binder-**E**thane**D**i**T**hiol (FlAsH-EDT2)*. The [organoarsenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organoarsenic_chemistry "Organoarsenic chemistry") FlAsH-EDT2 compound is used for site-specific labeling of proteins that possess a tetracysteine motif (ie., Cys-Cys-Xxx-Xxx-Cys-Cys) in living cells. Once bound, [excitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescence "Fluorescence") of the compound with 508 nm light causes a bright fluorescent green emission at 528 nm. FlAsH-EDT2 has been used as an alternative to [green fluorescent protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_fluorescent_protein "Green fluorescent protein") [(GFP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_fluorescent_protein "Green fluorescent protein"), as the molecule may better minimize perturbations to target protein activity given its small size (ie., molar mass <1 [kDa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_(unit) "Dalton (unit)")) relative to GFPs (ie., molar mass ~30 [kDa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_(unit) "Dalton (unit)")).

### Fluorescence-assisted cancer surgery

Mouse experiments by Tsien's group suggest that cancer surgery can be guided and assisted by fluorescent [peptides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptide "Peptide"). The peptides are used as probes, and are harmless to living tissues and organs. Their lifetime in the body is only 4 or 5 days. [Clinical trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_trial "Clinical trial") are awaited.

### Industrial activities

Tsien was also a notable biochemical inventor and held or coheld about 100 patents till 2010. In 1996, Tsien cofounded the Aurora Biosciences Corporation, which went public in 1997. In 2001, Aurora was acquired by the [Vertex Pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_Pharmaceuticals "Vertex Pharmaceuticals"). Similarly, Tsien was also a scientific cofounder of [Senomyx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senomyx "Senomyx") in 1999.

Tsien also promoted science education to promising young scientists through the first-ever San Diego Science Festival Lunch with a Laureate Program.

## Personal life

Tsien is a 34th-generational [descendant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lineal_descendant "Lineal descendant") of the King of [Wuyüeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuyue "Wuyue"), [Tsien Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qian_Liu "Qian Liu"). Tsien's parents Hsue-Chu Tsien and Yi-Ying Li (李懿穎) came from [Hangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou "Hangzhou") and Beijing, respectively.

Tsien had a number of engineers in his extended family, including his father [Hsue-Chu Tsien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsue-Chu_Tsien "Hsue-Chu Tsien") who was an [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology")-educated mechanical engineer and his mother's brothers [Yao-Tzu Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao-Tzu_Li "Yao-Tzu Li") and [Shih-Ying Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shih-Ying_Lee "Shih-Ying Lee"), who were engineering professors at MIT. Tsien's mother Yi-Ying Li was a nurse. The rocket scientist [Tsien Hsue-shen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsien_Hsue-shen "Tsien Hsue-shen"), regarded as the cofounding father of the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory "Jet Propulsion Laboratory") of the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") and, later, the director of the Chinese [ballistic-missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile "Ballistic missile") and [space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_space_program "Chinese space program") programs, is a cousin of Tsien's father.

Tsien was the younger brother of [Richard Tsien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_W._Tsien "Richard W. Tsien"), a neurobiologist at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University"), and Louis Tsien, a software engineer. Tsien, who called his own work molecular engineering, once said, "I'm doomed by heredity to do this kind of work."

He was married to Wendy Globe.

### Death

Tsien died on August 24, 2016. Although the specific cause of death was not disclosed, it was reported that he died while on a bike trail in [Eugene, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon "Eugene, Oregon"). Prior to his death, Tsien had survived cancer and suffered a stroke in 2013.

"He was ahead of us all," said Tsien's wife, Wendy. "He was ever the adventurer, the pathfinder, the free and soaring spirit. Courage, determination, creativity and resourcefulness were hallmarks of his character. He accomplished much. He will not be forgotten."
