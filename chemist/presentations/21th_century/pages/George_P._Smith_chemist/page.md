**George Pearson Smith** (born 10 March 1941) is an American biologist and [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate "Nobel laureate"). He is a Curators' Distinguished Professor Emeritus of Biological Sciences at the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri "University of Missouri") in [Columbia, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_Missouri "Columbia, Missouri"), US.

## Career

Born in [Norwalk, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwalk,_Connecticut "Norwalk, Connecticut"), he earned his [A.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree from [Haverford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College "Haverford College") in biology, was a high school teacher and lab technician for a year, and earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree in bacteriology and immunology from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). He was a postdoc at the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin "University of Wisconsin") (with future Nobel laureate [Oliver Smithies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Smithies "Oliver Smithies")) before moving to Columbia, Missouri and joining the University of Missouri faculty in 1975. He spent the 1983–1984 academic year at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University") with Robert Webster where he began the work that led to him being awarded a Nobel Prize.

He is best known for [phage display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phage_display "Phage display"), a technique where a specific [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") sequence is artificially inserted into the [coat protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_coat_protein "Viral coat protein") gene of a [bacteriophage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriophage "Bacteriophage"), causing the protein to be expressed on the outside of the bacteriophage. Smith first described the technique in 1985 when he displayed [peptides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptide "Peptide") on [filamentous phage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filamentous_phage "Filamentous phage") by fusing the peptide of interest onto gene III of filamentous phage. He was awarded the 2018 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for this work, sharing his prize with [Greg Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Winter "Greg Winter") and [Frances Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Arnold "Frances Arnold").

## Human rights advocacy

Smith is an advocate for equal rights for Palestinians and Israeli Jews in their common homeland, and a strong supporter of the [Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott,_Divestment_and_Sanctions "Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions") movement. On the topic of religion, Smith is quoted as saying "I'm not religious or Jewish by birth. But my wife is Jewish and our sons are bar-mitzvahed, and I'm very engaged with Jewish culture and politics."

## Awards and honors

- 2000 University of Missouri Curators' Professor
- 2001 Elected Fellow – [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science") (AAAS)
- 2007 [American Society for Microbiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Microbiology "American Society for Microbiology") Promega Biotechnology Research Award
- 2018 Nobel Prize in Chemistry together with [Greg Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Winter "Greg Winter") and [Frances Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Arnold "Frances Arnold")
- 2020 Elected Member – [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") (NAS)
- 2023 Inaugural Mizzou Medal of Distinction – [University of Missouri College of Arts and Sciences](https://coas.missouri.edu/)
