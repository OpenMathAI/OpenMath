**Martin Lee Chalfie** (born January 15, 1947) is an American scientist. He is [University Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_university_professors_at_Columbia_University "List of university professors at Columbia University") at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"). He shared the 2008 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") along with [Osamu Shimomura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osamu_Shimomura "Osamu Shimomura") and [Roger Y. Tsien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Y._Tsien "Roger Y. Tsien") "for the discovery and development of the [green fluorescent protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_fluorescent_protein "Green fluorescent protein"), GFP". He holds a PhD in [neurobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurobiology "Neurobiology") from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University").

## Early life and education

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Krugman-Tsien-Chalfie-Shimomura-Kobayashi-Masukawa-press_conference_Dec_07th%2C_2008-1.jpg/250px-Krugman-Tsien-Chalfie-Shimomura-Kobayashi-Masukawa-press_conference_Dec_07th%2C_2008-1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Krugman-Tsien-Chalfie-Shimomura-Kobayashi-Masukawa-press_conference_Dec_07th,_2008-1.jpg)

[Paul Krugman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Krugman "Paul Krugman"), [Roger Tsien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Tsien "Roger Tsien"), Martin Chalfie, [Osamu Shimomura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osamu_Shimomura "Osamu Shimomura"), [Makoto Kobayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makoto_Kobayashi_(physicist) "Makoto Kobayashi (physicist)") and [Toshihide Masukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshihide_Masukawa "Toshihide Masukawa"), Nobel Prize Laureates 2008, at a press conference at the [Swedish Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy_of_Science "Swedish Academy of Science") in Stockholm.

Chalfie grew up in Chicago, Illinois, son of the guitarist Eli Chalfie (1910–1996) and owner of an apparel store Vivian Chalfie (née Friedlen, 1913–2005). His maternal grandfather, Meyer L. Friedlen, immigrated to Chicago from Moscow at an early age; his paternal grandparents, Benjamin and Esther Chalfie, came to [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati "Cincinnati") from [Brest-Litovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_Belarus "Brest, Belarus") and are [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews").

He matriculated at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") in 1965, intending to be a math major, but he switched to biochemistry because it combined his interests in chemistry, math, and biology. He spent the summer after his junior year working in the laboratory of [Klaus Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Weber "Klaus Weber") at Harvard, but "It was so disheartening to completely fail that I decided I shouldn't be in biology." As a result, in his senior year, he completed his major and took courses in law, theater, and Russian literature.

He also competed on the swim team at Harvard and was named captain in his senior year. At the time, swimming coach Bill Brooks said, "Marty will make an excellent captain because he has the admiration of the entire team."
As captain, he won the Harold S. Ulen trophy, awarded "to a senior on the Harvard team who best demonstrates those qualities of leadership, sportsmanship, and team cooperation as exemplified by Harold S. Ulen." Following the announcement of Chalfie's Nobel award, his freshman-year roommate observed of Chalfie, "He would always identify himself as a swimmer."

After graduating in 1969, he worked at a variety of temporary jobs, such as selling dresses for his parents' dress manufacturing business in Chicago and teaching at [Hamden Hall Country Day School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamden_Hall_Country_Day_School "Hamden Hall Country Day School") in [Hamden, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamden,_Connecticut "Hamden, Connecticut"). In the summer of 1971, his research at the laboratory of [Jose Zadunaisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Zadunaisky?action=edit&redlink=1 "Jose Zadunaisky") at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") resulted in his first publication. With revived confidence, he returned to Harvard for graduate studies under [Robert Perlman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Perlman?action=edit&redlink=1 "Robert Perlman"), and received his PhD in 1977.

## Career and research

Chalfie conducted his [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral "Postdoctoral") research at the [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") (LMB) with [Sydney Brenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Brenner "Sydney Brenner") and [John Sulston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Sulston "John Sulston"), and the three published a paper in 1985 on "The Neural Circuit for Touch Sensitivity in *[C. elegans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caenorhabditis_elegans "Caenorhabditis elegans")*". Chalfie then left the LMB in 1982 to join the faculty of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in the department of biological sciences and continued to study *[C. elegans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caenorhabditis_elegans "Caenorhabditis elegans")* touch mutants.

He married [Tulle Hazelrigg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulle_Hazelrigg "Tulle Hazelrigg"). She later joined him on the faculty of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"). She gave him permission to cite her unpublished research in his seminal Science paper "Green Fluorescent Protein as a Marker for Gene Expression" on condition that he made coffee, cooked, and emptied the garbage nightly for a month.

Chalfie and his wife had a daughter, Sarah, in July 1992.

Chalfie was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") in 2004.

He slept through the phone call from the Nobel Prize Committee. When he woke up, he knew the prize would have been announced already, so he said "Okay, who's the [schnook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schnook "Schnook") that got the Prize this time?" And so he opened up his laptop, got to the Nobel Prize site and found out that he was the schnook!

In 2015, Chalfie signed the *[Mainau Declaration 2015 on Climate Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainau_Declaration "Mainau Declaration")* on the final day of the 65th [Lindau Nobel Laureate Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindau_Nobel_Laureate_Meetings "Lindau Nobel Laureate Meetings"). The declaration was signed by a total of 76 Nobel Laureates and handed to then-President of the French Republic, [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Hollande "François Hollande"), as part of the successful [COP21 climate summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Climate_Change_Conference "Paris Climate Change Conference") in Paris.

Chalfie's lab uses the nematode ''C. elegans'' to investigate aspects of nerve cell development and function. The developmental, anatomical, genetic, and molecular information available for ''C. elegans'' makes it a well-studied model organism for these research areas.

He has published over 100 papers of which at least 25 have over 100 citations.

He traces his work on [green fluorescent protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_fluorescent_protein "Green fluorescent protein") to a 1988 seminar from [Paul Brehm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Brehm "Paul Brehm") about bioluminescent organisms, which led to some crucial experiments in 1992, detailed in his paper "Green fluorescent protein as a marker for gene expression", which is among the 20 most-cited papers in the field of Molecular Biology & Genetics. Chalfie won a [Golden Goose Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Goose_Award "Golden Goose Award") for this work in 2012.

He received an honorary degree in physics from the University of Parma on July 4, 2023.
