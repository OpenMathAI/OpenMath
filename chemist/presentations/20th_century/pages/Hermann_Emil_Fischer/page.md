**Hermann Emil Louis Fischer** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh "Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh") [FCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Chemical_Society "Fellow of the Chemical Society") (German pronunciation: [[ˈeːmiːl ˈfɪʃɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Emil_Fischer.ogg "File:De-Emil Fischer.ogg"); 9 October 1852 – 15 July 1919) was a German [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") and [1902 recipient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Chemistry "List of Nobel laureates in Chemistry") of the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"). He discovered the [Fischer esterification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer_esterification "Fischer esterification"). He also developed the [Fischer projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer_projection "Fischer projection"), a symbolic way of drawing asymmetric carbon atoms. He also hypothesized the lock and key mechanism of enzyme action. He never used his first given name, and was known throughout his life simply as Emil Fischer.

## Early years and career

Fischer was born in [Euskirchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euskirchen "Euskirchen"), near [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne "Cologne"), the son of Laurenz Fischer, a businessman, and his wife Julie Poensgen. After graduating he wished to study [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science "Natural science"), but his father compelled him to work in the family business until determining that his son was unsuitable. Fischer then attended the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn "University of Bonn") in 1871, but switched to the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg "University of Strasbourg") in 1872. He obtained his doctorate in 1874 under [Adolf von Baeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_von_Baeyer "Adolf von Baeyer") with his study of [phthaleins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phthaleins "Phthaleins").

Fischer remained with Baeyer at the University of Strasbourg as an independent research student. In the fall of 1874, he was appointed assistant of the organic laboratory. There in 1875, he discovered and named [hydrazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrazines "Hydrazines"), including [unsymmetrical dimethylhydrazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsymmetrical_dimethylhydrazine "Unsymmetrical dimethylhydrazine"), which became important much later during the [Space Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Race "Space Race"), and [phenylhydrazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenylhydrazine "Phenylhydrazine"). The latter compound reacts with carboxylic compounds ([aldehydes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldehyde "Aldehyde") and [ketones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketone "Ketone")) producing crystalline solids. The phenylhydrazones of sugars allowed him to develop his work on the synthesis of sugars and purines, which earned him the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1902. Using the phenylhydrazone of pyruvic acid, he developed the synthesis of indole.

In 1875, von Baeyer was asked to succeed [Justus von Liebig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justus_von_Liebig "Justus von Liebig") at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München"), and Fischer went there with him to become an assistant in organic chemistry. In 1878, Fischer qualified as a "Privatdozent" at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München, where he was appointed associate professor of analytical chemistry in 1879.

In 1882, he was appointed professor of chemistry at the [University of Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen "University of Erlangen") and in 1885 at the [University of Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Würzburg "University of Würzburg"). In 1892, he succeeded [von Hofmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Wilhelm_von_Hofmann "August Wilhelm von Hofmann") as professor of chemistry at the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin "Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin").

## Research

He investigated the derivatives of phenylhydrazines, establishing their relation to the [diazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diazo "Diazo") compounds, and he noted the readiness with which they entered into combination with other substances, giving origin to a wealth of hitherto unknown compounds. Of such condensation products undoubtedly the most important are the [hydrazones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrazones "Hydrazones"), which result from the interaction with [aldehydes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldehydes "Aldehydes") and [ketones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketones "Ketones"). His observations, published in 1886, that such hydrazones, by treatment with [hydrochloric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrochloric_acid "Hydrochloric acid") or [zinc chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc_chloride "Zinc chloride"), yielded derivatives of [indole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indole "Indole"), the parent substance of [indigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigo_dye "Indigo dye"), were a confirmation of the views advanced by von Baeyer on the subject of indigo and the many substances related to it.

He next turned to the [fuchsine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuchsine "Fuchsine") (then called "rosaniline") [magenta dyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magenta_dye?action=edit&redlink=1 "Magenta dye"), and in collaboration with his cousin Otto Fischer, he published papers in 1878 and 1879 which established that these dyes were derivatives of [triphenylmethane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triphenylmethane "Triphenylmethane"). Emil Fischer's next research was concerned with compounds related to [uric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uric_acid "Uric acid"). Here the ground had been broken by von Baeyer, but Fischer greatly advanced the field of knowledge of the [purines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purines "Purines"). In 1881 and 1882 he published papers which established the formulae of [uric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uric_acid "Uric acid"), [xanthine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xanthine "Xanthine"), [caffeine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caffeine "Caffeine") (achieving the first synthesis), [theobromine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theobromine "Theobromine") and some other compounds of this group. After purine itself was isolated, a variety of derivatives were prepared, some of which were patented in view of possible therapeutical applications.

Fischer is particularly noted for his work on sugars. Among his early discoveries related to hydrazine was that [phenylhydrazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenylhydrazine "Phenylhydrazine") reacted with sugars to form substances which he named [osazones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osazone "Osazone"), and which, being highly crystalline and readily formed, served to identify such carbohydrates more definitely than had been previously possible. Later, among other work, he is noted for the [organic synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_synthesis "Organic synthesis") of D-(+)-[glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose "Glucose"). He showed how to deduce the formulae of the 16 [stereoisomeric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereoisomerism "Stereoisomerism") glucoses, and prepared several stereoisomerides, helping to confirm the [Le Bel–Van 't Hoff rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Bel–Van_'t_Hoff_rule "Le Bel–Van 't Hoff rule") of the [asymmetric carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_carbon "Asymmetric carbon") atom.

In the field of enzymology, Fischer is known for his proposal of ["the lock and key"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme#.22Lock_and_key.22_model "Enzyme") model as a mechanism of substrate binding.

Fischer was also instrumental in the discovery of [barbiturates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbiturates "Barbiturates"), a class of sedative drugs used for insomnia, epilepsy, anxiety, and anesthesia. Along with the physician [Josef von Mering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_von_Mering "Josef von Mering"), he helped to launch the first barbiturate sedative, [barbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbital "Barbital"), in 1904. He next carried out pioneering work on [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein"). By the introduction of new methods, he succeeded in breaking down the complex [albumins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albumin "Albumin") into [amino acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid "Amino acid") and other nitrogenous compounds, the constitutions of most of which were known, and by bringing about the recombination of these units, he prepared [synthetic peptides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptide_synthesis "Peptide synthesis") which approximated to the natural products. His research group synthesised the first free dipeptide (Glycine-Glycine) in 1901. By 1906 about 65 peptides of different chain length and amino acid composition had been made by his research group. His researches made from 1899 to 1906 were published in 1907 with the title *Untersuchungen über Aminosauren, Polypeptides und Proteine*. Three years later the total number of peptides exceeded 100, with the longest being an 18 amino acid peptide containing 15 glycine and three leucine units. The 18 amino acid peptide gave the standard responses to tests for proteins used by physiological chemists - a positive [Biuret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biuret_test "Biuret test") test, precipitation by inorganic salts and cleavage by proteolytic enzymes

## Personal life

Fischer married Agnes Gerlach in 1888. She died seven years later, leaving him a widower with three sons. The younger two died during their military service in World War I, but the oldest, [Hermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Otto_Laurenz_Fischer "Hermann Otto Laurenz Fischer"), became an organic chemist. Fischer died in Berlin on 15 July 1919 at the age of 66. He was Protestant

## Honours, awards, and legacy

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Hermann_Emil_Fischer_berlin.jpg/250px-Hermann_Emil_Fischer_berlin.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hermann_Emil_Fischer_berlin.jpg)

Monument to Emil Fischer in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin")

In 1897 he put forward the idea to create the [International Atomic Weights Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Isotopic_Abundances_and_Atomic_Weights "Commission on Isotopic Abundances and Atomic Weights"). Fischer was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1899](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1899 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1899"). He was awarded the 1902 Nobel Prize in chemistry "in recognition of the extraordinary services he has rendered by his work on sugar and purine syntheses." He was elected an International Member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1904, an International Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1908, and an International Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1909.

Many names of chemical reactions and concepts are named after him:

- [Fischer indole synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer_indole_synthesis "Fischer indole synthesis")
- [Fischer projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer_projection "Fischer projection")
- [Fischer oxazole synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer_oxazole_synthesis "Fischer oxazole synthesis")
- [Fischer peptide synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer_peptide_synthesis "Fischer peptide synthesis")
- [Fischer phenylhydrazine and oxazone reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer_phenylhydrazine_and_oxazone_reaction "Fischer phenylhydrazine and oxazone reaction")
- [Fischer–Speier esterification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer–Speier_esterification "Fischer–Speier esterification")
- [Fischer glycosidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer_glycosidation "Fischer glycosidation")
- [Kiliani–Fischer synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiliani–Fischer_synthesis "Kiliani–Fischer synthesis")

The [Fischer–Tropsch process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer–Tropsch_process "Fischer–Tropsch process") is named after [Franz Emil Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_Emil_Fischer "Franz Joseph Emil Fischer"), who headed the [Max Planck Institute for Coal Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Coal_Research "Max Planck Institute for Coal Research") in [Mülheim an der Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mülheim_an_der_Ruhr "Mülheim an der Ruhr"), and is unrelated to Fischer.
