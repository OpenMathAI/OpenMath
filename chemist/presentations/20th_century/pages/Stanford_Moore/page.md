**Stanford Moore** (September 4, 1913 – August 23, 1982) was an American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). He shared a [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1972, with [Christian B. Anfinsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_B._Anfinsen "Christian B. Anfinsen") and [William Howard Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Stein "William Howard Stein"), for work done at [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University") on the structure of the enzyme [ribonuclease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribonuclease "Ribonuclease") and for contributing to the understanding of the connection between the [chemical structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_structure "Chemical structure") and [catalytic activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis "Catalysis") of the ribonuclease molecule.

Moore attended [Peabody Demonstration School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peabody_Demonstration_School "Peabody Demonstration School"), now known as University School of Nashville, and in 1935 graduated [summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude "Summa cum laude") from [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University "Vanderbilt University"), where he was a member of [Phi Kappa Sigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Kappa_Sigma "Phi Kappa Sigma"). He earned his doctorate in Organic Chemistry from the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison") in 1938. Moore then joined the staff of the Rockefeller Institute, later Rockefeller University, where he spent his entire professional career, with the exception of a period of government service during World War II. He became Professor of Biochemistry in 1952.

In 1958, he and [William H. Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H._Stein "William H. Stein") developed the first automated amino acid analyzer, which facilitated the determination of [protein sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_sequence "Protein sequence"). In 1959, Moore and Stein announced the first determination of the complete amino acid sequence of an enzyme, ribonuclease, work which was cited in the Nobel award.
