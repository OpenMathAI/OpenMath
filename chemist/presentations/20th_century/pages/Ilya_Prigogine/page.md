[Viscount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscount "Viscount") **Ilya Romanovich Prigogine** ([/prɪˈɡoʊʒiːn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Илья Романович Пригожин; 25 January [[O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates "Old Style and New Style dates") 12 January] 1917 – 28 May 2003) was a Belgian [physical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry") of Russian origin, noted for his work on [dissipative structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissipative_system "Dissipative system"), [complex systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_systems "Complex systems"), and [irreversibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreversibility "Irreversibility").

Prigogine's work most notably earned him the 1977 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") “for his contributions to non-equilibrium thermodynamics, particularly the theory of dissipative structures”, as well as the [Francqui Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francqui_Prize "Francqui Prize") in 1955, and the [Rumford Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumford_Medal "Rumford Medal") in 1976.

## Biography

### Early life and studies

Prigogine was born in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow") a few months before the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution "October Revolution") of 1917, into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") family. His father, Ruvim Abramovich Prigogine (1884–1974), was a [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") who studied at the [Imperial Moscow Technical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauman_Moscow_State_Technical_University "Bauman Moscow State Technical University") and owned a paint factory; his mother, Yulia Leivikovna Vikhman (1892–?), was a pianist who attended the [Moscow Conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Conservatory "Moscow Conservatory"). In 1921, the factory having been [nationalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_communism "War communism") by the [new Soviet regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Soviet_Russia_and_the_Soviet_Union_(1917–1927) "History of Soviet Russia and the Soviet Union (1917–1927)") and the feeling of insecurity rising amidst the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War "Russian Civil War"), the family left Russia. After a brief period in [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania "Lithuania"), they went to [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic") and settled in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin"); 8 years later, the poor economic situation and the creeping emergence of [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism") caused them to move on to [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels "Brussels"), where Prigogine received Belgian nationality in 1949. His brother [Alexandre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Prigogine "Alexandre Prigogine") (1913–1991) became an ornithologist.

As a teenager, Prigogine was interested in music, history and archeology. He graduated from the *Athenée d'[Ixelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ixelles "Ixelles")* in 1935, majoring in Greek and Latin. His parents encouraged him to become a lawyer, and he initially enrolled in law studies at the [Free University of Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Brussels_(1834–1969) "Free University of Brussels (1834–1969)"). At that time he developed an interest in [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology") and the [study of behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioural_sciences "Behavioural sciences"); in turn, reading about these subjects triggered an interest in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"), as chemical processes impact the [mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind "Mind") and [body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind–body_problem "Mind–body problem"); this also triggered a more fundamental interest in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics"), as they explain chemistry. He ended up dropping out from the law faculty.

Prigogine afterwards simultaneously enrolled in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") and [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") at the [Free University of Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Brussels_(1834–1969) "Free University of Brussels (1834–1969)"), something he achieved with "uncommon success"; he earned the equivalent of a Master's degree in both disciplines in 1939, and a PhD in chemistry in 1941 under [Théophile de Donder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Théophile_de_Donder "Théophile de Donder").

### Early career, World War II

He started his research career under the [German occupation of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Belgium_during_World_War_II "German occupation of Belgium during World War II"). From 1940 onwards he gave clandestine lectures to students. In 1941, the university formally closed to protest the forced appointment of [Flemish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_people "Flemish people") pro-Nazi [New Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Order_(Nazism) "New Order (Nazism)") professors by the occupiers; he continued giving clandestine lectures until the [Liberation of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Belgium "Liberation of Belgium") in 1944. During that time window he also published 21 articles. In 1943, Prigogine and his future wife Hélène Jofé were arrested by the Germans; after multiple interventions including by the [Queen Elisabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_of_Bavaria,_Queen_of_the_Belgians "Elisabeth of Bavaria, Queen of the Belgians"), they were eventually released a couple of weeks later.

### Later career

In 1951, he became a full professor at his alma mater; at 34 years old, he was the youngest ever full professor at the science faculty in [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels "Brussels"). In 1959, he was appointed director of the [International Solvay Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvay_Conference "Solvay Conference") in [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels "Brussels"), [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium "Belgium"). In that year, he also started teaching at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin "University of Texas at Austin") in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), where he later was appointed Regental Professor and Ashbel Smith Professor of Physics and Chemical Engineering. From 1961 until 1966 he was affiliated with the [Enrico Fermi Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi_Institute "Enrico Fermi Institute") at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") and was a visiting professor at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University"). In Austin, in 1967, he co-founded the Center for Thermodynamics and Statistical Mechanics, now the [Center for Complex Quantum Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Complex_Quantum_Systems "Center for Complex Quantum Systems"). In that year, he also returned to [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium "Belgium"), where he became director of the Center for Statistical Mechanics and Thermodynamics.

He was a member of numerous scientific organizations, and received numerous awards, prizes and 53 honorary degrees. In 1955, Prigogine was awarded the [Francqui Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francqui_Prize "Francqui Prize") for Exact Sciences. For his study in [irreversible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreversibility "Irreversibility") [thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamics "Thermodynamics"), he received the [Rumford Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumford_Medal "Rumford Medal") in 1976, and in 1977, the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") "for his contributions to non-equilibrium thermodynamics, particularly the theory of [dissipative structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissipative_structures "Dissipative structures")". In 1989, he was awarded the title of [viscount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscount "Viscount") in the [Belgian nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_nobility "Belgian nobility") by the [King of the Belgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_Belgians "King of the Belgians"). Until his death, he was president of the International Academy of Science, Munich and was in 1997, one of the founders of the International Commission on Distance Education (CODE), a worldwide accreditation agency. Prigogine received an Honorary Doctorate from [Heriot-Watt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heriot-Watt_University "Heriot-Watt University") in 1985 and in 1998 he was awarded an *[honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa "Honoris causa")* doctorate by the [UNAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNAM "UNAM") in [Mexico City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_City "Mexico City").

Prigogine was first married to Belgian poet Hélène Jofé (as an author also known as Hélène Prigogine) and in 1945 they had a son Yves. After their divorce, he married Polish-born [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") Maria Prokopowicz (also known as Maria Prigogine) in 1961. In 1970 they had a son, Pascal.

In 2003 he was one of 22 Nobel Laureates who signed the [Humanist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism_and_Its_Aspirations "Humanism and Its Aspirations").

## Research

Prigogine defined [dissipative structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissipative_structures "Dissipative structures") and their role in [thermodynamic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic_system "Thermodynamic system") far from [equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic_equilibrium "Thermodynamic equilibrium"), a discovery that won him the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in Chemistry in 1977. In summary, Ilya Prigogine discovered that importation and dissipation of energy into chemical systems could result in the emergence of new structures (hence dissipative structures) due to internal self reorganization. In his 1955 text, Prigogine drew connections between dissipative structures and the [Rayleigh-Bénard instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh–Bénard_convection "Rayleigh–Bénard convection") and the [Turing mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_pattern "Turing pattern"). And his 1977 work on self-reorganization was recognized as relevant for psychology.

### Dissipative structures theory

[Dissipative structure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissipative_structure "Dissipative structure") led to pioneering research in [self-organizing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organizing_system "Self-organizing system"), as well as philosophical inquiries into the formation of [complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity "Complexity") in biological entities and the quest for a creative and irreversible role of time in the [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science "Natural science").

With professor [Robert Herman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Herman "Robert Herman"), he also developed the basis of the [two fluid model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_fluid_model "Two fluid model"), a traffic model in [traffic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_engineering_(transportation) "Traffic engineering (transportation)") for urban networks, analogous to the two fluid model in classical statistical mechanics, a common problem that had attracted Prigogine's attention some years before.

Prigogine's formal concept of [self-organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organization "Self-organization") was used also as a "complementary bridge" between [general systems theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Systems_Theory "General Systems Theory") and [thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamics "Thermodynamics"), conciliating the cloudiness of some important systems theory concepts such as [entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy "Entropy") instead of molecular disorder, and [emergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergence "Emergence"), fluctuations and irreversibility instead of “birth and death” with scientific rigor.

### Work on unsolved problems in physics

In his later years, his work concentrated on the fundamental role of [indeterminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminism "Indeterminism") in [nonlinear systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_dynamics "Nonlinear dynamics") on both the [classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_physics "Classical physics") and [quantum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics "Quantum physics") level. Prigogine and coworkers proposed a [Liouville space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liouville's_theorem_(Hamiltonian) "Liouville's theorem (Hamiltonian)") extension of quantum mechanics.A Liouville space is the [vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_space "Vector space") formed by the set of (self-adjoint) [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator "Linear operator"), equipped with an inner product, that act on a [Hilbert space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_space "Hilbert space"). There exists a mapping of each linear operator into Liouville space, yet not every self-adjoint operator of Liouville space has a counterpart in Hilbert space, and in this sense Liouville space has a richer structure than Hilbert space. The Liouville space extension proposal by Prigogine and co-workers aimed to solve the [arrow of time problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_(arrow_of_time)#Current_research "Entropy (arrow of time)") of thermodynamics and the [measurement problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurement_problem "Measurement problem") of quantum mechanics.

Prigogine co-authored several books with [Isabelle Stengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabelle_Stengers "Isabelle Stengers"), including *The End of Certainty* and *La Nouvelle Alliance* (*[Order Out of Chaos: Man's New Dialogue with Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_Out_of_Chaos:_Man's_New_Dialogue_with_Nature "Order Out of Chaos: Man's New Dialogue with Nature")*).

### *The End of Certainty*

In his 1996 book, *La Fin des certitudes*, written in collaboration with Isabelle Stengers and published in English in 1997 as *The End of Certainty: Time, Chaos, and the New Laws of Nature*, Prigogine contends that determinism is no longer a viable scientific belief: "The more we know about our universe, the more difficult it becomes to believe in determinism." This is a major departure from the approach of [Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton "Isaac Newton"), [Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") and [Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger "Erwin Schrödinger"), all of whom expressed their theories in terms of deterministic equations. According to Prigogine, determinism loses its explanatory power in the face of [irreversibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreversibility "Irreversibility") and [instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instability "Instability").

Prigogine traces the dispute over determinism back to [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin"), whose attempt to explain individual variability according to evolving populations inspired [Ludwig Boltzmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Boltzmann "Ludwig Boltzmann") to explain the behavior of gases in terms of populations of particles rather than individual particles. This led to the field of [statistical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics") and the realization that gases undergo [irreversible processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreversible_process "Irreversible process"). In deterministic physics, all processes are time-reversible, meaning that they can proceed backward as well as forward through time. As Prigogine explains, determinism is fundamentally a denial of the [arrow of time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_of_time "Arrow of time"). With no arrow of time, there is no longer a privileged moment known as the "present," which follows a determined "past" and precedes an undetermined "future." All of time is simply given, with the future as determined or as undetermined as the past. With irreversibility, the arrow of time is reintroduced to physics. Prigogine notes numerous examples of irreversibility, including [diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion "Diffusion"), [radioactive decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_decay "Radioactive decay"), [solar radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_radiation "Solar radiation"), [weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather "Weather") and the emergence and evolution of [life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life "Life"). Like weather systems, organisms are unstable systems existing far from [thermodynamic equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic_equilibrium "Thermodynamic equilibrium"). Instability resists standard deterministic explanation. Instead, due to sensitivity to initial conditions, unstable systems can only be explained statistically, that is, in terms of [probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability "Probability").

Prigogine asserts that [Newtonian physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtonian_physics "Newtonian physics") has now been "extended" three times: first with the introduction of [spacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacetime "Spacetime") in [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity"), then with the use of the [wave function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_function "Wave function") in [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"), and finally with the recognition of [indeterminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminism "Indeterminism") in the study of unstable systems ([chaos theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_theory "Chaos theory")).

## Ilya Prigogine Prize for Thermodynamics

The Ilya Prigogine Prize for Thermodynamics was initialized in 2001 and patronized by Ilya Prigogine himself until his death in 2003. It is awarded on a biennial basis during the Joint European Thermodynamics Conference (JETC) and considers all branches of thermodynamics (applied, theoretical, and experimental as well as quantum thermodynamics and classical thermodynamics).
