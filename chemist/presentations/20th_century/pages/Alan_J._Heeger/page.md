**Alan Jay Heeger** (born January 22, 1936) is an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"), [academic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_staff "Academic staff") and [Nobel Prize laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_laureate "Nobel Prize laureate") [in chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry").

Heeger was elected as a member into the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering "National Academy of Engineering") in 2002 for co-founding the field of conducting polymers and for pioneering work in making these novel materials available for technological applications.

## Life and career

Heeger was born in [Sioux City, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux_City,_Iowa "Sioux City, Iowa"), into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") family. He grew up in [Akron, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akron,_Iowa "Akron, Iowa"), where his father owned a general store. At age nine, following his father's death, the family moved back to Sioux City.

Heeger earned a B.S. in physics and mathematics from the [University of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska–Lincoln "University of Nebraska–Lincoln") in 1957, and a [Ph.D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in physics from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1961. From 1962 to 1982 he was on the faculty of the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania"). In 1982 he commenced his present appointment as a professor in the [Physics Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Santa_Barbara_Physics_Department "UC Santa Barbara Physics Department") and the Materials Department at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara "University of California, Santa Barbara"). His research has led to the formation of numerous start-up companies including Uniax, [Konarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konarka "Konarka"), and Sirigen, founded in 2003 by [Guillermo C. Bazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_C._Bazan "Guillermo C. Bazan"), Patrick J. Dietzen, Brent S. Gaylord. Alan Heeger was a founder of Uniax, which was acquired by [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont").

He won the [Nobel Prize for Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Chemistry "Nobel Prize for Chemistry") in 2000 along with [Alan G. MacDiarmid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_G._MacDiarmid "Alan G. MacDiarmid") and [Hideki Shirakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Shirakawa "Hideki Shirakawa") "for their discovery and development of [conductive polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductive_polymers "Conductive polymers")"; They published their results on [polyacetylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyacetylene "Polyacetylene") a [conductive polymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductive_polymer "Conductive polymer") in 1977. This led to the construction of the [Su–Schrieffer–Heeger model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su–Schrieffer–Heeger_model "Su–Schrieffer–Heeger model"), a simple model for [topological insulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_insulator "Topological insulator").

He had won the [Oliver E. Buckley Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_E._Buckley_Condensed_Matter_Prize "Oliver E. Buckley Condensed Matter Prize") of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") in 1983 and, in 1995, the [Balzan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balzan_Prize "Balzan Prize") for Science of Non-Biological Materials.

His sons are the [neuroscientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscientist "Neuroscientist") [David Heeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Heeger "David Heeger") and the immunologist Peter Heeger.

In October 2010, Heeger participated in the [USA Science and Engineering Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Science_and_Engineering_Festival "USA Science and Engineering Festival")'s Lunch with a Laureate program where middle and high school students engage in an informal conversation with a Nobel Prize-winning scientist over a brown-bag lunch. Heeger is also a member of the [USA Science and Engineering Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Science_and_Engineering_Festival "USA Science and Engineering Festival")'s Advisory Board. Heeger has been a judge of the [*STAGE*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientists,_Technologists_and_Artists_Generating_Exploration "Scientists, Technologists and Artists Generating Exploration") International Script Competition three times (2006, 2007, 2010).

"Perhaps the greatest pleasure of being a scientist is to have an abstract idea, then to do an experiment (more often a series of experiments is required) that demonstrates the idea was correct; that is, Nature actually behaves as conceived in the mind of the scientist. This process is the essence of creativity in science. I have been fortunate to have experienced this intense pleasure many times in my life."
Alan J Heeger, [Never Lose Your Nerve!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Lose_Your_Nerve!?action=edit&redlink=1 "Never Lose Your Nerve!")
