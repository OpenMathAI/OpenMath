**Jerome Karle** (born **Jerome Karfunkle**; June 18, 1918 – June 6, 2013) was an American [physical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemist "Physical chemist"). Jointly with [Herbert A. Hauptman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_A._Hauptman "Herbert A. Hauptman"), he was awarded the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") in 1985, for the direct analysis of [crystal structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_structure "Crystal structure") using [X-ray scattering techniques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_scattering_techniques "X-ray scattering techniques").

## Early life and education

Karle was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), on June 18, 1918, the son of Sadie Helen (Kun) and Louis Karfunkle. He was born into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family with a strong interest in the arts. He had played piano as a youth and had participated in a number of competitions, but he was far more interested in science. He attended [Abraham Lincoln High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln_High_School_(Brooklyn) "Abraham Lincoln High School (Brooklyn)") in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"), and would later join [Arthur Kornberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Kornberg "Arthur Kornberg") (awarded the Nobel in Medicine in 1959) and [Paul Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Berg "Paul Berg") (a winner in Chemistry in 1980), as graduates of the school to win Nobel Prizes. As a youth, Karle enjoyed [handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_handball "American handball"), [ice skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_skating "Ice skating"), [touch football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch_football_(American) "Touch football (American)") and swimming in the nearby [Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean").

He started college at the age of 15 and received his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") from the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York "City College of New York") in 1937, where he took additional courses in biology, chemistry and math in addition to the required curriculum there. He earned a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree "Master's degree") from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") in 1938, having majored in biology.

As part of a plan to accumulate enough money to pay for further graduate studies, Karle took a position in [Albany, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany,_New_York "Albany, New York") with the [New York State Department of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Department_of_Health "New York State Department of Health"), where he developed a method to measure dissolved [fluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluoride "Fluoride") levels, a technique that would become a standard for [water fluoridation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_fluoridation "Water fluoridation").

He enrolled at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan") in 1940 and met his future wife, [Isabella Lugoski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_Karle "Isabella Karle"), who was sitting at an adjoining desk during his first course in physical chemistry. The two married in 1942. They were both supervised in their PhD studies by physical chemist [Lawrence Brockway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Brockway "Lawrence Brockway"). Though Karle completed his studies in 1943, he was awarded his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") the following year.

Jerome Karle was a former president of both the [American Crystallographic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Crystallographic_Association "American Crystallographic Association") (ACA) (1972) and the [IUCr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCr "IUCr") (1981-1984), as well as a co-recipient of the 1985 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his work on [direct methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_methods_(crystallography) "Direct methods (crystallography)"). Among the many additional honors he received for his work, he was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1976, the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") in 1986, and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1990.

## Research and Nobel Prize

Starting in 1943, after completing graduate studies, Karle worked on the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project") at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") with his wife Dr. [Isabella Karle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_Karle "Isabella Karle"), one of the youngest scientists and few women on the project. In 1944, they returned to the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan"), where Karle worked on a project for the [United States Naval Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Research_Laboratory "United States Naval Research Laboratory"). In 1946, they moved to [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") to work for the Naval Research Laboratory.

Karle and Herbert A. Hauptman were awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1985 for their work in using X-ray scattering techniques to determine the structure of crystals, a technique that is used to study the biological, chemical, metallurgical and physical characteristics. They were able to employ the [Sayre equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayre_equation "Sayre equation") in centrosymmetric structure, developing the so-called [direct methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_methods_(crystallography) "Direct methods (crystallography)"). Through isolating the position of the atoms in a crystal, the molecular structure of the material being studied can be determined, allowing processes to be designed to duplicate the molecules being studied. This technique has played a major role in the development of new pharmaceutical products and other synthesized materials.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Karle_Retirement.jpg/250px-Karle_Retirement.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Karle_Retirement.jpg)

Jerome (left foreground) and [Isabella Karle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_Karle "Isabella Karle") (seated center) at their 2009 retirement ceremony

Karle and his wife retired from the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory on July 31, 2009, after a combined 127 years of service to the United States Government, with Karle joining the NRL in 1944 and his wife two years later. At the time of his departure from government service, Karle held the chair of science as chief scientist of the Laboratory for the Structure of Matter. Retirement ceremonies for the Karles were attended by [United States Secretary of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Navy "United States Secretary of the Navy") [Ray Mabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Mabus "Ray Mabus"), who presented the couple with the Department of the Navy Distinguished Civilian Service Award, the Navy's highest form of recognition to civilian employees.

## Personal

Karle was married to [Isabella Helen Lugoski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_Karle "Isabella Karle") (1921-2017) with whom he had three daughters, all of whom work in scientific fields:

- Louise Karle (born 1946) is a theoretical chemist
- Jean Karle (born 1950) is an organic chemist
- Madeleine Karle (born 1955) is a museum specialist with expertise in the field of geology.

## Death

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Grave_of_Jerome_Karle_%281918-2013%29_and_Isabella_Lugoski_Karle_%281921-2017%29.jpg/250px-Grave_of_Jerome_Karle_%281918-2013%29_and_Isabella_Lugoski_Karle_%281921-2017%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Grave_of_Jerome_Karle_(1918-2013)_and_Isabella_Lugoski_Karle_(1921-2017).jpg)

Grave of Karle and his wife at [Columbia Gardens Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Gardens_Cemetery "Columbia Gardens Cemetery")

Karle died of [liver cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_cancer "Liver cancer") on June 6, 2013, at the Leewood Healthcare Center in [Annandale, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annandale,_Virginia "Annandale, Virginia"). Karle is interred at the [Columbia Gardens Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Gardens_Cemetery "Columbia Gardens Cemetery") in [Arlington, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Virginia "Arlington, Virginia").
