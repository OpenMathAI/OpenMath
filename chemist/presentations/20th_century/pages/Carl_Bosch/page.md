**Carl Bosch** (German pronunciation: [[kaʁl ˈbɔʃ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Carl_Bosch.ogg "File:De-Carl Bosch.ogg"); 27 August 1874 – 26 April 1940) was a German [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") and [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer "Engineer") and [Nobel Laureate in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"). He was a pioneer in the field of [high-pressure industrial chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-pressure_chemistry "High-pressure chemistry") and founder of [IG Farben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IG_Farben "IG Farben"), at one point the world's largest chemical company.

He also developed the [Haber–Bosch process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haber_process "Haber process"), important for the large-scale synthesis of fertilizers and explosives. It is estimated that one-third of annual global food production uses ammonia from the Haber–Bosch process, and that this supports nearly half of the world's population. In addition, he co-developed the so-called [Bosch-Meiser process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosch-Meiser_process "Bosch-Meiser process") for the industrial production of [urea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urea "Urea").

## Biography

### Early years

Carl Bosch was born in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne "Cologne") to a successful gas and plumbing supplier. His father was Carl Friedrich Alexander Bosch (1843–1904) and his uncle was [Robert Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bosch "Robert Bosch"), who pioneered the development of the [spark plug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spark_plug "Spark plug") and founded the multinational company [Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bosch_GmbH "Robert Bosch GmbH"). Carl, trying to decide between a career in metallurgy or chemistry, studied at the *Königlich [Technische Hochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule "Technische Hochschule") in [Charlottenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottenburg "Charlottenburg")* (now [Technische Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Berlin "Technische Universität Berlin")) and the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig "University of Leipzig") from 1892 to 1898.

### Career

Farben AG*, Carl Bosch and [Carl Duisberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Duisberg "Carl Duisberg") (in front sitting), [Edmund ter Meer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_ter_Meer "Edmund ter Meer") (third person from right with newspaper)

Carl Bosch attended the University of Leipzig, and this is where he studied under [Johannes Wislicenus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Wislicenus "Johannes Wislicenus"), and he obtained his doctorate in 1898 for research in organic chemistry. After he left in 1899 he took an entry-level job at [BASF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASF "BASF"), then Germany's largest chemical and dye firm. From 1909 until 1913 he transformed [Fritz Haber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Haber "Fritz Haber")'s tabletop demonstration of a method to fix nitrogen using [high-pressure chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-pressure_chemistry "High-pressure chemistry") through the [Haber–Bosch process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haber–Bosch_process "Haber–Bosch process") to produce synthetic nitrate, a process that has countless industrial applications for making a near-infinite variety of industrial compounds, consumer goods, and commercial products. His primary contribution was to expand the scale of the process, enabling the industrial production of vast quantities of synthetic nitrate. To do this, he had to construct a plant and equipment that would function effectively under high gas pressures and high temperatures. Bosch was also responsible for finding a more practical catalyst than the scarce [osmium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmium "Osmium") and expensive [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium "Uranium") being used by Haber.

There were many more obstacles as well, such as designing large compressors and safe high-pressure furnaces. A means was needed to provide pure hydrogen gas in quantity as the [feedstock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedstock "Feedstock"). Also, cheap and safe means had to be developed to clean and process the product [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia "Ammonia"). The first full-scale Haber–Bosch plant was erected in Oppau, Germany, now part of [Ludwigshafen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwigshafen "Ludwigshafen"). With the process complete he was able to synthesize large amounts of ammonia, which was available for the industrial and agricultural fields. In fact, this production has increased the agricultural yields throughout the world. This work won him the Nobel prize for Chemistry in 1931.

After [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") Bosch extended high-pressure techniques to the production of [synthetic fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_fuel "Synthetic fuel") via the [Bergius process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergius_process "Bergius process") and [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol "Methanol"). In 1925 Bosch helped found [IG Farben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IG_Farben "IG Farben"), and was the first head of the company.

He received the [Siemens-Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens-Ring "Siemens-Ring") in 1924 for his contributions to applied research and his support of basic research. In 1931 he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") together with [Friedrich Bergius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Bergius "Friedrich Bergius") for the introduction of high pressure chemistry. Today the Haber–Bosch process produces 100 million tons of nitrogen fertilizer every year.

In mid-1932, Bosch instructed two IG Farben executives to meet [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") in an attempt to stop the Nazi Party's political attacks on the company's synthetic fuel project. In December 1933, the Nazi government granted IG Farben a price and purchase guarantee to expand the production of synthetic oil at [Leuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuna_works "Leuna works"), in a context of increasing state oversight of the company's operations.

Advocating open international cooperation and limited state control over science and industry, Bosch opposed the Nazi regime's autarkic and repressive policies from 1933 onward, which led to his gradual marginalization within IG Farben. From 1935, Bosch was chairman of the board of directors, a role that has become largely ceremonial as he was increasingly sidelined from operational decision-making under the [Nazi regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism").

Bosch married Else Schilbach in 1902. Carl and Else had a son and a daughter together. A critic of many Nazi policies, including [anti-Semitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Semitism "Anti-Semitism"), Bosch was gradually relieved of his high positions, and fell into depression and alcoholism. He died in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg").

## Legacy

The Haber–Bosch Process today consumes more than one percent of humanity's energy production and is responsible for feeding roughly one-third of its population. On average, one-half of the nitrogen in a human body comes from synthetically fixed sources, the product of a Haber–Bosch plant. Bosch was an ardent collector of insects, minerals, and gems. His collected meteorites and other mineral samples were loaned to [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"), and eventually purchased by the [Smithsonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution "Smithsonian Institution"). He was an amateur astronomer with a well-equipped private observatory. The [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid "Asteroid") [7414 Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7414_Bosch "7414 Bosch") was named in his honour.

Carl Bosch along with Fritz Haber were voted the world's most influential chemical engineers of all time by members of the Institution of Chemical Engineers.

The [Haber–Bosch process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haber–Bosch_process "Haber–Bosch process"), which captures nitrogen from the air and converts it to ammonia, heavily impacted the [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution "Green Revolution") that has been feeding the increasing population of the world.

Bosch also won numerous awards including an honorary doctorate from [Technische Hochschule Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Institute_of_Technology "Karlsruhe Institute of Technology") (1918), the Liebig Memorial Medal of the Association of German Chemists along with the Bunsen Medal of the German Bunsen Society, the Siemens Ring, and the Golden Grashof Memorial medal of the VDI. In 1931 he was awarded the Nobel Prize for Chemistry for the contribution to the invention of [chemical high pressure methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-pressure_chemistry "High-pressure chemistry"). He also received the Exner medal from the Austrian Trade Association and the Carl Lueg Memorial Medal. Bosch also enjoyed his membership of various German and foreign scientific academics, and his chairmanship of the Kaiser Wilhelm Society of which he became the President in 1937.
