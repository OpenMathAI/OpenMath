**Friedrich Karl Rudolf Bergius** (German pronunciation: [[ˈfʁiːdʁɪç ˈbɛʁɡi̯ʊs]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Friedrich_Bergius.ogg "File:De-Friedrich Bergius.ogg"), 11 October 1884 – 30 March 1949) was a German [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") known for the [Bergius process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergius_process "Bergius process") for producing [synthetic fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_fuel "Synthetic fuel") from [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal "Coal"), [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in Chemistry (1931, together with [Carl Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Bosch "Carl Bosch")) in recognition of contributions to the invention and development of chemical [high-pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-pressure "High-pressure") methods. Having worked with [IG Farben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IG_Farben "IG Farben") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), his citizenship came into question following the war, causing him to ultimately flee to [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina "Argentina"), where he acted as adviser to the Ministry of Industry.

## Early life

Bergius was born near [Breslau (Wrocław)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław "Wrocław"), then in the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire").

## Academic career

Before studying chemistry, Bergius was sent to work for 6 months at the Friedrich Wilhelms [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel "Steel") works in [Mülheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mülheim_an_der_Ruhr "Mülheim an der Ruhr"). His studies started at the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau "University of Breslau") in 1903 and ended with a PhD in chemistry at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig "University of Leipzig") in 1907, after only 4 years. His thesis on [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid "Sulfuric acid") as solvent was supervised by [Arthur Rudolf Hantzsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rudolf_Hantzsch "Arthur Rudolf Hantzsch"). In 1909 Bergius worked for one semester with [Fritz Haber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Haber "Fritz Haber") and [Carl Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Bosch "Carl Bosch") at the [University of Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Karlsruhe "University of Karlsruhe") in the development of the [Haber-Bosch Process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haber-Bosch_Process "Haber-Bosch Process"). On the same year he was invited to work at the [Leibniz University Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_University_Hannover "Leibniz University Hannover") with [Max Bodenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Bodenstein "Max Bodenstein"), who developed the idea of [chemical kinetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_kinetics "Chemical kinetics") and held a position as professor.

## Work

### Synthetic fuel from coal

During his habilitation, techniques for the high-pressure and high-temperature chemistry of carbon-containing substrates were developed, yielding a patent on the [Bergius process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergius_process "Bergius process") in 1913. In this process [liquid hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_hydrocarbon "Liquid hydrocarbon") used as [synthetic fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_fuel "Synthetic fuel") are produced by [hydrogenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogenation "Hydrogenation") of [lignite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite "Lignite") (brown coal). He developed the process well before the commonly known [Fischer–Tropsch process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer–Tropsch_process "Fischer–Tropsch process"). [Theodor Goldschmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Goldschmidt "Theodor Goldschmidt") invited him to build an industrial plant at his factory the [Th. Goldschmidt AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th._Goldschmidt_AG?action=edit&redlink=1 "Th. Goldschmidt AG") in 1914. The production began only in 1919, after the [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") ended, when the need for fuel was already declining. The technical problems, [inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation "Inflation") and the constant criticism of [Franz Joseph Emil Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_Emil_Fischer "Franz Joseph Emil Fischer"), which changed to support after a personal demonstration of the process, made the progress slow and Bergius sold his patent to [BASF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASF "BASF"), where [Carl Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Bosch "Carl Bosch") worked on it. Before [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") several plants were built with an annual capacity of 4 million tons of synthetic fuel.

### Sugar from wood

The hydrolysis of [wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood "Wood") to produce [sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar "Sugar") for industrial use became a hard task for Bergius. After he moved to Heidelberg he started to improve the process and planned an industrial scale production. The high costs and technical problems nearly led him to bankruptcy. A [bailiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailiff "Bailiff") followed Bergius to [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm") to get the money from his [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1931.

The [autarky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autarky "Autarky") movement before the [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") boosted the process and several plants were built. Bergius moved to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin") where he was only marginally involved in the development. While he was in [Bad Gastein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Gastein "Bad Gastein") [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria "Austria"), his laboratory and his house were destroyed by an [air raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airstrike "Airstrike"). The rest of the war he stayed in Austria.

### International engagement

After the war his citizenship was called into question because of his collaboration with [IG Farben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IG_Farben "IG Farben"), resulting in his departure from Germany to work as an adviser in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy"), [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland") and [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain"). He emigrated to [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina "Argentina"), where he worked as an adviser to the Ministry of Industry. He died in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires") on 30 March 1949, and is buried in the Cementerio Alemán next to [La Chacarita Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Chacarita_Cemetery "La Chacarita Cemetery").

## Awards

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Friedrich_Bergius_with_wife_1931.jpg/330px-Friedrich_Bergius_with_wife_1931.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Friedrich_Bergius_with_wife_1931.jpg)

Bergius with wife in Stockholm in 1931

He and [Carl Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Bosch "Carl Bosch") won the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1931 in recognition of their contributions to the invention and development of chemical high-pressure methods. In 1937, he was awarded the [Wilhelm Exner Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Exner_Medal "Wilhelm Exner Medal").

## Personal life

Bergius was the father of [Renate Burgess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Burgess "Renate Burgess").
