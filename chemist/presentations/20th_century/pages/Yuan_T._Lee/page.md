**Yuan Tseh Lee** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters "Traditional Chinese characters"): 李遠哲; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): *Lǐ Yuǎnzhé*; [Wade–Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade–Giles "Wade–Giles"): *Li³ Yüan³-che²*; [Pe̍h-ōe-jī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe̍h-ōe-jī "Pe̍h-ōe-jī"): *Lí Oán-tiat*; born 19 November 1936) is a Taiwanese [physical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemist "Physical chemist") who was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1986 for his contributions to the development of [reaction dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_dynamics "Reaction dynamics").

Lee is a professor emeritus at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), and honorary director of the [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University") Institute for Advanced Study along with [Ryoji Noyori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryoji_Noyori "Ryoji Noyori"). He was awarded the Nobel with [John C. Polanyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Polanyi "John C. Polanyi") and [Dudley R. Herschbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_R._Herschbach "Dudley R. Herschbach") for "contributions to the dynamics of chemical elementary processes". He was the first Taiwanese person be awarded the Nobel Prize. His research in [physical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry") concerned the use of advanced [chemical kinetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_kinetics "Chemical kinetics") techniques to investigate and manipulate the behavior of chemical reactions using [crossed molecular beams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossed_molecular_beam "Crossed molecular beam"). From 1994 to 2006, Lee served as the President of the [Academia Sinica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Sinica "Academia Sinica"). In 2011, he was elected head of the [International Council for Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Council_for_Science "International Council for Science").

## Early life and education

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Yuan_Tseh_Lee_HD2008_Othmer_Gold_Medal_portrait.JPG/250px-Yuan_Tseh_Lee_HD2008_Othmer_Gold_Medal_portrait.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Yuan_Tseh_Lee_HD2008_Othmer_Gold_Medal_portrait.JPG)

Yuan Tseh Lee, Othmer Gold Medal recipient, 2008

Lee was born in northern Taiwan in [Shinchiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinchiku_Prefecture "Shinchiku Prefecture") (now [Hsinchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsinchu "Hsinchu")) on November 19, 1936. His father, [Lee Tze-fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Tze-fan "Lee Tze-fan") (李澤藩), was an artist, and his mother, Ts'ai P'ei (蔡配; *Cài Péi*), was an elementary school teacher from Goseikō Town (梧棲港街) in [Taichū Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taichū_Prefecture "Taichū Prefecture") (now [Wuqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuqi_District "Wuqi District"), [Taichung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taichung "Taichung")). Their [ancestral home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_home_(Chinese) "Ancestral home (Chinese)") is in [Nan'an, Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nan'an,_Fujian "Nan'an, Fujian").

Lee played on the [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball "Baseball") and [ping-pong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping-pong "Ping-pong") teams of Hsinchu Elementary School, and later studied at the [Hsinchu Senior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsinchu_Senior_High_School "Hsinchu Senior High School"), where he played [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis"), [trombone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trombone "Trombone"), and the [flute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flute "Flute"). He was exempted from the [entrance examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrance_examination "Entrance examination") and directly admitted to [National Taiwan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Taiwan_University "National Taiwan University"). He earned a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in 1959. He earned his [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") from [National Tsing Hua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Tsing_Hua_University "National Tsing Hua University") in 1961 and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), in 1965 under the supervision of [Bruce H. Mahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_H._Mahan "Bruce H. Mahan"). He was a member of the Chemistry International Board from 1977 to 1984.

## Scientific career

### Chemistry

In February 1967, he started working with [Dudley Herschbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Herschbach "Dudley Herschbach") at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") on reactions between [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen "Hydrogen") atoms and diatomic [alkali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkali "Alkali") molecules and the construction of a universal crossed molecular beams apparatus. After the postdoctoral year with Herschbach he joined the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") faculty in 1968. In 1974, he returned to Berkeley as professor of chemistry and principal investigator at the [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Laboratory "Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory"), becoming a U.S. citizen the same year. Lee is a University Professor Emeritus of the [University of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California "University of California") system.

### Nobel prize

One of the major goals of [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") is the study of material transformations where [chemical kinetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_kinetics "Chemical kinetics") plays an important role. Scientists during the 19th century stated macroscopic chemical processes consist of many elementary chemical reactions that are themselves simply a series of encounters between [atomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom "Atom") or [molecular species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule"). In order to understand the time dependence of [chemical reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reaction "Chemical reaction"), [chemical kineticists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry") have traditionally focused on sorting out all of the elementary chemical reactions involved in a macroscopic chemical process and determining their respective rates.

[Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_people "Swedish people") chemist [Svante Arrhenius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svante_Arrhenius "Svante Arrhenius") studied this phenomenon during the late 1880s, and stated the relations between reactive molecular encounters and [rates of reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_of_reaction "Rate of reaction") (formulated in terms of [activation energies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activation_energy "Activation energy")).

Other scientists at the time also stated a chemical reaction is fundamentally a [mechanical event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_event?action=edit&redlink=1 "Mechanical event"), involving the rearrangement of atoms and molecules
during a [collision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collision "Collision"). Although these initial theoretical studies were only qualitative, they heralded a new era in the field of chemical kinetics; allowing the prediction of the dynamical course of a chemical reaction.

In the 1950s, 1960s and 1970s, with the development of many sophisticated experimental techniques, it became possible to study the dynamics of elementary chemical reactions in the laboratory. Such as the analysis of the threshold operating conditions of a chemical [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser "Laser") or the [spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum "Spectrum") obtained using various linear or non-linear laser spectroscopic techniques.

Professor Lee's research focused on the possibility to control the energies of the reagents, and to understand the dependence of chemical reactivity on molecular orientation, among other studies related to the nature of reaction intermediates, [decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_decay "Nuclear decay") dynamics, and identifying complex [reaction mechanisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_mechanism "Reaction mechanism"). To do so, Professor Lee used a breakthrough [laboratory technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_technique "Laboratory technique") at the time, called the *"crossed molecular beams technique"*, where the information derived from the measurements of angular and velocity distributions allowed him and his team to understand the dynamics of elementary chemical reactions.

In 2021, Lee donated his Nobel Prize medal to the [National Museum of Taiwan History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Taiwan_History "National Museum of Taiwan History") for exhibition.

## Later career

During his tenure, Lee has worked to create new research institutes, advance scientific research within Taiwan, and to recruit and cultivate top scholars for the [Academia Sinica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Sinica "Academia Sinica").

In 2010, Lee said that global warming would be much more serious than scientists previously thought, and that Taiwanese people needed to cut their per-capita carbon emissions from the current 12 tons per year to just three. This would take more than a few slogans, turning off the lights for one hour, or cutting meat consumption, noting: "We will have to learn to live the simple lives of our ancestors." Without such efforts, he said, "Taiwanese will be unable to survive long into the future".

Lee was appointed the president of [Academia Sinica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Sinica "Academia Sinica") in 1994 and [renounced his U.S. citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renunciation_of_United_States_citizenship "Renunciation of United States citizenship") to take the post. As president of Academia Sinica he presided over the creation of the Taiwanese history textbook *[Knowing Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowing_Taiwan "Knowing Taiwan")*.

At the request of president [Chen Shui-bian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Shui-bian "Chen Shui-bian"), Lee was Taiwan's [representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_Taipei_representatives_to_APEC "List of Chinese Taipei representatives to APEC") in the 2002 [APEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APEC "APEC") leaders' summit in [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico "Mexico"). ([Presidents of the Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Republic_of_China "President of the Republic of China") have been barred from joining the APEC summits because of objections from the People's Republic of China.) Lee represented Chen again in the 2003 and 2004 APEC summits in [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand") and [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile "Chile"), respectively.

In July 2024, Lee accepted president [Lai Ching-te](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lai_Ching-te "Lai Ching-te")'s invitation to serve as a consultant on the newly formed National Climate Change Strategy Committee.

Lee was then elected President of the [International Council for Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Council_for_Science "International Council for Science") in 2008 and started his term in 2011. He has been involved with the Malta Conferences, an initiative designed to bring together Middle Eastern scientists. As part of the initiative, he offered six fellowships to work on the synchrotron in Taiwan. He is also a member of International Advisory Council in [Universiti Tunku Abdul Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universiti_Tunku_Abdul_Rahman "Universiti Tunku Abdul Rahman").

## Personal life

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/APEC2004_Chamanto.jpg/330px-APEC2004_Chamanto.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:APEC2004_Chamanto.jpg)

Lee (upper far left) represented Taiwan at the 2004 [APEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APEC "APEC") leaders' summit.

Lee's father was a painter in Taiwan, and his mother was an elementary school teacher. His elder brother [Yuan-Chuan Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan-Chuan_Lee "Yuan-Chuan Lee") has been a professor at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University") for 40 years and was awarded the honor Special Chair Lectureship in Academia Sinica in Taiwan. His younger brother [Yuan-Pern Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan-Pern_Lee "Yuan-Pern Lee") was also awarded this honor. Lee's sister Chi-Mei Lee has served as a professor at [National Chung Hsing University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Chung_Hsing_University "National Chung Hsing University").

In 2003, he was one of 22 Nobel Laureates who signed the [Humanist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism_and_Its_Aspirations "Humanism and Its Aspirations").

### Politics

During the [2000 Taiwanese presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Taiwanese_presidential_election "2000 Taiwanese presidential election"), Lee supported the [pan-green coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-green_coalition "Pan-green coalition") which advocates [Taiwan independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_independence "Taiwan independence"). In the last week of the election he announced his support for the candidacy of [Chen Shui-bian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Shui-bian "Chen Shui-bian") who subsequently defeated [James Soong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Soong "James Soong") in the election. Chen intended to nominate Lee to become [premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_the_Republic_of_China "Premier of the Republic of China").

In January 2004, he and industrial tycoon [Wang Yung-ching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Yung-ching "Wang Yung-ching") and theatre director [Lin Hwai-min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin_Hwai-min "Lin Hwai-min") issued a joint statement to both Chen Shui-bian and [Lien Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lien_Chan "Lien Chan"). He backed Chen again in the [2004 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Taiwanese_presidential_election "2004 Taiwanese presidential election"), issuing a statement of support for the DPP on 17 March, three days before polls opened.

During the [2012 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Taiwanese_presidential_election "2012 Taiwanese presidential election"), Lee expressed his support for DPP candidate [Tsai Ing-wen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsai_Ing-wen "Tsai Ing-wen"). In early 2016, he appeared and addressed a rally by [New Power Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Power_Party "New Power Party"), a party formed by student activists involved in the [Sunflower Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower_Movement "Sunflower Movement").

### Climate change

Yuan Lee has signed the 2015 [Mainau Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainau_Declaration "Mainau Declaration") expressing concern about [anthropogenic climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming "Global warming").

### Wu Chien-Shiung Foundation

Lee was one of the four Nobelists who established the [Wu Chien-Shiung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Chien-Shiung "Wu Chien-Shiung") Foundation.

## Recognition

In addition to the Nobel Prize, his awards and distinctions include [Sloan Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellow "Sloan Fellow") (1969); Fellow of [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") (1975); Fellow Am. Phys. Soc. (1976); [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow "Guggenheim Fellow") (1977); Member [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") (1979); Member International Academy of Science, Munich, Member [Academia Sinica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Sinica "Academia Sinica") (1980); E.O. Lawrence Award (1981); Miller Professor, Berkeley (1981); Fairchild Distinguished Scholar (1983); Harrison Howe Award (1983); Peter Debye Award (1986); [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") (1986); Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") (1987) and [Faraday Lectureship Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraday_Lectureship_Prize "Faraday Lectureship Prize") (1992). Yuan Tseh Lee was awarded the [Othmer Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Othmer_Gold_Medal "Othmer Gold Medal") in 2008 in recognition of his outstanding contributions to progress in chemistry and science. His post-doctoral supervisor and fellow Nobel Laureate [Dudley Herschbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Herschbach "Dudley Herschbach") congratulated Lee. In 2019 Yuan T. Lee was also awarded the Fray International Sustainability Award by FLOGEN Star Outreach at SIPS 2019.
