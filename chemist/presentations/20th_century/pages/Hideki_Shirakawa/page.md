**Hideki Shirakawa** (白川 英樹, *Shirakawa Hideki*; born August 20, 1936) is a Japanese [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist"), [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer "Engineer"), and [Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus "Professor Emeritus") at the [University of Tsukuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tsukuba "University of Tsukuba") and [Zhejiang University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang_University "Zhejiang University"). He is best known for his discovery of [conductive polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductive_polymers "Conductive polymers"). He was co-recipient of the 2000 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") jointly with [Alan MacDiarmid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_MacDiarmid "Alan MacDiarmid") and [Alan Heeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Heeger "Alan Heeger").

## Early life and education

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/62/Hideki_Shirakawa_and_Yoshiro_Mori_20001018.jpg/250px-Hideki_Shirakawa_and_Yoshiro_Mori_20001018.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hideki_Shirakawa_and_Yoshiro_Mori_20001018.jpg)

Shirakawa with [Yoshirō Mori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshirō_Mori "Yoshirō Mori") (at the [Prime Minister's Official Residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister's_Official_Residence "Prime Minister's Official Residence") on October 18, 2000)

Hideki Shirakawa was born in [Tokyo, Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo,_Japan "Tokyo, Japan"), the second son of a [military doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_medicine "Military medicine"). He had one elder and one younger brother and sister. [Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics "2000 Summer Olympics") [marathoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon "Marathon") champion [Naoko Takahashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naoko_Takahashi "Naoko Takahashi") is his second cousin-niece. He lived in [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo "Manchukuo") and [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") during childhood. Around third grade, he moved to [Takayama, Gifu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takayama,_Gifu "Takayama, Gifu"), which is the hometown of his mother.

Shirakawa graduated from [Tokyo Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Institute_of_Technology "Tokyo Institute of Technology") (Tokyo Tech) with a bachelor's degree in chemical engineering in 1961, and his doctorate in 1966. Afterward, he obtained the post of assistant in Chemical Resources Laboratory at Tokyo Tech.

## Career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3b/Emperor_Akihito_Yoshiro_Mori_and_Hideki_Shirakawa_20001103.jpg/250px-Emperor_Akihito_Yoshiro_Mori_and_Hideki_Shirakawa_20001103.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Emperor_Akihito_Yoshiro_Mori_and_Hideki_Shirakawa_20001103.jpg)

[Emperor Akihito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Akihito "Emperor Akihito") conferred the [Order of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Culture "Order of Culture") on Shirakawa (at the [Imperial Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Imperial_Palace "Tokyo Imperial Palace") on November 3, 2000)

While employed as an assistant at [Tokyo Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Institute_of_Technology "Tokyo Institute of Technology") (Tokyo Tech) in Japan, Shirakawa developed [polyacetylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyacetylene "Polyacetylene"), which has a metallic appearance. This result interested [Alan MacDiarmid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_MacDiarmid "Alan MacDiarmid") when MacDiarmid visited Tokyo Tech in 1975.

In 1976, he was invited to work in the laboratory of [Alan MacDiarmid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_MacDiarmid "Alan MacDiarmid") as a [post-doctoral fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-doctoral_fellow "Post-doctoral fellow") at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania"). The two developed the [electrical conductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_conductivity "Electrical conductivity") of polyacetylene along with American physicist [Alan Heeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Heeger "Alan Heeger").

In 1977 they discovered that doping with [iodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine "Iodine") vapor could enhance the conductivity of polyacetylene. The three scientists were awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2000 in recognition of the discovery. With regard to the mechanism of electric conduction, it is strongly believed that nonlinear [excitations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excited_state "Excited state") in the form of [solitons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitons "Solitons") play a role.

In 1979, Shirakawa became an assistant professor in the [University of Tsukuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tsukuba "University of Tsukuba"); three years later, he advanced to a full professor. In 1991 he was appointed as Tsukuba's Chief of Science and Engineering Department of Graduate School (until March, 1993), and as Tsukuba's Chief of Category #3 group (until March, 1997).

## Research

Source:

Shirakawa's research on [conductive polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductive_polymers "Conductive polymers") can be broken down into four main categories: [polyacetylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyacetylene "Polyacetylene") thin film synthesis, the causation of metallic conductivity due to [chemical doping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doping_(semiconductor) "Doping (semiconductor)"), the creation of conjugated (double or triple bonds in a molecule which are separated by a single bond) liquid crystalline polymers, and acetylene polymerization development that used liquid crystals as solvents.

1. **Polyacetylene Synthesis**: Polyacetylene was expected to have certain properties, with insolubility making the substance difficult to work with. Dr. Shirakawa found that polyacetylene thin films can be synthesized, and with the thin films, the doctor clarified the molecular and solidified structures of polyacetylene.
2. **Creation of Metallic Conductivity**: Dr. Shirakawa found that, when a trace of a halogen such as bromine or iodine is added to thin film polyacetylene, its electric conductivity increases, and it exhibits metallic conductivity. Shirakawa found that partial electron transfer between dopants and p-electrons of polyacetylene can generate metallic conductivity.
3. **Using Liquid Crystals to Develop Acetylene Polymerization**: Dr. Shirakawa developed a method for the production of highly conductive polyacetylene thin films which paralleled the polymerization of acetylene. Furthermore, he succeeded in the synthesis of thin films of helical polyacetylene whose chirality is controllable.
   1. 'Chirality: a property of asymmetry, meaning a molecule is distinguishable from its [mirror image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image "Mirror image"); that is, it cannot be superimposed onto it
4. **Creation of Conjugated Liquid Crystalline Polymers**: Dr. Shirakawa created self-oriented, conjugated liquid crystalline polymers by introducing liquid crystalline groups into the side chains of p-conjugated polymers such as polyacetylene. He also macroscopically oriented the polymers with electric or magnetic fields and succeeded in having the molecules electric anisotropy.

The general definition of electrical anisotropy describes the variation of an electrical property depending on the lateral or vertical direction (x,y,z) in which a current flows.

### The Nobel Prize

Shirakawa was awarded the 2000 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") together with [UPenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania")'s physics professor [Alan J. Heeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_J._Heeger "Alan J. Heeger") and chemistry professor [Alan G. MacDiarmid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_MacDiarmid "Alan MacDiarmid"), "for the discovery and development of conductive polymers". He also became the first [Japanese Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Japanese_Nobel_laureates "List of Japanese Nobel laureates") who did not graduate from one of the [National Seven Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Seven_Universities "National Seven Universities") and the second Japanese [chemistry Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Chemistry "List of Nobel laureates in Chemistry").

Over the years, Shirakawa has expressed that he does not want the [Nobel Prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prizes "Nobel Prizes") to receive too much special treatment from mass media (especially the [Japanese media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_Japan "Media of Japan")). He hopes that many vital areas in fields outside the Nobel Prize categories will also become more widely known.

## Relatives

One of his relatives, [Hitomi Yoshizawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitomi_Yoshizawa "Hitomi Yoshizawa"), is a member of the singing group [Morning Musume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Musume "Morning Musume") *Morning Girls*. He is also related to [Naoko Takahashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naoko_Takahashi "Naoko Takahashi"), the women's marathon gold medalist of the [2000 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics "2000 Summer Olympics").

## Public issues

On 6 December 2013, the [House of Councillors (Japan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Councillors_(Japan) "House of Councillors (Japan)") approved the bill of the [State Secrecy Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Secrecy_Law "State Secrecy Law"). Shirakawa and physics Nobel laureate [Toshihide Maskawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshihide_Maskawa "Toshihide Maskawa") issued a statement saying that the law:

> "threatens the pacifist principles and fundamental human rights established by the constitution and should be rejected immediately*...(omitted)...*Even in difficult times, protecting the freedom of the press, of thought and expression and of academic research is indispensable."
