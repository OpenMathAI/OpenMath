**Johann Friedrich Wilhelm Adolf von Baeyer** (German: [[ˈaːdɔlf fɔn ˈbaɪɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Adolf_von_Baeyer.ogg "File:De-Adolf von Baeyer.ogg"); 31 October 1835, [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin") – 20 August 1917, [Starnberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starnberg "Starnberg")) was a German chemist who synthesized [indigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigo_dye "Indigo dye") and developed a [nomenclature for cyclic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Baeyer_nomenclature "Von Baeyer nomenclature") (that was subsequently extended and adopted as part of the [IUPAC organic nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUPAC_nomenclature_of_organic_chemistry "IUPAC nomenclature of organic chemistry")). He was [ennobled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobility "Nobility") in the [Kingdom of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria "Kingdom of Bavaria") in 1885 and was the 1905 recipient of the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry").

## Family and education

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Baeyer.jpg/250px-Baeyer.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Baeyer.jpg)

Father [Johann Jacob Baeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Jacob_Baeyer "Johann Jacob Baeyer"), Prussian lieutenant-general, the noted [geodesist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesist "Geodesist")

Baeyer was born in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin") as the son of the noted [geodesist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesist "Geodesist") and captain of the Royal Prussian Army [Johann Jacob Baeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Jacob_Baeyer "Johann Jacob Baeyer") and his wife Eugenie Baeyer née Hitzig (1807–1843). Both his parents were [Lutherans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutherans "Lutherans") at the time of his birth and he was raised in the Lutheran religion. His mother was the daughter of [Julius Eduard Hitzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Eduard_Hitzig "Julius Eduard Hitzig") and a member of the originally [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") [Itzig family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itzig_family "Itzig family"), and had converted to Christianity before marrying his father, who was of non-Jewish German descent. Baeyer had four sisters: Clara (born 1826) Emma (born 1831), Johanna (Jeanette) (born 1839), Adelaide (died 1843) and two brothers: Georg (born 1829) and Edward (born 1832). Baeyer lost his mother at a young age while she was giving birth to his sister Adelaide.

Although his birth name was Johann Friedrich Wilhelm Adolf Baeyer, he was known simply as Adolf throughout most of his life. The poet [Adelbert von Chamisso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelbert_von_Chamisso "Adelbert von Chamisso") and the astronomer [Friedrich Wilhelm Bessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Bessel "Friedrich Bessel") were his godparents. On his 50th birthday he was raised to the hereditary nobility by King [Ludwig II of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_II_of_Bavaria "Ludwig II of Bavaria"), conferring on him the "von" distinction.

Baeyer became interested in science early, performing experiments on plant nutrition at his paternal grandfather's Müggelsheim farm as a boy. In Berlin, he began chemical experimentation at the age of nine. Three years later, he synthesized a previously unknown chemical compound -double carbonate of copper and sodium. On his 13th birthday, he initiated his lifework, buying a chunk of indigo worth two [Thalers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Thaler "Prussian Thaler") for his first dye experiments.

When a schoolboy, his chemistry teacher at the [Friedrich Wilhelm Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_Gymnasium "Friedrich Wilhelm Gymnasium") appointed him as his assistant. After graduating from secondary school in 1853, he entered the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin "Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin") to study [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") and mathematics. A stint in the Prussian army interrupted his study until 1856, when he returned to academia at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University "Heidelberg University"), intending to study [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") under [Robert Bunsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bunsen "Robert Bunsen"). After an argument with the renowned chemist he changed his mentor to [August Kekulé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Kekulé "August Kekulé"). He continued to collaborate with Kekulé even after he returned to Berlin in 1858 for the completion of his doctorate on arsenic methyl chloride, or [cacodylic chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacodylic_acid "Cacodylic acid").

## Academic career and achievements

After completing his doctorate, he followed Kekulé to the [University of Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghent_University "Ghent University"), when Kekulé became professor there. He became a lecturer at the [Gewerbeinstitut Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gewerbeinstitut_Berlin?action=edit&redlink=1 "Gewerbeinstitut Berlin") (Royal Trade Academy) in 1860 and a professor at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg "University of Strasbourg") in 1871. In 1875, he succeeded [Justus von Liebig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justus_von_Liebig "Justus von Liebig") as Chemistry Professor at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München").

Baeyer's chief achievements include the [synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_synthesis "Chemical synthesis") and description of the plant [dye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dye "Dye") [indigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigo_dye "Indigo dye"), the discovery of the [phthalein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phthalein "Phthalein") dyes, and the investigation of [polyacetylenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyacetylene "Polyacetylene"), [oxonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxonium_ion "Oxonium ion") [salts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_(chemistry) "Salt (chemistry)"), [nitroso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitroso "Nitroso") compounds (1869) and [uric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uric_acid "Uric acid") derivatives (1860 and onwards) (including the discovery of [barbituric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbituric_acid "Barbituric acid") (1864), the parent compound of the [barbiturates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbiturate "Barbiturate")). He was the first to propose the correct formula for [indole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indole "Indole") in 1869, after publishing the first synthesis three years earlier. His contributions to [theoretical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_chemistry "Theoretical chemistry") include the 'strain' (*Spannung*) theory of [triple bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_bond "Triple bond") and [strain theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baeyer_strain_theory "Baeyer strain theory") in small [carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon "Carbon") rings.

In 1871, he discovered the synthesis of [phenolphthalein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenolphthalein "Phenolphthalein") by [condensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensation_reaction "Condensation reaction") of [phthalic anhydride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phthalic_anhydride "Phthalic anhydride") with two equivalents of [phenol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenol "Phenol") under [acidic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid "Acid") conditions (hence the name). That same year he was the first to obtain synthetic [fluorescein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescein "Fluorescein"), a [fluorophore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorophore "Fluorophore") [pigment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigment "Pigment") which is similar to naturally occurring [pyoverdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyoverdin "Pyoverdin") that is synthesized by microorganisms (e.g., by some [fluorescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescence "Fluorescence") strains of *[Pseudomonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudomonas "Pseudomonas")*). Baeyer named his finding "resorcinphthalein" as he had synthesized it from [phthalic anhydride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phthalic_anhydride "Phthalic anhydride") and [resorcinol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resorcinol "Resorcinol"). The term [fluorescein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescein "Fluorescein") would not start to be used until 1878.

In 1872, he experimented with [phenol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenol "Phenol") and [formaldehyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaldehyde "Formaldehyde"); the resinous product was a precursor for [Leo Baekeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Baekeland "Leo Baekeland")'s later commercialization of [Bakelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakelite "Bakelite").

In 1881, the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London "Royal Society of London") awarded Baeyer the [Davy Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davy_Medal "Davy Medal") for his work with indigo. He was elected a Foreign Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1884. In 1905 he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") "in recognition of his services in the advancement of organic chemistry and the chemical industry, through his work on organic dyes and hydroaromatic compounds", and he continued in full active work as one of the best-known teachers in the world of organic chemistry up to within a year of his death.

## Honors

- 1881: [Davy Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davy_Medal "Davy Medal") of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") in London
- 1884: [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") fellow.
- 1884: [Prussian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences "Prussian Academy of Sciences")
- 1884: International Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences")
- 1885: foreign member of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry").
- 1885: survey in the hereditary nobility of the Kingdom of Bavaria
- 1895: admission into the [Order Pour le Mérite for Sciences and Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pour_le_Mérite "Pour le Mérite")
- 1892: elected to honorary membership of the [Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_and_Philosophical_Society "Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society")
- 1989: International Member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences")
- 1903: [Liebig Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liebig_Medal "Liebig Medal"), awarded by the German Chemical Society
- 1905: Nobel Prize in Chemistry
- 1910: International Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society")

The [Adolf von Baeyer Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf-von-Baeyer-Denkmünze?action=edit&redlink=1 "Adolf-von-Baeyer-Denkmünze") has been awarded annually since 1911.

His name is reflected in various "name reactions" as the [Baeyer–Villiger oxidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baeyer–Villiger_oxidation "Baeyer–Villiger oxidation") and [Baeyer's reagent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baeyer's_reagent "Baeyer's reagent"). There is also the [Von Baeyer nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Baeyer_nomenclature "Von Baeyer nomenclature") in structural chemistry and Baeyer [strain theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_strain "Ring strain") (which granted him the Nobel Prize) of [alicyclic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicyclic_compound "Alicyclic compound").

In 2009 [von Baeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Baeyer_(crater) "Von Baeyer (crater)") [lunar crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_craters "Lunar craters") was named after him.

## Personal life

In 1868, Baeyer married Adelheid (Lida) Bendemann, the daughter of a family friend, and together the couple had three children: Eugenie, Hans, and [Otto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Baeyer?action=edit&redlink=1 "Otto von Baeyer").

He died on 20 August 1917 in [Starnberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starnberg "Starnberg") at the age of 81.
