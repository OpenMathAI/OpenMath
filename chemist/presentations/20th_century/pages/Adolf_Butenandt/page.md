**Adolf Friedrich Johann Butenandt** (German pronunciation: [[ˈaːdɔlf ˈbuːtənant]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Adolf_Butenandt.ogg "File:De-Adolf Butenandt.ogg"); 24 March 1903, Lehe near [Bremerhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerhaven "Bremerhaven") – 18 January 1995, [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich")) was a German [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). He was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1939 for his "work on [sex hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_hormones "Sex hormones")." He initially rejected the award in accordance with government policy, but accepted it in 1949 after [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). He was President of the [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society "Max Planck Society") from 1960 to 1972. He was also the first, in 1959, to discover the structure of the sex pheromone of silkworms, which he named [bombykol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombykol "Bombykol").

## Education and early life

Born in Lehe, near [Bremerhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerhaven "Bremerhaven"), he started his studies at [Marburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg_University "Marburg University"). For his PhD, he joined the working group of the Nobel laureate [Adolf Windaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Windaus "Adolf Windaus") at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen") and he finished his studies with a PhD in chemistry in 1927. His doctoral research was on the chemistry of the insecticidal toxin found in the roots of *[Derris elliptica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derris_elliptica "Derris elliptica")* which he isolated and characterized. After his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation "Habilitation") he became lecturer in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen "Göttingen") 1931.

## Professional career

He became a professor ordinarius at the [Technical University of Danzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Danzig "Technical University of Danzig") from 1933 until 1936. In 1933, Butenandt signed the *[Vow of allegiance of the Professors of the German Universities and High-Schools to Adolf Hitler and the National Socialistic State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vow_of_allegiance_of_the_Professors_of_the_German_Universities_and_High-Schools_to_Adolf_Hitler_and_the_National_Socialistic_State "Vow of allegiance of the Professors of the German Universities and High-Schools to Adolf Hitler and the National Socialistic State")*. In 1936, he applied for the directorship of the [Kaiser Wilhelm Institut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Wilhelm_Institute_for_Biochemistry "Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Biochemistry") (later the [Max Planck Institute for Biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Biochemistry "Max Planck Institute for Biochemistry")) in Berlin-Dahlem while also joining the [NSDAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSDAP "NSDAP") on 1 May 1936 (party member No. 3716562). The earlier director of the Kaiser Wilhelm institute was [Carl Neuberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Neuberg "Carl Neuberg"), who had been removed for being a Jew. His work on [rotenones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotenone "Rotenone") was considered useful by the Nazi leadership as it could be useful for controlling lice among soldiers in the trenches. As the head of a leading institute, he applied for government funding on concentrated research labeled *kriegswichtig* (important for the war), some of which focused on military projects like the improvement of oxygen uptake for high-altitude bomber pilots.

Estrone

Adolf Windaus and Walter Schöller of [Schering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schering_AG "Schering AG") gave him the advice to work on hormones extracted from [ovaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovaries "Ovaries"). This research lead to the discovery of [estrone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrone "Estrone") and other primary female [sex hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_hormone "Sex hormone"), which were extracted from several thousand liters of [urine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urine "Urine"). While working as professor in [Danzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danzig "Danzig") at the [*Chemisches Institut*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_of_Chemistry,_Gdańsk_University_of_Technology "Faculty of Chemistry, Gdańsk University of Technology") he was continuing his works over hormones extracting [progesterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progesterone "Progesterone") in 1934 and [testosterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testosterone "Testosterone") a year later, the research results were along with the synthesis of steroids by [Leopold Ružička](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Ružička "Leopold Ružička") considered significant enough to be awarded later by Nobel Committee in 1939. In 1940 he was involved in research on a hormone treatment to make long submarine voyages more comfortable for submariners in the Kriegsmarine.

Butenand's involvement with the Nazi regime and various themes of research led to criticism after the war, and even after his death the exact nature of his political orientation during the Nazi era has never been fully resolved. When the institute moved to [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tübingen "Tübingen") in 1945 he became a professor at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen "University of Tübingen"). In 1948, he was considered for the chair for physiological medicine at the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel "University of Basel"). He entered in negotiations but eventually was convinced by the chemical industry to stay in Germany. In 1956, when the institute relocated to [Martinsried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinsried "Martinsried"), a suburb of [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich"), Butenandt became a professor at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München"). He also served as president of the [Max Planck Society for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society "Max Planck Society") following [Otto Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn "Otto Hahn") from 1960 to 1972.

Butenandt is credited with the discovery and naming of the [silkworm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silkworm "Silkworm") moth [pheromone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheromone "Pheromone") [Bombykol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombykol "Bombykol") in 1959.

Butenandt died in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich") in 1995, at the age of 91. His wife [Erika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erika_Butenandt?action=edit&redlink=1 "Erika Butenandt"), born in 1906, died in 1995 at 88. They had seven children.

## Honours and awards

- 1939: [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") (shared with [Leopold Ruzicka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Ruzicka "Leopold Ruzicka")) for the identification of the sex hormones, [oestrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oestrogen "Oestrogen"), [progesterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progesterone "Progesterone") and [androsterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Androsterone "Androsterone")
- 1942: [War Merit Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Merit_Cross "War Merit Cross"), Second Class (Germany)
- 1943: War Merit Cross, First Class (Germany)
- 1953: [Paul Ehrlich and Ludwig Darmstaedter Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrlich_and_Ludwig_Darmstaedter_Prize "Paul Ehrlich and Ludwig Darmstaedter Prize")
- 1959/1964: Knight Commander's Cross and Grand Cross of the [Order of Merit of the Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit_of_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany "Order of Merit of the Federal Republic of Germany")
- 1960: Honorary Citizen of the City of [Bremerhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerhaven "Bremerhaven")
- 1960: President of the [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society "Max Planck Society")
- 1961: Wilhelm Normann Medal of the German Society for Fat Research
- 1962: [Bavarian Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Order_of_Merit "Bavarian Order of Merit")
- 1962: [Pour le Mérite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pour_le_Mérite "Pour le Mérite")
- 1964: [Austrian Decoration for Science and Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Decoration_for_Science_and_Art "Austrian Decoration for Science and Art")
- 1967: [Cultural Honor Prize of the City of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Honor_Prize_of_the_City_of_Munich "Cultural Honor Prize of the City of Munich")
- 1969: Commander of the French [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour "Legion of Honour")
- 1972: [Ordre des Palmes Académiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_des_Palmes_Académiques "Ordre des Palmes Académiques")
- 1981: [Bavarian Maximilian Order for Science and Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Maximilian_Order_for_Science_and_Art "Bavarian Maximilian Order for Science and Art")

### Honorary doctorates

Butenandt received 14 honorary doctorates, including the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen "University of Tübingen") (1949), the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München") (1950), the [University of Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Graz "University of Graz") (1957), the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds "University of Leeds") (1961), [Aristotle University of Thessaloniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle_University_of_Thessaloniki "Aristotle University of Thessaloniki") (1961), [Complutense University of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complutense_University_of_Madrid "Complutense University of Madrid") (1963), the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna "University of Vienna") (1965), [Saint Louis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Louis_University "Saint Louis University") (1965), the [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin") (1966), the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") (1966) and [Gdańsk University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gdańsk_University_of_Technology "Gdańsk University of Technology") (1994).
