**Fritz Pregl** ([Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_language "Slovene language"): *Friderik Pregl*; 3 September 1869 – 13 December 1930), was a Slovenian-Austrian [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") and [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician "Physician") from a mixed [Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_Language "Slovene Language")-[German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Language "German Language")-speaking background. He won the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1923 for making important contributions to [quantitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analysis_(chemistry) "Quantitative analysis (chemistry)") [organic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry") [microanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microanalysis "Microanalysis"), one of which was the improvement of the [combustion train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combustion_train "Combustion train") technique for [elemental analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elemental_analysis "Elemental analysis").

## Biography

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Ljubljana%2C_birth_house_of_Friderik_Pregl.jpg/250px-Ljubljana%2C_birth_house_of_Friderik_Pregl.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ljubljana,_birth_house_of_Friderik_Pregl.jpg)

Pregl's birthplace on *Gosposka ulica* / *Herrengasse* ("Lords' Street"), Ljubljana

Pregl was born in [Ljubljana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana "Ljubljana") within [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary "Austria-Hungary") to a Slovene-speaking father and German-speaking mother. He was baptized *Friedrich Michael Raimund Pregl*. He died in [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz "Graz"), Austria in 1930.

Pregl started his career as [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") after he studied medicine at the [University of Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Graz "University of Graz"). With his focus on [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology") and especially chemical physiology, he suffered from the limitations of [quantitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analysis_(chemistry) "Quantitative analysis (chemistry)") [organic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry") [microanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microanalysis "Microanalysis"). The small quantities of substances he obtained during the research of [bile acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bile_acid "Bile acid") made it necessary to improve the process of [elemental analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elemental_analysis "Elemental analysis") by reducing the necessary components. At the end of his research, he had lowered the minimal amount of substance necessary for the analysis process by a factor of 50. He invited chemists to learn his method of elemental analysis, so that the method was soon widely accepted.

## Commemoration

In 1950, the department of the [University of Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Graz "University of Graz") where Fritz Pregl had worked was named the [Institute of Medical Chemistry and Pregl Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Medical_Chemistry_and_Pregl_Laboratory?action=edit&redlink=1 "Institute of Medical Chemistry and Pregl Laboratory"). Streets in Graz, Innsbruck, Vienna and Klagenfurt were named after him. In Slovenia, [Pregl Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregl_Award?action=edit&redlink=1 "Pregl Award") have been bestowed annually since 2007 by the [National Institute of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Chemistry_(Slovenia) "National Institute of Chemistry (Slovenia)") for the research work and for outstanding [doctorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate "Doctorate"). Slovenian pupils are conferred Pregl Recognition Awards, whereas secondary school students are conferred Pregl Citations for excellent results in national competitions in chemistry. A square in Ljubljana is named after Pregl. The [Fritz Pregl Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Pregl_Prize "Fritz Pregl Prize") has been awarded annually since 1931 in chemistry by the [Austrian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Academy_of_Sciences "Austrian Academy of Sciences") from the funds left at its disposal by Pregl.
