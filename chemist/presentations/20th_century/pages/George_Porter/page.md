**George Porter, Baron Porter of Luddenham**, [OM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_Merit "Member of the Order of Merit"), [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society"), [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh "Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh") (6 December 1920 – 31 August 2002) was a British [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist"). He was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1967.

## Education and early life

Porter was born in [Stainforth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stainforth,_South_Yorkshire "Stainforth, South Yorkshire"), near [Thorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorne,_South_Yorkshire "Thorne, South Yorkshire"), in the then [West Riding of Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Riding_of_Yorkshire "West Riding of Yorkshire"). He was educated at [Thorne Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorne_Grammar_School "Thorne Grammar School"), then won a scholarship to the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds "University of Leeds") and gained his [first degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"). During his degree, Porter was taught by [Meredith Gwynne Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meredith_Gwynne_Evans "Meredith Gwynne Evans"), who he said was the most brilliant chemist he had ever met. He was awarded a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") in 1949 for research investigating [free radicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_radical "Free radical") produced by [photochemical means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photochemistry "Photochemistry"). He later became a Fellow of [Emmanuel College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_College,_Cambridge "Emmanuel College, Cambridge").

## Career and research

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") Porter served in the [Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_Reserve "Royal Naval Reserve"). He then went on to do research supervised by [Ronald George Wreyford Norrish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_George_Wreyford_Norrish "Ronald George Wreyford Norrish") at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), where he began the work that ultimately led to them becoming [Nobel laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize").

His original research in developing the technique of [flash photolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_photolysis "Flash photolysis") to obtain information on short-lived molecular species provided the first evidence of free [radicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_(chemistry) "Radical (chemistry)"). His later research utilised the technique to study the detailed aspects of the [light-dependent reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-dependent_reaction "Light-dependent reaction") of [photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis "Photosynthesis"), with particular regard to possible applications to a [hydrogen economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_economy "Hydrogen economy"), of which he was a strong advocate.

From 1953 to 1954 he served as Assistant Director of the [British Rayon Research Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rayon_Research_Association "British Rayon Research Association"), where he studied the [phototendering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phototendering "Phototendering") of dyed cellulose fabrics in sunlight.

Porter served as professor in the Chemistry department at the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield "University of Sheffield") from 1954 to 1965. Here he started his work on flash photolysis with equipment designed and made in the departmental workshop. During this tenure he took part in a television programme describing his work in the "Eye on Research" series. Porter became [Fullerian Professor of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullerian_Professor_of_Chemistry "Fullerian Professor of Chemistry") at the [Royal Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institution "Royal Institution") in London and Director of the Royal Institution in 1966. During his directorship of the Royal Institution, Porter was instrumental in the setting up of Applied Photophysics, a company formed to supply instrumentation based on his group's work. He was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1967 along with [Manfred Eigen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_Eigen "Manfred Eigen") and [Ronald George Wreyford Norrish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_George_Wreyford_Norrish "Ronald George Wreyford Norrish"). In the same year he became a visiting professor at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London").

Porter was a major contributor to the  [public understanding of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_awareness_of_science "Public awareness of science"), and championed the cause of "not-yet-applied science".
He became president of the [British Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Association "British Association") in 1985 and was the founding Chair of the [Committee on the Public Understanding of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_on_the_Public_Understanding_of_Science "Committee on the Public Understanding of Science") (COPUS). He gave the [Romanes Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanes_Lecture "Romanes Lecture"), entitled "Science and the human purpose", at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") in 1978; and in 1988 he gave the [Dimbleby Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dimbleby_Lecture "Richard Dimbleby Lecture"), "Knowledge itself is power". From 1990 to 1993 he gave the [Gresham lectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham_College "Gresham College") in astronomy.

### Awards and honours

Porter was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1960 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1960"), a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1979, a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1986, and served as [President of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Royal_Society "List of presidents of the Royal Society") from 1985 to 1990. He was also awarded the [Davy Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davy_Medal "Davy Medal") in 1971, the [Rumford Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumford_Medal "Rumford Medal") in 1978, the [Ellison-Cliffe Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellison-Cliffe_Lecture "Ellison-Cliffe Lecture") in 1991 and the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal "Copley Medal") in 1992.

Porter received an [Honorary Doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree "Honorary degree") from [Heriot-Watt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heriot-Watt_University "Heriot-Watt University") in 1971.

He was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in 1972, appointed to the [Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit "Order of Merit") in 1989, and became a [life peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peer "Life peer") as **Baron Porter of Luddenham**, of Luddenham in the County of [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent "Kent"), in 1990. In 1995, he was awarded an Honorary Degree (Doctor of Laws) from the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath "University of Bath").

In 1976 he gave the [Royal Institution Christmas Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institution_Christmas_Lectures "Royal Institution Christmas Lectures") on *The Natural History of a Sunbeam*.

Porter served as [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_(education) "Chancellor (education)") of the [University of Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leicester "University of Leicester") between 1984 and 1995. In 2001, the university named its chemistry building the [George Porter Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Porter_Building "George Porter Building") in his honour.
