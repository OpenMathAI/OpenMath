**Odd Hassel** (17 May 1897 – 11 May 1981) was a Norwegian [physical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry") and [Nobel Laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Laureate "Nobel Laureate").

## Biography

Hassel was born in [Kristiania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo "Oslo") (now Oslo), Norway. His parents were Ernst Hassel (1848–1905), a [gynaecologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gynaecology "Gynaecology"), and Mathilde Klaveness (1860–1955). In 1915, he entered the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo "University of Oslo") where he studied [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"), [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") and [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"), and graduated in 1920. [Victor Goldschmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Goldschmidt "Victor Goldschmidt") was Hassel's tutor when he began studies in Oslo, while [Heinrich Jacob Goldschmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Jacob_Goldschmidt "Heinrich Jacob Goldschmidt"), Victor's father, was Hassel's thesis advisor. Father and son were important figures in Hassel's life and they remained friends. After taking a year off from studying, he went to [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich"), Germany to work in the laboratory of Professor [Kasimir Fajans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasimir_Fajans "Kasimir Fajans").

His work there led to the detection of [absorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorption_(electromagnetic_radiation) "Absorption (electromagnetic radiation)") indicators. After moving to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin"), he worked at the [Kaiser Wilhelm Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Wilhelm_Institute "Kaiser Wilhelm Institute"), where he began to do research on [X-ray crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography "X-ray crystallography"). He furthered his research with a [Rockefeller Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Fellowship "Rockefeller Fellowship"), obtained with the help of [Fritz Haber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Haber "Fritz Haber"). In 1924, he obtained his PhD from [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin"), before moving to his alma mater, the University of Oslo, where he worked from 1925 through 1964. He became a professor in 1934.

His work was interrupted in October, 1943 when he and other university staff members were arrested by the [Nasjonal Samling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasjonal_Samling "Nasjonal Samling") and handed over to the [occupation authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Norway_by_Nazi_Germany "Occupation of Norway by Nazi Germany"). He spent time in several detention camps, until he was released in November, 1944.

## Work

.

Hassel originally focused on [inorganic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inorganic_chemistry "Inorganic chemistry"), but beginning in 1930 his work concentrated on problems connected with [molecular structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_structure "Molecular structure"), particularly the structure of [cyclohexane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclohexane "Cyclohexane") and its derivatives. He introduced the Norwegian scientific community to the concepts of the [electric dipole moments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_dipole_moment "Electric dipole moment") and [electron diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_diffraction "Electron diffraction"). The work for which he is best known established the three-dimensionality of [molecular geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_geometry "Molecular geometry"). He focused his research on ring-shaped [carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon "Carbon") [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecules "Molecules"), which he suspected filled three dimensions instead of two, the common belief of the time. By using the number of [bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_bond "Chemical bond") between the carbon and hydrogen atoms, Hassel demonstrated the impossibility of the molecules existing on only one plane. This discovery led to him being awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for 1969.

## Honors

Hassel was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1969, shared with English chemist [Derek Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Barton "Derek Barton").

He received the [Guldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_Maximilian_Guldberg "Cato Maximilian Guldberg")-[Waage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Waage "Peter Waage") Medal (*Guldberg-Waage Medal*) from the [Norwegian Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Chemical_Society "Norwegian Chemical Society") and the [Gunnerus Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunnerus_Medal "Gunnerus Medal") from the [Royal Norwegian Society of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Society_of_Science_and_Letters "Royal Norwegian Society of Science and Letters"), both in 1964.

Hassel held honorary degrees from the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen") (1950) and [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University "Stockholm University") (1960). An annual lecture named in his honor is given at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo "University of Oslo").

He was an honorary Fellow of the Norwegian Chemical Society, [Chemical Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Society_of_London "Chemical Society of London"), [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters "Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters"), [Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters "Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters") and [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences").

He was made a Knight of the [Order of St. Olav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St._Olav "Order of St. Olav") in 1960.
