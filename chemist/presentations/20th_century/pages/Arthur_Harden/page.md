**Sir Arthur Harden**, [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (12 October 1865 – 17 June 1940) was a British [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). He shared the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1929 with [Hans Karl August Simon von Euler-Chelpin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Karl_August_Simon_von_Euler-Chelpin "Hans Karl August Simon von Euler-Chelpin") for their investigations into the [fermentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fermentation "Ethanol fermentation") of [sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar "Sugar") and fermentative [enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzymes "Enzymes"). He was a founding member of the Biochemical Society and editor of the *[Biochemical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemical_Journal "Biochemical Journal")* for 25 years.

## Biography

### Early years

Harden was born to Scottish Presbyterian businessman Albert Tyas Harden and Eliza Macalister. His early education was at a private school in Victoria Park run by Dr Ernest Adam. He went to study in 1877 at [Tettenhall College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tettenhall_College "Tettenhall College"), [Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire "Staffordshire"), and entered [Owens College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owens_College "Owens College"), now the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester "University of Manchester"), in 1882, graduating in 1885. He studied chemistry under Professor Roscoe at Owens College and was influenced by J.B. Cohen (author of *The Owens College Course of Practical Organic Chemistry*).

### Research

In 1886 Harden was awarded the Dalton Scholarship in Chemistry and spent a year working with Otto Fischer at [Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlangen "Erlangen") where he worked on the synthesis of β-nitroso-α-naphthylamine and studied its properties. After receiving a Ph.D. he returned to Manchester as a lecturer and demonstrator  and taught along with Sir [Philip Hartog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Hartog "Philip Hartog"). He researched the life and work of [John Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dalton "John Dalton") during these years. In 1895 he wrote a textbook on *Practical Organic Chemistry* together with F.C. Garrett. Harden continued to work at Manchester until 1897 when he was appointed chemist to the newly founded British Institute of Preventive Medicine, which later became the [Lister Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lister_Institute "Lister Institute"). He earned the degree [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science "Doctor of Science") (D.Sc.) from the [Victoria University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_(United_Kingdom) "Victoria University (United Kingdom)") (which included Owens College) in June 1902. Five years later, in 1907 he was appointed Head of the Biochemical Department, a position which he held until his retirement in 1930 (though he continued his scientific work at the Institute after his retirement).

At Manchester, Harden had studied the action of light on mixtures of [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide "Carbon dioxide") and [chlorine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorine "Chlorine"), and when he entered the Institute he applied his methods to the investigation of biological phenomena such as the chemical action of [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterium "Bacterium") and alcoholic fermentation. He studied the breakdown products of [glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose "Glucose") and the chemistry of the [yeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast "Yeast") cell, and produced a series of papers on the [antiscorbutic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiscorbutic "Antiscorbutic") and anti-neuritic [vitamins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin "Vitamin").

Harden was knighted in 1926, and received several honorary doctorates. A Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society"), he received the [Davy Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davy_Medal "Davy Medal") in 1935.

#### "Harden–Young ester"

Harden's work on glycolysis in yeast with [William John Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_John_Young_(biochemist) "William John Young (biochemist)") led to the discovery of a phosphorylated ester that was known as *Harden–Young ester* until chemical analysis showed it to be [fructose 1,6-bisphosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fructose_1,6-bisphosphate "Fructose 1,6-bisphosphate"). It is now known to be the product of phosphorylating [fructose 6-phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fructose_6-phosphate "Fructose 6-phosphate") by the action of [phosphofructokinase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphofructokinase "Phosphofructokinase"); it is broken down into [glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyceraldehyde_3-phosphate "Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate") and [dihydroxyacetone phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihydroxyacetone_phosphate "Dihydroxyacetone phosphate") by the action of [aldolase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldolase "Aldolase").

### Personal life

Harden married Georgina Sydney Bridge (died January 1928) in 1900 and they had no children. He was elected to membership of the [Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_and_Philosophical_Society "Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society") on 14 April 1896
