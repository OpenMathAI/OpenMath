**Sir Derek Harold Richard Barton** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh "Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh") (8 September 1918 – 16 March 1998) was an English [organic chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemist "Organic chemist") and [Nobel Prize laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_laureate "Nobel Prize laureate") for 1969.

## Education and early life

Barton was born in [Gravesend, Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravesend,_Kent "Gravesend, Kent"), to William Thomas and Maude Henrietta Barton (*née* Lukes).

He attended [Gravesend Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravesend_Grammar_School "Gravesend Grammar School") (1926–29), [The King's School, Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King's_School,_Rochester "The King's School, Rochester") (1929–32), [Tonbridge School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonbridge_School "Tonbridge School") (1932–35) and Medway Technical College (1937–39). In 1938 he entered [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London "Imperial College London"), where he graduated in 1940 and obtained his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree in [Organic Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_Chemistry "Organic Chemistry") in 1942.

## Career and research

From 1942 to 1944, Barton was a government research chemist, then from 1944 to 1945 he worked for [Albright and Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albright_and_Wilson "Albright and Wilson") in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham "Birmingham"). He then became Assistant Lecturer in the Department of Chemistry of Imperial College, and from 1946 to 1949 he was [ICI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Chemical_Industries "Imperial Chemical Industries") Research Fellow.

During 1949 and 1950, he was a visiting lecturer in natural products chemistry at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), and was then appointed reader in organic chemistry and, in 1953, professor at [Birkbeck College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkbeck,_University_of_London "Birkbeck, University of London"). In 1955, he became [Regius Professor of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regius_Professor_of_Chemistry_(Glasgow) "Regius Professor of Chemistry (Glasgow)") at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow "University of Glasgow"), and in 1957, he was appointed professor of organic chemistry at [Imperial College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College,_London "Imperial College, London"). In 1950, Barton showed that organic molecules could be assigned a preferred [conformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformational_isomerism "Conformational isomerism") based upon results accumulated by chemical physicists, in particular by [Odd Hassel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_Hassel "Odd Hassel"). Using this new technique of [conformational analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformational_analysis "Conformational analysis"), he later determined the geometry of many other natural product molecules.

In 1969, Barton shared the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with Odd Hassel for “contributions to the development of the concept of conformation and its application in chemistry."

In 1958, Barton was appointed Arthur D. Little Visiting Professor of [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"), and in 1959 Karl Folkers Visiting Professor at the [Universities of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois "University of Illinois") and [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin "University of Wisconsin"). The same year, he was elected a foreign honorary member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences").

In 1949, he was the first recipient of the [Corday-Morgan Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corday-Morgan_Medal "Corday-Morgan Medal") and Prize awarded by the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry"). In 1954 he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") and the International Academy of Science, Munich as well as, in 1956, a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh "Royal Society of Edinburgh"); in 1965 he was appointed member of the [Council for Scientific Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_Scientific_Policy "Council for Scientific Policy"). He was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight "Knight") in 1972, becoming formally [styled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_(manner_of_address) "Style (manner of address)") Sir Derek in Britain. In 1978, he became Director of the [Institut de Chimie des Substances Naturelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_de_Chimie_des_Substances_Naturelles "Institut de Chimie des Substances Naturelles") (ICSN - Gif Sur-Yvette) in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France").

In 1977, on the occasion of the centenary of the Royal Institute of Chemistry, the British Post Office honoured him, and 5 other Nobel Prize-winning British chemists, with a series of four postage stamps featuring aspects of their discoveries.

He moved to the United States in 1986 (specifically [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas")) and became [distinguished professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_professor "Distinguished professor") at [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University "Texas A&M University") and held this position for 12 years until his death.

In 1996, Barton published a comprehensive volume of his works, entitled *Reason and Imagination: Reflections on Research in Organic Chemistry.*

As well as for his work on conformation, his name is remembered in a number of reactions in organic chemistry, such as the [Barton reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_reaction_(disambiguation) "Barton reaction (disambiguation)"), the [Barton decarboxylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_decarboxylation "Barton decarboxylation"), and the [Barton-McCombie deoxygenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton-McCombie_deoxygenation "Barton-McCombie deoxygenation").

The newly built Barton Science Centre at [Tonbridge School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonbridge_School "Tonbridge School") in [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent "Kent"), where he was educated for 4 years, completed in 2019, is named after him.

## Honours and awards

Barton was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1954](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1954 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1954").
In 1966 he was elected a Member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina"). He was elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1970 and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1978.

## Personal life

Sir Derek married three times: Jeanne Kate Wilkins (on 20 December 1944); Christiane Cognet (died 1992) (in 1969); and Judith Von-Leuenberger Cobb (1939-2012) (in 1993). He had a son by his first marriage.
