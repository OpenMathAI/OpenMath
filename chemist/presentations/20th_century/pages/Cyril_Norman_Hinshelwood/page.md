**Sir Cyril Norman Hinshelwood** [OM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_Merit "Member of the Order of Merit") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (19 June 1897 – 9 October 1967) was a British [physical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemist "Physical chemist") and expert in [chemical kinetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_kinetics "Chemical kinetics"). His work in reaction mechanisms earned the 1956 [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry").

## Education

Born in London, his parents were Norman Macmillan Hinshelwood, a chartered accountant, and Ethel Frances née Smith. He was educated first in Canada, returning in 1905 on the death of his father to a flat in [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea,_London "Chelsea, London") where he lived for the rest of his life. He was educated at [Westminster City School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_City_School "Westminster City School") and [Balliol College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford "Balliol College, Oxford").

## Career

During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War"), Hinshelwood was a [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") in an [explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_material "Explosive material") factory. He was a [tutor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutor "Tutor") in [Trinity College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Oxford "Trinity College, Oxford"), from 1921 to 1937 and was [Dr Lee's Professor of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_Lee's_Professorships "Dr Lee's Professorships") in the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") from 1937. He served on several advisory councils on scientific matters to the [British Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Government "British Government").

His early studies of molecular [kinetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_kinetics "Chemical kinetics") led to the publication of *Thermodynamics for Students of Chemistry* and *The Kinetics of Chemical Change* in 1926. With [Harold Warris Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Warris_Thompson "Harold Warris Thompson") he studied the explosive reaction of [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen "Hydrogen") and [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen "Oxygen") and described the phenomenon of [chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_reaction "Chain reaction"). His subsequent work on chemical changes in the bacterial cell proved to be of great importance in later research work on [antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotics "Antibiotics") and therapeutic agents, and his book, *The Chemical Kinetics of the Bacterial Cell* was published in 1946, followed by *Growth, Function and Regulation in Bacterial Cells* in 1966. In 1951 he published *The Structure of Physical Chemistry*. It was republished as an Oxford Classic Texts in the Physical Sciences by Oxford University Press in 2005.

The [Langmuir-Hinshelwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactions_on_surfaces "Reactions on surfaces") process in heterogeneous catalysis, in which the adsorption of the reactants on the surface is the rate-limiting step, is named after him. He was a senior research fellow at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London "Imperial College London") from 1964 to 1967.

## Awards and honours

In addition to being named the second Dr. Lee's Professor of Chemistry at Oxford, Hinshelwood was elected [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1929 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1929"), serving as president from 1955 to 1960. He was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight "Knight") in 1948 and appointed to the [Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit "Order of Merit") in 1960. With [Nikolay Semenov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Nikolayevich_Semyonov "Nikolay Nikolayevich Semyonov") of the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), Hinshelwood was jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1956 for his researches into the mechanism of chemical reactions. He was also an elected member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"), and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society").

Hinshelwood was president of the [Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Society "Chemical Society"), the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society"), the [Classical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Association "Classical Association"), and the [Faraday Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraday_Society "Faraday Society"), and received numerous awards and honorary degrees. He was elected on 1 January 1960 to honorary membership of the [Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_and_Philosophical_Society "Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society") who awarded him its Dalton medal in 1966.

## Personal life

Hinshelwood never married. He was fluent in seven classical and modern languages and his main hobbies were painting, collecting Chinese pottery, and foreign literature. As an artist, Hinshelwood painted scenes in Oxford, as well as portraits of Oxford University people including [Harold Hartley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Hartley_(chemist) "Harold Hartley (chemist)"), his doctoral supervisor, and [Herbert Blakiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Blakiston "Herbert Blakiston"), the President of Trinity College. The portrait of Hartley is now owned by the Royal Society, and that of Blakiston is owned by Trinity College, as are a number of Hinshelwood's other paintings.

He died, at home, on 9 October 1967. In 1968, his Nobel Prize medal was sold by his estate to a collector, who then sold it in 1976 for $15,000. In 2017, the medal was sold at auction for $128,000.
