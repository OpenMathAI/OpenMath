**Hartmut Michel** (German pronunciation: [[ˈhaʁtmuːt ˈmɪçl̩]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Hartmut_Michel.ogg "File:De-Hartmut Michel.ogg"); born 18 July 1948) is a German [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"), who received the 1988 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for determination of the first crystal structure of an integral membrane protein, a membrane-bound complex of proteins and co-factors that is essential to [photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis "Photosynthesis").

He is currently a professor at [Jilin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jilin_University "Jilin University"), China.

## Early life and education

He was born on 18 July 1948 in [Ludwigsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwigsburg "Ludwigsburg"). After compulsory military service, he studied biochemistry at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen "University of Tübingen"), working for his final year at Dieter Oesterhelt's laboratory on [ATPase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATPase "ATPase") activity of [halobacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halobacteria "Halobacteria").

## Career and research

Michel later worked on the crystallisation of [membrane proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_protein "Membrane protein") – essential for their structure elucidation by [X-ray crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography "X-ray crystallography"). He received the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") jointly with [Johann Deisenhofer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Deisenhofer "Johann Deisenhofer") and [Robert Huber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Huber "Robert Huber") in 1988. Together with Michel and Huber, Deisenhofer determined the three-dimensional structure of a protein complex found in certain photosynthetic bacteria. This membrane protein complex, called a [photosynthetic reaction center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthetic_reaction_center "Photosynthetic reaction center"), was known to play a crucial role in initiating a simple type of photosynthesis. Between 1982 and 1985, the three scientists used [X-ray crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography "X-ray crystallography") to determine the exact arrangement of the more than 10,000 atoms that make up the protein complex. Their research increased the general understanding of the mechanisms of photosynthesis, revealed similarities between the photosynthetic processes of plants and bacteria and established a methodology for crystallising membrane proteins.

Since 1987 he has been director of the Molecular Membrane Biology department at
the [Max Planck Institute for Biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Biophysics "Max Planck Institute for Biophysics") in [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt "Frankfurt"), Germany, and professor of biochemistry at the [Goethe University Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt "Goethe University Frankfurt").

In 2026, Michel accepted a position as full-time professor at [Jilin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jilin_University "Jilin University") in [Changchun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changchun "Changchun"), China. His work is expected to include [translational medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translational_medicine "Translational medicine"), drug development and [structural biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_biology "Structural biology").

## Awards and honours

In 1986, he received the [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize "Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize") of the [Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Forschungsgemeinschaft "Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft"), which is the highest honour awarded in German research. In 1988, he received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"). He received the Bijvoet Medal at the [Bijvoet Center for Biomolecular Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijvoet_Center_for_Biomolecular_Research "Bijvoet Center for Biomolecular Research") of [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University "Utrecht University") in 1989. In 1995 he became a member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina"). He also became a foreign member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1995. He was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2005 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 2005").
