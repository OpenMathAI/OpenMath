**Alexander Robertus Todd, Baron Todd** (2 October 1907 – 10 January 1997) was a British [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist") whose research on the structure and [synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosynthesis "Biosynthesis") of [nucleotides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleotide "Nucleotide"), [nucleosides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleoside "Nucleoside"), and nucleotide [coenzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coenzyme "Coenzyme") gained him the [Nobel Prize for Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Chemistry "Nobel Prize for Chemistry") in 1957.

## Early life and education

Todd was born at [Cathcart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathcart "Cathcart") in outer [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow "Glasgow"), the elder son of Alexander Todd, JP, a clerk with the [Glasgow Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Subway "Glasgow Subway"), and his wife, Jane Lowry.

He attended [Allan Glen's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Glen's_School "Allan Glen's School") and graduated from the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow "University of Glasgow") with a bachelor's degree (BSc) in 1928. He received a doctorate (Dr Phil.nat.) from [Goethe University Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt "Goethe University Frankfurt") in 1931 for his thesis on the chemistry of the [bile acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bile_acid "Bile acid").

Todd was awarded an [1851 Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1851_Research_Fellowship "1851 Research Fellowship") from the [Royal Commission for the Exhibition of 1851](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_for_the_Exhibition_of_1851 "Royal Commission for the Exhibition of 1851"), and, after studying at [Oriel College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriel_College,_Oxford "Oriel College, Oxford"), he received another doctorate (DPhil) in 1933.

## Career

Todd held posts with the [Lister Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lister_Institute "Lister Institute"), the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh "University of Edinburgh") (staff, 1934–1936) and the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London "University of London"), where he was appointed [Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_(academic_rank) "Reader (academic rank)") in biochemistry.

In 1938, Alexander Todd spent six months as a visiting professor at [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology"), eventually declining an offer of faculty position.

Todd became the [Sir Samuel Hall Chair of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Samuel_Hall_Chair_of_Chemistry "Sir Samuel Hall Chair of Chemistry") and director of the Chemical Laboratories of the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester "Victoria University of Manchester") in 1938, where he began working on nucleosides, compounds that form the structural units of nucleic acids ([DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") and [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA")).
At 31, he was the youngest professor of chemistry since Frankland. He was elected to membership of the [Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_and_Philosophical_Society "Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society") in 1938.

In 1944, he was appointed to the [1702 Chair of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BP_Professor_of_Organic_Chemistry "BP Professor of Organic Chemistry") in the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), which he held until his retirement in 1971. In 1949, he synthesised [adenosine triphosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenosine_triphosphate "Adenosine triphosphate") (ATP) and [flavin adenine dinucleotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavin_adenine_dinucleotide "Flavin adenine dinucleotide") (FAD). Todd served as a visiting professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") in Autumn 1948 and [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney "University of Sydney") in 1950.

By 1951, Todd and collaborators at Cambridge had determined by biochemical methods how the backbone of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") is structured via the successive linking of carbon atoms 3 and 5 of the sugar to phosphates. This helped corroborate [Francis Crick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Crick "Francis Crick") and [James\_Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Watson "James Watson")'s X-ray structural work published in 1953.

In 1955, he helped elucidate the structure of [vitamin B12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_B12 "Vitamin B12"), although the final formula and definite structure was determined by [Dorothy Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Hodgkin "Dorothy Hodgkin") and her team, and later worked on the structure and synthesis of [vitamin B1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_B1 "Vitamin B1") and [vitamin E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_E "Vitamin E"), the [anthocyanins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthocyanins "Anthocyanins") (the pigments of flowers and fruits) from insects (aphids, beetles) and studied [alkaloids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkaloids "Alkaloids") found in [cannabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_(drug) "Cannabis (drug)"). He served as chairman of the [Government of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_United_Kingdom "Government of the United Kingdom")'s advisory committee on [scientific policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_policy "Science policy") from 1952 to 1964.

He is credited as the first person to synthesize [H4-CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H4-CBD "H4-CBD") and [H2-CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H2-CBD "H2-CBD") from [Cannabidiol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabidiol "Cannabidiol") by [hydrogenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogenation "Hydrogenation") as early as 1940.

He received the 1957 Nobel Prize in Chemistry "for his work on nucleotides and nucleotide co-enzymes."

Elected a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow "Fellow") of [Christ's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_College,_Cambridge "Christ's College, Cambridge") in 1944, he served as [Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_College,_Cambridge#Masters_of_Christ's "Christ's College, Cambridge") from 1963 to 1978. Lord Todd became the first [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_(education) "Chancellor (education)") of the new [University of Strathclyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strathclyde "University of Strathclyde") in 1965, and a visiting professor at [Hatfield Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hertfordshire "University of Hertfordshire") (1978–1986). Among his many honours, including over 40 honorary degrees, he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") in 1942, a member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1955, a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1957, and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1965. [President of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Royal_Society "President of the Royal Society") from 1975 to 1980, The Queen awarded him [the Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_Merit "Member of the Order of Merit") in 1977.

In 1981, Todd became a founding member of the [World Cultural Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cultural_Council "World Cultural Council").

## Personal life and death

In 1937, Todd married Alison Sarah Dale (d. 1987), daughter of Nobel Prize winner [Henry Hallett Dale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hallett_Dale "Henry Hallett Dale"), who like Todd, served as President of the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London "Royal Society of London"). They had a son and two daughters:

- Dr the Hon Alexander Henry Todd (b. 1939), educated at [Oriel College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriel_College,_Oxford "Oriel College, Oxford"), Master [Salters' Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salters'_Company "Salters' Company") (1999/2000), m. 1stly 1967 (div 1981) Joan Margaret Koester, m. 2ndly Patricia Mary Harvey Jones, daughter of Brigadier Alan Harvey Jones [CBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire "Commander of the Order of the British Empire") [TD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Decoration "Territorial Decoration"), of [Somerford Booths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerford_Booths "Somerford Booths"), [Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire "Cheshire");
- The Hon Helen Jean Todd (b. 1941), m. 1963 Philip Edgar Brown, and has two sons and a daughter;
- The Hon Hilary Alison Todd (b. 1946).

Todd died in Cambridge on 10 January 1997 at the age of 89 following a heart attack.

## Honours

Todd was honoured as a Nieuwland Lecturer at the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame "University of Notre Dame") in 1948, an Arthur D. Little Visiting Professor at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") in 1954, and a Hitchcock Lecturer at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), in 1957.

[Knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") as Sir Alexander Todd in 1954 he was elevated as a [Life Peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Peer "Life Peer") on 16 April 1962, being created **Baron Todd** of [Trumpington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpington,_Cambridgeshire "Trumpington, Cambridgeshire") in the [County of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Cambridge "County of Cambridge").

Lord Todd, Master of the [Worshipful Company of Salters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worshipful_Company_of_Salters "Worshipful Company of Salters") (1961/62) and then [Master of Christ's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_masters_of_Christ's_College,_Cambridge "List of masters of Christ's College, Cambridge") (1963–78), is commemorated by a [blue plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_plaque "Blue plaque") erected by the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry") at the [Department of Chemistry in the University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Hamied_Department_of_Chemistry "Yusuf Hamied Department of Chemistry").
