**Archer John Porter Martin** [CBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire "Commander of the Order of the British Empire") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (1 March 1910 – 28 July 2002) was an English chemist who shared the 1952 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for the invention of partition [chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatography "Chromatography") with [Richard Synge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Synge "Richard Synge").

## Early life

Martin's father was a [GP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_practitioner "General practitioner"). Martin was educated at [Bedford School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_School "Bedford School"), and [Peterhouse, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge "Peterhouse, Cambridge").

## Career

Working first in the [Physical Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Chemistry "Physical Chemistry") Laboratory, he moved to the [Dunn Nutritional Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunn_Nutritional_Laboratory "Dunn Nutritional Laboratory"), and in 1938 moved to Wool Industries Research Institution in [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds "Leeds"). He was head of the [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") division of Boots Pure Drug Company from 1946 to 1948, when he joined the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(UK) "Medical Research Council (UK)"). There, he was appointed head of the physical chemistry division of the [National Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Medical_Research "National Institute for Medical Research") in 1952, and was chemical consultant from 1956 to 1959.

He specialised in biochemistry, in some aspects of [vitamins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin "Vitamin") E and B2, and in techniques that laid the foundation for several new types of [chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatography "Chromatography"). He developed [partition chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_chromatography "Partition chromatography") whilst working on the separation of [amino acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid "Amino acid"), and later developed [gas-liquid chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas-liquid_chromatography "Gas-liquid chromatography") with [Anthony T. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_T._James "Anthony T. James"). Amongst many honours, he received his Nobel Prize in 1952.

After his retirement from the University of Sussex, he was visiting professor at both the [University of Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Houston "University of Houston") in Texas and the [EPFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne "École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne") (École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne) in Switzerland.

He published far fewer papers than the typical Nobel winners—only 70 in all—but his ninth paper contained the work that would eventually win him the Nobel Prize. The [University of Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Houston "University of Houston") dropped him from its chemistry faculty in 1979 (when he was 69 years old) because he was not publishing enough.

## Awards

Archer Martin shared the 1952 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for the invention of partition [chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatography "Chromatography") with [Richard Synge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Synge "Richard Synge").

Archer Martin's 1954 paper with [Anthony T. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_T._James "Anthony T. James"), "Gas-Liquid Chromatography: A Technique for the Analysis and Identification of Volatile Materials" reported the discovery of [gas-liquid chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas-liquid_chromatography "Gas-liquid chromatography"). This was performed at the [National Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Medical_Research "National Institute for Medical Research") in [Mill Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_Hill "Mill Hill"), London, which became the [Francis Crick Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Crick_Institute "Francis Crick Institute") in 2016. It was honoured by a Citation for Chemical Breakthrough Award from the Division of History of Chemistry of the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society") presented in 2016 to the Francis Crick Institute.

Martin was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") in 1950, and made a CBE in 1960.

## Personal life

In 1943 he married Judith Bagenal (1918–2006), and together they had two sons and three daughters. In the last years of his life he suffered from [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease "Alzheimer's disease").
