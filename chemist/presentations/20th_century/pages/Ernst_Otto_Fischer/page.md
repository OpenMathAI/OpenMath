**Ernst Otto Fischer** (German pronunciation: [[ˈɛʁnst ˌɔto ˈfɪʃɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Ernst_Otto_Fischer.ogg "File:De-Ernst Otto Fischer.ogg"); 10 November 1918 – 23 July 2007) was a German chemist who won the Nobel Prize for pioneering work in the area of [organometallic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organometallic_chemistry "Organometallic chemistry").

## Early life

He was born in [Solln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solln "Solln"), a borough of [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich"). His parents were Karl T. Fischer, Professor of Physics at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich "Technical University of Munich") (TUM), and Valentine [née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Danzer. He graduated in 1937 with *Abitur*. Before the completion of two years' [compulsory military service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_military_service "Compulsory military service"), the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War") broke out, and he served in Poland, France, and Russia. During a period of study leave, towards the end of 1941 he began to study chemistry at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich "Technical University of Munich"). Following the end of the War, he was released by the Americans in the autumn of 1945 and resumed his studies.

## Training

Fischer graduated from TUM in 1949. He then started his doctoral thesis as an assistant to Professor [Walter Hieber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Hieber "Walter Hieber") in the [Inorganic Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inorganic_chemistry "Inorganic chemistry") Institute, His thesis was entitled "The Mechanisms of Carbon Monoxide Reactions of Nickel(II) Salts in the Presence of Dithionites and Sulfoxylates".

## Research career

After receiving his doctorate in 1952, he remained at TUM. He continued his research on the [organometallic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organometallic_chemistry "Organometallic chemistry") of the [transition metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_metal "Transition metal"). He almost immediately challenged the structure for ferrocene as postulated by Pauson and Keally. Shortly thereafter, he published the structural data of [ferrocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocene "Ferrocene") and the new complexes [nickelocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelocene "Nickelocene") and [cobaltocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobaltocene "Cobaltocene"). Near the same time, he focused also on the then baffling chemistry resulting from Hein's reactions of chromium(III) chloride with [phenylmagnesium bromide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenylmagnesium_bromide "Phenylmagnesium bromide"). This effort resulted in his isolation of [bis(benzene)chromium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bis(benzene)chromium "Bis(benzene)chromium"), foretelling an entirely new class of sandwich complexes.

## Professional advances and recognition

He was appointed a lecturer at the TUM in 1955 and, in 1957, professor and then, in 1959, C4 professor. In 1964, he took the Chair of Inorganic Chemistry at the TUM.

In 1964, he was elected a member of the Mathematics/Natural Science section of the Bavarian Academy of Sciences. In 1969, he was appointed a member of the [German Academy of Natural Scientists, Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Natural_Scientists_Leopoldina "German Academy of Natural Scientists Leopoldina") and in 1972 was given an honorary doctorate by the Faculty of Chemistry and Pharmacy at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich "LMU Munich").

He lectured across the world on metal complexes of [cyclopentadienyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclopentadienyl "Cyclopentadienyl"), indenyl, arenes, olefins, and metal carbonyls. In the 1960s his group discovered a metal alkylidene and alkylidyne complexes, since referred to as [Fischer carbenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer_carbene "Fischer carbene") and Fischer-[carbynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbyne "Carbyne"). Overall he published about 450 journal articles, and he trained many PhD and postdoctoral students, many of whom went on to noteworthy careers. Among his many foreign lectureships, he was Firestone Lecturer at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison") (1969), visiting professor at the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida "University of Florida") (1971), and [Arthur D. Little](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_D._Little "Arthur D. Little") visiting professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (1973).

He has received many awards including, in 1973 with [Geoffrey Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Wilkinson "Geoffrey Wilkinson"), the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his work on [organometallic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organometallic_compound "Organometallic compound").

## Death

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Grabstaette_Ernst_Otto_Fischer.jpg/250px-Grabstaette_Ernst_Otto_Fischer.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Grabstaette_Ernst_Otto_Fischer.jpg)

Tomb of Ernst Otto Fischer

He died on 23 July 2007 in Munich. At the time of his death, Fischer was the oldest living German Nobel laureate. He was succeeded by [Manfred Eigen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_Eigen "Manfred Eigen"), who shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1967 and is nine years younger than Fischer was.
