**Richard Laurence Millington Synge** (28 October 1914 – 18 August 1994) was an English [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"), and shared the 1952 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for the invention of partition [chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatography "Chromatography") with [Archer Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archer_Martin "Archer Martin").

## Life

Richard Laurence Millington Synge was born in [West Kirby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Kirby "West Kirby") on 28 October 1914, the son of Lawrence Millington Synge, a Liverpool stock-broker, and his wife, Katherine C. Swan.

Synge was educated at the Old Hall in [Wellington, Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington,_Shropshire "Wellington, Shropshire") and at [Winchester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_College "Winchester College"). He then studied Chemistry at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge").

He spent his entire career in research, at the [Wool Industries Research Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool_Industries_Research_Association "Wool Industries Research Association"), Leeds (1941–1943), [Lister Institute for Preventive Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lister_Institute_for_Preventive_Medicine "Lister Institute for Preventive Medicine"), London (1943–1948), [Rowett Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowett_Research_Institute "Rowett Research Institute"), Aberdeen (1948–1967), and [Food Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Food_Research "Institute of Food Research"), Norwich (1967–1976).

It was during his time in [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds "Leeds") that he worked with [Archer Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archer_John_Porter_Martin "Archer John Porter Martin"), developing [partition chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_chromatography "Partition chromatography"), a technique used in the separation mixtures of similar chemicals, that revolutionised analytical chemistry. Between 1942 and 1948 he studied [peptides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptide "Peptide") of the protein group [gramicidin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramicidin "Gramicidin"), work later used by [Frederick Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Sanger "Frederick Sanger") in determining the structure of [insulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin "Insulin"). In March 1950 he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") for which his candidature citation read:

> Distinguished as a biochemist. Was the first to show the possibility of using counter-current liquid-liquid extraction in the separation of N-acetylamino acids. In collaboration with A.J.P. Martin this led to the development of partition chromatography, which they have applied with conspicuous success in problems related to the composition and structure of proteins, particularly wool keratin. Synge's recent work on the composition and structure of gramicidins is outstanding and illustrates vividly the great advances in technique for which he and Martin are responsible.

In 1963 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh "Royal Society of Edinburgh"). His proposers were [Magnus Pyke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Pyke "Magnus Pyke"), [Andrew Phillipson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._T._Phillipson "A. T. Phillipson"), Sir [David Cuthbertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Cuthbertson "David Cuthbertson") and [John Andrew Crichton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Andrew_Crichton?action=edit&redlink=1 "John Andrew Crichton").

He was for several years the treasurer of the Chemical Information Group of the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry"), and was an honorary Professor in Biological Sciences at the [University of East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_East_Anglia "University of East Anglia") from 1968 to 1984. He was awarded an honorary Doctor of Science (ScD) from the [University of East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_East_Anglia "University of East Anglia") in 1977, and an honorary doctorate from the Faculty of Mathematics and Science at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University"), Sweden in 1980.

## Personal life

In 1943 Synge married Ann Davies Stephen (1916–1997). Ann Stephen was the daughter of psychologist [Karin Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karin_Stephen "Karin Stephen") and psychoanalyst [Adrian Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Stephen "Adrian Stephen"). Ann's sister Judith (1918–1972) was married to documentary artist and photographer [Nigel Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Henderson_(artist) "Nigel Henderson (artist)").
