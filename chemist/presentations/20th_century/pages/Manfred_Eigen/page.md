**Manfred Eigen** (German pronunciation: [[ˈmanˌfʁeːt ˈaɪ̯ɡn̩]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Manfred_Eigen.ogg "File:De-Manfred Eigen.ogg"); 9 May 1927 – 6 February 2019) was a German [biophysical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysical_chemistry "Biophysical chemistry") who won the 1967 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for work on measuring fast chemical reactions.

Eigen's research helped solve major problems in physical chemistry and aided in the understanding of chemical processes that occur in living organisms.

In later years, he explored the biochemical roots of life and evolution. He worked to install a multidisciplinary program at the [Max Planck Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute "Max Planck Institute") to study the underpinnings of life at the molecular level. His work was hailed for creating a new scientific and technological discipline: evolutionary [biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology "Biotechnology").

## Education and early life

Eigen was born on 9 May 1927 in [Bochum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochum "Bochum"), the son of Ernst Eigen, a chamber musician, and Hedwig née Feld. As a child, he developed a deep passion for music and studied piano.

[World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") interrupted his formal education. At age fifteen, he was drafted into service in a German anti-aircraft unit. He was captured by the Americans toward the end of the war. He managed to escape (he said later that escape was relatively easy), and walked hundreds of miles across defeated Germany, arriving in Göttingen in 1945. He lacked the necessary documentation for acceptance to university, but was admitted after he demonstrated his knowledge in an exam. He entered the university's first postwar class.

Eigen desired to study physics, but since returning soldiers who were enrolled previously received priority, he enrolled in Geophysics. He earned an undergraduate degree and began graduate study in natural sciences. One of his advisors was [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg "Werner Heisenberg"), the noted proponent of the [uncertainty principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncertainty_principle "Uncertainty principle"). He received his doctorate in 1951.

## Career and research

Eigen received his Ph.D. at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen") in 1951 under the supervision of [Arnold Eucken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Eucken "Arnold Eucken"). In 1964, he presented the results of his research at a meeting of the [Faraday Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraday_Society "Faraday Society") in London. His findings demonstrated for the first time that it was possible to determine the rates of chemical reactions that occurred during time intervals as brief as a nanosecond.

Beginning in 1953, Eigen worked at the [Max Planck Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute "Max Planck Institute") for Physical Chemistry in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen "Göttingen"), becoming its director in 1964 and joining it with the Max Planck Institute for Spectroscopy to become the [Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Biophysical_Chemistry "Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry"). He was an honorary professor of the [Braunschweig University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweig_University_of_Technology "Braunschweig University of Technology"). From 1982 to 1993, Eigen was president of the [German National Merit Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_National_Merit_Foundation "German National Merit Foundation"). Eigen was a member of the Board of Sponsors of *The [Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_the_Atomic_Scientists "Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists")*.

In 1967, Eigen was awarded, along with [Ronald George Wreyford Norrish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_George_Wreyford_Norrish "Ronald George Wreyford Norrish") and [George Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Porter "George Porter"), the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"). They were cited for their studies of extremely fast [chemical reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reaction "Chemical reaction") induced in response to very short [pulses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse_(physics) "Pulse (physics)") of [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy "Energy").

In addition, Eigen's name is linked with the theory of [quasispecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasispecies "Quasispecies"), the [error threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_threshold "Error threshold"), [error catastrophe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_catastrophe "Error catastrophe"), [Eigen's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigen's_paradox "Eigen's paradox"), and the chemical [hypercycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercycle_(chemistry) "Hypercycle (chemistry)"), the cyclic linkage of reaction cycles as an explanation for the [self-organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organization "Self-organization") of [prebiotic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_life "Origin of life"), which he described with [Peter Schuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Schuster_(theoretical_chemist) "Peter Schuster (theoretical chemist)") in 1977.

Eigen founded two biotechnology companies, *[Evotec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evotec "Evotec")* and *Direvo*.

In 1981, Eigen became a founding member of the [World Cultural Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cultural_Council "World Cultural Council").

Eigen was a member of the [Pontifical Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Academy_of_Sciences "Pontifical Academy of Sciences") even though he was an atheist. He died on 6 February 2019 at the age of 91.

## Personal life

Eigen was married to Elfriede Müller. The union produced two children, a boy and a girl. He later married [Ruthild Winkler-Oswatitsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthild_Winkler "Ruthild Winkler"), a longtime scientific partner.

## Honours and awards

Eigen won numerous awards for his research, including:

- [Otto Hahn Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn_Prize "Otto Hahn Prize") (1962)
- Elected a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") (1964)
- Elected a member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") (1966)
- [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") (1967), shared with [Ronald George Wreyford Norrish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_George_Wreyford_Norrish "Ronald George Wreyford Norrish") and [George Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Porter "George Porter"), for his studies on the [kinetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_kinetics "Chemical kinetics") of extremely fast running [chemical reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reaction "Chemical reaction") with [relaxation methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperature_jump "Temperature jump")
- Elected a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") (1968)
- Member of the [Soviet Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Academy_of_Sciences "Soviet Academy of Sciences") (now the Russian Academy of Sciences) (1976)
- Elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1973 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1973")
- [Pour le Mérite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pour_le_Mérite "Pour le Mérite") (1973)
- [Faraday Lectureship Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraday_Lectureship_Prize "Faraday Lectureship Prize") from the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry") in 1977
- [Austrian Decoration for Science and Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Decoration_for_Science_and_Art "Austrian Decoration for Science and Art")
- [Lower Saxony State Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Saxony_State_Prize "Lower Saxony State Prize") for Science (1980)
- [Paul Ehrlich and Ludwig Darmstaedter Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrlich_and_Ludwig_Darmstaedter_Prize "Paul Ehrlich and Ludwig Darmstaedter Prize") (1992)
- Helmholtz Medal ([Berlin-Brandenburg Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-Brandenburg_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities "Berlin-Brandenburg Academy of Sciences and Humanities"), 1994)
- Max Planck Research Award (1994), jointly with Rudolf Rigler of the [Karolinska Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karolinska_Institute "Karolinska Institute")
- Honorary member of the [Ruhr University Bochum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_University_Bochum "Ruhr University Bochum") (2001)
- Lifetime Achievement Award from the [Institute of Human Virology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Human_Virology "Institute of Human Virology") in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore") (2005)
