**Sir John Ernest Walker** (born 7 January 1941) is a British [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") who won the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1997. As of 2015 Walker is Emeritus Director and Professor at the [MRC Mitochondrial Biology Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRC_Mitochondrial_Biology_Unit "MRC Mitochondrial Biology Unit") in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge"), and a Fellow of [Sidney Sussex College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Sussex_College,_Cambridge "Sidney Sussex College, Cambridge").

## Early life and education

Walker was born in [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_West_Yorkshire "Halifax, West Yorkshire"), [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire "Yorkshire"), the son of Thomas Ernest Walker, a stonemason, and Elsie Lawton, an amateur musician. He was brought up with his two younger sisters (Judith and Jen) in a rural environment and went to [Rastrick Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastrick_Grammar_School "Rastrick Grammar School"). At school, he was a keen sportsman and specialized in physical sciences and mathematics during his final three years there. He received a 3rd class [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree in chemistry from [St Catherine's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Catherine's_College,_Oxford "St Catherine's College, Oxford"). Walker began his study of [peptide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptide "Peptide") antibiotics with [Edward Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Abraham "Edward Abraham") at Oxford in 1965 and received his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree in 1969. During this period, he became interested in developments in [molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology").

## Career

From 1969 to 1971, Walker worked at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison"), and from 1971 to 1974 in France. He met [Fred Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Sanger "Fred Sanger"), in 1974, at a workshop at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"). This resulted in an invitation to work at the [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRC_Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology") of the Medical Research Council, which became a long-term appointment. Among the other staff was [Francis Crick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Crick "Francis Crick"), who was well known for his discovery of the molecular structure of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA"). At first, he analyzed the sequences of [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") and then uncovered details of the modified genetic code in [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondria "Mitochondria"). In 1978, he decided to apply protein chemical methods to membrane proteins. In this way, Walker characterized the subunit composition of proteins in the mitochondrial membrane and the DNA sequence of the mitochondrial genome.

His landmark crystallographic studies of the F1-ATPase, the catalytic region of the [ATP synthase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_synthase "ATP synthase") (done in collaboration with crystallographer Andrew Leslie), from bovine heart mitochondria revealed the three catalytic sites in three different conformations imposed by the position of the asymmetric central stalk. This structure supported the binding change mechanism and rotary catalysis for the ATP synthase (and related enzymes), one of the catalytic mechanisms proposed by Paul Boyer. This work, published in 1994, led to Walker's share of the 1997 Nobel prize for chemistry. Since this structure, Walker and his colleagues have produced most of the crystal structures in the PDB of mitochondrial ATP synthase, including transition state structures and protein with bound inhibitors and antibiotics. Scientists trained in Walker's group at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology in Cambridge or MRC Mitochondrial Biology Unit have gone on to determine crystal bacterial complex I and cryo-EM maps of mitochondrial complex I and vacuolar type ATPases.

### Teaching and mentoring

Many students and [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") fellows who studied with John Walker have gone on to independent research careers, including
[Leonid Sazanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Sazanov "Leonid Sazanov"), Postdoctoral Fellow (ISTA) and
[Daniela Stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniela_Stock "Daniela Stock"), Postdoctoral Fellow (Sydney).

## Awards and recognition

Walker was elected an [EMBO Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMBO_Member "EMBO Member") in 1984. He shared his Nobel Prize with the American chemist [Paul D. Boyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_D._Boyer "Paul D. Boyer") for their elucidation of the enzymatic mechanism underlying the synthesis of [adenosine triphosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenosine_triphosphate "Adenosine triphosphate"). They also shared the prize with [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark "Denmark") chemist [Jens C. Skou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_C._Skou "Jens C. Skou") for research unrelated to theirs (Discovery of the [Na+/K+-ATPase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na+/K+-ATPase "Na+/K+-ATPase")). Sir John was knighted in 1999 for services to molecular biology. He is a member of the Advisory Council for the [Campaign for Science and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_Science_and_Engineering "Campaign for Science and Engineering"). He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1995 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1995"). Walker is also a Foreign Associate of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") and an Honorary Fellow of St Catherine's College, Oxford. He became a foreign member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1999. He was elected a Fellow of the [Academy of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Medical_Sciences_(United_Kingdom) "Academy of Medical Sciences (United Kingdom)") in 2011 and in 2012 he was awarded the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal "Copley Medal").

## Personal life

Walker married Christina Westcott in 1963, and has two daughters.
