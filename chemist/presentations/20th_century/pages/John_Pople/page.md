**Sir John Anthony Pople** (31 October 1925 – 15 March 2004) was a British [theoretical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_chemistry "Theoretical chemistry") who was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with [Walter Kohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Kohn "Walter Kohn") in 1998 for his development of [computational methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_chemistry "Computational chemistry") in [quantum chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chemistry "Quantum chemistry").

## Early life and education

Pople was born in [Burnham-on-Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnham-on-Sea "Burnham-on-Sea"), Somerset, and attended the [Bristol Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Grammar_School "Bristol Grammar School"). He won a scholarship to [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"), in 1943. He received his Bachelor of Arts degree in 1946. Between 1945 and 1947 he worked at the [Bristol Aeroplane Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Aeroplane_Company "Bristol Aeroplane Company"). He then returned to the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") and was awarded his PhD in [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") in 1951 on [lone pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_pair "Lone pair") [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron").

## Career

After obtaining his PhD, he was a research fellow at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge") and then from 1954 a lecturer in the [mathematics faculty at Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Cambridge "Faculty of Mathematics, University of Cambridge"). In 1958, he moved to the [National Physical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Physical_Laboratory,_UK "National Physical Laboratory, UK"), near London as head of the new basics physics division. He moved to the United States of America in 1964, where he lived the rest of his life, though he retained British citizenship. Pople considered himself more of a mathematician than a chemist, but theoretical chemists consider him one of the most important of their number. In 1964 he moved to [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University "Carnegie Mellon University") in [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh "Pittsburgh"), [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania"), where he had experienced a sabbatical in 1961 to 1962. In 1993 he moved to [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University") in [Evanston, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanston,_Illinois "Evanston, Illinois"), where he was Trustees Professor of Chemistry until his death.

## Research

Pople's major scientific contributions were in four different areas:

### Statistical mechanics of water

Pople's early paper on the [statistical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics") of water, according to [Michael J. Frisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Frisch?action=edit&redlink=1 "Michael J. Frisch"), "remained the standard for many years". This was his thesis topic for his PhD at Cambridge supervised by [John Lennard-Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennard-Jones "John Lennard-Jones").

### Nuclear magnetic resonance

In the early days of [nuclear magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance") he studied the underlying theory, and in 1959 he co-authored the textbook *High Resolution Nuclear Magnetic Resonance* with [W.G. Schneider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_G._Schneider "William G. Schneider") and H.J. Bernstein.

### Semi-empirical theory

He made major contributions to the theory of approximate [molecular orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_orbital "Molecular orbital") (MO) calculations, starting with one identical to the one developed by [Rudolph Pariser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolph_Pariser "Rudolph Pariser") and [Robert G. Parr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_G._Parr "Robert G. Parr") on [pi electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi_electron "Pi electron") systems, and now called the [Pariser–Parr–Pople method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pariser–Parr–Pople_method "Pariser–Parr–Pople method"). Subsequently, he developed the methods of Complete Neglect of Differential Overlap ([CNDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNDO/2 "CNDO/2")) (in 1965) and Intermediate Neglect of Differential Overlap ([INDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INDO "INDO")) for approximate MO calculations on three-dimensional molecules, and other developments in [computational chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_chemistry "Computational chemistry"). In 1970 he and David Beveridge coauthored the book *Approximate Molecular Orbital Theory* describing these methods.

### Ab initio electronic structure theory

Pople pioneered the development of more sophisticated computational methods, called [ab initio quantum chemistry methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ab_initio_quantum_chemistry_methods "Ab initio quantum chemistry methods"), that use [basis sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_sets_used_in_computational_chemistry "Basis sets used in computational chemistry") of either [Slater type orbitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slater_type_orbital "Slater type orbital") or [Gaussian orbitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_orbital "Gaussian orbital") to model the wave function. While in the early days these calculations were extremely expensive to perform, the advent of high speed microprocessors has made them much more feasible today. He was instrumental in the development of one of the most widely used [computational chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_chemistry "Computational chemistry") packages, the [Gaussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_(software) "Gaussian (software)") suite of programs, including coauthorship of the first version, Gaussian 70. One of his most important original contributions is the concept of a model chemistry whereby a method is rigorously evaluated across a range of molecules. His research group developed the [quantum chemistry composite methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chemistry_composite_methods "Quantum chemistry composite methods") such as Gaussian-1 (G1) and Gaussian-2 (G2). In 1991, Pople stopped working on Gaussian and several years later he developed (with others) the [Q-Chem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-Chem "Q-Chem") [computational chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_chemistry "Computational chemistry") program. Prof. Pople's departure from Gaussian, along with the subsequent banning of many prominent scientists, including himself, from using the software gave rise to considerable controversy among the quantum chemistry community.

The Gaussian molecular orbital methods were described in the 1986 book *Ab initio molecular orbital theory* by Warren Hehre, Leo Radom, Paul v.R. Schleyer and Pople.

## Awards and honours

Pople received the [Wolf Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Chemistry "Wolf Prize in Chemistry") in 1992, and the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1998. He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1961 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1961"). He was made a Knight Commander (KBE) of the [Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire "Order of the British Empire") in 2003. He was a founding member of the [International Academy of Quantum Molecular Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_Quantum_Molecular_Science "International Academy of Quantum Molecular Science").

An [IT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology "Information technology") room and a scholarship are named after him at [Bristol Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Grammar_School "Bristol Grammar School"), as is a [supercomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputer "Supercomputer") at the [Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Supercomputing_Center "Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center").

## Personal life

Pople married Joy Bowers in 1952 and was married until her death from cancer in 2002. Pople died of liver cancer in Chicago in 2004. He was survived by his daughter Hilary, and sons Adrian, Mark and Andrew. In accordance with his wishes, Pople's Nobel Medal was given to [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University "Carnegie Mellon University") by his family on 5 October 2009. He was a Christian.
