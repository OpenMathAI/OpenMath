**Sir William Ramsay** ([/ˈræmzi/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); 2 October 1852 – 23 July 1916) was a Scottish chemist who discovered the [noble gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_gases "Noble gases") and received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1904 "in recognition of his services in the discovery of the inert gaseous elements in air" along with his collaborator, [John William Strutt, 3rd Baron Rayleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_William_Strutt,_3rd_Baron_Rayleigh "John William Strutt, 3rd Baron Rayleigh"), who received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") that same year for their discovery of [argon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argon "Argon"). After the two men identified argon, Ramsay investigated other atmospheric gases. His work in isolating argon, [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium "Helium"), [neon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon "Neon"), [krypton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krypton "Krypton"), and [xenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenon "Xenon") led to the development of a new section of the [periodic table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table "Periodic table").

## Early life

Ramsay was born at 2 Clifton Street in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow "Glasgow") on 2 October 1852, the son of civil engineer and surveyor, William C. Ramsay, and his wife, Catherine Robertson. The family lived at 2 Clifton Street in the city centre, a three-storey and basement Georgian townhouse. The family moved to 1 Oakvale Place in the [Hillhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillhead "Hillhead") district in his youth. He was a nephew of the [geologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologist "Geologist") [Sir Andrew Ramsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Ramsay_(geologist) "Andrew Ramsay (geologist)").

He was educated at [Glasgow Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Academy "Glasgow Academy") and then apprenticed to Robert Napier, a shipbuilder in [Govan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govan "Govan"). However, he instead decided to study chemistry at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow "University of Glasgow"), matriculating in 1866 and graduating in 1869. He then undertook practical training with the chemist [Thomas Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Anderson_(chemist) "Thomas Anderson (chemist)") and then went to study in Germany at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen "University of Tübingen") with [Wilhelm Rudolph Fittig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Rudolph_Fittig "Wilhelm Rudolph Fittig") where his doctoral thesis was entitled *Investigations in the [Toluic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluene "Toluene") and [Nitrotoluic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mononitrotoluene "Mononitrotoluene") Acids*.

Ramsay went back to Glasgow as Anderson's assistant at [Anderson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strathclyde "University of Strathclyde"). He was appointed as Professor of Chemistry at the [University College of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Bristol "University College, Bristol") in 1879 and married Margaret Buchanan in 1881. In the same year he became the Principal of University College, Bristol, and somehow managed to combine that with active research both in [organic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry") and on gases.

## Career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/William_Ramsay%27s_Nobel_Prize.jpg/250px-William_Ramsay%27s_Nobel_Prize.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:William_Ramsay's_Nobel_Prize.jpg)

William Ramsay's Nobel Prize certificate

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/WilliamRamsay_BluePlaque.jpg/250px-WilliamRamsay_BluePlaque.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:WilliamRamsay_BluePlaque.jpg)

Blue plaque at 12 [Arundel Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arundel_Gardens "Arundel Gardens") commemorating the work of William Ramsay

William Ramsay formed [pyridine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyridine "Pyridine") in 1876 from [acetylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetylene "Acetylene") and [hydrogen cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_cyanide "Hydrogen cyanide") in an iron-tube furnace in what was the first synthesis of a [heteroaromatic compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterocyclic_compound "Heterocyclic compound"). In 1887, he succeeded [Alexander Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_William_Williamson "Alexander William Williamson") as the chair of Chemistry at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London") (UCL). It was here at UCL that his most celebrated discoveries were made. As early as 1885–1890, he published several notable papers on the [oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxide "Oxide") of [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen "Nitrogen"), developing the skills that he needed for his subsequent work. On the evening of 19 April 1894, Ramsay attended a lecture given by [Lord Rayleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Rayleigh "Lord Rayleigh"). Rayleigh had noticed a discrepancy between the density of nitrogen made by [chemical synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_synthesis "Chemical synthesis") and nitrogen isolated from the air by removal of the other known components. After a short conversation, he and Ramsay decided to investigate this. In August Ramsay told Rayleigh he had isolated a new, heavy component of air, which did not appear to have any [chemical reactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactivity_(chemistry) "Reactivity (chemistry)"). He named this inert gas "[argon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argon "Argon")", from the Greek word meaning "lazy". In the following years, working with [Morris Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Travers "Morris Travers"), he discovered [neon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon "Neon"), [krypton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krypton "Krypton"), and [xenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenon "Xenon"). He also isolated [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium "Helium"), which had only been observed in the [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun#Observation_and_ugly_effects "Sun") of the sun, and had not previously been found on earth. In 1910 he isolated and characterised [radon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon "Radon").

During 1893–1902, Ramsay collaborated with [Emily Aston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Aston "Emily Aston"), a British chemist, in experiments on mineral analysis and atomic weight determination. Their work included publications on the molecular surface energies of mixtures of non-associating liquids.

Ramsay was elected an International Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1899.

He was appointed a Knight Commander of the [Order of the Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Bath "Order of the Bath") (KCB) in the [1902 Coronation Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1902_Coronation_Honours "1902 Coronation Honours") list published on 26 June 1902, and invested as such by King [Edward VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VII "Edward VII") at [Buckingham Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Palace "Buckingham Palace") on 24 October 1902.

In 1904, Ramsay received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry").
That same year, he was elected an International Member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"). Ramsay's standing among scientists led him to become an adviser to the [Indian Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Science "Indian Institute of Science"). He suggested [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore "Bangalore") as the location for the institute.

Ramsay endorsed the Industrial and Engineering Trust Ltd., a company that claimed it could extract [gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold "Gold") from [seawater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawater "Seawater"), in 1905. It bought property on the English coast to begin its secret process. The company never produced any gold.

Ramsay was the president of the [British Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Science_Association "British Science Association") in 1911–1912.

## Personal life

In 1881, Ramsay was married to Margaret Johnstone Marshall (née Buchanan), daughter of George Stevenson Buchanan. They had a daughter, Catherine Elizabeth (Elska) and a son, William George, who died at 40.

Ramsay lived in [Hazlemere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazlemere "Hazlemere"), [Buckinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckinghamshire "Buckinghamshire"), until his death. He died in [High Wycombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Wycombe "High Wycombe"), Buckinghamshire, on 23 July 1916 from [nasal cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasopharynx_cancer "Nasopharynx cancer") at the age of 63 and was buried in Hazlemere [parish church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish_church "Parish church").

## Legacy

A [blue plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_plaque "Blue plaque") at number 12 [Arundel Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arundel_Gardens "Arundel Gardens"), [Notting Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notting_Hill "Notting Hill"), commemorates his life and work.

The [Sir William Ramsay School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_William_Ramsay_School "Sir William Ramsay School") in Hazlemere and [Ramsay grease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsay_grease "Ramsay grease") are named after him.

There is a memorial to him by [Charles Hartwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Leonard_Hartwell "Charles Leonard Hartwell") in the north aisle of the choir at [Westminster Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Abbey "Westminster Abbey").

In 1923, [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London") named its new Chemical Engineering department and seat after Ramsay, which had been funded by the Ramsay Memorial Fund. One of Ramsay's former graduates, [H. E. Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._E._Watson "H. E. Watson") was the third Ramsay professor of chemical engineering.

On 2 October 2019, [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google") celebrated his 167th birthday with a [Google Doodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Doodle "Google Doodle").
