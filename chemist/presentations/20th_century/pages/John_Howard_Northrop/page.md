**John Howard Northrop** (July 5, 1891 – May 27, 1987) was an American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist") who, with [James Batcheller Sumner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Batcheller_Sumner "James Batcheller Sumner") and [Wendell Meredith Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_Meredith_Stanley "Wendell Meredith Stanley"), won the 1946 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"). The award was given for these scientists' isolation, [crystallization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallization "Crystallization"), and study of [enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme "Enzyme"), proteins, and viruses. Northrop was a Professor of Bacteriology and Medical Physics, Emeritus, at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley").

## Biography

### Early years

Northrop was born in [Yonkers, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonkers,_New_York "Yonkers, New York"), to [John Isaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Isaiah_Northrop "John Isaiah Northrop"), a [zoologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoologist "Zoologist") and instructor at Columbia University who is a member of the [Havemeyer family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havemeyer_family "Havemeyer family"), and [Alice Rich Northrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Rich_Northrop "Alice Rich Northrop"), a teacher of botany at Hunter College. His father died in a lab explosion two weeks before John H. Northrop was born. The son was educated at [Yonkers High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonkers_High_School "Yonkers High School") and [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), where he earned his BA in 1912 and PhD in chemistry in 1915. During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), he conducted research for the [U.S. Chemical Warfare Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Corps "Chemical Corps") on the production of [acetone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetone "Acetone") and [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol "Ethanol") through [fermentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermentation_(biochemistry) "Fermentation (biochemistry)"). This work led to studying enzymes.

### Research

In 1929, Northrop isolated and crystallized the gastric enzyme [pepsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsin "Pepsin") and determined that it was a [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein"). For this achievement, he was elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1934. In 1938 he isolated and crystallized the first [bacteriophage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriophage "Bacteriophage") (a small virus that attacks [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterium "Bacterium")), and determined that it was a [nucleoprotein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleoprotein "Nucleoprotein"). He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") that same year. Northrop also isolated and crystallized pepsinogen (the precursor to pepsin), [trypsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trypsin "Trypsin"), [chymotrypsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chymotrypsin "Chymotrypsin"), and [carboxypeptidase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboxypeptidase "Carboxypeptidase").

For his 1939 book, *Crystalline Enzymes: The Chemistry of Pepsin, Trypsin, and Bacteriophage*, Northrop was awarded the [Daniel Giraud Elliot Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Giraud_Elliot_Medal "Daniel Giraud Elliot Medal") from the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences"). He was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1949. Northrop was employed by the [Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Institute_for_Medical_Research "Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research") in New York City from 1916 until his retirement in 1961. In 1949 he joined the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") as Professor of Bacteriology, and later, he was appointed Professor of Biophysics.

### Personal life

In 1917, Northrop married Louise Walker (1891–1975), with whom he had two children: John, an oceanographer, and Alice, who married Nobel laureate [Frederick C. Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_C._Robbins "Frederick C. Robbins"). The family lived in a small home just outside of Mt. Vernon, New York. As their children grew older and Northrop looked for a more desirable workplace, the family bought a home in Cotuit, Massachusetts. This move shortened Northrop's commute to the laboratory in Princeton, New Jersey, and also put him in closer contact with the wilderness which he greatly enjoyed. Northrop committed suicide in [Wickenburg, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wickenburg,_Arizona "Wickenburg, Arizona") in 1987.
