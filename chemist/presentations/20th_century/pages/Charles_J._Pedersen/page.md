**Charles John Pedersen** ([Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): 安井 良男, *Yasui Yoshio*, October 3, 1904 – October 26, 1989) was an American [organic chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry") best known for discovering [crown ethers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_ether "Crown ether") and describing methods of synthesizing them during his entire 42-year career as a chemist for [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont") at [DuPont Experimental Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont_Experimental_Station "DuPont Experimental Station") in [Wilmington, Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_Delaware "Wilmington, Delaware"), and at DuPont's Jackson Laboratory in Deepwater, New Jersey. Often associated with [Reed McNeil Izatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_McNeil_Izatt "Reed McNeil Izatt"), Pedersen also shared the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1987 with [Donald J. Cram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_J._Cram "Donald J. Cram") and [Jean-Marie Lehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Lehn "Jean-Marie Lehn"). He is one of three Nobel Prize laureates born in [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea "Korea"), along with Peace Prize laureate [Kim Dae-jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Dae-jung "Kim Dae-jung") and Literature laureate [Han Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Kang "Han Kang").

Pedersen made many other discoveries in chemistry, such as discovering and developing [metal deactivators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_deactivator "Metal deactivator"). His early investigations also led to the development of a dramatically improved process for manufacturing [tetraethyl lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetraethyllead "Tetraethyllead"), an important gasoline additive. He also contributed to the development of [neoprene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoprene "Neoprene").

## Early life and education

Born on October 3, 1904, in [Busan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busan "Busan"), [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Empire "Korean Empire"), Charles J. Pedersen was the youngest of three children. His father, Brede Pedersen, was a [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") marine engineer who immigrated to [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea "Korea") in order to join the Korean customs service after leaving home due to family issues. Later, he worked as a mechanical engineer at the [Unsan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsan_County "Unsan County") mines in present-day [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea"). His Japanese mother, Takino Yasui, immigrated from [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") to [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea "Korea") with her family and established a successful line of work by trading [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean "Soybean") and [silkworms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silkworm "Silkworm") located close to the [Unsan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsan_County "Unsan County") mines, where the couple ultimately met. Although not much is mentioned about his elder brother, who died in childhood shortly before Pedersen was born, he had an older sister named Astrid, who was five years older than him. In [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), he used the Japanese given name **Yoshio** (良男), which he spelled using the [kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji "Kanji") for "good" and "man". According to Pedersen in a separate autobiographical account of his childhood, he had been born prior to the [Russo-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Japanese_War "Russo-Japanese War") and because his mother had still been grieving over the then-recent death of his older brother, he did not feel welcomed as a child.

Despite living in what is now [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea"), because Pedersen lived in the vicinity of the American-owned [Unsan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsan_County "Unsan County") mines, which spanned approximately 500 square miles in area, he grew up speaking primarily [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language").

At around 8 years old, Pedersen was sent by his family to study abroad in [Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki "Nagasaki"), Japan and then later transferred to [St. Joseph College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph_College,_Yokohama "Saint Joseph College, Yokohama") in [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama "Yokohama"), Japan.

After successfully completing his education at St. Joseph College, due to the close ties his family had with the [Society of Mary (Marianists)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Mary_(Marianists) "Society of Mary (Marianists)"), Pedersen decided to attend college in [America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America "America") at the [University of Dayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dayton "University of Dayton") in [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio "Ohio").

While spending his undergraduate life in 1922 studying [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering "Chemical engineering") at the [University of Dayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dayton "University of Dayton") in [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio "Ohio"), Pedersen had been a well balanced student who immersed himself in the sports, academic and social aspects of his college. With a passion for the sport of [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis"), Pedersen played on his school's varsity tennis team under Coach Frank Kronauge, a former [University of Dayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dayton "University of Dayton") tennis captain. Playing for all four years of his undergraduate years, Pedersen became captain for both of his junior and senior seasons on the team. Furthermore, Pedersen spent his time as both the vice-president of the Engineers' Club as well as in charge of Literary in the Daytonian Editorial Department. Graduating from the [University of Dayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dayton "University of Dayton") in 1926 with a degree in [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering "Chemical engineering"), he was dedicated for his time at the university as well as the various accomplishments he made while studying as an undergraduate.

Earning a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") in [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering "Chemical engineering"), Pedersen decided to attend the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") in order to obtain a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree "Master's degree") in organic chemistry. Although his professors at the time encouraged him to stay and pursue a PhD in organic chemistry, Pedersen decided to start his career instead, partially because he no longer wanted to be supported by his father. He is one of the few people to win a [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in the sciences without having a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD").

### Du Pont

After leaving the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"), Pedersen became employed at the [DuPont Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont") in [Wilmington, Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_Delaware "Wilmington, Delaware"), in 1927 through connections from his research advisor, Professor [James F. Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_F._Norris "James F. Norris"). While at [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont"), Pedersen was able to begin research at the Jackson Laboratory under William S. Calcott and finished his career with DuPont at the Experimental Station in Wilmington, Delaware. As a young chemist at [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont"), Pedersen witnessed and gained inspiration many flourishing chemists such as Julian Hill and [Roy J. Plunkett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_J._Plunkett "Roy J. Plunkett"), and also breakthroughs in polymers and work in the field of organic chemistry. Pedersen had a particular interest in [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_industry "Private industry") as he started his focus on his chemical career, which influenced the direction of problems he set out to solve as a chemist. As Pedersen began working on problems as a new chemist, he was free to work on whatever problems fascinated him and he quickly became interested in [oxidative degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidative "Oxidative") and stabilization of [substrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substrate_(chemistry) "Substrate (chemistry)"). Pedersen's papers and work expanded beyond this, however it was a major influence to his eventual [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") awarded research.

Retiring at the age of 65, his work resulted in 25 papers and 65 patents, and in 1967, Pedersen published two works describing the methods of synthesizing [crown ethers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_ether "Crown ether") (cyclic polyethers). The donut-shaped molecules were the first in a series of extraordinary compounds that form stable structures with [alkali metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkali_metal "Alkali metal") [ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion "Ion"). In 1987, he shared the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his work in this area with [Donald Cram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Cram "Donald Cram") and [Jean-Marie Lehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Lehn "Jean-Marie Lehn"), whom expanded upon his original discoveries. In the whole process of the Nobel Prize winning, the Dupont Company fully supported Pedersen by providing him a full-time public relations man, and a part-time secretary. DuPont Company also utilized their own corporate aircraft to accompany Pedersen and his family, as he could not travel on commercial aircraft.

## Discovery of the crown ethers

At around 1960, Pedersen went back to research in the field of [Coordination Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_Chemistry "Coordination Chemistry"), focusing on the [synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_synthesis "Chemical synthesis") of [multidentate ligands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multidentate "Multidentate"). It was recommended by his colleague [Herman Schroeder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Schroeder "Herman Schroeder") to work on the [coordination chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_Chemistry "Coordination Chemistry") of [vanadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanadium "Vanadium") before working on the [polymerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerization "Polymerization") and [oxidative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidative "Oxidative") [catalytic activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalytic_activity "Catalytic activity") of [vanadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanadium "Vanadium"). It was while working on this research that Pedersen made his discovery of [crown ether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_ether "Crown ether"). While studying the synthesis of [bis[2-(*o*-hydroxyphenoxy)ethyl] ether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bis(2-(2-hydroxyphenoxy)ethyl)_ether?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bis(2-(2-hydroxyphenoxy)ethyl) ether"), Pedersen accidentally discovered an unknown substances described as a "goo" while purifying the compound. Using [ultraviolet–visible spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet–visible_spectroscopy "Ultraviolet–visible spectroscopy") to study its reactions with [phenol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenol "Phenol") groups, after treating the samples with [alkali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkali "Alkali"), although the [absorption curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorption_curve "Absorption curve") initially showed no changes, it was observed to have shifted to higher [absorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorption_(electromagnetic_radiation) "Absorption (electromagnetic radiation)") readings if one or more of the [hydroxy groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxy_group "Hydroxy group") were [unpaired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unpaired_electron "Unpaired electron"). Basing further research on this observation, Pedersen then dipped the unknown product in [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol "Methanol") and [sodium hydroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_hydroxide "Sodium hydroxide"). Although the solution was not soluble in [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol "Methanol"), it became [alkaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_(chemistry) "Base (chemistry)") when in contact with the [sodium hydroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_hydroxide "Sodium hydroxide").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Reaction_1.jpg/250px-Reaction_1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Reaction_1.jpg)

Due to not being [soluble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soluble "Soluble") in [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol "Methanol"), Pedersen then proceeded to treat the [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol "Methanol") with [soluble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soluble "Soluble") [sodium salts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_salts "Sodium salts"), to which the unknown substance became [soluble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soluble "Soluble"), allowing him to conclude that the [solubility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solubility "Solubility") was due to [sodium ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_ion "Sodium ion") instead of [alkalinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkalinity "Alkalinity"). Since the behavior of this substance mirrored that of 2,3-benzo-1,4,7-trioxacyclononane, with twice the [molecular-weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular-weight "Molecular-weight"), the unknown molecule was then coined as [dibenzo-18-crown-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibenzo-18-crown-6 "Dibenzo-18-crown-6"), the first of the aromatic crown compounds discovered.

## Associations with other chemists

### Reed M. Izatt

In 1968 [Izatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_Izatt "Reed Izatt") was on a train ride home when he stopped in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") to meet with the [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiologist "Physiologist") George Eisenman who informed [Izatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_Izatt "Reed Izatt") about Pedersen's paper on [crown ethers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_ether "Crown ether") published only months earlier. [Izatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_Izatt "Reed Izatt") was looking forward to the possibilities of studying these [crown ethers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_ether "Crown ether") with his [thermionic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermionic_emission "Thermionic emission") [titration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titration "Titration") technique. [Izatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_Izatt "Reed Izatt") called Pedersen and was the first scientist not at [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont") to meet with Pedersen about his discovery and Pedersen supplied him with a sample of his new [crown ether compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_ether "Crown ether"). [Izatt's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_Izatt "Reed Izatt") work on [molecular recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_recognition "Molecular recognition") was greatly influenced by his interaction with Pedersen. In [Izatt's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_Izatt "Reed Izatt") last visit with Pedersen prior to his death in 1988 he found a personal letter written by Pedersen that stated, "*Most men achieve 'Immortality' through their progeny. I have no child of my own. Possibly, the crown ethers will serve, in a small way, to mark my footprint on earth"* and [Izatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_Izatt "Reed Izatt") believing this too shares Pedersen's message.

### Donald J. Cram

[Cram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_J._Cram "Donald J. Cram") shared the 1987 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with Pedersen but expanding on Pedersen's monumental discovery in [macrocyclic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrocycle "Macrocycle") of [crown ethers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_ether "Crown ether"). Pedersen's work was in [two-dimensional structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_(mathematics) "Plane (mathematics)") but [Cram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_J._Cram "Donald J. Cram") was able to synthesize similar molecules in [three-dimensional space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-dimensional_space "Three-dimensional space"). Cram's synthesis of these [three-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-dimensional_space "Three-dimensional space") molecules provided large gains in the production of [enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme "Enzyme") made in labs as these structures have [selectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemoselectivity "Chemoselectivity") based on complementary structures.

### Jean-Marie Lehn

[Lehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Lehn "Jean-Marie Lehn") was the other scientist who shared the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with Pedersen and was fundamental in starting the field of [Supramolecular chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supramolecular_chemistry "Supramolecular chemistry"). [Lehn's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Lehn "Jean-Marie Lehn") work specifically identified in his recognition for the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") was in his work on [cryptands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptand "Cryptand").

## Miscellaneous research

Although minimal research has been conducted on this [compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_compound "Chemical compound"), Pedersen observed that it had potential to work as a deactivator for [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper"). Nevertheless, it was the first of the many [metal deactivators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_deactivator "Metal deactivator"), which function by converting an inactive [complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_complex "Coordination complex") from an otherwise [catalytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis "Catalysis") [metal ion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_ion "Metal ion").

## Personal life

Pedersen was married to Susan J. Ault in 1947 and the couple then moved to [Salem, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem,_New_Jersey "Salem, New Jersey"), where they resided until Ault died on February 8, 1983, at 72 years old. Pedersen was diagnosed with [myeloma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myeloma "Myeloma") in 1983, and though he was becoming increasingly frail, he traveled to [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm") to accept the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in late 1987. Shortly thereafter, he was awarded a medal for excellence by the [DuPont Research Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont"). He died on 26 October 1989 in [Salem, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem,_New_Jersey "Salem, New Jersey").

## Legacy

Following Pedersen's breakthrough in realizing his accidental product and structure of [dibenzo-18-crown-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibenzo-18-crown-6 "Dibenzo-18-crown-6"), huge advancements have been made in the fields of [macrocyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrocycle "Macrocycle") and [supramolecular chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supramolecular_chemistry "Supramolecular chemistry"). Pedersen devoted the rest of his research career to studying these [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule") and started one of the largest growths recently seen in a specific field of chemistry. This growth in Pedersen's field of work following his momentous discovery for [macrocyclic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrocycle "Macrocycle") can be seen in the work of the 2016 [Nobel Prize winner in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_winner_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize winner in Chemistry") for [molecular motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_motor "Molecular motor") where Pedersen's work allowed for the insight in how to create the molecular specific structures. The molecular machines were recognized as the focus of the 2016 [Nobel Prize Winners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_Winners "Nobel Prize Winners"), which were produced by connecting [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule") to various molecular rings.
