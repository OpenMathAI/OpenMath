**James Batcheller Sumner** (November 19, 1887 – August 12, 1955) was an American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). He discovered that [enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme "Enzyme") can be crystallized, for which he shared the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1946 with [John Howard Northrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Howard_Northrop "John Howard Northrop") and [Wendell Meredith Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_Meredith_Stanley "Wendell Meredith Stanley"). He was also the first to prove that enzymes are [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein").

## Biography

Sumner was born on November 19, 1887, in [Canton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton,_Massachusetts "Canton, Massachusetts").

While hunting at age 17, Sumner was accidentally shot by a companion and as a result his left arm had to be amputated just below the elbow. He had been left-handed before the accident, after which he had to learn to do things with his right hand.

Sumner graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") with a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") in 1910 where he was acquainted with prominent chemists [Roger Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Adams "Roger Adams"), [Farrington Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farrington_Daniels "Farrington Daniels"), [Frank C. Whitmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_C._Whitmore "Frank C. Whitmore"), [James Bryant Conant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bryant_Conant "James Bryant Conant") and [Charles Loring Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Loring_Jackson "Charles Loring Jackson"). After a short period of working in the cotton knitting factory owned by his uncle, he accepted a teaching position at [Mount Allison University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Allison_University "Mount Allison University") in [Sackville, New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sackville,_New_Brunswick "Sackville, New Brunswick"), Canada. For the year 1911-12 he worked as a teaching assistantship in chemistry at [Worcester Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester_Polytechnic_Institute "Worcester Polytechnic Institute").

In 1912, he went to study [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") in [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School "Harvard Medical School") and obtained his Ph.D. degree in 1914 with [Otto Folin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Folin "Otto Folin"). He then worked as assistant professor of biochemistry at [Cornell Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weill_Cornell_Medicine "Weill Cornell Medicine") in Ithaca, NY.

Sumner married Cid Ricketts (born Bertha Louise Ricketts in Brookhaven, Mississippi) when she attended medical school at Cornell. They married on July 10, 1915, and had four children. They were divorced in 1930, but she kept her married name. [Cid Ricketts Sumner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cid_Ricketts_Sumner "Cid Ricketts Sumner") went on to become an author, writing books that included *Tammy Tell Me True*, which was made into the movie *[Tammy and the Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammy_and_the_Bachelor "Tammy and the Bachelor")*, and *Quality*, which became the movie *[Pinky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinky_(film) "Pinky (film)")*. Cid Ricketts Sumner was murdered by their grandson, John R. Cutler, in 1970.

In 1931 Sumner married Agnes Lundkvist. In 1943 they divorced; later that year he married Mary Beyer, with whom he had two children.

## Research

It was in 1917 at Cornell where Sumner began his research into isolating [enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme "Enzyme") in pure form; a feat which had never been achieved before. The enzyme he worked with was [urease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urease "Urease"), which he isolated from [jack beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_bean "Jack bean"). Sumner's work was unsuccessful for many years and many of his colleagues were doubtful, believing that what he was trying to achieve was impossible, but in 1926 he demonstrated that urease could be isolated and [crystallized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal "Crystal"). He accomplished this by mixing purified urease with [acetone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetone "Acetone") and then chilling it; the chilled solution produced crystallized urease. He was also able to show by chemical tests that his pure urease was a [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein"). This was the first experimental proof that an enzyme is a protein, a controversial question at the time.

His successful research brought him to full professorship at Cornell in 1929. From 1924 on his laboratory was located on the second floor of the new dairy science building, Stocking Hall (today home to Food Science), at Cornell where he did his Nobel Prize–winning research. In 1937 he succeeded in isolating and crystallizing a second enzyme, [catalase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalase "Catalase"). By this time, [John Howard Northrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Howard_Northrop "John Howard Northrop") of the [Rockefeller Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University") had obtained other crystalline enzymes by similar methods, starting with [pepsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsin "Pepsin") in 1929. It had become clear that Sumner had devised a general crystallization method for enzymes, and also that all enzymes are proteins.

## Honors and awards

In 1937, he was given a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship "Guggenheim Fellowship") and he spent five months in [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden") working with Professor [Theodor Svedberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Svedberg "Theodor Svedberg"). Also that year, he was awarded the [Scheele Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheele_Award "Scheele Award") in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm").

Both Sumner and Northrop, along with Wendell M Stanley, shared the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in 1946 for crystallization of enzymes. In 1947 Sumner became the director of Cornell's enzyme chemistry laboratory. Sumner was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1948. In 1949, he was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences").

## Death

Sumner died at age 67 of cancer in Buffalo, New York on August 12, 1955.
