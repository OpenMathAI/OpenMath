**Ahmed Hassan Zewail** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic "Arabic"): أَحْمَد حَسَن زُوَيْل; February 26, 1946 – August 2, 2016) was an [Egyptian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Egyptian_Americans "List of Egyptian Americans") chemist, known as the "father of [femtochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femtochemistry "Femtochemistry")". He was awarded the 1999 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his work on femtochemistry and became the first Egyptian and Arab to win a Nobel Prize in a scientific field, and the first African to win a Nobel Prize in Chemistry. He was a professor of chemistry and physics at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") (Caltech), where he was the first Caltech faculty member to be named the [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling "Linus Pauling") Chair of Chemical Physics and was the director of the Physical Biology Center for Ultrafast Science and Technology.

## Early life and education

Ahmed Hassan Zewail was born on February 26, 1946, in [Damanhur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damanhur "Damanhur"), Egypt, and was raised in [Desouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desouk "Desouk"). He received bachelor's and master's degrees in chemistry from [Alexandria University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_University "Alexandria University") before moving to the United States to complete his PhD at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") under the supervision of [Robin M. Hochstrasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_M._Hochstrasser "Robin M. Hochstrasser").

## Career

After completing his PhD, Zewail did [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), supervised by [Charles B. Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_B._Harris "Charles B. Harris"). Following this, he was awarded a faculty appointment at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") in 1976, and eventually became the first [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling "Linus Pauling") Chair in Chemical Physics there. He became a [naturalized citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen "Naturalized citizen") of the United States on March 5, 1982. Zewail was the director of the Physical Biology Center for Ultrafast Science and Technology at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology").

## Research

Zewail's key work was a pioneer of [femtochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femtochemistry "Femtochemistry")—i.e. the study of [chemical reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reaction "Chemical reaction") on a [femtosecond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femtosecond "Femtosecond") timescale. Using a rapid [ultrafast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafast "Ultrafast") [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser "Laser") technique (consisting of [ultrashort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrashort_pulse "Ultrashort pulse") laser flashes), the technique allows the description of reactions on very short time scales – short enough to analyse [transition states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_state "Transition state") in selected chemical reactions.

[![Ahmed_Zewail_1986](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Ahmed_Zewail_1986.png/250px-Ahmed_Zewail_1986.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ahmed_Zewail_1986.png)

Zewail in 1986

Zewail became known as the "father of femtochemistry". He also made critical contributions in [ultrafast electron diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafast_electron_diffraction "Ultrafast electron diffraction"), which uses short electron pulses rather than light pulses to study chemical reaction dynamics.

## Political work

In a [speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_New_Beginning_(speech) "A New Beginning (speech)") at [Cairo University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_University "Cairo University") on June 4, 2009, US President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") proclaimed a new Science Envoy program as part of a "new beginning between the United States and Muslims around the world." In January 2010, Ahmed Zewail, [Elias Zerhouni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elias_Zerhouni "Elias Zerhouni"), and [Bruce Alberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Alberts "Bruce Alberts") became the first US science envoys to the Muslim world, visiting Muslim-majority countries from North Africa to Southeast Asia.

[![Ahmed_Zewail_(2010)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Ahmed_Zewail_%282010%29.jpg/250px-Ahmed_Zewail_%282010%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ahmed_Zewail_(2010).jpg)

Zewail in 2010

When asked about rumors that he might contest the [2012 Egyptian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_presidential_election,_2011 "Egyptian presidential election, 2011"), Ahmed Zewail said: "I am a frank man... I have no political ambition, as I have stressed repeatedly that I only want to serve Egypt in the field of science and die as a scientist."

During the [2011 Egyptian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Egyptian_protests "2011 Egyptian protests") he announced his return to the country. Zewail said that he would join a committee for constitutional reform alongside [Ayman Nour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_Nour "Ayman Nour"), Mubarak's rival at the 2005 presidential elections and a leading lawyer.
Zewail was later mentioned as a respected figure working as an intermediary between the military regime ruling after Mubarak's resignation, and revolutionary youth groups such as the [April 6 Youth Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_6_Youth_Movement "April 6 Youth Movement") and young supporters of [Mohamed ElBaradei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_ElBaradei "Mohamed ElBaradei"). He played a critical role during this time as described by Egyptian Media.

## Awards and honours

Zewail's work brought him international attention, receiving awards and honors throughout most of his career for his work in chemistry and physics. In 1999, Zewail became the first Egyptian to receive a science [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") when he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"). Zewail gave his Nobel Lecture on "Femtochemistry: Atomic-Scale Dynamics of the Chemical Bond Using Ultrafast Lasers".

In 1999, he received Egypt's highest state honour, the [Grand Collar of the Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Nile "Order of the Nile"). Other notable awards include the [Alexander von Humboldt Senior Scientist Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Prize "Humboldt Prize") (1983), the [King Faisal International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_International_Prize "King Faisal International Prize") (1989), the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize "Wolf Prize") in Chemistry (1993), the [Earle K. Plyler Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earle_K._Plyler_Prize_for_Molecular_Spectroscopy "Earle K. Plyler Prize for Molecular Spectroscopy") (1993), the [Herbert P. Broida Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_P._Broida_Prize "Herbert P. Broida Prize") (1995), the [Peter Debye Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Debye_Award "Peter Debye Award") (1996), the [Tolman Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolman_Award "Tolman Award") (1997), the [Robert A. Welch Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welch_Award_in_Chemistry "Welch Award in Chemistry") (1997), the [Linus Pauling Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling_Medal "Linus Pauling Medal") (1997), the [Franklin Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Medal "Franklin Medal") (1998) and the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") (2000). In October 2006, Zewail received the [Albert Einstein World Award of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_World_Award_of_Science "Albert Einstein World Award of Science") for "his pioneering development of the new field of femtoscience and for his seminal contributions to the revolutionary discipline of physical biology, creating new ways for better understanding the functional behavior of biological systems by directly visualizing them in the four dimensions of space and time." Zewail was awarded the [Othmer Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Othmer_Gold_Medal "Othmer Gold Medal") (2009), the [Priestley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priestley_Medal "Priestley Medal") (2011) from the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society") and the [Davy Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davy_Medal "Davy Medal") (2011) from the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society").

In 1982 he was named as a Fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society"). Zewail became a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1989, the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1993, and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1998. Zewail was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") (ForMemRS) in 2001. He was also elected as a Fellow of the [African Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Academy_of_Sciences "African Academy of Sciences") in 2001.

Zewail was made a Foreign Member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences"). In 2005, the Ahmed Zewail Award for Ultrafast Science and Technology was established by the American Chemical Society and the [Newport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Corporation_(company) "Newport Corporation (company)") in his honor. In 2010 the journal [Chemical Physics Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Physics_Letters "Chemical Physics Letters") established the Ahmed Zewail Prize in Molecular Sciences. In May 2010, Zewail gave the commencement address at [Southwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_University "Southwestern University"). The [Zewail City of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zewail_City_of_Science_and_Technology "Zewail City of Science and Technology"), established in 2000 and revived in 2011, is named in his honour.

## Personal life

Zewail and his first wife, Mervat, were married in 1967, just before leaving Egypt to attend the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") for his PhD. He had two daughters with Mervat, Maha and Amani. Mervat and Ahmed however, separated in 1979.

Zewail married Dema Faham, a [Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrians "Syrians"), in 1989. Zewail and Faham had two sons, Nabeel and Hani.

## Death and funeral

Zewail died aged 70 on the morning of August 2, 2016. He was recovering from cancer, however, the exact cause of his death is unknown. Zewail returned to Egypt, but only his body was received at Cairo Airport. A military funeral was held for Zewail on August 7, 2016, at the El-Mosheer Tantawy mosque in Cairo, Egypt. Those attending included President [Abdel Fattah el-Sisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel_Fattah_el-Sisi "Abdel Fattah el-Sisi"), Prime Minister [Sherif Ismail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherif_Ismail "Sherif Ismail"), al-Azhar Grand Imam [Ahmed el-Tayeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_el-Tayeb "Ahmed el-Tayeb"), Defence Minister [Sedki Sobhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedki_Sobhi "Sedki Sobhi"), former President [Adly Mansour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adly_Mansour "Adly Mansour"), former Prime Minister [Ibrahim Mahlab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Mahlab "Ibrahim Mahlab") and heart surgeon [Magdi Yacoub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdi_Yacoub "Magdi Yacoub"). The funeral prayers were led by [Ali Gomaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Gomaa "Ali Gomaa"), former [Grand Mufti of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mufti_of_Egypt "Grand Mufti of Egypt").
