**Otto Wallach** (German pronunciation: [[ˈɔto ˈvalax]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Otto_Wallach.ogg "File:De-Otto Wallach.ogg"); 27 March 1847 – 26 February 1931) was a German [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") and recipient of the 1910 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his work on [alicyclic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicyclic_compound "Alicyclic compound").

## Biography

Wallach was born in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg "Königsberg"), the son of a [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia "Prussia") civil servant. His father, Gerhard Wallach, descended from a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family that had converted to [Lutheranism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheranism "Lutheranism"). His mother, Otillie (Thoma), was an ethnic German of Protestant religion. Wallach's father was transferred to [Stettin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stettin "Stettin") (Szczecin) and later to [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam "Potsdam"). Otto Wallach went to school, a *Gymnasium*, in Potsdam, where he learned about [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature "Literature") and the [history of art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_art "History of art"), two subjects he was interested his whole life. At this time he also started private chemical experiments at the house of his parents.

In 1867 he started studying chemistry at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"), where at this time [Friedrich Wöhler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wöhler "Friedrich Wöhler") was head of organic chemistry. After one semester at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin "University of Berlin") with [August Wilhelm von Hofmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Wilhelm_von_Hofmann "August Wilhelm von Hofmann"), Wallach received his Doctoral degree from the University of Göttingen in 1869, and worked as a professor in the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn "University of Bonn") (1870–89) and the University of Göttingen (1889–1915). Two of his doctoral students were [Adolf Sieverts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Sieverts "Adolf Sieverts") and [Walter Haworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Haworth "Walter Haworth"). Wallach died at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen "Göttingen"). In 1912, he was awarded the [Davy Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davy_Medal "Davy Medal").

He died on 26 February 1931, and was buried in the [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen "Göttingen").

## Major works

During his work with [Friedrich Kekulé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_August_Kekulé_von_Stradonitz "Friedrich August Kekulé von Stradonitz") in Bonn he started a systematic analysis of the [terpenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terpene "Terpene") present in [essential oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_oil "Essential oil"). Up to this time only a few had been isolated in pure form, and structural information was sparse. [Melting point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_point "Melting point") comparison and the measurement of mixtures was one of the methods to confirm identical substances. For this method the mostly liquid terpenes had to be transformed into crystalline compounds. With stepwise derivatisation, especially additions to the [double bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_bond "Double bond") present in some of the terpenes, he achieved the goal of obtaining crystalline compounds. The investigation of the [rearrangement reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rearrangement_reaction "Rearrangement reaction") of cyclic [unsaturated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturated_and_unsaturated_compounds "Saturated and unsaturated compounds") terpenes made it possible to obtain the structure of an unknown terpene by following the rearrangement to a known structure of a terpene. With these principal methods he opened the path to systematic research on terpenes.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/AlphaPinene.png/250px-AlphaPinene.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:AlphaPinene.png)

He was responsible for naming [terpene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terpene "Terpene") and [pinene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinene "Pinene"), and for undertaking the first systematic study of pinene.

He wrote a book about the chemistry of terpenes, "Terpene und Campher" (1909).

Otto Wallach is known for [Wallach's rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallach's_rule "Wallach's rule"), [Wallach degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallach_degradation "Wallach degradation"), the [Leuckart-Wallach reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuckart-Wallach_reaction "Leuckart-Wallach reaction") (which he developed along with [Rudolf Leuckart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Leuckart_(chemist) "Rudolf Leuckart (chemist)")) and the [Wallach rearrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallach_rearrangement "Wallach rearrangement").
