**Thomas Robert Cech**{{efn|Pronounced "check" (born 8 December 1947) is an American chemist who shared the 1989 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with [Sidney Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Altman "Sidney Altman") “for their discovery of catalytic properties of [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA").”

Cech discovered that RNA could itself [cut strands of RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribozyme "Ribozyme"), suggesting that [life might have started as RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_world "RNA world"). He found that RNA can not only transmit instructions, but can act as a [catalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalyst "Catalyst") to speed up the necessary reactions.

He has also studied [telomeres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telomeres "Telomeres"), and his lab discovered an enzyme, TERT (telomerase reverse transcriptase), which is part of the process of restoring telomeres after they are shortened during cell division.

As president of [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute") (2000–2008) he promoted science education, and he teaches an undergraduate chemistry course at the [University of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado "University of Colorado").

## Early life and career

Cech was born to parents of [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs "Czechs") origin (his grandfather was Czech, his other grandparents were first-generation Americans) in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"). He grew up in [Iowa City, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_City,_Iowa "Iowa City, Iowa"). In junior high school, he knocked on the doors of [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology "Geology") professors at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa "University of Iowa"), and asked them to discuss [crystal structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_structures "Crystal structures"), [meteorites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorites "Meteorites") and [fossils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossils "Fossils").

A [National Merit Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Merit_Scholarship_Program "National Merit Scholarship Program"), Cech entered [Grinnell College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grinnell_College "Grinnell College") in 1966. There he studied [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer "Homer")'s *[Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey "Odyssey")*, [Dante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante "Dante")'s [*Inferno*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inferno_(Dante) "Inferno (Dante)"), [constitutional history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_history "Constitutional history") and [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"). He married his [organic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry") lab partner, Carol Lynn Martinson, and graduated with a B.A. in 1970.

In 1975, Cech completed his PhD in chemistry at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") and in the same year, he entered the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") where he engaged in postdoctoral research. In 1978, he obtained his first faculty position at the [University of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_at_Boulder "University of Colorado at Boulder") where he lectured undergraduate students in chemistry and [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry"), and where he remains on the faculty, currently as distinguished professor in the department of biochemistry. In 2000, Cech succeeded [Purnell Choppin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purnell_Choppin "Purnell Choppin") as president of the [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute") in [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "Maryland"). He also continued to head his biochemistry laboratory at the University of Colorado, Boulder. On April 1, 2008, Cech announced that he would step down as the president of [HHMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HHMI "HHMI"), to return to teaching and research, in spring 2009. Returning to Boulder, Cech became the first executive director of the BioFrontiers Institute, a position he held until 2020. He also taught general chemistry to freshmen.

Cech is the author of *The Catalyst: RNA and the Quest to Unlock Life’s Deepest Secrets*, published in June 2024.

## Research

Cech's main research area is that of the process of [transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_(genetics) "Transcription (genetics)") in the nucleus of cells. He studies how the [genetic code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_code "Genetic code") of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") is transcribed into [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA"). In the 1970s, Cech had been studying the [splicing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_splicing "RNA splicing") of RNA in the unicellular organism *[Tetrahymena thermophila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahymena_thermophila "Tetrahymena thermophila")* when he discovered that an unprocessed RNA molecule could splice itself. In 1982, Cech became the first to show that RNA molecules are not restricted to being passive carriers of genetic information – they can have [catalytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalytic "Catalytic") functions and can participate in cellular reactions. RNA-processing reactions and protein synthesis on [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome") in particular are catalysed by RNA. RNA [enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme "Enzyme") are known as [ribozymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribozymes "Ribozymes") and have provided a new tool for gene technology. They also have the potential to provide new therapeutic agents – for example, they have the ability to destroy and cleave invading, [viral RNAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus_rna "Virus rna").

Cech's second area of research is on [telomeres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telomeres "Telomeres"), the structure that protects the ends of [chromosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosomes "Chromosomes"). Telomeres are shortened with every duplication of DNA, and must be lengthened again. He studies [telomerase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telomerase "Telomerase"), the enzyme that copies the telomeric sequences and lengthens them. The active site protein subunits of telomerase comprise a new class of [reverse transcriptases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_transcriptase "Reverse transcriptase"), enzymes previously thought to be restricted to viruses and [transposable elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transposable_elements "Transposable elements"). [Telomerase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telomerase "Telomerase") is activated in 90% of human cancers. Therefore, a drug that would inhibit its activity could be useful in treating [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer").

## Awards

Cech's work has been recognised by many awards and prizes including: lifetime professorship by the [American Cancer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Cancer_Society "American Cancer Society") (1987), the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") (1988), the [Heineken Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_A.H._Heineken_Prize "Dr A.H. Heineken Prize") of the Royal Netherlands Academy of Sciences (1988), the [Albert Lasker Basic Medical Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker_Award "Lasker Award") (1988), the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") (1989, shared with [Sidney Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Altman "Sidney Altman")), the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") in 1990 and the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") (1995). In 1987, Cech was elected to the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") and in 1988 he was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"). Cech was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 2001. In 2003, Cech gave the University of Colorado's [George Gamow Memorial Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow_Memorial_Lectures "George Gamow Memorial Lectures"). In 2007, he received the [Othmer Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Othmer_Gold_Medal "Othmer Gold Medal") for outstanding contributions to progress in chemistry and science.
