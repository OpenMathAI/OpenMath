**Vincent du Vigneaud** (May 18, 1901 – December 11, 1978) was an American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). He was recipient of the 1955 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") "for his work on biochemically important sulphur compounds, especially for the first synthesis of a polypeptide hormone," a reference to his work on the [peptide hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptide_hormone "Peptide hormone") [oxytocin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxytocin "Oxytocin").

## Biography

Vincent du Vigneaud was born in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") in 1901. Of French descent, he was the son of inventor and mechanic Alfred du Vigneaud and Mary Theresa. He studied at the [Schurz High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schurz_High_School "Schurz High School") and completed secondary education in 1918. His interest in [sulfur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur "Sulfur") began when he entered high school and his new friends invited him to run chemical experiments on explosives using sulfur. During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), senior students were made to work on farms, and du Vigneaud worked near [Caledonia, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caledonia,_Illinois "Caledonia, Illinois"). There he became an expert in milking cows, which inspired him to become a farmer. However, his elder sister, Beatrice, persuaded him to take up chemistry at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign "University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign"), after which he enrolled in the chemical engineering course. He later recalled:

> I found during the first year that it was chemistry rather than engineering that appealed to me most. I switched to a major in chemistry, since I was deeply impressed by the senior student's work, especially in [organic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry"). I also found that I was most interested in those aspects of organic chemistry that had to do with medical substances and began to develop an interest in biochemistry.

His interest was aroused by lectures of [Carl Shipp Marvel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Shipp_Marvel "Carl Shipp Marvel") and [Howard B. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_B._Lewis "Howard B. Lewis"), whom he remembered as being 'extremely enthusiastic about sulfur." With little support from the family, he found odd jobs to support himself. After receiving his [MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") in 1924 he joined [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont").

He married Zella Zon Ford, whom he met on June 12, 1924, while working as a waiter during his university course. During the fall of 1924, Marvel found him a job as an assistant biochemist at the [Philadelphia General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_General_Hospital "Philadelphia General Hospital") that helped him to teach clinical chemistry at the Graduate School of Medicine, [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania"). Marvel would pay for the trip to Pennsylvania in exchange for du Vigneaud's preparation of 10 pounds of [cupferron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupferron "Cupferron"). Resuming his academic career in 1925, du Vigneaud joined the group of John R. Murlin at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester "University of Rochester") for his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis "Thesis"). He graduated in 1927 with his work *The Sulfur of Insulin*.

After a post-doctoral position with [John Jacob Abel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jacob_Abel "John Jacob Abel") at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University") Medical School (1927–1928), he traveled to Europe as a National Research Council Fellow in 1928–1929, where he worked with [Max Bergmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Bergmann "Max Bergmann") and [Leonidas Zervas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonidas_Zervas "Leonidas Zervas") at the [Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Leather Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Wilhelm_Institute_for_Leather_Research "Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Leather Research") in [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden "Dresden"), and with [George Barger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Barger "George Barger") at the [University of Edinburgh Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh_Medical_School "University of Edinburgh Medical School"). He then returned to the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois "University of Illinois") as a professor.

In 1932, he started working at the [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University "George Washington University") Medical School in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), and in 1938, he attended the [Cornell Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_Medical_College "Cornell Medical College") in New York City, where he stayed until his [emeritation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus "Emeritus") in 1967. Following retirement, he held a position at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") in [Ithaca, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithaca,_New_York "Ithaca, New York").

In 1974, du Vigneaud had a stroke which forced his retirement. He died in 1978, one year after his wife's death in 1977.

## Scientific contributions

Du Vigneaud's career was characterized by an interest in sulfur-containing peptides, proteins, and especially [peptide hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptide_hormone "Peptide hormone"). Even before his Nobel-Prize-winning work on elucidating and synthesizing [oxytocin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxytocin "Oxytocin") and [vasopressin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasopressin "Vasopressin") via manipulating the [AVP gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AVP_gene "AVP gene"), he had established a reputation from his research on [insulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin "Insulin"), [biotin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotin "Biotin"), [transmethylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmethylation "Transmethylation"), and [penicillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicillin "Penicillin").

He also carried out a series of structure-activity relationships for oxytocin and vasopressin, perhaps the first of their type for peptides. That work culminated in the publication of a book entitled *A Trail of Research in Sulphur Chemistry and Metabolism and Related Fields*.

## Honours

Du Vigneaud joined [Alpha Chi Sigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Chi_Sigma "Alpha Chi Sigma") while at the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign "University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign") in 1930. He was elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1944, and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1948. He received the 1955 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") "for his work on biochemically important sulphur compounds, especially for the first synthesis of a polypeptide hormone," a reference to his work on the [peptide hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptide_hormone "Peptide hormone") [oxytocin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxytocin "Oxytocin").
