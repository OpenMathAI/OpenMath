**Wendell Meredith Stanley** (August 16, 1904 – June 15, 1971) was an American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"), [virologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virology "Virology") and [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate "Nobel laureate"). Stanley's work contributed to [lepracidal compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepracidal_compounds?action=edit&redlink=1 "Lepracidal compounds"), diphenyl [stereochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereochemistry "Stereochemistry"), and the chemistry of the sterols. His research on the [virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus "Virus") causing the [mosaic disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic_disease "Mosaic disease") in [tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco "Tobacco") plants led to the isolation of a [nucleoprotein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleoprotein "Nucleoprotein") which displayed [tobacco mosaic virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_mosaic_virus "Tobacco mosaic virus") activity.

## Biography

Stanley was born in [Ridgeville, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridgeville,_Indiana "Ridgeville, Indiana"), and earned a BSc in chemistry at [Earlham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earlham_College "Earlham College") in [Richmond, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Indiana "Richmond, Indiana"). He then studied at the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign "University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign"), gaining an MS in science in 1927 followed by a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in chemistry two years later. His later accomplishments include writing the book "Chemistry: A Beautiful Thing" and being a [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize "Pulitzer Prize") nominee.

### Research

Although a member of [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council "United States National Research Council"), he moved to [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich") for temporary academic work with [Heinrich Wieland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Wieland "Heinrich Wieland") before he returned to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") in 1931. On return he was approved as an assistant at [The Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University"). He remained with the Institute until 1948, becoming an Associate Member in 1937, and a Member in 1940. In 1948, he became Professor of Biochemistry at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") and built the Virus Laboratory and a free-standing Department of Biochemistry building, which is now called Stanley Hall.

In 1935, Stanley managed to produce crystals from [tobacco mosaic virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_mosaic_virus "Tobacco mosaic virus") particles. The discovery was widely publicized, appearing for example on the front page of the [New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times "New York Times") of June 28, 1935, as people at the time were surprised that living-like creatures such as viruses could form crystals. It was also the first time that a virus could be "seen" in some form, which was notable since viral particles were originally found and characterized as an infectious agent which was exceedingly small and able to penetrate the most narrow ceramic filters. Individual viral particles would only be truly seen for the first time in 1942 after the invention of the [electron microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscope "Electron microscope") due to the work by [Thomas F. Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_F._Anderson "Thomas F. Anderson") and [Salvador Luria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Luria "Salvador Luria") on [bacteriophages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriophages "Bacteriophages").

Stanley was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1940 and the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1941. He was awarded a ¼ share in the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for 1946. In 1949, he was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"). His other notable awards included the Rosenburger Medal, Alder Prize, Scott Award, the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") and the [AMA Scientific Achievement Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMA_Scientific_Achievement_Award "AMA Scientific Achievement Award"). He was also awarded [honorary degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree "Honorary degree") by many universities both American and foreign, including [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"), [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") and the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris"). Most of the conclusions Stanley had presented in his Nobel-winning research were soon shown to be incorrect (in particular, that the [crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallization "Crystallization") of mosaic virus he had [isolated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolation_(microbiology) "Isolation (microbiology)") were pure protein, and assembled by [autocatalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocatalysis "Autocatalysis")).

### Personal life

Stanley married Marian Staples (1905–1984) in 1929 and had three daughters (Marjorie, Dorothy and Janet) and a son (Wendell Meredith Junior). Stanley Hall at UC Berkeley (now Stanley Biosciences and Bioengineering Facility) and Stanley Hall at Earlham College are named in his honor. His daughter, Marjorie, married [Robert Albo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Albo "Robert Albo"), physician to the [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors "Golden State Warriors") basketball team as well as the [Oakland Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Raiders "Oakland Raiders") football team. He died in [Salamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salamanca "Salamanca"), Spain on June 15, 1971.
