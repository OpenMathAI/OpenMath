**Roald Hoffmann** (born **Roald Safran**; July 18, 1937) is a Polish-American [theoretical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_chemistry "Theoretical chemistry") who shared the 1981 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with [Kenichi Fukui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenichi_Fukui "Kenichi Fukui") “for their theories, developed independently, concerning the course of chemical reactions”. He has also published [plays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play_(theatre) "Play (theatre)"), [poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry "Poetry") and [popular science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_science "Popular science"). He is the Frank H. T. Rhodes Professor of Humane Letters Emeritus at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University").

## Early life

### Escape from the Holocaust

Hoffmann was born in [Złoczów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zolochiv,_Lviv_Oblast "Zolochiv, Lviv Oblast"), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic "Second Polish Republic") (now Zolochiv, [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine")), to a [Polish-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-Jewish "Polish-Jewish") family, and was named in honor of the Norwegian explorer [Roald Amundsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roald_Amundsen "Roald Amundsen"). His parents were Clara (Rosen), a teacher, and Hillel Safran, a civil engineer. After Germany invaded Poland and occupied the town, his family was placed in a labor camp where his father, who was familiar with much of the local infrastructure, was a valued prisoner. As the situation grew more dangerous, with prisoners being transferred to extermination camps, the family bribed guards to allow an escape. They arranged with a Ukrainian neighbor named Mykola Dyuk for Hoffmann, his mother, two uncles and an aunt to hide in the attic and a storeroom of the local schoolhouse, where they remained for eighteen months, from January 1943 to June 1944, while Hoffmann was aged 5 to 7.

His father remained at the labor camp, but was able to occasionally visit, until he was tortured and killed by the Germans for his involvement in a plot to arm the camp prisoners. When she received the news, his mother attempted to contain her sorrow by writing down her feelings in a notebook her husband had been using to take notes on a relativity textbook he had been reading. While in hiding his mother kept Hoffmann entertained by teaching him to read and having him memorize geography from textbooks stored in the attic, then quizzing him on it. He referred to the experience as having been enveloped in a cocoon of love. In 1944 they moved to [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków") where his mother remarried. They adopted her new husband's surname Hoffmann.

Most of the rest of the family was killed in [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust"), though one grandmother and a few others survived. They migrated to the United States on the troop carrier *Ernie Pyle* in 1949.

Hoffmann visited [Zolochiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zolochiv,_Lviv_Oblast "Zolochiv, Lviv Oblast") with his adult son (by then a parent of a five-year-old) in 2006 and found that the attic where he had hidden was still intact, but the storeroom had been incorporated, ironically enough, into a chemistry classroom. In 2009, a monument to Holocaust victims was built in Zolochiv on Hoffmann's initiative.

## Personal life

Hoffmann married Eva Börjesson in 1960. They have two children, Hillel Jan and Ingrid Helena.

He describes himself as "an atheist who is moved by religion."

### Education and academic credentials

Hoffmann graduated in 1955 from New York City's [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School "Stuyvesant High School"), where he won a [Westinghouse science scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Science_Talent_Search "Westinghouse Science Talent Search"). He received his [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_degree "Academic degree") at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") (Columbia College) in 1958. He earned his [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") degree in 1960 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). He earned his [doctor of philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_philosophy "Doctor of philosophy") degree from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") while working under joint supervision of [Martin Gouterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Gouterman "Martin Gouterman") and subsequent 1976 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") winner [William N. Lipscomb Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lipscomb "William Lipscomb") Hoffman worked on the molecular orbital theory of polyhedral molecules. Under Lipscomb's direction the [Extended Hückel method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_Hückel_method "Extended Hückel method") was developed by Lawrence Lohr and by Roald Hoffmann. This method was later extended by Hoffmann. In 1965, he went to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") and has remained there, where he is a professor emeritus.

Hoffmann's research and interests have been in the electronic structure of stable and unstable molecules, and in the study of transition states in reactions. He has investigated the structure and reactivity of both [organic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry") and [inorganic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inorganic_chemistry "Inorganic chemistry") molecules, and examined problems in organo-metallic and solid-state chemistry. Hoffman has developed semiempirical and nonempirical [computational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_chemistry "Computational chemistry") tools and methods such as the [extended Hückel method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_Hückel_method "Extended Hückel method") which he proposed in 1963 for determining molecular orbitals.

With [Robert Burns Woodward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Burns_Woodward "Robert Burns Woodward") he developed the [Woodward–Hoffmann rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodward–Hoffmann_rules "Woodward–Hoffmann rules") for elucidating [reaction mechanisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_mechanism "Reaction mechanism") and their [stereochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereochemistry "Stereochemistry"). They realized that chemical transformations could be approximately predicted from subtle symmetries and asymmetries in the [electron orbitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_orbital "Molecular orbital") of complex molecules. Their rules predict differing outcomes, such as the types of products that will be formed when two compounds are activated by heat compared with those produced under activation by light. For this work Hoffmann received the 1981 Nobel Prize in chemistry, sharing it with Japanese chemist [Kenichi Fukui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenichi_Fukui "Kenichi Fukui"), who had independently resolved similar issues. (Woodward was not included in the prize, which is given only to living persons, although he had won the 1965 prize for other work.) In his Nobel Lecture, Hoffmann introduced the [isolobal analogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolobal_principle "Isolobal principle") for predicting the bonding properties of [organometallic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organometallic_compounds "Organometallic compounds").

Some of Hoffman's most recent work, with [Neil Ashcroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Ashcroft "Neil Ashcroft") and Vanessa Labet, examines bonding in matter under extreme high pressure.

> What gives me the greatest joy in this work? That as we tease apart what goes on in hydrogen under pressures such as those that one finds at the center of the earth, two explanations subtly contend with each other ... [physical and chemical] ... Hydrogen under extreme pressure is doing just what an inorganic molecule at 1 atmosphere does!

## Artistic interests

Hoffmann in 2009

### *The World Of Chemistry* with Roald Hoffmann

In 1988 Hoffmann became the series host in a 26-program [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS "PBS") education series by Annenberg/CPB, *[The World of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_of_Chemistry "The World of Chemistry")*, opposite with series demonstrator [Don Showalter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Showalter "Don Showalter"). While Hoffmann introduced a series of concepts and ideas, Showalter provided a series of demonstrations and other visual representations to help students and viewers to better understand the information.

### *Entertaining Science*

Since the spring of 2001, Hoffmann has been the host of the monthly series *Entertaining Science* at New York City's [Cornelia Street Cafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelia_Street_Cafe "Cornelia Street Cafe"), which explores the juncture between the arts and science.

### Non-fiction

He has published books on the connections between art and science: *Roald Hoffmann on the Philosophy, Art, and Science of Chemistry* and *Beyond the Finite: The Sublime in Art and Science*.

### Poetry

Hoffmann is also a writer of [poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry "Poetry"). His collections include *The Metamict State* (1987, [ISBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISBN_(identifier) "ISBN (identifier)") [0-8130-0869-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:BookSources/0-8130-0869-7 "Special:BookSources/0-8130-0869-7")), *Gaps and Verges* (1990, [ISBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISBN_(identifier) "ISBN (identifier)") [0-8130-0943-X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:BookSources/0-8130-0943-X "Special:BookSources/0-8130-0943-X")), and *Chemistry Imagined* (1993, [ISBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISBN_(identifier) "ISBN (identifier)") [978-1-56098-539-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:BookSources/978-1-56098-539-6 "Special:BookSources/978-1-56098-539-6"), co-produced with artist Vivian Torrence.

### Plays

He co-authored with [Carl Djerassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Djerassi "Carl Djerassi") the play *Oxygen*, about the [discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_(observation) "Discovery (observation)") of [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen "Oxygen") and the experience of being a scientist. Hoffman's play, "Should've" (2006) about ethics in science and art, has been produced in workshops, as has a play based on his experiences in the holocaust, "We Have Something That Belongs to You" (2009), later retitled "Something That Belongs to You.

### Nobel Prize in Chemistry

In 1981, Hoffmann received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"), which he shared with [Kenichi Fukui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenichi_Fukui "Kenichi Fukui") "for their theories, developed independently, concerning the course of chemical reactions".

Hoffmann is a member of the [International Academy of Quantum Molecular Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_Quantum_Molecular_Science "International Academy of Quantum Molecular Science") and the Board of Sponsors of The [Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_the_Atomic_Scientists "Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists").

In August 2007, the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society") held a symposium at its biannual national meeting to honor Hoffmann's 70th birthday.

In 2008, the [Göttingen Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities "Göttingen Academy of Sciences and Humanities") awarded him its [Lichtenberg Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichtenberg_Medal "Lichtenberg Medal").

In August 2017, another symposium was held at the 254th American Chemical Society National Meeting in Washington DC, to honor Hoffmann's 80th birthday.

The [Hoffmann Institute of Advanced Materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoffmann_Institute_of_Advanced_Materials "Hoffmann Institute of Advanced Materials") in Shenzhen, named after him, was founded in his honor in February 2018 and formally opened in his presence in May 2019.

In 2023, Roald Hoffmann was named by [Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Corporation_of_New_York "Carnegie Corporation of New York") as an honoree of the [Great Immigrants Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Immigrants_Award "Great Immigrants Award").
