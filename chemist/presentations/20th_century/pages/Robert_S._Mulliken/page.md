**Robert Sanderson Mulliken** (June 7, 1896 – October 31, 1986) was an American [physical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry"), primarily responsible for the early development of [molecular orbital theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_orbital_theory "Molecular orbital theory"), i.e. the elaboration of the [molecular orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_orbital "Molecular orbital") method of computing the structure of [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule"). Mulliken received the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in Chemistry in 1966 and the [Priestley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priestley_Medal "Priestley Medal") in 1983.

## Early years

Robert Mulliken was born in [Newburyport, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newburyport,_Massachusetts "Newburyport, Massachusetts") on June 7 1896. His father, [Samuel Parsons Mulliken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Parsons_Mulliken "Samuel Parsons Mulliken"), was a professor of [organic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry") at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"). As a child, Robert Mulliken learned the name and [botanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany "Botany") classification of plants and, in general, had an excellent, but selective, memory. For example, he learned [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language "German language") well enough to skip the course in scientific German in college, but could not remember the name of his high school German teacher. He also made the acquaintance, while still a child, of the physical chemist [Arthur Amos Noyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Amos_Noyes "Arthur Amos Noyes").

Mulliken helped with some of the editorial work when his father wrote his four-volume text on organic compound identification, and thus became an expert on [organic chemical nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_nomenclature "Organic nomenclature").

### Education

In high school in Newburyport, Mulliken followed a scientific curriculum. He graduated in 1913 and succeeded in getting a scholarship to MIT which had earlier been won by his father. Like his father, he majored in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"). Already as an undergraduate, he conducted his first publishable research: on the synthesis of organic chlorides. Because he was unsure of his future direction, he included some [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering "Chemical engineering") courses in his curriculum and spent a summer touring chemical plants in [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts "Massachusetts") and [Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine "Maine"). He received his [B. S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_degree "Academic degree") in chemistry from MIT in 1917.

#### Early career

At this time, the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") had just entered [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), and Mulliken took a position at [American University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University "American University") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), making poison gas under [James B. Conant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_B._Conant "James B. Conant"). After nine months, he was drafted into the Army's [Chemical Warfare Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Warfare_Service "Chemical Warfare Service"), but continued on the same task. His laboratory techniques left much to be desired, and he was out of service for months with burns. Later, he contracted a bad case of influenza, and was still hospitalized at war's end.

After the war, he took a job investigating the effects of [zinc oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc_oxide "Zinc oxide") and [carbon black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_black "Carbon black") on [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber "Rubber"), but quickly decided that this was not the kind of chemistry he wanted to pursue. Hence, in 1919 he entered the Ph.D. program at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago").

### Graduate and early postdoctoral education

Mulliken got his doctorate in 1921 based on research into the separation of [isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope "Isotope") of [mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_(element) "Mercury (element)") by [evaporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporation "Evaporation"), and continued in his isotope separation by this method. While at [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"), he took a course under the [Nobel Prize-winning physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") [Robert A. Millikan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Millikan "Robert A. Millikan"), which exposed him to the [old quantum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_quantum_theory "Old quantum theory"). He also became interested in strange molecules after exposure to work by [Hermann I. Schlesinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_I._Schlesinger "Hermann I. Schlesinger") on [diborane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diborane "Diborane").

Robert Mulliken, Chicago 1929 (third from right)

At Chicago, he had received a grant from the [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council "United States National Research Council") (NRC) which had paid for much of his work on isotope separation. The NRC grant was extended in 1923 for two years so he could study isotope effects on band spectra of such diatomic molecules as boron nitride (BN) (comparing molecules with B10 and B11). He went to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") to learn spectrographic technique from Frederick A. Saunders and quantum theory from [E. C. Kemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._C._Kemble "E. C. Kemble"). At the time, he was able to associate with [J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer "J. Robert Oppenheimer") and many future Nobel laureates, including [John H. Van Vleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_H._Van_Vleck "John H. Van Vleck") and [Harold C. Urey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_C._Urey "Harold C. Urey"). He also met [John C. Slater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Slater "John C. Slater"), who had worked with [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr "Niels Bohr").

In 1925 and 1927, Mulliken traveled to Europe, working with outstanding spectroscopists and quantum theorists such as [Erwin Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger "Erwin Schrödinger"), [Paul A. M. Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac"), [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg "Werner Heisenberg"), [Louis de Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis-Victor_de_Broglie "Louis-Victor de Broglie"), [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born "Max Born"), and [Walther Bothe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Bothe "Walther Bothe") (all of whom eventually received Nobel Prizes) and [Friedrich Hund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hund "Friedrich Hund"), who was at the time Born's assistant. They all, as well as [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli"), were developing the new [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") that would eventually supersede the old quantum theory. Mulliken was particularly influenced by Hund, who had been working on quantum interpretation of band spectra of diatomic molecules, the same spectra which Mulliken had investigated at Harvard. In 1927 Mulliken worked with Hund and as a result developed his [molecular orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_orbital "Molecular orbital") theory, in which electrons are assigned to states that extend over an entire molecule. In consequence, molecular orbital theory was also referred to as the **Hund-Mulliken theory.**

### Early scientific career

From 1926 to 1928, he taught in the [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") department at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University") (NYU). This was his first recognition as a physicist. Though his work had been considered important by chemists, it clearly was on the borderline between the two sciences and both would claim him from this point on. In 1930, he travelled as a Guggenheim Fellow to work at Leipzig University with [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg "Werner Heisenberg"), [Edward Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Teller "Edward Teller"), and [Friedrich Hund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hund "Friedrich Hund"). Then he returned to the University of Chicago as an associate professor of physics, being promoted to full professor in 1931. He ultimately held a position jointly in both the physics and chemistry departments. At both NYU and Chicago, he continued to refine his molecular-orbital theory.

Up to this point, the primary way to calculate the [electronic structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_structure "Electronic structure") of molecules was based on a calculation by [Walter Heitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Heitler "Walter Heitler") and [Fritz London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_London "Fritz London") on the [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen "Hydrogen") molecule (H2) in 1927. With the conception of hybridized atomic orbitals by [John C. Slater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Slater "John C. Slater") and [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling "Linus Pauling"), which rationalized observed molecular geometries, the method was based on the premise that the [bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_bond "Chemical bond") in any molecule could be described in a manner similar to the bond in H2, namely, as overlapping atomic orbitals centered on the atoms involved. Since it corresponded to chemists' ideas of localized bonds between pairs of atoms, this method (called the **Valence-Bond (VB)** or **Heitler-London-Slater-Pauling (HLSP)** method), was very popular. In attempting to calculate the properties of excited states (molecules that have been excited by an energy source), the VB method does not always work well. With its description of the electron wave functions in molecules as delocalized molecular orbitals that possess the same symmetry as the molecule, Hund and Mulliken's molecular-orbital method, including contributions by [John Lennard-Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennard-Jones "John Lennard-Jones"), proved to be more flexible and applicable to a vast variety of types of molecules and molecular fragments, and has eclipsed the valence-bond method. As a result of this development, he received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1966.

Mulliken became a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") in 1936, the youngest member in the organization's history at the time. He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1940 and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1965. He was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRs) in 1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1967 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1967").

[Mulliken population analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulliken_population_analysis "Mulliken population analysis") is named after him, a method of assigning charges to atoms in a molecule.

## Personal life

On December 24, 1929, he married Mary Helen von Noé, daughter of [Adolf Carl Noé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Carl_Noé "Adolf Carl Noé"), a geology professor at the University of Chicago. They had two daughters.

## Later years

In 1934, he derived a new scale for measuring the [electronegativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronegativity "Electronegativity") of elements, which he defined as the average of an atom's ionization enthalpy and electron affinity. This does not entirely correlate with the scale of [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling "Linus Pauling"), but is generally in close correspondence.

In [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), from 1942 to 1945, he directed the Information Office for the University of Chicago's [Plutonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutonium "Plutonium") project. Afterward, he developed mathematical formulas to enable the progress of the molecular-orbital theory.

In 1952. he began to apply [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") to the analysis of the reaction between [Lewis acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_acids_and_bases "Lewis acids and bases") and [base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_(chemistry) "Base (chemistry)") [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule"). (See [Acid-base reaction theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid-base_reaction_theories "Acid-base reaction theories")). In 1961, he became Distinguished Professor of Physics and Chemistry at [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University "Florida State University"), and continued in his studies of [molecular structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_structure "Molecular structure") and [spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy "Spectroscopy"), ranging from diatomic molecules to large complex aggregates. In 1981, Mulliken became a founding member of the [World Cultural Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cultural_Council "World Cultural Council"). In 1983, Mulliken received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement"). He retired in 1985. His wife died in 1975.

At the age of 90, Mulliken died of congestive heart failure at his daughter's home in [Arlington County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia "Arlington County, Virginia") on October 31, 1986. His body was returned to [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") for burial.
