**Artturi Ilmari Virtanen** (Finnish: [[ˈɑrtːuri ˈilmɑri ˈʋirtɑnen]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Finnish "Help:IPA/Finnish") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Fi-Artturi_Ilmari_Virtanen.ogg "File:Fi-Artturi Ilmari Virtanen.ogg"); 15 January 1895 – 11 November 1973) was a [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland "Finland") chemist and recipient of the 1945 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") "for his research and inventions in agricultural and nutrition chemistry, especially for his fodder preservation method".

He invented AIV silage which improved milk production and a method of preserving butter, the AIV salt, which led to increased Finnish butter exports.

## Personal life

Artturi Ilmari Virtanen was born on 15 January 1895, in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki "Helsinki"), [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland "Finland") . He is the son of Kaarlo Virtanen, a railway engine driver and Serafina Isotalo.

He completed his school education at the Classical Lyceum in [Viipuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viipuri "Viipuri"), Finland. He married the [botanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanist "Botanist") Lilja Moisio (1894-1972) in 1920 and had two sons with her.

In 1933, he bought a farm near Helsinki where he tested some of his scientific results in practice. He saw in the overproduction of food only a temporary phenomenon. He lived a simple life, he never had a car of his own, never smoked and never consumed alcohol. He died of pneumonia in November 1973, following a broken femur from a fall few weeks prior. He was buried at the [Hietaniemi Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hietaniemi_Cemetery "Hietaniemi Cemetery").

## Academics

Virtanen began his studies at the University of Helsinki in chemistry 1913 earning his Master and in 1918 his PhD in organic chemistry. In 1919 he started to work in the laboratories of [Valio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valio "Valio"), a large producer of [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_products "Dairy products") and became director of the laboratory in 1920. Feeling not fully qualified and following his interest in [botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany "Botany") and [zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology "Zoology") led him to further scientific education and so he left [Valio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valio "Valio") and studied at the [ETH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH "ETH"), the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster "University of Münster") and the [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University "Stockholm University") he studied [physical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry"), [soil chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_chemistry "Soil chemistry") and [microbiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiology "Microbiology"). In 1923 in Sweden he worked with [Hans von Euler-Chelpin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_von_Euler-Chelpin "Hans von Euler-Chelpin"), who was awarded with the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1929. Back in Finland he became lecturer at the University of Helsinki in 1924, known for his lectures on chemistry of life. He worked in the laboratory of the Butter Export Association, which became a laboratory of the university. In 1930 the Institute for Biochemistry was founded and Virtanen stayed there until his death in 1973. He became professor of biochemistry at the [Helsinki University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_University_of_Technology "Helsinki University of Technology") in 1931 and at the [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki "University of Helsinki") in 1939.

## Career

His research started with work on the [phosphorylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorylation "Phosphorylation") of [hexoses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexose "Hexose") in 1924. He was able to show that phosphorylation is the first step in many fermentation reactions, which was the foundation of the [Embden–Meyerhof pathway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embden–Meyerhof_pathway "Embden–Meyerhof pathway").

In 1925 his interests shifted to the nitrogen-fixing bacteria in the root nodules of [leguminous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabaceae "Fabaceae") plants. The improved methods of butter preservation, by adding [disodium phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disodium_phosphate "Disodium phosphate") to prevent acidic hydrolysis. This method was in use for several decades in Finland. His research from 1925 till 1932 included the invention of a fodder preservation method ([AIV fodder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIV_fodder "AIV fodder")). The method, patented in 1932, was basically a kind of [silage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silage "Silage") that improved the storage of green fodder, which is important during long winters. The process includes adding dilute hydrochloric or [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid "Sulfuric acid") to newly stored grain. Increased acidity stops harmful fermentation and has no adverse effect on the nutritive value of the fodder or the animals it is fed to. In 1945, Virtanen received the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in chemistry "for his research and inventions in agricultural and nutrition chemistry".

His later years studies included the development of partially synthetic cattle feeds. The nitrogen for the synthesis of [amino acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid "Amino acid") normally comes from [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") in the fodder. A special bacterial environment in the [rumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumen "Rumen") of cattle allows them to use [urea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urea "Urea") and [ammonium salts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonium_salt "Ammonium salt") as source for the nitrogen instead of plant proteins like [soybean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean "Soybean") or [meat and bone meal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat_and_bone_meal "Meat and bone meal"). He also headed the Valio Laboratory from 1921 to 1969.

## Awards and honours

The prestige conferred by the Nobel Prize brought Virtanen invitations, honorary doctorates and membership in foreign academies of science. He was a member of the Finnish, Norwegian, Swedish, Flemish, Bavarian, and Pontifical Academies of Science, and of the Swedish and Danish Academies of Engineering Sciences. He was an honorary member of learned societies in Finland, Sweden, Austria, Edinburgh, and the US, and received honorary degrees of the Universities of Lund, Paris, Giessen, and Helsinki, the Royal Technical College at Stockholm, and the Finland Institute of Technology.

The [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid "Asteroid") [1449 Virtanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1449_Virtanen "1449 Virtanen"), discovered by the renowned Finnish astronomer and physicist [Yrjö Väisälä](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yrjö_Väisälä "Yrjö Väisälä"), was named after him. The lunar crater [Virtanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtanen_(crater) "Virtanen (crater)") is also named after him.
