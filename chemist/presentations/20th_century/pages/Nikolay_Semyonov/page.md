**Nikolay Nikolayevich Semyonov [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society")**, sometimes **Semenov**, **Semionov** or **Semenoff** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Никола́й Никола́евич Семёнов; 15 April [[O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates "Old Style and New Style dates") 3 April] 1896 – 25 September 1986) was a Soviet [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist"). Semyonov was awarded the 1956 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his work on the mechanism of [chemical transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_transformation "Chemical transformation").

## Life and career

Semyonov was born in [Saratov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratov "Saratov") in April 1896, the son of Yelena Dmitrieva and Nikolai Aleksandrovich Semyonov. He graduated from the department of [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") of [Petrograd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University "Saint Petersburg State University") (1913–1917), where he was a student of [Abram Fyodorovich Ioffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abram_Ioffe "Abram Ioffe"). After graduating from Saint Petersburg State University, Semyonov worked as an assistant and lecturer in [Tomsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomsk "Tomsk") at the [Tomsk University Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomsk_University "Tomsk University"), where he published his first research paper in 1916. In 1918, he moved to [Samara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara,_Russia "Samara, Russia"), where he was enlisted into [Kolchak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Kolchak "Aleksandr Kolchak")'s [White Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement "White movement") in 1919 during the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War "Russian Civil War"), although he obtained a teaching deferment and managed to avoid service. In December of that same year, he was pressed into the radio service of the Red Army. After his discharge in the winter of 1920, Semyonov obtained work at universities in [Perm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_(city) "Perm (city)") and Tomsk. After the war, Semyonov returned to [Petrograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg "Saint Petersburg") and took charge of the [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") phenomena laboratory of the [Petrograd Physico-Technical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioffe_Institute "Ioffe Institute"), continuing to occasionally travel back to Tomsk as well as at times meeting with officials from [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow"). He also became the vice-director of the institute.

In 1921, he [married](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage "Marriage") [philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology "Philology") Maria Boreishe-Liverovsky (student of [Zhirmunsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Zhirmunsky "Viktor Zhirmunsky")). She died two years later. On September 15, 1924, Nikolay married Maria's niece, Natalia Nikolayevna Burtseva. They had two children, son Yuri and daughter Lyudmila.

During that difficult time, Semyonov, together with [Pyotr Kapitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Kapitsa "Pyotr Kapitsa"), discovered a way to measure the [magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field "Magnetic field") of an [atomic nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus "Atomic nucleus") (1922). Later the experimental setup was improved by [Otto Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Stern "Otto Stern") and [Walther Gerlach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Gerlach "Walther Gerlach") and became known as [Stern–Gerlach experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stern–Gerlach_experiment "Stern–Gerlach experiment").

In 1925, Semyonov, together with [Yakov Frenkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakov_Frenkel "Yakov Frenkel"), studied [kinetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_theory_of_gases "Kinetic theory of gases") of [condensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensation "Condensation") and [adsorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adsorption "Adsorption") of [vapors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vapor "Vapor"). In 1927, he studied [ionisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion "Ion") in [gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas "Gas") and published an important book, *Chemistry of the Electron*. In 1928, he, together with [Vladimir Fock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Fock "Vladimir Fock"), created a theory of thermal [disruptive discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disruptive_discharge "Disruptive discharge") of [dielectrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dielectric "Dielectric").

In 1927, Semyonov studied the ionization of gases, the chemistry of the electron. In 1928, he created the theory of the broken discharge of dielectrics with [Vladimir Fock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Fock "Vladimir Fock").

He lectured at the [Petrograd Polytechnical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrograd_Polytechnical_Institute "Petrograd Polytechnical Institute") and was appointed Professor in 1928. In 1931, he organized the Institute of Physical Chemistry of the [USSR Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") (which moved to [Chernogolovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernogolovka "Chernogolovka") in 1943) and became its first director. In 1932, he became a full member of the Soviet Academy of Sciences.

The ideas of Semyonov have been applied in the science of reaction and production of polymerization reactions. His ideas are also applied in catalysis studies in biological systems.

Semyonov married Natalya Nikolayevna Semyonov and together they both have a son and a daughter. Semyonov died on September 25, 1986, in Moscow, and was buried at the [Novodevichy Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novodevichy_Cemetery "Novodevichy Cemetery").

## Significant works

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/KustodiyevSemenov_Kapitsa.JPG/250px-KustodiyevSemenov_Kapitsa.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KustodiyevSemenov_Kapitsa.JPG)

Semyonov (right) and [Kapitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Leonidovich_Kapitsa "Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsa"), portrait by [Boris Kustodiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Kustodiev "Boris Kustodiev"), 1921.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/NN_Semyonov_2021_stamp_of_Russia.jpg/250px-NN_Semyonov_2021_stamp_of_Russia.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:NN_Semyonov_2021_stamp_of_Russia.jpg)

Semyonov on a 2021 stamp of Russia

Semyonov's outstanding work on the mechanism of chemical transformation includes an exhaustive analysis of the application of the [chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_reaction "Chain reaction") theory to varied reactions (1934–1954) and, more significantly, to [combustion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combustion "Combustion") processes. He proposed a theory of degenerate branching, which led to a better understanding of the phenomena associated with the induction periods of [oxidation processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidation_process "Oxidation process"). He spent most of his career focusing and developing the field of chemical chain reactions.

Semyonov wrote two important books outlining his work. *Chemical Kinetics and Chain Reactions* was published in 1934, with an English edition in 1935. It was the first book in the U.S.S.R. to develop a detailed theory of unbranched and branched chain reactions in chemistry. *Some Problems of Chemical Kinetics and Reactivity*, first published in 1954, was revised in 1958; there are also English, American, German, and Chinese editions. He is the only Soviet/Russian Chemistry Nobel Laureate, who received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") (together with Sir [Cyril N. Hinshelwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_N._Hinshelwood "Cyril N. Hinshelwood")) for his work in 1956.

Semyonov had long been a supporter of the Communist Party and the Soviet Union. After the [Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_the_Atomic_Scientists "Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists") accused the Soviet Union of heavy scientific censorship in 1953, he coauthored the Soviet response which denied all accusations. He is also noted as being the most famous signatory to a 1971 public letter from Soviet scientists to United States president [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon"), on displeasure in the murder trial of [Angela Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Davis "Angela Davis").

Semyonov trained Russian organometallic chemist [Alexander Shilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Shilov_(chemist) "Alexander Shilov (chemist)"), who discovered platinum catalyzed C-H activation.

## Legacy

- In 1990, the IHF RAS was named after N. N. Semyonov;
- In 1994, a memorial plaque was unveiled in honor of Nikolay Nikolayevich Semyonov on the main building of the St. Petersburg State Polytechnic University (29 Politechnicheskaya Street);
- The memorial plaque is installed on the facade of the Main building of the A.F. Ioffe Institute of Physics and Technology in St. Petersburg (26 Politechnicheskaya str.).
- On July 27, 1996, and one of the streets in the South-Western Administrative District of the city of Moscow on the territory of the [Yuzhnoye Butovo District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuzhnoye_Butovo_District "Yuzhnoye Butovo District") was given the name Academician Semyonov Street;
- In Saratov, one of the streets is named after Semyonov;
- In Tyumen, one of the streets is named after Semyonov;
- A memorial plaque is installed on the house in Moscow at 24 Frunzenskaya Embankment, where Semyonov spent his last years of life;
- The Presidium of the Russian Academy of Sciences has a Commission for the development of the scientific heritage of Academician N. N. Semyonov;
- Airliner [Airbus A321](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A321 "Airbus A321") (VQ-BOI) of the airline "[Aeroflot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroflot "Aeroflot")" "N. Semyonov";
- On April 5, 2019, in Moscow on the territory of [National Research Nuclear University "MEPhI"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Nuclear_University_"MEPhI" "National Research Nuclear University \"MEPhI\"") a monument was erected by the sculptor Alexandra Mironova
- At the beginning of April 2022, a monument to Academician Nikolai Semyonov was unveiled in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow") on Kosygin Street, house 4.
