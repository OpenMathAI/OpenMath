**Jean Frédéric Joliot-Curie** (French: [[fʁedeʁik ʒɔljo kyʁi]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); [né](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") **Joliot**; 19 March 1900 – 14 August 1958) was a French [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who received the 1935 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with his wife, [Irène Joliot-Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irène_Joliot-Curie "Irène Joliot-Curie"), for their discovery of [induced radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_radioactivity "Induced radioactivity"). They were the [second married couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize#Statistics "Nobel Prize"), after his parents-in-law, to win the Nobel Prize, adding to the [Curie family legacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize#Family_laureates "Nobel Prize") of four Nobel Prizes. Joliot-Curie and his wife also founded the [Orsay Faculty of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Saclay_Faculty_of_Sciences "Paris-Saclay Faculty of Sciences"), part of the [Paris-Saclay University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Saclay_University "Paris-Saclay University").

## Biography

### Early years

Born in Paris, France, Frédéric Joliot was a graduate of [ESPCI Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPCI_Paris "ESPCI Paris"). In 1925 he became an assistant to [Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie "Marie Curie"), at the [Radium Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curie_Institute_(Paris) "Curie Institute (Paris)"). He fell in love with her daughter [Irène Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irène_Joliot-Curie "Irène Joliot-Curie"), and soon after their marriage in 1926 they both changed their surnames to Joliot-Curie. At the insistence of Marie, Joliot-Curie obtained a second [baccalauréat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baccalauréat "Baccalauréat"), a bachelor's degree, and a doctorate in science, doing his thesis on the electrochemistry of radio-elements.

### Career

While a lecturer at the Paris Faculty of Science, he collaborated with his wife on research on the structure of the [atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom "Atom"), in particular on the projection, or recoil, of nuclei that had been struck by other particles, which was an essential step in the discovery of the [neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron "Neutron") by [James Chadwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Chadwick "James Chadwick") in 1932. In 1935 they were awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for their discovery of [Induced radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_radioactivity "Induced radioactivity"), resulting from the creation of short-lived [radioisotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioisotope "Radioisotope") by [nuclear transmutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_transmutation "Nuclear transmutation") from the bombardment of stable [nuclides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclide "Nuclide") such as boron, magnesium, and aluminium with [alpha particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_particle "Alpha particle").

In 1937, he left the Radium Institute to become a professor at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France"). In January 1939, he wrote a letter to his Soviet colleague, [Abram Ioffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abram_Ioffe "Abram Ioffe"), alerting him to the fact that German physicists had recently discovered nuclear fission of uranium bombarded by [neutrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron "Neutron"), releasing large amounts of energy. He went on to work on [nuclear chain reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_chain_reaction "Nuclear chain reaction") and the requirements for the successful construction of a [nuclear reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactor "Nuclear reactor") that uses controlled nuclear fission to generate energy. Joliot-Curie was mentioned in [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein")'s [1939 letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein–Szilárd_letter "Einstein–Szilárd letter") to [President Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Delano_Roosevelt "Franklin Delano Roosevelt") as one of the leading scientists on the course to nuclear chain reactions. The [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"), however, largely stalled Joliot's research, as did his subsequent post-war administrative duties.

Stamp issued by Romania commemorating Frédéric Joliot-Curie ("The 10th Anniversary of the World Peace Movement")

At the time of the [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism") [invasion in 1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_France "Battle of France"), Joliot-Curie managed to smuggle his working documents and materials to England with [Hans von Halban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_von_Halban "Hans von Halban"), [Moshe Feldenkrais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshé_Feldenkrais "Moshé Feldenkrais") and [Lew Kowarski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lew_Kowarski "Lew Kowarski"). During the French occupation, he took an active part in the [French Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance "French Resistance"). In June 1941, he took part in the founding of the [National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_(French_Resistance) "National Front (French Resistance)"), and became its president. In the spring of 1942, he joined the [French Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Communist_Party "French Communist Party") to become a member of its [Central Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Committee "Central Committee") in 1956. Collins and LaPierre in their book *Is Paris Burning?* note that during the [Paris uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Paris "Liberation of Paris") in August 1944, he served in the Prefecture of Police, manufacturing [Molotov cocktails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov_cocktail "Molotov cocktail") for his fellow insurgents, the Resistance's principal weapon against German tanks. The Prefecture was the scene of some of the most intense fighting during the uprising.

A team of scientists and intelligence officers from the allied [Alsos Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsos_Mission "Alsos Mission") later found Curie at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France"). He was sent to England to be interviewed and gave important information about the names and activities of German scientists.

### Post-war

He served as director of the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research "French National Centre for Scientific Research") (CNRS), and appointed by [Charles De Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle "Charles de Gaulle") in 1945, he became France's first [High Commissioner for Atomic Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissariat_à_l'Énergie_Atomique "Commissariat à l'Énergie Atomique"). In 1948, he oversaw the construction of the first [French atomic reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoé_(reactor) "Zoé (reactor)"). He ensured that the Japanese physicist [Toshiko Yuasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshiko_Yuasa "Toshiko Yuasa") who had undertaken her PhD under his supervision was able to return to France and her nuclear research at the CNRS after being forced to return to Japan towards the end of the war.

He and Irène visited Moscow in June 1945 for the two hundred and twentieth anniversary of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") and returned sympathizing with "hard-working Russians". His affiliation with the Communist party caused Irène to be detained on [Ellis Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellis_Island "Ellis Island") during her third trip to the US, coming to speak in support of Spanish refugees, at the [Joint Antifascist Refugee Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Anti-Fascist_Refugee_Committee "Joint Anti-Fascist Refugee Committee")'s invitation. A devoted [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism"), he was purged in 1950 and relieved of most of his duties, but retained his professorship at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France"). Joliot-Curie was one of the eleven signatories to the [Russell–Einstein Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell–Einstein_Manifesto "Russell–Einstein Manifesto") in 1955. On the death of his wife in 1956, he took over her position as Chair of Nuclear Physics at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris"). Frédéric's health was by that time declining, and he died in 1958 from liver disease, which, like the death of his wife, was said to be the result of overexposure to radiation.

## Honours and awards

Joliot-Curie was a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences") and of the Academy of Medicine and named a Commander of the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Légion_d'honneur "Légion d'honneur").

He was elected a [Foreign Member of England's Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1946 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1946") (ForMemRS) and a foreign member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1946.

Joliot-Curie appeared as himself in *Kampen om tungtvannet* (*La bataille de l'eau lourde* in French; 1948), a French–Norwegian semi-documentary film about sabotage of the [Vemork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vemork "Vemork") heavy water plant in Norway during World War II. His assistants Hans Halban and [Lev Kovarski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lew_Kowarski "Lew Kowarski") also appear. Joliot-Curie is shown lecturing about nuclear fission and chain reaction at the Collège de France.

He was the recipient of the first (1950) [Stalin Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalin_Peace_Prize "Stalin Peace Prize"), awarded on 6 April 1951 for his work as president of the [World Council of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Council_of_Peace "World Council of Peace"), which he carried out from 1950 until his death in 1958.

A street in [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia "Sofia"), Bulgaria, and the nearby [Joliot-Curie Metro Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joliot-Curie_Metro_Station "Joliot-Curie Metro Station") are named after Frédéric Joliot-Curie. Other streets or squares bearing his name can be found in the Rivière-des-Prairies borough of north [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal"), Canada; in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest "Bucharest"), [Târgu-Mureș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Târgu-Mureș "Târgu-Mureș"), and [Cluj-Napoca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj-Napoca "Cluj-Napoca"), Romania; in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw "Warsaw") and [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław "Wrocław"), Poland; and in [Poprad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poprad "Poprad"), Slovakia; in [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam "Potsdam"), [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_(Saale) "Halle (Saale)") and [Gera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gera "Gera"), Germany.

The crater [Joliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joliot_(crater) "Joliot (crater)") on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon") is named after him.

As part of the [dispute over the discovery and naming of
the transactinides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfermium_Wars "Transfermium Wars"), the name "joliotium" and symbol Jl were proposed for [element 102](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobelium "Nobelium") and later [element 105](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubnium "Dubnium").

## Personal life

The Joliot-Curies in the 1940s

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Famille_Ouang_avec_Familles_Joliot-Curie_et_Biquard.jpg/250px-Famille_Ouang_avec_Familles_Joliot-Curie_et_Biquard.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Famille_Ouang_avec_Familles_Joliot-Curie_et_Biquard.jpg)

The Joliot-Curies, Biquards and Wangs in summer 1941

Frédéric and Irène hyphenated their surnames to Joliot-Curie after they married on 4 October 1926 in Paris, France, although their daughter has said, "Many people used to name my parents Joliot-Curie, but they signed their scientific papers Irène Curie and Frédéric Joliot".

Joliot-Curie's children are [Hélène Langevin-Joliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hélène_Langevin-Joliot "Hélène Langevin-Joliot"), born in 1927, and her brother, [Pierre Joliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Joliot "Pierre Joliot"), born in 1932.

Frédéric Joliot-Curie devoted the last years of his life to the creation of the [Orsay Faculty of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Saclay_Faculty_of_Sciences "Paris-Saclay Faculty of Sciences") and a centre for [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics "Nuclear physics") at [Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orsay "Orsay"), now part of [Paris-Saclay University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Saclay_University "Paris-Saclay University").
