**Theodor Svedberg** (30 August 1884 – 25 February 1971) was a Swedish chemist and [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his research on [colloids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colloid "Colloid") and proteins using the [ultracentrifuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultracentrifuge "Ultracentrifuge"). Svedberg was active at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University") from the mid-1900s to late 1940s. While at Uppsala, Svedberg started as a [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent "Docent") before becoming the university's [physical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry") head in 1912. After leaving Uppsala in 1949, Svedberg was in charge of the Gustaf Werner Institute until 1967. Apart from his 1926 Nobel Prize, Svedberg was named a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") in 1944 and became part of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1945.

## Early life and education

Svedberg was born in [Valbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valbo "Valbo"), Sweden on 30 August 1884. He was the son of Augusta Alstermark and Elias Svedberg. Growing up, Svedberg enjoyed botany and other branches of science. While in [grammar school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar_school "Grammar school"), Svedberg conducted individual laboratorial research and performed [scientific demonstrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_demonstrations "Scientific demonstrations"). For his post-secondary education, Svedberg entered a chemistry program at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University") in the early mid 1900s. He earned his Bachelor of Arts degree in 1905, his master's degree in 1907, and in 1908, he earned his Ph.D.

## Career

While at Uppsala, Svedberg started his scientific career in 1905 as an assistant chemist with the university. After becoming a chemistry [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent "Docent") for Uppsala in 1907, he became the university's [physical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry") head in 1912. For his academic tenure, Svedberg remained with Uppsala until 1949. During the early 1920s, he also temporarily taught for the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin "University of Wisconsin"). After leaving Uppsala, Svedberg led the Gustaf Werner Institute from 1949 to 1967.

## Research

Svedberg's work with [colloids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colloid "Colloid") supported the theories of [Brownian motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownian_motion "Brownian motion") put forward by [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") and the Polish physicist [Marian Smoluchowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Smoluchowski "Marian Smoluchowski"). During this work, he developed the technique of [analytical ultracentrifugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_ultracentrifugation "Analytical ultracentrifugation"), and demonstrated its utility in distinguishing pure [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") one from another.

## Awards and honours

The unit [svedberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svedberg "Svedberg") (symbol **S**), a unit of time amounting to 10−13 s or 100 [fs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femtosecond "Femtosecond"), is named after him, as well as [The Svedberg Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Svedberg_Laboratory "The Svedberg Laboratory") in Uppsala.

Svedberg's candidacy for the Royal Society reads:

> "distinguished for his work in physical and colloid chemistry and the development of the ultracentrifuge"

Svedberg was elected an International Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1941. After becoming a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") in 1944, Svedberg was named to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1945. He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1948. From the 1910s to 1920s, Svedberg was awarded the [Björkénska priset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Björkénska_priset "Björkénska priset") three times from Uppsala University for his contributions to science in Sweden. From the [Franklin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Institute "Franklin Institute"), Svedberg was given the [Franklin Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Medal "Franklin Medal") in 1949 for his work with the ultracentrifuge.

## Death and personal life

On 25 February 1971, Svedberg died in [Kopparberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopparberg "Kopparberg"), Sweden. He was married four times and had a total of twelve children.His widow died in 2019. He was a Lutheran
