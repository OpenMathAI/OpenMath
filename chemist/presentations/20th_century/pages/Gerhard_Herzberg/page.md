**Gerhard Heinrich Friedrich Otto Julius Herzberg**, [PC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Privy_Council_for_Canada "King's Privy Council for Canada") [CC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Canada "Order of Canada") [FRSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Canada "Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (German: [[ˈɡeːɐ̯.haʁt ˈhɛʁt͡sˌbɛʁk]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Gerhard_Herzberg.ogg "File:De-Gerhard Herzberg.ogg"); December 25, 1904 – March 3, 1999) was a German-Canadian pioneering [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and [physical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemist "Physical chemist"), who won the [Nobel Prize for Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Chemistry "Nobel Prize for Chemistry") in 1971, "for his contributions to the knowledge of electronic structure and geometry of molecules, particularly free radicals". Herzberg's main work concerned atomic and molecular [spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy "Spectroscopy"). He is well known for using these techniques that determine the structures of [diatomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatomic "Diatomic") and [polyatomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyatomic "Polyatomic") molecules, including [free radicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_radicals "Free radicals") which are difficult to investigate in any other way, and for the chemical analysis of astronomical objects. Herzberg served as Chancellor of [Carleton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_University "Carleton University") in [Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa "Ottawa"), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada") from 1973 to 1980.

## Early life and family

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/DrGerhardHerzberg.jpg/250px-DrGerhardHerzberg.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:DrGerhardHerzberg.jpg)

Commemorative plaque at [College Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Building_(Saskatchewan) "College Building (Saskatchewan)"), [University of Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Saskatchewan "University of Saskatchewan")

Herzberg was born in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg "Hamburg"), Germany on December 25, 1904, to Albin H. Herzberg and Ella Biber. He had an older brother, Walter, who was born in January 1904. Herzberg started [Vorschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorschule?action=edit&redlink=1 "Vorschule") (pre-school) late, after contracting measles. Gerhard and his family were atheists and kept this fact hidden. His father died in 1914, at 43 years of age, after having suffered from [dropsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dropsy "Dropsy") and complications due to an earlier heart condition. Herzberg graduated Vorschule shortly after his father's death. He married [Luise Herzberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luise_Herzberg?action=edit&redlink=1 "Luise Herzberg") (née Oettinger), a spectroscopist and fellow researcher in 1929. (Luise Herzberg, died in 1971.)

## Nazi persecution and immigration to Canada

In 1933, the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party "Nazi Party") introduced a law banning men with Jewish wives from teaching at universities. Herzberg was working as a lecturer at the university in [Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt "Darmstadt"). His wife and fellow researcher, Luise Herzberg, was Jewish so they began making plans to leave Germany near the end of 1933. Leaving Germany was a daunting task as many barriers faced the thousands of Germans trying to flee Nazi persecution. However Herzberg had earlier worked with a visiting physical chemist named John Spinks, from the [University of Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Saskatchewan "University of Saskatchewan"). Spinks helped Herzberg get a job at the university in Saskatoon. When Herzberg and his wife left Germany in 1935, the Nazis let them take only the equivalent of $2.50 each and personal belongings.

## Education and career

Initially, Herzberg considered a career in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy"), but his application to the [Hamburg Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Observatory "Hamburg Observatory") was returned advising him not to pursue a career in the field without private financial support. After completing high school at the [Gelehrtenschule des Johanneums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelehrtenschule_des_Johanneums "Gelehrtenschule des Johanneums"), Herzberg continued his education at [Darmstadt University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt_University_of_Technology "Darmstadt University of Technology") with the help of a private scholarship. Herzberg completed his Dr.-Ing. degree under [Hans Rau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Rau_(physicist)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Hans Rau (physicist)") in 1928.

## Honours and awards

Herzberg's most significant award was the 1971 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"), which he was awarded "for his contributions to the knowledge of electronic structure and geometry of molecules, particularly free radicals". During the presentation speech, it was noted that at the time of the award, Herzberg was "generally considered to be the world's foremost molecular spectroscopist."

Herzberg was honoured with memberships or fellowships by a very large number of scientific societies, received many awards and honorary degrees in different countries. The [NSERC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSERC "NSERC") [Gerhard Herzberg Canada Gold Medal for Science and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Herzberg_Canada_Gold_Medal_for_Science_and_Engineering "Gerhard Herzberg Canada Gold Medal for Science and Engineering"), Canada's highest research award, was named in his honour in 2000. The [Canadian Association of Physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Association_of_Physicists "Canadian Association of Physicists") also has an annual award named in his honour. The [Herzberg Institute of Astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzberg_Institute_of_Astrophysics "Herzberg Institute of Astrophysics") is named for him. He was made a member of the [International Academy of Quantum Molecular Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_Quantum_Molecular_Science "International Academy of Quantum Molecular Science"). Asteroid [3316 Herzberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3316_Herzberg "3316 Herzberg") is named after him. In 1964 he was awarded the [Frederic Ives Medal](https://www.optica.org/get_involved/awards_and_honors/awards/award_descriptions/ivesquinn/) by the [OSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_Society_of_America "Optical Society of America"). He was later named an Honorary Member of the Society. At Carleton University, there is a building named after him that belongs to the Physics and Mathematics/Statistics Departments, Herzberg Laboratories. Herzberg was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1951](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1951 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1951").

The main building of [John Abbott College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Abbott_College "John Abbott College") in [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal") is named after him. Carleton University named the Herzberg Laboratories building after him. A public park in the [College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Park,_Saskatoon "College Park, Saskatoon") neighbourhood of [Saskatoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatoon "Saskatoon") also bears his name.

## Books and publications

Herzberg authored some classic works in the field of spectroscopy, including *Atomic Spectra and Atomic Structure* and the encyclopaedic four volume work: *Molecular Spectra and Molecular Structure*, which is often called the spectroscopist's bible. The three volumes of Molecular Spectra and Molecular Structure were re-issued by Krieger in 1989, including extensive new footnotes by Herzberg. Volume IV of the series, "Constants of diatomic molecules" is purely a reference work, a compendium of known spectroscopic constants (and therefore a bibliography of molecular spectroscopy) of diatomic molecules up until 1978.
