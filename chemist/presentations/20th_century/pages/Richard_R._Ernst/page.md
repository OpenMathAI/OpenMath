**Richard Robert Ernst** (14 August 1933 – 4 June 2021) was a Swiss [physical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemist "Physical chemist") and [Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") laureate.

Ernst was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1991 for his contributions towards the development of [Fourier transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_transform "Fourier transform") [nuclear magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance") (NMR) [spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy "Spectroscopy") while at [Varian Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varian_Associates "Varian Associates") and [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich"). These underpin applications to both to chemistry with [NMR spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance_spectroscopy "Nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy") and to medicine with [magnetic resonance imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging "Magnetic resonance imaging") (MRI).

He humbly referred to himself as a "tool-maker" rather than a scientist.

## Early life

Ernst was born in [Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterthur "Winterthur"), Switzerland on 14 August 1933 to Robert Ernst and Irma Ernst-Brunner. He was the oldest of three children of Irma Brunner and Robert Ernst. He grew up in a house built in 1898 by his grandfather, who was a merchant. During his childhood, he was interested in music, playing the [violoncello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cello "Cello") and even considering a career as a musical composer. At 13-years old, Ernst stumbled upon a box of chemicals belonging to his late uncle, a metallurgical engineer. Young Ernst was excited by what he found, and set about trying all conceivable reactions, some of which resulted in explosions that terrified his parents.

## Education

He enrolled in the [Eidgenössische Technische Hochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich") (ETH) in Zurich to study chemistry and received his diploma in 1957 as a “Diplomierter Ingenieur Chemiker''. He was disappointed in the course content, so conducted further research and taught himself quantum mechanics and thermodynamics in his spare time. After a break to complete his military service, Ernst earned his Ph.D. in [physical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry") in 1962 from ETH Zurich. His dissertation was on [nuclear magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance") in the field of physical chemistry.

## Career

Ernst entered [Varian Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varian_Associates "Varian Associates") as a scientist in 1963 and invented Fourier transform NMR, noise decoupling, and a number of other methods. He returned to ETH Zurich in 1968 and became a lecturer. His career developed into assistant professor in 1970 and associate professor in 1972. From 1976, Richard R. Ernst was Full Professor of Physical Chemistry.

Ernst led a research group dedicated to magnetic resonance spectroscopy, and was the director of the Physical Chemistry Laboratory at the ETH Zurich. He developed two-dimensional NMR and several novel pulse techniques. He retired in 1998. He participated in the development of medical magnetic resonance tomography, as well as the NMR structure determination of [biopolymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biopolymer "Biopolymer") in solution collaborating with Professor [Kurt Wüthrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Wüthrich "Kurt Wüthrich"). He also participated in the study of intra-molecular dynamics.

## Awards and honours

[![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Science_and_Peace_-_Richard_Ernst.webm/250px--Science_and_Peace_-_Richard_Ernst.webm.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser)](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/65/Science_and_Peace_-_Richard_Ernst.webm?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=job&utm_content=original)

Richard R. Ernst, UNESCO 2011

Ernst was a foreign fellow of the Estonian Academy of Sciences (elected 2002), the [US National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_National_Academy_of_Sciences "US National Academy of Sciences"), the [Royal Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society"), [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London"), the [German National Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_National_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German National Academy of Sciences Leopoldina"), the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences"), the [Korean Academy of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Academy_of_Science_and_Technology "Korean Academy of Science and Technology") and [Bangladesh Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Academy_of_Sciences "Bangladesh Academy of Sciences"). He was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1993 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1993"). He was awarded the [John Gamble Kirkwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gamble_Kirkwood "John Gamble Kirkwood") Medal in 1989.

In 1991, Ernst was on an aeroplane flying over the Atlantic when he discovered he had been awarded The Nobel Prize in Chemistry. He was invited into the cockpit, where he was given a radio to talk to the Nobel committee. Here they told him he was being honoured "for his contributions to the development of the methodology of high resolution nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy".

Ernst was a member of the World Knowledge Dialogue Scientific Board. He was awarded the [Marcel Benoist Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Benoist_Prize "Marcel Benoist Prize") in 1986, the [Wolf Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Chemistry "Wolf Prize in Chemistry") in 1991, and [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in 1991. He was also awarded the [Tadeus Reichstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Reichstein "Tadeusz Reichstein") Medal in 2000 and the [Order of the Star of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Star_of_Romania "Order of the Star of Romania") in 2004. He also held [Honorary Doctorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree "Honorary degree") from the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich "Technical University of Munich"), [EPF Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne "École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne"), [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich"), [University Antwerpen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Antwerp "University of Antwerp"), [Babes-Bolyai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babeș-Bolyai_University "Babeș-Bolyai University"), and [University Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montpellier "University of Montpellier").

The 2009 [Bel Air Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel_Air_Film_Festival "Bel Air Film Festival") featured the [world premiere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_premiere "World premiere") of a [documentary film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_film "Documentary film") on Ernst *Science Plus Dharma Equals Social Responsibility*. Produced by [Carlo Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Burton "Carlo Burton"), the film takes place in Ernst's hometown in Switzerland. In 2022, another movie about Richard R. Ernst premiered at the Cameo cinema in Winterthur, produced by Lukas Schwarzenbacher and Susanne Schmid. The documentary contains a retrospective of Richard R. Ernsts life, which was filmed only a few months before his death.

## Personal life

Ernst was married to Magdalena (Kielholz) until his death. Together, they had three children: Anna Magdalena, Katharina Elisabeth and Hans-Martin Walter. Besides toiling with his work, Ernst also enjoyed music and art, specifically Tibetan scroll art. Using scientific techniques, Ernst would research the pigments on the scrolls to learn about their geographic origin and age.

Ernst died on 4 June 2021 in Winterthur at the age of 87.
