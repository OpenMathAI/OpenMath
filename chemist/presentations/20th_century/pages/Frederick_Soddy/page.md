**Frederick Soddy** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") (2 September 1877 – 22 September 1956) was an English [radiochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiochemistry "Radiochemistry") who explained, with [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford "Ernest Rutherford"), that [radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity "Radioactivity") is due to the [transmutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_transmutation "Nuclear transmutation") of [elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element "Chemical element"), now known to involve [nuclear reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactions "Nuclear reactions"). He also proved the existence of [isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope "Isotope") of certain radioactive elements. In 1921, he received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Chemistry "List of Nobel laureates in Chemistry") "for his contributions to our knowledge of the chemistry of radioactive substances, and his investigations into the origin and nature of isotopes". Soddy was a [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath "Polymath") who mastered [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"), [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics "Nuclear physics"), [statistical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics"), [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance "Finance"), and [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics").

## Biography

Soddy was born at 6 Bolton Road, [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne "Eastbourne"), England, the son of Benjamin Soddy, corn merchant, and his wife Hannah Green. He went to school at [Eastbourne College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne_College "Eastbourne College"), before going on to study at [University College of Wales at Aberystwyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth_University "Aberystwyth University") and at [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford "Merton College, Oxford"), where he graduated in 1898 with first class honours in chemistry. He was a researcher at [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") from 1898 to 1900.

## Scientific career

In 1900, he became a demonstrator in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University "McGill University") in [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal"), [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec "Quebec"), where he worked with [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford "Ernest Rutherford") on [radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity "Radioactivity").
He and Rutherford realized that the anomalous behaviour of radioactive elements was because they [decayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_decay "Radioactive decay") into other elements.
This decay also produced [alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_ray "Alpha ray"), [beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_ray "Beta ray"), and [gamma radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_ray "Gamma ray"). When radioactivity was first discovered, no one was sure what the cause was. It needed careful work by Soddy and Rutherford to prove that atomic [transmutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_transmutation "Nuclear transmutation") was in fact occurring.

In 1903, with Sir [William Ramsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ramsay "William Ramsay") at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London"), Soddy showed that the decay of [radium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radium "Radium") produced [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium "Helium") gas. In the experiment a sample of radium was enclosed in a thin-walled glass envelope sited within an evacuated glass bulb. After leaving the experiment running for a long period of time, a spectral analysis of the contents of the former evacuated space revealed the presence of helium. Later in 1907, Rutherford and [Thomas Royds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Royds "Thomas Royds") showed that the helium was first formed as positively charged nuclei of helium (He2+) which were identical to [alpha particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_particle "Alpha particle"), which could pass through the thin glass wall but were contained within the surrounding glass envelope.

From 1904 to 1914, Soddy was a lecturer at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow "University of Glasgow"). [Ruth Pirret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Pirret "Ruth Pirret") worked as his research assistant during this time. In May 1910 Soddy was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society"). In 1914 he was appointed to a chair at the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen "University of Aberdeen"), where he worked on research related to [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I").

In 1913, Soddy showed that an atom moves lower in [atomic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_number "Atomic number") by two places on alpha emission, higher by one place on beta emission. This was discovered at about the same time by [Kazimierz Fajans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Fajans "Kazimierz Fajans"), and is known as the [radioactive displacement law of Fajans and Soddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_displacement_law_of_Fajans_and_Soddy "Radioactive displacement law of Fajans and Soddy"), a fundamental step toward understanding the relationships among families of radioactive elements. In 1913 Soddy also described the phenomenon in which a radioactive element may have more than one [atomic mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_mass "Atomic mass") though the chemical properties are identical. He named this concept [isotope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope "Isotope") meaning "same place". The word was initially suggested to him by [Margaret Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Todd_(doctor) "Margaret Todd (doctor)"). Later, [J. J. Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Thomson "J. J. Thomson") showed that non-radioactive elements can also have multiple isotopes.

The work that Soddy and his research assistant [Ada Hitchins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Hitchins "Ada Hitchins") did at Glasgow and Aberdeen showed that [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium "Uranium") decays to [radium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radium "Radium").

Soddy published [*The Interpretation of Radium*](https://archive.org/details/interpretationof00sodd "iarchive:interpretationof00sodd") (1909) and *Atomic Transmutation* (1953).

In 1918, working with the Scottish scientist [John Arnold Cranston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Arnold_Cranston "John Arnold Cranston"), he announced the discovery of an isotope of the element later named [protactinium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protactinium "Protactinium"). This slightly post-dated its discovery by the Germans [Lise Meitner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lise_Meitner "Lise Meitner") and [Otto Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn "Otto Hahn"); however, it is said their discovery was actually made in 1915 but its announcement was delayed due to Cranston's notes being locked away whilst on active service in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War").

In 1919, he moved to the University of Oxford as the first [Dr. Lee's Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_Lee's_Professorships "Dr Lee's Professorships") of Chemistry, where, in the period up till 1936, he reorganized the laboratories and the syllabus in chemistry. He received the 1921 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his research in radioactive decay and particularly for his formulation of the theory of isotopes.

In Soddy's 1922 paper "Radioactivity and the Evolution of the World" there is a section on Soddy's belief that knowledge in the form of myths had been passed down to the present day from an ancient, advanced civilisation.

His work and essays popularising the new understanding of radioactivity was the main inspiration for [H. G. Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._G._Wells "H. G. Wells")'s *[The World Set Free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Set_Free "The World Set Free")* (1914), which features atomic bombs dropped from biplanes in a war set many years in the future. Wells's novel is also known as *The Last War* and imagines a peaceful world emerging from the chaos. In *[Wealth, Virtual Wealth and Debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth,_Virtual_Wealth_and_Debt "Wealth, Virtual Wealth and Debt")* Soddy praises Wells's *The World Set Free*. He also says that radioactive processes probably power the stars.

In four books written from 1921 to 1934, Soddy carried on a "campaign for a radical restructuring of global monetary relationships", offering a perspective on economics rooted in physics – the [laws of thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_thermodynamics "Laws of thermodynamics"), in particular – and was "roundly dismissed as a crank". While most of his proposals – "to abandon the [gold standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_standard "Gold standard"), let international [exchange rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_rate "Exchange rate") float, use federal [surpluses and deficits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deficit_spending "Deficit spending") as [macroeconomic policy tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomic_policy_instruments "Macroeconomic policy instruments") that could counter [cyclical trends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_cycle "Business cycle"), and establish bureaus of [economic statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_statistics "Economic statistics") (including a [consumer price index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_price_index "Consumer price index")) in order to facilitate this effort" – are now conventional practice, his critique of [fractional-reserve banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional-reserve_banking "Fractional-reserve banking") still "remains outside the bounds of conventional wisdom" although a recent paper by the [IMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund") reinvigorated his proposals. Soddy wrote that financial debts grew exponentially at compound interest but the real economy was based on exhaustible stocks of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels "Fossil fuels"). Energy obtained from the fossil fuels could not be used again. This criticism of [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth "Economic growth") is echoed by his intellectual heirs in the emergent field of [ecological economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_economics "Ecological economics").

*[The New Palgrave Dictionary of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Palgrave_Dictionary_of_Economics "The New Palgrave Dictionary of Economics")*, an influential reference text in economics, recognized Soddy as a "reformer" for his works on monetary reforms.

## Political views

In *[Wealth, Virtual Wealth and Debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth,_Virtual_Wealth_and_Debt "Wealth, Virtual Wealth and Debt"),* Soddy cited the *[Protocols of the Learned Elders of Zion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocols_of_the_Learned_Elders_of_Zion "Protocols of the Learned Elders of Zion")*, which had been widely disseminated by [Henry Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ford "Henry Ford") in the United States, as evidence that the belief in a "financial conspiracy to enslave the world" was widespread at the time. He further wrote that "conscious conspiracy or not ... a corrupt monetary system strikes at the very life of the nation". Later in life he published a pamphlet *Abolish Private Money, or Drown in Debt* (1939).

The influence of his writing can be gauged, for example, in this quote from [Ezra Pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezra_Pound "Ezra Pound"):

> Professor Frederick Soddy states that the Gold Standard monetary system has wrecked a scientific age! ... The world's bankers... have not been content to take their share of modern wealth production – great as it has been – but they have refused to allow the masses of mankind to receive theirs.

Though some activists have accused Soddy of anti-Semitism, some argue that among Soddy's friends and students were some Jews who held positive views of him.

## Descartes' theorem

He rediscovered [Descartes' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes'_theorem "Descartes' theorem") in 1936 and published it as a poem, "The Kiss Precise", quoted at [Problem of Apollonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_of_Apollonius#kiss_precise "Problem of Apollonius"). The [kissing circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osculating_circle "Osculating circle") in this problem are sometimes known as **Soddy circles**.

## Honours and awards

He received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1921 and the same year was elected member of the [International Atomic Weights Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Isotopic_Abundances_and_Atomic_Weights "Commission on Isotopic Abundances and Atomic Weights"). A small [crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soddy_(crater) "Soddy (crater)") on the [far side of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_side_of_the_Moon "Far side of the Moon") as well as the radioactive uranium mineral [soddyite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soddyite "Soddyite") are named after him. The author [H. G. Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._G._Wells "H. G. Wells") dedicated his novel *[The World Set Free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Set_Free "The World Set Free")* to Soddy's *Interpretation of Radium* (1909).

## Personal life

In 1908, Soddy married [Winifred Moller Beilby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winifred_Moller_Beilby?action=edit&redlink=1 "Winifred Moller Beilby") (1885–1936), the daughter of industrial chemist Sir [George Beilby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Beilby "George Beilby") and Lady Emma Bielby, a philanthropist to women's causes. The couple worked together and co-published a paper in 1910 on the absorption of gamma rays from radium. He died in [Brighton, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton,_England "Brighton, England") in 1956, twenty days after his 79th birthday.
