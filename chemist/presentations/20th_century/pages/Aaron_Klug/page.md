**Sir Aaron Klug** (11 August 1926 – 20 November 2018) was a British [biophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysicist "Biophysicist") and chemist. He was a winner of the 1982 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his development of [crystallographic electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_crystallography "Electron crystallography") and his structural elucidation of biologically important [nucleic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleic_acid "Nucleic acid")-[protein complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_complexes "Protein complexes").

## Early life and education

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/71/Prince_Claus%2C_A._Klug_and_wife.jpg/330px-Prince_Claus%2C_A._Klug_and_wife.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Prince_Claus,_A._Klug_and_wife.jpg)

From right to left: [Prince Claus of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Claus_of_the_Netherlands "Prince Claus of the Netherlands"), Aaron Klug and his wife Liebe Bobrow, 1979

Klug was born in [Želva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Želva "Želva"), in Lithuania, to [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Jews "Lithuanian Jews") parents Lazar, a cattleman, and Bella (née Silin) Klug, with whom he emigrated to South Africa at the age of two. He was educated at [Durban High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durban_High_School "Durban High School"). [Paul de Kruif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_de_Kruif "Paul de Kruif")'s 1926 book,  *Microbe Hunters*, aroused his interest in microbiology.

Klug was part of the [Hashomer Hatzair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashomer_Hatzair "Hashomer Hatzair") Jewish Zionist youth movement in South Africa.

He started to study microbiology, but then moved into physics and maths, graduating with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree at the [University of the Witwatersrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Witwatersrand "University of the Witwatersrand"), in Johannesburg. He studied physics under [Reginald W. James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_W._James "Reginald W. James") and obtained his [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") degree at the [University of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town "University of Cape Town"). He was awarded an [1851 Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1851_Research_Fellowship "1851 Research Fellowship") from the [Royal Commission for the Exhibition of 1851](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_for_the_Exhibition_of_1851 "Royal Commission for the Exhibition of 1851"), which enabled him to move to England, completing his PhD in research physics at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge") in 1953.

## Career and research

Following his PhD, Klug moved to [Birkbeck College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkbeck,_University_of_London "Birkbeck, University of London") in the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London "University of London") in late 1953, and started working with chemist and X-ray crystallographer [Rosalind Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalind_Franklin "Rosalind Franklin") in the lab of crystallographer [John Bernal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._D._Bernal "J. D. Bernal"). This experience aroused a lifelong interest in the study of viruses, and during his time there he made discoveries in the structure of the [tobacco mosaic virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_mosaic_virus "Tobacco mosaic virus"). In 1962 he moved to the newly built [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(United_Kingdom) "Medical Research Council (United Kingdom)") (MRC) [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") (LMB) in Cambridge. Over the following decade Klug used methods from [X-ray diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_diffraction "X-ray diffraction"), microscopy and structural modelling to develop crystallographic electron microscopy in which a sequence of two-dimensional images of crystals taken from different angles are combined to produce three-dimensional images of the target. He studied the structure of [transfer RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_RNA "Transfer RNA"), and found what is known as [zinc fingers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc_fingers "Zinc fingers") as well as the neurofibrils in Alzheimer's disease.

Also in 1962, Klug became a Fellow of [Peterhouse, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge "Peterhouse, Cambridge"). He was later made an Honorary Fellow of the college.

Between 1986 and 1996, Klug was director of the LMB. He served on the Advisory Council for the [Campaign for Science and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_Science_and_Engineering "Campaign for Science and Engineering"). He also served on the Board of Scientific Governors at [The Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scripps_Research_Institute "The Scripps Research Institute").
He and [Dai Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Allan_Rees "David Allan Rees") approached the [Wellcome Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellcome_Trust "Wellcome Trust") to found the [Wellcome Sanger Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellcome_Sanger_Institute "Wellcome Sanger Institute"), which was a key player in the [Human Genome Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project "Human Genome Project").

### Awards and honours

Klug was awarded the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in 1981. He was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") by [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II") in 1988. In 1969 he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1969 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1969"), the oldest national [scientific institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") in the world. He was elected its [President (PRS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Royal_Society "List of presidents of the Royal Society") from 1995 to 2000. He was appointed to the [Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit "Order of Merit") in 1995 – as is customary for Presidents of the Royal Society. His certificate of election to the Royal Society reads:

> Mathematical physicist and crystallographer distinguished for his contributions to molecular biology, especially the structure of viruses. Development of a theory of simultaneous temperature and phase changes in steels led him to apply related mathematical methods to the problem of diffusion and chemical reactions of gases in thin layers of haemoglobin solutions and in red blood cells. Then the late [Rosalind Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalind_Franklin "Rosalind Franklin") introduced him to the x-ray study of tobacco mosaic virus to which he contributed by his application and further development of [Cochran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cochran_(physicist) "William Cochran (physicist)") and [Crick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Crick "Francis Crick")'s theory of diffraction from helical chain molecules. Klug's most important work is concerned with the structure of spherical viruses. Together with [D. Caspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Caspar "Donald Caspar") he developed a general theory of spherical shells built up of a regular array of asymmetric particles. Klug and his collaborators verified the theory by x-ray and electron microscope studies, thereby revealing new and hitherto unsuspected features of virus structure.

Klug was a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society")

In 2000, Klug received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement"). In 2005, he was awarded South Africa's [Order of Mapungubwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Mapungubwe "Order of Mapungubwe") (gold) for exceptional achievements in medical science. He was elected a [Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Academy_of_Medical_Sciences "Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences") (FMedSci), also in 2005.

In 2013, Israel's [Ben-Gurion University of the Negev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben-Gurion_University_of_the_Negev "Ben-Gurion University of the Negev") dedicated their centre for structural biology in Klug's name, *Aaron Klug Integrated Centre for Biomolecular Structure*. He, his family and the then-British Ambassador to Israel [Matthew Gould](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Gould "Matthew Gould"), were in attendance. Klug was associated with the university and the town of [Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be'er_Sheva "Be'er Sheva"), having visited them numerous times.

## Personal life

Klug married Liebe Bobrow in 1948; they had two sons, one of whom predeceased them in 2000. He died on 20 November 2018 in Cambridge.

Though Klug had faced discrimination in South Africa, he remained religious and according to [Sydney Brenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Brenner "Sydney Brenner"), he became more religious in his older age.
