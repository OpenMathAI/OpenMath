**Paul Karrer** (21 April 1889 – 18 June 1971) was a Swiss [organic chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemist "Organic chemist") best known for his research on [vitamins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin "Vitamin"). He and British chemist [Norman Haworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Haworth "Norman Haworth") won the [Nobel Prize for Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Chemistry "Nobel Prize for Chemistry") in 1937.

## Biography

### Early years

Karrer was born in Moscow, Russia to Paul Karrer and Julie Lerch, both Swiss nationals. In 1892 Karrer's family returned to Switzerland where he was educated at [Wildegg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildegg?action=edit&redlink=1 "Wildegg") and at the [Old Cantonal School Aarau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Cantonal_School_Aarau "Old Cantonal School Aarau") where he matriculated in 1908. He studied [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich") under [Alfred Werner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Werner "Alfred Werner") and after gaining his PhD in 1911, he spent a further year as an assistant in the Chemical Institute. He then took a post as a chemist with [Paul Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrlich "Paul Ehrlich") at the [Georg Speyer Haus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Speyer_Haus?action=edit&redlink=1 "Georg Speyer Haus"), [Frankfurt-am-Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt-am-Main "Frankfurt-am-Main"). In 1919 he became Professor of Chemistry and Director of the Chemical Institute.

### Research

Karrer's early research concerned complex metal compounds but his most important work has concerned [plant pigments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_pigment "Plant pigment"), particularly the yellow [carotenoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carotenoid "Carotenoid"). He elucidated their chemical structure and showed that some of these substances are transformed in the body into [vitamin A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_A "Vitamin A"). His work led to the establishment of the correct constitutional formula for [β-carotene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta-carotene "Beta-carotene"), the chief precursor of vitamin A; the first time that the structure of a vitamin or provitamin had been established. [George Wald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Wald "George Wald") worked briefly in Karrer's lab while studying the role of vitamin A in the [retina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retina "Retina"). Later, Karrer confirmed the structure of [ascorbic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascorbic_acid "Ascorbic acid") ([vitamin C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_C "Vitamin C")) and extended his researches into the vitamin B2 and E. His important contributions to the chemistry of the [flavins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavin_group "Flavin group") led to the identification of [lactoflavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactoflavin "Lactoflavin") as part of the complex originally thought to be [vitamin B2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_B2 "Vitamin B2").

Karrer published many papers and received many honours and awards, including the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in 1937. His textbook *Lehrbuch der Organischen Chemie (Textbook of Organic Chemistry)* was published in 1927, went through thirteen editions, and was published in seven languages.

### Personal life

Karrer married Helena Froelich in 1914 and had three sons, one of whom died in infancy. He died on 18 June 1971, at the age of 82 in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich "Zürich"). His wife died in 1972.

## Legacy

The prestigious [Paul Karrer Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Karrer_Gold_Medal "Paul Karrer Gold Medal") and lecture were established in his honour in 1959 by a group of leading companies such as CIBA AG, J.R. Geigy, F. Hoffmann-La Roche & Co. AG, Sandoz AG, Société des Produits Nestlé AG and Dr. A. Wander AG. It is awarded annually or biannually to an outstanding chemist who delivers a lecture at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich").

The Paul Karrer Lecture Foundation is based at the Chemistry Institute of the University of Zurich at Rämistrasse 71, in Zürich.
