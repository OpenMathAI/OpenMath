**Paul Sabatier** (French: [[sabatje]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 5 November 1854 – 14 August 1941) was a French [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist"), born in [Carcassonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcassonne "Carcassonne"). In 1912, Sabatier was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") along with [Victor Grignard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Grignard "Victor Grignard"). Sabatier was honoured for his work improving the [hydrogenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogenation "Hydrogenation") of organic species in the presence of metals.

## Education

Sabatier studied at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure "École normale supérieure"), starting in 1874. Three years later, he graduated at the top of his class. In 1880, he was awarded a [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science "Doctor of Science") degree from the [College de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France").

In 1883 Sabatier succeeded [Édouard Filhol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Édouard_Filhol "Édouard Filhol") at the Faculty of Science, and began a long collaboration with [Jean-Baptiste Senderens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Senderens "Jean-Baptiste Senderens"), so close that it was impossible to distinguish the work of either man.
They jointly published 34 notes in the *Accounts of the Academy of Science*, 11 memoirs in the *Bulletin of the French Chemical Society* and 2 joint memoirs to the *Annals of Chemistry and Physics*.

After the discovery of [nickel tetracarbonyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel_tetracarbonyl "Nickel tetracarbonyl") in 1890 they tried to synthesize similar compound with nitrogen oxides, but only discovered different types of oxidation. As late as 1912, Sabatier believed that it's possible to get "true nitro metals" with [dinitrogen tetroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinitrogen_tetroxide "Dinitrogen tetroxide"), but it was later proven that these were not real chemical compounds but just metal oxides with [nitrogen dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_dioxide "Nitrogen dioxide") physically absorbed on them.

In 1896 [Henri Moissan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Moissan "Henri Moissan") and [Charles Moureu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Moureu "Charles Moureu") discovered that [acetylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetylene "Acetylene") reacts with some [transition metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_metal "Transition metal"). Bearing in mind Prosper de Wilde (1835-1916) hydrogenated acetylene on [platinum black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum_black "Platinum black") in 1874, Sabatier and Senderens picked up the topic and continued investigations in the area.

The [methanation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanation "Methanation") reactions of COx were first discovered by Sabatier and Senderens in 1902.
Sabatier and Senderen shared the Academy of Science's [Jecker Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jecker_Prize "Jecker Prize") in 1905 for their discovery of the Sabatier–Senderens Process.

After 1905–06 Senderens and Sabatier published few joint works, perhaps due to the classic problem of recognition of the merit of contributions to joint work.
Sabatier taught science classes most of his life before he became Dean of the Faculty of Science at the [University of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toulouse "University of Toulouse") in 1905.

## Research

Sabatier's earliest research concerned the [thermochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermochemistry "Thermochemistry") of [sulfur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur "Sulfur") and metallic [sulfates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfate "Sulfate"), the subject for the thesis leading to his doctorate. In [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse "Toulouse"), he continued his [physical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") and chemical investigations to [sulfides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfide "Sulfide"), [chlorides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloride "Chloride"), [chromates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromate_ion "Chromate ion") and [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper") compounds. He also studied the [oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxide "Oxide") of [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen "Nitrogen") and nitrosodisulfonic acid and its [salts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_(chemistry) "Salt (chemistry)") and carried out fundamental research on [partition coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_coefficient "Partition coefficient") and [absorption spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorption_spectrum "Absorption spectrum"). Sabatier greatly facilitated the industrial use of [hydrogenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogenation "Hydrogenation"). In 1897, building on the recent biochemical work of the American chemist, [James F. Boyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_F._Boyce "James F. Boyce"), he discovered that the introduction of a trace amount of [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel "Nickel") (as a catalyst) facilitated the addition of hydrogen to molecules of most carbon compounds.

### Sabatier reaction

Sabatier is best known for the [Sabatier process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabatier_process "Sabatier process") and his works such as *La Catalyse en Chimie Organique* (Catalysis in organic chemistry) which was published in 1913. He won the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") jointly with fellow Frenchman [Victor Grignard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Grignard "Victor Grignard") in 1912.

The reduction of [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide "Carbon dioxide") using [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen "Hydrogen") at high temperature and pressure is another use of nickel [catalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalyst "Catalyst") to produce [methane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane "Methane").

### Sabatier principle

He is also known for the [Sabatier principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabatier_principle "Sabatier principle") of catalysis.

## Personal life

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Paul_Sabatier_Office_Toulouse_2012.jpg/250px-Paul_Sabatier_Office_Toulouse_2012.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Paul_Sabatier_Office_Toulouse_2012.jpg)

Sabatier's office desk and collection of chemicals at the University of Toulouse

Sabatier was married and had four daughters, one of whom wed the Italian chemist Emilio Pomilio.

The [Paul Sabatier University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Sabatier_University "Paul Sabatier University") in [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse "Toulouse"), France was named in honour of Paul Sabatier, as is one of [Carcassonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcassonne "Carcassonne")'s high schools. Paul Sabatier was a co-founder of the [Annales de la Faculté des Sciences de Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annales_de_la_Faculté_des_Sciences_de_Toulouse "Annales de la Faculté des Sciences de Toulouse"), together with the mathematician [Thomas Joannes Stieltjes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Joannes_Stieltjes "Thomas Joannes Stieltjes").

Sabatier died on 14 August, 1941 in [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse "Toulouse") at the age of 86. He was Catholic
