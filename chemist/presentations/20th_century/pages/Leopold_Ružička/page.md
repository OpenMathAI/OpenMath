**Leopold Ružička** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") (pronounced [[rǔʒitʃka]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Serbo-Croatian "Help:IPA/Serbo-Croatian"); born **Lavoslav Stjepan Ružička**; 13 September 1887 – 26 September 1976) was a Croatian-Swiss scientist and joint winner of the 1939 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") "for his work on [polymethylenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymethylenes "Polymethylenes") and higher [terpenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terpenes "Terpenes")" "including the first [chemical synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_synthesis "Chemical synthesis") of [male sex hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Male_sex_hormones "Male sex hormones")." He worked most of his life in Switzerland, and received eight doctorates *[honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree "Honorary degree")* in science, medicine, and law; seven prizes and medals; and twenty-four honorary memberships in chemical, [biochemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry"), and other scientific societies.

## Early life

Ružička was born in [Vukovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vukovar "Vukovar") (at the time in the [Syrmia County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrmia_County "Syrmia County") of [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary "Austria-Hungary"), today in [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia "Croatia")). His family of craftsmen and farmers was mostly of [Croat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croats "Croats") origin, with a [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs "Czechs") great-grandparent, [Ružička](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ružička "Ružička"), and a great-grandmother and a great-grandfather from [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduchy_of_Austria "Archduchy of Austria"). He lost his father, Stjepan, at the age of four, and his mother, Amalija Sever, took him and his younger brother Stjepan, to live in [Osijek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osijek "Osijek").

Ružička attended the classics program at the [gymnasium (secondary school) in Osijek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Gymnasium_Osijek "Real Gymnasium Osijek"). He changed his original [idea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idea "Idea") of becoming a priest and switched to studying technical disciplines. [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") was his choice, probably because he hoped to get a position at the newly opened [sugar refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_refinery "Sugar refinery") built in Osijek.

Owing to the excessive hardship of everyday and political life, he left and chose the High Technical School in [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe "Karlsruhe") in Germany. He was a good student in areas he liked and that he thought would be necessary and beneficial in the future, which was [organic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry"). That is why his [physical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry") professor, [Fritz Haber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Haber "Fritz Haber") ([Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate "Nobel laureate") in 1918), opposed his *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude "Summa cum laude")* [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_degree "Academic degree"). However, in the course of his studies, Ružička set up excellent cooperation with [Hermann Staudinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Staudinger "Hermann Staudinger") (a [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate "Nobel laureate") in 1953). Studying within Staudinger's department, he obtained his doctoral degree in 1910, then moved to [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich "Zürich") as Staudinger's assistant.

## Career in research

Ružička's first works originated in the field of chemistry of natural compounds. He remained in this field of research all his life. He investigated the ingredients of the [Dalmatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia "Dalmatia") insect powder [Pyrethrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrethrum "Pyrethrum") (from the herb *[Tanacetum cinerariifolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanacetum_cinerariifolium "Tanacetum cinerariifolium")*), a highly esteemed [insecticide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insecticide "Insecticide") found in [pyrethrins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrethrin "Pyrethrin"), which were the focus of his work with Staudinger. Ružička later said of this time: "Toward the end of five and a half years of mainly synthetic work on the pyrethrins I had come to the firm conclusion that we were barking up the wrong tree." In this way, he came into contact with the chemistry of [Terpineol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terpineol "Terpineol"), a [fragrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragrant "Fragrant") oil of vegetable origin, interesting to the perfume [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_(economics) "Industry (economics)"). He and Staudinger split company when he started cooperation with the Chuit & Naef Company (later known as [Firmenich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firmenich "Firmenich")) in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva "Geneva").

In 1916–1917, he received the support of the oldest perfume manufacturer in the world *[Haarman & Reimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symrise#Haarman_&_Reimer "Symrise")*, of [Holzminden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holzminden "Holzminden"), Germany. He became a Swiss citizen in 1917, and published his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation "Habilitation") in 1918. Fornasir and he isolated [linalool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linalool "Linalool") in 1919.

With expertise in the terpene field, he became senior lecturer in 1918, and in 1923, honorary professor at the [ETH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich") (Eidgenössische Technische Hochschule) as well as the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich"). Here, with a group of his doctoral students, he proved the structure of the compounds [muscone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscone "Muscone") and [civetone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civetone "Civetone"), [macrocyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrocycle "Macrocycle") ketone scents derived from the [musk deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musk_deer "Musk deer") (*Moschus moschiferus*) and the [civet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civet "Civet") cat (*Viverra civetta*). These were the first [natural products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_product "Natural product") shown to have rings with more than six atoms, and at the time that Ružička inferred that civetone as having a 17-member ring. Synthetic techniques at the time were only known for rings of up to eight members. Muscone had been isolated in 1904 but was not identified as 3-methylcyclopentadecanone until Ružička suspected a [macrocycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrocycle "Macrocycle"), having characterised civetone. He also developed a method for synthesising macrocycles, now known as the [Ružička large ring synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ružička_reaction "Ružička reaction"), which he demonstrated by preparing civetone in 1927.

Leopold Ružička Memorial Museum in his house in [Vukovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vukovar "Vukovar"), [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia "Croatia")

In 1921, the Geneva perfume manufacturers [Chuit & Naef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuit_&_Naef "Chuit & Naef") asked him to collaborate. Working here, Ružička achieved financial independence, but not as big as he had planned, so he left Zürich to start working for the [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel "Basel")-based [CIBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciba_Specialty_Chemicals "Ciba Specialty Chemicals"). In 1927, he took over the organic chemistry chair at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University "Utrecht University") in the Netherlands. In the Netherlands, he remained for three years and then returned to [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland"), which was superior in its chemical industry. A synergistic upheaval in both the administration and chemistry departments coincided to make his good fortune.

Ružička was the first to synthesize [musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musk "Musk") at an industrial scale. Firmenech named this product [Exaltone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exaltone?action=edit&redlink=1 "Exaltone"). Other Swiss manufacturers and [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont") were in competition with them.

In 1934, Ružička synthesized the male hormone [androsterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Androsterone "Androsterone") and also proved "its constitutional and configurational relation to the [sterols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterols "Sterols")." This was followed in 1935 by the partial synthesis of the much more active male hormone [testosterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testosterone "Testosterone"). Both discoveries led to the pre-eminence of the Swiss industry in the steroid hormone field. At ETH Zurich he became a professor of organic chemistry and started the most brilliant period of his professional career. He widened the area of his research, adding to it the chemistry of higher terpenes and [steroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steroids "Steroids"). After the successful synthesis in 1935 of sex [hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormones "Hormones") ([androsterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Androsterone "Androsterone") and [testosterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testosterone "Testosterone")), his laboratory became the world centre of organic chemistry. He was awarded in 1936 an honorary degree from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University").

In 1939 he won the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") with [Adolf Butenandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Butenandt "Adolf Butenandt"). Over the period 1934–1939 he published 70 papers in the field of medicinally important steroid sex hormones, and filed several dozen patents besides.

In 1940, following the award, he was invited by the [Croatian Chemical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Chemical_Association?action=edit&redlink=1 "Croatian Chemical Association"), where he delivered a lecture to an over-packed hall of dignitaries. The topic of the lecture was *From the Dalmatian Insect Powder to Sex Hormones*. In 1940 he became a foreign member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences"), in 1942 he was elected a foreign member of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society"), and in 1944 he became an international member of the US [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"). During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), some of his excellent collaborators were lost, but Ružička restructured his laboratory with new, younger and promising people; among them was young scientist and future Nobel laureate [Vladimir Prelog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Prelog "Vladimir Prelog"). With new people and ideas new research areas were opened.

In 1946, Ružička and Lardon "established that the fragrance of [ambergris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambergris "Ambergris") is based on the [triterpene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triterpene "Triterpene") (named) [ambrein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrein "Ambrein")".

Following 1950, Ružička returned to chemistry, which had entered a new era of research. Now he turned to the field of [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry"), the problems of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution "Evolution") and the genesis of life, particularly to the [biogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogenesis "Biogenesis") of terpenes. In 1953, he published his hypothesis, the *Biogenetic Isoprene Rule* (that the carbon skeleton of terpenes is composed variously of regularly or irregularly linked [isoprene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoprene "Isoprene") units), which was the peak of his scientific career. In 1952, [Oskar Jeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Jeger?action=edit&redlink=1 "Oskar Jeger") and he supervised a team which isolated [lanosterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanosterol "Lanosterol") and established the link between terpenes and steroids. Ružička retired in 1957, turning over the running of the laboratory to Prelog.

## Later life, legacy, honours and awards

Ružička was the recipient of eight honorary doctorates and the 1938 [Marcel Benoist Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Benoist_Prize "Marcel Benoist Prize"). He was listed as an author on 583 scientific papers. In 1965, he became an honorary member of the [Polish Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Chemical_Society "Polish Chemical Society"), and he was an honorary member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"). After the war he acquired a taste for Dutch masterpieces, which he later lodged in the [Kunsthaus Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunsthaus_Zürich "Kunsthaus Zürich") as the Ružička collection. He militated against [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons "Nuclear weapons").

In 1970, Ružička delivered to the [Nobel Laureate Conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindau_Nobel_Laureate_Meetings "Lindau Nobel Laureate Meetings") in [Lindau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindau "Lindau") a lecture entitled "Nobel Prizes and the chemistry of life".

In later years, he served as a consultant to [Sandoz A. G.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandoz_A._G. "Sandoz A. G.") of Basel.

Ružička dedicated significant efforts to the problems of education. He insisted on a better organization of academic education and scientific work in the new [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia "Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia"), and established the [Swiss-Yugoslav Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss-Yugoslav_Society?action=edit&redlink=1 "Swiss-Yugoslav Society"). Ružička became an honorary [academician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academician "Academician") at the then [Yugoslav Academy of Sciences and Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Arts "Yugoslav Academy of Sciences and Arts") in [Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb "Zagreb").

In 1974 he was awarded the [Order of the Yugoslav Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Yugoslav_Flag?action=edit&redlink=1 "Order of the Yugoslav Flag") with Golden Wreath.

At [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich"), the [Ružička Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ružička_Award?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ružička Award") was established in 1957 on the occasion of his retirement, for young chemists working in Switzerland.

In his native [Vukovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vukovar "Vukovar"), a museum was opened in his honour in 1977.

Ružička's archives are kept at ETH Zurich.

The [Ružička reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ružička_reaction "Ružička reaction") is named after him.

### Personal life

Ružička married twice: to Anna Hausmann in 1912, and in 1951 to Gertrud Acklin. From 1929, he lived at Freudenbergstrasse 101 until the last years of his life. He died in [Mammern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammern "Mammern"), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland"), a village on [Lake Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Constance "Lake Constance") at the age of 89.
