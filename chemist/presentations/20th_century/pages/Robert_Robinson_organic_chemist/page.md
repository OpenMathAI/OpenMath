**Sir Robert Robinson** [OM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_Merit "Member of the Order of Merit") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh "Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh") (13 September 1886 – 8 February 1975) was a British [organic chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemist "Organic chemist") and [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates "List of Nobel laureates") recognised in 1947 for his research on plant dyestuffs ([anthocyanins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthocyanin "Anthocyanin")) and [alkaloids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkaloid "Alkaloid"). In 1947, he also received the [Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Freedom_(1945) "Medal of Freedom (1945)") with Silver Palm.

## Biography

### Early life and education

He was born at Rufford House Farm, near [Chesterfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesterfield,_Derbyshire "Chesterfield, Derbyshire"), Derbyshire the son of James Bradbury Robinson, a maker of surgical dressings, and his wife, Jane Davenport.

Robinson went to school at the [Chesterfield Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookfield_Community_School,_Chesterfield "Brookfield Community School, Chesterfield") and the private [Fulneck School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulneck_School "Fulneck School"). He then studied chemistry at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester "Victoria University of Manchester"), graduating BSc in 1905. In 1907 he was awarded an [1851 Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1851_Research_Fellowship "1851 Research Fellowship") from the [Royal Commission for the Exhibition of 1851](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_for_the_Exhibition_of_1851 "Royal Commission for the Exhibition of 1851") to continue his research at the University of Manchester.

He was appointed as the first Professor of Pure and Applied Organic Chemistry in the [School of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Chemistry,_University_of_Sydney "School of Chemistry, University of Sydney") at the [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney "University of Sydney") in 1912. He then took up the Chair in Organic Chemistry at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool "University of Liverpool") (1915–20) and following this became the Director of Research at the [British Dyestuffs Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Dyestuffs_Corporation "British Dyestuffs Corporation"). He was briefly at [St Andrews University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrews_University "St Andrews University") (1921–22) and then took the Chair of Organic Chemistry at [Manchester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_University "Manchester University"), previously held by [Arthur Lapworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Lapworth "Arthur Lapworth") and [William Henry Perkin Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Perkin_Jr. "William Henry Perkin Jr.").
Like Lapworth and Perkin, Robinson presented a paper (The Conjugation of Partial Valencies) to the [Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_and_Philosophical_Society "Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society").

In 1928 he moved from there to be a professor at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London") where he stayed only two years. He was the [Waynflete Professor of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynflete_Professorship "Waynflete Professorship") at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") from 1930 and a Fellow of [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford "Magdalen College, Oxford").

Robinson was elected an International Member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1934, an International Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1944, and an International Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1948.

*Robinson Close*, in the [Science Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Area,_Oxford "Science Area, Oxford") at [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford "Oxford"), is named after him, as is the Robert Robinson Laboratory at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool "University of Liverpool"), the Sir Robert Robinson Laboratory of Organic Chemistry at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester "Victoria University of Manchester") and the Robinson and Cornforth Laboratories at the University of Sydney.

Robinson was a strong amateur [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess "Chess") player. He represented Oxford University in a friendly match with a team from [Bletchley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bletchley_Park "Bletchley Park") in December 1944; in which he lost his game to pioneering computer scientist [I. J. Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._J._Good "I. J. Good"). He was president of the [British Chess Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Chess_Federation "British Chess Federation") from 1950 to 1953, and with Raymond Edwards he co-authored the book *The Art and Science of Chess* (Batsford, 1972).

### Research

His synthesis of [tropinone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropinone "Tropinone") (a precursor for [atropine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atropine "Atropine") & [benzatropine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzatropine "Benzatropine")) in 1917 was not only a big step in [alkaloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkaloid "Alkaloid") chemistry but also showed that [tandem reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tandem_reaction "Tandem reaction") in a [one-pot synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-pot_synthesis "One-pot synthesis") are capable of forming [bicyclic molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicyclic_molecule "Bicyclic molecule").

[![Tropinone synthesis](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a5/Robinson_tropinone_synthesis.png/500px-Robinson_tropinone_synthesis.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Robinson_tropinone_synthesis.png "Tropinone synthesis")

Tropinone synthesis

Robinson created the symbol for [benzene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzene "Benzene") with a circle in the middle whilst working at [St Andrews University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrews_University "St Andrews University") in 1923. He was the first to use [curly arrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_pushing "Arrow pushing") to represent electron movement. He also discovered the molecular structures of [morphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphine "Morphine") and [penicillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicillin "Penicillin").
[Robinson annulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinson_annulation "Robinson annulation") has had application in the [total synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_synthesis "Total synthesis") of steroids.

Alongside [Edward Charles Dodds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Charles_Dodds "Edward Charles Dodds"), Robinson had also been involved in the original synthesis of [diethylstilboestrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diethylstilboestrol "Diethylstilboestrol").

In 1946 he determined the structure of [strychnine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strychnine "Strychnine").

In 1957 Robinson founded the journal *[Tetrahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedron_(journal) "Tetrahedron (journal)")* with fifty other editors for [Pergamon Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon_Press "Pergamon Press").

## Family

Robinson married twice. In 1912 he married Gertrude Maud Walsh. Following her death in 1954, in 1957 he married a widow, Mrs Stern Sylvia Hillstrom (née Hershey).
