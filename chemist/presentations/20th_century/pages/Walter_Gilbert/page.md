**Walter Gilbert** (born March 21, 1932) is an American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"), [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"), [molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology") pioneer, and [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate "Nobel laureate").

## Education and early life

Walter Gilbert was born in [Boston, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston,_Massachusetts "Boston, Massachusetts"), on March 21, 1932, into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") family, the son of Emma (Cohen), a child psychologist, and Richard V. Gilbert, an economist.

When Gilbert was seven years old, the family moved to the [Washington D.C. area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_metropolitan_area "Washington metropolitan area") so his father could work under [Harry Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Hopkins "Harry Hopkins") on the New Deal [brain trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_trust#Roosevelt's_"Brain_Trust" "Brain trust"). While living in Washington the family became friends with the family of [I.F. Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.F._Stone "I.F. Stone") and Wally met Stone's oldest daughter, Celia, when they were both 8. They later married at age 21.

He was educated at the [Sidwell Friends School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidwell_Friends_School "Sidwell Friends School"), and attended [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") for undergraduate and graduate studies, earning a [baccalaureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") and [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") in 1953 and a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree "Master's degree") in physics in 1954. He studied for his doctorate at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), where he earned a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") supervised by the Nobel laureate [Abdus Salam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdus_Salam "Abdus Salam") in 1957.

## Career and research

Gilbert returned to Harvard in 1956 and was appointed assistant professor of physics in 1959. Gilbert's wife Celia worked for [James Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Watson "James Watson"), leading Gilbert to become interested in molecular biology. Watson and Gilbert ran their laboratory jointly through most of the 1960s, until Watson left for [Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Spring_Harbor_Laboratory "Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory"). In 1964 he was promoted to associate professor of biophysics and promoted again in 1968 to professor of biochemistry.

Gilbert is a co-founder of the biotech start-up companies [Biogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogen "Biogen"), with Kenneth Murray, Phillip Sharp and Charles Weissman and [Myriad Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myriad_Genetics "Myriad Genetics") with Dr. Mark Skolnick and [Kevin Kimberlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Kimberlin "Kevin Kimberlin") where he was the first chairman on their respective boards of directors. Gilbert left his position at Harvard to run Biogen as CEO, but was later asked to resign by the company's board of directors. He is a member of the Board of Scientific Governors at [The Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scripps_Research_Institute "The Scripps Research Institute"). Gilbert has served as the chairman of the [Harvard Society of Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Society_of_Fellows "Harvard Society of Fellows").

In 1996, Gilbert and [Stuart B. Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_B._Levy "Stuart B. Levy") founded Paratek Pharmaceuticals. Gilbert served as chairman until 2014.

Gilbert was an early proponent of sequencing the [human genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_genome "Human genome"). At a March 1986 meeting in [Santa Fe New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_New_Mexico "Santa Fe New Mexico") he proclaimed "The total human sequence is the grail of human genetics". In 1987, he proposed starting a company called Genome Corporation to sequence the genome and sell access to the information. In an opinion piece in *Nature* in 1991, he envisioned completion of the human genome sequence transforming biology into a field in which computer databases would be as essential as laboratory reagents

Gilbert returned to Harvard in 1985. Gilbert was an outspoken critic of [David Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Baltimore "David Baltimore") in the handling of the scientific fraud accusations against [Thereza Imanishi-Kari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thereza_Imanishi-Kari "Thereza Imanishi-Kari"). Gilbert also joined the early [controversy over the cause of AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS_denialism "HIV/AIDS denialism").
In 1962, Gilbert's physics PhD student [Gerald Guralnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Guralnik "Gerald Guralnik") extended Gilbert's work on massless particles; Guralnik's work is widely recognized as an important thread in the discovery of the [Higgs Boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_Boson "Higgs Boson").

With his PhD student [Benno Müller-Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benno_Müller-Hill "Benno Müller-Hill"), Gilbert was the first to purify the [lac repressor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lac_repressor "Lac repressor"), just beating out [Mark Ptashne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ptashne "Mark Ptashne") for purifying the first gene regulatory protein.

Together with [Allan Maxam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Maxam "Allan Maxam"), Gilbert developed a new [DNA sequencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_sequencing "DNA sequencing") method, [Maxam–Gilbert sequencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxam–Gilbert_sequencing "Maxam–Gilbert sequencing"), using chemical methods developed by Andrei Mirzabekov. His approach to the first synthesis of [insulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin "Insulin") via [recombinant DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recombinant_DNA "Recombinant DNA") lost out to [Genentech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genentech "Genentech")'s approach which used genes built up from the nucleotides rather than from natural sources. Gilbert's effort was hampered by a temporary moratorium on recombinant DNA work in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts"), forcing his group to move their work to an English biological weapons site.

Gilbert first proposed the terms [introns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introns "Introns") (intragenic regions) and [exons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exons "Exons") (expressed regions) in reference to recently discovered phenomenon of splicing and suggested explanation for the evolution of introns in a seminal 1978 "News and Views" correspondence to *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")* titled "why genes in pieces?". In 1986, Gilbert proposed the [RNA world hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_world_hypothesis "RNA world hypothesis") for the [origin of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_life "Origin of life"), based on a concept first proposed by [Carl Woese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Woese "Carl Woese") in 1967.

## Awards and honors

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/WalterGilbert2.jpg/250px-WalterGilbert2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:WalterGilbert2.jpg)

Walter Gilbert portrait via the National Library of Medicine

In 1969, Gilbert was awarded Harvard's Ledlie Prize. In 1972 he was named [American Cancer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Cancer_Society "American Cancer Society") Professor of Molecular Biology. In 1979, Gilbert was awarded the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") together with [Frederick Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Sanger "Frederick Sanger"). That year he was also awarded the [Gairdner Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gairdner_Prize "Gairdner Prize") and the [Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Lasker_Award_for_Basic_Medical_Research "Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research").

Gilbert was awarded the 1980 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"), shared with [Frederick Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Sanger "Frederick Sanger") and [Paul Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Berg "Paul Berg"). Gilbert and Sanger were recognized for their pioneering work in devising methods for determining the sequence of [nucleotides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleotide "Nucleotide") in a [nucleic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleic_acid "Nucleic acid").

Gilbert has also been honored by the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") (US Steel Foundation Award, 1968); [Massachusetts General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_General_Hospital "Massachusetts General Hospital") (Warren Triennial Prize, 1977); the [New York Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Academy_of_Sciences "New York Academy of Sciences"); (Louis and Bert Freedman Foundation Award, 1977), the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences "Académie des Sciences") of France (Prix Charles-Leopold Mayer Award, 1977). Gilbert was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1987 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1987").

In 2002, he received the [Biotechnology Heritage Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology_Heritage_Award "Biotechnology Heritage Award"), from the [Biotechnology Industry Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology_Industry_Organization "Biotechnology Industry Organization") (BIO) and the [Chemical Heritage Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Heritage_Foundation "Chemical Heritage Foundation").

Allan Maxam and Walter Gilbert's 1977 paper "A new method for sequencing DNA" was honored by a Citation for Chemical Breakthrough Award from the Division of History of Chemistry of the American Chemical Society for 2017. It was presented to the Department of Molecular & Cellular Biology, Harvard University.

## Personal life

Gilbert married [Celia Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celia_Stone?action=edit&redlink=1 "Celia Stone"), the daughter of [I. F. Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._F._Stone "I. F. Stone"), in 1953 and has two children. After retiring from Harvard in 2001, Gilbert has launched an artistic career to combine art and science. His art format is centered on digital photography.
