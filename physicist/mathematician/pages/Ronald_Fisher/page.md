**Sir Ronald Aylmer Fisher** (17 February 1890 – 29 July 1962) was a British [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath "Polymath") who was active as a [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"), [statistician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistician "Statistician"), [biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist "Biologist"), [geneticist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneticist "Geneticist"), and academic. He has been described as "a genius who almost single-handedly created the foundations for modern statistical science" and "the single most important figure in 20th century statistics". In genetics, Fisher was the one to most comprehensively combine the ideas of [Gregor Mendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Mendel "Gregor Mendel") and [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin"), as his work used [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") to combine [Mendelian genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendelian_genetics "Mendelian genetics") and [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection "Natural selection"); this contributed to the revival of [Darwinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwinism "Darwinism") in the early 20th-century revision of the theory of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution "Evolution") known as the [modern synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_synthesis_(20th_century) "Modern synthesis (20th century)"). For his contributions to biology, [Richard Dawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dawkins "Richard Dawkins") declared Fisher to be the greatest of Darwin's successors. He is also considered one of the founding fathers of [Neo-Darwinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Darwinism "Neo-Darwinism"). According to statistician [Jeffrey T. Leek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_T._Leek "Jeffrey T. Leek"), Fisher is the most influential scientist of all time on the basis of the number of citations of his contributions.

From 1919, he worked at the [Rothamsted Experimental Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Research "Rothamsted Research") for 14 years; there, he analyzed its immense body of data from crop experiments since the 1840s, and developed the [analysis of variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_of_variance "Analysis of variance") (ANOVA). He established his reputation there in the following years as a [biostatistician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biostatistics "Biostatistics"). Fisher also made fundamental contributions to [multivariate statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_statistics "Multivariate statistics").

Fisher founded [quantitative genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_genetics "Quantitative genetics"), and, together with [J. B. S. Haldane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._S._Haldane "J. B. S. Haldane") and [Sewall Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewall_Wright "Sewall Wright"), is known as one of the three principal founders of [population genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_genetics "Population genetics"). Fisher outlined [Fisher's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher's_principle "Fisher's principle"), the [Fisherian runaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisherian_runaway "Fisherian runaway"), the [sexy son hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexy_son_hypothesis "Sexy son hypothesis") theories of [sexual selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_selection "Sexual selection"), [parental investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parental_investment "Parental investment"), and also pioneered [linkage analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linkage_analysis "Linkage analysis") and [gene mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_mapping "Gene mapping"). On the other hand, as the founder of [modern statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics "Statistics"), Fisher made countless contributions, including creating the modern method of [maximum likelihood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood "Maximum likelihood") and deriving the properties of maximum likelihood estimators, [fiducial inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiducial_inference "Fiducial inference"), the derivation of various sampling distributions, founding the principles of the [design of experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_of_experiments "Design of experiments"), and much more. Fisher's famous 1921 paper alone has been described as "arguably the most influential article" on [mathematical statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_statistics "Mathematical statistics") in the twentieth century, and equivalent to "Darwin on [evolutionary biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_biology "Evolutionary biology"), [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_Gauss "Carl Friedrich Gauss") on [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory"), [Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Kolmogorov "Andrey Kolmogorov") on [probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability "Probability"), and [Adam Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Smith "Adam Smith") on [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics")", and is credited with completely revolutionizing statistics. For his influence and numerous fundamental contributions, he has been described as "the most original evolutionary biologist of the twentieth century" and as "the greatest statistician of all time". His work is further credited with later initiating the [Human Genome Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project "Human Genome Project"). Fisher also contributed to the understanding of [human blood groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_type "Blood type").

Fisher has also been praised as a pioneer of the [Information Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Age "Information Age"). His work on a mathematical theory of information ran parallel to the work of [Claude Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Shannon "Claude Shannon") and [Norbert Wiener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Wiener "Norbert Wiener"), though based on statistical theory. A concept to have come out of his work is that of [Fisher information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_information "Fisher information"). He also had ideas about [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science "Social science"), which have been described as a "foundation for evolutionary social sciences".

Fisher held strong views on [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_(classification_of_humans) "Race (classification of humans)") and [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics "Eugenics"), insisting on racial differences, although there is debate as to whether Fisher supported [scientific racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_racism "Scientific racism") (see *[§ Views on race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Fisher#Views_on_race)*). He was the [Galton Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galton_Laboratory "Galton Laboratory") of Eugenics at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London") and editor of the *[Annals of Eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annals_of_Eugenics "Annals of Eugenics")*. Due to Fisher's association with eugenics, a number of institutions – including his alma mater [Gonville and Caius College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge "Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge") and his former employer University College London – have removed commemorations of their links to him.

Fisher was born in [East Finchley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Finchley "East Finchley") in London, England, into a middle-class household; his father, George, was a successful partner in Robinson & Fisher, auctioneers and fine art dealers. He was one of twins, the other being still-born, and grew up the youngest, with three sisters and one brother. From 1896 until 1904 they lived at [Inverforth House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverforth_House "Inverforth House") in London, where [English Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Heritage "English Heritage") installed a [blue plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_plaque "Blue plaque") in 2002, before moving to [Streatham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streatham "Streatham"). His mother, Kate, died from acute [peritonitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peritonitis "Peritonitis") when he was 14, and his father lost his business 18 months later.

Lifelong poor eyesight caused his rejection by the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army "British Army") for World War I, but also developed his ability to visualize problems in [geometrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry "Geometry") terms, not in writing mathematical solutions, or proofs. He entered [Harrow School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow_School "Harrow School") age 14 and won the school's Neeld Medal in mathematics. In 1909, he won a scholarship to study [Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") at [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge "Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge"). In 1912, he gained a First in Mathematics. In 1915 he published a paper, *The evolution of sexual preference*, on [sexual selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_selection "Sexual selection") and [mate choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mate_choice "Mate choice").

## Career

During 1913–1919, Fisher worked as a statistician in the City of London and taught [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") and maths at a sequence of [public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_(UK) "Public school (UK)"), at the [Thames Nautical Training College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thames_Nautical_Training_College "Thames Nautical Training College"), and at [Bradfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradfield_College "Bradfield College"). There he settled with his new bride, Eileen Guinness, with whom he had two sons and six daughters.

In 1918 he published "[The Correlation between Relatives on the Supposition of Mendelian Inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Correlation_between_Relatives_on_the_Supposition_of_Mendelian_Inheritance "The Correlation between Relatives on the Supposition of Mendelian Inheritance")", in which he introduced the term [variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variance "Variance") and proposed its formal analysis. He put forward a [genetics conceptual model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additive_model "Additive model") showing that [continuous variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_trait_locus "Quantitative trait locus") amongst [phenotypic traits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenotypic_trait "Phenotypic trait") measured by biostatisticians could be produced by the combined action of many discrete genes and thus be the result of [Mendelian inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendelian_inheritance "Mendelian inheritance"). This was the first step towards establishing [population genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_genetics "Population genetics") and [quantitative genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_genetics "Quantitative genetics"), which demonstrated that [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection "Natural selection") could change [allele frequencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele_frequency "Allele frequency") in a population, reconciling its discontinuous nature with gradual [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution "Evolution"). Joan Box, Fisher's biographer and daughter, says that Fisher had resolved this problem already in 1911. Today, Fisher's additive model is still regularly used in [genome-wide association studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome-wide_association_study "Genome-wide association study").

### Rothamsted Experimental Station, 1919–1933

In 1919, he began working at the [Rothamsted Experimental Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Research "Rothamsted Research") in Hertfordshire, where he would remain for 14 years. He had been offered a position at the [Galton Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galton_Laboratory "Galton Laboratory") of [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London") led by [Karl Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Pearson "Karl Pearson"), but instead accepted a temporary role at Rothamsted to investigate the feasibility of analysing the vast amount of crop data accumulated since 1842 from the "Classical Field Experiments". He analysed the data recorded over many years, and in 1921 published *Studies in Crop Variation I*, his first application of the [analysis of variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_of_variance "Analysis of variance") (ANOVA). *Studies in Crop Variation II* written with his first assistant, [Winifred Mackenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winifred_Mackenzie "Winifred Mackenzie"), became the model for later ANOVA work. Later assistants who mastered and propagated Fisher's methods were [Joseph Oscar Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Oscar_Irwin "Joseph Oscar Irwin"), [John Wishart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wishart_(statistician) "John Wishart (statistician)") and [Frank Yates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Yates "Frank Yates"). Between 1912 and 1922 Fisher recommended, analysed (with heuristic [proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_proof "Mathematical proof")) and vastly popularized the [maximum likelihood estimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood_estimation "Maximum likelihood estimation") method.

Rothamsted Research

Fisher's 1924 article *On a distribution yielding the error functions of several well known statistics* presented [Pearson's chi-squared test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson's_chi-squared_test "Pearson's chi-squared test") and [William Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Sealy_Gosset "William Sealy Gosset")'s [Student's t-distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student's_t-distribution "Student's t-distribution") in the same framework as the [Gaussian distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_distribution "Gaussian distribution"), and is where he developed [Fisher's z-distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher's_z-distribution "Fisher's z-distribution"), a new statistical method commonly used decades later as the [*F*-distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-distribution "F-distribution"). He pioneered the principles of the [design of experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_of_experiments "Design of experiments") and the statistics of small samples and the analysis of real data.

In 1925 he published *[Statistical Methods for Research Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_Methods_for_Research_Workers "Statistical Methods for Research Workers")*, one of the 20th century's most influential books on statistical methods. [Fisher's method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher's_method "Fisher's method") is a technique for [data fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_fusion "Data fusion") or "[meta-analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-analysis "Meta-analysis")" (analysis of analyses). Fisher formalized and popularized use of the [p-value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-value "P-value") in statistics, which plays a central role in his approach. Fisher proposes the level p=0.05, or a 1 in 20 chance of being exceeded by chance, as a limit for statistical significance, and applies this to a normal distribution (as a two-tailed test), yielding the rule of two standard deviations (on a normal distribution) for statistical significance. The significance of [1.96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1.96 "1.96"), the approximate value of the 97.5 percentile point of the normal distribution used in probability and statistics, also originated in this book.

> "The value for which P = 0.05, or 1 in 20, is 1.96 or nearly 2; it is convenient to take this point as a limit in judging whether a deviation is to be considered significant or not."

In Table 1 of the work, he gave the more precise value 1.959964.

In 1928, Fisher was the first to use [diffusion equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_equation "Diffusion equation") to attempt to calculate the distribution of [allele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele "Allele") frequencies and the estimation of [genetic linkage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_linkage "Genetic linkage") by maximum likelihood methods among populations.

In 1930, *[The Genetical Theory of Natural Selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Genetical_Theory_of_Natural_Selection "The Genetical Theory of Natural Selection")* was first published by [Clarendon Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press "Oxford University Press") and is dedicated to [Leonard Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Darwin "Leonard Darwin"). A core work of the neo-Darwinian [modern evolutionary synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_synthesis_(20th_century) "Modern synthesis (20th century)"), it helped define [population genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_genetics "Population genetics"), which Fisher founded alongside [Sewall Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewall_Wright "Sewall Wright") and [J. B. S. Haldane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._S._Haldane "J. B. S. Haldane"), and revived Darwin's neglected idea of [sexual selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_selection "Sexual selection").

One of Fisher's favourite aphorisms was "Natural selection is a mechanism for generating an exceedingly high degree of improbability."

Fisher's fame grew, and he began to travel and lecture widely. In 1931, he spent six weeks at the Statistical Laboratory at [Iowa State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_College "Iowa State College") where he gave three lectures per week, and met many American statisticians, including [George W. Snedecor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Snedecor "George W. Snedecor"). He returned there again in 1936.

### University College London, 1933–1943

In 1933, Fisher succeeded Karl Pearson as the Galton Professor of Eugenics and head of the Galton Laboratory at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London"). In 1934, he become editor of the *Annals of Eugenics* (now called *[Annals of Human Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annals_of_Human_Genetics "Annals of Human Genetics")*).

In 1935, he published *[The Design of Experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Design_of_Experiments "The Design of Experiments")*, which was "also fundamental, [and promoted] statistical technique and application... The mathematical justification of the methods was not stressed and proofs were often barely sketched or omitted altogether .... [This] led [H.B. Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Mann "Henry Mann") to fill the gaps with a rigorous mathematical treatment". In this book Fisher also outlined the [lady tasting tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_tasting_tea "Lady tasting tea"), now a famous [design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_of_experiments "Design of experiments") of a statistical [randomized experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_experiment "Randomized experiment") which uses [Fisher's exact test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher's_exact_test "Fisher's exact test") and is the original exposition of Fisher's notion of a [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis "Null hypothesis").

The same year he published a paper on [fiducial inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiducial_inference "Fiducial inference") and applied it to the [Behrens–Fisher problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behrens–Fisher_problem "Behrens–Fisher problem"), the solution to which, proposed first by [Walter Behrens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Behrens_(statistician) "Walter Behrens (statistician)") and a few years later by Fisher, is the [Behrens–Fisher distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behrens–Fisher_distribution "Behrens–Fisher distribution").

In 1936, he introduced the [Iris flower data set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iris_flower_data_set "Iris flower data set") as an example of [discriminant analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discriminant_analysis "Discriminant analysis").

In his 1937 paper *The wave of advance of advantageous genes* he proposed [Fisher's equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher's_equation "Fisher's equation") in the context of [population dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_dynamics "Population dynamics") to describe the spatial spread of an advantageous [allele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele "Allele"), and explored its travelling wave solutions. Out of this also came the [Fisher–Kolmogorov equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher–Kolmogorov_equation "Fisher–Kolmogorov equation"). In 1937, he visited the [Indian Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Statistical_Institute "Indian Statistical Institute") in Calcutta, and its one part-time employee, [P. C. Mahalanobis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._C._Mahalanobis "P. C. Mahalanobis"), often returning to encourage its development. He was the guest of honour at its 25th anniversary in 1957, when it had 2,000 employees.

In 1938, Fisher and [Frank Yates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Yates "Frank Yates") described the [Fisher–Yates shuffle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher–Yates_shuffle "Fisher–Yates shuffle") in their book *Statistical tables for biological, agricultural and medical research*. Their description of the algorithm used pencil and paper; a table of random numbers provided the randomness.

### University of Cambridge, 1943–1956

In 1943, along with [A. S. Corbet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Steven_Corbet "Alexander Steven Corbet") and [C. B. Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._B._Williams "C. B. Williams"), he published a paper on [relative species abundance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_species_abundance "Relative species abundance") where he developed the [log series distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log_series_distribution "Log series distribution") (sometimes called the logarithmic distribution) to fit two different abundance data sets. In the same year he took the [Balfour Chair of Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Chair_of_Genetics "Balfour Chair of Genetics") where the Italian researcher [Luigi Luca Cavalli-Sforza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Luca_Cavalli-Sforza "Luigi Luca Cavalli-Sforza") was recruited in 1948, establishing a one-man unit of bacterial genetics.

In 1936, Fisher used a [Pearson's chi-squared test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson's_chi-squared_test "Pearson's chi-squared test") to analyze Mendel's data and concluded that Mendel's results were far too perfect, suggesting that adjustments (intentional or unconscious) had been made to the data to make the observations fit the hypothesis. Later authors have claimed Fisher's analysis was flawed, proposing various statistical and botanical explanations for Mendel's numbers. In 1947, Fisher co-founded the journal *[Heredity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heredity_(journal) "Heredity (journal)")* with [Cyril Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Darlington "Cyril Darlington") and in 1949 he published *The Theory of Inbreeding.*

In 1950, he published "Gene Frequencies in a Cline Determined by Selection and Diffusion". He developed computational [algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithm "Algorithm") for analyzing data from his balanced experimental designs, with various editions and translations, becoming a standard reference work for scientists in many disciplines. In [ecological genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_genetics "Ecological genetics") he and [E. B. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._B._Ford "E. B. Ford") showed that the force of natural selection was much stronger than had been assumed, with many ecogenetic situations (such as [polymorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymorphism_(biology) "Polymorphism (biology)")) being maintained by the force of selection.

During this time he also worked on mouse chromosome mapping, breeding the mice in laboratories in his own house.

Fisher publicly spoke out against the 1950 study showing that smoking [tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco "Tobacco") causes [lung cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_cancer "Lung cancer"), arguing that [correlation does not imply causation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_does_not_imply_causation "Correlation does not imply causation"). To quote his biographers Yates and Mather, "It has been suggested that the fact that Fisher was employed as consultant by the tobacco firms in this controversy casts doubt on the value of his arguments. This is to misjudge the man. He was not above accepting financial reward for his labours, but the reason for his interest was undoubtedly his dislike and mistrust of puritanical tendencies of all kinds; and perhaps also the personal solace he had always found in tobacco." Others have suggested that his analysis was biased by professional conflicts and his own love of smoking; he was a heavy pipe smoker.

He gave the 1953 [Croonian lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croonian_lecture "Croonian lecture") on population genetics.

In the winter of 1954–1955 Fisher met [Debabrata Basu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debabrata_Basu "Debabrata Basu"), the Indian statistician who wrote in 1988, "With his reference set argument, Sir Ronald was trying to find a *[via media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_media "Via media")* between the two poles of Statistics – Berkeley and [Bayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Bayes "Thomas Bayes"). My efforts to understand this Fisher compromise led me to the [likelihood principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likelihood_principle "Likelihood principle")".

### Adelaide, 1957–1962

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Ronald_Aylmer_Fisher.jpg/250px-Ronald_Aylmer_Fisher.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ronald_Aylmer_Fisher.jpg)

Memorial plaque over his remains, lectern-side aisle of St Peter's Cathedral, Adelaide

In 1957, a retired Fisher immigrated to Australia, where he spent time as a senior research fellow at the Australian [Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Scientific_and_Industrial_Research_Organisation "Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation") (CSIRO) in [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide "Adelaide"), South Australia. During this time, he continued in his denial of tobacco harm, and enlisted German eugenicist [Otmar von Verschuer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otmar_von_Verschuer "Otmar von Verschuer") to his cause.

Following surgery for [colon cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colon_cancer "Colon cancer"), he died of post-operative complications in [Queen Elizabeth Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_Hospital,_Adelaide "Queen Elizabeth Hospital, Adelaide") in Adelaide on 29 July 1962. His remains are interred in [St Peter's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Peter's_Cathedral,_Adelaide "St Peter's Cathedral, Adelaide"), Adelaide.

## Legacy

Fisher's doctoral students included [Walter Bodmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Bodmer "Walter Bodmer"), [D. J. Finney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._J._Finney "D. J. Finney"), [Ebenezer Laing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebenezer_Laing "Ebenezer Laing"), [Mary F. Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_F._Lyon "Mary F. Lyon") and [C. R. Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._R._Rao "C. R. Rao"). Although a prominent opponent of [Bayesian statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_statistics "Bayesian statistics"), Fisher was the first to use the term "Bayesian", in 1950. The 1930 *[The Genetical Theory of Natural Selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Genetical_Theory_of_Natural_Selection "The Genetical Theory of Natural Selection")* is commonly cited in biology books, and outlines many important concepts, such as:

- [Parental investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parental_investment "Parental investment"), which is any parental expenditure (time, energy, etc.) that benefits one [offspring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offspring "Offspring") at a cost to [parents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parent "Parent")' ability to invest in other components of [fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitness_(biology) "Fitness (biology)"),
- [![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/37/Fisher-stainedglass-gonville-caius.jpg/250px-Fisher-stainedglass-gonville-caius.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Fisher-stainedglass-gonville-caius.jpg)

  The [Sir Ronald Fisher window](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Ronald_Fisher_window "Sir Ronald Fisher window") (now removed) in the dining hall of [Caius College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge "Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge"), Cambridge, representing a [Latin square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_square "Latin square"), discussed by Fisher in *[The Design of Experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Design_of_Experiments "The Design of Experiments")*

  [Fisherian runaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisherian_runaway "Fisherian runaway"), explaining how the desire for a [phenotypic trait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenotypic_trait "Phenotypic trait") in one sex combined with the trait in the other sex (for example a [peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peafowl "Peafowl")'s tail) creates a runaway evolutionary extremizing of the trait.
- [Fisher's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher's_principle "Fisher's principle"), which explains why the [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio "Sex ratio") is mostly 1:1 in nature.
- [Reproductive value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_value_(population_genetics) "Reproductive value (population genetics)") which implies that [sexually reproductive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_reproduction "Sexual reproduction") value measures the contribution of an individual of a given age to the future growth of the [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_(biology) "Population (biology)").
- [Fisher's fundamental theorem of natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher's_fundamental_theorem_of_natural_selection "Fisher's fundamental theorem of natural selection"), which states that "the rate of increase in [fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitness_(biology) "Fitness (biology)") of any [organism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism "Organism") at any time is equal to its [genetic variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_variance "Genetic variance") in fitness at that time."
- [Fisher's geometric model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher's_geometric_model "Fisher's geometric model"), an [evolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution "Evolution") model of the [effect sizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effect_size "Effect size") on [fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitness_(biology) "Fitness (biology)") of spontaneous [mutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutations "Mutations") proposed by Fisher to explain the distribution of effects of mutations that could contribute to [adaptive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptation_(biology) "Adaptation (biology)") evolution.
- [Sexy son hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexy_son_hypothesis "Sexy son hypothesis"), which hypothesizes that females may choose arbitrarily attractive male mates simply because they are attractive, thus increasing the attractiveness of their sons who attract more mates of their own. This is in contrast to theories of female mate choice based on the assumption that females choose attractive males because the attractive traits are markers of male viability.
- [Mimicry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimicry "Mimicry"), a similarity of one species to another that protects one or both.
- [The evolution of dominance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominance_(genetics) "Dominance (genetics)"), a relationship between [alleles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele "Allele") of one [gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene"), in which the effect on [phenotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenotype "Phenotype") of one allele masks the contribution of a second allele at the same [locus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locus_(genetics) "Locus (genetics)").
- [Heterozygote advantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterozygote_advantage "Heterozygote advantage") which was later found to play a frequent role in genetic polymorphism.
- Demonstrating that the probability of a mutation increasing the [fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitness_(biology) "Fitness (biology)") of an organism decreases proportionately with the magnitude of the mutation and that larger populations carry more variation so that they have a greater chance of survival.

Fisher is also known for:

- [Linear discriminant analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_discriminant_analysis "Linear discriminant analysis") is a generalization of Fisher's linear discriminant
- [Fisher information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_information "Fisher information"), see also [scoring algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoring_algorithm "Scoring algorithm") also known as Fisher's scoring, and [Minimum Fisher information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_Fisher_information "Minimum Fisher information"), a variational principle which, when applied with the proper constraints needed to reproduce empirically known expectation values, determines the best probability distribution that characterizes the system.
- [*F*-distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-distribution "F-distribution"), arises frequently as the null distribution of a [test statistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_statistic "Test statistic"), most notably in the analysis of variance
- [Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko_theorem "Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko theorem"): Fisher's contribution to this was made in 1927
- [Fisher–Tippett distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher–Tippett_distribution "Fisher–Tippett distribution")
- [Fisher–Yates shuffle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher–Yates_shuffle "Fisher–Yates shuffle") algorithm
- [Von Mises–Fisher distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Mises–Fisher_distribution "Von Mises–Fisher distribution")
- [Inverse probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_probability "Inverse probability"), a term Fisher used in 1922, referring to "the fundamental paradox of inverse probability" as the source of the confusion between statistical terms which refer to the true value to be estimated, with the actual value arrived at by estimation, which is subject to error.
- [Fisher's permutation test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resampling_(statistics) "Resampling (statistics)")
- [Fisher's inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher's_inequality "Fisher's inequality")
- [Sufficient statistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficient_statistic "Sufficient statistic"), when a statistic is *sufficient* with respect to a [statistical model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_model "Statistical model") and its associated unknown parameter if "no other statistic that can be calculated from the same [sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_(statistics) "Sample (statistics)") provides any additional information as to the value of the parameter".
- [Fisher's noncentral hypergeometric distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher's_noncentral_hypergeometric_distribution "Fisher's noncentral hypergeometric distribution"), a generalization of the [hypergeometric distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_distribution "Hypergeometric distribution"), where sampling probabilities are modified by weight factors.
- [Student's *t*-distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student's_t-distribution "Student's t-distribution"), widely used in statistics.
- The concept of an [ancillary statistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancillary_statistic "Ancillary statistic") and the notion (the ancillarity principle) that one should condition on ancillary statistics.

## Personal life and beliefs

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Ronald_Fisher_with_his_sons.png/250px-Ronald_Fisher_with_his_sons.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ronald_Fisher_with_his_sons.png)

Fisher with his sons

Fisher married Eileen Guinness, with whom he had two sons and six daughters. His marriage disintegrated during World War II, and his older son George, an [aviator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviator "Aviator"), was killed in combat. His daughter Joan, who wrote a biography of her father, married the statistician [George E. P. Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_E._P._Box "George E. P. Box").

According to Yates and Mather, "His large family, in particular, reared in conditions of great financial stringency, was a personal expression of his genetic and evolutionary convictions." Fisher was noted for being loyal, and was seen as a patriot, a member of the [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England "Church of England"), politically [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative "Conservative"), as well as a scientific rationalist. He developed a reputation for carelessness in his dress and was the archetype of the absent-minded professor. [H. Allen Orr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Allen_Orr "H. Allen Orr") describes him in the *Boston Review* as a "deeply devout [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican "Anglican") who, between founding modern statistics and population genetics, penned articles for church magazines". In a 1955 broadcast on Science and Christianity, he said:

> The custom of making abstract dogmatic assertions is not, certainly, derived from the teaching of [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus"), but has been a widespread weakness among religious teachers in subsequent centuries. I do not think that the word for the Christian virtue of faith should be prostituted to mean the credulous acceptance of all such piously intended assertions. Much self-deception in the young believer is needed to convince himself that he knows that of which in reality he knows himself to be ignorant. That surely is hypocrisy, against which we have been most conspicuously warned.

Fisher was involved with the [Society for Psychical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Psychical_Research "Society for Psychical Research").

## Views on race

Between 1950 and 1951, Fisher, along with other leading geneticists and anthropologists of his time, was asked to comment on a statement that [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") was preparing on the nature of race and racial differences, which was published in 1950 as the UNESCO *[Statement on Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_statements_on_race#Statement_on_the_nature_of_race_and_race_differences_(1951) "UNESCO statements on race")*. The statement, along with the comments and criticisms of a large number of scientists including Fisher, is published in "The Race Concept: Results of an Inquiry" (1952).

Fisher was one of four scientists who opposed the statement. In his own words, Fisher's opposition is based on "one fundamental objection to the Statement", which "destroys the very spirit of the whole document." He believes that human groups differ profoundly "in their innate capacity for intellectual and emotional development" and concludes from this that the "practical international problem is that of learning to share the resources of this planet amicably with persons of materially different nature, and that this problem is being obscured by entirely well-intentioned efforts to minimize the real differences that exist."

Fisher's opinions are clarified by his more detailed comments on Section 5 of the statement, which are concerned with psychological and mental differences between the races. Section 5 concludes as follows:

> Scientifically, however, we realized that any common psychological attribute is more likely to be due to a common historical and social background, and that such attributes may obscure the fact that, within different populations consisting of many human types, one will find approximately the same range of temperament and intelligence.

Of the entire statement, Section 5 recorded the most dissenting viewpoints. It was recorded that "Fisher's attitude … is the same as [Muller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Joseph_Muller "Hermann Joseph Muller")'s and [Sturtevant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Sturtevant "Alfred Sturtevant")'s". Muller's criticism was recorded in more detail and was noted to "represent an important trend of ideas":

> I quite agree with the chief intention of the article as a whole, which, I take it, is to bring out the relative unimportance of such genetic mental differences between races as may exist, in contrast to the importance of the mental differences (between individuals as well as between nations) caused by tradition, training and other aspects of the environment. However, in view of the admitted existence of some physically expressed hereditary differences of a conspicuous nature, between the averages or the medians of the races, it would be strange if there were not also some hereditary differences affecting the mental characteristics which develop in a given environment, between these averages or medians. At the same time, these mental differences might usually be unimportant in comparison with those between individuals of the same race…. To the great majority of geneticists it seems absurd to suppose that psychological characteristics are subject to entirely different laws of heredity or development than other biological characteristics. Even though the former characteristics are far more influenced than the latter by environment, in the form of past experiences, they must have a highly complex genetic basis.

Fisher's own words were quoted as follows:

> As you ask for remarks and suggestions, there is one that occurs to me, unfortunately of a somewhat fundamental nature, namely that the Statement as it stands appears to draw a distinction between the body and mind of men, which must, I think, prove untenable. It appears to me unmistakable that gene differences which influence the growth or physiological development of an organism will ordinarily [pari passu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pari_passu "Pari passu") influence the congenital inclinations and capacities of the mind. In fact, I should say that, to vary conclusion (2) on page 5, 'Available scientific knowledge provides a firm basis for believing that the groups of mankind differ in their innate capacity for intellectual and emotional development,' seeing that such groups do differ undoubtedly in a very large number of their genes.

Fisher also ended a 1954 letter to [Reginald Ruggles Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Ruggles_Gates "Reginald Ruggles Gates"), a Canadian-born geneticist who argued that different racial groups were different species, with the words:

> I am sorry that there should be propaganda in favour of [miscegenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscegenation "Miscegenation") in North America as I am sure it can do nothing but harm. Is it beyond human endeavour to give and justly administer equal rights to all citizens without fooling ourselves that these are equivalent items?

Nearly all of Fisher's writings discuss human populations or humanity as a whole without reference to race or specific racial groups, and none of his work explicitly supports the idea of racial superiority or white supremacy. Fisher had a close personal relationship with Indian statistician [P.C. Mahalanobis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.C._Mahalanobis "P.C. Mahalanobis"), and significantly contributed to the development of the [Indian Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Statistical_Institute "Indian Statistical Institute"); and Fisher's graduate students included [Walter Bodmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Bodmer "Walter Bodmer"), a child of Jewish-German parents who fled from Nazi Germany while he was young, and [Ebenezer Laing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebenezer_Laing "Ebenezer Laing"), an African geneticist from Ghana. [Daniel Kevles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kevles "Daniel Kevles"), an American [historian of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian_of_science "Historian of science"), described Fisher as an "anti-racist conservative". However, British historian [Richard J. Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_J._Evans "Richard J. Evans"), writing in *[The New Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Statesman_&_Nation "The New Statesman & Nation")*, argued that Fisher's views on eugenics and his opposition to UNESCO's statement about genetic racial differences were indicative of racism.

In 1911, Fisher became founding Chairman of the University of Cambridge Eugenics Society, whose other founding members included [John Maynard Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Keynes "John Maynard Keynes"), [R. C. Punnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._C._Punnett "R. C. Punnett"), and [Horace Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Darwin "Horace Darwin"). After members of the Cambridge Society – including Fisher – stewarded the First International Eugenics Congress in London in summer 1912, a link was forged with the [Eugenics Society (UK)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics_Society_(UK) "Eugenics Society (UK)"). He saw [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics "Eugenics") as addressing pressing social and scientific issues that encompassed and drove his interest in both genetics and statistics. During World War I Fisher started writing book reviews for *[The Eugenics Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eugenics_Review "The Eugenics Review")* and volunteered to undertake all such reviews for the journal, being hired for a part-time position.

The last third of *[The Genetical Theory of Natural Selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Genetical_Theory_of_Natural_Selection "The Genetical Theory of Natural Selection")* focused on eugenics, attributing the fall of [civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization "Civilization") to the fertility of their upper classes being diminished, and used British 1911 census data to show an inverse relationship between fertility and social class, which was partly due, he claimed, to the lower financial costs and hence increasing social status of families with fewer children. He proposed the abolition of extra allowances to large families, with the allowances proportional to the earnings of the father. He served in several official committees to promote eugenics, including the Committee for Legalizing Eugenic Sterilization which drafted legislation aiming to limit the fertility of "feeble minded high-grade defectives ... comprising a tenth of the total population". It was proposed that this policy would allow for voluntary sterilisation. Fisher was against the idea of forced sterilisation.

Beginning in 1934, Fisher became disillusioned with the Eugenics Society over concerns that its activities were increasingly aimed in a political rather than scientific direction; he formally dissociated with the Society in 1941.

Fisher wrote a testimony on behalf of the eugenicist [Otmar Freiherr von Verschuer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otmar_Freiherr_von_Verschuer "Otmar Freiherr von Verschuer"). He wrote that, although the Nazis used Verschuer's work to give scientific support for their ideology, it was "[Verschuer's] misfortune rather than his fault that racial theory was a part of the Nazi ideology." He conducted extensive correspondence with von Verschuer over decades, which is held at the [University of Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Adelaide "University of Adelaide").

## Recognition

### Appraisal of scientific merits

Fisher was elected to the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") in 1929, the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1934, the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1941, and the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1948. He was made a [Knight Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") by Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II") in 1952 and awarded the [Linnean Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linnean_Society_of_London "Linnean Society of London") [Darwin–Wallace Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin–Wallace_Medal "Darwin–Wallace Medal") in 1958.

He won the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal "Copley Medal") and the Royal Medal. He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") in 1924 in Toronto and in 1928 in Bologna.

In 1950, [Maurice Wilkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Wilkes "Maurice Wilkes") and [David Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Wheeler_(computer_scientist) "David Wheeler (computer scientist)") used the [Electronic Delay Storage Automatic Calculator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Delay_Storage_Automatic_Calculator "Electronic Delay Storage Automatic Calculator") to solve a [differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equation "Differential equation") relating to gene frequencies in a paper by Ronald Fisher. This represents the first use of a computer for a problem in the field of biology. The [Kent distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_distribution "Kent distribution") (also known as the Fisher–Bingham distribution) was named after him and [Christopher Bingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Bingham "Christopher Bingham") in 1982, while the [Fisher kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_kernel "Fisher kernel") was named after Fisher in 1998.

The [R. A. Fisher Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Fisher_Lectureship "R. A. Fisher Lectureship") was a North American [Committee of Presidents of Statistical Societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Presidents_of_Statistical_Societies "Committee of Presidents of Statistical Societies") (COPSS) annual lecture prize, established in 1963, until the name was changed to COPSS Distinguished Achievement Award and Lectureship in 2020. On 28 April 1998 a minor planet, [21451 Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21451_Fisher "21451 Fisher"), was named after him.

In 2010, the R.A. Fisher Chair in Statistical Genetics was established in University College London to recognise Fisher's extraordinary contributions to both statistics and genetics.

[Anders Hald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Hald "Anders Hald") called Fisher "a genius who almost single-handedly created the foundations for modern statistical science", while [Richard Dawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dawkins "Richard Dawkins") named him "the greatest biologist since [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin")":

> Not only was he the most original and constructive of the architects of the neo-Darwinian synthesis, Fisher also was the father of modern statistics and experimental design. He therefore could be said to have provided researchers in biology and medicine with their most important research tools, as well as with the modern version of biology's central theorem.

[Geoffrey Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Miller_(psychologist) "Geoffrey Miller (psychologist)") said of him:

> To biologists, he was an architect of the "modern synthesis" that used mathematical models to integrate Mendelian genetics with Darwin's selection theories. To psychologists, Fisher was the inventor of various statistical tests that are still supposed to be used whenever possible in psychology journals. To farmers, Fisher was the founder of experimental agricultural research, saving millions from starvation through rational crop breeding programs.

### Contentious views on eugenics

Fisher and Sewall Wright contributed to the development of [population genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_genetics "Population genetics"), which became part of the [modern synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_synthesis_(20th_century) "Modern synthesis (20th century)"). The interpretation of the mathematical theories of population genetics became a bone of contention between the two men by the mid-1920s, and the issue became acrimonious. Dispute persisted for the rest of Fisher's life. A 2021 paper, authored by trustees of the Fisher Memorial Trust, commented that recent criticism of Fisher could mostly be characterised as "reconsideration of the honour given to individuals from preceding times who are felt to have contributed to social injustice in the past, or to have held views that are felt to have promoted social injustice."

In June 2020, during the [international protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests "George Floyd protests") caused by the [murder of George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_George_Floyd "Murder of George Floyd"), [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge "Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge") announced that [a 1989 stained-glass window](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Ronald_Fisher_window "Sir Ronald Fisher window") commemorating Fisher's work would be removed because of his connection with eugenics. An accommodation building at [Rothamsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Research "Rothamsted Research"), given his name when built in 2018, was subsequently renamed too. [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London") also decided to remove his name from its Centre for Computational Biology.

Earlier, the renaming of research centres associated with eugenics such as Fisher, Galton, and Pearson had been a prominent demand of student campaigners at UCL, notably through its [Students' Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students'_Union_UCL "Students' Union UCL")'s Decolonise UCL campaign. Similarly, the removal of the stained-glass window had been called for through an online petition started by students, which gathered nearly 1,500 signatures. The petition argued: "The panel makes no mention of Fisher's significant contributions to the Eugenicist movement. Nor is it accompanied with a college programme to educate students about Fisher's racism. Caius students and Fellows eat, converse and celebrate in space that also acts as a commemoration of our racist history." In support of UCL's renaming of its Centre for Computational Biology, [Joe Cain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Cain_(historian_of_science) "Joe Cain (historian of science)"), a specialist in the history of evolutionary biology at the university, stressed the importance of separating the legacy of Fisher's "brilliant" work in statistics and mathematics from celebrating him as a person, a supporter of a racist ideology.

> Fisher was indeed a brilliant statistician and mathematical population geneticist. He contributed core concepts and methods widely used today. He did good in the world with applications in agriculture, tracking genetic diseases, general research methods, and other areas. He also was a valuable critical voice in science, eager to correct mistakes and to identify flawed assumptions. That work deserves respect.
> But the human being who created that work does not.

— Joe Cain

### Contentious views on smoking

Fisher rejected the notion of smoking cigarettes being dangerous, calling it "propaganda".
