**Lars Valerian Ahlfors** (18 April 1907 – 11 October 1996) was a Finnish [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") and the leading figure in [complex analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_analysis "Complex analysis") during the 20th century. He is remembered for his work on [Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surface "Riemann surface"), [quasiconformal mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiconformal_mapping "Quasiconformal mapping") and [Teichmüller spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teichmüller_space "Teichmüller space"), and for his textbook on [complex analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_analysis "Complex analysis"). In 1936 Ahlfors was one of the first two recipients of the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal"), along with American mathematician [Jesse Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Douglas "Jesse Douglas"), and in 1981 he received the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Mathematics "Wolf Prize in Mathematics").

## Biography

### Early life and education

Ahlfors was born in [Helsinki, Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki,_Finland "Helsinki, Finland"). His mother, Sievä Helander, died at his birth. His father, Karl Axel Mauritz Ahlfors, was a professor of engineering at the [Helsinki University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_University_of_Technology "Helsinki University of Technology"). The Ahlfors family was [Swedish-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish-speaking_Finns "Swedish-speaking Finns"), so he first attended the private school [Nya svenska samskolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nya_svenska_samskolan "Nya svenska samskolan") where all classes were taught in Swedish. Ahlfors studied at the [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki "University of Helsinki") from 1924, graduating in 1928 having studied under [Ernst Lindelöf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Lindelöf "Ernst Lindelöf") and [Rolf Nevanlinna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Nevanlinna "Rolf Nevanlinna"). In the autumn of 1928 he travelled with Nevanlinna to Zurich, where Nevanlinna had been invited to fill a vacant professorship at the [Swiss Federal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich"). Ahlfors later emphasised the importance of this journey, which brought him from the periphery of Europe to its mathematical centre. He assisted Nevanlinna with his work on [Denjoy's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denjoy–Carleman–Ahlfors_theorem "Denjoy–Carleman–Ahlfors theorem") on the number of asymptotic values of an [entire function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entire_function "Entire function"), and in 1929 published the first proof of this conjecture, now known as the Denjoy–Carleman–Ahlfors theorem. He completed his doctorate from the University of Helsinki in 1930.

### Academic career

Ahlfors began his academic career at [Åbo Akademi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Åbo_Akademi "Åbo Akademi") and was appointed associate professor of mathematics at the University of Helsinki in 1933. In 1935–1936 Ahlfors was a visiting researcher at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). In 1936 he was one of the first two people to be awarded the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") (the other was [Jesse Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Douglas "Jesse Douglas")). The prize committee noted that Ahlfors was one of the most brilliant representatives of the Finnish school of function theory founded by Ernst Lindelöf, which over thirty years had made many valuable contributions to mathematics and produced so many outstanding mathematicians. When offered an associate professorship at Harvard, he left his position at the University of Helsinki in autumn 1936. He returned to [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland "Finland") in 1938 to take up a Swedish-language professorship in mathematics at the University of Helsinki.

#### War years and emigration

The outbreak of war in 1939 led to problems although Ahlfors was unfit for military service. He sent his family to Sweden and continued to fulfil his professorial duties at the university. Foreseeing difficult times ahead for Finland in the summer of 1944, he indicated his wish to move to Zurich and joined his family in Sweden to await developments. He was offered a position at the [Swiss Federal Institute of Technology at Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich") and finally managed to travel there in March 1945, at which point he resigned his professorship in Helsinki. He did not enjoy his time in [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland"), so in 1946 he accepted a professorship at Harvard, where he remained until his retirement in 1977; he was William Caspar Graustein Professor of Mathematics from 1964. Ahlfors was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") in 1962 and again in 1966. He was awarded the [Wihuri International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wihuri_International_Prize "Wihuri International Prize") in 1968 and the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Mathematics "Wolf Prize in Mathematics") in 1981, whose citation concluded that every function theorist active at the time was in some measure a student of Ahlfors. He served as the Honorary President of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") in 1986 at [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California "Berkeley, California"), in celebration of his 50th year of the award of his Fields Medal.

## Mathematical contributions

Throughout his career Ahlfors made decisive contributions to several areas of complex analysis. Among his earliest results was the proof of the [Denjoy–Carleman–Ahlfors theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denjoy–Carleman–Ahlfors_theorem "Denjoy–Carleman–Ahlfors theorem"), which states that the number of asymptotic values approached by an entire function of order ρ along curves in the [complex plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_plane "Complex plane") going toward infinity is less than or equal to 2ρ. His book *Complex Analysis* (1953) is the classic text on the subject and is almost certainly referenced in any more recent text which makes heavy use of complex analysis. Ahlfors wrote several other significant books, including *Riemann surfaces* (1960) and *Conformal invariants* (1973). He made decisive contributions to [meromorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meromorphic "Meromorphic") curves, [value distribution theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_distribution_theory "Value distribution theory"), [Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surface "Riemann surface"), [conformal geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_geometry "Conformal geometry"), [quasiconformal mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiconformal_mapping "Quasiconformal mapping") and other areas during his career.

In 1954 Ahlfors proved that the results and conjectures of [Oswald Teichmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Teichmüller "Oswald Teichmüller") — whose pioneering work on [Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surface "Riemann surface") had been cut short when he disappeared on the German Eastern Front in 1943 — were correct. In doing so he defined the concept of [Teichmüller space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teichmüller_space "Teichmüller space"), which rapidly became an important field of research within function theory and later acquired significance in physics as well. His work made the theory of quasiconformal mappings a central area of complex analysis, and by the year 2000 this theory was assessed as perhaps the most important advance in function theory during the 20th century.

## Personal life

In 1933, he married Erna Lehnert, an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrians "Austrians") born in Vienna who with her parents had first settled in [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden") and then in [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland "Finland"). The couple had three daughters. Ahlfors died of pneumonia at the Willowwood nursing home in [Pittsfield, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsfield,_Massachusetts "Pittsfield, Massachusetts") in 1996.
