**Saul Perlmutter** (born September 22, 1959) is an American [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics "Astrophysics") who is a professor of physics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), where he holds the Franklin W. and Karen Weber Dabby Chair, and is head of the International Supernova Cosmology Project at the [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Laboratory "Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory"). He is a member of both the [American Academy of Arts & Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_&_Sciences "American Academy of Arts & Sciences") and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"), and was elected a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_AAAS "Fellow of the AAAS") of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science") in 2003. He is a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences_(Physics) "List of members of the National Academy of Sciences (Physics)"). Perlmutter shared the 2006 [Shaw Prize in Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize"), the [2011 Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "2011 Nobel Prize in Physics"), and the 2015 [Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Fundamental_Physics "Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics") with [Brian P. Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_P._Schmidt "Brian P. Schmidt") and [Adam Riess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Riess "Adam Riess") for providing evidence that the [expansion of the universe is accelerating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_universe "Accelerating universe"). Since 2021, he has been a member of the [President’s Council of Advisors on Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President’s_Council_of_Advisors_on_Science_and_Technology "President’s Council of Advisors on Science and Technology") (PCAST).

## Education

Saul Perlmutter was born to Felice (Feige) D. Perlmutter (née Davidson), now professor emerita of the [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University "Temple University") School of Social Administration, and [Daniel D. Perlmutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_D._Perlmutter?action=edit&redlink=1 "Daniel D. Perlmutter"), now professor emeritus of chemical and biomolecular engineering at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania"). His maternal grandfather, the [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish "Yiddish") teacher Samuel Davidson (1903–1989), emigrated from the [Bessarabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia "Bessarabia") town of [Floreşti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floreşti,_Moldova "Floreşti, Moldova") to [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada") in 1919 and then with his wife Chaika Newman to [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City").

Perlmutter spent his childhood in the [Mount Airy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Airy,_Philadelphia "Mount Airy, Philadelphia") neighborhood of [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia"). He went to Quaker schools in nearby [Germantown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germantown,_Philadelphia "Germantown, Philadelphia"); first [Greene Street Friends School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greene_Street_Friends_School "Greene Street Friends School") for the elementary grades, followed by [Germantown Friends School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germantown_Friends_School "Germantown Friends School") for grades seven through twelve. He graduated with an AB in physics from [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard "Harvard") *magna cum laude* in 1981 and received his PhD in physics from [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1986. Perlmutter's PhD thesis, entitled "An Astrometric Search for a Stellar Companion to the Sun" and supervised by [Richard A. Muller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_A._Muller "Richard A. Muller"), described the development and use of an automated telescope to search for [Nemesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemesis_(hypothetical_star) "Nemesis (hypothetical star)") candidates. At the same time, he was using this telescope to search for Nemesis and [supernovae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernova "Supernova"), which would lead him to his award-winning work in cosmology. Perlmutter attributes the idea for an automated supernova search to [Luis Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Walter_Alvarez "Luis Walter Alvarez"), a 1968 [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Physics "List of Nobel laureates in Physics"), who shared his idea with Perlmutter's research adviser.

## Work

Perlmutter heads the [Supernova Cosmology Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernova_Cosmology_Project "Supernova Cosmology Project") at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. It was this team along with the competing [High-z Supernova Search Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-z_Supernova_Search_Team "High-z Supernova Search Team") led by Riess and Schmidt, which found evidence of the [accelerating expansion of the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_universe "Accelerating universe") based on observing [Type Ia supernova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_Ia_supernova "Type Ia supernova") in the distant universe. Type Ia supernova occurs whenever a [white dwarf star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_dwarf_star "White dwarf star") gains enough additional mass to pass above the [Chandrasekhar limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrasekhar_limit "Chandrasekhar limit"), usually by stealing additional mass from a [companion star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_star "Companion star"). Since all Type Ia supernovae are believed to occur in essentially the same way, they form a [standard candle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_candle "Standard candle") whose intrinsic [luminosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminosity "Luminosity") can be assumed to be approximately the same in all cases. By measuring the apparent luminosity of the explosion from Earth, researchers can then infer the distance to supernova. Comparing this inferred distance to the apparent [redshift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redshift "Redshift") of the explosion allows the observer to measure both the distance and relative velocity of the supernova.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Saul_Perlmutter_in_2024_at_Berkeley_Lab_02.jpg/250px-Saul_Perlmutter_in_2024_at_Berkeley_Lab_02.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Saul_Perlmutter_in_2024_at_Berkeley_Lab_02.jpg)

Perlmutter in 2024

The Supernova Cosmology Project concluded that these distant supernovae were receding more quickly than would be expected due to the [Hubble expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_expansion "Hubble expansion") alone, and, by inference, the expansion of the universe must have been accelerated over the billions of years since the supernovae occurred. The High-z Team also came to a similar conclusion. The two teams' reports were published within weeks of each other, and their conclusions were readily accepted by the scientific community due to corroborating theories. This conclusion has subsequently been supported by other lines of evidence. These findings reinvigorated research into the nature of the universe, and especially into the role of [dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_energy "Dark energy"). For this work Perlmutter was awarded the [2011 Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "2011 Nobel Prize in Physics"), shared jointly with Riess and Schmidt.

Perlmutter is a lead investigator in the [Supernova/Acceleration Probe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernova/Acceleration_Probe "Supernova/Acceleration Probe") project, which aims to build a satellite dedicated to finding and studying more supernovae in the distant universe. The goal is to more precisely determine the rate at which the universe has been accelerating. He is also a participant in the [Berkeley Earth Surface Temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Earth_Surface_Temperature "Berkeley Earth Surface Temperature") project, which aims to increase our understanding of recent [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming "Global warming") through improved analyses of climate data.

Perlmutter is a professor and currently teaches at UC Berkeley.

## Awards and recognition

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Shaw2006astro.jpg/250px-Shaw2006astro.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Shaw2006astro.jpg)

Perlmutter, [Adam Riess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Riess "Adam Riess"), and [Brian P. Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_P._Schmidt "Brian P. Schmidt") after being awarded the 2006 [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize") in Astronomy - the same astronomers would be awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "2011 Nobel Prize in Physics") in 2011

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/Nobel_Prize_2011-Nobel_lectures_KVA-DSC_7973.jpg/250px-Nobel_Prize_2011-Nobel_lectures_KVA-DSC_7973.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nobel_Prize_2011-Nobel_lectures_KVA-DSC_7973.jpg)

Perlmutter presenting his Nobel lecture at Aula Magna

In 2002, Perlmutter won the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")'s [E. O. Lawrence Award in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._O._Lawrence_Award_in_Physics "E. O. Lawrence Award in Physics"). In 2003, he was awarded the California Scientist of the Year Award, and, in 2005, he won the [John Scott Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Scott_Award "John Scott Award") and the Padua Prize. In 2006, he shared the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize") in [Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy") with [Adam Riess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Riess "Adam Riess") and [Brian P. Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_P._Schmidt "Brian P. Schmidt"). The same year, Perlmutter won the [Antonio Feltrinelli International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Feltrinelli_International_Prize "Antonio Feltrinelli International Prize").

Perlmutter and his team shared the 2007 [Gruber Cosmology Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruber_Prize_in_Cosmology "Gruber Prize in Cosmology") (a $500,000 award) with Schmidt and the High-Z Team for discovering the accelerating expansion of the universe. In 2010, Perlmutter was named a Miller Senior Fellow of the [Miller Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Institute "Miller Institute") at the [University of California Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_Berkeley "University of California Berkeley"). In 2011, Perlmutter and Riess were named co-recipients of the [Albert Einstein Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_Medal "Albert Einstein Medal").

Perlmutter shared the [2011 Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "2011 Nobel Prize in Physics") with Riess and Schmidt. The Nobel Prize includes a [SEK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_krona "Swedish krona") 10 million cash award (approximately US$1.5 million). Perlmutter received one-half of the cash prize, while Riess and Schmidt shared the other half.

In 2014, Perlmutter received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").

Perlmutter, Schmidt, Riess, and their teams shared the 2015 [Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Fundamental_Physics "Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics") with $3 million to be split among them.

A [United States Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy") 2020 [supercomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputer "Supercomputer") is named [Perlmutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perlmutter_(supercomputer) "Perlmutter (supercomputer)") in his honor.

## Family

Perlmutter is married to [Laura Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Nelson_(anthropologist)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Laura Nelson (anthropologist)"), an anthropologist at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), and they have one daughter, Noa. Perlmutter has two sisters: [Shira Perlmutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shira_Perlmutter "Shira Perlmutter") (b. 1956), an attorney, law professor, and the fourteenth [Register of Copyrights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Register_of_Copyrights "Register of Copyrights"), and [Tova Perlmutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tova_Perlmutter?action=edit&redlink=1 "Tova Perlmutter") (b. 1967), a nonprofit executive.

## Popular culture

Reference to Saul Perlmutter was made on the CBS television comedy series *[The Big Bang Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Bang_Theory "The Big Bang Theory")* during the 2011 episodes "[The Speckerman Recurrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Speckerman_Recurrence "The Speckerman Recurrence")" and "[The Rhinitis Revelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rhinitis_Revelation "The Rhinitis Revelation")". In these episodes, the lead character [Sheldon Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Cooper "Sheldon Cooper"), who aspires to win a Nobel Prize himself, jealously berates Perlmutter and questions the decision to award Perlmutter a Nobel Prize.
