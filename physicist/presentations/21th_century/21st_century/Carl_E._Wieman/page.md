**Carl Edwin Wieman** (born March 26, 1951) is an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and [educationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogy "Pedagogy") at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"), and currently the A. D. White Professor at Large at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"). In 1995, while at the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder "University of Colorado Boulder"), he and [Eric Allin Cornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Allin_Cornell "Eric Allin Cornell") produced the first true [Bose–Einstein condensate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose–Einstein_condensate "Bose–Einstein condensate") (BEC), an ultracold state of matter using [rubidium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubidium "Rubidium") atoms. In 2001, they shared the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Wolfgang Ketterle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Ketterle "Wolfgang Ketterle") “for the achievement of Bose-Einstein condensation in dilute gases of alkali atoms, and for early fundamental studies of the properties of the condensates”.

Wieman currently holds a joint appointment as Professor of Physics and Professor in the [Stanford Graduate School of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Graduate_School_of_Education "Stanford Graduate School of Education"), as well as the DRC Professor in the [Stanford University School of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University_School_of_Engineering "Stanford University School of Engineering"). He has been active in education, promoting [peer instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_instruction "Peer instruction") and founding the open online [PhET Interactive Simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhET_Interactive_Simulations "PhET Interactive Simulations"). In 2020, Wieman was awarded the [Yidan Prize in Education Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yidan_Prize "Yidan Prize") for "his contribution in developing new techniques and tools in STEM education".

## Biography

Wieman was born in [Corvallis, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvallis,_Oregon "Corvallis, Oregon") to N. Orr Wieman and Alison Marjorie Fry and graduated from [Corvallis High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvallis_High_School_(Oregon) "Corvallis High School (Oregon)"). His paternal grandfather [Henry Nelson Wieman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Nelson_Wieman "Henry Nelson Wieman") was a religious philosopher of German descent and his mother had [white Anglo-Saxon Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Anglo-Saxon_Protestant "White Anglo-Saxon Protestant") family background. Wieman earned his [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in 1973 from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") in 1977; he was also awarded a [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science "Doctor of Science"), *honoris causa* from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") in 1997. He was awarded the [Lorentz Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_Medal "Lorentz Medal") in 1998. In 2001, he won the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), along with [Eric Allin Cornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Allin_Cornell "Eric Allin Cornell") and [Wolfgang Ketterle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Ketterle "Wolfgang Ketterle"), for fundamental studies of the Bose-Einstein condensate. In 2004, he was named United States Professor of the Year among all doctoral and research universities.

In a 2020 interview given to [Federal University of Pará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_University_of_Pará "Federal University of Pará") in Brazil, Wieman recalls his youth and his journey as a physicist; the influence of other people, like teachers and his parents, on his trajectory; his path through science education and the foundation of the open educational resource [PhET Interactive Simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhET_Interactive_Simulations "PhET Interactive Simulations").

Wieman joined the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia "University of British Columbia") on 1 January 2007 and headed a well-endowed science education initiative there; he retained a twenty percent appointment at the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder "University of Colorado Boulder") to head the science education project he founded in Colorado. On 1 September 2013, Wieman joined [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") with a joint appointment in the physics department and the Graduate School of Education.

In the past several years, Wieman has been particularly involved with efforts at improving [science education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_education "Science education") and has conducted educational research on science instruction. Wieman served as Chair of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences")' Board on Science Education from 2005 to 2009. He has used and promotes [Eric Mazur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Mazur "Eric Mazur")'s [peer instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_instruction "Peer instruction"), a pedagogical system where teachers repeatedly ask [multiple-choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_choice "Multiple choice") concept questions during class, and students reply on the spot with little wireless "clicker" devices. If a large proportion of the class chooses a wrong answer, students discuss among themselves and reply again. In 2007, Wieman was awarded the [Oersted Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oersted_Medal "Oersted Medal"), which recognizes notable contributions to the teaching of physics, by the [American Association of Physics Teachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_Physics_Teachers "American Association of Physics Teachers") (AAPT).

Wieman is the founder and chairman of [PhET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhET "PhET"), a web-based directive of [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder "University of Colorado Boulder") which provides an extensive suite of simulations to improve the way that physics, chemistry, biology, earth science and math are taught and learned. [Link](https://web.archive.org/web/20100405121036/http://phet.colorado.edu/index.php)

Wieman is a member of the [USA Science and Engineering Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Science_and_Engineering_Festival "USA Science and Engineering Festival")'s Advisory Board. Wieman was nominated to be The White House's Office of Science and Technology Policy Associate Director of Science on March 24, 2010. His hearing in front of the Commerce committee occurred on May 20, 2010, and he was passed by unanimous consent. On September 16, 2010, Dr. Wieman was confirmed by unanimous consent. He left that post in June 2012 to battle [multiple myeloma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_myeloma "Multiple myeloma").
