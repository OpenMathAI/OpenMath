**Peter Andreas Grünberg** (German: [[ˈpeːtɐ ˈɡʁyːnbɛʁk]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Peter_Gr%C3%BCnberg.ogg "File:De-Peter Grünberg.ogg"); 18 May 1939 – 7 April 2018) was a German [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"), and [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") [laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_laureate "Nobel Prize laureate") for his discovery with [Albert Fert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Fert "Albert Fert") of [giant magnetoresistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_magnetoresistance "Giant magnetoresistance") which brought about a breakthrough in [gigabyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigabyte "Gigabyte") [hard disk drives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_disk_drive "Hard disk drive").

## Life and career

Grünberg was born in [Plzeň](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plzeň "Plzeň"), [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia "Czechoslovakia"), which at the time was known as Pilsen in the German-occupied [Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia "Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia") (now the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic "Czech Republic")) to the [Sudeten German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudeten_German "Sudeten German") family of Anna and Feodor A. Grünberg. They first lived in [Dýšina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dýšina "Dýšina") to the east of Plzeň. Grünberg was a [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic").

After the war, the family was interned; the parents were brought to a camp. His father, a [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire")-born engineer who since 1928 had worked for Škoda, died on 27 November 1945 in Czech imprisonment and is buried in a mass grave in Plzeň which is also inscribed with *Grünberg Theodor † 27. November 1945*. His mother Anna (who died in 2002 aged 100) had to work in agriculture and stayed with her parents in the Petermann house in Untersekerschan (Dolní Sekyřany), where her children (Peter's sister was born in 1937) were brought later. The remaining Grünberg family, like almost all Germans, was [expelled from Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Germans_from_Czechoslovakia "Expulsion of Germans from Czechoslovakia") in 1946. Seven-year-old Peter came to [Lauterbach, Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauterbach,_Hesse "Lauterbach, Hesse") where he attended [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(Germany) "Gymnasium (Germany)").

Grünberg received his intermediate diploma in 1962 from the [Johann Wolfgang Goethe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Wolfgang_Goethe_University "Johann Wolfgang Goethe University") in [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt "Frankfurt"). He then attended the [Technische Universität Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt_University_of_Technology "Darmstadt University of Technology"), where he received his BSc diploma in physics in 1966 and his Ph.D. in 1969. While there, he met and married his wife, Helma Prauser, who became a schoolteacher. From 1969 to 1972, he did postdoctoral work at [Carleton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_University "Carleton University") in [Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa "Ottawa"), Canada. He later joined the Institute for Solid State Physics at [Forschungszentrum Jülich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forschungszentrum_Jülich "Forschungszentrum Jülich"), [Jülich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jülich "Jülich"), Germany, where he became a leading researcher in the field of thin film and multilayer magnetism until his retirement in 2004.

In 1984–1985 he served as visiting scientist at [Argonne National Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratories "Argonne National Laboratories"), [Lemont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemont,_Illinois "Lemont, Illinois"), [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois "Illinois"), USA. From 1984 to 1992 he had [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation "Habilitation") process and was a lecturer (Junior Professor), and since 1992 till 2004 a Tenured Professor (ausserplanmässiger Professor) at the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne "University of Cologne"), Germany. He was also a visiting professor at the [Tohoku University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohoku_University "Tohoku University") at [Sendai-shi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendai-shi "Sendai-shi"), [Miyagi-ken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyagi-ken "Miyagi-ken"), Japan from 1998 till 2004.

In 2007, Grünberg was awarded Honorary Doctorate from the [RWTH Aachen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWTH_Aachen_University "RWTH Aachen University"), Aachen, Germany, in 2008 Honorary Doctorate from the [Saarland University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland_University "Saarland University"), and from [Gebze Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebze_Institute_of_Technology "Gebze Institute of Technology"), and in 2009 from the [University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Athens "University of Athens").

## Important work

In 1986 he discovered the antiparallel exchange coupling between [ferromagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetic "Ferromagnetic") layers separated by a thin non-ferromagnetic layer, and in 1988 he discovered the [giant magnetoresistive effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_magnetoresistance "Giant magnetoresistance") (GMR). GMR was simultaneously and independently discovered by [Albert Fert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Fert "Albert Fert") from the [Université de Paris Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris-Sud "University of Paris-Sud"). It has been used extensively in read heads of modern [hard drives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_drives "Hard drives"). Another application of the GMR effect is non-volatile, [magnetic random access memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetoresistive_RAM "Magnetoresistive RAM").

Apart from the Nobel Prize, work also has been rewarded with shared prizes in the APS [International Prize for New Materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Prize_for_New_Materials "International Prize for New Materials"), the International Union of Pure and Applied Physics Magnetism Award, the Hewlett-Packard Europhysics Prize, the [Wolf Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Physics "Wolf Prize in Physics") and the 2007 [Japan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Prize "Japan Prize"). He won the [German Future Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Future_Prize "German Future Prize") for Technology and Innovation in 1998 and was named [European Inventor of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Inventor_of_the_Year "European Inventor of the Year") in the category "Universities and research institutions" by the [European Patent Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Patent_Office "European Patent Office") and [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission "European Commission") in 2006.
