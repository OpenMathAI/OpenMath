**John Michael Kosterlitz** (born June 22, 1943) is a British-American physicist. He is a professor of physics at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University "Brown University") and the son of [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist") [Hans Kosterlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Kosterlitz "Hans Kosterlitz"). He was awarded the 2016 [Nobel Prize in physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") along with [David Thouless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Thouless "David Thouless") and [Duncan Haldane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Haldane "Duncan Haldane") for work on [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics "Condensed matter physics").

## Education and early life

He was born in [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen "Aberdeen"), Scotland, to [German-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-Jewish "German-Jewish") émigrés, the son of the pioneering biochemist [Hans Walter Kosterlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Walter_Kosterlitz "Hans Walter Kosterlitz") and Hannah Gresshöner. He was educated independently at [Robert Gordon's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gordon's_College "Robert Gordon's College") before transferring to the [Edinburgh Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Academy "Edinburgh Academy") to prepare for his university entrance examinations. He received his [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree, subsequently converted to an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_(Oxbridge_and_Dublin) "Master of Arts (Oxbridge and Dublin)") degree, at [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge "Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge"). In 1969, he earned a [DPhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") as a postgraduate student of [Brasenose College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brasenose_College,_Oxford "Brasenose College, Oxford").

## Career and research

After a few [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral "Postdoctoral") positions, including positions at the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham "University of Birmingham"), collaborating with [David Thouless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Thouless "David Thouless"), and at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"), he was appointed to the faculty of the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham "University of Birmingham") in 1974, first as a lecturer and, later, as a reader. Since 1982, he has been professor of physics at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University "Brown University"). Kosterlitz was a visiting research fellow at [Aalto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalto_University "Aalto University") in Finland and since 2016 a distinguished professor at [Korea Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Korea Institute for Advanced Study").

Kosterlitz does research in [condensed matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter "Condensed matter") theory, one- and two-dimensional physics; in [phase transitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_transitions "Phase transitions"): random systems, [electron localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_localization "Electron localization"), and [spin glasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_glasses "Spin glasses"); and in critical dynamics: [melting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting "Melting") and [freezing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freezing "Freezing").

Kosterlitz (right) and fellow [2016 Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Nobel_Prizes "2016 Nobel Prizes") winners speak with President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") at the White House

Michael Kosterlitz was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 2016, “for theoretical discoveries of topological phase transitions and topological phases of matter”; the [Maxwell Medal and Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_Medal_and_Prize "Maxwell Medal and Prize") from the British [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics "Institute of Physics") in 1981, and the [Lars Onsager Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Onsager_Prize "Lars Onsager Prize") from the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") in 2000, especially, for his work on the [Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless_transition "Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition"). Since 1992, he has been a Fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society"). He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") in 2026.

The Kosterlitz Centre at the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen "University of Aberdeen") is named in honour of his father, Hans Kosterlitz, a pioneering biochemist specializing in endorphins, who joined the faculty after fleeing Nazi persecution of Jews in 1934.

## Climbing

Kosterlitz was a pioneer in [Alpine climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climbing "Alpine climbing") in the 1960s, known for working routes in the UK, Italian Alps, and Yosemite. His notable ascents include the first ascent of Fessura Kosterlitz (graded [f6B/6a+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_(climbing) "Grade (climbing)")) in the [Orco Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orco_Valley "Orco Valley") of the Italian Alps, which was subsequently named after him, and the first repeat of American Direct (graded [ED1 6c+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_(climbing) "Grade (climbing)")) on the West Face of the [Petit Dru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiguille_du_Dru "Aiguille du Dru") in the [Mont Blanc Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Blanc_massif "Mont Blanc massif"). Kosterlitz is credited with initiating the *Nuovo Mattino* ("New Morning") movement of alpinism in the 1970s, where free, [aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_climbing "Aid climbing")-less ascents made using new technologies (such as [camming devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring-loaded_camming_device "Spring-loaded camming device")) became favoured.

## Personal life

Kosterlitz is an American citizen and is an atheist. He was diagnosed with [multiple sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_sclerosis "Multiple sclerosis") in 1978.
