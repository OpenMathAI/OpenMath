**Sir Andre Konstantin Geim** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Андре́й Константи́нович Гейм; born 21 October 1958) is a Russian-born British [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") working in England in the [School of Physics and Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Physics_and_Astronomy,_University_of_Manchester "School of Physics and Astronomy, University of Manchester") at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester "University of Manchester").

Geim was awarded the 2010 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") jointly with [Konstantin Novoselov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Novoselov "Konstantin Novoselov") for his work on [graphene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphene "Graphene"). At that time he was a [Dutch citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_citizen "Dutch citizen"). He later became a [British citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_citizen "British citizen") to accept a [knighthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knighthood_(UK) "Knighthood (UK)") and considered himself [Dutch-British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people_in_the_United_Kingdom "Dutch people in the United Kingdom"). Geim is [Regius Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regius_Professor "Regius Professor") of Physics and [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") Research Professor at the [National Graphene Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Graphene_Institute "National Graphene Institute"). Geim was previously awarded an [Ig Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ig_Nobel_Prize "Ig Nobel Prize") in 2000 for levitating a frog using its intrinsic magnetism. He is the first and only individual, as of 2025, to have received both Nobel and Ig Nobel prizes, for which he holds a Guinness World Record.

Geim has been appointed as a [chair professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chair_(professorship) "Chair (professorship)") at the [University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hong_Kong "University of Hong Kong") in February 2026 and will assume his tenure in April 2026.

## Education

Andre Geim was born to Konstantin Alekseyevich Geim and Nina Nikolayevna Bayer in [Sochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochi "Sochi"), Russia, on 21 October 1958. Both his parents were engineers of German origin; Geim says his maternal great-grandmother was Jewish. His grandfather Nikolay N. Bayer (Mykola Baier in Ukrainian) was a notable public figure in Ukraine of the early 20th century, one of its first nature conservationists and the founder/first rector of [Kaminiets-Podilskyi University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamyanets-Podilsky_Ivan_Ohienko_National_University "Kamyanets-Podilsky Ivan Ohienko National University").

In 1965, the family moved to [Nalchik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalchik "Nalchik"), where he studied at a high school. After graduation, he applied to the [Moscow Engineering Physics Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Engineering_Physics_Institute "Moscow Engineering Physics Institute"). He took the entrance exams twice, but attributes his failure to qualify to discrimination on account of his German ethnicity. He then applied to the [Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Institute_of_Physics_and_Technology "Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology") (MIPT), where he was accepted.

He said that at the time he would not have chosen to study [solid-state physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_physics "Solid-state physics"), preferring particle physics or astrophysics, but is now happy with his choice. He received a [diplom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplom "Diplom") ([MSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") degree equivalent) from MIPT in 1982 and a [Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences "Candidate of Sciences") (PhD equivalent) degree in [metal physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_physics "Metal physics") in 1987 from the [Institute of Solid State Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Solid_State_Physics_(Russia) "Institute of Solid State Physics (Russia)") (ISSP) at the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") (RAS) in [Chernogolovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernogolovka "Chernogolovka").

## Academic career

After earning his PhD with Victor Petrashov, Geim worked as a research scientist at the Institute for Microelectronics Technology (IMT) at RAS, and from 1990 as a post-doctoral fellow at the universities of [Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham "University of Nottingham") (twice), [Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath "University of Bath"), and [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen"). He said that while at Nottingham he could spend his time on research rather than "swimming through Soviet treacle", and determined to leave the Soviet Union.

He obtained his first tenured position in 1994, when he was appointed associate professor at [Radboud University Nijmegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radboud_University_Nijmegen "Radboud University Nijmegen"), where he worked on [mesoscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoscopic_physics "Mesoscopic physics") [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity"). He later gained Dutch citizenship. One of his doctoral students at Nijmegen was [Konstantin Novoselov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Novoselov "Konstantin Novoselov"), who went on to become his main research partner. However, Geim has said that he had an unpleasant time during his academic career in the Netherlands.

He was offered professorships at Nijmegen and [Eindhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven "Eindhoven"), but turned them down as he found the Dutch academic system too hierarchical and full of petty politicking. "This can be pretty unpleasant at times," he says. "It's not like the British system where every staff member is an equal quantity." On the other hand, Geim writes in his Nobel lecture that "the situation was a bit surreal because outside the university walls I received a warm-hearted welcome from everyone around, including Jan Kees and other academics." (Prof. Jan Kees Maan was the research boss of Geim during his time at Radboud University Nijmegen.)

In 2001 he became a professor of physics at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester "University of Manchester"), and was appointed director of the Manchester Centre for Mesoscience and Nanotechnology in 2002. Geim's wife and long-standing co-author, [Irina Grigorieva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina_Grigorieva_(academic) "Irina Grigorieva (academic)"), also moved to Manchester as a lecturer in 2001. The same year, they were joined by Novoselov who moved to Manchester from Nijmegen, which awarded him a PhD in 2004. Geim served as [Langworthy Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langworthy_Professor "Langworthy Professor") between 2007 and 2013, leaving this endowed professorship to Novoselov in 2012. Also, between 2007 and 2010 Geim was an [EPSRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_and_Physical_Sciences_Research_Council "Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council") Senior Research Fellow before becoming one of [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") Research Professors.

Geim holds many honorary professorships including those from [Tsinghua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsinghua_University "Tsinghua University") (China), [Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Institute_of_Physics_and_Technology "Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology") (Russia), and [Radboud University Nijmegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radboud_University_Nijmegen "Radboud University Nijmegen") (Netherlands).

In April 2026, Geim will commence his tenure as a [chair professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chair_(professorship) "Chair (professorship)") at the Faculty of Science of the [University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hong_Kong "University of Hong Kong").

## Research

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Graphen.jpg/250px-Graphen.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Graphen.jpg)

Graphene is an [atomic-scale honeycomb lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_wire_(chemistry) "Chicken wire (chemistry)") made of [carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon "Carbon") [atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom "Atom").

Geim's achievements include the discovery of a simple method for isolating single atomic layers of graphite, known as [graphene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphene "Graphene"), in collaboration with researchers at the University of Manchester and IMT. The team published their findings in October 2004 in *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_(journal) "Science (journal)")*.

Graphene consists of one-atom-thick layers of [carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon "Carbon") [atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom "Atom") arranged in two-dimensional hexagons, and is the thinnest material in the world, as well as one of the strongest and hardest. The material has many potential applications.

Geim said one of the first applications of graphene could be in the development of flexible [touchscreens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touchscreen "Touchscreen"), and that he has not patented the material because he would need a specific application and an industrial partner.

Geim also developed a [biomimetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bionics "Bionics") adhesive which became known as [gecko tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gecko_tape "Gecko tape")—so called because it works on the same principle as adhesion of [gecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gecko "Gecko") feet—research of which is still in the early stages. It is hoped that the development will eventually allow humans to scale ceilings, like [Spider-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man "Spider-Man").

Geim's research in 1997 into the possible effects of magnetism on water scaling led to the famous discovery of direct [diamagnetic levitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamagnetic_levitation "Diamagnetic levitation") of water, and led to a frog being levitated. For this experiment, he and [Michael Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Berry_(physicist) "Michael Berry (physicist)") received the 2000 Ig Nobel Prize. "We were asked first whether we dared to accept this prize, and I take pride in our sense of humor and self-deprecation that we did".

Geim has also carried out research on [mesoscopic physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoscopic_physics "Mesoscopic physics") and [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity").

He said of the range of subjects he has studied: "Many people choose a subject for their PhD and then continue the same subject until they retire. I despise this approach. I have changed my subject five times before I got my first tenured position and that helped me to learn different subjects." "When one dares to try, rewards are not guaranteed but at least it is an adventure."

Expanding the scope of his research adventures, Geim started studying low-dimensional water in 2012, after his Nobel-prize achievements. A part of this work was acknowledged by the 2018 [Prince Sultan Bin Abdulaziz International Creativity Prize for Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Sultan_bin_Abdulaziz_International_Prize_for_Water#The_Creativity_Prize "Prince Sultan bin Abdulaziz International Prize for Water").

He named his favourite hamster, H.A.M.S. ter Tisha, co-author in a 2001 research paper.

## Honours and awards

In 2006 he appeared on the *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American "Scientific American")* 50. The [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics "Institute of Physics") awarded him the 2007 [Mott Medal and Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevill_Mott_Medal_and_Prize "Nevill Mott Medal and Prize") "for his discovery of a new class of materials—free-standing two-dimensional crystals—in particular graphene". Geim was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2007 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 2007"). His certificate of election reads:

> Geim's research is notable for its internationally-recognised quality, originality and breadth. He has recently discovered a conceptually new class of materials strictly two-dimensional atomic crystals, including graphene. This has opened up a prolific research area including a new paradigm of "relativistic-like condensed matter", where relativistic quantum physics can be studied in a bench-top experiment. Previously, Geim pioneered ballistic Hall micromagnetometry and discovered a paramagnetic [Meissner effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meissner_effect "Meissner effect") and new vortex physics in superconductors. He has also realised a microfabricated adhesive, based on the gecko's climbing mechanism, now being exploited by [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont"), [BAe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems "BAE Systems") and TESA. His experiments at [Nijmegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijmegen "Nijmegen") on magnetic levitation attracted worldwide media attention and stimulated international research in this field. His earlier research on mesoscopic physics included studies of non-local and interaction phenomena, and of the quantum motion of electrons in periodic and random magnetic fields. He disseminates science to the public and schoolchildren through broadcasts and "roadshow" lectures.

He shared the 2008 [EPS Europhysics Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPS_Europhysics_Prize "EPS Europhysics Prize") with Novoselov "for discovering and isolating a single free-standing atomic layer of carbon (graphene) and elucidating its remarkable electronic properties". In 2009 he received the [Körber European Science Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Körber_European_Science_Award "Körber European Science Award"). The US [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") honoured him with the 2010 [John J. Carty Award for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_J._Carty_Award_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "John J. Carty Award for the Advancement of Science") "for his experimental realisation and investigation of graphene, the two-dimensional form of carbon".

He was awarded one of six Royal Society 2010 Anniversary Research Professorships. The Royal Society added its 2010 [Hughes Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Medal "Hughes Medal") "for his revolutionary discovery of graphene and elucidation of its remarkable properties". He was awarded honorary doctorates from [Delft University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology "Delft University of Technology"), [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich "ETH Zürich"), the [University of Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Antwerp "University of Antwerp") and the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester "University of Manchester"). In 2010, Geim was appointed as [Knight Commander of the Order of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion "Order of the Netherlands Lion") for his contribution to Dutch Science.

In 2011, Geim became a corresponding member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences"). He is [Honorary Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_professorship "Honorary professorship") of Moscow Phys-Tech, Honorary Professor of the University of Nijmegen, [Honorary Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Fellow "Honorary Fellow") of the [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics "Institute of Physics") (HonFInstP), [Honorary Fellow of the Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Honorary Fellow of the Royal Society of Chemistry") (HonFRSC), Honorary Fellow of [Singapore Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Institute_of_Physics?action=edit&redlink=1 "Singapore Institute of Physics"), Honorary Professor of the [Chinese Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Academy_of_Sciences "Chinese Academy of Sciences"). Geim was furthermore made a [Knight Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in the [2012 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_New_Year_Honours "2012 New Year Honours") for services to science. He was elected a foreign associate of the [US National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_National_Academy_of_Sciences "US National Academy of Sciences") in May 2012. He was awarded the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal "Copley Medal") in 2013 and the [Carbon Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_Medal "Carbon Medal") in 2016. Geim received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") in 2017. In addition to this he also won the 2018 Fray International Sustainability Award given to him by FLOGEN Star Outreach at SIPS 2018.

### Ig Nobel Prize in Physics

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Frog_diamagnetic_levitation.jpg/250px-Frog_diamagnetic_levitation.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Frog_diamagnetic_levitation.jpg)

Magnetically levitating a live frog, an experiment that earned Geim and [Michael Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Berry_(physicist) "Michael Berry (physicist)") the 2000 [Ig Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ig_Nobel_Prize "Ig Nobel Prize")

Geim shared the [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ig_Nobel_Prize_winners#2000 "List of Ig Nobel Prize winners") [Ig Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ig_Nobel_Prize "Ig Nobel Prize") in Physics with [Sir Michael Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Michael_Berry "Sir Michael Berry") for the frog experiment. The experiment involved magnetic properties of water scaling to [levitate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_levitation "Magnetic levitation") a small [frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frog "Frog") with [magnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnets "Magnets"), which he and Berry reported in the *[European Journal of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Journal_of_Physics "European Journal of Physics")* in 1997.

By 2022, his Ig Nobel Prize-winning work on the magnetic levitation of a frog was reportedly part of the inspiration for China's [lunar gravity research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation_of_the_Moon#Simulating_lunar_gravity "Gravitation of the Moon") facility.

### Nobel Prize in Physics

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Nobel_Prize_2010-Press_Conference_KVA-DSC_8019.jpg/250px-Nobel_Prize_2010-Press_Conference_KVA-DSC_8019.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nobel_Prize_2010-Press_Conference_KVA-DSC_8019.jpg)

[Peter Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Diamond "Peter Diamond"), [Dale T. Mortensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_T._Mortensen "Dale T. Mortensen"), [Christopher A. Pissarides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_A._Pissarides "Christopher A. Pissarides"), [Konstantin Novoselov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Novoselov "Konstantin Novoselov"), Andre Geim, [Akira Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Suzuki_(chemist) "Akira Suzuki (chemist)"), [Ei-ichi Negishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ei-ichi_Negishi "Ei-ichi Negishi"), and [Richard Heck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Heck "Richard Heck"), Nobel Prize Laureates 2010, at a press conference at the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences") in Stockholm.

On 5 October 2010, Geim was awarded the 2010 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") jointly with Novoselov "for groundbreaking experiments regarding the two-dimensional material graphene". Upon hearing of the award he said, "I'm fine, I slept well. I didn't expect the Nobel Prize this year", and that his plans for the day would not change. The lecture for the award took place on 8 December 2010 at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University "Stockholm University"). He said he hopes that graphene and other two-dimensional crystals will change everyday life as plastics did for humanity, although we need to wait for a few decades to see the results.

[Mark Miodownik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Miodownik "Mark Miodownik") said that his Ig Nobel Prize shows that people can still win a Nobel by "mucking about in a lab". The awards made him the first person to win, as an individual, both a Nobel Prize and an Ig Nobel Prize. On winning both the prizes, he has stated that

> "Frankly, I value both my Ig Nobel prize and Nobel prize at the same level and for me Ig Nobel prize was the manifestation that I can take jokes, a little bit of [self-deprecation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-deprecation "Self-deprecation") always helps."

2010, Geim was inducted into the [Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records "Guinness World Records") as the "First individual to win both a Nobel and Ig Nobel prize".

## Personal life

### View and opinions

Geim was one of 38 Nobel laureates who signed a declaration in 2010 issued by Scholars for Peace in the Middle East protesting an international initiative to [boycott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_boycotts_of_Israel "Academic boycotts of Israel") Israeli academics, institutions, and research centers.

At the *Nobel Minds* symposium in December 2010, Geim said the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") committee's choice of Chinese dissident, the imprisoned [Liu Xiaobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Xiaobo "Liu Xiaobo"), as winner of the Nobel Peace Prize, was patronising, saying

> "Look at the people who give this Nobel prize. They are retired Norwegian politicians who have spent all their careers in a safe environment, in an oil-rich modern country. They try to extend their views of the world, how the world should work and how democracy works in another country. It's very, very patronising— they have not lived in these countries. In the past 10 years, China has developed not only economically, but even the strongest human rights supporter would agree also human rights have improved. Why do we need to distort this?"

Geim has written several opinion pieces for *[The Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Financial_Times "The Financial Times")*, examples of which can be found on his university webpage.

Geim was interviewed in 2014 for *[Desert Island Discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Island_Discs "Desert Island Discs")*, a popular BBC radio programme. His music choices included *[Non, je ne regrette rien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non,_je_ne_regrette_rien "Non, je ne regrette rien")* by [Édith Piaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Édith_Piaf "Édith Piaf"), and his luxury was [Amarone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarone "Amarone") wine.

A quote from Geim was deliberately doctored by the campaign group [Vote Leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_Leave "Vote Leave") in the run-up to the [2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum "2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum"). An open letter about science, signed by 13 Conservative MPs including [Boris Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Johnson "Boris Johnson"), attempted to paint European science funding as unnecessarily bureaucratic and deliberately misrepresented Geim's views on Europe:

> As the Nobel Prize winner Andre Geim said: 'I can offer no nice words for the EU framework programmes [for research] which ... can be praised only by Europhobes for discrediting the whole idea of an effectively working Europe.'

The [ellipsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellipsis "Ellipsis") (...) present in the quotation from Geim's Nobel lecture removed the words "except for the [European Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council "European Research Council")".

In 2025, Geim automatically lost his Dutch citizenship due to his dual British nationality.

### Identity

Geim has a complex ancestry which is described in detail in his Nobel Prize [autobiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autobiography "Autobiography"). There, Geim stated that most of his family are [ethnic Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Germans "Ethnic Germans"), his father descended from [Volga Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_German "Volga German") and his mother mostly an [ethnic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_German "Ethnic German") as well. Both his father and paternal grandfather had spent many years of their lives as prisoners in [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia") in [Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalin "Stalin")'s [Gulags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulags "Gulags"), and "some of the family had been prisoners in German concentration camps". He also states that he "suffered from [anti-Semitism in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_the_Soviet_Union "Antisemitism in the Soviet Union") because my name sounds Jewish".

Geim summarises his identity as follows. "To the best of my knowledge, the only Jew in the family was my great-grandmother, with the rest on both sides being German. Having lived and worked in several European countries, I consider myself European and do not believe that any further [taxonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy_(general) "Taxonomy (general)") is necessary, especially in such a fluid world as the world of science."
