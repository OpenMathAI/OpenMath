**David Jonathan Gross** ([/ɡroʊs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); born February 19, 1941) is an American [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics") and [string theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_theory "String theory"). He shared the 2004 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Frank Wilczek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wilczek "Frank Wilczek") and [Hugh David Politzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_David_Politzer "Hugh David Politzer") "for the discovery of [asymptotic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_freedom "Asymptotic freedom") in the theory of the [strong interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_interaction "Strong interaction")".

Gross is the Chancellor's Chair Professor of Theoretical Physics at the [Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavli_Institute_for_Theoretical_Physics "Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics") (KITP) of the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara "University of California, Santa Barbara") (UCSB), and was formerly the KITP director and holder of their [Frederick W. Gluck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Gluck "Frederick Gluck") Chair in Theoretical Physics. He is also a faculty member in the [UCSB Physics Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCSB_Physics_Department "UCSB Physics Department") and is affiliated with the Institute for Quantum Studies at [Chapman University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapman_University "Chapman University") in California. He is a foreign member of the [Chinese Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Academy_of_Sciences "Chinese Academy of Sciences").

## Early life and education

Gross was born in Washington, D.C., in February 1941 to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family from Austro-Hungary. His grandfather was born in Hungary. His parents were Nora (Faine) and [Bertram Myron Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertram_Myron_Gross "Bertram Myron Gross") (1912–1997). Gross studied in high school at the [Hebrew University Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_Secondary_School "Hebrew University Secondary School") in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem"). He received his bachelor's degree from the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem "Hebrew University of Jerusalem"), in 1962. He received his Ph.D. in physics from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1966, under the supervision of [Geoffrey Chew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Chew "Geoffrey Chew").

## Research and career

In 1973, Gross, working with his first graduate student, [Frank Wilczek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wilczek "Frank Wilczek"), at Princeton University, discovered [asymptotic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_freedom "Asymptotic freedom")—the primary feature of non-Abelian gauge theories—which led Gross and Wilczek to the formulation of [quantum chromodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chromodynamics "Quantum chromodynamics") (QCD), the theory of the strong nuclear force. Asymptotic freedom is a phenomenon where the nuclear force weakens at short distances, which explains why experiments at very high energy can be understood as if nuclear particles are made of non-interacting quarks. Therefore, the closer [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarks "Quarks") are to each other, the less the [strong interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_interaction "Strong interaction") (or [color charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge "Color charge")) is between them; when quarks are in extreme proximity, the nuclear force between them is so weak that they behave almost as free particles. The flip side of asymptotic freedom is that the force between quarks grows stronger as one tries to separate them. This is the reason why the nucleus of an atom can never be broken into its quark constituents.

QCD completed the [Standard Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Model "Standard Model"), which details the three basic forces of particle physics—the electromagnetic force, the weak force, and the strong force. Gross was awarded the 2004 Nobel Prize in Physics, with Politzer and Wilczek, for this discovery.

Gross, with [Jeffrey A. Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_A._Harvey "Jeffrey A. Harvey"), [Emil Martinec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Martinec "Emil Martinec"), and [Ryan Rohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Rohm "Ryan Rohm") also formulated the theory of the [heterotic string](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotic_string "Heterotic string"). The four were whimsically nicknamed the "Princeton String Quartet." He continues to do research in this field at the KITP.

He was a junior fellow at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") (1966–69) and a Eugene Higgins Professor of Physics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") until 1997, when he began serving as Princeton's Thomas Jones Professor of Mathematical Physics Emeritus. He has received many honors, including a MacArthur Foundation Fellowship in 1987 and the Dirac Medal in 1988.

## Activism

In 2003, Gross was one of 22 Nobel Laureates who signed the [Humanist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism_and_Its_Aspirations "Humanism and Its Aspirations").

Gross is one of the 20 American recipients of the Nobel Prize in Physics to sign a letter addressed to President George W. Bush in May 2008, urging him to "reverse the damage done to basic science research in the Fiscal Year 2008 Omnibus Appropriations Bill" by requesting additional emergency funding for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")'s [Office of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Science "Office of Science"), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), and the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology").

In 2015, Gross signed the *[Mainau Declaration 2015 on Climate Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainau_Declaration "Mainau Declaration")* on the final day of the 65th [Lindau Nobel Laureate Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindau_Nobel_Laureate_Meetings "Lindau Nobel Laureate Meetings"). The declaration was signed by a total of 76 Nobel Laureates and handed to then-President of the French Republic, François Hollande, as part of the successful [COP21 climate summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Climate_Change_Conference "Paris Climate Change Conference") in Paris.

In 2026, at the 75th Lindau Nobel Laureate Meeting, Gross and two other Laureates issued this urgent warning about the growing risk of nuclear war, that the half life of our civilization is 34 years, and presented concrete policy recommendations to governments worldwide. Their call to action was built on the [2024 Mainau Declaration on Nuclear Weapons](https://www.lindau-nobel.org/mainau-declaration-2024/), now signed by 104 Nobel Laureates.

In July 2026, the Vatican Nobel Assembly issued the groundbreaking "Rome Declaration for an Unarmed and Disarming Peace in the Age of Artificial Intelligence, Nuclear and Autonomous Weapons, New Digital Protocols, and Emerging Models of Digital Development, signed by more than 200 of the world’s most authoritative figures. Gross was a co-organizer of the Assembly, which was inspired by Pope Leo XIV’s Encyclical [Magnifica humanitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnifica_humanitas "Magnifica humanitas"), dedicated to safeguarding the human person in the age of artificial intelligence.

## Family

Gross' first wife was Shulamith (Toaff), and they had two children. He also has a stepdaughter by his second wife, Jacquelyn Savani. He has three brothers, including Larry Gross, professor of communication, [Samuel R. Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_R._Gross "Samuel R. Gross"), professor of law, and [Theodore (Teddy) Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Gross "Teddy Gross"), a playwright.
