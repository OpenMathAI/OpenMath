**Phillip James Edwin Peebles** [CC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Canada "Order of Canada") [OM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Manitoba "Order of Manitoba") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (born April 25, 1935) is a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians "Canadians")-[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans "Americans") [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysicist "Astrophysicist"), astronomer, and theoretical [cosmologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_cosmology "Physical cosmology") who gained the [Albert Einstein Professorship in Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_Professorship_in_Science "Albert Einstein Professorship in Science"), emeritus, at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"). He is widely regarded as one of the world's leading [theoretical cosmologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_cosmology "Theoretical cosmology") in the period since 1970, with major theoretical contributions to [primordial nucleosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primordial_nucleosynthesis "Primordial nucleosynthesis"), [dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter "Dark matter"), the [cosmic microwave background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_microwave_background "Cosmic microwave background"), and [structure formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_formation "Structure formation").

Peebles was awarded half of the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 2019 for his theoretical discoveries in [physical cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_cosmology "Physical cosmology"). He shared the prize with [Michel Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Mayor "Michel Mayor") and [Didier Queloz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Queloz "Didier Queloz") for their discovery of an exoplanet orbiting a sun-like star. While much of his work relates to the development of the universe from its first few seconds, he is more skeptical about what we can know about the very beginning, and stated, "It's very unfortunate that one thinks of the beginning whereas in fact, we have no good theory of such a thing as the beginning."

Peebles has described himself as a convinced [agnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnosticism "Agnosticism").

## Early life

Peebles was born on April 25, 1935, in St. Vital in present-day [Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg "Winnipeg"), [Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba "Manitoba"), Canada, the son of Ada Marion (Green), a homemaker, and Andrew Charles Peebles, who worked for the [Winnipeg Grain Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Grain_Exchange "Winnipeg Grain Exchange"). He completed his [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") at the [University of Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manitoba "University of Manitoba"). He then went on to pursue graduate studies at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), where he received his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree in physics in 1962, completing a doctoral dissertation titled "Observational Tests and Theoretical Problems Relating to the Conjecture That the Strength of the Electromagnetic Interaction May Be Variable" under the supervision of [Robert Dicke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Dicke "Robert Dicke"). He remained at Princeton for his whole career. Peebles was a Member in the School of Natural Sciences at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") during the academic year 1977–78; he made subsequent visits during 1990–91 and 1998–99.

## Academic career

Most of Peebles's work since 1964 has been in the field of [physical cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_cosmology "Physical cosmology") to determine the origins of the universe. In 1964, there was very little interest in this field and it was considered a "dead end" but Peebles remained committed to studying it. Peebles has made many important contributions to the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang "Big Bang") model. In 1965, Peebles, with Dicke and two other Princeton physicists, connected the [cosmic microwave background radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_microwave_background_radiation "Cosmic microwave background radiation") discovered by [Arno Allan Penzias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arno_Allan_Penzias "Arno Allan Penzias") and [Robert Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Woodrow_Wilson "Robert Woodrow Wilson") to a plasma cooling event in the early universe. This connection created a sensation leading to wide acceptance of the Big Bang model among astronomers and physicists. Peebles then used the temperature of that radiation, 3K, to compute the first accurate estimate of primordial elemental abundance based on nuclear physics and cosmic expansion, a model called [Big Bang nucleosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang_nucleosynthesis "Big Bang nucleosynthesis").

Along with making major contributions to [dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter "Dark matter"), and [dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_energy "Dark energy"), he was the leading pioneer in the theory of cosmic [structure formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_formation "Structure formation") in the 1970s. Long before it was considered a serious, quantitative branch of physics, Peebles was studying physical cosmology and has done much to establish its respectability. Peebles said, "It was not a single step, some critical discovery that suddenly made cosmology relevant but the field gradually emerged through a number of experimental observations. Clearly one of the most important during my career was the detection of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation that immediately attracted attention [...] both experimentalists interested in measuring the properties of this radiation and theorists, who joined in analyzing the implications". His Shaw Prize citation states "He laid the foundations for almost all modern investigations in cosmology, both theoretical and observational, transforming a highly speculative field into a precision science."

Peebles has a long record of innovating the basic ideas, which would be extensively studied later by other scientists. For instance, in 1987, he proposed the [primordial isocurvature baryon model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primordial_isocurvature_baryon_model "Primordial isocurvature baryon model") for the development of the early universe. Similarly, Peebles contributed to establishing the dark matter problem in the early 1970s. Peebles is also known for the [Ostriker–Peebles criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostriker–Peebles_criterion "Ostriker–Peebles criterion"), relating to the stability of [galactic formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy_formation_and_evolution "Galaxy formation and evolution").

Peebles's body of work was recognized with him being named a 2019 Nobel Laureate in Physics, "for theoretical discoveries in physical cosmology"; Peebles shared half the prize with [Michel Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Mayor "Michel Mayor") and [Didier Queloz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Queloz "Didier Queloz") who had been the first to discover an [exoplanet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoplanet "Exoplanet") around a [main sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_sequence "Main sequence") star.

Peebles was elected as a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1977 and a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1988.
