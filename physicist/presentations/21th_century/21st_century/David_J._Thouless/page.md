**David James Thouless** ([/ˈθaʊlɛs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); 21 September 1934 – 6 April 2019) was a British [condensed-matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics "Condensed matter physics") [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). He was awarded the 1990 [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Physics "Wolf Prize in Physics") and a laureate of the 2016 Nobel Prize for physics along with [F. Duncan M. Haldane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Haldane "Duncan Haldane") and [J. Michael Kosterlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Michael_Kosterlitz "J. Michael Kosterlitz") for theoretical discoveries of [topological phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_phase "Topological phase") transitions and topological phases of matter.

## Education

Born on 21 September 1934 in [Bearsden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bearsden "Bearsden"), Scotland, to English parents, Priscilla (Gorton) Thouless, an English teacher, and [Robert Thouless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Thouless "Robert Thouless") a psychologist and broadcaster. David Thouless was educated at [St Faith's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Faith's_School "St Faith's School") then [Winchester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_College "Winchester College") and earned a Bachelor of Arts degree in [Natural Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Sciences_(Cambridge) "Natural Sciences (Cambridge)") from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") as an undergraduate student of [Trinity Hall, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Hall,_Cambridge "Trinity Hall, Cambridge"). He obtained his PhD at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"), where [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe "Hans Bethe") was his doctoral advisor.

## Career and research

Thouless was a [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher "Postdoctoral researcher") at [Lawrence Berkeley Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_Laboratory "Lawrence Berkeley Laboratory"), [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), and also worked in the physics department from 1958 to 1959, giving a course on atomic physics. He was the first director of studies in physics at [Churchill College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge "Churchill College, Cambridge"), Cambridge, in 1961–1965, professor of mathematical physics at the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham "University of Birmingham") in the United Kingdom in 1965–1978, and professor of applied science at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") from 1979 to 1980, before becoming a professor of physics at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington "University of Washington") in [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle") in 1980. Thouless made many theoretical contributions to the understanding of extended systems of atoms and electrons, and of nucleons. He also worked on [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity") phenomena, properties of nuclear matter, and excited collective motions within nuclei.

Thouless made many important contributions to the theory of [many-body problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Many-body_problem "Many-body problem"). For [atomic nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus "Atomic nucleus"), he cleared up the concept of 'rearrangement energy' and derived an expression for the [moment of inertia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_of_inertia "Moment of inertia") of deformed nuclei. In [statistical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics"), he contributed many ideas to the understanding of ordering, including the concept of '[topological ordering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_ordering "Topological ordering")'. Other important results relate to localised [electron states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_configuration "Electron configuration") in disordered [lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_lattice "Crystal lattice").

### Awards and honours

Thouless was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1979 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1979"), a Fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") (1986), a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), and a member of the US [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") (1995). Among his awards are the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Physics "Wolf Prize in Physics") for Physics (1990), the [Paul Dirac Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_Medal_(IOP) "Dirac Medal (IOP)") of the [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics "Institute of Physics") (1993), the [Lars Onsager Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Onsager_Prize "Lars Onsager Prize") of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") (2000), and the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") (2016).

## Personal life

Thouless married Margaret Elizabeth Scrase in 1958 and together they had three children. In 2016, Thouless was reported to be suffering from [dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia "Dementia"). He died on 6 April 2019 in Cambridge, aged 84. His family donated his [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") to [Trinity Hall, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Hall,_Cambridge "Trinity Hall, Cambridge").
