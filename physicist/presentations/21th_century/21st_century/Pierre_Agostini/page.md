**Pierre Agostini** (French pronunciation: [[pjɛʁ aɡɔstini]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 23 July 1941) is a French [experimental physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics") and [Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus "Emeritus") professor at the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University "Ohio State University") in the United States, known for his pioneering work in strong-field laser physics and [attosecond science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attosecond_science "Attosecond science"). He is especially known for the observation of [above-threshold ionization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above-threshold_ionization "Above-threshold ionization") and the invention of the [reconstruction of attosecond beating by interference of two-photon transitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_of_attosecond_beating_by_interference_of_two-photon_transitions "Reconstruction of attosecond beating by interference of two-photon transitions") (RABBITT) technique for characterization of [attosecond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attosecond_physics "Attosecond physics") light pulses. He was jointly awarded the 2023 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics").

## Education and career

Pierre Agostini was born in [Tunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunis "Tunis"), in the [French protectorate of Tunisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_of_Tunisia "French protectorate of Tunisia"), in 1941. He obtained his *[baccalauréat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baccalauréat "Baccalauréat")* at the [Prytanée national militaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prytanée_national_militaire "Prytanée national militaire") school in 1959 in [La Flèche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Flèche "La Flèche"), France.

Agostini studied physics at [Aix-Marseille University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-Marseille_University "Aix-Marseille University"), where he subsequently received a [B.Ed.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Education "Bachelor of Education") degree (*licence d'enseignement*) in physics in 1961, and an [M.A.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Advanced_Studies "Master of Advanced Studies") degree (*diplôme d'études approfondies*) in 1962. In 1968 he completed a [doctoral degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_degree "Doctoral degree") there, on multilayer dielectric filters for the ultraviolet, titled *Appareillage permettant la réalisation de filtres multidiélectriques UV : Étude des couches Sb2O3*.

After his doctorate, he became a researcher at [CEA Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEA_Paris-Saclay "CEA Paris-Saclay") in 1969 and stayed there until 2002. During this time, Agostini worked in the lab of Gérard Mainfray and Claude Manus, where he researched on multiphoton ionization using the powerful lasers there. They are the first to observe [above-threshold ionization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above-threshold_ionization "Above-threshold ionization") in 1979 in [xenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenon "Xenon") gas.

In 2001, Agostini and his team at CEA Saclay along with Harm Geert Muller at the Dutch Foundation for Fundamental Research on Matter (FOM), using an advanced laser at the [Laboratoire d'Optique Appliquée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratoire_d'Optique_Appliquée?action=edit&redlink=1 "Laboratoire d'Optique Appliquée"), managed to create a train of pulses each 250 [attoseconds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attosecond "Attosecond") in duration. By recombining the ultrashort [ultraviolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet "Ultraviolet") pulses with the original [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared "Infrared") light they created an interference effect that allowed him to characterize the length and repetition rate of the pulses.

Agostini was a visiting scientist at the [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory") in the U.S. state of [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(state) "New York (state)") between 2002 and 2004, where he worked in [Louis F. DiMauro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_F._DiMauro "Louis F. DiMauro")'s group. He became professor of physics at the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University "Ohio State University") (OSU) in 2005 and ran a laboratory jointly with Louis F. DiMauro who moved a year earlier to OSU. Agostini became emeritus professor at OSU in 2018.

## Honors and awards

Agostini received the Gustave Ribaud prize in 1995 from the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences"). In 2003, he received the [Gay-Lussac–Humboldt Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay-Lussac–Humboldt_Prize "Gay-Lussac–Humboldt Prize") and the Joop Los fellowship from the Dutch Foundation for Fundamental Research on Matter (FOM), he also received the [William F. Meggers Award in Spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Meggers_Award_in_Spectroscopy "William F. Meggers Award in Spectroscopy") in 2007 from the [Optical Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optica_(society) "Optica (society)") (OSA), and is a [Humboldt Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation "Alexander von Humboldt Foundation"). He was elected a Fellow of OSA in 2008 “for leadership in the development of innovative experiments providing major insights into the dynamics of the nonlinear response of atoms and molecules submitted to strong infrared laser pulses.”

In 2023, he received the Nobel Prize in Physics for "experimental methods that generate attosecond pulses of light for the study of electron dynamics in matter" along with [Anne L'Huillier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_L'Huillier "Anne L'Huillier") and [Ferenc Krausz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferenc_Krausz "Ferenc Krausz").
