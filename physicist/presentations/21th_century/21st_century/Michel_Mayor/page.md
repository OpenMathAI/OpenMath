**Michel Gustave Édouard Mayor** (French pronunciation: [[miʃɛl majɔʁ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 12 January 1942) is a Swiss [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysicist "Astrophysicist") and professor emeritus at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva "University of Geneva")'s Department of [Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy"). He formally retired in 2007, but remains active as a researcher at the [Observatory of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatory_of_Geneva "Observatory of Geneva"). He is co-laureate of the 2019 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") along with [Jim Peebles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Peebles "Jim Peebles") and [Didier Queloz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Queloz "Didier Queloz"), and the winner of the 2010 [Viktor Ambartsumian International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Ambartsumian#Viktor_Ambartsumian_International_Prize "Viktor Ambartsumian") and the 2015 [Kyoto Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Prize "Kyoto Prize").

Together with [Didier Queloz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Queloz "Didier Queloz") in 1995, he discovered [51 Pegasi b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51_Pegasi_b "51 Pegasi b"), the first [extrasolar planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrasolar_planet "Extrasolar planet") orbiting a sun-like star, [51 Pegasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51_Pegasi "51 Pegasi"). For this achievement, they were awarded the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physics "for the discovery of an exoplanet orbiting a solar-type star" resulting in "contributions to our understanding of the evolution of the universe and Earth's place in the cosmos".
Related to the discovery, Mayor noted that humans will never migrate to such exoplanets since they are "much, much too far away ... [and would take] hundreds of millions of days using the means we have available today". However, due to discoveries by Mayor, searching for [extraterrestrial communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterrestrial_communications "Extraterrestrial communications") from [exoplanets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoplanet "Exoplanet") may now be a more practical consideration than thought earlier.

## Education and career

Mayor obtained an [MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") degree in [Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") from the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne "University of Lausanne") (1966) and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in [Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy") from the [Geneva Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Observatory "Geneva Observatory") in 1971. He was a researcher at the [Institute of Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Astronomy,_Cambridge "Institute of Astronomy, Cambridge") at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") in 1971. Subsequently, he spent [sabbatical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbatical "Sabbatical") semesters at the [European Southern Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Southern_Observatory "European Southern Observatory") (ESO) in northern Chile and at the [Institute for Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Astronomy_(Hawaii) "Institute for Astronomy (Hawaii)") of the [University of Hawaiʻi System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawaiʻi_System "University of Hawaiʻi System").

From 1971 to 1984, Mayor worked as a research associate at the [Observatory of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatory_of_Geneva "Observatory of Geneva"), which is home to the astronomy department of the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva "University of Geneva"). He became an associate professor at the university in 1984. In 1988, the university named him a full professor, a position he held until his retirement in 2007. Mayor was director of the Observatory of Geneva from 1998 to 2004. He is a professor emeritus at the University of Geneva.

## Research

Mayor's research interests include extrasolar planets (also known as [exoplanets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoplanets "Exoplanets")), instrumentation, statistical properties of [double stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_stars "Double stars"), [globular cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globular_cluster "Globular cluster") dynamics, galactic structure and [kinematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinematics "Kinematics"). Mayor's doctoral thesis at the University of Geneva was devoted to the spiral structure of galaxies.

During his time as a research associate, there had been strong interest in developing [photoelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoelectric_effect "Photoelectric effect")-based [Doppler spectrometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doppler_spectroscopy "Doppler spectroscopy") to obtain more accurate measurements of [radial velocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radial_velocity "Radial velocity") of stellar objects compared to existing photographic methods. Following preliminary work by [Roger Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Griffin_(astronomer) "Roger Griffin (astronomer)") in 1967 to show the feasibility of photoelectric measurements of radial velocities, Mayor worked with André Baranne at the [Marseille Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille_Observatory "Marseille Observatory") to develop CORAVEL, a photoelectric spectrometer capable of highly accurate radial velocity measurements, which allow measurement of star movements, orbital periods of [binary stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_stars "Binary stars"), and even the rotational speed of stars.

This research led to various fields of interest, including the study of statistical characteristics of solar-type binary stars. With fellow researcher Antoine Duquennoy, they examined the radial velocities of several systems believed to be binary stars in 1991. Their results found that a subset of these may in fact be single star systems with substellar secondary objects. Desiring more accurate radial velocity measurements, Mayor, along with Baranne at Marseille, and with graduate student [Didier Queloz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Queloz "Didier Queloz"), developed [ELODIE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELODIE_spectrograph "ELODIE spectrograph"), a new spectrograph based on the work of CORAVEL, which was estimated to have an accuracy of 15 m/s for bright stars, improving upon the 1 km/s from CORAVEL. ELODIE was developed with the specific intent to determine if the substellar secondary objects were [brown dwarf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_dwarf "Brown dwarf") stars or potentially giant planets.

By 1994, ELODIE was operational at Geneva and Mayor and Queloz began their survey of Sun-like systems with suspected substellar secondary objects. In July 1995, the pair's survey of [51 Pegasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51_Pegasi "51 Pegasi") affirmed that there was an [exoplanet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoplanet "Exoplanet") orbiting it, identified as [51 Pegasi b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51_Pegasi_b "51 Pegasi b"), which was later classified as a [hot-Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Jupiter "Hot Jupiter")–type planet. This was the first exoplanet to be found orbiting a [main-sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main-sequence "Main-sequence") star, as opposed to planets that orbited the remains of a star. Mayor's and Queloz's discovery of an exoplanet launched great interest is searching for other exoplanets since. On 21 March 2022, the 5000th exoplanet beyond the [Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System "Solar System") was confirmed.

Mayor's work focused more on improving instrumentation for radial velocity measurements to improve detecting exoplanets and measuring their properties. Mayor led a team to further improve ELODIE to increase velocity measurement accuracy to 1 m/s via the [High Accuracy Radial Velocity Planet Searcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Accuracy_Radial_Velocity_Planet_Searcher "High Accuracy Radial Velocity Planet Searcher") (HARPS) installed on the [ESO 3.6 m Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESO_3.6_m_Telescope "ESO 3.6 m Telescope") at [La Silla Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Silla_Observatory "La Silla Observatory") in Chile by 2003. Mayor led the team that used HARPS to seek out other exoplanets. In 2007, Mayor was one of 11 European scientists who discovered [Gliese 581c](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gliese_581c "Gliese 581c"), the first extrasolar planet in a star's habitable zone, from the ESO telescope. In 2009, Mayor and his team discovered the lightest exoplanet ever detected around a [main sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_sequence "Main sequence") star: [Gliese 581e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gliese_581e "Gliese 581e"). Nonetheless, Mayor noted that humans will never migrate to such exoplanets since they are "much, much too far away ... [and would take] hundreds of millions of days using the means we have available today". However, due to discoveries by Mayor, searching for extraterrestrial communications from exoplanets may now be a more practical consideration than thought earlier.

## Awards and distinctions

In 1998, Mayor was awarded the Swiss [Marcel Benoist Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Benoist_Prize "Marcel Benoist Prize") in recognition of his work and its significance for human life. As of 2003, he was a member of the board of trustees. He received the [Prix Jules Janssen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Jules_Janssen "Prix Jules Janssen") from the [Société astronomique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_astronomique_de_France "Société astronomique de France") (French Astronomical Society) in 1998.

In 2000, he was awarded the [Balzan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balzan_Prize "Balzan Prize"). Four years later, he was awarded the [Albert Einstein Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_Medal "Albert Einstein Medal"). In 2005, he received the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize") in Astronomy, along with American astrophysicist [Geoffrey Marcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Marcy "Geoffrey Marcy"). Mayor was made a knight of the French [Legion d'Honneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_d'Honneur "Legion d'Honneur") in 2004.

In collaboration with Pierre-Yves Frei, Mayor wrote a book in French called *Les Nouveaux mondes du Cosmos* ([Seuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seuil "Seuil"), 260 pages), which was awarded the *Livre de l'astronomie 2001* prize by the 17th Astronomy Festival Haute Maurienne.

Mayor has received honorary doctorate degrees from eight universities: [Katholieke Universiteit Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katholieke_Universiteit_Leuven "Katholieke Universiteit Leuven") ([Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium "Belgium")), 2001; [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne "École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne") ([Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne "Lausanne"), Switzerland) (2002); [Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_University_of_Rio_Grande_do_Norte "Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte") ([Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil "Brazil")), 2006; [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University") ([Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden")), 2007; [Paris Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Observatory "Paris Observatory") ([France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France")), 2008; [Université Libre de Bruxelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Libre_de_Bruxelles "Université Libre de Bruxelles") (Belgium), 2009; [University of Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Provence "University of Provence") ([Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille "Marseille"), France), 2011, and [Université Joseph Fourier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Joseph_Fourier "Université Joseph Fourier") ([Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenoble "Grenoble"), France), 2014.

Mayor has received the 2011 [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Awards "BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Awards") of Basic Sciences (together with his former student [Didier Queloz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Queloz "Didier Queloz")) for developing new astronomical instruments and experimental techniques that led to the first observation of planets around Sun-like stars. Asteroid [125076 Michelmayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/125076_Michelmayor "125076 Michelmayor"), discovered by Swiss amateur astronomer [Michel Ory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Ory "Michel Ory") at the [Jura Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_Observatory "Jura Observatory") in 2001, was named in his honor. The official [naming citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meanings_of_minor-planet_names:_125001–126000#m076 "Meanings of minor-planet names: 125001–126000") was published by the [Minor Planet Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Planet_Center "Minor Planet Center") on 21 August 2013 ([M.P.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Planet_Circulars "Minor Planet Circulars") 84674).

In 2015, he was awarded the [Gold Medal of the Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Medal_of_the_Royal_Astronomical_Society "Gold Medal of the Royal Astronomical Society"), and the [Kyoto Prize in Basic Sciences](https://www.kyotoprize.org/en/laureates/michel_mayor/). In 2017, he received the [Wolf Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Physics "Wolf Prize in Physics"). He and [Didier Queloz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Queloz "Didier Queloz") (also from Switzerland) were awarded one half of the 2019 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for the discovery of the exoplanet [51 Pegasi b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51_Pegasi_b "51 Pegasi b").
