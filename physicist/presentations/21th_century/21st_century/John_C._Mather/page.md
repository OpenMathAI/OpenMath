**John Cromwell Mather** (born August 7, 1946) is an American [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics "Astrophysics") and [cosmologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology "Cosmology"). He shared the 2006 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [George Smoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Smoot "George Smoot") for the "for their discovery of the [black body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_body "Black body") form and [anisotropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropy "Anisotropy") of the [cosmic microwave background radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_microwave_background_radiation "Cosmic microwave background radiation")".

This work helped cement the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang "Big Bang") theory of the universe. According to the Nobel Prize committee, "the COBE-project can also be regarded as the starting point for [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology "Cosmology") as a precision science."

Mather is a senior astrophysicist at the [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") [Goddard Space Flight Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddard_Space_Flight_Center "Goddard Space Flight Center") (GSFC) in [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "Maryland") and adjunct professor of physics at the [University of Maryland College of Computer, Mathematical, and Natural Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland_College_of_Computer,_Mathematical,_and_Natural_Sciences "University of Maryland College of Computer, Mathematical, and Natural Sciences"). In 2007, [*Time* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)") listed Mather among the 100 Most Influential People in The World. In October 2012, he was listed again by [*Time* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)") in a special issue on New Space Discoveries as one of the 25 most influential people in space.

Mather is one of the 20 American recipients of the Nobel Prize in Physics to sign a letter addressed to President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") in May 2008, urging him to "reverse the damage done to basic science research in the Fiscal Year 2008 Omnibus Appropriations Bill" by requesting additional emergency funding for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")'s [Office of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Science "Office of Science"), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), and the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology").

Mather served as the senior project scientist for the [James Webb Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope "James Webb Space Telescope") (JWST) from 1995 until 2023, when he was succeeded by [Jane Rigby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Rigby_(astrophysicist) "Jane Rigby (astrophysicist)").

In 2014, Mather delivered an address on the James Webb Space Telescope at the second [Starmus Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starmus_Festival "Starmus Festival") in the Canary Islands.
