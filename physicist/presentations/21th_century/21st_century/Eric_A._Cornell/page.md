**Eric Allin Cornell** (born December 19, 1961) is an American physicist who, along with [Carl E. Wieman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_E._Wieman "Carl E. Wieman"), synthesized the first [Bose–Einstein condensate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose–Einstein_condensate "Bose–Einstein condensate") in 1995. Cornell, Wieman and [Wolfgang Ketterle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Ketterle "Wolfgang Ketterle") shared the 2001 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") “for the achievement of Bose-Einstein condensation in dilute gases of alkali atoms, and for early fundamental studies of the properties of the condensates”.

## Biography

Cornell was born in [Palo Alto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto "Palo Alto"), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California"), where his parents were completing graduate degrees at nearby [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"). Two years later he moved to [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts"), where his father was a professor of [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering "Civil engineering") at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT "MIT"). Here he grew up with his younger brother and sister, with year-long stints in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California "Berkeley, California"), and [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon "Lisbon"), Portugal, accompanying his father whilst on sabbatical.

In Cambridge he attended [Cambridge Rindge and Latin School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Rindge_and_Latin "Cambridge Rindge and Latin"). The year before his graduation he moved back to California with his mother and finished high school at [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco")'s [Lowell High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowell_High_School_(San_Francisco) "Lowell High School (San Francisco)"), a local [magnet school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet_school "Magnet school") for academically talented students.

After high school he enrolled at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"), where he was to meet his future wife, Celeste Landry. As an undergraduate he earned money as an assistant in the various [low-temperature physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-temperature_physics "Low-temperature physics") groups on campus. He was doing well both in his courses and his jobs in the labs and seemed set for a career in physics. He however doubted whether he wished to pursue such a career, or rather a different one in literature or politics. Halfway through his undergraduate years he went to [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") and [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") for nine months to volunteer teaching conversational English and to study [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"). He returned to Stanford with the intent to study physics. He graduated with honors and distinction in 1985.

For graduate school he returned to MIT. There he joined [David Pritchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_E._Pritchard "David E. Pritchard")'s group, which had a running experiment that tried to measure the mass of the [electron neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_neutrino "Electron neutrino") from the [beta decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_decay "Beta decay") of [tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium "Tritium"). Although he was unable to determine the [mass of the neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_of_the_neutrino "Mass of the neutrino"), Cornell did obtain his PhD in 1990.

After obtaining his doctorate he joined [Carl Wieman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Wieman "Carl Wieman") at the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder "University of Colorado Boulder") as a postdoctoral researcher on a small [laser cooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_cooling "Laser cooling") experiment. During his two years as a postdoc he came up with a plan to combine [laser cooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_cooling "Laser cooling") and [evaporative cooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporative_cooler "Evaporative cooler") in a magnetic trap to create a [Bose–Einstein condensate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose–Einstein_condensate "Bose–Einstein condensate") (BEC). Based on his proposal he was offered a permanent position at [JILA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JILA "JILA")/[NIST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIST "NIST") in Boulder. In 1995 Cornell and Wieman gave the University of Colorado's [George Gamow Memorial Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow_Memorial_Lectures "George Gamow Memorial Lectures"). For synthesizing the first Bose–Einstein condensate in 1995, Cornell, Wieman, and [Wolfgang Ketterle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Ketterle "Wolfgang Ketterle") shared the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 2001. In 1997, [Deborah S. Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_S._Jin "Deborah S. Jin") joined Cornell's group at JILA, where she led the team that produced the [fermionic condensate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermionic_condensate "Fermionic condensate") in 2003.

He is currently a professor at the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder "University of Colorado Boulder") and a physicist (NIST fellow) at the [United States Department of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Commerce "United States Department of Commerce") [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology"). His lab is located at [JILA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JILA "JILA"). He was awarded the [Lorentz Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_Medal "Lorentz Medal") in 1998 and is a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_AAAS "Fellow of the AAAS") of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science").

He was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 2005.

## Personal life

Cornell married Celeste Landry in 1995 mere months before the BEC experiment succeeded. Their first daughter was born in 1996, and their second daughter in 1998.

In October 2004, his left arm and shoulder were amputated in an attempt to stop the spread of [necrotizing fasciitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necrotizing_fasciitis "Necrotizing fasciitis"). He was discharged from the hospital in mid-December, having recovered from the infection, and returned to work part-time in April 2005. Cornell has run in the [Bolder Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolder_Boulder "Bolder Boulder") several times since moving to Boulder in 1990, most recently in 2022.
