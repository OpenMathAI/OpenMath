**Hiroshi Amano** (天野 浩, *Amano Hiroshi*; born September 11, 1960) is a Japanese [materials scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_scientist "Materials scientist") specializing in the field of [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor") technology. He is a co-inventor of the blue [LED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LED "LED"), for which he was awarded the 2014 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") along with [Isamu Akasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isamu_Akasaki "Isamu Akasaki") and [Shuji Nakamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuji_Nakamura "Shuji Nakamura").

## Early life and education

Hiroshi Amano was born on September 11, 1960, in [Hamamatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamamatsu "Hamamatsu"), [Shizuoka Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shizuoka_Prefecture "Shizuoka Prefecture"), the son of father Tatsuji and mother Yoshiko.

During elementary school days, Amano played [soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer "Soccer") as a goalkeeper and [softball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softball "Softball") as a catcher. He was also passionate about [amateur radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_radio "Amateur radio") and despite hating studying, he was good at [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"). Upon entering high school, he began taking his studies seriously and became a top student by studying every day late into the night.

In 1979, Amano entered [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University"), where he graduated from the School of Engineering in 1983. He then completed a master's course in the Graduate School of Engineering in 1985, and received his [D.Eng.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.Eng. "D.Eng.") in 1989.

## Career

In 1988, Amano became a research associate in the School of Engineering at [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University"). From 1992 to 2010, he was an assistant professor, associate professor, and a professor in the Faculty of Science and Technology at [Meijo University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meijo_University "Meijo University"). In 2010, Amano returned to Nagoya University, where he is currently a professor in the Graduate School of Engineering. He has been director of the Akasaki Research Center since 2011.

## Research

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Blue_light_emitting_diodes_over_a_proto-board.jpg/250px-Blue_light_emitting_diodes_over_a_proto-board.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Blue_light_emitting_diodes_over_a_proto-board.jpg)

Blue [light-emitting diodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-emitting_diode "Light-emitting diode")

Amano joined Professor [Isamu Akasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isamu_Akasaki "Isamu Akasaki")'s group in 1982 as an undergraduate student. Since then, he has been doing research on the growth, characterization and device applications of group III [nitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitride "Nitride") semiconductors, which are well known as materials used in blue light-emitting diodes today. In 1985, he developed low-temperature deposited buffer layers for the growth of group III nitride semiconductor films on a sapphire substrate, which led to the realization of group-III-nitride semiconductor based light-emitting diodes and laser diodes. In 1989, he succeeded in growing p-type GaN and fabricating a p-n-junction-type GaN-based UV/blue light-emitting diode for the first time in the world.

Known to be keen on research, Amano's laboratory was always lit late at night, such as weekdays, holidays, New Year's Day, and was called "no night castle". According to his students in the laboratory, Amano has an optimistic and temperate personality, and is never angry.

## Family

Amano's wife was a Japanese lecturer at [Comenius University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comenius_University "Comenius University") in Bratislava, Slovakia.
