**Peter Ware Higgs** (29 May 1929 – 8 April 2024) was a British [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physicist "Theoretical physicist"), professor at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh "University of Edinburgh"), and [Nobel laureate in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate_in_Physics "Nobel laureate in Physics") for his work on the mass of [subatomic particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_particle "Subatomic particle").

In 1964, Higgs was the single author of one of the three [milestone papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_PRL_symmetry_breaking_papers "1964 PRL symmetry breaking papers") published in *[Physical Review Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Review_Letters "Physical Review Letters")* (*PRL*) that proposed that [spontaneous symmetry breaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_symmetry_breaking "Spontaneous symmetry breaking") in [electroweak theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroweak_theory "Electroweak theory") could explain the [origin of mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_mass "Origin of mass") of [elementary particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle "Elementary particle") in general and of the [W and Z bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W_and_Z_bosons "W and Z bosons") in particular. This [Higgs mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_mechanism "Higgs mechanism") predicted the existence of a new particle, the [Higgs boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_boson "Higgs boson"), the detection of which became one of the great goals of physics. In 2012, [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN") announced the [discovery of the Higgs boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_the_Higgs_boson "Discovery of the Higgs boson") at the [Large Hadron Collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Hadron_Collider "Large Hadron Collider"). The Higgs mechanism is generally accepted as an important ingredient in the [Standard Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Model "Standard Model") of [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics"), without which certain particles would have no mass.

For this work, Higgs received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), which he shared with [François Englert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Englert "François Englert") in 2013.

## Early life and education

Higgs was born in the [Elswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elswick,_Tyne_and_Wear "Elswick, Tyne and Wear") district of [Newcastle upon Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne "Newcastle upon Tyne"), England, to Thomas Ware Higgs (1898–1962) and his wife Gertrude Maude née Coghill (1895–1969). His father worked as a sound engineer for the BBC, and as a result of childhood asthma, together with the family moving around because of his father's job and later [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Higgs missed some early schooling and was taught at home. When his father relocated to [Bedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford "Bedford"), Higgs stayed behind in [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol "Bristol") with his mother, and was largely raised there. He attended [Cotham Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotham_Grammar_School "Cotham Grammar School") in Bristol from 1941 to 1946, where he was inspired by the work of one of the school's [alumni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alumni "Alumni"), [Paul Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac"), a founder of the field of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics").

In 1946, at the age of 17, Higgs moved to [City of London School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London_School "City of London School"), where he specialised in mathematics, then in 1947 to [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London "King's College London"), where he graduated with a first-class honours degree in physics in 1950 and achieved a master's degree in 1952. He was awarded an [1851 Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1851_Research_Fellowship "1851 Research Fellowship") from the [Royal Commission for the Exhibition of 1851](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_for_the_Exhibition_of_1851 "Royal Commission for the Exhibition of 1851"), and performed his doctoral research in molecular physics under the supervision of [Charles Coulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Coulson "Charles Coulson") and [Christopher Longuet-Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Longuet-Higgins "Christopher Longuet-Higgins"). He was awarded a PhD degree in 1954 with a thesis entitled *Some problems in the theory of molecular vibrations* from the university.

## Career and research

After finishing his doctorate, Higgs was appointed a Senior [Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Fellow "Research Fellow") at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh "University of Edinburgh") (1954–56). He then held various posts at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London "Imperial College London"), and [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London") (where he also became a temporary lecturer in mathematics). He returned to the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh "University of Edinburgh") in 1960 to take up the post of Lecturer at the Tait Institute of Mathematical Physics, allowing him to settle in the city he had enjoyed while [hitchhiking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchhiking "Hitchhiking") to the Western [Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Highlands "Scottish Highlands") as a student in 1949. He was promoted to [Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_(academic_rank) "Reader (academic rank)"), became a [Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh "Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh") (FRSE) in 1974 and was promoted to a personal chair of Theoretical Physics in 1980. On his retirement in 1996, he became an [emeritus professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_professor "Emeritus professor").

Higgs was elected [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1983 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1983") in 1983 and [Fellow of the Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Institute_of_Physics "Fellow of the Institute of Physics") (FInstP) in 1991. He was awarded the [Rutherford Medal and Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_Medal_and_Prize "Rutherford Medal and Prize") in 1984. He received an honorary degree from the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol "University of Bristol") in 1997. In 2008, he received an Honorary Fellowship from [Swansea University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swansea_University "Swansea University") for his work in particle physics.

At Edinburgh, Higgs first became interested in [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass "Mass"), developing the idea that particles – massless when the universe began – acquired mass a fraction of a second later as a result of interacting with a theoretical field (which became known as the [Higgs field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_field "Higgs field")). Higgs postulated that this field permeates space, giving mass to all elementary subatomic particles interacting with it.

The [Higgs mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_mechanism "Higgs mechanism") postulates the existence of the Higgs field, which confers mass on quarks and leptons; this causes only a tiny portion of the masses of other subatomic particles, such as protons and neutrons. In these, gluons that bind quarks together confer most of the particle mass. The original basis of Higgs's work came from the Japanese-born theorist and Nobel Prize laureate [Yoichiro Nambu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoichiro_Nambu "Yoichiro Nambu") from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"). Nambu had proposed a theory known as [spontaneous symmetry breaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_symmetry_breaking "Spontaneous symmetry breaking") based on what was known to happen in [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity") in condensed matter, which incorrectly predicted massless particles (the [Goldstone's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldstone's_theorem "Goldstone's theorem")).

Higgs reportedly developed the fundamentals of his theory after returning to his Edinburgh [New Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Town,_Edinburgh "New Town, Edinburgh") apartment from a failed weekend camping trip to the Highlands. He stated that there was no "eureka moment" in the development of the theory. He wrote a short paper exploiting a loophole in Goldstone's theorem (massless Goldstone particles need not occur when local symmetry is spontaneously broken in a relativistic theory) and published it in *[Physics Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_Letters "Physics Letters")*, a European physics journal edited at [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN"), in Switzerland, in 1964.

Higgs wrote a second paper describing a theoretical model (the [Higgs mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_mechanism "Higgs mechanism")), but the paper was rejected (the editors of *Physics Letters* judged it "of no obvious relevance to physics"). Higgs wrote an extra paragraph and sent his paper to *[Physical Review Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Review_Letters "Physical Review Letters")*, another leading physics journal, which published it later in 1964. This paper predicted a new massive spin-zero boson (later named the [Higgs boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_boson "Higgs boson")).
Other physicists, [Robert Brout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Brout "Robert Brout") and [François Englert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Englert "François Englert") and [Gerald Guralnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Guralnik "Gerald Guralnik"), [C. R. Hagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._R._Hagen "C. R. Hagen") and [Tom Kibble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Kibble "Tom Kibble")
had reached similar conclusions at about the same time. In the published version, Higgs quotes Brout and Englert, and the third paper quotes the previous ones. The three papers written on this boson discovery by Higgs, Guralnik, Hagen, Kibble, Brout, and Englert were each recognised as milestone papers by *[Physical Review Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Review_Letters "Physical Review Letters")* 50th-anniversary celebration. While each of these famous papers took similar approaches, the contributions and differences between the [1964 PRL symmetry breaking papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_PRL_symmetry_breaking_papers "1964 PRL symmetry breaking papers") are noteworthy. The mechanism had been proposed in 1962 by [Philip Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Warren_Anderson "Philip Warren Anderson") although he did not include a crucial relativistic model.

On 4 July 2012, CERN announced the [ATLAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATLAS_experiment "ATLAS experiment") and [Compact Muon Solenoid (CMS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Muon_Solenoid "Compact Muon Solenoid") experiments had seen strong indications for the presence of a new particle, which could be the Higgs boson, in the mass region around 126 gigaelectronvolts ([GeV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeV "GeV")).
Speaking at the seminar in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva "Geneva"), Higgs commented "It's really an incredible thing that it's happened in my lifetime." Ironically, this probable confirmation of the Higgs boson was made at the same place where the editor of *Physics Letters* rejected Higgs's paper.

## Awards and honours

Higgs was honoured with several awards in recognition of his work, including the 1981 [Hughes Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Medal "Hughes Medal") from the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society"); the 1984 [Rutherford Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_Medal_and_Prize "Rutherford Medal and Prize") from the [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics "Institute of Physics"); the 1997 [Dirac Medal and Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_Medal_(IOP) "Dirac Medal (IOP)") for outstanding contributions to theoretical physics from the [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics "Institute of Physics"); the 1997 [High Energy and Particle Physics Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Energy_and_Particle_Physics_Prize "High Energy and Particle Physics Prize") by the [European Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Physical_Society "European Physical Society"); the 2004 [Wolf Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Physics "Wolf Prize in Physics"); the 2009 [Oskar Klein Memorial Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Klein_Memorial_Lecture "Oskar Klein Memorial Lecture") medal from the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences"); the 2010 [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") [J. J. Sakurai Prize for Theoretical Particle Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Sakurai_Prize_for_Theoretical_Particle_Physics "J. J. Sakurai Prize for Theoretical Particle Physics"); a unique Higgs Medal from the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh "Royal Society of Edinburgh") in 2012; and the Royal Society awarded him the 2015 [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal "Copley Medal"), the world's oldest scientific prize.

### Civic awards

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Professor_Peter_Higgs%2C_The_Edinburgh_Award%2C_July_2014.jpg/250px-Professor_Peter_Higgs%2C_The_Edinburgh_Award%2C_July_2014.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Professor_Peter_Higgs,_The_Edinburgh_Award,_July_2014.jpg)

Edinburgh Award handprints

Higgs was the recipient of the Edinburgh Award for 2011. He was the fifth person to receive the Award, which was established in 2007 by the [City of Edinburgh Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Edinburgh_Council "City of Edinburgh Council") to honour an outstanding individual who has made a positive impact on the city and gained national and international recognition for [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh "Edinburgh").

Higgs was presented with an engraved [loving cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loving_cup "Loving cup") by [the Rt Hon George Grubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Grubb "George Grubb"), [Lord Provost of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Provost_of_Edinburgh "Lord Provost of Edinburgh"), in a ceremony held at the City Chambers on Friday, 24 February 2012. The event also marked the unveiling of his handprints in the City Chambers quadrangle, where they had been engraved in Caithness stone alongside those of previous Edinburgh Award recipients.

Higgs was awarded the Freedom of the City of [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol "Bristol") in July 2013. The Dirac-Higgs Science Centre in Bristol is also named in his honour. In April 2014, he was also awarded the Freedom of the City of Newcastle upon Tyne. He was also honoured with a brass plaque installed on the Newcastle Quayside as part of the Newcastle Gateshead Initiative Local Heroes Walk of Fame.

### Higgs Centre for Theoretical Physics

On 6 July 2012, [Edinburgh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_University "Edinburgh University") announced a new centre named after Professor Higgs to support future research in [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics"). The Higgs Centre for Theoretical Physics brings together scientists from around the world to seek "a deeper understanding of how the universe works". The centre is currently based within the [James Clerk Maxwell Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clerk_Maxwell "James Clerk Maxwell"), home of the university's [School of Physics and Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh_School_of_Physics_and_Astronomy "University of Edinburgh School of Physics and Astronomy") and the iGEM 2015 team (ClassAfiED). The university has also established a chair of theoretical physics in the name of Peter Higgs, currently held by cosmologist [Neil Turok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Turok "Neil Turok").

### Nobel Prize in Physics

On 8 October 2013, it was announced that Higgs and [François Englert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Englert "François Englert") would share the 2013 Nobel Prize in Physics "for the theoretical discovery of a mechanism that contributes to our understanding of the origin of mass of subatomic particles, and which recently was confirmed through the discovery of the predicted fundamental particle, by the ATLAS and CMS experiments at CERN's Large Hadron Collider". Higgs admitted he had gone out to avoid the media attention so he was informed he had been awarded the prize by an ex-neighbour on his way home, since he did not have a mobile phone.

In November 2025, it was reported that Higgs had left his [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") to Edinburgh University in his will.

### Member of the Order of the Companions of Honour

Higgs turned down a [knighthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knighthood "Knighthood") in 1999, but in 2012, he accepted membership of the [Order of the Companions of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Companions_of_Honour "Order of the Companions of Honour"). He later said that he only accepted the order because he was wrongly assured that the award was in the gift of the Queen alone. He also expressed cynicism towards the [honours system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honours_system_in_the_United_Kingdom "Honours system in the United Kingdom"), and the way the system "is used for political purposes by the government in power". The order confers no title or precedence, but recipients of the order are entitled to use the post-nominal letters CH*.* In the same interview he also stated that when people ask what the CH after his name stands for, he replies "it means I'm an honorary Swiss." He received the order from the Queen at an investiture at [Holyrood House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holyrood_House "Holyrood House") on 1 July 2014.

## Personal life and political views

Higgs married Jody Williamson, an American lecturer in linguistics at Edinburgh and a fellow activist with the [Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_Nuclear_Disarmament "Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament") (CND), in 1963. Their first son was born in August 1965. Higgs had two sons: Christopher and Jonny, a jazz musician. He also had two grandchildren: Jo, a writer, and Bonnie (Kemplay), a musician. Higgs and Williamson separated in 1972 but remained friends until she died in 2008.

Higgs was an activist in the CND while in London and later in Edinburgh but resigned his membership when the group extended its remit from campaigning against nuclear weapons to campaigning against nuclear power, too. He was a [Greenpeace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenpeace "Greenpeace") member until the group opposed [genetically modified organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetically_modified_organism "Genetically modified organism"). Higgs was awarded the 2004 [Wolf Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Physics "Wolf Prize in Physics") (sharing it with [Robert Brout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Brout "Robert Brout") and [François Englert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Englert "François Englert")) but declined to attend the awards ceremony in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem") in protest at
Israel's treatment of [Palestinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinians "Palestinians"). Higgs was actively involved in the Edinburgh University branch of the [Association of University Teachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_University_Teachers "Association of University Teachers"), through which he agitated for greater staff involvement in the management of the physics department.

Higgs was an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist"). He described [Richard Dawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dawkins "Richard Dawkins") as having adopted a "[fundamentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamentalist "Fundamentalist")" view of non-atheists. Higgs expressed displeasure with the nickname the "God particle". Although it has been reported that he believed the term "might offend people who are religious", Higgs stated that this is not the case, lamenting the letters he has received which claim the God particle was predicted in the [Torah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah "Torah"), the [Qur'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur'an "Qur'an") and [Buddhist scriptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_scriptures "Buddhist scriptures"). In a 2013 interview with [Decca Aitkenhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decca_Aitkenhead "Decca Aitkenhead"), Higgs was quoted as saying:

> I'm not a believer. Some people get confused between the science and the theology. They claim that what happened at [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN") proves the existence of God. The church in Spain has also been guilty of using that name as evidence for what they want to prove. [It] reinforces confused thinking in the heads of people who are already thinking in a confused way. If they believe that story about creation in seven days, are they being intelligent?

— *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")*, 6 December 2013

The nickname for the Higgs boson is usually attributed to [Leon M. Lederman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_M._Lederman "Leon M. Lederman"), the author of the book *[The God Particle: If the Universe Is the Answer, What Is the Question?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_God_Particle:_If_the_Universe_Is_the_Answer,_What_Is_the_Question%3F "The God Particle: If the Universe Is the Answer, What Is the Question?")* but the name is the result of the suggestion of Lederman's publisher; Lederman had initially intended to refer to it as the "goddamn particle".

## Death

Higgs died after a short illness at home in Edinburgh on 8 April 2024, aged 94.
