**Albert Fert** (French: [[albɛʁ fɛʁ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 7 March 1938) is a French [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and one of the discoverers of [giant magnetoresistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_magnetoresistance "Giant magnetoresistance") which brought about a breakthrough in [gigabyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigabyte "Gigabyte") [hard disks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_disks "Hard disks"). Currently, he is an emeritus professor at [Paris-Saclay University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Saclay_University "Paris-Saclay University") in [Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orsay "Orsay"), scientific director of a joint laboratory (*[Unité mixte de recherche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unité_mixte_de_recherche "Unité mixte de recherche")*) between the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique "Centre national de la recherche scientifique") (National Scientific Research Centre) and [Thales Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group "Thales Group"), and adjunct professor at [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University "Michigan State University"). He was awarded the 2007 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") together with [Peter Grünberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Grünberg "Peter Grünberg").

## Biography

In 1962 Albert Fert graduated from the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure "École Normale Supérieure") in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), where he attended courses by the physicists [Alfred Kastler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Kastler "Alfred Kastler") and [Jacques Friedel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Friedel "Jacques Friedel"). (As an undergraduate he had strong interests in photography and cinema, and was a great admirer of the work of [Ingmar Bergman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingmar_Bergman "Ingmar Bergman").)

After the École Normale Supérieure, Fert attended the [University of Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Grenoble "University of Grenoble") and in 1963 received his Ph.D. (*doctorat de troisième cycle*) from the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris") with a thesis prepared in the fundamental electronic [Orsay Faculty of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Saclay_Faculty_of_Sciences "Paris-Saclay Faculty of Sciences") and in the physical spectrometry laboratory of the University of Grenoble Faculty of Sciences.

On his return from military service in 1965, Fert became assistant professor at the Orsay Faculty of Sciences of the [University of Paris XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_XI "University of Paris XI") ([Université Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Paris-Sud "Université Paris-Sud")). Under the direction of Ian Campbell at the [Laboratory of Solid Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratoire_de_Physique_des_Solides "Laboratoire de Physique des Solides") he prepared for a doctorate Sc.D. (*doctorat des sciences*) in Physical Sciences on the electrical transport properties of [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel "Nickel") and [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron "Iron"), which he completed in 1970. He was named professor in 1976.

Fert worked as research director for the university's [condensed-matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics "Condensed matter physics") laboratory (1970–1995) prior to moving to Unité Mixte de Physique, a laboratory jointly run by the Université Paris-Sud and the technology company [Thales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group "Thales Group").

In 1988, Albert Fert at Orsay in France, and Peter Gruenberg at Jülich in Germany, simultaneously and independently discovered [giant magnetoresistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_magnetoresistance "Giant magnetoresistance") (GMR) in magnetic multilayers. This discovery is considered to mark the birth of spintronics, a new subfield of electronics that exploits not only the electric charge of the electrons but also their magnetism (associated with their intrinsic angular momentum, or spin). Spintronics has already contributed important applications; the introduction of GMR read heads in hard disks has led to a considerable increase in the density of information storage. Other spintronic properties are exploited in magnetic random access memory (MRAM), which may soon impact computer and phone technology. In 2007, Fert and Prof. Grünberg jointly received the [Japan Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Prize "Japan Prize") (300.000 Euro) for their discovery of GMR.

In October 2006, Professor Fert received an honorary doctorate from the Department of Physics of the [University of Kaiserslautern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Kaiserslautern "Technical University of Kaiserslautern").

Fert has made many contributions to the development of spintronics. Following his 2007 Nobel Prize, he began to explore possible spintronics applications of topological properties at surfaces and interfaces. His most recent works are on the topologically protected magnetic solitons called skyrmions and on the conversion between charge current and spin current by topological insulators.
