**Serge Haroche** (French pronunciation: [[sɛʁʒ aʁɔʃ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 11 September 1944) is a French [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who was awarded the 2012 [Nobel Prize for Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Physics "Nobel Prize for Physics") jointly with [David J. Wineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_J._Wineland "David J. Wineland") for "ground-breaking experimental methods that enable measuring and manipulation of individual quantum systems", a study of the particle of light, the [photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon "Photon"). This and his other works developed [laser spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_spectroscopy "Laser spectroscopy"). Since 2001, Haroche is a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor "Professor") at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France") and holds the chair of [quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") and in 2022 he had the Fermi Chair of Physics at [University of Rome La Sapienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_La_Sapienza "University of Rome La Sapienza").

In 1971 he defended his doctoral thesis in physics at the [University of Paris VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_VI "University of Paris VI"): his research had been conducted under the direction of [Claude Cohen-Tannoudji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Cohen-Tannoudji "Claude Cohen-Tannoudji").

## Early life and education

Haroche was born in [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca "Casablanca") to Albert Haroche (1920–1998), from a [Moroccan Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Morocco "History of the Jews in Morocco") family, and Valentine Haroche, born [Roubleva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rublev_(surname) "Rublev (surname)") (1921–1998), a teacher who was born in [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa "Odessa") to a Jewish family of physicians who relocated to [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco "Morocco") in the early 1920s. His family had mixed [Sephardic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jews "Sephardic Jews") and [Ashkenazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews "Ashkenazi Jews") origins. His father, a lawyer trained in [Rabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabat "Rabat"), was one of seven children born to a family of teachers, Isaac and Esther Haroche, who worked at the [*École de l’Alliance israélite*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Israélite_Universelle "Alliance Israélite Universelle") (AIU).

Both paternal grandparents of Serge Haroche had been AIU students in their respective hometowns of [Marrakesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrakesh "Marrakesh") and [Tétouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tétouan "Tétouan") (the school which Esther Azerad attended in Tétouan had been founded in 1862; it was the first school of the AIU network).

Haroche's family left Morocco in 1956 at the end of the [French protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_of_Morocco "French protectorate of Morocco") treaty, and settled in France.

## Career

Haroche worked in the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique "Centre national de la recherche scientifique") (CNRS) as a research scientist from 1967 to 1975 at the [French UMR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_UMR "French UMR") [Kastler–Brossel Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastler–Brossel_Laboratory "Kastler–Brossel Laboratory"), and spent a year (1972–1973) as a visiting post-doc in [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"), in [Arthur Leonard Schawlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Leonard_Schawlow "Arthur Leonard Schawlow")'s team. In 1975 he moved to a professor position at [Paris VI University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_VI_University "Paris VI University"). At the same time he taught in other institutions, in particular at the [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique "École polytechnique") (1973–1984), [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (1980), [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") (1981), [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") (1984–1993) and [Conservatoire national des arts et métiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatoire_national_des_arts_et_métiers "Conservatoire national des arts et métiers") (2000). He was head of the Physics department at the [École normale supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure "École Normale Supérieure") from 1994 to 2000.

Since 2001, Haroche has been a professor at the Collège de France and holds the [chair of quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"). He is a member of the [Société Française de Physique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_Française_de_Physique "Société Française de Physique"), the [European Physical society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Physical_Society "European Physical Society") and a fellow and member of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society").

In September 2012, Serge Haroche was elected by his peers to the position of administrator of the Collège de France.

On 9 October 2012 Haroche was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), together with the American physicist [David Wineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Wineland "David Wineland"), for their work regarding measurement and manipulation of individual quantum systems.

In 2020, Haroche was appointed by [European Commissioner for Innovation, Research, Culture, Education and Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commissioner_for_Innovation,_Research,_Culture,_Education_and_Youth "European Commissioner for Innovation, Research, Culture, Education and Youth") [Mariya Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariya_Gabriel "Mariya Gabriel") to serve on an independent search committee for the next president of the [European Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council "European Research Council") (ERC), chaired by [Helga Nowotny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helga_Nowotny "Helga Nowotny"). in 2022 he had the Fermi Chair of Physics at [University of Rome La Sapienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_La_Sapienza "University of Rome La Sapienza")

## Research

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Serge_Haroche_%28Nobel_in_Physics_2012%29_in_Stockholm%2C_June_2016.jpg/250px-Serge_Haroche_%28Nobel_in_Physics_2012%29_in_Stockholm%2C_June_2016.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Serge_Haroche_(Nobel_in_Physics_2012)_in_Stockholm,_June_2016.jpg)

Haroche, who won Nobel Prize in Physics in 2012, visited Stockholm, June 2016, as a member of the Wallenberg Foundation Scientific Advisory Board.

Haroche works primarily in [atomic physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_physics "Atomic physics") and [quantum optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_optics "Quantum optics"). He is principally known for showing [quantum decoherence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_decoherence "Quantum decoherence") by experimental observation, while working with colleagues at the École normale supérieure in Paris in 1996.

After a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") dissertation on dressed atoms under the supervision of [Claude Cohen-Tannoudji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Cohen-Tannoudji "Claude Cohen-Tannoudji") (who would receive the 1997 Nobel Prize) from 1967 to 1971, he developed new methods for [laser spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_spectroscopy "Laser spectroscopy"), based on the study of [quantum beats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_beats "Quantum beats") and [superradiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superradiance "Superradiance"). He then moved on to [Rydberg atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rydberg_atom "Rydberg atom"), giant atomic states particularly sensitive to [microwaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave "Microwave"), which makes them well adapted for studying the interactions between light and matter. He showed that such atoms, coupled to a [superconducting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity") cavity containing a few [photons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon "Photon"), are well-suited to the testing of [quantum decoherence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_decoherence "Quantum decoherence") and to the realization of [quantum logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_logic "Quantum logic") operations necessary for the treatment of [quantum information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_information "Quantum information").

## Personal life

Haroche lives in Paris; he is married to the sociologist Claudine Haroche (née Zeligson), also descending from the [Russian Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Russia "History of the Jews in Russia") émigrés family, with two children. He is the uncle of French singer–songwriter and actor [Raphaël Haroche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphaël_Haroche "Raphaël Haroche") (known as Raphaël, his stage name).
