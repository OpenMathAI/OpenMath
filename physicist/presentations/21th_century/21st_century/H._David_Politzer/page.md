**Hugh David Politzer** ([/ˈpɑːlɪtsər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); born August 31, 1949) is an American [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics"). He shared the 2004 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [David Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gross "David Gross") and [Frank Wilczek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wilczek "Frank Wilczek") "for the discovery of [asymptotic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_freedom "Asymptotic freedom") in the theory of the [strong interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_interaction "Strong interaction")".

He is the Richard Chace Tolman Professor of Theoretical Physics at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology").

## Life and career

Politzer was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"). His parents escaped to England from [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia "Czechoslovakia") in 1939 and immigrated to the U.S. after [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). He graduated from the [Bronx High School of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_High_School_of_Science "Bronx High School of Science") in 1966, received his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") in physics from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan") in 1969, and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in 1974 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), where his graduate advisor was [Sidney Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Coleman "Sidney Coleman").

In his first published article, which appeared in 1973, Politzer described the phenomenon of asymptotic freedom: the closer [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark "Quark") are to each other, the weaker the [strong interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_interaction "Strong interaction") will be between them. When quarks are in extreme proximity, the nuclear force between them is so weak that they behave almost like free particles. This result—independently discovered at around the same time by Gross and Wilczek at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University")—was extremely important in the development of [quantum chromodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chromodynamics "Quantum chromodynamics"). With [Thomas Appelquist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Appelquist "Thomas Appelquist"), Politzer also played a central role in predicting the existence of "[charmonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charmonium "Charmonium")", a subatomic particle formed of a [charm quark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charm_quark "Charm quark") and a [charm antiquark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiparticle "Antiparticle").

Politzer was a junior fellow at the [Harvard Society of Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Society_of_Fellows "Harvard Society of Fellows") from 1974 to 1977 before moving to the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") (Caltech), where he is currently professor of theoretical physics. In 1986, he was awarded the [J. J. Sakurai Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakurai_Prize "Sakurai Prize") for Theoretical Particle Physics by the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society"). In 1989, he appeared in a minor role in the movie *[Fat Man and Little Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat_Man_and_Little_Boy_(film) "Fat Man and Little Boy (film)")*, as [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project") physicist [Robert Serber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Serber "Robert Serber"). In 2003 he was awarded the [High Energy Particle Physics Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Energy_and_Particle_Physics_Prize "High Energy and Particle Physics Prize") of the [European Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Physical_Society "European Physical Society"), jointly with [David J. Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gross "David Gross") and [Frank Wilczek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wilczek "Frank Wilczek"). The Nobel Prize in Physics 2004 was awarded jointly to [David J. Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_J._Gross "David J. Gross"), H. David Politzer and [Frank Wilczek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wilczek "Frank Wilczek") "for the discovery of asymptotic freedom in the theory of the strong interaction."

Politzer is one of the 20 American recipients of the Nobel Prize in Physics to sign a letter addressed to President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") in May 2008, urging him to "reverse the damage done to basic science research in the Fiscal Year 2008 Omnibus Appropriations Bill" by requesting additional emergency funding for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")’s [Office of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Science "Office of Science"), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), and the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology").

Politzer was elected as a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 2011.

Politzer plays the [banjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo "Banjo") and has done research on the physics of the instrument.

## Trivia

Politzer was the lead vocalist in the 1980s for Professor Politzer and the Rho Mesons, which put out their single, "The Simple Harmonic Oscillator".

Politzer's [Erdős-Bacon number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős-Bacon_number "Erdős-Bacon number") is 5 – via appearing in *[Fat Man and Little Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat_Man_and_Little_Boy_(film) "Fat Man and Little Boy (film)")* with [Laura Dern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Dern "Laura Dern") (in *[Novocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novocaine_(2001_film) "Novocaine (2001 film)")* with [Kevin Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Bacon "Kevin Bacon")) and publishing once with [Sidney Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Coleman "Sidney Coleman") ([Erdős number 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős_number#Two "Erdős number")).
