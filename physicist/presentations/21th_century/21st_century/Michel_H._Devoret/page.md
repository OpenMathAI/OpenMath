**Michel Henri Devoret** (French: [[miʃɛl dəvɔʁɛ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 5 March 1953) is a French–American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). He is Professor of Physics at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara "University of California, Santa Barbara"), and Professor Emeritus of Applied Physics at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"). He serves as the Chief Scientist for Quantum Hardware at [Google Quantum AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Quantum_AI "Google Quantum AI"). He is known for the development of various [superconducting quantum computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing "Superconducting quantum computing") architectures, including the quantronium, the [transmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmon "Transmon"), and the [fluxonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#Fluxonium "Superconducting quantum computing").

Devoret shared the 2025 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [John Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Clarke_(physicist) "John Clarke (physicist)") and [John M. Martinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_M._Martinis "John M. Martinis") for their joint work on [macroscopic quantum phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroscopic_quantum_phenomena "Macroscopic quantum phenomena") in [superconducting circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_quantum_electrodynamics "Circuit quantum electrodynamics").

## Early life and education

Michel Henri Devoret was born on 5 March 1953 in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"). He has stated that his parents were of Jewish background, even though they were not religious.

Devoret graduated with an [engineer's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer's_degree "Engineer's degree") in 1975 from École nationale supérieure des télécommunications (now [Télécom Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Télécom_Paris "Télécom Paris")). He then obtained a DEA (*[Diplôme d'études approfondies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplôme_d'études_approfondies "Diplôme d'études approfondies")*) in Quantum Optics in 1976, followed by a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in Quantum Condensed Matter Physics in 1982, both from the University of Orsay (now [Paris-Saclay University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Saclay_University "Paris-Saclay University")). He performed his doctoral research at [CEA Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEA_Paris-Saclay "CEA Paris-Saclay") in the group of [Anatole Abragam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatole_Abragam "Anatole Abragam"), under the supervision of [Neil S. Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_S._Sullivan "Neil S. Sullivan").

## Career

Devoret worked as a [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher "Postdoctoral researcher") in [John Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Clarke_(physicist) "John Clarke (physicist)")'s group at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), from 1982 to 1984. Together, with [John M. Martinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_M._Martinis "John M. Martinis"), a graduate student at the time, they demonstrated for the first time the [quantized energy levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_qubit#Quantized_energy_levels "Phase qubit") of a [Josephson junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_effect "Josephson effect") in 1985.

Devoret then returned to France and founded the Quantronics group at the Orme des Merisiers laboratory of CEA Saclay together with [Daniel Esteve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Esteve?action=edit&redlink=1 "Daniel Esteve") and Cristian Urbina. The group measured the traversal time of tunnelling, invented an electron pump, observed the charge of Cooper pairs directly, and developed a type of [qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubit "Qubit") dubbed *quantronium*. They also observed the [Ramsey fringes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_interferometry "Ramsey interferometry") of quantronium.

In 1996, Devoret spent a research stay in the laboratory of [Hans Mooij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Mooij?action=edit&redlink=1 "Hans Mooij") at [Delft University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology "Delft University of Technology").

Devoret became a professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") in 2002. At Yale University, [Steven Girvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Girvin "Steven Girvin"), [Robert J. Schoelkopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_J._Schoelkopf "Robert J. Schoelkopf"), and Devoret devised a type of superconducting [charge qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_qubit "Charge qubit") called the [transmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmon "Transmon"). In 2009, Devoret also pioneered [fluxonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#Fluxonium "Superconducting quantum computing"), which can be understood as a special type of [flux qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flux_qubit "Flux qubit"). In 2010, he also developed a microwave [quantum limited amplifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_amplifier "Quantum amplifier") for qubit readout and sensing.

He also participated in 2018 in an experiment demonstrating the interruption and reversal of quantum jumps in a superconducting [artificial atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_atom "Artificial atom"), providing new insights into the dynamics of quantum measurement.

From 2007 to 2012, Michel Devoret held the Chair of Mesoscopic Physics at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France") where his inaugural lecture, "From the Atom to Quantum Machines," illustrated the connection between fundamental quantum phenomena and emerging quantum technologies. He resigned in 2013.

In 2023, he was named the Chief Scientist for Hardware at [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google") Quantum AI. In 2024, he moved to the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara "University of California, Santa Barbara") to serve as Professor of Physics.

## Recognition

In 1970, Michel Devoret received the Prix de la Couronne française, an award recognizing young French researchers.
