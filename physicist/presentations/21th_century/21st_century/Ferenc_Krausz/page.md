**Ferenc Krausz** (born 17 May 1962) is a Hungarian physicist working in [attosecond science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attosecond_physics "Attosecond physics"). He was a director at the [Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_of_Quantum_Optics "Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics") and a professor of [experimental physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics") at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich "LMU Munich") in Germany. His research team generated and measured the first [attosecond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attosecond "Attosecond") [light pulse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse_(physics) "Pulse (physics)") and used it for capturing [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron")' motion inside atoms, marking the birth of [attophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attophysics "Attophysics"). In 2023, jointly with [Pierre Agostini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Agostini "Pierre Agostini") and [Anne L'Huillier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_L'Huillier "Anne L'Huillier"), he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"). Since November 2025, he has been Chair Professor at Department of Physics at the [University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hong_Kong "University of Hong Kong"), [Pokfulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokfulam "Pokfulam"), [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong").

## Academic career

From 1981 until 1985 Krausz studied [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics") at [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University "Eötvös Loránd University") and [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering "Electrical engineering") at the [Technical University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Budapest "Technical University of Budapest") in Hungary. From 1987 to 1991 he graduated with a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") at the [Technical University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Vienna "Technical University of Vienna"), in Austria, and from 1991 to 1993 he also did his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation "Habilitation") there. 1996–1998 he became associate professor, from 1999 until 2004 professor of electrical engineering at the same institute.

In 2003, he was appointed director at the [Max Planck Institute for Quantum Optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Quantum_Optics "Max Planck Institute for Quantum Optics") in Garching, and in 2004 became chair of [experimental physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics") at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich "LMU Munich").

In November 2025, he was appointed Chair Professor at Department of Physics at the [University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hong_Kong "University of Hong Kong"), [Pokfulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokfulam "Pokfulam"), [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong").

## Honors and awards

- 2006 - [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize "Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize")
- 2006 – [Royal Photographic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Photographic_Society "Royal Photographic Society") Progress medal and Honorary Fellowship
- 2009 – elected a Fellow of [Optica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optica_(society) "Optica (society)")
- 2013 – [Otto Hahn Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn_Prize "Otto Hahn Prize")
- 2015 – selected [Clarivate Citation laureate in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Clarivate_Citation_laureates_in_Physics "List of Clarivate Citation laureates in Physics") with [Paul Corkum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Corkum "Paul Corkum") "for contributions to the development of attosecond physics."
- 2016 – Member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina").
- 2019 – Vladilen Letokhov Medal.
- 2022 – [Wolf Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Physics "Wolf Prize in Physics") with [Anne L'Huillier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_L'Huillier "Anne L'Huillier") and Corkum "for pioneering contributions to ultrafast laser science and attosecond physics".
- 2022 – [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Award "BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award") in Basic Sciences with L'Huillier and Corkum.
- 2023 – [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with L'Huillier and [Pierre Agostini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Agostini "Pierre Agostini") "for experimental methods that generate attosecond pulses of light for the study of electron dynamics in matter."
