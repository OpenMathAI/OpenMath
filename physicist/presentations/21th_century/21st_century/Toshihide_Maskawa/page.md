**Toshihide Maskawa** (or **Masukawa**) (益川 敏英, *Masukawa Toshihide*; 7 February 1940 – 23 July 2021) was a Japanese [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physicist "Theoretical physicist") known for his work on [CP-violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP-violation "CP-violation") who was awarded one quarter of the 2008 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") "for the discovery of the origin of the [broken symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_symmetry_breaking "Spontaneous symmetry breaking") which predicts the existence of at least three families of [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark "Quark") in nature."

## Early life and education

Maskawa was born in [Nagoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya "Nagoya"), Japan. After [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") ended, the Maskawa family operated as sugar wholesalers. A native of [Aichi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aichi_Prefecture "Aichi Prefecture"), Toshihide Maskawa graduated from [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University") in 1962 and received a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree in particle physics from the same university in 1967. His doctoral advisor was the physicist [Shoichi Sakata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoichi_Sakata "Shoichi Sakata").

From early life Maskawa liked [trivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivia "Trivia"), and studied [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"), [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"), [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics "Linguistics") and various books. In high school, he loved [novels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novel "Novel"), especially detective and mystery stories and novels by [Ryūnosuke Akutagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryūnosuke_Akutagawa "Ryūnosuke Akutagawa").

## Career

At [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University") in the early 1970s, he collaborated with [Makoto Kobayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makoto_Kobayashi_(physicist) "Makoto Kobayashi (physicist)") on explaining broken symmetry (the [CP violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP_violation "CP violation")) within the [Standard Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Model "Standard Model") of particle physics. Maskawa and Kobayashi's theory required that there be at least [three generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_(physics) "Generation (physics)") of quarks, a prediction that was confirmed experimentally four years later by the discovery of the [bottom quark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottom_quark "Bottom quark").

Maskawa and Kobayashi's 1973 article, "CP Violation in the Renormalizable Theory of Weak Interaction", is the fourth most cited high energy physics paper of all time as of 2010. The [Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa_matrix "Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa matrix"), which defines the [mixing parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbibo_angle "Cabbibo angle") between [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark "Quark"), was the result of this work. Kobayashi and Maskawa were jointly awarded half of the 2008 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for this work, with the other half going to [Yoichiro Nambu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoichiro_Nambu "Yoichiro Nambu").

Maskawa was director of the [Yukawa Institute for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukawa_Institute_for_Theoretical_Physics "Yukawa Institute for Theoretical Physics") from 1997 to 2003. He was special professor and director general of the Kobayashi-Maskawa Institute for the Origin of Particles and the Universe at [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University"), director of the Maskawa Institute for Science and Culture at [Kyoto Sangyo University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Sangyo_University "Kyoto Sangyo University") and professor emeritus at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University").

## Nobel lecture

On 8 December 2008, after Maskawa told the audience "Sorry, I cannot speak English", he delivered his Nobel lecture on “What Did CP Violation Tell Us?” in Japanese language, at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University "Stockholm University"). The audience followed the subtitles on the screen behind him.

## Personal life

Maskawa married Akiko Takahashi in 1967. The couple have two children, Kazuki and Tokifuji.

## Death

On 23 July 2021, on the same day as the [opening ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony "2020 Summer Olympics opening ceremony") of the [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo") [Summer Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics "2020 Summer Olympics"), Maskawa died of [oral cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_cancer "Oral cancer") at his home in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto "Kyoto") at the age of 81; his death was unrelated to the triple disaster and COVID-19 infection. He was [cremated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremation "Cremation") in October 2021 after a private funeral.

## Political proposition

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Toshihide_Maskawa%27s_slide_rule_at_the_Nobel_Museum_%2851963%29.jpg/250px-Toshihide_Maskawa%27s_slide_rule_at_the_Nobel_Museum_%2851963%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Toshihide_Maskawa's_slide_rule_at_the_Nobel_Museum_(51963).jpg)

Maskawa's [slide rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slide_rule "Slide rule") on display at the [Nobel Prize Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_Museum "Nobel Prize Museum")

In 2013, Maskawa and chemistry Nobel laureate [Hideki Shirakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Shirakawa "Hideki Shirakawa") issued a statement against the Japanese [State Secrecy Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Secrecy_Law "State Secrecy Law")." The following is Maskawa's main political proposition:

- Support for [Article 9 of the Japanese Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_9_of_the_Japanese_Constitution "Article 9 of the Japanese Constitution")
- Criticizing Japanese politician visits to the [Yasukuni Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasukuni_Shrine "Yasukuni Shrine")
- Support for selective couple surname system
