**John Joseph Hopfield** (born July 15, 1933) is an American physicist and [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus "Emeritus") professor of [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), most widely known for his study of [associative neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_neural_networks "Associative neural networks") in 1982. He is known for the development of the [Hopfield network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopfield_network "Hopfield network"). Before its invention, research in artificial intelligence (AI) was in a decay period or [AI winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_winter "AI winter"), Hopfield's work revitalized large-scale interest in this field.

In 2024 Hopfield, along with [Geoffrey Hinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Hinton "Geoffrey Hinton"), was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for "foundational discoveries and inventions that enable [machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning "Machine learning") with [artificial neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_neural_network "Artificial neural network")." He has been awarded various major physics awards for his work in multidisciplinary fields including [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics "Condensed matter physics"), [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics") and [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics").

## Biography

### Early life and education

John Joseph Hopfield was born in 1933 in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") to physicists [John Joseph Hopfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_J._Hopfield_(spectroscopist) "John J. Hopfield (spectroscopist)") (born in Poland as Jan Józef Chmielewski) and [Helen Hopfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Hopfield "Helen Hopfield") (née Staff).

Hopfield received a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") with a major in physics from [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College "Swarthmore College") in Pennsylvania in 1954 and a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in physics from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") in 1958. His doctoral dissertation was titled "A quantum-mechanical theory of the contribution of [excitons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exciton "Exciton") to the complex [dielectric constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_permittivity "Relative permittivity") of crystals". His [doctoral advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor "Doctoral advisor") was [Albert Overhauser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Overhauser "Albert Overhauser").

### Career

Hopfield spent two years in the theory group at [Bell Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Laboratories "Bell Laboratories") working on optical properties of semiconductors working with [David Gilbert Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gilbert_Thomas "David Gilbert Thomas") and later on a quantitative model to describe the cooperative behavior of [hemoglobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemoglobin "Hemoglobin") in collaboration with [Robert G. Shulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_G._Shulman "Robert G. Shulman"). Subsequently he became a faculty member at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") (physics, 1961–1964), [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") (physics, 1964–1980), [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") (Caltech, chemistry and biology, 1980–1997) and again at Princeton (1997–), where he is the Howard A. Prior Professor of Molecular Biology, emeritus.

In 1976, he participated in a science short film on the structure of the hemoglobin, featuring [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling "Linus Pauling").

From 1981 to 1983 [Richard Feynman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Feynman "Richard Feynman"), [Carver Mead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carver_Mead "Carver Mead") and Hopfield gave a one-year course at Caltech called "The Physics of Computation". This collaboration inspired the [Computation and Neural Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computation_and_Neural_Systems "Computation and Neural Systems") PhD program at Caltech in 1986, co-founded by Hopfield.

His former PhD students include [Gerald Mahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Mahan "Gerald Mahan") (PhD in 1964), [Bertrand Halperin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_Halperin "Bertrand Halperin") (1965), [Steven Girvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Girvin "Steven Girvin") (1977), [Terry Sejnowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Sejnowski "Terry Sejnowski") (1978), [Erik Winfree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Winfree "Erik Winfree") (1998), [José Onuchic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Onuchic "José Onuchic") (1987), [Li Zhaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Zhaoping "Li Zhaoping") (1990) and [David J. C. MacKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_J._C._MacKay "David J. C. MacKay") (1992).

## Work

In his doctoral work of 1958, he wrote on the interaction of [excitons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exciton "Exciton") in crystals, coining the term [polariton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polariton "Polariton") for a [quasiparticle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiparticle "Quasiparticle") that appears in [solid-state physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_physics "Solid-state physics"). He wrote: "The polarization field 'particles' analogous to [photons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon "Photon") will be called 'polaritons'." His polariton model is sometimes known as the [Hopfield dielectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopfield_dielectric "Hopfield dielectric").

From 1959 to 1963, Hopfield and David G. Thomas investigated the exciton structure of [cadmium sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadmium_sulfide "Cadmium sulfide") from its [reflection spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_spectrum#Materials_science "Reflection spectrum"). Their experiments and theoretical models allowed to understand the optical spectroscopy of [II-VI semiconductor compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/II-VI_semiconductor_compound "II-VI semiconductor compound").

Condensed matter physicist [Philip W. Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_W._Anderson "Philip W. Anderson") reported that John Hopfield was his "hidden collaborator" for his 1961–1970 works on the [Anderson impurity model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_impurity_model "Anderson impurity model") which explained the [Kondo effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondo_effect "Kondo effect"). Hopfield was not included as a co-author in the papers but Anderson admitted the importance of Hopfield's contribution in various of his writings.

William C. Topp and Hopfield introduced the concept of norm-conserving [pseudopotentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudopotential "Pseudopotential") in 1973.

In 1974 he introduced a mechanism for error correction in [biochemical reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme_catalysis "Enzyme catalysis") known as [kinetic proofreading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_proofreading "Kinetic proofreading") to explain the accuracy of [DNA replication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_replication "DNA replication").

Hopfield published his first paper in neuroscience in 1982, titled "Neural networks and physical systems with emergent collective computational abilities" where he introduced what is now known as [Hopfield network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopfield_network "Hopfield network"), a type of artificial network that can serve as a [content-addressable memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content-addressable_memory "Content-addressable memory"), made of binary neurons that can be 'on' or 'off'. He extended his formalism to continuous [activation functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activation_function "Activation function") in 1984. The 1982 and 1984 papers represent his two most cited works. Hopfield has said that the inspiration came from his knowledge of [spin glasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_glass "Spin glass") from his collaborations with P. W. Anderson.

Together with [David W. Tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_W._Tank "David W. Tank"), Hopfield developed a method in 1985–1986 for solving discrete optimization problems based on the continuous-time dynamics using a Hopfield network with continuous activation function. The optimization problem was encoded in the interaction parameters (weights) of the network. The effective temperature of the analog system was gradually decreased, as in global optimization with [simulated annealing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulated_annealing "Simulated annealing").

Hopfield is one of the pioneers of the [critical brain hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_brain_hypothesis "Critical brain hypothesis"), he was the first to link neural networks with [self-organized criticality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organized_criticality "Self-organized criticality") in reference to the [Olami–Feder–Christensen model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olami–Feder–Christensen_model "Olami–Feder–Christensen model") for earthquakes in 1994. In 1995, Hopfield and Andreas V. Herz showed that avalanches in neural activity follow power law distribution associated to earthquakes.

The original Hopfield networks had a limited memory, this problem was addressed by Hopfield and Dimitry Krotov in 2016. Large memory storage Hopfield networks are now known as [modern Hopfield networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Hopfield_network "Modern Hopfield network").

## Views on artificial intelligence

In March 2023, Hopfield signed an open letter titled "[Pause Giant AI Experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pause_Giant_AI_Experiments:_An_Open_Letter "Pause Giant AI Experiments: An Open Letter")", calling for a pause on the training of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") (AI) systems more powerful than [GPT-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-4 "GPT-4"). The letter, signed by over 30,000 individuals including AI researchers [Yoshua Bengio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshua_Bengio "Yoshua Bengio") and [Stuart Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_J._Russell "Stuart J. Russell"), cited risks such as human obsolescence and [society-wide loss of control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_problem "Control problem").

Upon being jointly awarded the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics, Hopfield revealed he was very unnerved by recent advances in AI capabilities, and said "as a physicist, I'm very unnerved by something which has no control". In a followup press conference in Princeton University, Hopfield compared AI with [discovery of nuclear fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_nuclear_fission "Discovery of nuclear fission"), which led to [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon") and [nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power "Nuclear power").

## Awards and honors

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/08/1969_Oliver_E._Buckley_Prize.jpg/330px-1969_Oliver_E._Buckley_Prize.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1969_Oliver_E._Buckley_Prize.jpg)

The 1969 ceremony of the [Oliver E. Buckley Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_E._Buckley_Prize "Oliver E. Buckley Prize") of condensed matter physics. [Luis Walter Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Walter_Alvarez "Luis Walter Alvarez") (left) congratulates [David Gilbert Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gilbert_Thomas "David Gilbert Thomas") (middle) and John Hopfield (right).

Hopfield received a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship "Sloan Research Fellowship") in 1962 and as his father, he received a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship "Guggenheim Fellowship") (1968). Hopfield was elected as a member of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") (APS) in 1969, a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1973, a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1975, and a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1988. He was the President of the APS in 2006.

In 1969 Hopfield and [David Gilbert Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gilbert_Thomas "David Gilbert Thomas") were awarded the [Oliver E. Buckley Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_E._Buckley_Prize "Oliver E. Buckley Prize") of condensed matter physics by the APS "for their joint work combining theory and experiment which has advanced the understanding of the interaction of light with solids".

In 1983 he was awarded the MacArthur Foundational Prize by the [MacArthur Fellows Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program "MacArthur Fellows Program"). In 1985, Hopfield received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") and the [Max Delbruck Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Delbruck_Prize "Max Delbruck Prize") in Biophysics by the APS. In 1988, he received the [Michelson–Morley Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelson–Morley_Award "Michelson–Morley Award") by [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University "Case Western Reserve University"). Hopfield received the Neural Networks Pioneer Award in 1997 by the [Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers "Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers") (IEEE).

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/John_J._Hopfield_and_Geoffrey_E._Hinton%2C_2024_Nobel_Prize_Laureate_in_Physics.jpg/250px-John_J._Hopfield_and_Geoffrey_E._Hinton%2C_2024_Nobel_Prize_Laureate_in_Physics.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:John_J._Hopfield_and_Geoffrey_E._Hinton,_2024_Nobel_Prize_Laureate_in_Physics.jpg)

Geoffrey E. Hinton (left) and Hopfield at 2024 Nobel Week

He was awarded the [Dirac Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_Medal_(ICTP) "Dirac Medal (ICTP)") of the [International Centre for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Theoretical_Physics "International Centre for Theoretical Physics") in 2001 "for important contributions in an impressively broad spectrum of scientific subjects" including "an entirely different [collective] organizing principle in [olfaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sense_of_smell "Sense of smell")" and "a new principle in which neural function can take advantage of the temporal structure of the 'spiking' interneural communication".

Hopfield received the [Harold Pender Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Pender_Award "Harold Pender Award") in 2002 for his accomplishments in [computational neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_neuroscience "Computational neuroscience") and [neural engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_engineering "Neural engineering") from the [Moore School of Electrical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore_School_of_Electrical_Engineering "Moore School of Electrical Engineering"), [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania"). He received the [Albert Einstein World Award of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_World_Award_of_Science "Albert Einstein World Award of Science") in 2005 in the field of life sciences. In 2007, he gave the [Fritz London Memorial Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fritz_London_Memorial_Lectures "List of Fritz London Memorial Lectures") at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University"), titled "How Do We Think So Fast? From Neurons to Brain Computation". Hopfield received the [IEEE Frank Rosenblatt Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Frank_Rosenblatt_Award "IEEE Frank Rosenblatt Award") in 2009 for his contributions in understanding information processing in biological systems. In 2012 he was awarded the [Swartz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swartz_Prize "Swartz Prize") by the [Society for Neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Neuroscience "Society for Neuroscience"). In 2019 he was awarded the [Benjamin Franklin Medal in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin_Medal_(Franklin_Institute) "Benjamin Franklin Medal (Franklin Institute)") by the [Franklin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Institute "Franklin Institute"), and in 2022 he shared the [Boltzmann Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_Medal "Boltzmann Medal") award in statistical physics with [Deepak Dhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepak_Dhar "Deepak Dhar").

He was jointly awarded the 2024 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Geoffrey E. Hinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_E._Hinton "Geoffrey E. Hinton") for "foundational discoveries and inventions that enable machine learning with artificial neural networks".

In 2025 he was awarded the [Queen Elizabeth Prize for Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_Prize_for_Engineering "Queen Elizabeth Prize for Engineering") jointly with [Yoshua Bengio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshua_Bengio "Yoshua Bengio"), [Bill Dally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Dally "Bill Dally"), [Geoffrey E. Hinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_E._Hinton "Geoffrey E. Hinton"), [Yann LeCun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yann_LeCun "Yann LeCun"), [Jen-Hsun Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jen-Hsun_Huang "Jen-Hsun Huang") and [Fei-Fei Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fei-Fei_Li "Fei-Fei Li") for the development of modern machine learning.
