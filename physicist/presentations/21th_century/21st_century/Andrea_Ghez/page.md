**Andrea Mia Ghez** (born June 16, 1965) is an American [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysicist "Astrophysicist"). She shared the 2020 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Reinhard Genzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Genzel "Reinhard Genzel") "for the discovery of a supermassive compact object at the centre of our galaxy".  [This object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagittarius_A* "Sagittarius A*") is generally recognized to be a [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole "Black hole").

Her research focuses on the [center of the Milky Way galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Center "Galactic Center"). She is a professor in the Department of Physics and Astronomy and the Lauren B. Leichtman & Arthur E. Levine chair in Astrophysics, at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles "University of California, Los Angeles"). With [Judith Love Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judith_Love_Cohen "Judith Love Cohen"), she is the author of the book *You Can Be a Woman Astronomer*.

## Early life

Ghez was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"). She is the daughter of Susanne (née Gayton) and Gilbert Ghez. Her father, of Jewish heritage, was born in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome"), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy"), to a family originally from [Tunisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisia "Tunisia") and [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt "Frankfurt"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany"). Her mother was from an [Irish Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Catholic "Irish Catholic") family from [North Attleborough, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Attleborough,_Massachusetts "North Attleborough, Massachusetts").

In 1969, her father completed his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") at Columbia University and accepted a position at the University of Chicago, where as the child of a faculty member Ghez was able to attend the [Laboratory School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago_Lab_School "University of Chicago Lab School"). The [Apollo program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program "Apollo program") Moon landings inspired Ghez to aspire to be the first female [astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut "Astronaut"), and her mother encouraged that goal by purchasing a telescope. Her most influential female role model was her high school chemistry teacher.

She began college by majoring in mathematics, then changed to physics. She received a [BS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") in 1987. While there, she was a member of the fraternity of [St. Anthony Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anthony_Hall "St. Anthony Hall"). She received a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") under the direction of [Gerry Neugebauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Neugebauer "Gerry Neugebauer") at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") in 1992.

## Career

Ghez's research employs high spatial resolution imaging techniques, such as the [adaptive optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_optics "Adaptive optics") system at the [Keck telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keck_telescopes "Keck telescopes"), to study star-forming regions and the [supermassive black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermassive_black_hole "Supermassive black hole") at the center of the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way "Milky Way") known as [Sagittarius A\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagittarius_A* "Sagittarius A*"). She uses the [kinematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinematics "Kinematics") of stars near the center of the Milky Way as a probe to investigate this region. The high resolution of the Keck telescopes gave a significant improvement over the first major study of galactic center kinematics by [Reinhard Genzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Genzel "Reinhard Genzel")'s group.

In 2004, Ghez was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences"), and in 2012, she was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"). In 2019, she was elected as a Fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") (APS).

Ghez has appeared in many television documentaries produced by networks such as the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC"), [Discovery Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_Channel "Discovery Channel"), and [The History Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_(U.S._TV_channel) "History (U.S. TV channel)"). In 2006 she was in an episode of the [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS "PBS") series *[Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_(American_TV_series) "Nova (American TV series)")*. She was identified as a Science Hero by [The My Hero Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_My_Hero_Project "The My Hero Project"). In 2000, *[Discover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discover_(magazine) "Discover (magazine)")* magazine listed Ghez as one of 20 promising young American scientists in their respective fields.

### Black hole at the Galactic Center (Sgr A\*)

By imaging the [Galactic Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Center "Galactic Center") at [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared "Infrared") wavelengths, Ghez and her colleagues have been able to peer through heavy dust that blocks visible light, to reveal images of the center of the Milky Way. Thanks to the 10-meter aperture of the [W.M. Keck Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._M._Keck_Observatory "W. M. Keck Observatory") and the use of [adaptive optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_optics "Adaptive optics") to correct for the turbulence of the atmosphere, these images of the Galactic Center are at very high spatial resolution and have made it possible to follow the orbits of stars around the black hole Sagittarius A\* (Sgr A\*). The partial orbits of many stars orbiting the black hole at the Galactic Center have been observed. One of the stars, [S2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S2_(star) "S2 (star)"), has made a complete [elliptical orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptical_orbit "Elliptical orbit") since detailed observations began in 1995. Several decades more will be required to document the orbits of some of these stars completely. These measurements may provide a test of the theory of [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity"). In October 2012, a second star, [S0-102](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S0-102 "S0-102"), was identified by her team at UCLA, orbiting the Galactic Center. Using [Kepler's third law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler's_laws_of_planetary_motion "Kepler's laws of planetary motion"), Ghez's team used the orbital motion to show that the mass of Sgr A\* is 4.1±0.6 million [solar masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_mass "Solar mass"). Because the Galactic Center where Sgr A\* is located, is one hundred times closer than [M31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andromeda_Galaxy "Andromeda Galaxy") where the next nearest supermassive black hole (M31\*) is, it is one of the best demonstrated cases for a supermassive black hole.

In 2020, Ghez shared the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Roger Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Penrose "Roger Penrose") and [Reinhard Genzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Genzel "Reinhard Genzel"), for their discoveries relating to [black holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole "Black hole"). Ghez and Genzel were awarded one half of the prize for their discovery that a supermassive black hole most likely governs the orbits of stars at the center of the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way "Milky Way"). Ghez was the fourth woman to win the physics Nobel since its inception, being preceded by [Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie "Marie Curie") (1903), [Maria Goeppert Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Goeppert_Mayer "Maria Goeppert Mayer") (1963), and [Donna Strickland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Strickland "Donna Strickland") (2018).

## Personal life

Andrea is married to Tom LaTourrette and has two sons. Ghez is an active swimmer in the UCLA Masters Swim Club.

In 2009, she gave a TED talk on “The Hunt For a Supermassive Black Hole”.

In a lecture to students, she emphasized the importance of [critical thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_thinking "Critical thinking").

She gave the 53rd [George Gamow Memorial Lectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow_Memorial_Lectures "George Gamow Memorial Lectures"), "From the Possibility to the Certainty of a Supermassive Black Hole".
