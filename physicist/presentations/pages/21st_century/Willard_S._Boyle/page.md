**Willard Sterling Boyle** (August 19, 1924 – May 7, 2011) was a Canadian applied [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared one half of the 2009 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [George E. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_E._Smith "George E. Smith") for their invention of the [charge-coupled device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge-coupled_device "Charge-coupled device").

As director of Space Science and Exploratory Studies at Bellcomm, Boyle helped select lunar landing sites and provided support for the [Apollo space program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_space_program "Apollo space program").

## Early life and education

Willard Sterling Boyle was born on August 19, 1924, in [Amherst, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst,_Nova_Scotia "Amherst, Nova Scotia"), and moved to [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec "Quebec") with his parents when he was about 2-years-old. He was homeschooled by his mother until the age of 14, when he began his formal education at [Lower Canada College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Canada_College "Lower Canada College") in [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal").

Boyle attended [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University "McGill University"), but his education was interrupted in 1943 when he joined the [Royal Canadian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Navy "Royal Canadian Navy") during World War II. He was loaned to the [Royal Canadian Naval Air Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Naval_Air_Service "Royal Canadian Naval Air Service"), where he learned how to land [Spitfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Spitfire "Supermarine Spitfire") on [aircraft carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_carrier "Aircraft carrier") as the war ended. After resuming his studies at McGill, Boyle gained a [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.Sc. "B.Sc.") in 1947, and an [M.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.Sc. "M.Sc.") the following year. In 1950, he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") with a thesis on [mass spectrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometry "Mass spectrometry").

## Career

After receiving his doctorate, Boyle spent one year at Canada's Radiation Lab and two years teaching physics at the [Royal Military College of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_College_of_Canada "Royal Military College of Canada").

In 1953, Boyle joined [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Telephone_Laboratories "Bell Telephone Laboratories"), where he invented the first continuously operating [ruby laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_laser "Ruby laser") with Don Nelson in 1962,
and was named on the first patent for a [semiconductor injection laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_diode "Laser diode"). He was made director of Space Science and Exploratory Studies at the Bell Labs subsidiary Bellcomm in 1962, providing support for the [Apollo space program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_space_program "Apollo space program") and helping to select lunar landing sites. He returned to Bell Labs in 1964, working on the development of [integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuits "Integrated circuits"). Boyle was Executive Director of Research at Bell Labs from 1975 until his retirement in 1979.

## Charge-coupled device

In 1969, Boyle and [George E. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_E._Smith "George E. Smith") invented the [charge-coupled device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge-coupled_device "Charge-coupled device") (CCD), for which they were jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 2009. The CCD allowed [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") to send clear pictures to Earth back from space. It is also the technology that powers many [digital cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_camera "Digital camera") today. Smith said of their invention: "After making the first couple of imaging devices, we knew for certain that chemistry photography was dead." [Eugene Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_I._Gordon "Eugene I. Gordon") and [Mike Tompsett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Francis_Tompsett "Michael Francis Tompsett"), two now-retired colleagues from Bell Labs, claim that its application to photography was not invented by Boyle.

## Personal life and death

In retirement, Boyle split his time between [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia "Halifax, Nova Scotia") and [Wallace, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace,_Nova_Scotia "Wallace, Nova Scotia"). In Wallace, he helped launch an art gallery with his wife, Betty, a [landscape artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_artist "Landscape artist"). He was married to Betty since 1946 and had four children, 10 grandchildren and 6 great-grandchildren.

In his later years, Boyle suffered from [kidney disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_disease "Kidney disease"). Due to complications from this disease, he died on May 7, 2011, at a hospital in [Truro, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truro,_Nova_Scotia "Truro, Nova Scotia"), at the age of 86.
