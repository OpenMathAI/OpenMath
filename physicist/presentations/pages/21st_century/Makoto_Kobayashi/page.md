**Makoto Kobayashi** (小林 誠, *Kobayashi Makoto*; born April 7, 1944) is a Japanese [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") known for his work on [CP-violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP-violation "CP-violation") who was awarded one-fourth of the 2008 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") "for the discovery of the origin of the [broken symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_symmetry_breaking "Spontaneous symmetry breaking") which predicts the existence of at least three families of quarks in nature."

## Early life and education

Makoto Kobayashi was born in [Nagoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya "Nagoya"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") in 1944. When he was two years old, Kobayashi's father Hisashi died. The Kobayashi family home was destroyed by the [Bombing of Nagoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Nagoya_in_World_War_II "Bombing of Nagoya in World War II"), so they stayed at his mother's (surnamed Kaifu) family house. One of Makoto's cousins, [Toshiki Kaifu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshiki_Kaifu "Toshiki Kaifu"), the 51st [Prime Minister of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Japan "Prime Minister of Japan"), was living in the same place. His other cousin was an [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer "Astronomer"), [Norio Kaifu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norio_Kaifu "Norio Kaifu"). Many years later, Toshiki Kaifu recalled Kobayashi: "when he was a child, he was a quiet and lovely boy, always reading some difficult books in my room. I think this is the beginning of his sudden change into a genius."

After graduating from the School of Science of [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University") in 1967, he obtained a [DSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science "Doctor of Science") degree from the Graduate School of Science of Nagoya University in 1972. During college years, he received guidance from [Shoichi Sakata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoichi_Sakata "Shoichi Sakata") and others.

## Career

[Paul Krugman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Krugman "Paul Krugman"), [Roger Tsien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Tsien "Roger Tsien"), [Martin Chalfie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Chalfie "Martin Chalfie"), [Osamu Shimomura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osamu_Shimomura "Osamu Shimomura"), Makoto Kobayashi and [Toshihide Masukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshihide_Masukawa "Toshihide Masukawa"), Nobel Prize Laureates 2008, at a press conference at the [Swedish Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy_of_Science "Swedish Academy of Science") in Stockholm

After completing his doctoral research at [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University") in 1972, Kobayashi worked as a research associate on particle physics at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University"). Together, with his colleague [Toshihide Maskawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshihide_Maskawa "Toshihide Maskawa"), he worked on explaining [CP-violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP-violation "CP-violation") within the [Standard Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Model "Standard Model") of particle physics. Kobayashi and Maskawa's theory required that there were at least three [generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_(physics) "Generation (physics)") of quarks, a prediction that was confirmed experimentally four years later by the discovery of the [bottom quark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottom_quark "Bottom quark").

Kobayashi and Maskawa's article, "CP Violation in the Renormalizable Theory of Weak Interaction", published in 1973, is the fourth most cited high energy physics paper of all time as of 2010. The [Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa_matrix "Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa matrix"), which defines the mixing parameters between [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark "Quark") was the result of this work. Kobayashi and Maskawa were jointly awarded half of the 2008 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for this work, with the other half going to [Yoichiro Nambu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoichiro_Nambu "Yoichiro Nambu").

In recognition of three [Nobel laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureates "Nobel laureates")' contributions, the bronze statues of [Shin'ichirō Tomonaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin'ichirō_Tomonaga "Shin'ichirō Tomonaga"), [Leo Esaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Esaki "Leo Esaki"), and Makoto Kobayashi was set up in the Central Park of Azuma 2 in [Tsukuba City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukuba_City "Tsukuba City") in 2015.

## Professional record

- April 1972 – Research Associate of the Faculty of Science, [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University")
- July 1979 – Associate Professor of the [National Laboratory of High Energy Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KEK "KEK") (KEK)
- April 1989 – Professor of the [National Laboratory of High Energy Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KEK "KEK") (KEK), Head of Physics Division II
- April 1997 – Professor of the Institute of Particle and Nuclear Science, [KEK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KEK "KEK"), Head of Physics Division II
- April 2003 – Director, Institute of Particle and Nuclear Studies, [KEK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KEK "KEK")
- April 2004 – Trustee (Director, Institute of Particle and Nuclear Studies), KEK (Inter-University Research Institute Corporation)
- June 2006 – Professor Emeritus of KEK
- 2008 – Distinguished Invited University Professor of [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University")
- 2009
  - Special Honored Professor of KEK
  - Trustee and Director of Academic System Institute, [Japan Society for the Promotion of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Society_for_the_Promotion_of_Science "Japan Society for the Promotion of Science")
  - University Professor of [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University")
- 2010
  - Chairperson of the Advisory Committee of the Kobayashi-Maskawa Institute for the Origin of Particles and the Universe (KMI) at [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University")
  - Member of the [Japan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy "Japan Academy")
- 2016 – Superadvisor of Yokohama Science Frontier High School
- 2018
  - April – Director of the Kobayashi-Maskawa Institute for the Origin of Particles and the Universe (KMI) at [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University")

- 2020
  - April – Director Emeritus of KMI at [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University "Nagoya University")

## Personal life

Kobayashi was born and educated in [Nagoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya "Nagoya"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"). He married Sachiko Enomoto in 1975; they had one son, Junichiro. After his first wife died, Kobayashi married Emiko Nakayama in 1990, they had a daughter, Yuka.
