**Alexei Alexeyevich Abrikosov** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Алексе́й Алексе́евич Абрико́сов; June 25, 1928 – March 29, 2017) was a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia")-American [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics") whose main contributions are in the field of [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics "Condensed matter physics"). He was the co-recipient of the 2003 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), with [Vitaly Ginzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaly_Ginzburg "Vitaly Ginzburg") and [Anthony James Leggett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_James_Leggett "Anthony James Leggett"), for theories about how matter can behave at extremely low temperatures.

## Education and early life

Abrikosov was born in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow"), [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_SFSR "Russian SFSR"), Soviet Union, on June 25, 1928, to a couple of [physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician "Physician"): [Aleksey Abrikosov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksey_Abrikosov "Aleksey Abrikosov") and Fani ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Wulf). His mother was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews"). After graduating from high school in 1943, Abrikosov began studying energy technology. He graduated from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University") in 1948. From 1948 to 1965, he worked at the Institute for Physical Problems of the [USSR Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Academy_of_Sciences "USSR Academy of Sciences"), where he received his Ph.D. in 1951 for the theory of thermal diffusion in [plasmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics) "Plasma (physics)"), and then his [Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science "Doctor of Science") (a "higher doctorate") degree in 1955 for a thesis on [quantum electrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_electrodynamics "Quantum electrodynamics") at high energies. Abrikosov moved to the US in 1991 and lived there until his death in 2017, in Palo Alto, California. While in the US, Abrikosov was elected to the National Academy of Sciences in 2000, and in 2001, to be a foreign member of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society").

## Career

From 1965 to 1988, he worked at the [Landau Institute for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau_Institute_for_Theoretical_Physics "Landau Institute for Theoretical Physics") (USSR Academy of Sciences). He has been a professor at Moscow State University since 1965. In addition, he held tenure at the [Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Institute_of_Physics_and_Technology "Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology") from 1972 to 1976, and at the [Moscow Institute of Steel and Alloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Institute_of_Steel_and_Alloys "Moscow Institute of Steel and Alloys") from 1976 to 1991. He served as a full member of the USSR Academy of Sciences from 1987 to 1991. In 1991, he became a full member of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences").

In two works in 1952 and 1957, Abrikosov explained how [magnetic flux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_flux "Magnetic flux") can penetrate a class of [superconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductors "Superconductors"). This class of materials are called [type-II superconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type-II_superconductors "Type-II superconductors"). The accompanying arrangement of magnetic flux lines is called the [Abrikosov vortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrikosov_vortex "Abrikosov vortex") lattice.

Together with [Lev Gor'kov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Gor'kov "Lev Gor'kov") and [Igor Dzyaloshinskii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Dzyaloshinskii "Igor Dzyaloshinskii"), Abrikosov has written an iconic book on theoretical [solid-state physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_physics "Solid-state physics"), which has been used to train physicists in the field for decades.

From 1991 until his retirement, he worked at [Argonne National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratory "Argonne National Laboratory") in the U.S. state of [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois "Illinois"). Abrikosov was an Argonne Distinguished Scientist at the Condensed Matter Theory Group in Argonne's Materials Science Division. When he received the Nobel Prize, his research was focused on the origins of magnetoresistance, a property of some materials that change their resistance to electrical flow under the influence of a magnetic field.

## Honours and awards

Abrikosov was awarded the [Lenin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin_Prize "Lenin Prize") in 1966, the [Fritz London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_London "Fritz London") Memorial Prize in 1972, and the [USSR State Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize "USSR State Prize") in 1982. In 1989 he received the Landau Prize from the Academy of Sciences, Russia. Two years later, in 1991, Abrikosov was awarded the Sony Corporation's John Bardeen Award. The same year he was elected a Foreign Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"). He shared the 2003 Nobel Prize in Physics. He was also a member of the Royal Academy of London, a fellow of the American Physical Society, and in 2000 was elected to the prestigious National Academy of Sciences. Other awards include:

- Member of the [Academy of Sciences of the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_USSR "Academy of Sciences of the USSR") (now Russian Academy of Sciences), 1964
- [Honorary Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Doctor "Honorary Doctor") of the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne "University of Lausanne"), 1975
- [Order of the Badge of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Badge_of_Honour "Order of the Badge of Honour"), 1975
- [Order of the Red Banner of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Red_Banner_of_Labour "Order of the Red Banner of Labour"), 1988
- Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the USSR (now Russian Academy of Sciences), 1987
- [Elected a Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2001 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 2001")
- Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement"), 2004
- Gold Medal of Vernadsky from [National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences_of_Ukraine "National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine"), 2015

## Personal life

Abrikosov was the son of the physicians [Alexei Ivanovich Abrikosov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Ivanovich_Abrikosov "Alexei Ivanovich Abrikosov") (1875-1955) and his second wife, Fania Davidovna Woolf (1895—1965). Through his father, Abrikosov was the nephew of the martyred Catholic nun [Anna Abrikosova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Abrikosova "Anna Abrikosova") (1882-1936).

His sister was Maria Alekseevna Abrikósova (1929-1998), physician.

He married Svetlana Yuriyevna Bunkova and had 3 children.

He died in [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California") on 29 March 2017 at the age of 88.
