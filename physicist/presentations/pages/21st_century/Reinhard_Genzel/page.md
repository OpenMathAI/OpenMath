**Reinhard Genzel** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") (German pronunciation: [[ˈʁaɪnhaʁt ˈɡɛntsl̩]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Reinhard_Genzel.ogg "File:De-Reinhard Genzel.ogg"); born 24 March 1952) is a German [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysicist "Astrophysicist"), co-director of the [Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Extraterrestrial_Physics "Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics"), a professor at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich "LMU Munich") and an emeritus professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"). He was awarded the 2020 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") "for the discovery of a [supermassive compact object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Center#Supermassive_black_hole "Galactic Center") at the [centre of our galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Center "Galactic Center")", which he shared with [Andrea Ghez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Ghez "Andrea Ghez") and [Roger Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Penrose "Roger Penrose"). In a 2021 interview given to [Federal University of Pará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_University_of_Pará "Federal University of Pará") in Brazil, Genzel recalls his journey as a physicist; the influence of his father, [Ludwig Genzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Genzel?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ludwig Genzel"); his experiences working with [Charles H. Townes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_H._Townes "Charles H. Townes"); and more.

## Life and career

Genzel was born in [Bad Homburg vor der Höhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Homburg_vor_der_Höhe "Bad Homburg vor der Höhe"), Germany, the son of Eva-Maria Genzel and Ludwig Genzel, a professor of [solid state physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_state_physics "Solid state physics") (1922–2003). He studied [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") at the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg "University of Freiburg") and the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn "University of Bonn"), graduating in 1978 with a PhD in radioastronomy which he prepared at the [Max Planck Institute for Radio Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Radio_Astronomy "Max Planck Institute for Radio Astronomy"). Subsequently he worked at the [Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard-Smithsonian_Center_for_Astrophysics "Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics") in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_(Massachusetts) "Cambridge (Massachusetts)"), [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts "Massachusetts"). He was a [Miller Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Fellow "Miller Fellow") from 1980 until 1982, and also Associate and finally Full Professor in the Department of Physics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") from 1981. In 1986, he left Berkeley to become a director at the [Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck-Institut_für_extraterrestrische_Physik "Max-Planck-Institut für extraterrestrische Physik") in [Garching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garching "Garching") and Scientific Member of the [Max-Planck-Gesellschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck-Gesellschaft "Max-Planck-Gesellschaft"). During that time he also lectured at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich "LMU Munich"), where he has been Honorary Professor since 1988. From 1999 to 2016, he also had a part-time joint appointment as [Full Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Professor "Full Professor") at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"). Additional activities include sitting on the selection committee for the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize") in astronomy.

## Work

Reinhard Genzel studies [infrared-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_astronomy "Infrared astronomy") and [submillimetre astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submillimetre_astronomy "Submillimetre astronomy"). He and his group are
active in developing ground- and space-based instruments for astronomy. They used these to track the motions of stars at the centre of the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way "Milky Way"), around [Sagittarius A\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagittarius_A* "Sagittarius A*"), and show that they were orbiting a very massive object, now known to be a [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole "Black hole"). Genzel is also active in studies of the formation and evolution of galaxies.

In July 2018, Reinhard Genzel *et al.* reported that [star S2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S2_(star) "S2 (star)") orbiting Sgr A\* had been recorded at 7,650 km/s or 2.55% the speed of light leading up to the [pericentre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pericentre "Pericentre") approach in May 2018 at about 120 [AU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_unit "Astronomical unit") ≈ 1400 [Schwarzschild radii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarzschild_radius "Schwarzschild radius") from Sgr A\*. This allowed them to test the [redshift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redshift "Redshift") predicted by [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity") at [relativistic velocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativistic_velocities "Relativistic velocities"), finding additional confirmation of the theory.
