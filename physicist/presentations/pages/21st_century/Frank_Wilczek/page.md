**Frank Anthony Wilczek** ([/ˈvɪltʃɛk/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") or [/ˈwɪltʃɛk/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); born May 15, 1951) is an American [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics"). He shared the 2004 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [David Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gross "David Gross") and [H. David Politzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._David_Politzer "H. David Politzer") "for the discovery of [asymptotic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_freedom "Asymptotic freedom") in the theory of the [strong interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_interaction "Strong interaction")".

Wilczek is the [Herman Feshbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Feshbach "Herman Feshbach") Professor of Physics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (MIT), Founding Director of T. D. Lee Institute (Shanghai) and Chief Scientist at the Wilczek Quantum Center, [Shanghai Jiao Tong University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Jiao_Tong_University "Shanghai Jiao Tong University") (SJTU), distinguished professor at [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University "Arizona State University") (ASU) during February and March and full professor at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University "Stockholm University"). He has written [popular science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_science "Popular science"). *Longing For the Harmonies* (1988), cowritten with his wife [Betsy Devine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betsy_Devine "Betsy Devine"), draws on parallels between physics and [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music "Music"). *A Beautiful Question* (2015) looks at the universe as a work of art. In May 2022, he was awarded the [Templeton Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Templeton_Prize "Templeton Prize") for his "investigations into the fundamental laws of nature", that have "transformed our understanding of the forces that govern our universe".

## Early life and education

Born in [Mineola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineola,_New_York "Mineola, New York"), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(state) "New York (state)"), Wilczek is of Polish and Italian origin. His grandparents were immigrants who "really did work with their hands", according to Wilczek, but his father took night school classes to educate himself, working as a repairman to support his family. Wilczek's father became a "self-taught engineer", whose interests in technology and science inspired his son.

Wilczek was educated in the public schools of [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens,_New_York "Queens, New York"), attending [Martin Van Buren High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Van_Buren_High_School_(New_York_City) "Martin Van Buren High School (New York City)"). It was around this time Wilczek's parents realized that he was exceptional, in part as a result of their son having been administered an [IQ test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IQ_test "IQ test").

After skipping two grades, Wilczek started high school in the 10th grade, when he was 13 years old. He was particularly inspired by two of his high school physics teachers, one of whom taught a course that helped students with the national [Westinghouse Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Science_Talent_Search "Westinghouse Science Talent Search"). Wilczek was a finalist in 1967 and ultimately won fourth place, based on a mathematical project involving [group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_theory "Group theory").

He received his [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in [Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") and membership in [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa "Phi Beta Kappa") at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") in 1970. During his last year as a math major at Chicago, he attended a course taught by [Peter Freund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Freund "Peter Freund") on [group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_theory "Group theory") in physics, which Wilczek later described as being "basically [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics")", and very influential:

> Peter Freund played a big role in my life, though, because he taught this course on group theory, or [symmetry in physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_(physics) "Symmetry (physics)") that—he was so enthusiastic, and he really gushed—and it's beautiful material. Still to this day I think the [quantum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") theory of angular momentum is one of the absolute pinnacles of human achievement. Just beautiful.

Wilczek attended graduate school at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"). After a year and a half, he transferred from mathematics to physics, with [David Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gross "David Gross") as his thesis advisor. He earned a Master of Arts in Mathematics in 1972 and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in physics in 1974.

## Research

Wilczek's 2004 Nobel Prize was for asymptotic freedom, but he has helped reveal and develop [axions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axion "Axion"), [anyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyon "Anyon"), [asymptotic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_freedom "Asymptotic freedom"), the [color superconducting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_superconductivity "Color superconductivity") phases of [quark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark_matter "Quark matter"), and other aspects of [quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field_theory "Quantum field theory"). He has worked on [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics "Condensed matter physics"), [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics "Astrophysics"), and [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics").

### Asymptotic freedom

In 1973, while a graduate student working with [David Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gross "David Gross") at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), Wilczek (together with Gross) discovered [asymptotic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_freedom "Asymptotic freedom"), which holds that the closer [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarks "Quarks") are to each other, the less the [strong interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_interaction "Strong interaction") (or [color charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge "Color charge")) between them; when quarks are in extreme proximity, the nuclear force between them is so weak that they behave almost as free particles. The theory, which was independently discovered by [H. David Politzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._David_Politzer "H. David Politzer"), was important for the development of [quantum chromodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chromodynamics "Quantum chromodynamics"). According to the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences") when awarding Wilczek its Lorentz Medal in 2002,

> This [asymptotic freedom] is a phenomenon whereby the building blocks which make up the nucleus of an atom – 'quarks' – behave as free particles when they are close together, but become more strongly attracted to each other as the distance between them increases. This theory forms the key to the interpretation of almost all experimental studies involving modern particle accelerators.

### Axions

The axion is a hypothetical [elementary particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle "Elementary particle"). If axions exist and have low mass within a specific range, they are of interest as a possible component of [cold dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_dark_matter "Cold dark matter").

In 1977, [Roberto Peccei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Peccei "Roberto Peccei") and [Helen Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Quinn "Helen Quinn") postulated a solution to the strong CP problem, the [Peccei–Quinn mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peccei–Quinn_theory "Peccei–Quinn theory"). This is accomplished by adding a new global symmetry (called a [Peccei–Quinn symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peccei–Quinn_symmetry "Peccei–Quinn symmetry").) When that symmetry is spontaneously broken, a new particle results, as shown independently by Wilczek and by [Steven Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Weinberg "Steven Weinberg"). Wilczek named this new hypothetical particle the "axion" after a [brand of laundry detergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axion_(brand) "Axion (brand)"), while Weinberg called it "Higglet". Weinberg later agreed to adopt Wilczek's name for the particle.

Although most experimental searches for dark matter candidates have targeted [WIMPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_interacting_massive_particles "Weakly interacting massive particles"), there have also been many attempts to detect axions. In June, 2020, an international team of physicists working in Italy detected a signal that could be axions.

### Anyons

In [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics"), an anyon is a type of [quasiparticle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiparticle "Quasiparticle") that occurs only in [*two*-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-dimensional "Two-dimensional") [systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_system "Physical system"), with properties much less restricted than [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion "Fermion") and [bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson "Boson"). In particular, anyons can have properties intermediate between fermions and bosons, including fractional electric charge. This anything-goes behavior inspired Wilczek in 1982 to name them "anyons".

In 1977, a group of [theoretical physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics") working at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo "University of Oslo"), led by [Jon Leinaas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Magne_Leinaas "Jon Magne Leinaas") and [Jan Myrheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Myrheim "Jan Myrheim"), calculated that the traditional division between fermions and bosons would not apply to theoretical particles existing in two [dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension "Dimension"). When [Daniel Tsui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Tsui "Daniel Tsui") and [Horst Störmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horst_Störmer "Horst Störmer") discovered the [fractional quantum Hall effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_quantum_Hall_effect "Fractional quantum Hall effect") in 1982, [Bertrand Halperin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_Halperin "Bertrand Halperin") (1984) expanded the math Wilczek proposed in 1982 for fractional statistics in two dimensions to help explain it.

Frank Wilczek, Dan Arovas, and [Robert Schrieffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Schrieffer "Robert Schrieffer") analyzed the fractional quantum Hall effect in 1984, proving that anyons were required to describe it.

In 2020, experimenters from the [Ecole Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecole_Normale_Supérieure "Ecole Normale Supérieure") and from the [Centre for Nanosciences and Nanotechnologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Nanosciences_and_Nanotechnologies "Centre for Nanosciences and Nanotechnologies") (C2N) reported in *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_(journal) "Science (journal)")* that they had made a direct detection of anyons.

### Time crystals

In 2012 he proposed the idea of a [time crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_crystal "Time crystal"). In 2018, several research teams reported the existence of time crystals. In 2018, he and Qing-Dong Jiang calculated that the so-called "quantum atmosphere" of materials should theoretically be capable of being probed using existing technology such as diamond probes with [nitrogen-vacancy centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen-vacancy_center "Nitrogen-vacancy center").

### Current research

- "Pure" particle physics: connections between theoretical ideas and observable phenomena;
- behavior of matter: phase structure of [quark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark_matter "Quark matter") at ultra-high temperature and density; [color superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_superconductivity "Color superconductivity");
- application of particle physics to [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_cosmology "Physical cosmology");
- application of [field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_(physics) "Field (physics)") techniques to [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics "Condensed matter physics");
- [quantum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_gravity "Quantum gravity") of [black holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_holes "Black holes"); see, e.g., the application of the moving mirror model to black holes.

## Personal life

Wilczek met [Betsy Devine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betsy_Devine "Betsy Devine") at Princeton, when both watched the televised [1972 Fisher-Spassky chess matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1972 "World Chess Championship 1972"). They married in 1973, and together they have two daughters. His favorite physicist is [James Clerk Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clerk_Maxwell "James Clerk Maxwell").

### Religious views

Wilczek was raised [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic") but later "lost faith in conventional religion" although he told *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American "Scientific American")* that religion "had meant a lot to me as a teenager". He has been described as an [agnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnostic "Agnostic") but tweeted in 2013 that "'[pantheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantheist "Pantheist")' is closer to the mark".

Wilczek said that "the world embodies beautiful ideas" but "although this may inspire a spiritual interpretation, it does not require one".

### Science outreach and activism

Wilczek is a member of the Scientific Advisory Board for the [Future of Life Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_Life_Institute "Future of Life Institute"), an organization that works to mitigate [existential risks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_catastrophic_risk "Global catastrophic risk") facing humanity, particularly [existential risk from advanced artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_risk_from_artificial_general_intelligence "Existential risk from artificial general intelligence"). Wilczek appeared on an episode of *[Penn & Teller: Bullshit!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_&_Teller:_Bullshit! "Penn & Teller: Bullshit!")*, where [Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_Jillette "Penn Jillette") referred to him as "the smartest person [they have] ever had on the show".

In 2014, Wilczek penned a letter, along with [Stephen Hawking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking "Stephen Hawking") and two other scholars, warning that "Success in creating [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_Intelligence "Artificial Intelligence") would be the biggest event in human history. Unfortunately, it might also be the last, unless we learn how to avoid the risks."

Wilczek is also a supporter of the [Campaign for the Establishment of a United Nations Parliamentary Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_the_Establishment_of_a_United_Nations_Parliamentary_Assembly "Campaign for the Establishment of a United Nations Parliamentary Assembly"), an organization which advocates for democratic reform in the United Nations, and the creation of a more accountable international political system.

Wilczek is on the board for [Society for Science & the Public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Science_&_the_Public "Society for Science & the Public"). He is a co-founding member of the [Kosciuszko Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosciuszko_Foundation "Kosciuszko Foundation") of the Collegium of Eminent Scientists of Polish Origin and Ancestry.

## Honors

In 1982, Wilczek was awarded a [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellowship "MacArthur Fellowship"). He was elected as a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1990, a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1993, and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 2005.

Wilczek became a foreign member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 2000. He was awarded the [Lorentz Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_Medal "Lorentz Medal") in 2002. Wilczek won the [Lilienfeld Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilienfeld_Prize "Lilienfeld Prize") of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") in 2003. In the same year, he was awarded the Faculty of Mathematics and Physics Commemorative Medal from [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University "Charles University") in Prague. He was the co-recipient of the 2003 [High Energy and Particle Physics Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Energy_and_Particle_Physics_Prize "High Energy and Particle Physics Prize") of the [European Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Physical_Society "European Physical Society"). The Nobel Prize in Physics 2004 was awarded jointly to [David J. Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_J._Gross "David J. Gross"), [H. David Politzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._David_Politzer "H. David Politzer") and Frank Wilczek "for the discovery of asymptotic freedom in the theory of the strong interaction". Wilczek was also the co-recipient of the 2005 [King Faisal International Prize for Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_International_Prize_for_Science "King Faisal International Prize for Science"). In that same year, he received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement"). On January 25, 2013, Wilczek received an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree "Honorary degree") from the Faculty of Science and Technology at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University"), Sweden. He also served on the Physical Sciences jury for the Infosys Prize from 2009 to 2011. In 2011, Wilczek gave the [George Gamow Memorial Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow_Memorial_Lectures "George Gamow Memorial Lectures") at the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder "University of Colorado Boulder"). In 2022 he was awarded the [Templeton Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Templeton_Prize "Templeton Prize") for the work that reveals "a vision of a universe that he regards as embodying mathematical beauty at the scales of the magnificently large and unimaginably small".

Wilczek holds the [Herman Feshbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Feshbach "Herman Feshbach") Professorship of Physics at [MIT Center for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Center_for_Theoretical_Physics "MIT Center for Theoretical Physics"). He has also worked at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") in Princeton and the [Institute for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavli_Institute_for_Theoretical_Physics "Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics") at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara "University of California, Santa Barbara") and was also a visiting professor at [NORDITA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NORDITA "NORDITA").
