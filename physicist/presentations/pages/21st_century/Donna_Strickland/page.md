**Donna Theo Strickland** (born May 27, 1959) is a Canadian [optical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_Physics "Optical Physics") and pioneer in the field of [pulsed lasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsed_lasers "Pulsed lasers"). She was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 2018, together with [Gérard Mourou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gérard_Mourou "Gérard Mourou"), for the practical implementation of [chirped pulse amplification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirped_pulse_amplification "Chirped pulse amplification"). She is a professor at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo "University of Waterloo") in Ontario, Canada.

Strickland served as fellow, vice president, and president of [Optica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optica_(society) "Optica (society)") (formerly OSA), and is currently chair of its Presidential Advisory Committee. In 2018, she was listed as one of [BBC's 100 Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_Women_(BBC) "100 Women (BBC)"). She has gone on to have the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada Prize being set in her name.

## Early life and education

Strickland was born on May 27, 1959, in [Guelph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelph "Guelph"), Ontario, Canada to Edith J. ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Ranney), an English teacher, and Lloyd Strickland, an electrical engineer. After graduating from [Guelph Collegiate Vocational Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelph_Collegiate_Vocational_Institute "Guelph Collegiate Vocational Institute"), she decided to attend [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University "McMaster University") because its [engineering physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_physics "Engineering physics") program included lasers and [electro-optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro-optics "Electro-optics"), areas of particular interest to her. At McMaster, she was one of three women in a class of twenty-five. Strickland graduated with a [Bachelor of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Engineering "Bachelor of Engineering") degree in engineering physics in 1981.

Strickland studied for her graduate degree in [The Institute of Optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Institute_of_Optics "The Institute of Optics") at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester "University of Rochester"), receiving a Doctor of Philosophy degree in 1989.
She conducted her doctoral research at the associated [Laboratory for Laser Energetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_for_Laser_Energetics "Laboratory for Laser Energetics"), supervised by [Gérard Mourou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gérard_Mourou "Gérard Mourou").
Strickland and Mourou worked to develop an experimental setup that could raise the peak power of laser pulses, to overcome a limitation: that when the maximal intensity of laser pulses reached gigawatts per square centimetre, [self-focusing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-focusing "Self-focusing") of the pulses severely damaged the amplifying part of the laser.
Their 1985 technique of [chirped pulse amplification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirped_pulse_amplification "Chirped pulse amplification") stretched out each laser pulse both spectrally and in time before amplifying it, then compressed each pulse back to its original duration, generating ultrashort optical pulses of [terawatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terawatt "Terawatt") to [petawatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petawatt "Petawatt") intensity.
Using chirped pulse amplification allowed smaller high-power laser systems to be built on a typical laboratory [optical table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_table "Optical table"), as "table-top terawatt lasers".
The work received the 2018 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics").

## Career and research

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Ultrafast_Laser_group.jpg/500px-Ultrafast_Laser_group.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ultrafast_Laser_group.jpg)

Strickland's [ultrafast laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafast_laser "Ultrafast laser") group at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo "University of Waterloo") in 2017

From 1988 to 1991, Strickland was a research associate at the [National Research Council of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_of_Canada "National Research Council of Canada"), where she worked with [Paul Corkum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Corkum "Paul Corkum") in the Ultrafast Phenomena Section, which had the distinction at that time of having produced the most powerful short-pulse laser in the world. She worked in the laser division of [Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Livermore_National_Laboratory "Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory") from 1991 to 1992 and joined the technical staff of [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University")'s Advanced Technology Center for Photonics and Opto-electronic Materials in 1992. She joined the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo "University of Waterloo") in 1997 as an assistant professor. She became the first full-time female professor in physics at the University of Waterloo. Strickland is currently a professor, leading an [ultrafast laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafast_laser "Ultrafast laser") group that develops high-intensity laser systems for nonlinear optics investigations. She has described herself as a "laser jock":

> I think it's because we thought we were good with our hands. As an experimentalist, you need to understand the physics, but you also need to be able to actually make something work, and the lasers were very finicky in those days.

Strickland's recent work has focused on pushing the boundaries of ultrafast optical science to new [wavelength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength "Wavelength") ranges such as the [mid-infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-infrared "Mid-infrared") and the [ultraviolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet "Ultraviolet"), using techniques such as two-colour or multi-frequency methods, as well as [Raman generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raman_scattering "Raman scattering"). She is also working on the role of high-power lasers in the microcrystalline lens of the human eye, during the process of micromachining of the eye lens to cure [presbyopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyopia "Presbyopia").

Strickland, 2016

Strickland became a fellow of [Optica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optica_(society) "Optica (society)") in 2008. She served as its vice president and president in 2011 and 2013 respectively, and was a topical editor of its journal *[Optics Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics_Letters "Optics Letters")* from 2004 to 2010. She is currently the chair of Optica's Presidential Advisory Committee. She is a member of and previously served as a board member and Director of Academic Affairs for the [Canadian Association of Physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Association_of_Physicists "Canadian Association of Physicists"). She is an inaugural and active co-director of the University of Waterloo's Trust in Research Undertaken in Science and Technology (TRuST) Scholarly Network, established in 2023.

## Nobel Prize

On 2 October 2018, Strickland was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for her work on [chirped pulse amplification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirped_pulse_amplification "Chirped pulse amplification") with her doctoral adviser Gérard Mourou. [Arthur Ashkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ashkin "Arthur Ashkin") received the other half of the prize for unrelated work on [optical tweezers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_tweezers "Optical tweezers"). She became the third woman ever to be awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics, after [Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie "Marie Curie") in 1903 and [Maria Goeppert Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Goeppert_Mayer "Maria Goeppert Mayer") in 1963.

Strickland and Mourou published their pioneering work "Compression of amplified chirped optical pulses" in 1985, while Strickland was still a doctoral student under Mourou. Their invention of chirped pulse amplification for lasers at the Laboratory for Laser Energetics in Rochester led to the development of the field of high-intensity ultrashort pulses of light beams. Because the ultrabrief and ultrasharp light beams are capable of making extremely precise cuts, the technique is used in [laser micromachining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_machining "Laser machining"), [laser surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_surgery "Laser surgery"), medicine, [fundamental science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_science "Fundamental science") studies, and other applications. It has enabled doctors to perform millions of corrective [laser eye surgeries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_eye_surgery "Laser eye surgery"). She said that after developing the technique, they knew it would be a significant discovery.

When she received the Nobel Prize, many commentators were surprised that she had not reached the rank of full professor. In response, Strickland said that she had "never applied" for a professorship; "it doesn't carry necessarily a pay raise ... I never filled out the paper work ... I do what I want to do and that wasn't worth doing." Strickland had not applied to be a full professor prior to her Nobel prize, but in October 2018, she told the BBC that she had subsequently applied and was promoted to full professorship at the University of Waterloo.

## Personal life

Strickland is married to Douglas Dykaar, who received a doctorate in electrical engineering from the University of Rochester. They have two children: Hannah, a graduate student in [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics "Astrophysics") at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto "University of Toronto"), and Adam, who is studying comedy at [Humber College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humber_College "Humber College"). Strickland is an active member of the [United Church of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Canada "United Church of Canada").
