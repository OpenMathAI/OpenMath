**Anne Geneviève L'Huillier** (French: [[an lɥije]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 16 August 1958) is a French [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). She is a professor of [atomic physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_physics "Atomic physics") at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University "Lund University") in Sweden.

She leads an [attosecond physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attophysics "Attophysics") group which works through the movements of [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") in real time, which is used to understand chemical reactions on the atomic level.
Her [experimental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics") and [theoretical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics") research are credited with laying the foundation for the field of attochemistry.
In 2003 she and her group beat the world record for the shortest laser pulse, of 170 [attoseconds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attosecond "Attosecond").

L'Huillier became a member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences") in 2004. She has received various physics awards including the [Wolf Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Physics "Wolf Prize in Physics") in 2022 and the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 2023.

## Life

### Education

Anne L'Huillier was born in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") in 1958. She was awarded a double [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree "Master's degree") in [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics") and [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"), but switched for her [doctorate degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate_degree "Doctorate degree") to [experimental physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics") at [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University "Pierre and Marie Curie University").
Her dissertation was on multiple multiphoton [ionization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionization "Ionization") in [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser "Laser") fields of high intensity. She carried out her dissertation research at the [Commissariat à l'énergie atomique et aux énergies alternatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissariat_à_l'énergie_atomique_et_aux_énergies_alternatives "Commissariat à l'énergie atomique et aux énergies alternatives") (CEA), near Paris.

### Career

As a post-doctoral student, L'Huillier worked at the [Chalmers Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalmers_Institute_of_Technology "Chalmers Institute of Technology") in [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg "Gothenburg"), Sweden, and at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California "University of Southern California")  in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), United States.
In 1986, L'Huillier obtained a permanent position as a researcher at the CEA, at the [Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEA_Saclay "CEA Saclay") site.

In 1992, she took part in an experiment in [Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund "Lund"), where one of the first [titanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium "Titanium")-[sapphire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapphire "Sapphire") [solid-state laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_laser "Solid-state laser") systems for [femtosecond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femtosecond "Femtosecond") pulses in Europe had been installed. In 1994 she moved to Sweden, where she was appointed at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University "Lund University") as a lecturer in 1995 and a professor in 1997. She also serves as governing board member at the [Institut d’optique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_d'optique_Graduate_School "Institut d'optique Graduate School"), France.

## Research

L'Huillier's research involves experimental and theoretical aspects of [high harmonic generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_harmonic_generation "High harmonic generation") in gases, which corresponded to extremely short light pulses in the [ultraviolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet "Ultraviolet") spectral range, lasting tens or hundreds of attoseconds. In 1987, L’Huillier first observed that gases like [argon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argon "Argon") would react to a laser by becoming excited and emitting additional radiation or [overtones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtone "Overtone"), at various multiples of the frequency of laser.

In 1991, L'Huillier in collaboration with [Kenneth Schafer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_J._Schafer "Kenneth J. Schafer") and Kenneth Kulander presented numerical simulations of the time-dependent [Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_equation "Schrödinger equation") in order to understand the generation of high-order harmonics. They first predicted the shape of the high harmonics spectrum and the [phase-matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase-matching "Phase-matching") conditions. In 1994, [Maciej Lewenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maciej_Lewenstein "Maciej Lewenstein"), L’Huiller and [Paul Corkum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Corkum "Paul Corkum") presented a full [quantum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") of high harmonic generation.

L'Huillier uses attosecond sources to study ultrafast dynamics of electrons in atomic and molecular systems. In 2003 her group beat the world record for the shortest laser pulse, lasting 170 [attoseconds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attosecond "Attosecond"). These attosecond sources are considered the world's fastest cameras, using extremely short pulses of light to measure electrons as they move or change in energy. L’Huillier's methods for studying and manipulating electrons using light have pioneered the field of attochemistry as they allow the study electronic processes during chemical reactions.

In 2010, an experiment led by [Ferenc Krausz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferenc_Krausz "Ferenc Krausz") at the [Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_of_Quantum_Optics "Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics"), highlighted a discrepancy between theory and experimental results concerning [photoemission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoelectric_effect "Photoelectric effect") delays in [neon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon "Neon") atoms. L'Huillier group in Lund solved this problem in 2017 by experimentally uncovering the contribution of [shake-up electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiative_Auger_effect "Radiative Auger effect"). By correcting for this effect L'Huillier group found an excellent agreement with theory.

## Honors and awards

L'Huillier was on the [Nobel Committee for Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Committee_for_Physics "Nobel Committee for Physics") between 2007 and 2015, and has been a member of the [Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Swedish Academy of Sciences") since 2004. In 2003, she received the [Julius Springer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Springer_Prize?action=edit&redlink=1 "Julius Springer Prize"). In 2011 she received a [UNESCO L'Oréal Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_L'Oréal_Award "UNESCO L'Oréal Award"). In 2013, she was awarded the [Carl-Zeiss Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl-Zeiss_Research_Award?action=edit&redlink=1 "Carl-Zeiss Research Award"), the [Blaise Pascal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Pascal_Medal?action=edit&redlink=1 "Blaise Pascal Medal") and an Honorary Degree at [Université Pierre et Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Pierre_et_Marie_Curie "Université Pierre et Marie Curie") (UPMC), Paris. She was elected a foreign associate of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 2018. One year later, in 2019, she was recognized with the Prize for Fundamental Aspects of Quantum Electronics and Optics, announced by the [European Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Physical_Society "European Physical Society"). Anne L'Huillier is a fellow member of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") and [Optica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optica_(society) "Optica (society)").

In 2021 L'Huillier was awarded the [Optical Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Optical_Society "The Optical Society") Max Born Award for "pioneering work in ultrafast laser science and attosecond physics, realizing and understanding high harmonic generation and applying it to time-resolved imaging of electron motion in atoms and molecules". In 2022 she received the [Wolf Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Physics "Wolf Prize in Physics") for "pioneering contributions to ultrafast laser science and attosecond physics" jointly with [Ferenc Krausz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferenc_Krausz "Ferenc Krausz") and [Paul Corkum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Corkum "Paul Corkum"). Also for 2022, the three were awarded the [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Award "BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award") in Basic Sciences. She also received the French [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour "Legion of Honour") the same year.

In September 2023, she received the [Berthold Leibinger Zukunftspreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berthold_Leibinger_Zukunftspreis "Berthold Leibinger Zukunftspreis") for "high harmonic generation and attosecond physics**."** In October 2023, she was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), jointly with Krausz and [Pierre Agostini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Agostini "Pierre Agostini") "for experimental methods that generate attosecond pulses of light for the study of electron dynamics in matter".

In November 2023, she received a [doctor honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree "Honorary degree") from [Paris-Saclay University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Saclay_University "Paris-Saclay University"). During the ceremony, 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics, [Alain Aspect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Aspect "Alain Aspect") was the guest of honor.

In March 2024, she received a doctor honoris causa from [University of Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bordeaux "University of Bordeaux"), and in May from the [University of Porto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Porto "University of Porto").

## Personal life

L'Huillier is married to [Claes-Göran Wahlström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claes-Göran_Wahlström?action=edit&redlink=1 "Claes-Göran Wahlström") who is also a professor at Lund University. They have two children.
