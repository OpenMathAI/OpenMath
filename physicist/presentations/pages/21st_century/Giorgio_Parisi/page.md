**Giorgio Parisi** (born 4 August 1948) is an Italian [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physicist "Theoretical physicist"), whose research has focused on [quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field_theory "Quantum field theory"), [statistical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics") and [complex systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_systems "Complex systems"). His best known contributions are the [QCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chromodynamics "Quantum chromodynamics") evolution equations for parton densities, obtained with [Guido Altarelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Altarelli "Guido Altarelli"), known as the Altarelli–Parisi or [DGLAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DGLAP "DGLAP") equations, the exact solution of the [Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherrington–Kirkpatrick_model "Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model") of spin glasses, the [Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kardar–Parisi–Zhang_equation "Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation") describing dynamic scaling of growing interfaces, and the study of whirling [flocks of birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flocks_of_birds "Flocks of birds"). He was awarded the 2021 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") jointly with [Klaus Hasselmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Hasselmann "Klaus Hasselmann") and [Syukuro Manabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syukuro_Manabe "Syukuro Manabe") for groundbreaking contributions to theory of [complex systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_system "Complex system"), in particular "for the discovery of the interplay of disorder and [fluctuations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_fluctuation "Quantum fluctuation") in physical systems from atomic to planetary scales".

## Early life and education

Giorgio Parisi was born in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome"). He is one-quarter Umbrian, one-quarter Piedmontese, one-quarter Sicilian, and one-quarter Roman for seven generations. He attended the "San Gabriele" High School in Rome in 1966, and later received his degree from the [University of Rome La Sapienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_La_Sapienza "University of Rome La Sapienza") in 1970 under the supervision of [Nicola Cabibbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Cabibbo "Nicola Cabibbo") with a thesis on the Higgs boson.

## Career

After graduating, Parisi began working first at the [National Research Council (Italy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_(Italy) "National Research Council (Italy)") (CNR), then at the [Istituto Nazionale di Fisica Nucleare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Nazionale_di_Fisica_Nucleare "Istituto Nazionale di Fisica Nucleare") (INFN) at the Frascati National Laboratories (1971 to 1981). Introduced by [Sidney David Drell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_David_Drell "Sidney David Drell")
to [Tsung-Dao Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsung-Dao_Lee "Tsung-Dao Lee"), he also worked at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") (1973–1974) and later at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques "Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques") (1976–1977) and the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure "École Normale Supérieure") in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") (1977–1978), before returning to [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy") as a researcher for the INFN.
From 1982 until 1992 he was a full professor of [Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_Physics "Theoretical Physics") at the [University of Rome Tor Vergata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_Tor_Vergata "University of Rome Tor Vergata"), and from 1992 at the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome "Sapienza University of Rome"), where he taught several courses, including [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics"), [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"), [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics "Statistical physics"), and [probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability "Probability").

He also took part in the [APE100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APE100 "APE100") project for the study of [lattice gauge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_gauge_theory "Lattice gauge theory").
He was a member of the Simons Collaboration "Cracking the Glass Problem".

After retiring in 2018, until 2021, he was elected president of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei "Accademia dei Lincei") and in 2023 he was also elected Fellow of [The World Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Academy_of_Sciences "The World Academy of Sciences").

In February 2021, he won the [Wolf Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Physics "Wolf Prize in Physics"), and in October of the same year, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics").

## Research

Parisi's research interests are broad and cover [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics "Statistical physics"), [field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_(physics) "Field theory (physics)"), [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_system "Dynamical system"), [mathematical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physics "Mathematical physics") and [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics "Condensed matter physics"), where he is particularly known for his work on [spin glasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_glasses "Spin glasses") and related statistical mechanics models originating in [optimization theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_theory "Optimization theory") and biology. In particular, he made significant contributions in terms of systematic applications of the replica method to disordered systems, even though the replica method itself was originally discovered in 1971 by Sir [Sam Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Edwards_(physicist) "Sam Edwards (physicist)").

He has also contributed to the field of [elementary particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle_physics "Elementary particle physics"), in particular to [quantum chromodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chromodynamics "Quantum chromodynamics") and [string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_theory "String theory"). Together with Guido Altarelli, he introduced the so-called Dokshitzer–Gribov–Lipatov–Altarelli–Parisi equations. In the field of [fluid dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_dynamics "Fluid dynamics") he is known for having introduced, together with [Uriel Frisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uriel_Frisch "Uriel Frisch"), multifractal models to describe the phenomenon of [intermittency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermittency "Intermittency") in turbulent flows. He is also known for the [Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kardar–Parisi–Zhang_equation "Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation") modelling stochastic aggregation. From the point of view of complex systems, he worked on the collective motion of animals (such as swarms and flocks). He also introduced, together with other Italian physicists, the concept of [stochastic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_resonance "Stochastic resonance") in the study of climate change.

- [Dirac Medal of the ICTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_Medal_(ICTP) "Dirac Medal (ICTP)"), 1999.

## Activism

Since 2016, Giorgio Parisi has been leading the movement "Salviamo la Ricerca Italiana" to put pressure on the Italian and European governments to start funding basic research above the subsistence level.
