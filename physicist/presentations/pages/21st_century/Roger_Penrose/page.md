**Sir Roger Penrose** (born 8 August 1931) is an English mathematician, [mathematical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physicist "Mathematical physicist"), and [philosopher of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science "Philosophy of science"). He is [Emeritus](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Emeritus "wikt:Emeritus") [Rouse Ball Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouse_Ball_Professor_of_Mathematics "Rouse Ball Professor of Mathematics") at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford"), an emeritus fellow of [Wadham College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadham_College,_Oxford "Wadham College, Oxford"), and an honorary fellow of [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge "St John's College, Cambridge"), and [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London"). He shared the 1988 [Wolf Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Physics "Wolf Prize in Physics") with [Stephen Hawking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking "Stephen Hawking") for the [Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose–Hawking_singularity_theorems "Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems"), and the 2020 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") "for the discovery that [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole "Black hole") formation is a robust prediction of the [general theory of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_theory_of_relativity "General theory of relativity")". He proposed the [Penrose triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_triangle "Penrose triangle") and corresponded with [M. C. Escher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._C._Escher "M. C. Escher"), influencing his [*Waterfall*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfall_(M._C._Escher) "Waterfall (M. C. Escher)") and *[Ascending and Descending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascending_and_Descending "Ascending and Descending")*. Penrose's eponymous [aperiodic tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_tiling "Penrose tiling") presaged the discovery of [quasicrystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystals "Quasicrystals") by [Dan Shechtman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shechtman "Dan Shechtman").

Penrose won the [Royal Society Science Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_Prizes_for_Science_Books "Royal Society Prizes for Science Books") for *[The Emperor's New Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emperor's_New_Mind "The Emperor's New Mind")* (1989), which outlines his views on physics and [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness "Consciousness"). He followed it with *[The Road to Reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Road_to_Reality "The Road to Reality")* (2004), billed as "A Complete Guide to the Laws of the Universe".

## Early life and education

Born in [Colchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colchester "Colchester"), Essex, Roger Penrose is a son of Margaret (née Leathes), a physician, and [Lionel Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Penrose "Lionel Penrose"), a psychiatrist and geneticist. His paternal grandparents were [J. Doyle Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Doyle_Penrose "J. Doyle Penrose"), an Irish-born painter, and the Hon. Elizabeth Josephine Peckover, daughter of [Alexander Peckover, 1st Baron Peckover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Peckover,_1st_Baron_Peckover "Alexander Peckover, 1st Baron Peckover"); his maternal grandparents were [John Beresford Leathes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Beresford_Leathes "John Beresford Leathes"), a physiologist, and Sonia Marie Natanson, a concert pianist. His uncle was the artist Sir [Roland Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Penrose "Roland Penrose"), whose son with the American photographer [Lee Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Miller "Lee Miller") is [Antony Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Penrose "Antony Penrose"). Penrose is the brother of the physicist [Oliver Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Penrose "Oliver Penrose"), of the geneticist [Shirley Hodgson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Hodgson "Shirley Hodgson") and of the chess grandmaster [Jonathan Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Penrose "Jonathan Penrose"). Their stepfather was the mathematician and computer scientist [Max Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Newman "Max Newman"). He found inspiration in [George Gamow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow "George Gamow")'s [Mr. Tompkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Tompkins "Mr. Tompkins") books: "I remember reading (or being read) the Tompkins stories as a quite young child, and I'm sure that their magic was responsible, to a very considerable extent, for the great excitement that fundamental physics has held for me for the rest of my life."

Penrose spent the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War") as a child in Canada where his father worked in [London, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_Ontario "London, Ontario"), at the Ontario Hospital and [Western University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Ontario "University of Western Ontario"). Penrose studied at [University College School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_School "University College School"). He then attended [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London"), where he obtained a [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree with [First Class Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Class_Honours "First Class Honours") in mathematics in 1952.

In 1955, while a doctoral student, Penrose reintroduced the [E. H. Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._H._Moore "E. H. Moore") generalised matrix inverse, also known as the [Moore–Penrose inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore–Penrose_inverse "Moore–Penrose inverse"), after it had been reinvented by [Arne Bjerhammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arne_Bjerhammar "Arne Bjerhammar") in 1951. Having started research under the professor of geometry and astronomy [W. V. D. Hodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._V._D._Hodge "W. V. D. Hodge"), Penrose received his PhD in [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry") at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge "St John's College, Cambridge"), in 1957, with his thesis titled "Tensor Methods in Algebraic Geometry" supervised by the algebraist and geometer [John A. Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._A._Todd "J. A. Todd"). He devised and popularised the [Penrose triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_triangle "Penrose triangle") in the 1950s in collaboration with his father, describing it as "impossibility in its purest form", and exchanged material with the artist [M. C. Escher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._C._Escher "M. C. Escher"), whose earlier depictions of impossible objects partly inspired it. Escher's [*Waterfall*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfall_(M._C._Escher) "Waterfall (M. C. Escher)") and *[Ascending and Descending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascending_and_Descending "Ascending and Descending")* were in turn inspired by Penrose.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Penrose-dreieck.svg/250px-Penrose-dreieck.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Penrose-dreieck.svg)

The [Penrose triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_triangle "Penrose triangle")

As the reviewer [Manjit Kumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjit_Kumar "Manjit Kumar") puts it:

> As a student in 1954, Penrose was attending a conference in Amsterdam when by chance he came across an exhibition of Escher's work. Soon he was trying to conjure up impossible figures of his own and discovered the tribar – a triangle that looks like a real, solid three-dimensional object, but isn't. Together with his father, a physicist and mathematician, Penrose went on to design a [staircase that simultaneously loops up and down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_stairs "Penrose stairs"). An article followed and a copy was sent to Escher. Completing a cyclical flow of creativity, the Dutch master of geometrical illusions was inspired to produce his two masterpieces.

## Research and career

Penrose spent the academic year 1956–57 as an assistant lecturer at Bedford College (now [Royal Holloway, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Holloway,_University_of_London "Royal Holloway, University of London")) and was then a research fellow at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge "St John's College, Cambridge"). During that three-year post, he married Joan Isabel Wedge, in 1959. Before the fellowship ended Penrose won a [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") Research Fellowship for 1959–61, first at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") and then at [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University "Syracuse University"). Returning to the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London "University of London"), Penrose spent 1961–1963 as a researcher at [King's College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_London "King's College, London"), before returning to the United States to spend 1963–64 as a visiting associate professor at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin "University of Texas at Austin"). He later held visiting positions at [Yeshiva University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_University "Yeshiva University"), Princeton and [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") during 1966–67 and 1969.

[Dennis Sciama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Sciama "Dennis Sciama") drew his attention from pure mathematics to astrophysics. In 1964, while a [reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_(academic_rank) "Reader (academic rank)") at [Birkbeck College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkbeck_College "Birkbeck College"), Penrose, per [Kip Thorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kip_Thorne "Kip Thorne") of the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology"), "revolutionised the mathematical tools that we use to analyse the properties of spacetime". Until then, work on the curved geometry of general relativity had been confined to configurations with sufficiently high symmetry for Einstein's equations to be solvable explicitly, and there was doubt about whether such cases were typical. One approach to this issue was by the use of [perturbation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perturbation_theory "Perturbation theory"), as developed under the leadership of [John Archibald Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Archibald_Wheeler "John Archibald Wheeler") at Princeton. The other, and more radically innovative, approach initiated by Penrose was to overlook the detailed geometrical structure of spacetime and instead concentrate attention just on the topology of the space, or at most its [conformal structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_structure "Conformal structure"), since it is the latter – as determined by the lay of the lightcones – that determines the trajectories of lightlike geodesics, and hence their causal relationships. The importance of Penrose's paper "Gravitational Collapse and Space-Time Singularities" (summarised roughly as that if an object such as a dying star implodes beyond a certain point, then nothing can prevent the gravitational field getting so strong as to form some kind of singularity) was not its only result. It also showed a way to obtain similarly general conclusions in other contexts, notably that of the cosmological [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang "Big Bang"), which he dealt with in collaboration with Sciama's student [Stephen Hawking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking "Stephen Hawking").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3b/CNRSblackhole.jpg/330px-CNRSblackhole.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:CNRSblackhole.jpg)

Predicted view from outside the [event horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Event_horizon "Event horizon") of a [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole "Black hole") lit by a thin accretion disc

It was in the local context of gravitational collapse that the contribution of Penrose was most decisive, starting with his 1969 cosmic censorship conjecture, to the effect that any ensuing singularities would be confined within a well-behaved [event horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Event_horizon "Event horizon") surrounding a hidden space-time region for which Wheeler coined the term [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole "Black hole"), leaving a visible exterior region with strong but finite curvature, from which some of the gravitational energy may be extractable by what is known as the [Penrose process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_process "Penrose process"), while accretion of surrounding matter may release further energy that can account for astrophysical phenomena such as [quasars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasars "Quasars").

Following up his "weak [cosmic censorship hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_censorship_hypothesis "Cosmic censorship hypothesis")", Penrose went on, in 1979, to formulate a stronger version called the "strong censorship hypothesis".

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Roger_Penrose_1978.jpg/250px-Roger_Penrose_1978.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Roger_Penrose_1978.jpg)

Penrose in 1978

Together with the [Belinski–Khalatnikov–Lifshitz conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BKL_conjecture "BKL conjecture") and issues of nonlinear stability, settling the censorship conjectures is one of the most important outstanding problems in [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity"). Also from 1979 dates Penrose's influential [Weyl curvature hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_curvature_hypothesis "Weyl curvature hypothesis") on the initial conditions of the observable part of the universe and the origin of the [second law of thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_law_of_thermodynamics "Second law of thermodynamics"). Penrose and James Terrell independently realised that objects travelling near the speed of light will appear to undergo a peculiar skewing or rotation. This effect has come to be called the [Terrell rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrell_rotation "Terrell rotation") or Penrose–Terrell rotation.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Penrose_Tiling_%28Rhombi%29.svg/250px-Penrose_Tiling_%28Rhombi%29.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Penrose_Tiling_(Rhombi).svg)

A [Penrose tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_tiling "Penrose tiling")

In 1967 Penrose invented the [twistor theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twistor_theory "Twistor theory"), which maps geometric objects in [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space "Minkowski space") into the 4-dimensional complex space with the metric signature (2,2).

Penrose is well known for his 1974 discovery of [Penrose tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_tiling "Penrose tiling"), which are formed from two tiles that can only [tile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessellation "Tessellation") the plane nonperiodically, and are the first tilings to exhibit fivefold rotational symmetry. In 1984 such patterns were observed in the arrangement of atoms in [quasicrystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal "Quasicrystal"). Another noteworthy contribution is his 1971 invention of [spin networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_network "Spin network"), which later came to form the geometry of [spacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacetime "Spacetime") in [loop quantum gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loop_quantum_gravity "Loop quantum gravity"). He was influential in popularising what are commonly known as [Penrose diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_diagram "Penrose diagram") (causal diagrams).

In 1983, Penrose was invited to teach at [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University "Rice University") in Houston, by the then provost Bill Gordon. He worked there from 1983 to 1987. His doctoral students have included, among others, [Andrew Hodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Hodges "Andrew Hodges"), [Lane Hughston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lane_P._Hughston "Lane P. In 2004 Penrose released *[The Road to Reality: A Complete Guide to the Laws of the Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Road_to_Reality:_A_Complete_Guide_to_the_Laws_of_the_Universe "The Road to Reality: A Complete Guide to the Laws of the Universe")*, a 1,099-page comprehensive guide to the [Laws of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_law "Physical law") that includes an explanation of his own theory. The [Penrose Interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_interpretation "Penrose interpretation") predicts the relationship between [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") and [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity"), and proposes that a [quantum state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_state "Quantum state") remains in [superposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_superposition "Quantum superposition") until the difference of [space-time curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-time_curvature "Space-time curvature") attains a significant level.

Penrose is the Francis and Helen Pentz Distinguished Visiting professor of Physics and Mathematics at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University "Pennsylvania State University").

### An earlier universe

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/WMAP_2010.png/330px-WMAP_2010.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:WMAP_2010.png)

[WMAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkinson_Microwave_Anisotropy_Probe "Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe") image of the (extremely tiny) anisotropies in the [cosmic background radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_background_radiation "Cosmic background radiation")

In 2010 Penrose reported possible evidence, based on concentric circles found in [Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WMAP "WMAP") data of the [cosmic microwave background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMB "CMB") sky, of an earlier universe existing before the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang "Big Bang") of the present universe. He mentions this evidence in the epilogue of his 2010 book *[Cycles of Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycles_of_Time "Cycles of Time")*, a book in which he presents his reasons, to do with [Einstein's field equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein's_field_equations "Einstein's field equations"), the [Weyl curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_curvature "Weyl curvature") C, and the [Weyl curvature hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_curvature_hypothesis "Weyl curvature hypothesis") (WCH), that the transition at the Big Bang could have been smooth enough for a previous universe to survive it. He made several conjectures about C and the WCH, some of which were subsequently proved by others, and he also popularized his [conformal cyclic cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_cyclic_cosmology "Conformal cyclic cosmology") (CCC) theory. In this theory, Penrose postulates that at the end of the universe all matter is eventually contained within black holes, which subsequently evaporate via [Hawking radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawking_radiation "Hawking radiation"). At this point, everything contained within the universe consists of [photons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photons "Photons"), which "experience" neither time nor space. There is essentially no difference between an infinitely large universe consisting only of photons and an infinitely small universe consisting only of photons. Therefore, a singularity for a Big Bang and an infinitely expanded universe are equivalent.

In simple terms, Penrose believes that the singularity in [Einstein's field equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein's_field_equation "Einstein's field equation") at the Big Bang is only an apparent singularity, similar to the well-known apparent singularity at the [event horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Event_horizon "Event horizon") of a [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole "Black hole"). The latter singularity can be removed by a change of [coordinate system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_system "Coordinate system"), and Penrose proposes a different change of coordinate system that will remove the singularity at the big bang. One implication of this is that the major events at the Big Bang can be understood without unifying general relativity and quantum mechanics, and therefore we are not necessarily constrained by the [Wheeler–DeWitt equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheeler–DeWitt_equation "Wheeler–DeWitt equation"), which disrupts time. Alternatively, one can use the Einstein–Maxwell–Dirac equations.

### Consciousness

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Roger_Penrose_9671.JPG/250px-Roger_Penrose_9671.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Roger_Penrose_9671.JPG)

Penrose at a conference c. 2011

Penrose has written books on the connection between fundamental physics and human (or animal) consciousness. In *[The Emperor's New Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emperor's_New_Mind "The Emperor's New Mind")* (1989), he argues that known laws of physics are inadequate to explain the phenomenon of consciousness. Penrose proposes the characteristics this new physics may have and specifies the requirements for a bridge between classical and quantum mechanics (what he calls *correct quantum gravity*). Penrose uses a variant of [Turing's halting theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing's_halting_theorem "Turing's halting theorem") to demonstrate that a system can be [deterministic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic "Deterministic") without being [algorithmic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithm "Algorithm"). (For example, imagine a system with only two states, ON and OFF. If the system's state is ON when a given [Turing machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_machine "Turing machine") halts and OFF when the Turing machine does not halt, then the system's state is completely determined by the machine; nevertheless, there is no algorithmic way to determine whether the Turing machine stops.)

Penrose believes that such deterministic yet non-algorithmic processes may come into play in the quantum mechanical [wave function reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_function_collapse "Wave function collapse"), and may be harnessed by the brain. He argues that computers today are unable to have intelligence because they are algorithmically deterministic systems. He argues against the viewpoint that the rational processes of the mind are completely algorithmic and can thus be duplicated by a sufficiently complex computer. This contrasts with supporters of [strong artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_theory_of_mind "Computational theory of mind"), who contend that thought can be simulated algorithmically. He bases this on claims that consciousness transcends [formal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic "Formal logic") because factors such as the insolubility of the [halting problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halting_problem "Halting problem") and [Gödel's incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_incompleteness_theorem "Gödel's incompleteness theorem") prevent an algorithmically based system of logic from reproducing such traits of human intelligence as mathematical insight. These claims were originally espoused by the philosopher [John Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lucas_(philosopher) "John Lucas (philosopher)") of [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford "Merton College, Oxford").

The [Penrose–Lucas argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose–Lucas_argument "Penrose–Lucas argument") about the implications of Gödel's incompleteness theorem for computational theories of human intelligence has been criticised by mathematicians, computer scientists and philosophers. Many experts in these fields assert that Penrose's argument fails, though different authors choose different aspects of the argument to attack. [Marvin Minsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Minsky "Marvin Minsky"), a leading proponent of artificial intelligence, was particularly critical, writing that Penrose "tries to show, in chapter after chapter, that human thought cannot be based on any known scientific principle." Minsky's position is exactly the opposite – he believed that humans are, in fact, machines, whose functioning, although complex, is fully explainable by current physics. Minsky maintained that "one can carry that quest [for scientific explanation] too far by only seeking new basic principles instead of attacking the real detail. This is what I see in Penrose's quest for a new basic principle of physics that will account for consciousness."

Penrose responded to criticism of *The Emperor's New Mind* with his follow-up 1994 book *[Shadows of the Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadows_of_the_Mind "Shadows of the Mind")*, and in 1997 with *[The Large, the Small and the Human Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Large,_the_Small_and_the_Human_Mind "The Large, the Small and the Human Mind")*. In those works, he also combined his observations with those of anesthesiologist [Stuart Hameroff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Hameroff "Stuart Hameroff").

Penrose and Hameroff have argued that consciousness is the result of quantum gravity effects in [microtubules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtubule "Microtubule"), which they dubbed [Orch-OR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orch-OR "Orch-OR") (orchestrated objective reduction). [Max Tegmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Tegmark "Max Tegmark"), in a paper in *Physical Review E*, calculated that the time scale of neuron firing and excitations in microtubules is slower than the [decoherence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_decoherence "Quantum decoherence") time by a factor of at least 10 billion. The paper's reception is summed up by this statement in Tegmark's support: "Physicists outside the fray, such as IBM's [John A. Smolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Smolin "John A. Smolin"), say the calculations confirm what they had suspected all along. 'We're not working with a brain that's near absolute zero. It's reasonably unlikely that the brain evolved quantum behavior'". Tegmark's paper has been widely cited by critics of the Penrose–Hameroff position.

Phillip Tetlow, although himself supportive of Penrose's views, acknowledges that Penrose's ideas about the human thought process are a minority view in scientific circles, citing Minsky's criticisms and quoting the science journalist [Charles Seife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Seife "Charles Seife")'s description of Penrose as "one of a handful of scientists" who believe that the nature of consciousness suggests a quantum process.

In January 2014 Hameroff and Penrose ventured that a discovery of quantum vibrations in microtubules by [Anirban Bandyopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anirban_Bandyopadhyay "Anirban Bandyopadhyay") of the National Institute for Materials Science in Japan supports the hypothesis of [Orch-OR theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestrated_objective_reduction "Orchestrated objective reduction"). A reviewed and updated version of the theory was published along with critical commentary and debate in the March 2014 issue of *[Physics of Life Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_of_Life_Reviews "Physics of Life Reviews")*.

He appeared on *[In Our Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Our_Time_(radio_series) "In Our Time (radio series)")* discussing consciousness with [Ted Honderich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Honderich "Ted Honderich").

In 2020 Penrose was awarded one half of the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") by the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences") for the discovery that black hole formation is a robust prediction of the general theory of relativity, a half-share also going to [Reinhard Genzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Genzel "Reinhard Genzel") and [Andrea Ghez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Ghez "Andrea Ghez") for the discovery of a [supermassive compact object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Center#Supermassive_black_hole "Galactic Center") at the [centre of our galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_of_our_galaxy "Centre of our galaxy"). In the same year, he was also awarded the honorary degree of [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science "Doctor of Science") (DSc) by the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge").

In 2025 Penrose received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").

## Personal life

Penrose's first marriage was to Joan Isabel Penrose (born Wedge), an American he married in 1959. They had three sons. Penrose is now married to Vanessa Thomas, director of Academic Development at [Cokethorpe School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cokethorpe_School "Cokethorpe School") in [Witney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witney "Witney"), Oxfordshire, and former head of mathematics at [Abingdon School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abingdon_School "Abingdon School"). They have one son. Roger Penrose is a devotee of [Johann Sebastian Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Sebastian_Bach "Johann Sebastian Bach"). On *[Desert Island Discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Island_Discs "Desert Island Discs")*, he said he was tempted to choose an all Bach playlist. His first choice was the [Mass in B minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_in_B_minor "Mass in B minor").

### Religious views

During an interview with [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4 "BBC Radio 4") on 25 September 2010, Penrose stated, "I'm not a believer myself. I don't believe in established religions of any kind." He regards himself as an [agnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnostic "Agnostic"). In the 1991 film *[A Brief History of Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Brief_History_of_Time_(film) "A Brief History of Time (film)")*, he also said, "I think I would say that the universe has a purpose, it's not somehow just there by chance ... some people, I think, take the view that the universe is just there and it runs along—it's a bit like it just sort of computes, and we happen somehow by accident to find ourselves in this thing. But I don't think that's a very fruitful or helpful way of looking at the universe, I think that there is something much deeper about it."

Penrose is a patron of [Humanists UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanists_UK "Humanists UK").
