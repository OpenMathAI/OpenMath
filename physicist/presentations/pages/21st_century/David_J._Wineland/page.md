**David Jeffery Wineland** (born February 24, 1944) is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans "Americans") physicist at the Physical Measurement Laboratory of the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology") (NIST). His most notable contributions include the [laser cooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_cooling "Laser cooling") of trapped ions and the use of ions for [quantum-computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computing "Quantum computing") operations. He received the 2012 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), jointly with [Serge Haroche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serge_Haroche "Serge Haroche"), for "ground-breaking experimental methods that enable measuring and manipulation of individual quantum systems."

## Early life and career

Wineland was born in Wauwatosa, Wisconsin. He lived in Denver until he was three years old, at which time his family moved to [Sacramento, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento,_California "Sacramento, California"). Wineland graduated from [Encina High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encina_High_School "Encina High School") in Sacramento in 1961. In Sept. 1961–Dec. 1963, he studied at [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis "University of California, Davis"). He received his bachelor's degree in physics from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1965 and his master's and doctoral degrees in physics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). He completed his PhD in 1970, supervised by [Norman Foster Ramsey, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Foster_Ramsey,_Jr. "Norman Foster Ramsey, Jr.") His doctoral dissertation is titled "The Atomic Deuterium Maser". He then performed postdoctoral research in [Hans Dehmelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Dehmelt "Hans Dehmelt")'s group at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington "University of Washington") where he investigated electrons in [ion traps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_trap "Ion trap"). In 1975, he joined the National Bureau of Standards (now called [NIST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIST "NIST")), where he started the ion storage group and is on the physics faculty of the [University of Colorado at Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_at_Boulder "University of Colorado at Boulder"). In January 2018, Wineland moved to the Department of Physics [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon "University of Oregon") as a [Knight Research Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_H._Knight_Chairs_and_Professorships "Philip H. Knight Chairs and Professorships"), while still being engaged with the Ion Storage Group at NIST in a consulting role.

Wineland was the first to laser-cool ions in 1978. His NIST group uses trapped ions in many experiments on fundamental physics, and quantum state control. They have demonstrated optical techniques to prepare ground, superposition and entangled states. This work has led to advances in spectroscopy, [atomic clocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_clock "Atomic clock") and quantum information. In 1995 he created the first single atom quantum logic gate and was the first to quantum teleport information in massive particles in 2004. Wineland implemented the most precise atomic clock using quantum logic on a single aluminum ion in 2005.

Wineland is a fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") and the Optical Society of America, and was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") in 1992. He shared the 2012 Nobel Prize in Physics with [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") physicist [Serge Haroche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serge_Haroche "Serge Haroche") "for ground-breaking experimental methods that enable measuring and manipulation of individual quantum systems."

## Family

Wineland is married to Sedna Quimby-Wineland, and they have two sons.

Sedna Helen Quimby is the daughter of George I. Quimby (1913-2003), an archaeologist and [anthropologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropologist "Anthropologist"), who was Professor of Anthropology at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington "University of Washington") and Director of the Thomas Burke Memorial Washington State Museum, and his wife Helen Ziehm Quimby.
