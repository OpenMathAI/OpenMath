**Arthur Ashkin** (September 2, 1922 – September 21, 2020) was an American scientist and [Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") laureate who worked at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs "Bell Labs"). Ashkin has been considered by many as the father of [optical tweezers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_tweezers "Optical tweezers"),
for which he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics 2018 at age 96, becoming the oldest Nobel laureate until 2019 when [John B. Goodenough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_B._Goodenough "John B. Goodenough") was awarded at 97. He resided in [Rumson, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumson,_New_Jersey "Rumson, New Jersey").

Ashkin started his work on manipulation of microparticles with laser light in the late 1960s which resulted in the invention of [optical tweezers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_tweezers "Optical tweezers") in 1986. He also pioneered the optical trapping process that eventually was used to manipulate atoms, molecules, and biological cells. The key phenomenon is the [radiation pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_pressure "Radiation pressure") of light; this pressure can be dissected down into optical gradient and scattering forces.

## Early life and family

Arthur Ashkin was born in Brooklyn, New York, in 1922, to a family of [Ukrainian-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Ukraine "History of the Jews in Ukraine") background. His parents were Isadore and Anna Ashkin. He had two siblings, a brother, [Julius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Ashkin "Julius Ashkin"), also a physicist, and a sister, Ruth. One older sibling, Gertrude, died while young. The family home was in Brooklyn, New York, at 983 E 27 Street. Isadore (né Aschkinase) had emigrated to the United States from [Odesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa "Odesa") (then [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire"), now [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine")), at the age of 18. Anna, five years younger, also came from today's Ukraine, then [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Galicia_(Eastern_Europe) "History of the Jews in Galicia (Eastern Europe)"), [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire "Austro-Hungarian Empire"). Within a decade of his landing in New York, Isadore had become a U.S. citizen and was running a dental laboratory at 139 Delancey Street in Manhattan.

Ashkin met his wife, Aline, at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"), and they were married for over 60 years with three children and five grandchildren. She was a chemistry teacher at [Holmdel High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmdel_High_School "Holmdel High School"), and their son [Michael Ashkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ashkin "Michael Ashkin"), is an art professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University").

### Education

Ashkin graduated from Brooklyn's [James Madison High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison_High_School_(Brooklyn) "James Madison High School (Brooklyn)") in 1940. He then attended [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") and was also a technician for MIT's [Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Lab "MIT Radiation Lab") tasked with building [magnetrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetron "Magnetron") for U.S. military radar systems. He joined the U.S. Army reserves on July 31, 1945. He continued working in the Columbia University lab. During this period by Ashkin's own account, three [Nobel laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate "Nobel laureate") were in attendance.

Ashkin finished his course work and obtained his [BS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree in physics at Columbia University in 1947. He then attended [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"), where he studied [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics "Nuclear physics"). This was during the era of the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project"), and Ashkin's brother, [Julius Ashkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Ashkin "Julius Ashkin"), was successfully part of it. This led to Arthur Ashkin's introduction to [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe "Hans Bethe"), [Richard Feynman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Feynman "Richard Feynman") and others who were at Cornell at the time.

He received his PhD degree at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") in 1952, and then went to work for [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs "Bell Labs") at the request and recommendation of Sidney Millman, who was Ashkin's supervisor at Columbia University.

## Career

At Bell Labs, Ashkin worked in the [microwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave "Microwave") field until about 1960 to 1961, and then switched to laser research. His research and published articles at that time pertained to [nonlinear optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_optics "Nonlinear optics"), [optical fibers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_fibers "Optical fibers"), [parametric oscillators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_oscillator "Parametric oscillator") and [parametric amplifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_amplifier "Parametric amplifier"). Also, at Bell Labs during the 1960s, he was the co-discoverer of the [photorefractive effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photorefractive_effect "Photorefractive effect") in the [piezoelectric crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezoelectric_crystal "Piezoelectric crystal").

Within various professional society memberships, Ashkin attained the rating of fellow in the [Optical Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_Society_of_America "Optical Society of America") (OSA), the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") (APS), and the [Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers "Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers") (IEEE). Ashkin received the [Charles Hard Townes Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hard_Townes_Award "Charles Hard Townes Award") in 1988 and the [Frederic Ives Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_Ives_Medal "Frederic Ives Medal") in 1998, both from The Optical Society. He was later named an Honorary Member of the organization. He retired from Bell Labs in 1992 after a 40-year career during which he contributed to many areas of [experimental physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics"). He authored many [research papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_paper "Academic paper") over the years and held 47 patents. He was recipient of the [Joseph F. Keithley Award For Advances in Measurement Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_F._Keithley_Award "Joseph F. Keithley Award") in 2003 and the [Harvey Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Prize "Harvey Prize") in 2004. He was elected to the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Engineering "United States National Academy of Engineering") in 1984 and to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") in 1996. He was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2013. He continued to work in his home lab.

Besides optical tweezers, Ashkin is also known for his studies in [photorefraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photorefraction "Photorefraction"), [second harmonic generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_harmonic_generation "Second harmonic generation"), and
non-linear optics in fibers.

Recent advances in physics and biology using optical micromanipulation include achievement of [Bose–Einstein condensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose–Einstein_condensation "Bose–Einstein condensation") in atomic vapors at submillikelvin temperatures, demonstration of atom lasers, and detailed measurements on individual motor molecules.

Ashkin's work formed the basis for [Steven Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Chu "Steven Chu")'s work on cooling and trapping atoms, which earned Chu the 1997 Nobel Prize in physics.

### Nobel Prize

On October 2, 2018, Arthur Ashkin was awarded a Nobel Prize in Physics for his work on optical trapping. Ashkin "was honoured for his invention of 'optical tweezers' that grab particles, atoms, viruses and other living cells with their laser beam fingers. With this he was able to use the radiation pressure of light to move physical objects, 'an old dream of science fiction', the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences said." He was awarded half of the Prize while the other half was shared between [Gérard Mourou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gérard_Mourou "Gérard Mourou") and [Donna Strickland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Strickland "Donna Strickland") for their work on [chirped-pulse amplification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirped-pulse_amplification "Chirped-pulse amplification"), a technique "now used in laser machining [that] enables doctors to perform millions of corrective laser eye surgeries every year".

At 96, Ashkin was the oldest Nobel Prize laureate to be awarded the prize, until [John B. Goodenough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_B._Goodenough "John B. Goodenough") received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 2019 at the age of 97. He died on September 21, 2020, at the age of 98.
