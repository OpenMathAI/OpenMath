**Adam Guy Riess** (born December 16, 1969) is an American [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics "Astrophysics") and [Bloomberg Distinguished Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Distinguished_Professorships "Bloomberg Distinguished Professorships") at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University") and the [Space Telescope Science Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Telescope_Science_Institute "Space Telescope Science Institute"). He is known for his research in using [supernovae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernovae "Supernovae") as cosmological probes. Riess shared both the 2006 [Shaw Prize in Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize") and the [2011 Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "2011 Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Saul Perlmutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Perlmutter "Saul Perlmutter") and [Brian Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Schmidt "Brian Schmidt") for providing evidence that the [expansion of the universe is accelerating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_universe "Accelerating universe").

Riess has been at the center of a growing scientific debate about the so-called “Hubble tension” — a discrepancy between measurements of the universe’s expansion rate using nearby supernovae, and measurements inferred from the cosmic microwave background radiation using the [Standard Model of cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_cosmology#Standard_model_of_Big_Bang_cosmology "Physical cosmology"). Riess’s data has prompted questions and further testing to determine if the Standard Model still adequately describes the universe.

## Family

Riess was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), and grew up in [Warren Township, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Township,_New_Jersey "Warren Township, New Jersey"). He has two sisters: [Gail Saltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gail_Saltz "Gail Saltz"), a psychiatrist, and Holly Hagerman, an artist.

His father, Michael Riess (1931–2007), served in the [U.S. Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Navy "U.S. Navy") and later owned a frozen-foods distribution company; his mother, Doris Riess, worked as a clinical psychologist. His paternal grandfather was the journalist and war correspondent [Curt Martin Riess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curt_Martin_Riess "Curt Martin Riess").

Riess married Nancy Joy Schondorf in 1998. They have two children, Noah and Gabrielle.

## Education

He attended [Watchung Hills Regional High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchung_Hills_Regional_High_School "Watchung Hills Regional High School"), graduating in the class of 1988. He also attended the prestigious New Jersey Governor's School in the Sciences in 1987. Riess then graduated Phi Beta Kappa from [The Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "The Massachusetts Institute of Technology") in 1992 where he was a member of the [Phi Delta Theta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Delta_Theta "Phi Delta Theta") fraternity. He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD#United_States "PhD") from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") in 1996; it resulted in measurements of over twenty new [Type Ia supernovae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_Ia_supernovae "Type Ia supernovae") and a method to utilize Type Ia supernovae as accurate distance indicators by correcting for intervening dust and intrinsic inhomogeneities. Riess's PhD thesis was supervised by [Robert Kirshner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kirshner "Robert Kirshner") and [William H. Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H._Press "William H. Press") and won the [Robert J. Trumpler Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_J._Trumpler_Award "Robert J. Trumpler Award") in 1999 for PhD theses of unusual importance to astronomy.

## Research

Riess was a [Miller Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Fellow "Miller Fellow") at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), from 1996 through 1999, during which period his first seminal paper on the discovery of an accelerating universe was published. In 1999, he moved to the [Space Telescope Science Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Telescope_Science_Institute "Space Telescope Science Institute") and took up a position at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University") in 2006. He also sits on the selection committee for the Astronomy award given under the auspices of the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize"). In July 2016, Riess was named a [Bloomberg Distinguished Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Distinguished_Professorships "Bloomberg Distinguished Professorships") at Johns Hopkins University for his accomplishments as an interdisciplinary researcher and excellence in teaching the next generation of scholars. The Bloomberg Distinguished Professorships were established in 2013 by a gift from [Michael Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg "Michael Bloomberg").

Riess jointly led the study with [Brian Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Schmidt "Brian Schmidt") in 1998 for the [High-z Supernova Search Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-z_Supernova_Search_Team "High-z Supernova Search Team") which first reported evidence that the universe's expansion rate is accelerating through monitoring of Type Ia supernovae. The team's observations were contrary to the existing theory that the expansion of the universe was slowing down; instead, by monitoring the color shifts in the light from supernovae from Earth, they discovered that these billion-year old novae were still accelerating. This result was also found nearly simultaneously by the [Supernova Cosmology Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernova_Cosmology_Project "Supernova Cosmology Project"), led by [Saul Perlmutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Perlmutter "Saul Perlmutter"). The corroborating evidence between the two competing studies led to the acceptance of the [accelerating universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_universe "Accelerating universe") theory, and initiated new research to understand the nature of the universe, such as the existence of [dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_energy "Dark energy"). The discovery of the accelerating universe was named 'Breakthrough of the Year' by *Science* magazine in 1998, and Riess was jointly awarded the [2011 Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "2011 Nobel Prize in Physics") along with Schmidt and Perlmutter for their groundbreaking work.

From 2002 to 2007 Riess led the Higher-Z SN Team which used the Hubble Space Telescope to find dozens of type Ia supernovae at z>1, first demonstrating that the expansion of the Universe was decelerating before it began accelerating and ruling out astrophysical contamination of SN Ia.

Riess is also known for his efforts to measure the local value of the Hubble constant while leading the SH0ES Team since 2005 with measurements that approach 1% precision and which indicate a discrepancy with the model-based prediction from the CMB, a problem widely known in cosmology as the [Hubble tension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble's_law "Hubble's law").

## Awards and honors

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Shaw2006astro.jpg/250px-Shaw2006astro.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Shaw2006astro.jpg)

[Saul Perlmutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Perlmutter "Saul Perlmutter"), Riess, and [Brian P. Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_P._Schmidt "Brian P. Schmidt") being awarded the 2006 [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize") in Astronomy. The trio would later be awarded the [2011 Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "2011 Nobel Prize in Physics").

Riess received the [Astronomical Society of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_Society_of_the_Pacific "Astronomical Society of the Pacific")'s [Robert J. Trumpler Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_J._Trumpler_Award "Robert J. Trumpler Award") in 1999 and [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University")'s Bok Prize in 2001. He won the American Astronomical Society's [Helen B. Warner Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_B._Warner_Prize "Helen B. Warner Prize") in 2003 and the Raymond and Beverly [Sackler Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sackler_Prize "Sackler Prize") in Physics in 2004 for the discovery of cosmic acceleration.

In 2006, he shared the $1 million [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize") in [Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy") with [Saul Perlmutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Perlmutter "Saul Perlmutter") and [Brian P. Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_P._Schmidt "Brian P. Schmidt") for contributions to the discovery of the [acceleration of the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_universe "Accelerating universe").

Schmidt and all the members of the High-Z Team (as defined by the co-authors of Riess et al. 1998) shared the 2007 [Gruber Cosmology Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruber_Prize_in_Cosmology "Gruber Prize in Cosmology"), a $500,000 award, with the Supernova Cosmology Project (the set defined by the co-authors of Perlmutter et al. 1999) for their discovery of the accelerating expansion of the universe. Riess was the winner of MacArthur "Genius" Grant in 2008. He was also elected in 2009 to the National Academy of Sciences.

Along with Perlmutter and Schmidt, he was awarded the 2011 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for his contributions to the discovery of the acceleration of the expansion of the universe.

Riess, along with [Brian P. Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_P._Schmidt "Brian P. Schmidt"), and the [High-Z Supernova Search Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-Z_Supernova_Search_Team "High-Z Supernova Search Team") shared in the 2015 [Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_Physics_Prize "Fundamental Physics Prize").

In 2012, Riess received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").

In 2020, Riess was made fellow of the [American Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Astronomical_Society "American Astronomical Society").

## Publications

Riess has more than 137,000 citations in Google Scholar and an h-index of 130. His most cited work, "Observational evidence from supernovae for an accelerating universe and a cosmological constant," has been cited over 28,000 times. Riess has been among the top 1% most cited in the world for subject field and year of publication in the [Thomson Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_Reuters "Thomson Reuters") Highly Cited Researchers reports for multiple years, including 2014-2016 and 2020-2023.
