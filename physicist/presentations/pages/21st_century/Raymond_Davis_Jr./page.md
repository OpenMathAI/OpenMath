**Raymond Davis Jr.** (October 14, 1914 – May 31, 2006) was an American [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). He is best known as the leader of the [Homestake experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestake_experiment "Homestake experiment") in the 1960s-1980s, which was the first experiment to detect [neutrinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrinos "Neutrinos") emitted from the Sun; for this he shared the 2002
[Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics").

## Early life and education

Davis was born in Washington, D.C., where his father was a photographer for the [National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Standards "National Bureau of Standards"). He spent several years as a choirboy to please his mother, although he could not carry a tune. He enjoyed attending the concerts at the Watergate before air traffic was loud enough to drown out the music. His brother Warren, 14 months younger than he, was his constant companion in boyhood. He received his B.S. from the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park "University of Maryland, College Park") in 1938 in chemistry, which is part of the [University of Maryland College of Computer, Mathematical, and Natural Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland_College_of_Computer,_Mathematical,_and_Natural_Sciences "University of Maryland College of Computer, Mathematical, and Natural Sciences"). He also received a master's degree from that school and a Ph.D. from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") in [physical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry") in 1942.

## Career

After entering the [Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army") as a [reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Reserve "United States Army Reserve") officer in 1942, Davis spent most of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") at [Dugway Proving Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugway_Proving_Ground "Dugway Proving Ground"), [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah "Utah") observing the results of chemical weapons tests and exploring the [Great Salt Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Salt_Lake "Great Salt Lake") basin for evidence of its predecessor, [Lake Bonneville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Bonneville "Lake Bonneville").

After his discharge from the Army in 1945, Davis went to work at [Monsanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsanto "Monsanto")'s Mound Laboratory, in [Miamisburg, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miamisburg,_Ohio "Miamisburg, Ohio"), doing applied [radiochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiochemistry "Radiochemistry") of interest to the [United States Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Atomic_Energy_Commission "United States Atomic Energy Commission"). In 1948, he joined [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory"), which was attempting to find peaceful uses for [nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power "Nuclear power").

Davis reports that he was asked "to find something interesting to work on," and dedicated his career to the study of [neutrinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrinos "Neutrinos"), particles which had been predicted to explain the process of [beta decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_decay "Beta decay"), but whose separate existence had not been confirmed. Davis investigated the detection of neutrinos by beta decay, the process by which a neutrino brings enough energy to a nucleus to make certain stable isotopes into radioactive ones. Since the rate for this process is very low, the number of radioactive atoms created in neutrino experiments is very small, and Davis began investigating the rates of processes other than beta decay that would mimic the signal of neutrinos.

Using barrels and tanks of [carbon tetrachloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_tetrachloride "Carbon tetrachloride") as detectors, Davis characterized the rate of the production of [argon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argon "Argon")-37 as a function of altitude and as a function of depth underground. He deployed a detector containing chlorine atoms at the Brookhaven Reactor in 1954 and later one of the reactors at Savannah River. These experiments failed to detect a surplus of radioactive argon when the reactors were operating over when the reactors were shut down, and this was taken as the first experimental evidence that neutrinos causing the chlorine reaction, and antineutrinos produced in reactors, were distinct. Detecting neutrinos proved considerably more difficult than not detecting antineutrinos. Davis was the lead scientist behind the [Homestake Experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestake_Experiment "Homestake Experiment"), the large-scale radiochemical neutrino detector which first detected evidence of neutrinos from the sun.

Davis retired from Brookhaven in 1984.

Davis shared the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 2002 with Japanese physicist [Masatoshi Koshiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masatoshi_Koshiba "Masatoshi Koshiba") and Italian [Riccardo Giacconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riccardo_Giacconi "Riccardo Giacconi") for pioneering contributions to [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics "Astrophysics"), Davis was recognized for his work on the detection of cosmic [neutrinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino "Neutrino"), looking at the [solar neutrino problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_neutrino_problem "Solar neutrino problem") in the [Homestake Experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestake_Experiment "Homestake Experiment"). He was 88 years old when awarded the prize.

## Personal life

Davis met his wife Anna Torrey at Brookhaven and together they built a 21-foot wooden sailboat, the *Halcyon*. They had five children and lived in the same house in Blue Point, New York for over 50 years. On May 31, 2006, he died in [Blue Point, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Point,_New_York "Blue Point, New York"), from complications of [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease "Alzheimer's disease").
