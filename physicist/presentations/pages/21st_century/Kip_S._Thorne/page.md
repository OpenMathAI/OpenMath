**Kip Stephen Thorne** (born June 1, 1940) is an American astrophysicist and author. He shared the 2017 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Rainer Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainer_Weiss "Rainer Weiss") and [Barry C. Barish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_C._Barish "Barry C. Barish") "for decisive contributions to the [LIGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIGO "LIGO") detector and the observation of [gravitational waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_wave "Gravitational wave")".

With [John A. Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Wheeler "John A. Wheeler") and [Charles Misner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Misner "Charles Misner"), he coauthored the [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity") textbook *[Gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation_(book) "Gravitation (book)")* in 1973. He has also written popular science, notably *[Black Holes and Time Warps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Holes_and_Time_Warps "Black Holes and Time Warps")* in 1994 which was awarded the [Phi Beta Kappa Award in Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa_Award_in_Science "Phi Beta Kappa Award in Science") for that year.

Thorne was the [Richard P. Feynman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_P._Feynman "Richard P. Feynman") Professor of Theoretical Physics at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") from 1991 until 2009. A longtime friend and colleague of [Stephen Hawking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking "Stephen Hawking") and [Carl Sagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sagan "Carl Sagan"), he advised Sagan on the physics of [wormholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wormholes "Wormholes") for his novel [*Contact*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_(novel) "Contact (novel)"). He was a scientific consultant for [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan")'s films *[Interstellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstellar_(film) "Interstellar (film)")* and *[Tenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenet "Tenet")*. He received the 2018 [Lewis Thomas Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Thomas_Prize "Lewis Thomas Prize") for science writing.

## Life and career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/%C3%89cole_de_Physique_des_Houches_%28Les_Houches_Physics_School%29_main_lecture_hall_1972.jpg/330px-%C3%89cole_de_Physique_des_Houches_%28Les_Houches_Physics_School%29_main_lecture_hall_1972.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:École_de_Physique_des_Houches_(Les_Houches_Physics_School)_main_lecture_hall_1972.jpg)

Discussion in the main lecture hall at the [École de Physique des Houches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_de_Physique_des_Houches "École de Physique des Houches") (Les Houches Physics School), 1972. From left, [Yuval Ne'eman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuval_Ne'eman "Yuval Ne'eman"), [Bryce DeWitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryce_DeWitt "Bryce DeWitt"), Thorne, [Demetrios Christodoulou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrios_Christodoulou "Demetrios Christodoulou").

Thorne was born on June 1, 1940, in [Logan, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan,_Utah "Logan, Utah"). His father, D. Wynne Thorne (1908–1979), was a professor of [soil chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_chemistry "Soil chemistry") at [Utah State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_State_University "Utah State University"), and his mother, Alison (née Comish; 1914–2004), was an economist and the first woman to receive a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in economics from [Iowa State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University "Iowa State University"). Raised in an academic environment, two of his four siblings also became professors. Thorne's parents were members of [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints "The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints") (LDS Church) and raised Thorne in the LDS faith, though he now identifies as an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist"). Shortly before his mother's death, she urged Kip and his siblings to leave the LDS Church because of its discrimination against women, which they all did. Regarding his views on science and religion, Thorne has stated: "There are large numbers of my finest colleagues who are quite devout and believe in God .... There is no fundamental incompatibility between science and religion. I happen to not believe in God."

Thorne excelled at academics early in life, winning recognition in the [Westinghouse Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regeneron_Science_Talent_Search "Regeneron Science Talent Search") as a senior at [Logan High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_High_School_(Utah) "Logan High School (Utah)"). He received his [BS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in physics degree from [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech "Caltech") in 1962, and his master and PhD in physics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in 1964 and 1965 under the supervision of [John Archibald Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Archibald_Wheeler "John Archibald Wheeler") with a [doctoral dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_dissertation "Doctoral dissertation") on *[Geometrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrodynamics "Geometrodynamics") of Cylindrical Systems*.

He returned to Caltech as an associate professor in 1967 and became a professor of theoretical physics in 1970, becoming one of the youngest full professors in the history of Caltech at age 30. He became the William R. Kenan Jr. Professor in 1981, and the [Feynman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Feynman "Richard Feynman") Professor of Theoretical Physics in 1991. He was an adjunct professor at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah "University of Utah") from 1971 to 1998 and Andrew D. White Professor at Large at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") from 1986 to 1992. Since 2009, Thorne has been the Feynman Professor of Theoretical Physics, emeritus.

Thorne and Linda Jean Peterson married in 1960. Their children are Kares Anne and Bret Carter, an architect. Thorne and Peterson divorced in 1977. Thorne was set up on a [blind date](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_date "Blind date") with [Lynda Obst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynda_Obst "Lynda Obst"), later a [film producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_producer "Film producer"), by physicist [Carl Sagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sagan "Carl Sagan"). They dated in 1979–1980, and parted and remained friends, to the extent that they later collaborated on the movie *[Interstellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstellar_(film) "Interstellar (film)")*. Thorne and his second wife, Carolee Joyce Winstein, a professor of [biokinesiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinesiology "Kinesiology") and physical therapy at [USC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California "University of Southern California"), married in 1984. In 2015, he collaborated with Obst and Stephen Hawking on a movie script, for which they wrote several drafts.

Throughout the years, Thorne has served as a mentor and thesis advisor to many students who became leaders in observational, experimental, or astrophysical aspects of general relativity. Approximately 50 physicists have received PhDs at Caltech under Thorne's personal mentorship.

Thorne is known for his ability to convey the excitement and significance of discoveries in gravitation and astrophysics to both professional and lay audiences. His presentations on subjects such as [black holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole "Black hole"), [gravitational radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_radiation "Gravitational radiation"), [relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_relativity "Theory of relativity"), [time travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_travel "Time travel"), and [wormholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wormhole "Wormhole") have been included in [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS "PBS") shows in the U.S. and on the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") in the United Kingdom.

In 2017, Thorne gave the [George Gamow Memorial Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow_Memorial_Lectures "George Gamow Memorial Lectures") at the University of Colorado Boulder, on “Probing the Warped Side of the Universe with Gravitational Waves: From the Big Bang to Black Holes.”

## Research

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Kip_S._Thorne%2C_1972.jpg/250px-Kip_S._Thorne%2C_1972.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kip_S._Thorne,_1972.jpg)

Thorne in 1972

Thorne's research has principally focused on [relativistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity") [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics#Theoretical_astrophysics "Astrophysics") and [gravitation physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation "Gravitation"), with emphasis on [relativistic stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativistic_star "Relativistic star"), black holes and especially [gravitational waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_waves "Gravitational waves"). He is perhaps best known to the public for his controversial theory that wormholes can conceivably be used for time travel. However, Thorne's scientific contributions, which center on the general nature of [space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space "Space"), time, and [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity "Gravity"), span the full range of topics in general relativity.

### Gravitational waves and LIGO

Thorne's work has dealt with the prediction of gravitational wave strengths and their temporal signatures as observed on Earth. These "signatures" are of great relevance to [LIGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIGO "LIGO") (Laser Interferometer Gravitational Wave Observatory), a multi-institution gravitational wave experiment for which Thorne has been a leading proponent – in 1984, he cofounded the LIGO Project (the largest project ever funded by the [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation")) to discern and measure any fluctuations between two or more 'static' points; such fluctuations would be evidence of gravitational waves, as calculations describe. A significant aspect of his research is developing the [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") necessary to analyze these objects. Thorne also carries out [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering "Engineering") design analyses for features of the LIGO that cannot be developed on the basis of [experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experiment "Experiment") and he gives advice on [data analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_analysis "Data analysis") [algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithm "Algorithm") by which the waves will be sought. He has provided theoretical support for LIGO, including identifying gravitational wave sources that LIGO should target, designing the baffles to control [scattered light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering "Scattering") in the LIGO beam tubes, and – in collaboration with [Vladimir Braginsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Braginsky "Vladimir Braginsky")'s (Moscow, Russia) research group – inventing [quantum nondemolition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Nondemolition_measurement "Quantum Nondemolition measurement") designs for advanced gravity-wave detectors and ways to reduce the most serious kind of [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise "Noise") in advanced detectors: [thermoelastic noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson–Nyquist_noise "Johnson–Nyquist noise"). With [Carlton M. Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton_M._Caves "Carlton M. Caves"), Thorne invented the back-action-evasion approach to quantum nondemolition measurements of the [harmonic oscillators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_oscillator "Harmonic oscillator") – a technique applicable both in gravitational wave detection and [quantum optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_optics "Quantum optics").

On February 11, 2016, a team of four physicists representing the [LIGO Scientific Collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIGO_Scientific_Collaboration "LIGO Scientific Collaboration"), announced that in September 2015, LIGO recorded the signature of two black holes colliding 1.3 billion light-years away. This recorded detection was the first direct observation of the [fleeting chirp of a gravitational wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_observation_of_gravitational_waves "First observation of gravitational waves") and confirmed a prediction of the general theory of relativity.

### Black hole cosmology

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Cylindrical_Bundle_of_Magnetic_Field_Lines.svg/250px-Cylindrical_Bundle_of_Magnetic_Field_Lines.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cylindrical_Bundle_of_Magnetic_Field_Lines.svg)

A cylindrical bundle of magnetic field lines

In 1963–1964, Thorne's PhD mentor at Princeton, [John Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Archibald_Wheeler "John Archibald Wheeler"), assigned him a problem to think about: find out whether or not a cylindrical bundle of repulsive magnetic field lines will implode under its own attractive gravitational force. After several months wrestling with the problem, he proved that it was impossible for cylindrical [magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field "Magnetic field") lines to [implode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implosion_(mechanical_process) "Implosion (mechanical process)").

Thorne determined that the gravitational force can overcome all interior pressure only when an object has been compressed in all directions, as in the case of a spherical star imploding under gravitational force. To express this realization, he proposed his [hoop conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoop_conjecture "Hoop conjecture"), which describes an imploding star turning into a black hole when the critical circumference of the designed hoop can be placed around it and set into rotation. Thorne and his students have developed an approach to the theory of black holes called the "[membrane paradigm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_paradigm "Membrane paradigm")", and used it to clarify the [Blandford–Znajek mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blandford–Znajek_mechanism "Blandford–Znajek mechanism") by which black holes may power some [quasars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasar "Quasar") and [active galactic nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_galactic_nuclei "Active galactic nuclei").

Thorne has investigated the [quantum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum "Quantum") [statistical mechanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics") origin of the [entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy "Entropy") of a black hole. With his [postdoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher "Postdoctoral researcher") Wojciech Zurek, he showed that the entropy of a black hole is the [logarithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithm "Logarithm") of the number of ways that the hole could have been made.

With [Igor Novikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Dmitriyevich_Novikov "Igor Dmitriyevich Novikov") and [Don Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Page_(physicist) "Don Page (physicist)"), he developed the general relativistic theory of thin [accretion disks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accretion_disk "Accretion disk") around black holes, and using this theory he deduced that with a doubling of its mass by such [accretion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accretion_(astrophysics) "Accretion (astrophysics)") a black hole will be spun up to 0.998 of the maximum [spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(physics) "Spin (physics)") allowed by general relativity, but not any farther. This is probably the maximum black-hole spin allowed in nature.

### Wormholes and time travel

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Wormhole-demo.png/250px-Wormhole-demo.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wormhole-demo.png)

A wormhole is a short cut connecting two separate regions in space. In the figure the green line shows the short way through wormhole, and the red line shows the long way through normal space.

Thorne and his co-workers at Caltech conducted scientific research on whether the laws of physics permit space and time to be [multiply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected_space "Simply connected space") (can there exist classical, [traversable wormholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wormhole#Traversable_wormholes "Wormhole") and "time machines"?). With Sung-Won Kim, Thorne identified a universal physical mechanism (the explosive growth of [vacuum polarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_polarization "Vacuum polarization") of [quantum fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_(physics)#Quantum_fields "Field (physics)")), that may always prevent spacetime from developing [closed timelike curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_timelike_curve "Closed timelike curve") (i.e., prevent [backward time travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_travel#Time_travel_in_physics "Time travel")).

With [Mike Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Morris_(physicist) "Mike Morris (physicist)") and [Ulvi Yurtsever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulvi_Yurtsever?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ulvi Yurtsever"), he showed that traversable wormholes can exist in the structure of [spacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacetime "Spacetime") only if they are threaded by quantum fields in [quantum states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_state "Quantum state") that violate the [averaged null energy condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_condition#Mathematical_statement "Energy condition") (i.e. have negative renormalized energy spread over a sufficiently large region). This has triggered research to explore the ability of quantum fields to possess such extended [negative energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_energy "Negative energy"). Recent calculations by Thorne indicate that simple masses passing through traversable wormholes could never engender [paradoxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradox "Paradox") – there are *no* initial conditions that lead to paradox once time travel is introduced. If his results can be generalized, they would suggest that none of the supposed paradoxes formulated in time travel stories can actually be formulated at a precise physical level: that is, that *any* situation in a time travel story turns out to permit *many* consistent solutions.

### Relativistic stars, multipole moments and other endeavors

With [Anna Żytkow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_N._Żytkow "Anna N. Żytkow"), Thorne predicted the existence of [red supergiant stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_giant "Red giant") with [neutron-star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_star "Neutron star") cores ([Thorne–Żytkow objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorne–Żytkow_object "Thorne–Żytkow object")). He laid the foundations for the theory of [pulsations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_frequency "Angular frequency") of relativistic stars and the gravitational radiation they emit. With [James Hartle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hartle "James Hartle"), Thorne derived from general relativity the laws of motion and precession of black holes and other relativistic bodies, including the influence of the coupling of their [multipole moments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multipole_moments "Multipole moments") to the spacetime curvature of nearby objects, as well as writing down the [Hartle-Thorne metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartle-Thorne_metric "Hartle-Thorne metric"), an approximate solution which describes the exterior of a slowly and rigidly rotating, stationary and axially symmetric body.

Thorne has also theoretically predicted the existence of universally antigravitating "[exotic matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exotic_matter "Exotic matter")" – the element needed to accelerate the expansion rate of the universe, keep traversable wormhole "Star Gates" open and keep [timelike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space "Minkowski space") [geodesic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic "Geodesic") free float "[warp drives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcubierre_drive "Alcubierre drive")" working. With Clifford Will and others of his students, he laid the foundations for the theoretical interpretation of experimental [tests of relativistic theories of gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tests_of_general_relativity "Tests of general relativity") – foundations on which Will and others then built. As of 2005, Thorne was interested in the origin of classical space and time from the [quantum foam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_foam "Quantum foam") of [quantum gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_gravity "Quantum gravity") theory.

## Publications

[![Kip Thorne lecture at science festival in Lviv, Ukraine 31.05.25](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/Kip_Thorne_on_OL_Science_%26_Tech_Fest_2025_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Kip_Thorne_on_OL_Science_%26_Tech_Fest_2025_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kip_Thorne_on_OL_Science_&_Tech_Fest_2025_(cropped).jpg)

Kip Thorne lecture in Lviv, Ukraine, May 2025

In 1973, Thorne co-authored *[Gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation_(book) "Gravitation (book)")* with [Charles Misner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Misner "Charles Misner") and [John A. Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Wheeler "John A. Wheeler"); that according to [John C. Baez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Baez "John C. Baez") and Chris Hillman, is one of the great scientific books of all time and has inspired two generations of students. In 1994, he published *[Black Holes and Time Warps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Holes_and_Time_Warps "Black Holes and Time Warps")*, a [popular science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_science "Popular science") book that traces black holes from [John Michell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Michell "John Michell") to [Stephen Hawking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking "Stephen Hawking") and beyond. It has been published in at least twelve languages: English, German (1994), Spanish (1995), French and Japanese (1997), Greek (1999), Czech (2004), Polish, Chinese and Korean (2005), Thai (2008), and Italian (2013). In 2014, he published *[The Science of Interstellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Science_of_Interstellar "The Science of Interstellar")*, in which he explains the science behind [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan")'s film; Nolan wrote the foreword. In the book Thorne discusses the major role of the fictional black hole [Gargantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstellar_(film) "Interstellar (film)"): "First a weird claim: Black holes are made from warped space and warped time. Nothing else--no matter whatsoever."

In September 2017, Thorne and Roger D. Blandford published *Modern Classical Physics: Optics, Fluids, Plasmas, Elasticity, Relativity, and Statistical Physics*, a graduate-level textbook covering the six major areas of physics listed in the title. With artist [Lia Halloran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lia_Halloran "Lia Halloran"), Thorne used poetry and illustrations to explore astrophysics in *The Warped Side of Our Universe*.

Thorne has published more than 150 articles in scholarly journals. His articles have appeared in publications such as *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American "Scientific American")*, the [McGraw-Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGraw-Hill "McGraw-Hill") *[Yearbook of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yearbook_of_Science_and_Technology?action=edit&redlink=1 "Yearbook of Science and Technology")*, and *[Collier's Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collier's_Encyclopedia "Collier's Encyclopedia")*, among others.

## Adaptation in media

- Thorne contributed ideas on wormhole travel to [Carl Sagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sagan "Carl Sagan") for use in his novel *[Contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_(novel) "Contact (novel)")*.
- Thorne and his friend, producer [Lynda Obst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynda_Obst "Lynda Obst"), also developed the concept for the Christopher Nolan film *Interstellar*. He also wrote a tie-in book, *[The Science of Interstellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Science_of_Interstellar "The Science of Interstellar")*. Thorne later advised Nolan on the physics of his movie *[Tenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenet "Tenet")*, and advised [Cillian Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cillian_Murphy "Cillian Murphy") on his portrayal of [J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer "J. Robert Oppenheimer") in Nolan's film *[Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_(film) "Oppenheimer (film)")*.
- In [Larry Niven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Niven "Larry Niven")'s novel *[Rainbow Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Mars "Rainbow Mars")*, the time travel technology used in the novel is based on the wormhole theories of Thorne, which in the context of the novel was when time travel first became possible, rather than just fantasy. As a result, any attempts to travel in time prior to Thorne's development of wormhole theory results in the time traveller entering a fantastic version of reality, rather than the actual past.
- In the film *[The Theory of Everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Theory_of_Everything_(2014_film) "The Theory of Everything (2014 film)")*, Thorne was portrayed by actor [Enzo Cilenti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Cilenti "Enzo Cilenti").
- Thorne played himself in the episode of *[The Big Bang Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Bang_Theory "The Big Bang Theory")* entitled "The Laureate Accumulation".
- Thorne is featured in an episode of the documentary series *[The Craftsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Craftsman_(TV_series) "The Craftsman (TV series)")* entitled "Science, Art & Inspiration".
