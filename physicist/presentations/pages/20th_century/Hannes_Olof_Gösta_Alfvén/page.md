**Hannes Olof Gösta Alfvén** (Swedish: [[alˈveːn]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Swedish "Help:IPA/Swedish"); 30 May 1908 – 2 April 1995) was a Swedish [electrical engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineer "Electrical engineer"), [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics) "Plasma (physics)") physicist and winner of the 1970 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for his work on [magnetohydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetohydrodynamics "Magnetohydrodynamics") (MHD). He described the class of MHD waves now known as [Alfvén waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfvén_wave "Alfvén wave"). He was originally trained as an electrical power engineer and later moved to research and teaching in the fields of plasma physics and [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering "Electrical engineering"). Alfvén made many contributions to plasma physics, including theories describing the behavior of [aurorae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_(astronomy) "Aurora (astronomy)"), the [Van Allen radiation belts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Allen_radiation_belt "Van Allen radiation belt"), the effect of [magnetic storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetic_storm "Geomagnetic storm") on the [Earth's magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_magnetic_field "Earth's magnetic field"), the terrestrial [magnetosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetosphere "Magnetosphere"), and the dynamics of plasmas in the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way "Milky Way") galaxy.

## Education

Alfvén received his PhD from the [University of Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Uppsala "University of Uppsala") in 1934. His thesis was titled "Investigations of High-frequency Electromagnetic Waves".

## Early years

In 1934, Alfvén taught [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") at both the [University of Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Uppsala "University of Uppsala") and the Nobel Institute for Physics (later renamed the [Manne Siegbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manne_Siegbahn "Manne Siegbahn") Institute of Physics) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm"), Sweden. In 1940, he became professor of electromagnetic theory and [electrical measurements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_measurements "Electrical measurements") at the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology "Royal Institute of Technology") in Stockholm. In 1945, he acquired the nonappointive position of Chair of Electronics. His title was changed to Chair of Plasma Physics in 1963. From 1954 to 1955, Alfvén was a [Fulbright Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program#Scholar_grants "Fulbright Program") at the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park "University of Maryland, College Park"). In 1967, after leaving Sweden and spending time in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), he moved to the United States. Alfvén worked in the departments of [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering "Electrical engineering") at both the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego") and the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California "University of Southern California").

## Later years

Alfven in 1976

In 1991, Alfvén retired as professor of electrical engineering at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego") and professor of [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics "Plasma physics") at the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology "Royal Institute of Technology") in Stockholm.

Alfvén spent his later adult life alternating between [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California") and Sweden. He died at the age of 86.

## Research

In 1937, Alfvén argued that if [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics) "Plasma (physics)") pervaded the universe, it could then carry electric currents capable of generating a galactic magnetic field. After winning the Nobel Prize for his works in [magnetohydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetohydrodynamics "Magnetohydrodynamics"), he emphasized that:

> In order to understand the phenomena in a certain plasma region, it is necessary to map not only the magnetic but also the electric field and the electric currents. Space is filled with a network of currents which transfer energy and momentum over large or very large distances. The currents often pinch to filamentary or surface currents. The latter are likely to give space, as also interstellar and intergalactic space, a [cellular structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)").

His theoretical work on field-aligned electric currents in the aurora (based on earlier work by [Kristian Birkeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristian_Birkeland "Kristian Birkeland")) was confirmed in 1967, these currents now being known as [Birkeland currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkeland_current "Birkeland current").

British scientist [Sydney Chapman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Chapman_(astronomer) "Sydney Chapman (astronomer)") was a strong critic of Alfvén. Many physicists regarded Alfvén as espousing [unorthodox opinions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fringe_science "Fringe science") [R. H. Stuewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_H._Stuewer "Roger H. Stuewer") noting that "... he remained an embittered outsider, winning little respect from other scientists even after he received the Nobel Prize..." and was often forced to publish his papers in obscure journals. Alfvén recalled:

> When I describe [plasma phenomena] according to this formalism most referees do not understand what I say and turn down my papers. With the referee system which rules US science today, this means that my papers are rarely accepted by the leading US journals.

Alfvén played a central role in the development of:

- [Plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics "Plasma physics")
- [Charged particle beams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charged_particle_beam "Charged particle beam")
- [Interplanetary medium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interplanetary_medium "Interplanetary medium")
- [Magnetospheric physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetospheric_physics "Magnetospheric physics")
- [Magnetohydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetohydrodynamics "Magnetohydrodynamics")
- [Solar phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Solar_phenomena "Category:Solar phenomena") investigation (such as the [solar wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_wind "Solar wind"))
- [Aurorae science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_(astronomy) "Aurora (astronomy)")

In 1939, Alfvén proposed the theory of [magnetic storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetic_storm "Geomagnetic storm") and auroras and the theory of [plasma dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics) "Plasma (physics)") in the [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth")'s [magnetosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetosphere "Magnetosphere"). This was the paper rejected by the U.S. journal *Terrestrial Magnetism and Atmospheric Electricity*.

Applications of Alfvén's research in space science include:

- [Van Allen radiation belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Allen_radiation_belt "Van Allen radiation belt") theory
- Reduction of the [Earth's magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_magnetic_field "Earth's magnetic field") during [magnetic storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_storm "Magnetic storm")
- [Magnetosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetosphere "Magnetosphere") (protective plasma covering the Earth)
- Formation of [comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comet "Comet") tails
- Formation of the [Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System "Solar System")
- [Dynamics of plasmas in the galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysical_plasma "Astrophysical plasma")
- [Physical cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_cosmology "Physical cosmology")

Alfvén's views followed those of the founder of magnetospheric physics, [Kristian Birkeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristian_Birkeland "Kristian Birkeland"). At the end of the nineteenth century, Birkeland proposed (backed by extensive data) that electric currents flowing down along the Earth's magnetic fields into the atmosphere caused the aurora and polar magnetic disturbances.

Areas of technology benefiting from Alfvén's contributions include:

- [Particle accelerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator "Particle accelerator")
- Controlled [thermonuclear fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermonuclear_fusion "Thermonuclear fusion")
- [Hypersonic flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_flight "Hypersonic flight")
- [Rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket "Rocket") propulsion
- [Reentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_reentry "Atmospheric reentry") braking of [space vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_vehicle "Space vehicle")

Contributions to astrophysics:

- Galactic magnetic field (1937)
- Identified nonthermal [synchrotron radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchrotron_radiation "Synchrotron radiation") from astronomical sources (1950)

[Alfvén waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfvén_wave "Alfvén wave") (low [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency "Frequency") hydromagnetic plasma [oscillations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillator "Oscillator")) are named in his honor, and propagate at the Alfvén speed. Many of his theories about the solar system were verified as late as the 1980s through external measurements of cometary and planetary magnetospheres. However, Alfvén himself noted that astrophysical textbooks poorly represented known plasma phenomena:

> A study of how a number of the most used textbooks in astrophysics treat important concepts such as [double layers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_layer_(plasma) "Double layer (plasma)"), [critical velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_ionization_velocity "Critical ionization velocity"), [pinch effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinch_effect "Pinch effect"), and circuits is made. It is found that students using these textbooks remain essentially ignorant of even the existence of these concepts, despite the fact that some of them have been well known for half a century (e.g, double layers, Langmuir, 1929; pinch effect, Bennet, 1934).

Alfvén reported that of 17 of the most used textbooks on astrophysics, none mention the pinch effect, none mentioned critical ionization velocity, only two mentioned circuits, and three mentioned double layers.

Alfvén believed the problem with the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang "Big Bang") was that astrophysicists tried to extrapolate the origin of the universe from [mathematical theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_theory "Mathematical theory") developed on the blackboard, rather than starting from known observable phenomena. He also considered the Big Bang to be a [myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythology "Mythology") devised to explain creation. Alfvén and colleagues proposed the [Alfvén–Klein model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfvén–Klein_cosmology "Alfvén–Klein cosmology") as an alternative [cosmological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_cosmology "Physical cosmology") theory to both the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang "Big Bang") and [steady state theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steady_state_theory "Steady state theory") cosmologies.

## Personal life

Alfvén was married for 67 years to his wife Kerstin (1910–1992). They raised five children, one boy and four girls. Their son became a physician, while one daughter became a writer and another a lawyer in Sweden. The writer was [Inger Alfvén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inger_Alfvén "Inger Alfvén") and is well known for her work in Sweden. The composer [Hugo Alfvén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Alfvén "Hugo Alfvén") was Hannes Alfvén's uncle.

Alfvén studied the [history of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science "History of science"), [oriental philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_philosophy "Oriental philosophy"), and [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion "Religion"). On his religious views, Alfven was [irreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligious "Irreligious") and critical of religion. He spoke Swedish, English, German, French, and Russian, and some Spanish and Chinese. He expressed great concern about the difficulties of permanent [high-level radioactive waste management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-level_radioactive_waste_management "High-level radioactive waste management")." Alfvén was also interested in problems in cosmology and all aspects of auroral physics, and used Schröder's well known book on aurora, *Das Phänomen des Polarlichts*. Letters of Alfvén, Treder, and Schröder were published on the occasion of Treder's 70th birthday. The relationships between Hans-Jürgen Treder, Hannes Alfvén and Wilfried Schröder were discussed in detail by Schröder in his publications.

Alfvén died on 2 April 1995 at [Djursholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djursholm "Djursholm") aged 86.

The [Hannes Alfvén Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannes_Alfvén_Prize "Hannes Alfvén Prize"), awarded annually by the [European Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Physical_Society "European Physical Society") for outstanding contributions in plasma physics, is named after him. The [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid "Asteroid") [1778 Alfvén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1778_Alfvén "1778 Alfvén") is named in his honour.
