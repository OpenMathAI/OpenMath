**Igor Yevgenyevich Tamm** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): И́горь Евге́ньевич Тамм; 8 July 1895 – 12 April 1971) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who received the 1958 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), jointly with [Pavel Cherenkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Cherenkov "Pavel Cherenkov") and [Ilya Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Frank "Ilya Frank"), for their 1934 discovery and demonstration of [Cherenkov radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherenkov_radiation "Cherenkov radiation"). He also predicted the [quasi-particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-particle "Quasi-particle") of sound: the [phonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonon "Phonon"); and in 1951, together with [Andrei Sakharov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Sakharov "Andrei Sakharov"), proposed the [Tokamak system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokamak "Tokamak").

## Biography

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/Igor_Tamm_2000_Russian_stamp.jpg/250px-Igor_Tamm_2000_Russian_stamp.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Igor_Tamm_2000_Russian_stamp.jpg)

Tamm on the 2000 Russian stamp "Idea of phonons, 1929"

Igor Tamm was born in 1895 in [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok "Vladivostok") into the family of Eugene Tamm, a civil engineer, and his wife Olga Davydova. According to Russian sources, Tamm had [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people "German people") noble descent on his father's side through his grandfather Theodor Tamm, who emigrated from [Thuringia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thuringia "Thuringia"). Although his surname "[Tamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamm_(surname) "Tamm (surname)")" is rather common in [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia "Estonia"), other sources state he was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_people "Jewish people") or had Jewish ancestry.

He studied at a [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)") in [Elisavetgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisavetgrad "Elisavetgrad") (now [Kropyvnytskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kropyvnytskyi "Kropyvnytskyi"), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine")). In 1913–1914 he studied at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh "University of Edinburgh") together with his school-friend [Boris Hessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Hessen "Boris Hessen").

At the outbreak of World War I in 1914 he joined the army as a volunteer field medic. In 1917 he joined the Revolutionary movement and became an active anti-war campaigner, serving on revolutionary committees after the March Revolution. He returned to the [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University") from which he graduated in 1918.

Tamm married Nataliya Shuyskaya (1894–1980) in September 1917. Shе belonged to a noble [Rurikid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rurikid "Rurikid") Shuysky family. They eventually had two children, Irina (1921–2009, chemist) and Evgeny (1926–2008, experimental physicist and famous mountain climber, leader of the [Soviet Everest expedition in 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_climbing_Mount_Everest "Timeline of climbing Mount Everest")).

On 1 May 1923, Tamm began teaching physics at the [Second Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Moscow_State_University "Second Moscow State University"). The same year, he finished his first scientific paper, *Electrodynamics of the Anisotropic Medium in the Special Theory of Relativity*. In 1928, he spent a few months with [Paul Ehrenfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrenfest "Paul Ehrenfest") at the [University of Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University "Leiden University") and made a life-long friendship with [Paul Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac"). From 1934 until his death in 1971 Tamm was the head of the theoretical department at [Lebedev Physical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebedev_Physical_Institute "Lebedev Physical Institute") in Moscow.

In 1932, Tamm published a paper with his proposal of the concept of [surface states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_states "Surface states"). This concept is important for metal–oxide–semiconductor field-effect transistor ([MOSFET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOSFET "MOSFET")) physics.

In 1934, Tamm and [Semen Altshuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semen_Altshuller "Semen Altshuller") suggested that the neutron has a non-zero magnetic moment, the idea was met with scepticism at that time, as the neutron was supposed to be an elementary particle with zero charge, and thus could not have a magnetic moment. The same year, Tamm coined an idea that proton-neutron interactions can be described as an exchange force transmitted by a yet unknown massive particle, this idea was later developed by [Hideki Yukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Yukawa "Hideki Yukawa") into a theory of [meson forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukawa_interaction "Yukawa interaction").

In 1945 he developed an approximation method for many-body physics. As [Sidney Dancoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Dancoff "Sidney Dancoff") developed it independently in 1950, it is now called the Tamm-Dancoff approximation.

He was the [Nobel Laureate in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for the year 1958 together with [Pavel Cherenkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Cherenkov "Pavel Cherenkov") and [Ilya Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Frank "Ilya Frank") for the discovery and the interpretation of the [Cherenkov-Vavilov effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherenkov_effect "Cherenkov effect").

In late 1940s to early 1950s Tamm was involved in the [Soviet thermonuclear bomb project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_atomic_bomb_project "Soviet atomic bomb project"); in 1949–1953 he spent most of his time in the "secret city" of [Sarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarov "Sarov"), working as a head of the theoretical group developing the hydrogen bomb, however he retired from the project and returned to the Moscow [Lebedev Physical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebedev_Physical_Institute "Lebedev Physical Institute") after the first successful test of a [hydrogen bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_bomb "Hydrogen bomb") in 1953.

In 1951, together with [Andrei Sakharov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Sakharov "Andrei Sakharov"), Tamm proposed a [tokamak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokamak "Tokamak") system for the realization of [controlled thermonuclear fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_thermonuclear_fusion "Controlled thermonuclear fusion") on the basis of [toroidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toroid "Toroid") magnetic [thermonuclear reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion "Nuclear fusion") and soon after the first such devices were built by the  INF. Results from the T-3 Soviet magnetic confinement device in 1968, when the [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics) "Plasma (physics)") parameters unique for that time were obtained, showed temperatures in their machine to be over an order of magnitude higher than what was expected by the rest of the community. The western scientists visited the experiment and verified the high temperatures and confinement, sparking a wave of optimism for the prospects of the tokamak as well as construction of new experiments, which is still the dominant magnetic confinement device today.

In 1964 he was elected a Member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina").

Tamm was a student of [Leonid Isaakovich Mandelshtam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Isaakovich_Mandelshtam "Leonid Isaakovich Mandelshtam") in science and life.

Tamm was an atheist.

Tamm died in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow"), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") on 12 April 1971, the Lunar crater [Tamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamm_(crater) "Tamm (crater)") is named after him. He is buried at [Novodevichy Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novodevichy_Cemetery "Novodevichy Cemetery").
