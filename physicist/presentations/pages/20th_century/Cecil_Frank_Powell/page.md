**Cecil Frank Powell** (5 December 1903 – 9 August 1969) was a British experimental [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1950 for heading the team that developed the photographic method of studying nuclear processes, and for the resulting discovery of the [pion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pion "Pion") (pi-[meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meson "Meson")).

## Education

Cecil Frank Powell was born on 5 December 1903 in [Tonbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonbridge "Tonbridge"), England, the son of Frank Powell, a gunsmith, and Elizabeth Caroline Bisacre. Powell was educated at a local primary school, before gaining a scholarship to [The Judd School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Judd_School "The Judd School"). He entered [Sidney Sussex College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Sussex_College,_Cambridge "Sidney Sussex College, Cambridge"), graduating in 1925 with [First Class Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Class_Honours "First Class Honours") in the [Natural Sciences Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Sciences_Tripos "Natural Sciences Tripos"). He then worked under [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford "Ernest Rutherford") and [C. T. R. Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._T._R._Wilson "C. T. R. Wilson") in the [Cavendish Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Laboratory "Cavendish Laboratory"), and received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in Physics in 1927.

In 1932, Powell married Isobel Therese Artner (1907–1995). They had two daughters, Jane and Annie.

## Career and research

In 1927, Powell became a research assistant to [Arthur Mannering Tyndall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Mannering_Tyndall "Arthur Mannering Tyndall") in the H. H. Wills Physical Laboratory at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol "University of Bristol"). He was later appointed lecturer and, in 1948, Melville Wills Professor of Physics. In 1936, he took part in a [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") expedition to [Montserrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montserrat "Montserrat") in the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies "West Indies") as part of a study of a damaging [earthquake swarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_swarm "Earthquake swarm"). He appears on a stamp issued in [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada "Grenada").

During his time at Bristol University, Powell applied himself to the development of techniques for measuring the mobility of positive ions, to establishing the nature of the ions in common gases, and to the construction and use of a [Cockcroft–Walton generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockcroft–Walton_generator "Cockcroft–Walton generator") to study the scattering of [atomic nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus "Atomic nucleus"). He also began to develop methods employing specialised [photographic emulsions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_emulsion "Nuclear emulsion") to facilitate the recording of the tracks of [elementary particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle "Elementary particle"), and in 1938 began applying this technique to the study of [cosmic radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_radiation "Cosmic radiation"), exposing photographic plates at high-altitude, at the tops of mountains and using specially designed balloons, collaborating in the study with [Giuseppe Occhialini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Occhialini "Giuseppe Occhialini"), [Hugh Muirhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Muirhead "Hugh Muirhead"), and [César Lattes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/César_Lattes "César Lattes"). This work led in 1947 to the discovery of the [pion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pion "Pion") (pi-[meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meson "Meson")), which proved to be the hypothetical particle proposed in 1935 by [Hideki Yukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Yukawa "Hideki Yukawa") in his theory of [nuclear forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_force "Nuclear force").

In 1950, Powell was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") "for his development of the photographic method of studying nuclear processes and his discoveries regarding mesons made with this method." Lattes was working with him at the time of the discovery and had improved the sensitivity of the photographic emulsion. Lattes was the first to write an article describing the discovery that would lead to the Nobel Prize. [Debendra Mohan Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debendra_Mohan_Bose "Debendra Mohan Bose") and [Bibha Chowdhuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibha_Chowdhuri "Bibha Chowdhuri") published three consecutive papers in *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")*, but could not continue further investigation on account of "non-availability of more sensitive emulsion plates" during the war years.

Seven years after this discovery of mesons by Bose and Chowdhuri, Powell made the same discovery of [pions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pion "Pion") and [muons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muon "Muon") and further decay of muons to [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron")… using the same technique". He acknowledged in his book, "In 1941, Bose and Chaudhuri (sic) had pointed it out that it is possible, in principle, to distinguish between the tracks of [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") and mesons in an emulsion… They concluded that many of the [charged particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charged_particle "Charged particle") arrested in their plates were lighter than protons, their mean mass being … the physical basis of their method was correct and their work represents the first approach to the scattering method of determining momenta of charged particles by observation of their tracks in emulsion". In fact, the measured mass of the particle by Bose and Chowdhuri was very close to the accepted value measured by Powell who used improved "full-tone" plates. From 1952, Powell was appointed director of several expeditions to Sardinia and the Po Valley, Italy, utilizing high-altitude balloon flights.

In 1955, Powell, also a member of the [World Federation of Scientific Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Federation_of_Scientific_Workers "World Federation of Scientific Workers"), added his signature to the [Russell–Einstein Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell–Einstein_Manifesto "Russell–Einstein Manifesto") put forward by [Bertrand Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_Russell "Bertrand Russell"), [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein"), and [Joseph Rotblat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Rotblat "Joseph Rotblat"), and was involved in preparations for the first [Pugwash Conference on Science and World Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pugwash_Conferences_on_Science_and_World_Affairs "Pugwash Conferences on Science and World Affairs"). As Rotblat put it, "Cecil Powell has been the backbone of the Pugwash Movement. He gave it coherence, endurance and vitality." Powell chaired the meetings of the Pugwash Continuing Committee, often standing in for Bertrand Russell, and attended meetings until 1968.

In 1961, Powell served on the Scientific Policy Committee of the European Organization for Nuclear Research ([CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN")).

## Global policy

He was one of the signatories of the agreement to convene a convention for drafting a [world constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_constitution "World constitution"). As a result, for the first time in human history, a [World Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Constituent_Assembly "World Constituent Assembly") convened to draft and adopt the [Constitution for the Federation of Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_for_the_Federation_of_Earth "Constitution for the Federation of Earth").

## Death

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/C.F.Powell_-_Bench_%26_Plaque.jpg/250px-C.F.Powell_-_Bench_%26_Plaque.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:C.F.Powell_-_Bench_&_Plaque.jpg)

[Memorial bench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_bench "Memorial bench") dedicated to Powell outside the Capanna Vittoria restaurant, on the Alpe Giumello in [Casargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casargo "Casargo"), Italy.

On 9 August 1969, Powell died of a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack "Heart attack") while on holiday with his wife in the [Valsassina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valsassina "Valsassina") region of Italy, lodging in a house in Sanico, in the [Province of Lecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lecco "Province of Lecco").

[Giuseppe Occhialini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Occhialini "Giuseppe Occhialini") had a wooden bench built with Powell's name carved into a [commemorative plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commemorative_plaque "Commemorative plaque"), and then transported it to [Premana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premana "Premana"), a village in the mountains above [Lake Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Como "Lake Como"). It was installed on the path where he died, outside the Rifugio Capanna Vittoria (now the Capanna Vittoria restaurant), on the Alpe Giumello, in [Casargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casargo "Casargo"). Occhialini's reason was, "...if that bench had already been there, Powell would probably have stopped to rest there."

## Commemoration

- The Cecil F. Powell Memorial Medal was named in his honour by the [European Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Physical_Society "European Physical Society").
- In October 2011, a replacement [commemorative plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commemorative_plaque "Commemorative plaque") was installed in Downside Road, Bristol.
- In 2013, the [International Inner Wheel Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Wheel_Club "Inner Wheel Club") of [Lecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecco "Lecco"), in [Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardy "Lombardy"), Northern Italy, erected a sign in memory of Powell, close to where he died, at the Capanna Vittoria restaurant, on the Alpe Giumello, in Casargo.
