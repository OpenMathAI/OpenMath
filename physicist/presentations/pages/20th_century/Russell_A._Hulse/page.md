**Russell Alan Hulse** (born November 28, 1950) is an American [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysicist "Astrophysicist"). He shared the 1993 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Joseph Hooton Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Hooton_Taylor "Joseph Hooton Taylor") "for the discovery of a [new type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSR_B1913+16 "PSR B1913+16") of [pulsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsar "Pulsar"), a discovery that has opened up new possibilities for the study of [gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation "Gravitation")". This was the first indirect detection of [gravitational waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_wave "Gravitational wave"), later directly detected by [Kip Thorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kip_Thorne "Kip Thorne"), [Barry Barish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Barish "Barry Barish") and [Rainer Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainer_Weiss "Rainer Weiss").

## Biography

Hulse was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") and graduated from the [Bronx High School of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_High_School_of_Science "Bronx High School of Science") and the [Cooper Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Union "Cooper Union"). He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") from the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst "University of Massachusetts Amherst") in 1975.

While working on his PhD dissertation, he was a scholar in 1974 at the [Arecibo Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecibo_Observatory "Arecibo Observatory") in [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico") of [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"). There he worked with Taylor on a large-scale survey for pulsars. It was this work that led to the discovery of the first [binary pulsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_pulsar "Binary pulsar").

In 1974, Hulse and Taylor discovered [binary pulsar PSR B1913](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSR_B1913+16 "PSR B1913+16"), which is made up of a pulsar and black companion [star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star "Star"). [Neutron star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_star "Neutron star") rotation emits pulses that are extremely regular and stable in the [radio wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_wave "Radio wave") region and is nearby condensed material body gravitation (non-detectable in the visible field). Hulse, Taylor, and other colleagues have used this first [binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_star "Binary star") [pulsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsar "Pulsar") to make high-precision tests of [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity"), demonstrating the existence of [gravitational radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_radiation "Gravitational radiation"). An [approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation "Approximation") of this [radiant energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiant_energy "Radiant energy") is described by the formula of the quadrupolar radiation of [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") (1918).

In 1979, researchers announced measurements of small acceleration effects of the orbital movements of a pulsar. This was initial proof that the system of these two moving masses emits gravitational waves.

In 1993, Hulse and Taylor shared the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for the discovery of the first binary pulsar.

## Later years

After receiving his PhD, Hulse did postdoctoral work at the [National Radio Astronomy Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Radio_Astronomy_Observatory "National Radio Astronomy Observatory") in [Green Bank, West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bank,_West_Virginia "Green Bank, West Virginia"). He moved to Princeton, where he has worked for many years at the [Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_Plasma_Physics_Laboratory "Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory"). He has also worked on science education, and in 2003 joined the [University of Texas at Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Dallas "University of Texas at Dallas") as a [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor "Professor") of [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") and of [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") and [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science "Science") [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education "Education").

Hulse was elected a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_AAAS "Fellow of the AAAS") of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science") in 2003, and is cited in the [American Men and Women of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Men_and_Women_of_Science "American Men and Women of Science").

In 2004, Hulse joined [University of Texas at Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Dallas "University of Texas at Dallas") and became the Founding Director of UT Dallas Science and Engineering Education Center (SEEC).

In July 2007 Hulse joined the [Aurora Imaging Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_Imaging_Technology?action=edit&redlink=1 "Aurora Imaging Technology") advisory board.
