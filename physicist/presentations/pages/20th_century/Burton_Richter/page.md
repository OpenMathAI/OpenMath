**Burton Richter** (March 22, 1931 – July 18, 2018) was an American physicist. He led the [Stanford Linear Accelerator Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Linear_Accelerator_Center "Stanford Linear Accelerator Center") (SLAC) team which co-discovered the [J/ψ meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J/ψ_meson "J/ψ meson") in 1974, alongside the [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory") (BNL) team led by [Samuel Ting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_C._C._Ting "Samuel C. C. Ting") for which they won [Nobel Prize for Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Physics "Nobel Prize for Physics") in 1976. This discovery was part of the [November Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_Revolution_(physics) "November Revolution (physics)") of particle physics. He was the SLAC director from 1984 to 1999.

## Life and work

A native of [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), Richter was born into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") family in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"), and was raised in the [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens "Queens") neighborhood of [Far Rockaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway,_Queens "Far Rockaway, Queens"). His parents were Fanny (Pollack) and Abraham Richter, a textile worker. He graduated from [Far Rockaway High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway_High_School "Far Rockaway High School"), a school that also produced fellow laureates [Baruch Samuel Blumberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_Samuel_Blumberg "Baruch Samuel Blumberg") and [Richard Feynman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Feynman "Richard Feynman"). He attended [Mercersburg Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercersburg_Academy "Mercersburg Academy") in Pennsylvania, then continued on to study at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"), where he received his bachelor's degree in 1952 and his PhD in 1956. He then joined the faculty of [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"), becoming a full professor in 1967. Richter was director of the [Stanford Linear Accelerator Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Linear_Accelerator_Center "Stanford Linear Accelerator Center") (SLAC) from 1984 to 1999. He was a senior fellow of the [Freeman Spogli Institute for International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeman_Spogli_Institute_for_International_Studies "Freeman Spogli Institute for International Studies") and Paul Pigott Professor in the Physical Sciences Emeritus of Stanford University.

As a professor at Stanford, Richter designed the [SPEAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPEAR "SPEAR") (Stanford Positron-Electron Asymmetric Ring) [particle accelerator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator "Particle accelerator") with the help of another Stanford physics professor, [David Ritson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ritson?action=edit&redlink=1 "David Ritson"). When eventually resources were secured, Richter led the building of SPEAR, with the support of the [U.S. Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Atomic_Energy_Commission "U.S. Atomic Energy Commission"). With it he led a team that discovered a new [subatomic particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_particle "Subatomic particle") he called a *ψ* ([psi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi_(letter) "Psi (letter)")). This discovery was also made by the team led by [Samuel Ting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_C._C._Ting "Samuel C. C. Ting") at [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory"), but he called the particle *J*. The particle thus became known as the [J/ψ meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J/ψ_meson "J/ψ meson"). Richter and Ting were jointly awarded the 1976 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for their work.

During 1975 Richter spent a sabbatical year at [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN") where he worked on the [ISR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersecting_Storage_Rings "Intersecting Storage Rings") experiment R702.

In 1987, Richter received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").

Richter was a member of the [JASON advisory group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JASON_(advisory_group) "JASON (advisory group)") and served on the board of directors of [Scientists and Engineers for America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientists_and_Engineers_for_America "Scientists and Engineers for America"), an organization focused on promoting sound science in [American government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_government "American government").

Richter was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 2003.

In May 2007, he visited [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") and [Sharif University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_University_of_Technology "Sharif University of Technology").

Richter is one of the 20 American recipients of the Nobel Prize in Physics to sign a letter addressed to President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") in May 2008, urging him to "reverse the damage done to basic science research in the Fiscal Year 2008 Omnibus Appropriations Bill" by requesting additional emergency funding for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")'s [Office of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Science "Office of Science"), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), and the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology").

In 2012, President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") announced that Burton Richter was a co-recipient of the [Enrico Fermi Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi_Award "Enrico Fermi Award"), along with [Mildred Dresselhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mildred_Dresselhaus "Mildred Dresselhaus").

In 2014, President Obama also awarded Richter the 2012 [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science"). His citation read, "For pioneering contributions to the development of electron accelerators, including circular and linear colliders, synchrotron light sources, and for discoveries in elementary particle physics and contributions to energy policy."

In 2013, Richter commented on an open letter from [Tom Wigley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Wigley "Tom Wigley"), [Kerry Emanuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerry_Emanuel "Kerry Emanuel"), [Ken Caldeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Caldeira "Ken Caldeira"), and [James Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hansen "James Hansen"), that [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel "Angela Merkel") was "wrong to shut down nuclear".

In 2014, Richter was among the residents of a continuing care retirement center who filed an unsuccessful lawsuit against a continuing care retirement home's financial practices.

Richter died on July 18, 2018, in [Stanford, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford,_California "Stanford, California"), at the age 87.
