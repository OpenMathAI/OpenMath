**Frederick Reines** ([/ˈraɪnəs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*RY-nəs*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); March 16, 1918 – August 26, 1998) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). He was awarded the 1995 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for his co-detection of the [neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino "Neutrino") with [Clyde Cowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_Cowan "Clyde Cowan") in the [neutrino experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino_experiment "Neutrino experiment"). He may be the only scientist in history "so intimately associated with the discovery of an [elementary particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle "Elementary particle") and the subsequent thorough investigation of its fundamental properties."

A graduate of [Stevens Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevens_Institute_of_Technology "Stevens Institute of Technology") and [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University"), Reines joined the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project")'s [Los Alamos Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_Laboratory "Los Alamos Laboratory") in 1944, working in the Theoretical Division in [Richard Feynman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Feynman "Richard Feynman")'s group. He became a group leader there in 1946. He participated in a number of [nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_tests "Nuclear tests"), culminating in his becoming the director of the [Operation Greenhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Greenhouse "Operation Greenhouse") test series in the Pacific in 1951.

In the early 1950s, working in [Hanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanford_Site "Hanford Site") and [Savannah River Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah_River_Site "Savannah River Site"), Reines and Cowan developed the equipment and procedures with which they first detected the supposedly undetectable neutrinos in June 1956. Reines dedicated the major part of his career to the study of the neutrino's properties and interactions, which work would influence study of the neutrino for many researchers to come. This included the detection of neutrinos created in the atmosphere by [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray "Cosmic ray"), and the 1987 detection of neutrinos emitted from [Supernova SN1987A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SN_1987A "SN 1987A"), which inaugurated the field of [neutrino astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino_astronomy "Neutrino astronomy").

## Early life

Frederick Reines was born in [Paterson, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paterson,_New_Jersey "Paterson, New Jersey"), one of four children of Gussie ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Cohen) and Israel Reines. His parents were Jewish emigrants from the same town in Russia, but only met in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), where they were later married. He had an older sister, Paula, who became a doctor, and two older brothers, David and William, who became lawyers. He said that his "early education was strongly influenced" by his studious siblings. He was the great-nephew of the [Rabbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbi "Rabbi") [Yitzchak Yaacov Reines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzchak_Yaacov_Reines "Yitzchak Yaacov Reines"), the founder of [Mizrachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizrachi_(religious_Zionism) "Mizrachi (religious Zionism)"), a [religious Zionist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionist_movement "Religious Zionist movement").

The family moved to [Hillburn, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillburn,_New_York "Hillburn, New York"), where his father ran the [general store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_store "General store"), and he spent much of his childhood. He was an [Eagle Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Scout_(Boy_Scouts_of_America) "Eagle Scout (Boy Scouts of America)"). Looking back, Reines said: "My early childhood memories center around this typical American country store and life in a small American town, including [Independence Day July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_(United_States) "Independence Day (United States)") celebrations marked by fireworks and patriotic music played from a pavilion bandstand."

Reines sang in a chorus, and as a soloist. For a time he considered the possibility of a singing career, and was instructed by a vocal coach from the [Metropolitan Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Opera "Metropolitan Opera") who provided lessons for free because the family did not have the money for them. The family later moved to [North Bergen, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bergen,_New_Jersey "North Bergen, New Jersey"), residing on [Kennedy Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Route_501_(New_Jersey) "County Route 501 (New Jersey)") and 57th Street. Because North Bergen did not have a high school, he attended [Union Hill High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Hill_High_School "Union Hill High School") in [Union Hill, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Hill,_New_Jersey "Union Hill, New Jersey") (today [Union City, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_City,_New_Jersey "Union City, New Jersey")), from which he graduated in 1935.

From an early age, Reines exhibited an interest in science, and liked creating and building things. He later recalled that:

> The first stirrings of interest in science that I remember occurred during a moment of boredom at religious school, when, looking out of the window at twilight through a hand curled to [simulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulate "Simulate") a [telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope "Telescope"), I noticed something peculiar about the light; it was the phenomenon of [diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffraction "Diffraction"). That began for me a fascination with light.

Ironically, Reines excelled in literary and history courses, but received average or low marks in science and math in his [freshman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshman "Freshman") year of high school, though he improved in those areas by his [junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_(education_year) "Junior (education year)") and [senior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_(education) "Senior (education)") years through the encouragement of a teacher who gave him a key to the school laboratory. This cultivated a love of science by his senior year. In response to a question seniors were asked about what they wanted to do for a yearbook quote, he responded: "To be a physicist extraordinaire."

Reines was accepted into the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"), but chose instead to attend [Stevens Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevens_Institute_of_Technology "Stevens Institute of Technology") in [Hoboken, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoboken,_New_Jersey "Hoboken, New Jersey"), where he earned his [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") (B.S.) degree in [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering "Mechanical engineering") in 1939, and his [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") (M.S.) degree in mathematical physics in 1941, writing a thesis on "A Critical Review of Optical Diffraction Theory". He married Sylvia Samuels on August 30, 1940. They had two children, Robert and Alisa. He then entered [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University"), where he earned his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") (Ph.D.) in 1944. He studied [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_rays "Cosmic rays") there under [Serge A. Korff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serge_A._Korff "Serge A. Korff"), but wrote his [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis "Thesis") under the supervision of Richard D. Present on "Nuclear fission and the liquid drop model of the nucleus". Publication of the thesis was delayed until after the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"); it appeared in [Physical Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Review "Physical Review") in 1946.

## Los Alamos Laboratory

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Reines-frederick.jpg/250px-Reines-frederick.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Reines-frederick.jpg)

Frederick Reines Los Alamos badge

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Greenhouse_Dog_001.jpg/250px-Greenhouse_Dog_001.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Greenhouse_Dog_001.jpg)

[Operation Greenhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Greenhouse "Operation Greenhouse") – [Dog shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Greenhouse#Dog "Operation Greenhouse")

In 1944 [Richard Feynman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Feynman "Richard Feynman") recruited Reines to work in the Theoretical Division at the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project")'s [Los Alamos Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_Laboratory "Los Alamos Laboratory"), where he would remain for the next fifteen years. He joined Feynman's T-4 (Diffusion Problems) Group, which was part of [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe "Hans Bethe")'s T (Theoretical) Division. [Diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion "Diffusion") was an important aspect of [critical mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_mass "Critical mass") calculations. In June 1946, he became a group leader, heading the T-1 (Theory of Dragon) Group. An outgrowth of the "[tickling the Dragon's tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demon_core#Second_incident "Demon core")" experiment, the Dragon was a machine that could attain a critical state for short bursts of time, which could be used as a research tool or power source.

Reines participated in a number of [nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_test "Nuclear test"), and writing reports on their results. These included [Operation Crossroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Crossroads "Operation Crossroads") at [Bikini Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bikini_Atoll "Bikini Atoll") in 1946, [Operation Sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sandstone "Operation Sandstone") at [Eniwetok Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eniwetok_Atoll "Eniwetok Atoll") in 1948, and [Operation Ranger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ranger "Operation Ranger") and [Operation Buster–Jangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Buster–Jangle "Operation Buster–Jangle") at the [Nevada Test Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_Test_Site "Nevada Test Site"). In 1951 he was the director of [Operation Greenhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Greenhouse "Operation Greenhouse") series of [nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_tests "Nuclear tests") in the Pacific. This saw the first American tests of [boosted fission weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boosted_fission_weapon "Boosted fission weapon"), an important step towards [thermonuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermonuclear_weapon "Thermonuclear weapon"). He studied the effects of nuclear blasts, and co-authored a paper with [John von Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann "John von Neumann") on Mach stem formation, an important aspect of an air [blast wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_wave "Blast wave").

In spite or perhaps because of his role in these nuclear tests, Reines was concerned about the dangers of radioactive pollution from atmospheric nuclear tests, and became an advocate of [underground nuclear testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_nuclear_testing "Underground nuclear testing"). In the wake of the [Sputnik crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik_crisis "Sputnik crisis"), he participated in [John Archibald Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Archibald_Wheeler "John Archibald Wheeler")'s Project 137, which evolved into [JASON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JASON_(advisory_group) "JASON (advisory group)"). He was also a delegate at the [Atoms for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atoms_for_Peace "Atoms for Peace") Conference in Geneva in 1958.

## Discovery of the neutrino and the inner workings of stars

[![Photo of Clyde Cowan and Frederick Reines](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Clyde_Cowan_and_Frederick_Reines.jpg/250px-Clyde_Cowan_and_Frederick_Reines.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Clyde_Cowan_and_Frederick_Reines.jpg)

Reines and [Clyde Cowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_Cowan "Clyde Cowan")

The [neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino "Neutrino") is a [subatomic particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_particle "Subatomic particle") first proposed by [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli") on December 4, 1930. The particle was required to resolve the problem of missing energy in observations of [beta decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_decay "Beta decay"), when a [neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron "Neutron") decays into a [proton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") and an [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron"). The new hypothetical particle was required to preserve the fundamental law of [conservation of energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_energy "Conservation of energy"). [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi") renamed it the [neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino "Neutrino"), Italian for "little neutral one", and in 1934, proposed his [theory of beta decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi's_interaction "Fermi's interaction") by which the electrons emitted from the nucleus were created by the decay of a neutron into a proton, an electron, and a neutrino:

:   n0
     → p+
     + e−
     + ν
    e

The neutrino accounted for the missing energy, but Fermi's theory described a particle with little mass and no electric charge that appeared to be impossible to observe directly. In a 1934 paper, [Rudolf Peierls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Peierls "Rudolf Peierls") and [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe "Hans Bethe") calculated that neutrinos could easily pass through the Earth, and concluded "there is no practically possible way of observing the neutrino."

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Poltergeist_Team_1953.jpg/250px-Poltergeist_Team_1953.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Poltergeist_Team_1953.jpg)

Frederick Reines (far right) with Clyde Cowan (far left) and other members of Project Poltergeist

In 1951, Reines and his colleague [Clyde Cowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_Cowan "Clyde Cowan") decided to see if they could detect neutrinos and so prove their existence. At the conclusion of the Greenhouse test series, Reines had received permission from the head of T Division, [J. Carson Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Carson_Mark "J. Carson Mark"), for a leave in residence to study fundamental physics. "So why did we want to detect the free neutrino?" he later explained, "Because everybody said, you couldn't do it."

According to Fermi's theory, there was also a corresponding reverse reaction, in which a neutrino combines with a proton to create a neutron and a [positron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron "Positron"):

:   ν
    e + p+
     → n0
     + e+

The positron would soon be annihilated by an electron and produce two 0.51 MeV [gamma rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_rays "Gamma rays"), while the neutron would be captured by a proton and release a 2.2 MeV gamma ray. This would produce a distinctive signature that could be detected. They then realised that by adding [cadmium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadmium "Cadmium") salt to their [liquid scintillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_scintillation_counting "Liquid scintillation counting") they would enhance the neutron capture reaction, resulting in a burst of gamma rays with a total energy of 9 MeV. For a neutrino source, they proposed using an atomic bomb. Permission for this was obtained from the laboratory director, [Norris Bradbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norris_Bradbury "Norris Bradbury"). The plan was to detonate a "20-kiloton nuclear bomb, comparable to that dropped on Hiroshima, Japan". The detector was proposed to be dropped at the moment of explosion into a hole 40 meters from the detonation site "to catch the flux at its maximum"; it was named "El Monstro". Work began on digging a shaft for the experiment when [J. M. B. Kellogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._M._B._Kellogg?action=edit&redlink=1 "J. M. B. Kellogg") convinced them to use a [nuclear reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactor "Nuclear reactor") instead of a bomb. Although a less intense source of neutrinos, it had the advantage in allowing for multiple experiments to be carried out over a long period of time.

In 1953, they made their first attempts using one of the large reactors at the [Hanford nuclear site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanford_nuclear_site "Hanford nuclear site") in what is now known as the [Cowan–Reines neutrino experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowan–Reines_neutrino_experiment "Cowan–Reines neutrino experiment"); they named the experiment "Project Poltergeist". Their detector included 300 litres (66 imp gal; 79 US gal) of scintillating fluid and 90 [photomultiplier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photomultiplier "Photomultiplier") tubes, but the effort was frustrated by background noise from cosmic rays. With encouragement from [John A. Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Wheeler "John A. Wheeler"), they tried again in 1955, this time using one of the newer, larger 700 MW reactors at the [Savannah River Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah_River_Site "Savannah River Site") that emitted a high neutrino flux of 1.2 trillion / cm2 sec. They also had a convenient, well-shielded location 11 metres (36 ft) from the reactor and 12 metres (39 ft) underground. On June 14, 1956, they were able to send Pauli a telegram announcing that the neutrino had been found. When Bethe was informed that he had been proven wrong, he said, "Well, you shouldn't believe everything you read in the papers."

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Supernova_SN1987A_in_the_Large_Magellanic_Cloud_-_GPN-2000-000948.jpg/250px-Supernova_SN1987A_in_the_Large_Magellanic_Cloud_-_GPN-2000-000948.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Supernova_SN1987A_in_the_Large_Magellanic_Cloud_-_GPN-2000-000948.jpg)

[Supernova SN1987A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SN_1987A "SN 1987A") (the bright object in the center), as seen through the [Hubble Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_Space_Telescope "Hubble Space Telescope")

From then on Reines dedicated the major part of his career to the study of the neutrino's properties and interactions, which work would influence study of the neutrino for future researchers to come. Cowan left Los Alamos in 1957 to teach at [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University "George Washington University"), ending their collaboration. On the basis of his work in first detecting the [neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino "Neutrino"), Reines became the head of the physics department of [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University "Case Western Reserve University") from 1959 to 1966. At Case, he led a group that was the first to detect neutrinos created in the atmosphere by [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray "Cosmic ray"). Reines had a booming voice, and had been a singer since childhood. During this time, besides performing his duties as a research supervisor and chairman of the physics department, Reines sang in the Cleveland Orchestra Chorus under the direction of [Robert Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Shaw_(conductor) "Robert Shaw (conductor)") in performances with [George Szell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Szell "George Szell") and the [Cleveland Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Orchestra "Cleveland Orchestra").

In the early 1960s, Reines built a detector in the [East Rand gold mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Rand_gold_mine "East Rand gold mine") near [Johannesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg "Johannesburg"), South Africa. The site was chosen because of its depth, 3.5 km; on February 23, 1965, the new detector captured its first atmospheric neutrinos. Reines brought his friends, an engineer August "Gus" Hruschka from the US, they worked together with South African physicist [Friedel Sellschop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedel_Sellschop "Friedel Sellschop") of the [University of Witwatersrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Witwatersrand "University of Witwatersrand"). Equipment was made in the [Case Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Institute "Case Institute"), and 20 tonnes of scintillation fluid in 50 containment tanks were transported from the US. The decision to work in an [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid") racist country was challenged by many colleagues of Reines, he himself said that "science transcended politics". The laboratory team in the mine was led by Reines' graduate students, first by William Kropp, and then by Henry Sobel. The experiment ran from 1963 and was closed in 1971, capturing 167 neutrino events.

In 1966, Reines took most of his neutrino research team with him when he left for the new [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine "University of California, Irvine") (UCI), becoming its first dean of physical sciences. At UCI, Reines extended the research interests of some of his graduate students into the development of [medical radiation detectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_medicine#Diagnostic_medical_imaging "Nuclear medicine"), such as for measuring total radiation delivered to the whole human body in [radiation therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_therapy "Radiation therapy").

Reines had prepared for the possibility of measuring the distant events of a supernova explosion. Supernova explosions are rare, but Reines thought he might be lucky enough to see one in his lifetime, and be able to catch the neutrinos streaming from it in his specially designed detectors. During his wait for a supernova to explode, he put signs on some of his large neutrino detectors, calling them "Supernova Early Warning Systems". In 1987, neutrinos emitted from [Supernova SN1987A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SN_1987A "SN 1987A") were detected by the [Irvine–Michigan–Brookhaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine–Michigan–Brookhaven_(detector) "Irvine–Michigan–Brookhaven (detector)") (IMB) Collaboration, which used an 8,000 ton [Cherenkov detector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherenkov_detector "Cherenkov detector") located in a salt mine near [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland "Cleveland"). Normally, the detectors recorded only a few background events each day. The supernova registered 19 events in just ten seconds. This discovery is regarded as inaugurating the field of [neutrino astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino_astronomy "Neutrino astronomy").

In 1995 Reines was honored, along with [Martin L. Perl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_L._Perl "Martin L. Perl"), with the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for his work with Cowan in first detecting the neutrino. Unfortunately, Cowan had died in 1974 and the Nobel Prize is not awarded posthumously. Reines also received many other awards, including the [J. Robert Oppenheimer Memorial Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer_Memorial_Prize "J. Robert Oppenheimer Memorial Prize") in 1981, the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in 1985, the [Bruno Rossi Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Rossi_Prize "Bruno Rossi Prize") in 1989, the [Michelson–Morley Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelson–Morley_Award "Michelson–Morley Award") in 1990, the [Panofsky Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panofsky_Prize "Panofsky Prize") in 1992, and the [Franklin Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Medal "Franklin Medal") in 1992. He was elected a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1980 and a foreign member of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") in 1994. He remained dean of physical sciences at UCI until 1974, and became a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus "Professor emeritus") in 1988, but he continued teaching until 1991, and remained on UCI's faculty until his death.

## Death

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/UC_Irvine_Reines_Hall.jpg/250px-UC_Irvine_Reines_Hall.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:UC_Irvine_Reines_Hall.jpg)

Frederick Reines Hall at the University of California, Irvine houses the Physics and Astronomy Department, and part of the Chemistry Department.

Reines died after a long illness at the [University of California, Irvine Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine_Medical_Center "University of California, Irvine Medical Center") in [Orange, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange,_California "Orange, California"), on August 26, 1998. He was survived by his wife and children. His papers are compiled in the UCI Libraries. Frederick Reines Hall, which houses the Physics and Astronomy Department at the University of California, Irvine, was named in his honor.
