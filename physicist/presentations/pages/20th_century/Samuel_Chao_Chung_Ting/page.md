**Chao Chung Ting** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 丁肇中; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): *Dīng Zhàozhōng*, born January 27, 1936), also known by his English name **Samuel**, is a Taiwanese-American [particle physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics") who was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1976 with [Burton Richter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_Richter "Burton Richter") for discovering the [subatomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_particle "Subatomic particle") [J/ψ particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J/ψ_particle "J/ψ particle"). He is the Thomas Dudley Cabot Institute Chair at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (MIT).

## Early life and education

Ting was born on January 27, 1936, at the [University of Michigan Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan_Hospital "University of Michigan Hospital") in [Ann Arbor, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor,_Michigan "Ann Arbor, Michigan"), to first generation immigrant parents from [Ju County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ju_County "Ju County"), [Shandong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong "Shandong"), China. His parents, Kuan-hai Ting and [Tsun-ying Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsun-ying_Wong "Tsun-ying Wong"), met and married as graduate students at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan"). When Ting was born, his parents had just earned their master's degrees from the University of Michigan and his father, a civil engineer, had received a professorship to teach at the [China University of Mining and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_University_of_Mining_and_Technology "China University of Mining and Technology").

Ting's parents returned to China two months after his birth. Throughout the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War"), Ting was homeschooled by his parents. After the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War "Chinese Civil War"), Ting moved to Taiwan with his family in 1949 during the [Great Retreat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retreat_of_the_government_of_the_Republic_of_China_to_Taiwan "Retreat of the government of the Republic of China to Taiwan"). He would live there as a Taiwanese *[waishengren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waishengren "Waishengren")* from 1949 to 1956 and conducted most of his formal schooling there. His mother taught psychology as a professor at [National Taiwan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Taiwan_University "National Taiwan University") (NTU).

Ting attended and finished middle school in Taiwan. After graduating from [Taipei Municipal Chien Kuo High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Municipal_Chien_Kuo_High_School "Taipei Municipal Chien Kuo High School"), he attained a perfect score on [the college entrance examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Scholastic_Ability_Test "General Scholastic Ability Test") and entered [National Cheng Kung University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cheng_Kung_University "National Cheng Kung University") in September 1955 to study [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering "Mechanical engineering"). As an undergraduate, he completed one semester at the university with high grades in mathematics and science.

In 1956, Ting, who barely spoke English, returned to the United States at the age of 20 and won a scholarship to attend the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan"), where a family friend, G. G. Brown (dean of the [College of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan_College_of_Engineering "University of Michigan College of Engineering")), invited him to enroll. He studied engineering, [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"), and [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") there, completing his bachelor's degrees, master's degree, and doctorate in only six years. He earned two [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science_in_Information_Technology "Bachelor of Science in Information Technology") (B.S.) degrees in engineering, mathematics, and physics in 1959, a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") (M.S.) in physics in 1960, and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in physics in 1962. His doctoral studies were funded by a grant by the [United States Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Atomic_Energy_Commission "United States Atomic Energy Commission").

As a graduate student at Michigan, Ting befriended [Homer Neal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer_Neal "Homer Neal"), a classmate. One of Ting's doctoral advisors, the physicist [Lawrence W. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_W._Jones "Lawrence W. Jones"), described him as "very talented academically" and "a young man in a hurry".

## Career

In 1963, Ting worked at the [European Organization for Nuclear Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN") (CERN). From 1965, he taught at [Columbia University in the City of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") and worked at the [Deutsches Elektronen-Synchrotron (DESY)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DESY "DESY") in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany"). Since 1969, Ting has been a professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (MIT).

Ting received the [Ernest Orlando Lawrence Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Orlando_Lawrence_Award "Ernest Orlando Lawrence Award") in 1976, [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1976, [Eringen Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eringen_Medal "Eringen Medal") in 1977, DeGaspari Award in Science from the [Government of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Italy "Government of Italy") in 1988, Gold Medal for Science from [Brescia, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia "Brescia") in 1988, and the [NASA Public Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Exceptional_Public_Service_Medal "NASA Exceptional Public Service Medal") in 2001.

## Nobel Prize

In 1976, Ting was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), which he shared with [Burton Richter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_Richter "Burton Richter") of the [Stanford Linear Accelerator Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Linear_Accelerator_Center "Stanford Linear Accelerator Center"), for the discovery of the [J/ψ meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J/psi_meson "J/psi meson") nuclear particle. They were chosen for the award, in the words of the Nobel committee, "for their pioneering work in the discovery of a heavy elementary particle of a new kind." The discovery was made in 1974 when Ting was heading a research team at MIT exploring new regimes of [high energy particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_energy_physics "High energy physics").

Ting gave his Nobel Prize acceptance speech in [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese "Standard Chinese"). Although there had been Chinese Nobel Prize recipients before ([Tsung-Dao Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsung-Dao_Lee "Tsung-Dao Lee") and [Chen Ning Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Ning_Yang "Chen Ning Yang")), none had previously delivered the acceptance speech in [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Language "Chinese Language"). In his Nobel banquet speech, Ting emphasized the importance of experimental work:

:   *In reality, a theory in natural science cannot be without experimental foundations; physics, in particular, comes from experimental work. I hope that awarding the Nobel Prize to me will awaken the interest of students from the developing nations so that they will realize the importance of experimental work.*

## Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Samuel_Ting_nobel_laureate.jpg/250px-Samuel_Ting_nobel_laureate.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Samuel_Ting_nobel_laureate.jpg)

Ting in the Space Station Processing Facility, 2000

In 1995, not long after the cancellation of the [Superconducting Super Collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_Super_Collider "Superconducting Super Collider") project had severely reduced the possibilities for experimental high-energy physics on Earth, Ting proposed the [Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Magnetic_Spectrometer "Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer"), a space-borne [cosmic-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray "Cosmic ray") detector. The proposal was accepted and he became the principal investigator and has been directing the development since then. A prototype, *AMS-01*, was flown and tested on [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle") mission [STS-91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-91 "STS-91") in 1998. The main mission, *AMS-02*, was then planned for launch by the Shuttle and mounting on the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station").

This project is a massive $2 billion undertaking involving 500 scientists from 56 institutions and 16 countries. After the 2003 [Space Shuttle Columbia disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Columbia_disaster "Space Shuttle Columbia disaster"), [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") announced that the Shuttle was to be retired by 2010 and that *AMS-02* was not on the manifest of any of the remaining Shuttle flights. Dr. Ting was forced to (successfully) lobby the [United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress") and the public to secure an additional Shuttle flight dedicated to this project. Also during this time, Ting had to deal with numerous technical problems in fabricating and qualifying the large, extremely sensitive and delicate detector module for space. *AMS-02* was successfully launched on Shuttle mission [STS-134](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-134 "STS-134") on May 16, 2011, and was installed on the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station") on May 19, 2011.

## Research

- Discovery of nuclear anti-matter (the anti-deuteron).
- Measuring the size of the [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") family (the electron, the muon, and the tau) showing that the electron family has zero size (with a radius smaller than 10−17 cm).
- Precision study of [light rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rays "Light rays") and massive light rays showing that light rays and massive light rays (vector mesons) can transform into each other at high energies and providing a critical verification of the [quark model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark_model "Quark model").
- Precision measurement of the radius of the [atomic nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nuclei "Atomic nuclei").
- Discovery of a new kind of matter (the [J particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_particle "J particle")) at the [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory"). The [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") was awarded to Ting for this discovery.
- Discovery of the [gluon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluon "Gluon") (the particle responsible for transmitting the nuclear force).
- A systematic study of the properties of gluons.
- A precision measurement of [muon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muon "Muon") charge asymmetry, demonstrating for the first time the validity of the Standard [Electroweak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroweak "Electroweak") Model ([Steven Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Weinberg "Steven Weinberg"), [Sheldon Glashow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Glashow "Sheldon Glashow") and [Abdus Salam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdus_Salam "Abdus Salam")).
- Determination of the number of electron families and [neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino "Neutrino") species in the Universe and the precision verification of the [Electroweak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroweak "Electroweak") Unification Theory.
- Proposed, constructed and leads the [Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Magnetic_Spectrometer "Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer") (AMS) experiment on the International Space Station involving the participation of a 16 nation collaboration searching for the existence of [antimatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimatter "Antimatter"), the origin of [dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter "Dark matter") and the properties of [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_rays "Cosmic rays").
- Development of the first large [superconducting magnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_magnet "Superconducting magnet") for space application.
- AMS results, based on 15 years in space and more than 260 billion cosmic rays, have changed our understanding of the cosmos.

## Personal life

Ting lived in a turbulent age during his childhood and his family was a big influence on him. In his biographical for the Nobel Prize, he recalled:

:   *Since both my parents were working, I was brought up by my maternal grandmother. My maternal grandfather lost his life during the first Chinese Revolution. After that, at the age of thirty-three, my grandmother decided to go to school, became a teacher, and brought my mother up alone. When I was young I often heard stories from my mother and grandmother recalling the difficult lives they had during that turbulent period and the efforts they made to provide my mother with a good education. Both of them were daring, original, and determined people, and they have left an indelible impression on me.*

:   *When I was twenty years old I decided to return to the United States for a better education. My parents' friend, G.G. Brown, Dean of the School of Engineering, University of Michigan, told my parents I would be welcome to stay with him and his family. At that time I knew very little English and had no idea of the cost of living in the United States. In China, I had read that many American students go through college on their own resources. I informed my parents that I would do likewise. I arrived at the Detroit airport on 6 September 1956 with $100, which at the time seemed more than adequate. I was somewhat frightened, did not know anyone, and communication was difficult.*

Ting is the eldest son of his family. He has one brother, Ting Chao-hua (丁肇華) and one sister, Ting Chao-min (丁肇民). In an interview with [China Central Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Central_Television "China Central Television"), he explained that the combination of his siblings' and his name is the first three characters of "中華民國" ([Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_(1912–1949) "Republic of China (1912–1949)")). His parents named them after the country to commemorate their grandfather, who was a [martyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr_(politics) "Martyr (politics)") in the [Xinhai Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhai_Revolution "Xinhai Revolution").

In 1960, Ting married Kay Louise Kuhne, an architect, and together they had two daughters: Jeanne Ting Chowning and Amy Ting. In 1985, he married Dr. Susan Carol Marks, and they had one son, Christopher, born in 1986.
