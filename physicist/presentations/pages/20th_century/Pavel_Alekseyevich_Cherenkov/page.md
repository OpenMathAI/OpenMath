**Pavel Alekseyevich Cherenkov** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Па́вел Алексе́евич Черенко́в [[ˈpavʲɪl ɐlʲɪkˈsʲe(j)ɪvʲɪtɕ tɕɪrʲɪnˈkof]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Russian "Help:IPA/Russian"); 28 July 1904 – 6 January 1990) was a Soviet [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared the 1958 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Ilya Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Frank "Ilya Frank") and [Igor Tamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Tamm "Igor Tamm") "for the discovery and interpretation of the [Cherenkov effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherenkov_effect "Cherenkov effect")".

## Biography

Cherenkov was born into a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians "Russians") family on July 28, 1904, to Alexey Cherenkov and Mariya Cherenkova, in the small village of [Novaya Chigla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Chigla "Novaya Chigla") in [Voronezh Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh_Governorate "Voronezh Governorate") of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire").

In 1928, he graduated from the Department of Physics and Mathematics of [Voronezh State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh_State_University "Voronezh State University"). In 1930, he took a post as a senior researcher in the [Lebedev Physical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebedev_Physical_Institute "Lebedev Physical Institute"). That same year he married Maria Putintseva, daughter of A. M. Putintsev, a professor of [Russian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_literature "Russian literature"). They had a son, Alexey, and a daughter, Yelena.

Cherenkov was promoted to section leader, and in 1940 was awarded the degree of Doctor of Physico-Mathematical Sciences. In 1953, he was confirmed as Professor of Experimental Physics. Starting in 1959, he headed the institute's [photo-meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photo-meson "Photo-meson") processes laboratory. He remained a professor for fourteen years. In 1970, he became an [academician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academician "Academician") of the [USSR Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Academy_of_Sciences "USSR Academy of Sciences").

Cherenkov died in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow") on 6 January 1990 and was buried in [Novodevichy Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novodevichy_Cemetery "Novodevichy Cemetery").

## Discoveries in physics

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/Advanced_Test_Reactor.jpg/250px-Advanced_Test_Reactor.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Advanced_Test_Reactor.jpg)

[Cherenkov radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherenkov_radiation "Cherenkov radiation") is named for his discovery of the phenomenon; pictured here glowing is the core of the [Advanced Test Reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Test_Reactor "Advanced Test Reactor").

In 1934, while working under [S. I. Vavilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._I._Vavilov "S. I. Vavilov"), Cherenkov observed the emission of blue light from a bottle of water subjected to radioactive bombardment. This phenomenon, associated with charged [subatomic particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_particle "Subatomic particle") moving at [velocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocities "Velocities") greater than the [phase velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_velocity "Phase velocity") of light, proved to be of great importance in subsequent experimental work in [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics "Nuclear physics"), and for the study of [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray "Cosmic ray"). Eponymously, it was dubbed the [Cherenkov effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherenkov_radiation "Cherenkov radiation"), as was the [Cherenkov detector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherenkov_detector "Cherenkov detector"), which has become a standard piece of equipment in particle-physics research for observing the existence and velocity of high-speed particles. Such a device was installed in [Sputnik 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik_3 "Sputnik 3").

Pavel Cherenkov also shared in the development and construction of [electron accelerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator "Particle accelerator") and in the investigation of photo-nuclear and [photo-meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photo-meson "Photo-meson") reactions.

## Awards and honours

Cherenkov was awarded two [Stalin Prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Stalin_Prize "State Stalin Prize"), the first in 1946, sharing the honor with Vavilov, [Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Frank "Ilya Frank") and [Tamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Yevgenyevich_Tamm "Igor Yevgenyevich Tamm"), and another in 1952. He was also awarded the [USSR State Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize "USSR State Prize") in 1977. In 1958, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for the discovery of the Cherenkov effect. He was also awarded the Soviet Union's [Hero of Socialist Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_Socialist_Labour "Hero of Socialist Labour") title in 1984. Cherenkov was a member of the [Communist Party of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union "Communist Party of the Soviet Union") since 1946.

## In popular culture

The novel  *[Ghost Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_Fleet_(novel) "Ghost Fleet (novel)")* makes the claim that many believe the *[Star Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek "Star Trek")* character [Pavel Chekov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Chekov "Pavel Chekov") is named after Pavel Cherenkov.

In *[Starship Troopers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_Troopers "Starship Troopers")*, spaceships travel faster than light using Cherenkov Drive.
