**Arthur Holly Compton** (September 10, 1892 – March 15, 1962) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared the 1927 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [C. T. R. Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._T._R._Wilson "C. T. R. Wilson") for his discovery of the [Compton effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_effect "Compton effect"), which demonstrated the particle nature of [electromagnetic radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_radiation "Electromagnetic radiation"). It was a significant discovery at the time; the wave nature of light had been well-demonstrated, but the idea that light had both [wave and particle properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave–particle_duality "Wave–particle duality") was not easily accepted.

In 1919, Compton was awarded one of the first two [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council "United States National Research Council") Fellowships that allowed students to study abroad. He chose to go to the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge")'s [Cavendish Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Laboratory "Cavendish Laboratory") in England, where he studied the [scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering "Scattering") and [absorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorption_(electromagnetic_radiation) "Absorption (electromagnetic radiation)") of [gamma rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_ray "Gamma ray"). Further research along these lines led to the discovery of the Compton effect.

Compton used X-rays to investigate [ferromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism "Ferromagnetism"), concluding that it was a result of the alignment of [electron spins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_spin "Electron spin"), and studied [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray "Cosmic ray"), discovering that they were made principally of positively charged particles.

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Compton was a key figure in the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project") that developed the first [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon"). His reports were important in launching the project. In 1942, he became a member of the [S-1 Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-1_Executive_Committee "S-1 Executive Committee"), and then head of the "X" projects overseeing the [Metallurgical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgical_Laboratory "Metallurgical Laboratory"), with responsibility for producing [nuclear reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactor "Nuclear reactor") to convert [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium "Uranium") into [plutonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutonium "Plutonium"), finding ways to separate the plutonium from the uranium and to design an atomic bomb. Compton oversaw [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi")'s creation of [Chicago Pile-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Pile-1 "Chicago Pile-1"), the first nuclear reactor, which went critical on December 2, 1942. The Metallurgical Laboratory was also responsible for the design and operation of the [X-10 Graphite Reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-10_Graphite_Reactor "X-10 Graphite Reactor") at [Oak Ridge, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Ridge,_Tennessee "Oak Ridge, Tennessee"). Plutonium began being produced in the [Hanford Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanford_Site "Hanford Site") reactors in 1945.

After the War, Compton became Chancellor of [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis"). During his tenure, the university formally desegregated its undergraduate divisions, named its first female full professor, and enrolled a record number of students after wartime veterans returned to the United States.

## Education

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Portrait_of_Compton_Family_1913.jpg/250px-Portrait_of_Compton_Family_1913.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Portrait_of_Compton_Family_1913.jpg)

Arthur, top middle, with his siblings and parents

Arthur Holly Compton was born on September 10, 1892, in [Wooster, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooster,_Ohio "Wooster, Ohio"), the son of Elias Compton and Otelia Catherine Augspurger, who was named American Mother of the Year in 1939 and was of German [Mennonite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mennonite "Mennonite") descent.
They were an academic family. Elias was Dean of the University of Wooster (now the [College of Wooster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Wooster "College of Wooster")), which Arthur also attended. Arthur's eldest brother, [Karl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Compton "Karl Compton"), who also attended Wooster, earned a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in physics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in 1912, and was President of the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") from 1930 to 1948. His second brother, [Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilson_Martindale_Compton "Wilson Martindale Compton"), likewise attended Wooster, earned his Ph.D. in economics from Princeton in 1916 and was President of the State College of Washington (now [Washington State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_University "Washington State University")) from 1944 to 1951. All three brothers were members of the [Alpha Tau Omega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Tau_Omega "Alpha Tau Omega") fraternity.

Compton was initially interested in astronomy, and took a photograph of [Halley's Comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halley's_Comet "Halley's Comet") in 1910. Around 1913, he described an experiment where an examination of the motion of water in a circular tube demonstrated the rotation of the earth, a device now known as the [Compton generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_generator "Compton generator"). That year, he graduated from Wooster with a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S. "B.S.") and entered Princeton, where he received his [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.A. "M.A.") in 1914. Compton then studied for his Ph.D. in physics under the supervision of Hereward Lester Cooke, writing his thesis titled *The Intensity of X-Ray Reflection, and the Distribution of the Electrons in Atoms*.

When Arthur Compton earned his Ph.D. in 1916, he, Karl, and Wilson became the first group of three brothers to earn PhDs from Princeton. Later, they would become the first such trio to simultaneously head American colleges. Their sister, Mary, married a missionary, C. Herbert Rice, who became the Principal of [Forman Christian College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forman_Christian_College "Forman Christian College") in [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore "Lahore"). In June 1916, Compton married Betty Charity McCloskey, a Wooster classmate and fellow graduate. They had two sons, Arthur Alan Compton and [John Joseph Compton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Joseph_Compton "John Joseph Compton").

In 1916, Compton became a physics instructor at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota"). In 1917, he became a research engineer at the [Westinghouse Lamp Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Electric_(1886) "Westinghouse Electric (1886)") in [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh "Pittsburgh"), where he worked on the development of the [sodium-vapor lamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium-vapor_lamp "Sodium-vapor lamp"). During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), he developed aircraft instrumentation for the [Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Corps_(United_States_Army) "Signal Corps (United States Army)").

In 1919, Compton was awarded one of the first two [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council "United States National Research Council") Fellowships that allowed students to study abroad. He chose to go to the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge")'s [Cavendish Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Laboratory "Cavendish Laboratory") in England. Working with [George Paget Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Paget_Thomson "George Paget Thomson"), the son of [J. J. Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Thomson "J. J. Thomson"), Compton studied the [scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering "Scattering") and [absorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorption_(electromagnetic_radiation) "Absorption (electromagnetic radiation)") of [gamma rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_rays "Gamma rays"). He observed that the scattered rays were more easily absorbed than the original source. Compton was greatly impressed by the Cavendish scientists—especially [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford "Ernest Rutherford"), [Charles Galton Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Galton_Darwin "Charles Galton Darwin"), and [Arthur Eddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Eddington "Arthur Eddington")—and he ultimately named his second son after J. J. Thomson.

From 1926 to 1927, Compton taught at the Department of Chemistry of the [University of the Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Punjab "University of the Punjab") where he was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship "Guggenheim Fellowship").

For a time, Compton was a deacon at a Baptist church. "Science can have no quarrel", he said, "with a religion which postulates a God to whom men are as His children."

## Career and research

### Compton effect

Returning to the United States, Compton was appointed Wayman Crow Professor of Physics, and head of the Department of Physics at [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis") in 1920. In 1922, he found that [X-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray "X-ray") [quanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum "Quantum") scattered by free electrons had longer [wavelengths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength "Wavelength") and, in accordance with [Planck's relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_constant "Planck constant"), less [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy "Energy") than the incoming X-rays, the surplus energy having been transferred to the electrons. This discovery, known as the "[Compton effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_effect "Compton effect")" or "Compton scattering", demonstrated the [particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics") concept of [electromagnetic radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_radiation "Electromagnetic radiation").

In 1923, Compton published a paper in the *[Physical Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Review "Physical Review")* that explained the X-ray shift by attributing particle-like momentum to [photons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon "Photon"), something Einstein had invoked for his 1905 Nobel Prize-winning explanation of the [photoelectric effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoelectric_effect "Photoelectric effect"). First postulated by [Max Planck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck "Max Planck") in 1900, these were conceptualized as elements of light "quantized" by containing a specific amount of energy depending only on the frequency of the light. In his paper, Compton derived the mathematical relationship between the shift in wavelength and the scattering angle of the X-rays by assuming that each scattered X-ray photon interacted with only one electron. The quantity *h*⁄*mec* is known as the [Compton wavelength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_wavelength "Compton wavelength") of the electron; it is equal to 2.43×10−12 m. The wavelength shift *λ′* − *λ* lies between zero (for *θ* = 0°) and twice the Compton wavelength of the electron (for *θ* = 180°). He found that some X-rays experienced no wavelength shift despite being scattered through large angles; in each of these cases the photon failed to eject an electron. Thus the magnitude of the shift is related not to the Compton wavelength of the electron, but to the Compton wavelength of the entire atom, which can be upwards of 10,000 times smaller.

"When I presented my results at a meeting of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") in 1923", Compton later recalled, "it initiated the most hotly contested scientific controversy that I have ever known." The wave nature of light had been well demonstrated, and the idea that it could have a dual nature was not easily accepted. It was particularly telling that diffraction in a crystal lattice could only be explained with reference to its wave nature. It earned Compton the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1927. Compton and Alfred W. Simon developed the method for observing at the same instant individual scattered X-ray photons and the [recoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recoil "Recoil") [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron"). In Germany, [Walther Bothe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Bothe "Walther Bothe") and [Hans Geiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Geiger "Hans Geiger") independently developed a similar method.

### X-rays

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Alvarez_and_Compton.jpeg/250px-Alvarez_and_Compton.jpeg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Alvarez_and_Compton.jpeg)

Compton at the University of Chicago with graduate student [Luis Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Walter_Alvarez "Luis Walter Alvarez") next to his cosmic ray telescope, 1933.

In 1923, Compton moved to the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") as Professor of Physics, a position he would occupy for the next 22 years. In 1925, he demonstrated that the scattering of 130,000-volt X-rays from the first sixteen elements in the [periodic table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table "Periodic table") (hydrogen through sulfur) were [polarized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polarization_(waves) "Polarization (waves)"), a result predicted by J. J. Thomson. [William Duane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Duane_(physicist) "William Duane (physicist)") from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") spearheaded an effort to prove that Compton's interpretation of the Compton effect was wrong. Duane carried out a series of experiments to disprove Compton, but instead found evidence that Compton was correct. In 1924, Duane conceded that this was the case.

Compton investigated the effect of X-rays on the sodium and chlorine nuclei in [salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt "Salt"). He used X-rays to investigate [ferromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism "Ferromagnetism"), concluding that it was a result of the alignment of [electron spins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_spin "Electron spin"). In 1926, he became a consultant for the Lamp Department at [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric "General Electric"). In 1934, he returned to England as Eastman visiting professor at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University "Oxford University"). While there, General Electric asked him to report on activities at [General Electric Company plc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_Company_plc "General Electric Company plc")'s research laboratory at [Wembley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley "Wembley"). Compton was intrigued by the possibilities of the research there into [fluorescent lamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescent_lamp "Fluorescent lamp"). His report prompted a research program in America that developed it.

Compton's first book, *X-Rays and Electrons*, was published in 1926. In it he showed how to calculate the densities of diffracting materials from their X-ray diffraction patterns. He revised his book with the help of [Samuel K. Allison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_K._Allison "Samuel K. Allison") to produce *X-Rays in Theory and Experiment* (1935). This work remained a standard reference for the next three decades.

### Cosmic rays

By the early 1930s, Compton had become interested in [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray "Cosmic ray"). At the time, their existence was known but their origin and nature remained speculative. Their presence could be detected using a spherical "bomb" containing compressed air or argon gas and measuring its electrical conductivity. Trips to Europe, India, Mexico, Peru and Australia gave Compton the opportunity to measure cosmic rays at different altitudes and latitudes. Along with other groups who made observations around the globe, they found that cosmic rays were 15% more intense at the poles than at the equator. In September 1932, Compton attributed this to the effect of cosmic rays being made principally of charged particles, rather than photons as [Robert Millikan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Millikan "Robert Millikan") had suggested, with the latitude effect being due to [Earth's magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_magnetic_field "Earth's magnetic field"). This resulted in a public altercation between Millikan and Compton in December. Ultimately, Compton was proven correct.

## Manhattan Project

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Compton_ID_badge.JPG/250px-Compton_ID_badge.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Compton_ID_badge.JPG)

Arthur Compton's ID badge from the Hanford Site. For security reasons he used a pseudonym.

In April 1941, [Vannevar Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vannevar_Bush "Vannevar Bush"), head of the wartime [National Defense Research Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Research_Committee "National Defense Research Committee") (NDRC), created a special committee headed by Compton to report on the NDRC uranium program. Compton's report, which was submitted in May 1941, foresaw the prospects of developing [radiological weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiological_weapon "Radiological weapon"), [nuclear propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_propulsion "Nuclear propulsion") for ships, and [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons "Nuclear weapons") using [uranium-235](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium-235 "Uranium-235") or the recently discovered [plutonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutonium "Plutonium"). In October he wrote another report on the practicality of an atomic bomb. For this report, he worked with [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi") on calculations of the [critical mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_mass "Critical mass") of uranium-235, conservatively estimating it to be between 20 kilograms (44 lb) and 2 tonnes (2.0 long tons; 2.2 short tons). He also discussed the prospects for [uranium enrichment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium_enrichment "Uranium enrichment") with [Harold Urey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Urey "Harold Urey"), spoke with [Eugene Wigner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Wigner "Eugene Wigner") about how plutonium might be produced in a [nuclear reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactor "Nuclear reactor"), and with [Robert Serber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Serber "Robert Serber") about how the plutonium produced in a reactor might be separated from uranium. His report, submitted in November, stated that a bomb was feasible, although he was more conservative about its destructive power than [Mark Oliphant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Oliphant "Mark Oliphant") and his British colleagues.

The final draft of Compton's November report made no mention of using plutonium, but after discussing the latest research with [Ernest Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Lawrence "Ernest Lawrence"), Compton became convinced that a plutonium bomb was also feasible. In December, Compton was placed in charge of the plutonium project. He hoped to achieve a controlled [chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_reaction "Chain reaction") by January 1943, and to have a bomb by January 1945. To tackle the problem, he had the research groups working on plutonium and nuclear reactor design at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), Princeton University and the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), concentrated together as the [Metallurgical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgical_Laboratory "Metallurgical Laboratory") in Chicago. Its objectives were to produce reactors to convert uranium to plutonium, to find ways to chemically separate the plutonium from the uranium, and to design and build an [atomic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bomb "Atomic bomb").

In June 1942, the [United States Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers "United States Army Corps of Engineers") assumed control of the nuclear weapons program and Compton's Metallurgical Laboratory became part of the Manhattan Project. That month, Compton gave [Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Oppenheimer "Robert Oppenheimer") responsibility for bomb design. It fell to Compton to decide which of the different types of reactor designs that the Metallurgical Laboratory scientists had devised should be pursued, even though a successful reactor had not yet been built.

When labor disputes delayed construction of the Metallurgical Laboratory's new home in the [Argonne Forest preserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palos_Forest_Preserves "Palos Forest Preserves"), Compton decided to build [Chicago Pile-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Pile-1 "Chicago Pile-1"), the first nuclear reactor, under the stands at [Stagg Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagg_Field "Stagg Field"). Under Fermi's direction, it went critical on December 2, 1942. Compton arranged for [Mallinckrodt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallinckrodt "Mallinckrodt") to undertake the purification of uranium ore, and with [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont") to build the plutonium semi-works at [Oak Ridge, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Ridge,_Tennessee "Oak Ridge, Tennessee").

A major crisis for the plutonium program occurred in July 1943, when [Emilio Segrè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Segrè "Emilio Segrè")'s group confirmed that plutonium created in the [X-10 Graphite Reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-10_Graphite_Reactor "X-10 Graphite Reactor") at Oak Ridge contained high levels of [plutonium-240](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutonium-240 "Plutonium-240"). Its [spontaneous fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_fission "Spontaneous fission") ruled out the use of plutonium in a [gun-type nuclear weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun-type_nuclear_weapon "Gun-type nuclear weapon"). Oppenheimer's [Los Alamos Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_Laboratory "Los Alamos Laboratory") met the challenge by designing and building an [implosion-type nuclear weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implosion-type_nuclear_weapon "Implosion-type nuclear weapon").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Arthur_H._Compton_House_%287373049818%29.jpg/250px-Arthur_H._Compton_House_%287373049818%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Arthur_H._Compton_House_(7373049818).jpg)

Compton's house in Chicago, now a national landmark.

Compton was at the [Hanford site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanford_site "Hanford site") in September 1944 to watch the first reactor being brought online. The first batch of uranium slugs was fed into Reactor B at Hanford in November 1944, and shipments of plutonium to Los Alamos began in February 1945. Throughout the war, Compton would remain a prominent scientific adviser and administrator. In 1945, he served, along with Lawrence, Oppenheimer, and Fermi, on the Scientific Panel that recommended military use of the atomic bomb against Japan. He was awarded the [Medal for Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_for_Merit "Medal for Merit") for his services to the Manhattan Project.

## Later life

After the war ended, Compton resigned his chair as Charles H. Swift Distinguished Service Professor of Physics at the University of Chicago and returned to Washington University in St. Louis, where he was inaugurated as the university's ninth chancellor in 1946. During Compton's time as chancellor, the university formally desegregated its undergraduate divisions in 1952, named its first female full professor, and enrolled record numbers of students as wartime veterans returned to the United States. His reputation and connections in national scientific circles allowed him to recruit many nationally renowned scientific researchers to the university. Despite Compton's accomplishments, he was criticized then, and subsequently by historians, for moving too slowly toward full [racial integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_integration "Racial integration"), making Washington University the last major institution of higher learning in St. Louis to open its doors to [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American "African American").

During the period, in 1953, he is credited with the prototyping of the "traffic control bump", the predecessor of the speed bump, in order to slow down traffic near the university and make roads overall safer by slowing down vehicles.

Compton retired as chancellor in 1954, but remained on the faculty as Distinguished Service Professor of Natural Philosophy until his retirement from the full-time faculty in 1961. In retirement he wrote *Atomic Quest*, a personal account of his role in the Manhattan Project, which was published in 1956.

Compton died of a [cerebral hemorrhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_hemorrhage "Cerebral hemorrhage") on March 15, 1962, in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California "Berkeley, California"), at the age of 69. He was outlived by his wife (who died in 1980) and sons. Compton is buried in the Wooster Cemetery in [Wooster, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooster,_Ohio "Wooster, Ohio"). Before his death, he was professor-at-large at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") for spring 1962.

## Philosophy

Compton was one of a handful of scientists and philosophers to propose a two-stage model of [free will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_will "Free will"). Others include [William James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_James "William James"), [Henri Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Poincaré "Henri Poincaré"), [Karl Popper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Popper "Karl Popper"), [Henry Margenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Margenau "Henry Margenau"), and [Daniel Dennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Dennett "Daniel Dennett"). In 1931, Compton championed the idea of human freedom based on [quantum indeterminacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_indeterminacy "Quantum indeterminacy"), and invented the notion of amplification of microscopic quantum events to bring [chance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminism "Indeterminism") into the macroscopic world. In his somewhat bizarre mechanism, he imagined sticks of dynamite attached to his amplifier, anticipating the [Schrödinger's cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger's_cat "Schrödinger's cat") paradox, which was published in 1935.

Reacting to criticisms that his ideas made chance the direct cause of people's actions, Compton clarified the two-stage nature of his idea in an *[Atlantic Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Monthly "Atlantic Monthly")* article in 1955. First there is a range of random possible events, then one adds a determining factor in the act of choice.

> A set of known physical conditions is not adequate to specify precisely what a forthcoming event will be. These conditions, insofar as they can be known, define instead a range of possible events from among which some particular event will occur. When one exercises freedom, by his act of choice he is himself adding a factor not supplied by the physical conditions and is thus himself determining what will occur. That he does so is known only to the person himself. From the outside one can see in his act only the working of physical law. It is the inner knowledge that he is in fact doing what he intends to do that tells the actor himself that he is free.

## Religious views

Compton was a [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism "Presbyterianism"). His father Elias was an ordained Presbyterian minister.

Compton lectured on a "Man's Place in God's World" at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"), [Western Theological Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Theological_Seminary "Western Theological Seminary") and the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan") in 1934–35. The lectures formed the basis of his book *The Freedom of Man*. His chapter "Death, or Life Eternal?" argued for Christian immortality and quoted verses from the Bible. From 1948 to 1962, Compton was an elder of the [Second Presbyterian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Presbyterian_Church_(St._Louis,_Missouri) "Second Presbyterian Church (St. Louis, Missouri)") in St. Louis. In his later years, he co-authored the book *Man's Destiny in Eternity*. Compton set [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus") as the center of his faith in God's eternal plan. He once commented that he could see Jesus' spirit at work in the world as an aspect of God alive in men and women.

## Commemoration

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/CGRO_s37-96-010.jpg/250px-CGRO_s37-96-010.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:CGRO_s37-96-010.jpg)

The [Compton Gamma Ray Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_Gamma_Ray_Observatory "Compton Gamma Ray Observatory"), released into Earth's orbit in 1991.

[Compton crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_(crater) "Compton (crater)") on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon") is co-named for Compton and his brother, Karl. The physics research building at Washington University in St Louis is named in his honor, as is the university's top fellowship for undergraduate students studying math, physics, or planetary science. Compton invented a more gentle, elongated, and ramped version of the [speed bump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_bump "Speed bump") called the "Holly hump", many of which were installed on the roads of the Washington University campus. However, they were later removed. The University of Chicago remembered Compton and his achievements by dedicating the [Arthur H. Compton House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_H._Compton_House "Arthur H. Compton House") in his honor. It is now listed as a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark "National Historic Landmark"). Compton also has a star on the [St. Louis Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Walk_of_Fame "St. Louis Walk of Fame"). [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA")'s [Compton Gamma Ray Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_Gamma_Ray_Observatory "Compton Gamma Ray Observatory") was named in honor of Compton. The Compton effect is central to the [gamma ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_ray "Gamma ray") detection instruments aboard the observatory.
