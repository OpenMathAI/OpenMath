**Louis Victor Pierre Raymond, 7th Duc de Broglie** (French: [[də bʁɔj]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De_Broglie.ogg "File:De Broglie.ogg"); 15 August 1892 – 19 March 1987) was a French theoretical [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and [aristocrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy_(class) "Aristocracy (class)") known for his contributions to [quantum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_quantum_theory "Old quantum theory"). In his 1924 Ph.D. thesis, he postulated the wave nature of [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") and suggested that [all matter has wave properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter_wave "Matter wave"). This concept is known as the [de Broglie hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Broglie_hypothesis "De Broglie hypothesis"), an example of [wave–particle duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave–particle_duality "Wave–particle duality"), and forms a central part of the theory of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"). In 1929, de Broglie won the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), after the wave-like behaviour of matter was [experimentally confirmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter_wave#Experimental_confirmation "Matter wave") in 1927. This [confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davisson-Germer_experiment "Davisson-Germer experiment") earned [George Paget Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Paget_Thomson "George Paget Thomson") and [Clinton Davisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Davisson "Clinton Davisson") the Nobel in 1937.

The wave-like behaviour of particles theorized by de Broglie was used by [Erwin Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger "Erwin Schrödinger") in his formulation of [wave mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_equation "Schrödinger equation").
De Broglie presented an alternative interpretation of these mechanics called the [pilot-wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot-wave "Pilot-wave") concept at the 1927 [Solvay Conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvay_Conference "Solvay Conference"), but then abandoned it. In 1952, [David Bohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bohm "David Bohm") developed a new form of the concept which became known as the [de Broglie–Bohm theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Broglie–Bohm_theory "De Broglie–Bohm theory"). De Broglie revisited the idea in 1956, creating another version that incorporated ideas from Bohm and [Jean-Pierre Vigier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Vigier "Jean-Pierre Vigier").

Louis de Broglie was the sixteenth member elected to occupy [seat 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_Académie_française#Seat_1 "List of members of the Académie française") of the [Académie Française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_Française "Académie Française") in 1944, and served as Perpetual Secretary of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences"). De Broglie became the first high-level scientist to call for establishment of a multi-national laboratory, a proposal that led to the establishment of the European Organization for Nuclear Research ([CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN")). Among his publications were *The Revolution in Physics* and *Matter and Light*. He was honorary president of the French Association of Science Writers and received the inaugural [Kalinga Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_Prize "Kalinga Prize") from [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") for his efforts to popularize science.

## Biography

### Family and education

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Portrait_of_Fran%C3%A7ois_Marie_de_Broglie%2C_Duke_of_Broglie%2C_Marshal_of_France_%28member_of_the_circle_of_Hyacinthe_Rigaud%29.jpg/250px-Portrait_of_Fran%C3%A7ois_Marie_de_Broglie%2C_Duke_of_Broglie%2C_Marshal_of_France_%28member_of_the_circle_of_Hyacinthe_Rigaud%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Portrait_of_François_Marie_de_Broglie,_Duke_of_Broglie,_Marshal_of_France_(member_of_the_circle_of_Hyacinthe_Rigaud).jpg)

[François-Marie, 1st duc de Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François-Marie,_1st_duc_de_Broglie "François-Marie, 1st duc de Broglie") (1671–1745), ancestor of Louis de Broglie and [Marshal of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshal_of_France "Marshal of France") under [Louis XV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XV "Louis XV")

Louis Victor Pierre Raymond was born on 15 August 1892 in [Dieppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieppe "Dieppe"), France, into the aristocratic [House of Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Broglie "House of Broglie"), a noble family of Italian origin, whose representatives for several centuries occupied important military and political posts in France. His father, [Louis-Alphonse-Victor, 5th duc de Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor,_5th_duc_de_Broglie "Victor, 5th duc de Broglie"), was married to Pauline d'Armaille, the granddaughter of the [Napoleonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon "Napoleon") General [Philippe Paul, comte de Ségur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Paul,_comte_de_Ségur "Philippe Paul, comte de Ségur"), and his wife, the biographer [Marie Célestine Amélie d'Armaillé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Célestine_Amélie_d'Armaillé "Marie Célestine Amélie d'Armaillé"). They had five children; in addition to Louis, these were: Albertina (1872–1946), subsequently the Marquise de Luppé; [Maurice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_de_Broglie "Maurice de Broglie") (1875–1960), subsequently a famous experimental physicist; Philip (1881–1890), who died two years before the birth of Louis, and Pauline, Comtesse de Pange (1888–1972), subsequently a famous writer. Per *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*, "a great-grandfather fought on [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington "George Washington")'s side in the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War "American Revolutionary War") as the chief lieutenant of the [Marquis de Lafayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquis_de_Lafayette "Marquis de Lafayette")."

De Broglie was said to have been a good looking and charming child with an aptitude for history. Though sometimes lonely, he had a strong memory and interest in politics. He had intended a career in [humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanities "Humanities"), and received his first degree (*[licence ès lettres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licentiate_(degree)#France "Licentiate (degree)")*) in history in 1910. Afterwards, he turned his attention towards science, receiving a degree (*[licence ès sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licentiate_(degree)#France "Licentiate (degree)")*) in 1913. With the outbreak of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War") in 1914, he offered his services to the army in the development of radio communications.

### Military service

After graduation, de Broglie joined the engineering forces to undergo compulsory service. It began at [Fort Mont Valérien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Mont_Valérien "Fort Mont Valérien"), but soon, on the initiative of his brother, he was seconded to the Wireless Communications Service and worked on the [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower "Eiffel Tower"), where the radio transmitter was located. Louis de Broglie remained in military service throughout the First World War, dealing with purely technical issues. In particular, together with [Léon Brillouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Léon_Brillouin "Léon Brillouin") and brother Maurice, he participated in establishing wireless communications with submarines. Louis de Broglie was demobilized in August 1919 with the rank of [adjudant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjudant "Adjudant"). Later, the scientist regretted that he had to spend about six years away from the fundamental problems of science that interested him.

### Scientific and pedagogical career

His 1924 thesis *Recherches sur la théorie des quanta* (Research on the Theory of the Quanta) introduced his theory of [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") waves. This included the [wave–particle duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave–particle_duality "Wave–particle duality") theory of matter, based on the work of [Max Planck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck "Max Planck") and [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") on light. This research culminated in the [de Broglie hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter_wave "Matter wave") stating that *any moving particle or object had an associated wave*. De Broglie thus created a new field in physics, the *mécanique ondulatoire*, or wave mechanics, uniting the physics of energy (wave) and matter (particle). He won the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1929 "for his discovery of the wave nature of electrons".

In his later career, de Broglie worked to develop a [causal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality_(physics) "Causality (physics)") explanation of wave mechanics, in opposition to the wholly [probabilistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic "Probabilistic") models which dominate [quantum mechanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanical "Quantum mechanical") theory; it was refined by [David Bohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bohm "David Bohm") in the 1950s. The theory has since been known as the [De Broglie–Bohm theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Broglie–Bohm_theory "De Broglie–Bohm theory").

In addition to strictly scientific work, de Broglie thought and wrote about the [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science "Philosophy of science"), including the value of modern scientific discoveries. In 1930 he founded the book series Actualités scientifiques et industrielles published by [Éditions Hermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Éditions_Hermann "Éditions Hermann").

De Broglie became a member of the [Académie des sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_sciences "Académie des sciences") in 1933, and was the academy's perpetual secretary from 1942. He was asked to join *Le Conseil de l'Union Catholique des Scientifiques Francais*, but declined because he was non-religious.
In 1941, he was made a member of the National Council of [Vichy France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France "Vichy France"). On 12 October 1944, he was elected to the [Académie Française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_Française "Académie Française"), replacing mathematician [Émile Picard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émile_Picard "Émile Picard"). Because of the deaths and imprisonments of Académie members during the occupation and other effects of the war, the Académie was unable to meet the quorum of twenty members for his election; due to the exceptional circumstances, however, his unanimous election by the seventeen members present was accepted. In an event unique in the history of the Académie, he was received as a member by his own brother Maurice, who had been elected in 1934. [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") awarded him the first [Kalinga Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_Prize "Kalinga Prize") in 1952 for his work in popularizing scientific knowledge, and he was elected a Foreign Member of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") on 23 April 1953.

Louis became the 7th [duc de Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duc_de_Broglie "Duc de Broglie") in 1960 upon the death without heir of his elder brother, [Maurice, 6th duc de Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice,_6th_duc_de_Broglie "Maurice, 6th duc de Broglie"), also a physicist.

In 1961, de Broglie received the title of Knight of the Grand Cross in the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour "Legion of Honour"). De Broglie was awarded a post as counselor to the French High Commission of Atomic Energy in 1945 for his efforts to bring industry and science closer together. He established a center for applied mechanics at the [Henri Poincaré Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Poincaré_Institute "Henri Poincaré Institute"), where research into optics, cybernetics, and atomic energy were carried out. He inspired the formation of the [International Academy of Quantum Molecular Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_Quantum_Molecular_Science "International Academy of Quantum Molecular Science") and was an early member.

De Broglie never married. When he died on 19 March 1987 in [Louveciennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louveciennes "Louveciennes") at the age of 94, he was succeeded as [duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke "Duke") by a distant cousin, [Victor-François, 8th duc de Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor-François,_8th_duc_de_Broglie "Victor-François, 8th duc de Broglie"). His funeral was held 23 March 1987 at the Church of Saint-Pierre-de-Neuilly. Jean-Claude Lehmann, director of the physics department at France's National Center for Scientific Research, said: "The death of Louis de Broglie marks the disappearance of one of the most brilliant pioneers in contemporary physics."

## Research

### Physics of X-ray and photoelectric effect

The first works of Louis de Broglie (early 1920s) were performed in the laboratory of his [older brother Maurice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_de_Broglie "Maurice de Broglie") and dealt with the features of the [photoelectric effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoelectric_effect "Photoelectric effect") and the properties of [x-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-rays "X-rays"). These publications examined the absorption of X-rays and described this phenomenon using the [Bohr theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr_theory "Bohr theory"), applied quantum principles to the interpretation of [photoelectron spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoemission_spectroscopy "Photoemission spectroscopy"), and gave a systematic classification of X-ray spectra. The studies of X-ray spectra were important for elucidating the structure of the internal electron shells of atoms (optical spectra are determined by the outer shells). Thus, the results of experiments conducted together with Alexandre Dauvillier, revealed the shortcomings of the existing schemes for the distribution of electrons in atoms; these difficulties were eliminated by [Edmund Stoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Stoner "Edmund Stoner"). Another result was the elucidation of the insufficiency of the Sommerfeld formula for determining the position of lines in X-ray spectra; this discrepancy was eliminated after the discovery of the electron spin. In 1925 and 1926, Leningrad physicist [Orest Khvolson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orest_Khvolson "Orest Khvolson") nominated the de Broglie brothers for the Nobel Prize for their work in the field of X-rays.

### Matter and wave–particle duality

Studying the nature of X-ray radiation and discussing its properties with his brother Maurice, who considered these rays to be some kind of combination of waves and particles, contributed to Louis de Broglie's awareness of the need to build a theory linking particle and wave representations. In addition, he was familiar with the works (1919–1922) of [Marcel Brillouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Brillouin "Marcel Brillouin"), which proposed a hydrodynamic model of an atom and attempted to relate it to the results of Bohr's theory. The starting point in the work of Louis de Broglie was the idea of Einstein about the [quanta of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon "Photon"). In his first article on this subject, published in 1922, the French scientist considered blackbody radiation as a gas of light quanta and, using classical statistical mechanics, derived the [Wien radiation law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wien_approximation "Wien approximation") in the framework of such a representation. In his next publication, he tried to reconcile the concept of light quanta with the phenomena of interference and diffraction and came to the conclusion that it was necessary to associate a certain periodicity with quanta. In this case, light quanta were interpreted by him as relativistic particles of very small mass.

It remained to extend the wave considerations to any massive particles, and in the summer of 1923 a decisive breakthrough occurred. De Broglie outlined his ideas in a short note "Waves and quanta" ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language"): *Ondes et quanta*, presented at a meeting of the Paris Academy of Sciences on 10 September 1923), which marked the beginning of the creation of wave mechanics. This theory set the basis of wave mechanics. It was supported by Albert Einstein, confirmed by the [electron diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_diffraction "Electron diffraction") experiments of [George Paget Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Paget_Thomson "George Paget Thomson") in the United Kingdom and [Clinton Davisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Davisson "Clinton Davisson") and [Lester Germer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Germer "Lester Germer") in the United States, and generalized by the work of Erwin Schrödinger.

Originally, de Broglie thought that a real wave (i.e., having a direct physical interpretation) was associated with particles. However, when the wave aspect of matter was formalized by a [wavefunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavefunction "Wavefunction") defined by the [Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_equation "Schrödinger equation"), it came out as a pure mathematical entity having a [probabilistic interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Born_rule "Born rule"), without the support of physical elements. This wavefunction gives wave behavior to matter but it is only observed through individual quantum samples. However, in 1956 de Broglie again attempted a theory of a direct and physical interpretation of matter-waves, following the work of [David Bohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bohm "David Bohm") and suggestions of [Jean-Pierre Vigier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Vigier "Jean-Pierre Vigier").

### Conjecture of an internal clock of the electron

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/Solvay_conference_1927_%28group_photograph%29_with_frame.jpg/250px-Solvay_conference_1927_%28group_photograph%29_with_frame.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Solvay_conference_1927_(group_photograph)_with_frame.jpg)

De Broglie presented at the Solvay conference 1927 (third from right in middle row).

In his 1924 thesis, de Broglie conjectured that the electron has an internal clock that constitutes part of the mechanism by which a [pilot wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_wave "Pilot wave") guides a particle. Subsequently, [David Hestenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hestenes "David Hestenes") has proposed a link to the [zitterbewegung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zitterbewegung "Zitterbewegung") that was suggested by Schrödinger.

While attempts at verifying the internal clock hypothesis and measuring clock frequency are so far not conclusive, recent experimental data is at least compatible with de Broglie's conjecture.

### Non-nullity and variability of mass

According to de Broglie, the [neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino "Neutrino") and the [photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon "Photon") have rest masses that are non-zero, though very low. That a photon is not quite massless is imposed by the coherence of his theory. Incidentally, this rejection of the hypothesis of a massless photon enabled him to doubt the hypothesis of the expansion of the universe.

In addition, he believed that the true mass of particles is not constant, but variable, and that each particle can be represented as a [thermodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic "Thermodynamic") machine equivalent to a cyclic integral of action.

### Generalization of the principle of least action

In the second part of his 1924 thesis, de Broglie used the equivalence of the mechanical principle of least action with [Fermat's optical principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat's_principle "Fermat's principle"): "Fermat's principle applied to phase waves is identical to [Maupertuis' principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maupertuis'_principle "Maupertuis' principle") applied to the moving body; the possible dynamic trajectories of the moving body are identical to the possible rays of the wave." This latter equivalence had been pointed out by [William Rowan Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Rowan_Hamilton "William Rowan Hamilton") a century earlier, and published by him around 1830, for the case of light.

### Duality of the laws of nature

Far from claiming to make "the contradiction disappear" which [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born "Max Born") thought could be achieved with a statistical approach, de Broglie extended wave–particle duality to all particles (and to crystals which revealed the effects of diffraction) and extended the principle of duality to the [laws of nature.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_law "Natural law")

His last work made a single system of laws from the two large systems of thermodynamics and of mechanics:

> When [Boltzmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann "Boltzmann") and his continuators developed their statistical interpretation of Thermodynamics, one could have considered Thermodynamics to be a complicated branch of Dynamics. But, with my actual ideas, it's Dynamics that appear to be a simplified branch of Thermodynamics. I think that, of all the ideas that I've introduced in quantum theory in these past years, it's that idea that is, by far, the most important and the most profound.

That idea seems to match the continuous–discontinuous duality, since its dynamics could be the limit of its thermodynamics when transitions to continuous limits are postulated. It is also close to that of [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz "Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz"), who posited the necessity of "architectonic principles" to complete the system of mechanical laws.

However, according to him, there is less duality, in the sense of opposition, than synthesis (one is the limit of the other) and the effort of synthesis is constant according to him, like in his first formula, in which the first member pertains to mechanics and the second to optics:

:   m

    c

    2
    =
    h
    ν
    {\displaystyle mc^{2}=h\nu }
    ![{\displaystyle mc^{2}=h\nu }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/1bdb9402ca2bd5f8e54ddc3718bb1d181cbfc24f)

### Neutrino theory of light

The [neutrino theory of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino_theory_of_light "Neutrino theory of light"), which dates from 1934, introduces the idea that the photon is equivalent to the fusion of two [Dirac neutrinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_fermion "Dirac fermion"). In 1938, the concept was challenged as not rotationally invariant and work on the concept was largely discontinued.

### Hidden thermodynamics

De Broglie's final idea was the hidden thermodynamics of isolated particles. It is an attempt to bring together the three furthest principles of physics: the principles of Fermat, Maupertuis, and [Carnot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnot's_theorem_(thermodynamics) "Carnot's theorem (thermodynamics)").

In this work, [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_(physics) "Action (physics)") becomes a sort of opposite to [entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy "Entropy"), through an equation that relates the only two universal dimensions of the form:

:   action
    h
    =
    −

    entropy
    k
    {\displaystyle {{\text{action}} \over h}=-{{\text{entropy}} \over k}}
    ![{\displaystyle {{\text{action}} \over h}=-{{\text{entropy}} \over k}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5d6f8254083f4063042ec6f742e912e97f0f121e)

As a consequence of its great impact, this theory brings back the [uncertainty principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncertainty_principle "Uncertainty principle") to distances around extrema of action, distances corresponding to *reductions in entropy*.
