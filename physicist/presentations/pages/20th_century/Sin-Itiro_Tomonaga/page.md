**Shinichiro Tomonaga** (朝永 振一郎, *Tomonaga Shin'ichirō*; March 31, 1906 – July 8, 1979), usually cited as **Sin-Itiro Tomonaga** in English, was a Japanese [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). He shared the 1965 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Richard Feynman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Feynman "Richard Feynman") and [Julian Schwinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Schwinger "Julian Schwinger") "for their fundamental work in [quantum electrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_electrodynamics "Quantum electrodynamics") (QED), with deep-ploughing consequences for the physics of elementary particles".

## Biography

Tomonaga was born in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo") in 1906. He was the second child and eldest son of a Japanese [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher "Philosopher"), [Tomonaga Sanjūrō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomonaga_Sanjūrō "Tomonaga Sanjūrō"). He entered the [Kyoto Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University") in 1926. [Hideki Yukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Yukawa "Hideki Yukawa"), also a [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates "List of Nobel laureates"), was one of his classmates during [undergraduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate "Undergraduate") school. During graduate school at the same university, he worked as an assistant in the university for three years. In 1931, after graduate school, he joined [Nishina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshio_Nishina "Yoshio Nishina")'s group in [RIKEN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIKEN "RIKEN"). In 1937, while working at [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University "Leipzig University") ([Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig,_Germany "Leipzig, Germany")), he collaborated with the research group of [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg "Werner Heisenberg"). Two years later, he returned to Japan due to the outbreak of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"), but finished his doctoral degree (Dissertation PhD from [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo "University of Tokyo")) on the study of [nuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics "Nuclear physics") materials with his thesis on work he had done while in Leipzig.

In Japan, he was appointed to a professorship in the Tokyo University of Education (a forerunner of [Tsukuba University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukuba_University "Tsukuba University")). During the war he studied the [magnetron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetron "Magnetron"), [meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meson "Meson") theory, and his [super-many-time theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super-many-time_theory?action=edit&redlink=1 "Super-many-time theory"). In 1948, he and his students re-examined a 1939 paper by [Sidney Dancoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Dancoff "Sidney Dancoff") that attempted, but failed, to show that the infinite quantities that arise in [quantum electrodynamics (QED)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_electrodynamics "Quantum electrodynamics") can be canceled with each other. Tomonaga applied his super-many-time theory and a relativistic method based on the non-relativistic method of [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli") and [Fierz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_Fierz "Markus Fierz") to greatly speed up and clarify the calculations. Then he and his students found that Dancoff had overlooked one term in the [perturbation series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perturbation_theory_(quantum_mechanics) "Perturbation theory (quantum mechanics)"). With this term, the theory gave finite results; thus Tomonaga discovered the [renormalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renormalization "Renormalization") method independently of [Julian Schwinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Schwinger "Julian Schwinger") and calculated physical quantities such as the [Lamb shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_shift "Lamb shift") at the same time.

In 1949, he was invited by [Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Oppenheimer "Robert Oppenheimer") to work at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey "Princeton, New Jersey"). He studied a [many-body problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Many-body_problem "Many-body problem") on the collective oscillations of a quantum-mechanical system. In the following year, he returned to Japan and proposed the [Tomonaga–Luttinger liquid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomonaga–Luttinger_liquid "Tomonaga–Luttinger liquid"). In 1955, he took the leadership in establishing the Institute for Nuclear Study, [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo "University of Tokyo"). In 1965, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), with [Julian Schwinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Schwinger "Julian Schwinger") and [Richard P. Feynman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_P._Feynman "Richard P. Feynman"), for the study of QED, specifically for the discovery of the [renormalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renormalization "Renormalization") method. He died of [throat cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throat_cancer "Throat cancer") in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo") in 1979.

Tomonaga was married in 1940 to Ryōko Sekiguchi. They had two sons and one daughter. He was awarded the Order of Culture in 1952, and the Grand Cordon of the Order of the Rising Sun in 1976.

In recognition of three [Nobel laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureates "Nobel laureates")' contributions, the bronze statues of Shin'ichirō Tomonaga, [Leo Esaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Esaki "Leo Esaki"), and [Makoto Kobayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makoto_Kobayashi_(physicist) "Makoto Kobayashi (physicist)") was set up in the Central Park of Azuma 2 in [Tsukuba City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukuba_City "Tsukuba City") in 2015.
