**Wolfgang Paul** (German pronunciation: [[ˈvɔlfɡaŋ ˈpaʊ̯l]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Wolfgang_Paul.ogg "File:De-Wolfgang Paul.ogg"); 10 August 1913 – 7 December 1993) was a German [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"), who co-developed the non-magnetic quadrupole mass filter which laid the foundation for what is now called an [ion trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_trap "Ion trap"). He shared one-half of the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1989 for this work with [Hans Georg Dehmelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Georg_Dehmelt "Hans Georg Dehmelt"); the other half of the Prize in that year was awarded to [Norman Foster Ramsey, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Foster_Ramsey,_Jr. "Norman Foster Ramsey, Jr.")

## Early life

Wolfgang Paul was born on 10 August 1913 in [Lorenzkirch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzkirch "Lorenzkirch"), Germany. He grew up in Munich where his father was a professor of [pharmaceutical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_chemistry "Medical chemistry"). After the first few years at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich "Technical University of Munich"), he changed to [Technische Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Berlin "Technische Universität Berlin") in 1934 where he finished his Diploma in 1937 at the group of [Hans Geiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Geiger "Hans Geiger"). He followed his doctorate adviser [Hans Kopfermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Kopfermann "Hans Kopfermann") to the [University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel "University of Kiel"), and after being drafted to the air force, he finished his PhD in 1940 at [Technische Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Berlin "Technische Universität Berlin").

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), he researched [isotope separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope_separation "Isotope separation"), which is necessary to produce [fissionable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fissionable "Fissionable") material for use in making [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon").

## Academic career

For several years, he was a private lecturer at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen") with Hans Kopfermann. He became a professor of [Experimental Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics") at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn "University of Bonn") and stayed there from 1952 until 1993. For two years, from 1965 to 1967, he was director of the Division of [Nuclear Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Physics "Nuclear Physics") at [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN"). In 1970, he spent some weeks as Morris Loeb lecturer at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). He lectured in 1978 as distinguished scientist at the FERMI Institute of the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") and in a similar position at [The University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Tokyo "The University of Tokyo"). From 1981, he was Professor Emeritus at the [Bonn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_University "Bonn University").

## Scientific results

He developed techniques for trapping charged particles in mass spectrometry by electric quadrupole fields in the 1950s. [Paul traps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_trap "Paul trap") are used extensively today to contain and study ions. He developed [molecular beam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_beam "Molecular beam") lenses and worked on a 500 MeV electron synchrotron, followed by one at 2500 MeV in 1965. Later he worked on containing slow neutrons in magnetic storage rings, measuring the free neutron lifetime.

He humorously referred to [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli") as his [imaginary part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_part "Imaginary part") if their surnames were considered as complex numbers.

## Göttingen Manifesto

In 1957, Paul was a signatory of the [Göttingen Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttinger_Manifest "Göttinger Manifest"), a declaration of 18 leading nuclear scientists of West Germany against arming the West German army with [tactical nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_nuclear_weapon "Tactical nuclear weapon").

## Sons

His son [Stephan Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephan_Paul?action=edit&redlink=1 "Stephan Paul") is a professor of experimental physics at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich "Technical University of Munich"). His son [Lorenz Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenz_Paul?action=edit&redlink=1 "Lorenz Paul") is a professor of physics at the [University of Wuppertal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wuppertal "University of Wuppertal").
