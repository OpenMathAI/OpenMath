**Arno Allan Penzias** ([/ˈpɛnziəs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); April 26, 1933 – January 22, 2024) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and [radio astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_astronomer "Radio astronomer"). He shared the 1978 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Robert Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Woodrow_Wilson "Robert Woodrow Wilson") "for their discovery of [cosmic microwave background radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_microwave_background_radiation "Cosmic microwave background radiation")".

## Early life, family and education

Penzias was born in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich"), Germany, the son of Justine (née Eisenreich) and Karl Penzias, who ran a leather business. His grandparents had come to Munich from [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland") and were among the leaders of the [Reichenbachstrasse synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synagogue_on_Reichenbachstraße_in_Munich "Synagogue on Reichenbachstraße in Munich"). At age six, he and his brother Gunther were among the [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Germany "History of the Jews in Germany") children evacuated to Britain as part of the [Kindertransport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindertransport "Kindertransport") rescue operation. Later, his parents also fled [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany"), first for the UK, and then for the US, and the family settled in [the Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx "The Bronx"), New York City, New York, in 1940. In 1946, Penzias [became a US citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen_of_the_United_States "Naturalized citizen of the United States").

After he graduated from [Brooklyn Technical High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Technical_High_School "Brooklyn Technical High School") in 1951, he enrolled at the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York "City College of New York") to study chemistry, but he changed majors to physics and graduated in 1954, ranked near the top of his class. Penzias thereafter served in the [U.S. Army Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Signal_Corps "U.S. Army Signal Corps") as a [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar") officer for two years.

## Career

Penzias' involvement with radar in the Signal Corps led to a research assistantship in the [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") Radiation Laboratory, which was then heavily involved in [microwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave "Microwave") physics. Penzias worked under [Charles H. Townes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_H._Townes "Charles H. Townes"), who later invented the [maser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maser "Maser"). Penzias enrolled as a graduate student at Columbia University in 1956, where he earned a master's degree and a PhD in physics, the latter in 1962.

Thereafter, at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs "Bell Labs") in [Holmdel Township, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmdel_Township,_New_Jersey "Holmdel Township, New Jersey"), he and Robert Woodrow Wilson worked on ultra-sensitive cryogenic microwave receivers, intended for radio astronomy observations. In 1964, on building their most sensitive [antenna/receiver system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmdel_Horn_Antenna "Holmdel Horn Antenna"), the pair encountered radio noise that they could not explain. It was far less energetic than the radiation given off by the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way "Milky Way"), and it was [isotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotropic "Isotropic"), so they assumed their instrument was subject to interference by terrestrial sources. They tried, and then rejected, the hypothesis that the radio noise emanated from New York City. An examination of the microwave [horn antenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_antenna "Horn antenna") showed it was full of bat and pigeon droppings, which Penzias described as "white [dielectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dielectric "Dielectric") material". After the pair removed the dung buildup the noise remained. Having rejected all sources of interference, Penzias contacted [Robert H. Dicke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._Dicke "Robert H. Dicke"), who suggested it might be the background radiation predicted by some cosmological theories. The pair agreed with Dicke to publish side-by-side letters in the Astrophysical Journal, with Penzias and Wilson describing their observations and Dicke suggesting the interpretation as the [cosmic microwave background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_microwave_background "Cosmic microwave background") (CMB), the radio remnant of the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang "Big Bang"). This proved to be landmark evidence for the Big Bang and provided substantial confirmation for predictions made by [Ralph Asher Alpher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Asher_Alpher "Ralph Asher Alpher"), [Robert Herman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Herman "Robert Herman") and [George Gamow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow "George Gamow") in the 1940s and 1950s.

## Honors and awards

Penzias was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") and the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1975. In 1977, Penzias and Wilson received the [Henry Draper Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Draper_Medal "Henry Draper Medal") of the National Academy of Sciences. The two were awarded the 1978 Nobel Prize in Physics for their discovery of cosmic microwave background radiation, sharing it with [Pyotr Kapitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Kapitsa "Pyotr Kapitsa"). Kapitsa's work on [low-temperature physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-temperature_physics "Low-temperature physics") was unrelated to Penzias's and Wilson's. In 1979, Penzias received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Achievement "American Academy of Achievement"). He was also the recipient of The International Center in New York's Award of Excellence. In 1998, he was awarded the [IRI Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRI_Medal "IRI Medal") from the [Industrial Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Research_Institute "Industrial Research Institute").

On April 26, 2019, the Nürnberger Astronomische Gesellschaft e.V. (NAG) inaugurated the 3 m (10 ft) [radio telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_telescope "Radio telescope") at the Regiomontanus-Sternwarte, the [public observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_observatory "Public observatory") of [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg "Nuremberg"), and dedicated this instrument to Arno Penzias.

On September 11, 2023, the Radio Club of America said announced it had named an award, the Dr. Arno A. Penzias Award for Contributions to Basic Research in the Radio Sciences, to recognize his significant contributions to basic research involving [radio frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency "Radio frequency") and related subjects. The club also announced that the first recipient of the award would be named in 2024.

## Personal life

In 1954, Penzias married Anne Barras; the couple had three children, David, Mindy, and Laurie, before they divorced. Penzias was a resident of [Highland Park, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_New_Jersey "Highland Park, New Jersey"), in the 1990s.

In 1996, he married [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley "Silicon Valley") executive Sherry Levit, becoming stepfather to her son Carson and daughter Victoria.

Penzias died from complications of [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease "Alzheimer's disease") at an assisted living facility in San Francisco, on January 22, 2024, at age 90.
