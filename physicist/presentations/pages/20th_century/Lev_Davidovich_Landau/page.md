**Lev Davidovich Landau** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Лев Дави́дович Ланда́у; 22 January 1908 – 1 April 1968) was a Soviet [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who made fundamental contributions to many areas of [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics"). He was considered as one of the last scientists who were universally well-versed and made seminal contributions to all branches of physics. He is credited with laying the foundations of twentieth century [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics "Condensed matter physics"), and is also considered arguably the greatest Soviet theoretical physicist.

His accomplishments include the independent co-discovery of the [density matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_matrix "Density matrix") method in [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") (alongside [John von Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann "John von Neumann")), the quantum mechanical theory of [diamagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamagnetism "Diamagnetism"), the theory of [superfluidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluidity "Superfluidity"), the theory of [second-order phase transitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau_theory "Landau theory"), invention of [order parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_parameter "Order parameter") technique, the [Ginzburg–Landau theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginzburg–Landau_theory "Ginzburg–Landau theory") of [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity"), the theory of [Fermi liquids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi_liquid "Fermi liquid"), the explanation of [Landau damping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau_damping "Landau damping") in [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics "Plasma physics"), the [Landau pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau_pole "Landau pole") in [quantum electrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_electrodynamics "Quantum electrodynamics"), the two-component theory of [neutrinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino "Neutrino"), and Landau's equations for [*S*-matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-matrix "S-matrix") singularities. He received the 1962 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for his development of a mathematical theory of [superfluidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluidity "Superfluidity") that accounts for the properties of [liquid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_helium "Liquid helium") [helium II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium_II "Helium II") at a temperature below 2.17 [K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin "Kelvin") (−270.98 [°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius "Celsius")).

## Life

### Early years

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5e/Landau1910.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Landau1910.jpg)

Landau family in 1910

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/Rundyna5.jpg/250px-Rundyna5.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rundyna5.jpg)

Young Landau in 1914

Landau was born on 22 January 1908 to [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") parents in [Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku "Baku"), the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire"), in what is now [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan "Azerbaijan"). Landau's father, David Lvovich Landau, was an engineer with the local oil industry, and his mother, Lyubov Veniaminovna Garkavi-Landau, was a doctor. Both came to Baku from [Mogilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogilev "Mogilev") and both graduated the Mogilev gymnasium. He learned [differential calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_calculus "Differential calculus") at age 12 and [integral calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_calculus "Integral calculus") at age 13. Landau graduated in 1920 at age 13 from [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)"). His parents considered him too young to attend university, so for a year he attended the Baku Economical Technical School. In 1922, at age 14, he [matriculated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculation "Matriculation") at the [Baku State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_State_University "Baku State University"), studying in two departments simultaneously: the Departments of Physics and Mathematics, and the Department of Chemistry. Subsequently, he ceased studying chemistry, but remained interested in the field throughout his life.

### Leningrad and Europe

In 1924, he moved to the main centre of Soviet physics at the time: the Physics Department of [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University "Saint Petersburg State University"), where he dedicated himself to the study of theoretical physics, graduating in 1927. Landau subsequently enrolled for post-graduate studies at the [Leningrad Physico-Technical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioffe_Physico-Technical_Institute "Ioffe Physico-Technical Institute") where he eventually received a doctorate in Physical and Mathematical Sciences in 1934. Landau got his first chance to travel abroad during the period 1929–1931, on a Soviet government—[People's Commissariat for Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Commissariat_for_Education "People's Commissariat for Education")—travelling fellowship supplemented by a [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation "Rockefeller Foundation") fellowship. By that time he was fluent in German and French and could communicate in English. He later improved his English and learned Danish.

After brief stays in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen "Göttingen") and [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig "Leipzig"), he went to [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen "Copenhagen") on 8 April 1930 to work at the [Niels Bohr's Institute for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr_Institute "Niels Bohr Institute"). He stayed there until 3 May of the same year. After the visit, Landau always considered himself a pupil of [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr "Niels Bohr") and Landau's approach to physics was greatly influenced by Bohr. After his stay in Copenhagen, he visited [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge") (mid-1930), where he worked with [Paul Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac"), Copenhagen (September to November 1930), and [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich "Zürich") (December 1930 to January 1931), where he worked with [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli"). From Zürich Landau went back to Copenhagen for the third time and stayed there from 25 February until 19 March 1931 before returning to Leningrad the same year.

### Ukrainian Physics and Technology Institute, Kharkov

Between 1932 and 1937, Landau headed the Department of Theoretical Physics at the [Ukrainian Physics and Technology Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_Institute_of_Physics_and_Technology "Kharkiv Institute of Physics and Technology") (UPTI) in [Kharkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkov "Kharkov"), and he lectured at the [University of Kharkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kharkov "University of Kharkov") and the [Kharkov Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkov_Polytechnic_Institute "Kharkov Polytechnic Institute"). Apart from his theoretical accomplishments, Landau was the principal founder of a great tradition of theoretical physics in Kharkov, Ukraine, sometimes referred to as the "Landau school". In Kharkov, he and his friend and former student, [Evgeny Lifshitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Lifshitz "Evgeny Lifshitz"), began writing the *[Course of Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Course_of_Theoretical_Physics "Course of Theoretical Physics")*, ten volumes that together span the whole of the subject and are still widely used as [graduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_school "Graduate school")-level physics texts. During the [Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge "Great Purge"), Landau was investigated within the [UPTI Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPTI_Affair "UPTI Affair") in Kharkov, but he managed to leave for [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow") to take up a new post.

Landau developed a famous comprehensive exam called the "Theoretical Minimum" which students were expected to pass before admission to the school. The exam covered all aspects of theoretical physics, and between 1934 and 1961 only 43 candidates passed, but those who did later became quite notable theoretical physicists.

In 1932, Landau computed the [Chandrasekhar limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrasekhar_limit "Chandrasekhar limit"); however, he did not apply it to white dwarf stars.

### Institute for Physical Problems, Moscow

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/1938-LandauL.jpg/250px-1938-LandauL.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1938-LandauL.jpg)

Photo in prison, 1938-1939

From 1937 until 1962, Landau was the head of the Theoretical Division at the [Institute for Physical Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Physical_Problems "Institute for Physical Problems").

On 27 April 1938, Landau was arrested for the possession of a [leaflet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korets–Landau_leaflet "Korets–Landau leaflet") which compared [Stalinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinism "Stalinism") to [German Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism") and [Italian Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_fascism "Italian fascism"). He was held in the [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD "NKVD")'s [Lubyanka prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubyanka_Building "Lubyanka Building") until his release, on 29 April 1939, after [Pyotr Kapitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Kapitsa "Pyotr Kapitsa") (an [experimental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics") low-temperature physicist and the founder and head of the institute) and Bohr wrote letters to [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin "Joseph Stalin"). Kapitsa personally vouched for Landau's behaviour and threatened to quit the institute if Landau was not released. After his release, Landau discovered how to explain Kapitsa's superfluidity using sound waves, or [phonons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonon "Phonon"), and a new excitation called a [roton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roton "Roton").

Landau led a team of mathematicians supporting Soviet atomic and hydrogen bomb development. He calculated the dynamics of the first Soviet thermonuclear bomb, including predicting the [yield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon_yield "Nuclear weapon yield"). For this work Landau received the [Stalin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize "USSR State Prize") in 1949 and 1953, and was awarded the title "[Hero of Socialist Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_Socialist_Labour "Hero of Socialist Labour")" in 1954.

Landau's students included [Lev Pitaevskii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Pitaevskii "Lev Pitaevskii"), [Alexei Abrikosov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Abrikosov_(physicist) "Alexei Abrikosov (physicist)"), [Aleksandr Akhiezer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Akhiezer "Aleksandr Akhiezer"), [Igor Dzyaloshinskii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Dzyaloshinskii "Igor Dzyaloshinskii"), [Evgeny Lifshitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Lifshitz "Evgeny Lifshitz"), [Lev Gor'kov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Gor'kov "Lev Gor'kov"), [Isaak Khalatnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaak_Khalatnikov "Isaak Khalatnikov"), [Roald Sagdeev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roald_Sagdeev "Roald Sagdeev") and [Isaak Pomeranchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaak_Pomeranchuk "Isaak Pomeranchuk").

### Scientific achievements

Landau's accomplishments include the independent co-discovery of the [density matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_matrix "Density matrix") method in quantum mechanics (alongside [John von Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann "John von Neumann")), the quantum mechanical theory of [diamagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamagnetism "Diamagnetism"), the theory of [superfluidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluidity "Superfluidity"), the theory of [second-order phase transitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_transition "Phase transition"), the [Ginzburg–Landau theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginzburg–Landau_theory "Ginzburg–Landau theory") of superconductivity, the theory of [Fermi liquids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi_liquid_theory "Fermi liquid theory"), the explanation of [Landau damping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau_damping "Landau damping") in plasma physics, the [Landau pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau_pole "Landau pole") in quantum electrodynamics, the [two-component theory of neutrinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-component_theory_of_neutrinos?action=edit&redlink=1 "Two-component theory of neutrinos"), the explanation of flame instability (the [Darrieus-Landau instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darrieus-Landau_instability "Darrieus-Landau instability")), and [Landau's equations for S matrix singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau's_equations_for_S_matrix_singularities?action=edit&redlink=1 "Landau's equations for S matrix singularities").

Landau received the 1962 Nobel Prize in Physics for his development of a mathematical theory of superfluidity that accounts for the properties of liquid helium II at a temperature below 2.17 K (−270.98 °C)."

### Personal life and views

In 1937, Landau married Kora T. Drobanzeva from Kharkov. Their son Igor (1946–2011) became a theoretical physicist. Lev Landau believed in "[free love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_love "Free love")" rather than monogamy and encouraged his wife and his students to practise "free love". However, his wife was not enthusiastic.

Landau is generally described as an atheist, although when Soviet filmmaker [Andrei Tarkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Tarkovsky "Andrei Tarkovsky") asked Landau whether he believed in the existence of God, Landau pondered the matter in silence for three minutes before responding, "I think so." In 1957, a lengthy report to the [CPSU Central Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Committee_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union "Central Committee of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union") by the KGB recorded Landau's views on the [1956 Hungarian Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956 "Hungarian Revolution of 1956"), [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin "Vladimir Lenin") and what he termed "red fascism". [Hendrik Casimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Casimir "Hendrik Casimir") recalls him as a passionate communist, emboldened by his revolutionary ideology. Landau's drive in establishing Soviet science was in part due to his devotion to socialism. In 1935 he published a piece titled “Bourgeoisie and Contemporary Physics” in the Soviet newspaper *[Izvestia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izvestia "Izvestia")* in which he criticized religious superstition and the dominance of capital, which he saw as bourgeois tendencies, citing “unprecedented opportunities for the development of physics in our country, provided by the Party and the government.”

### Last years

On 7 January 1962, Landau's car collided with an oncoming truck. He was severely injured and spent two months in a [coma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coma "Coma"). Although Landau recovered in many ways, his scientific creativity was destroyed, and he never returned fully to scientific work. His injuries prevented him from accepting the 1962 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in person.

Throughout his life Landau was known for his sharp humour, as illustrated by the following dialogue with a psychologist, [Alexander Luria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Luria "Alexander Luria"), who tried to test for possible brain damage while Landau was recovering from the car crash:

:   Luria: "Please draw me a circle"
:   Landau draws a cross
:   Luria: "Hm, now draw me a cross"
:   Landau draws a circle
:   Luria: "Landau, why don't you do what I ask?"
:   Landau: "If I did, you might come to think I've become mentally retarded".

In 1965 former students and co-workers of Landau founded the [Landau Institute for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau_Institute_for_Theoretical_Physics "Landau Institute for Theoretical Physics"), located in the town of [Chernogolovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernogolovka "Chernogolovka") near [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow"), and led for the following three decades by [Isaak Khalatnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaak_Khalatnikov "Isaak Khalatnikov").

In June 1965, Lev Landau and [Evsei Liberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evsei_Liberman "Evsei Liberman") published a letter in the *New York Times*, stating that as [Soviet Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_the_Soviet_Union "History of the Jews in the Soviet Union") they opposed U.S. intervention on behalf of the [Student Struggle for Soviet Jewry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_Struggle_for_Soviet_Jewry "Student Struggle for Soviet Jewry"). However, there are doubts that Landau authored this letter.

### Death

Landau died on 1 April 1968, aged 60, from complications of the injuries sustained in the car accident six years earlier. He was buried at the [Novodevichy Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novodevichy_Cemetery "Novodevichy Cemetery").

## Landau's ranking of physicists

Landau kept a list of names of [physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") which he ranked on a logarithmic scale of productivity and [genius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genius "Genius"), such as [creativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creativity "Creativity") and innate [talent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aptitude "Aptitude"), ranging from 0 to 5. The highest ranking, 0, was assigned to [Isaac Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton "Isaac Newton"). [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") was ranked 0.5. A rank of 1 was awarded to the founding fathers of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"), [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr "Niels Bohr"), [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg "Werner Heisenberg"), [Satyendra Nath Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satyendra_Nath_Bose "Satyendra Nath Bose"), [Paul Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac") and [Erwin Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger "Erwin Schrödinger"), and others, while members of rank of 5 were deemed "pathologists". Landau ranked himself as a 2.5 but later promoted to a 2. [N. David Mermin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._David_Mermin "N. David Mermin"), writing about Landau, referred to the scale, and ranked himself in the fourth division, in the article "My Life with Landau: Homage of a 4.5 to a 2".

Landau had a lesser-known scale to measure the genius of a scientist using diagrams instead. He had four classes of diagrams, with the first being a simple triangle, which included those who were the most original and brilliant, such as Dirac and Einstein. The diagrams were formed by two parallel lines, bottom representing tenacity, while the top measured genius and originality.

## Works

Landau wrote his first paper *[On the derivation of Klein–Fock equation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On%20the%20derivation%20of%20Klein–Fock%20equation "s:On the derivation of Klein–Fock equation")*, co-authored with [Dmitri Ivanenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Ivanenko "Dmitri Ivanenko") in 1926, when he was 18 years old. His last paper titled *Fundamental problems* appeared in 1960 in an edited version of tributes to [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli"). A complete list of Landau's works appeared in 1998 in the Russian journal *Physics-Uspekhi*. Landau would allow himself to be listed as a co-author of a journal article on two conditions: 1) he brought up the idea of the work, partly or entirely, and 2) he performed at least some calculations presented in the article. Consequently, he removed his name from numerous publications of his students where his contribution was less significant.
