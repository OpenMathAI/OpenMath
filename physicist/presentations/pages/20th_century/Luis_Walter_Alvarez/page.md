**Luis Walter Alvarez** (June 13, 1911 – September 1, 1988) was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans "Americans") [experimental physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physicist "Experimental physicist"), inventor, and professor who was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1968 for his discovery of [resonance states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resonance_(particle_physics) "Resonance (particle physics)") in particle physics using the [hydrogen bubble chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_chamber "Bubble chamber"). In 2007 the *[American Journal of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Physics "American Journal of Physics")* commented, "Luis Alvarez was one of the most brilliant and productive experimental physicists of the twentieth century."

After receiving his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") in 1936, Alvarez went to work for [Ernest Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Lawrence "Ernest Lawrence") at the [Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Lab "Lawrence Berkeley National Lab") at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"). Alvarez devised a set of experiments to observe K-[electron capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_capture "Electron capture") in [radioactive nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity "Radioactivity"), predicted by the [beta decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_decay "Beta decay") theory but never before observed. He produced [tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium "Tritium") using the [cyclotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotron "Cyclotron") and measured its lifetime. In collaboration with [Felix Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Bloch "Felix Bloch"), he measured the [magnetic moment of the neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_magnetic_moment "Neutron magnetic moment").

In 1940, Alvarez joined the [MIT Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Laboratory "MIT Radiation Laboratory"), where he contributed to a number of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar") projects, from early improvements to [Identification friend or foe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_friend_or_foe "Identification friend or foe") (IFF) radar beacons, now called [transponders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transponder "Transponder"), to a system known as VIXEN for preventing enemy submarines from realizing that they had been found by the new airborne [microwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave "Microwave") radars. The radar system for which Alvarez is best known and which has played a major role in aviation, most particularly in the post war [Berlin airlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_airlift "Berlin airlift"), was [Ground Controlled Approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Controlled_Approach "Ground Controlled Approach") (GCA). Alvarez spent a few months at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") working on [nuclear reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactor "Nuclear reactor") for [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi") before coming to [Los Alamos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory "Los Alamos National Laboratory") to work for [Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Oppenheimer "Robert Oppenheimer") on the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project"). Alvarez worked on the design of [explosive lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_lens "Explosive lens"), and the development of [exploding-bridgewire detonators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploding-bridgewire_detonator "Exploding-bridgewire detonator"). As a member of [Project Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Alberta "Project Alberta"), he observed the [Trinity nuclear test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_(nuclear_test) "Trinity (nuclear test)") from a [B-29 Superfortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-29_Superfortress "B-29 Superfortress"), and later the [bombing of Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombing_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki "Atomic bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki") from the B-29 *[The Great Artiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Artiste "The Great Artiste")*.

After the war Alvarez was involved in the design of a [liquid hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_hydrogen "Liquid hydrogen") [bubble chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_chamber "Bubble chamber") that allowed his team to take millions of photographs of particle interactions, develop complex computer systems to measure and analyze these interactions, and discover entire families of new particles and resonance states. This work resulted in his being awarded the Nobel Prize in 1968. He was involved in a project to [x-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray "X-ray") the [Egyptian pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramids "Egyptian pyramids") to search for unknown chambers. With his son, geologist [Walter Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Alvarez "Walter Alvarez"), he developed the [Alvarez hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvarez_hypothesis "Alvarez hypothesis") which proposes that the [extinction event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous–Paleogene_extinction_event "Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event") that wiped out the non-avian dinosaurs was the result of an asteroid impact.

## Early life

Luis Walter Alvarez was born into a [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic") family in San Francisco on June 13, 1911, the second child and oldest son of [Walter C. Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_C._Alvarez "Walter C. Alvarez"), a physician, and his wife Harriet ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Smyth). His grandfather, [Luis F. Álvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_F._Álvarez "Luis F. Álvarez"), was a physician born in Asturias, Spain, who lived briefly in Cuba, settled in the United States, and discovered a better method for diagnosing [macular leprosy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leprosy#Classification "Leprosy"). He had an older sister, Gladys, a younger brother, Bob, and a younger sister, Bernice. His aunt, [Mabel Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabel_Alvarez "Mabel Alvarez"), was a California artist specializing in [oil painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_painting "Oil painting").

He attended Madison School in San Francisco from 1918 to 1924, and then [San Francisco Polytechnic High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Polytechnic_High_School "San Francisco Polytechnic High School"). In 1926, his father became a researcher at the [Mayo Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_Clinic "Mayo Clinic"), and the family moved to [Rochester, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_Minnesota "Rochester, Minnesota"), where Alvarez attended Rochester High School. He had always expected to attend the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), but at the urging of his teachers at Rochester, he instead went to the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"), where he received his bachelor's degree in 1932, his master's degree in 1934, and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in 1936. As an undergraduate, he belonged to the [Phi Gamma Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Gamma_Delta "Phi Gamma Delta") [fraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraternities_and_sororities "Fraternities and sororities"). As a postgraduate he moved to [Gamma Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_Alpha "Gamma Alpha").

In 1932, as a [graduate student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_student "Graduate student") at Chicago, he discovered the field of physics and had the rare opportunity to use the equipment of legendary physicist [Albert A. Michelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_A._Michelson "Albert A. Michelson"). Alvarez also constructed an apparatus of [Geiger counter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geiger_counter "Geiger counter") tubes arranged as a [cosmic ray telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic-ray_observatory "Cosmic-ray observatory"), and under the aegis of his faculty advisor [Arthur Compton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Compton "Arthur Compton"), conducted an experiment in Mexico City to measure the so-called [East–West effect of cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray#Identification "Cosmic ray"). Observing more incoming radiation from the west, Alvarez concluded that primary cosmic rays were positively charged. Compton submitted the resulting paper to the *[Physical Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Review "Physical Review")*, with Alvarez's name at the top.

Alvarez was an [agnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnostic "Agnostic") even though his father had been a deacon in a Congregational church.

## Early work

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Alvarez_and_Compton.jpeg/250px-Alvarez_and_Compton.jpeg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Alvarez_and_Compton.jpeg)

Nobel Laureate [Arthur Compton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Compton "Arthur Compton"), left, with young graduate student Luis Alvarez at the University of Chicago in 1933. Compton served as his doctoral adviser.

Alvarez's sister, Gladys, worked for Berkeley physicist [Ernest Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Lawrence "Ernest Lawrence") as a part-time secretary, and mentioned Alvarez to Lawrence. Lawrence then invited Alvarez to tour the [Century of Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_of_Progress "Century of Progress") exhibition in Chicago with him. After he completed his [oral exams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_exam "Oral exam") in 1936, Alvarez, now engaged to be married to Geraldine Smithwick, asked his sister to see if Lawrence had any jobs available at the [Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Lab "Lawrence Berkeley National Lab"). A telegram soon arrived from Gladys with a job offer from Lawrence. This started a long association with the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"). Alvarez and Smithwick were married in one of the chapels at the University of Chicago and then headed for California. They had two children, [Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Alvarez "Walter Alvarez") and Jean. They were divorced in 1957. On December 28, 1958, he married Janet L. Landis, and had two more children, Donald and Helen.

At the Radiation Laboratory he worked with Lawrence's experimental team, which was supported by a group of theoretical physicists headed by [Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Oppenheimer "Robert Oppenheimer"). Alvarez devised a set of experiments to observe K-[electron capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_capture "Electron capture") in [radioactive nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity "Radioactivity"), predicted by the [beta decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_decay "Beta decay") theory but never observed. Using [magnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet "Magnet") to sweep aside the [positrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron "Positron") and [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") emanating from his radioactive sources, he designed a special purpose Geiger counter to detect only the "soft" [X-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-rays "X-rays") coming from K capture. He published his results in the *[Physical Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Review "Physical Review")* in 1937.

When [deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium "Deuterium") (hydrogen-2) is bombarded with deuterium, the [fusion reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_reaction "Fusion reaction") yields either [tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium "Tritium") (hydrogen-3) plus a [proton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") or [helium-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium-3 "Helium-3") plus a [neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron "Neutron") (2
H + 2
H → 3
H + p or 3
He + n). This is one of the most basic [fusion reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_reaction "Fusion reaction"), and the foundation of both the [thermonuclear weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermonuclear_weapon "Thermonuclear weapon") and the current research on [controlled nuclear fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_thermonuclear_fusion "Controlled thermonuclear fusion"). At that time the stability of these two reaction products was unknown, but based on existing theories [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe "Hans Bethe") thought that tritium would be stable and helium-3 unstable. Alvarez proved the reverse by using his knowledge of the details of the 60-inch [cyclotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotron "Cyclotron") operation. He tuned the machine to accelerate doubly ionized helium-3 nuclei and was able to get a [beam of accelerated ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_beam "Ion beam"), thus using the cyclotron as a kind of super [mass spectrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometer "Mass spectrometer"). As the accelerated helium came from deep [gas wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_well "Gas well") where it had been for millions of years, the helium-3 component had to be stable. Afterwards Alvarez produced the radioactive tritium using the cyclotron and the 2
H + 2
H reaction and measured its lifetime.

In 1938, again using his knowledge of the cyclotron and inventing what are now known as [time-of-flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-of-flight "Time-of-flight") techniques, Alvarez created a mono-energetic beam of [thermal neutrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_neutron "Thermal neutron"). With this he began a long series of experiments, collaborating with [Felix Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Bloch "Felix Bloch"), to measure the [magnetic moment of the neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_magnetic_moment "Neutron magnetic moment"). Their result of μ0 = 1.93±0.02 [μN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magneton "Nuclear magneton"), published in 1940, was a major advance over earlier work.

## World War II

### Radiation Laboratory

The British [Tizard Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tizard_Mission "Tizard Mission") to the United States in 1940 demonstrated to leading American scientists the successful application of the [cavity magnetron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavity_magnetron "Cavity magnetron") to produce short wavelength pulsed [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar"). The [National Defense Research Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Research_Committee "National Defense Research Committee"), established only months earlier by President [Franklin Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Roosevelt "Franklin Roosevelt"), created a central national laboratory at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (MIT) for the purpose of developing military applications of microwave radar. Lawrence immediately recruited his best "cyclotroneers", among them Alvarez, who joined this new laboratory, known as the [Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_Laboratory "Radiation Laboratory"), on November 11, 1940. Alvarez contributed to a number of [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar") projects, from early improvements to [Identification Friend or Foe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_Friend_or_Foe "Identification Friend or Foe") (IFF) radar beacons, now called [transponders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transponders "Transponders"), to a system known as VIXEN for preventing enemy submarines from realizing that they had been found by the new airborne microwave radars. Enemy submarines would wait until the radar signal was getting strong and then submerge, escaping attack. But VIXEN transmitted a radar signal whose strength was the cube of the distance to the submarine so that as they approached the sub, the signal—as measured by the sub—got progressively weaker, and the sub assumed the plane was getting farther away and did not submerge.

One of the first projects was to build equipment to transition from the British long-wave radar to the new microwave centimeter-band radar made possible by the [cavity magnetron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavity_magnetron "Cavity magnetron"). In working on the [Microwave Early Warning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_Early_Warning "Microwave Early Warning") system (MEW), Alvarez invented a [linear dipole array antenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phased_array "Phased array") that not only suppressed the unwanted [side lobes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side_lobe "Side lobe") of the radiation field but also could be electronically scanned without the need for mechanical scanning. This was the first microwave phased-array antenna, and Alvarez used it not only in MEW but in two additional radar systems. The antenna enabled the Eagle [precision bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_bombing "Precision bombing") radar to support precision bombing in bad weather or through clouds. It was completed rather late in the war; although a number of [B-29s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-29 "B-29") were equipped with Eagle and it worked well, it came too late to make much difference.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Luis_Alvarez_with_president_Harry_Truman.jpg/250px-Luis_Alvarez_with_president_Harry_Truman.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Luis_Alvarez_with_president_Harry_Truman.jpg)

Receiving the [Collier Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collier_Trophy "Collier Trophy") from President [Harry Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Truman "Harry Truman"), White House, 1946

The radar system for which Alvarez is best known and which has played a major role in aviation, most particularly in the post-war [Berlin airlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_airlift "Berlin airlift"), was [Ground Controlled Approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Controlled_Approach "Ground Controlled Approach") (GCA). Using Alvarez's dipole antenna to achieve a very high [angular resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_resolution "Angular resolution"), GCA allows ground-based radar operators to watch special precision displays to guide a landing airplane to the runway by transmitting verbal commands to the pilot. The system was simple, direct, and worked well, even with previously untrained pilots. It was so successful that the military continued to use it for many years after the war, and it was still in use in some countries in the 1980s. Alvarez was awarded the [National Aeronautic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aeronautic_Association "National Aeronautic Association")'s [Collier Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collier_Trophy "Collier Trophy") in 1945 "for his conspicuous and outstanding initiative in the concept and development of the Ground Control Approach system for safe landing of aircraft under all weather and traffic conditions".

Alvarez spent the summer of 1943 in England testing GCA, landing planes returning from battle in bad weather, and also training the British in the use of the system. While there he encountered the young [Arthur C. Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_C._Clarke "Arthur C. Clarke"), who was an RAF radar technician. Clarke subsequently used his experiences at the radar research station as the basis for his novel *[Glide Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glide_Path "Glide Path")*, which contains a thinly disguised version of Alvarez. Clarke and Alvarez developed a long-term friendship.

### Manhattan Project

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Alvarez-luis.jpg/250px-Alvarez-luis.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Alvarez-luis.jpg)

Los Alamos badge photo

In the fall of 1943, Alvarez returned to the United States with an offer from [Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Oppenheimer "Robert Oppenheimer") to work at [Los Alamos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory "Los Alamos National Laboratory") on the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project"). However, Oppenheimer suggested that he first spend a few months at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") working with [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi") before coming to Los Alamos. During these months, General [Leslie Groves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Groves "Leslie Groves") asked Alvarez to think of a way that the US could find out if the Germans were operating any [nuclear reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactor "Nuclear reactor"), and, if so, where they were. Alvarez suggested that an airplane could carry a system to detect the radioactive gases that a reactor produces, particularly [xenon-133](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenon_133 "Xenon 133"). The equipment did fly over Germany, but detected no radioactive xenon because the Germans had not built a reactor capable of a chain reaction. This was the first idea of monitoring [fission products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fission_product "Fission product") for [intelligence gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_gathering "Intelligence gathering"). It would become extremely important after the war.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/Luis_Alvarez_-_Flak_Jacket.JPG/250px-Luis_Alvarez_-_Flak_Jacket.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Luis_Alvarez_-_Flak_Jacket.JPG)

Wearing a helmet and [flak jacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flak_jacket "Flak jacket") and standing in front of the bomber, *[The Great Artiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Artiste "The Great Artiste")*, [Tinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinian "Tinian") island in 1945

As a result of his radar work and the few months spent with Fermi, Alvarez arrived at Los Alamos in the spring of 1944, later than many of his contemporaries. The work on the "[Little Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Boy "Little Boy")" (a uranium bomb) was far along so Alvarez became involved in the design of the "[Fat Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat_Man "Fat Man")" (a plutonium bomb). The technique used for uranium, that of forcing the two sub-[critical masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_mass "Critical mass") together using a [type of gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun-type_fission_weapon "Gun-type fission weapon"), would not work with plutonium because the high level of background [spontaneous neutrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_fission "Spontaneous fission") would cause fissions as soon as the two parts approached each other, so heat and expansion would force the system apart before much energy has been released. It was decided to use a nearly critical sphere of [plutonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutonium "Plutonium") and compress it quickly by explosives into a much smaller and denser [core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutonium_core "Plutonium core"), a technical challenge at the time.

To create the symmetrical [implosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon_design "Nuclear weapon design") required to compress the plutonium core to the required density, thirty-two explosive charges were to be simultaneously detonated around the spherical core. Using conventional explosive techniques with [blasting caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasting_cap "Blasting cap"), progress towards achieving simultaneity to within a small fraction of a microsecond was discouraging. Alvarez directed his graduate student, [Lawrence H. Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_H._Johnston "Lawrence H. Johnston"), to use a large [capacitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor "Capacitor") to deliver a [high voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_voltage "High voltage") charge directly to each [explosive lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_lens "Explosive lens"), replacing blasting caps with [exploding-bridgewire detonators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploding-bridgewire_detonator "Exploding-bridgewire detonator"). The exploding wire detonated the thirty-two charges to within a few tenths of a microsecond. The invention was critical to the success of the [implosion-type nuclear weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implosion-type_nuclear_weapon "Implosion-type nuclear weapon"). He also supervised the [RaLa Experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RaLa_Experiment "RaLa Experiment"). Alvarez later wrote that:

> With modern weapons-grade uranium, the background neutron rate is so low that terrorists, if they had such material, would have a good chance of setting off a high-yield explosion simply by dropping one half of the material onto the other half. Most people seem unaware that if separated [U-235](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium-235 "Uranium-235") is at hand, it's a trivial job to set off a nuclear explosion, whereas if only plutonium is available, making it explode is the most difficult technical job I know.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Agnew_on_Tinian.jpg/250px-Agnew_on_Tinian.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Agnew_on_Tinian.jpg)

Alvarez (top right) on Tinian island with [Harold Agnew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Agnew "Harold Agnew") (top left), [Lawrence H. Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_H._Johnston "Lawrence H. Johnston") (bottom left) and [Bernard Waldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Waldman "Bernard Waldman") (bottom right)

Again working with Johnston, Alvarez's last task for the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project") was to develop a set of calibrated [microphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microphone "Microphone")/[transmitters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmitter "Transmitter") to be parachuted from an aircraft to measure the strength of the blast wave from the atomic explosion, so as to allow the scientists to calculate the bomb's energy. After being commissioned as a [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Colonel_(United_States) "Lieutenant Colonel (United States)") in the [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army"), he observed the [Trinity nuclear test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_(nuclear_test) "Trinity (nuclear test)") from a [B-29 Superfortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-29_Superfortress "B-29 Superfortress") that also carried fellow [Project Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Alberta "Project Alberta") members [Harold Agnew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Agnew "Harold Agnew") and [Deak Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deak_Parsons "Deak Parsons") (who were respectively commissioned at the rank of [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_(United_States_O-3) "Captain (United States O-3)")).

Flying in the [B-29 Superfortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-29_Superfortress "B-29 Superfortress") *[The Great Artiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Artiste "The Great Artiste")* in formation with the *[Enola Gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enola_Gay "Enola Gay")*, Alvarez and Johnston measured the blast effect of the [Little Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Boy "Little Boy") bomb which was [dropped on Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombing_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki "Atomic bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki"). A few days later, again flying in *The Great Artiste*, Johnston used the same equipment to measure the strength of the [Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki_bomb "Nagasaki bomb") explosion.

Following the conclusion of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War") in 1945 Alvarez would become a professor of physics at the school he had originally desired to attend, the University of California, Berkeley and in 1978 he would be granted the title of professor emeritus.

## Bubble chamber

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Luis_Alvarez_-_Nobel_with_Balloon.jpg/250px-Luis_Alvarez_-_Nobel_with_Balloon.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Luis_Alvarez_-_Nobel_with_Balloon.jpg)

Celebrating winning the Nobel Prize, October 30, 1968. The balloons are inscribed with the names of subatomic particles that his group discovered.

Returning to the University of California, Berkeley as a [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_professor "Full professor"), Alvarez had many ideas about how to use his wartime radar knowledge to improve [particle accelerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator "Particle accelerator"). Though some of these were to bear fruit, the "big idea" of this time would come from [Edwin McMillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_McMillan "Edwin McMillan") with his concept of [phase stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchrocyclotron "Synchrocyclotron") which led to the [synchrocyclotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchrocyclotron "Synchrocyclotron"). Refining and extending this concept, the Lawrence team would build the world's then-largest proton accelerator, the [Bevatron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bevatron "Bevatron"), which began operating in 1954. Though the Bevatron could produce copious amounts of interesting particles, particularly in secondary collisions, these complex interactions were hard to detect and analyze at the time.

Seizing upon a new development to visualize particle tracks, created by [Donald Glaser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_A._Glaser "Donald A. Glaser") and known as a [bubble chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_chamber "Bubble chamber"), Alvarez realized the device was just what was needed, if only it could be made to function with [liquid hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_hydrogen "Liquid hydrogen"). [Hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen "Hydrogen") nuclei, which are [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton"), made the simplest and most desirable target for interactions with the particles produced by the Bevatron. He began a development program to build a series of small chambers, and championed the device to Ernest Lawrence.

The Glaser device was a small glass cylinder (1 cm × 2 cm) filled with [ether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diethyl_ether "Diethyl ether"). By suddenly reducing the pressure in the device, the liquid could be placed into a temporary [superheated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superheated "Superheated") state, which would boil along the disturbed track of a particle passing through. Glaser was able to maintain the superheated state for a few seconds before spontaneous boiling took place. The Alvarez team built chambers of 1.5 in, 2.5 in, 4 in, 10 in, and 15 in using liquid hydrogen, and constructed of metal with glass windows, so that the tracks could be photographed. The chamber could be cycled in synchronization with the accelerator beam, a picture could be taken, and the chamber recompressed in time for the next beam cycle.

This program built a liquid hydrogen bubble chamber almost 7 feet (2.1 meters) long, employed dozens of physicists and graduate students together with hundreds of engineers and technicians, took millions of photographs of particle interactions, developed computer systems to measure and analyze the interactions, and discovered families of new particles and [resonance states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resonance_(particle_physics) "Resonance (particle physics)"). This work resulted in the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for Alvarez in 1968, "For his decisive contributions to elementary particle physics, in particular the discovery of a large number of resonant states, made possible through his development of the technique of using hydrogen bubble chambers and data analysis."

## Scientific detective work

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/Luis_Alvarez%2C_Ahmed_Fakhry%2C_Jerry_Anderson_-_Pyramid_Project.JPG/250px-Luis_Alvarez%2C_Ahmed_Fakhry%2C_Jerry_Anderson_-_Pyramid_Project.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Luis_Alvarez,_Ahmed_Fakhry,_Jerry_Anderson_-_Pyramid_Project.JPG)

X-Raying the Pyramids with Egyptologist [Ahmed Fakhry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Fakhry "Ahmed Fakhry") and Team Leader Jerry Anderson, Berkeley, 1967

In 1964, Alvarez proposed what became known as the [High Altitude Particle Physics Experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Altitude_Particle_Physics_Experiment?action=edit&redlink=1 "High Altitude Particle Physics Experiment") (HAPPE), originally conceived as a large [superconducting magnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_magnet "Superconducting magnet") carried to high altitude by a [balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balloon "Balloon") in order to study extremely high-energy particle interactions. In time the focus of the experiment changed toward the study of [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology "Cosmology") and the role of both particles and radiation in the [early universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_universe "Early universe"). This work was a large effort, carrying detectors aloft with [high-altitude balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-altitude_balloon "High-altitude balloon") flights and high-flying [U-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_U-2 "Lockheed U-2") aircraft, and an early precursor of the [COBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_Background_Explorer "Cosmic Background Explorer") satellite-born experiments on the cosmic background radiation (which resulted in the award of the 2006 Nobel Prize, shared by [George Smoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Smoot "George Smoot") and [John Mather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Mather "John C. Mather")).

Alvarez proposed [muon tomography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muon_tomography "Muon tomography") in 1965 to search the [Egyptian pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramids "Egyptian pyramids") for unknown chambers. Using naturally occurring [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray "Cosmic ray"), his plan was to place [spark chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spark-chamber_detector "Spark-chamber detector"), standard equipment in the high-energy [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics") of this time, beneath the [Pyramid of Khafre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_of_Khafre "Pyramid of Khafre") in a known chamber. By measuring the counting rate of the cosmic rays in different directions the detector would reveal the existence of any void in the overlaying rock structure.

Alvarez assembled a team of physicists and archeologists from the United States and Egypt, the recording equipment was constructed and the experiment carried out, though it was interrupted by the 1967 [Six-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-Day_War "Six-Day War"). Restarted after the war, the effort continued, recording and analyzing the penetrating cosmic rays until 1969, when he reported to the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") that no chambers had been found in the 19% of the pyramid surveyed.

In November 1966, *[Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_(magazine) "Life (magazine)")* magazine published a series of photographs from the 1963 "[Zapruder film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapruder_film "Zapruder film")", believed to be the most complete document of the [assassination of John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_John_F._Kennedy "Assassination of John F. Kennedy"). Alvarez, an expert in optics and [photoanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoanalysis "Photoanalysis"), became intrigued by the pictures and began to study what could be learned from the film. Alvarez demonstrated both in theory and experiment that the backward snap of the President's head was consistent with his being shot from behind being called the "jet-effect" theory. Alvarez's theory was later refined and corroborated by other researchers. Alvarez also investigated the timing of the gunshots and the shockwave that disturbed the camera, and the speed of the camera, pointing out a number of details that the FBI photo analysts either overlooked or got wrong. He produced a paper intended as a tutorial, with informal advice for the physicist intent on arriving at the truth of the case.

## Dinosaur extinction hypothesis

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/LWA_with_Walt.JPG/250px-LWA_with_Walt.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:LWA_with_Walt.JPG)

Luis and [Walter Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Alvarez "Walter Alvarez") at the [K-Pg Boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous–Paleogene_boundary "Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary") in [Gubbio, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gubbio,_Italy "Gubbio, Italy"), 1981

In 1980 Alvarez and his son, geologist [Walter Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Alvarez "Walter Alvarez"), along with nuclear chemists [Frank Asaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Asaro "Frank Asaro") and [Helen Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Michel "Helen Michel"), "uncovered a calamity that literally shook the Earth and is one of the great discoveries about Earth's history".

During the 1970s, Walter Alvarez was doing geological research in central Italy. There he had located an outcrop on the walls of a gorge whose [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone "Limestone") layers included [strata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strata "Strata") both above and below the [Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous–Paleogene_boundary "Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary"). Exactly at the boundary is a thin layer of [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay "Clay"). Walter told his father that the layer marked where the [dinosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur "Dinosaur") and much else became extinct and that nobody knew why, or what the clay was about—it was a big mystery, and he intended to solve it.

Alvarez had access to the [nuclear chemists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_chemistry "Nuclear chemistry") at the [Lawrence Berkeley Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_Laboratory "Lawrence Berkeley Laboratory") and was able to work with [Frank Asaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Asaro "Frank Asaro") and [Helen Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Michel "Helen Michel"), who used the technique of [neutron activation analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_activation_analysis "Neutron activation analysis") to study the clay. In 1980, Alvarez, Alvarez, Asaro, and Michel published a seminal paper proposing an extraterrestrial cause for the Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction (then called the Cretaceous-Tertiary extinction). In the years following the publication of their article, the clay was also found to contain [soot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soot "Soot"), [glassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass "Glass") spherules, [shocked quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shocked_quartz "Shocked quartz") crystals, microscopic [diamonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond "Diamond"), and rare minerals formed only under conditions of great temperature and pressure.

Publication of the 1980 paper brought criticism from the geologic community, and an often acrimonious scientific debate ensued. Ten years later, after Alvarez's death, evidence was found of a large [impact crater off the coast of the Yucatán peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicxulub_crater "Chicxulub crater") in Mexico, providing support for the theory. Other researchers later found that the [end-Cretaceous extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous–Paleogene_extinction_event "Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event") of the dinosaurs may have occurred rapidly in geologic terms, over thousands of years, rather than millions of years as had previously been supposed. While alternative extinction theories have been proposed, including increased [volcanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanism "Volcanism") at the [Deccan Traps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Traps "Deccan Traps"), the impact crater theory remains dominant among relevant scholars.

## Aviation

In his autobiography, Alvarez said, "I think of myself as having had two separate careers, one in science and one in aviation. I've found the two almost equally rewarding." An important contributor to this was his enjoyment of flying. He learned to fly in 1933, later earning [instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_rating "Instrument rating") and multi-engine ratings. Over the next 50 years he accumulated over 1000 hours of flight time, most of it as pilot in command. He said, "I found few activities as satisfying as being pilot in command with responsibility for my passengers' lives."

Alvarez made numerous professional contributions to aviation. During World War II he led the development of multiple aviation-related technologies. Several of his projects are described above, including Ground Controlled Approach (GCA) for which he was awarded the Collier Trophy in 1945. He also held the basic patent for the radar [transponder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transponder "Transponder"), for which he assigned rights to the U.S. government for $1.

Later in his career Alvarez served on multiple high level advisory committees related to civilian and military aviation. These included a [Federal Aviation Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Aviation_Administration "Federal Aviation Administration") task group on future [air navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_navigation "Air navigation") and [air traffic control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_traffic_control "Air traffic control") systems, the [President's Science Advisory Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_Science_Advisory_Committee "President's Science Advisory Committee") Military Aircraft Panel, and a committee studying how the scientific community could help improve the United States' capabilities for fighting a nonnuclear war.

Alvarez's aviation responsibilities led to many adventures. For example, while working on GCA he became the first civilian to fly a low approach with his view outside the cockpit obstructed. He also flew many military aircraft from the co-pilot's seat, including a [B-29 Superfortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-29_Superfortress "B-29 Superfortress") and a [Lockheed F-104 Starfighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_F-104_Starfighter "Lockheed F-104 Starfighter"). In addition, he survived a crash during World War II as a passenger in a [Miles Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Master "Miles Master").

## Other activities

Alvarez was a member of the [JASON Defense Advisory Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JASON_(advisory_group) "JASON (advisory group)") and the [Bohemian Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Club "Bohemian Club").

## Death

Alvarez died on September 1, 1988, of complications from a succession of operations for [esophageal cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esophageal_cancer "Esophageal cancer"). His remains were cremated, and his ashes were scattered over [Monterey Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterey_Bay "Monterey Bay"). His papers are in [The Bancroft Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bancroft_Library "The Bancroft Library") at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley").
