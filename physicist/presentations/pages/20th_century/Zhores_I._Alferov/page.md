**Zhores Ivanovich Alferov** (15 March 1930 – 1 March 2019) was a Russian applied [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who contributed significantly to the creation of modern heterostructure physics and electronics. In 2000, Alferov shared the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for the development of the semiconductor [heterojunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterojunction "Heterojunction") for optoelectronics. He also became a politician in his later life, serving in the [State Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Duma "State Duma") as a member of the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Russian_Federation "Communist Party of the Russian Federation") from 1999.

## Biography

Zhores Ivanovich Alferov was born on 15 March 1930 in [Vitebsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitebsk "Vitebsk"), [Byelorussian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic "Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic") (now [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus "Belarus")), the son of Ivan Karpovich Alferov and Anna Vladimirovna. In 1952, Alferov graduated from the Department of Electronics at V. I. Ulyanov Electrotechnical Institute (now [Saint Petersburg Electrotechnical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Electrotechnical_University "Saint Petersburg Electrotechnical University")). The following year, he joined the staff of the [Ioffe Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioffe_Institute "Ioffe Institute"), becoming its director in 1987. He obtained his [Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences "Candidate of Sciences") in Technology in 1961, and his [Doctor of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Sciences "Doctor of Sciences") in Physics and Mathematics in 1970, both from the Ioffe Institute.

Alferov was elected a Corresponding Member of the [Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union "Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union") in 1972, and a full member in 1979. In 1973, he was appointed to the Chair of Optoelectronics at the Electrotechnical Institute, and became Dean of the Faculty of Physics and Technology at the Kalinin Politechnical Institute (now [Peter the Great St. Petersburg Polytechnic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great_St._Petersburg_Polytechnic_University "Peter the Great St. Petersburg Polytechnic University")) in 1988. From 1989, he was Vice-President of the Academy of Sciences and President of its [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg "Saint Petersburg") Scientific Center.

## Research

Alferov worked with a group led by Vladimir Tuchkevich, who became director of the [Ioffe Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioffe_Institute "Ioffe Institute") in 1967, on planar semiconductor amplifiers for use in [radio receivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_receiver "Radio receiver"). These planar semiconductor amplifiers would be referred to as transistors in the present day. His contribution included work on [germanium diodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanium_diode "Germanium diode") for use as a rectifier.

In the early 1960s, Alferov organized an effort at Ioffe Institute to develop semiconductor heterostructures. [Heterojunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterojunction "Heterojunction") transistors enabled higher frequency use than their [homojunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homojunction "Homojunction") predecessors, and this capability plays a key role in modern mobile phone and satellite communications. Alferov and colleagues worked on [GaAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallium_arsenide "Gallium arsenide") and [AlAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium_arsenide "Aluminium arsenide") [III-V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/III-V "III-V") heterojunctions. A particular focus was the use of heterojunctions to create [semiconductor lasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_lasers "Semiconductor lasers") capable of lasing at room temperature. In 1963, Alferov filed a patent application proposing double-heterostructure lasers; [Herbert Kroemer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Kroemer "Herbert Kroemer") independently filed a US patent several months later. In 1966, Alferov's lab created the first lasers based on heterostructures, although they did not lase continuously. Then in 1968, Alferov and coworkers produced the first [continuous-wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous-wave "Continuous-wave") semiconductor heterojunction laser operating at [room temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_temperature "Room temperature"). This achievement came a month ahead of [Izuo Hayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izuo_Hayashi "Izuo Hayashi") and [Morton B. Panish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morton_B._Panish "Morton B. Panish") of [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs "Bell Labs") also producing a continuous-wave room-temperature heterojunction laser.

It was for this work that Alferov received the 2000 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") together with Herbert Kroemer "for developing semiconductor heterostructures used in high-speed- and opto-electronics."

In the 1960s and 1970s Alferov continued his work on the physics and technology of semiconductor heterostructures in his lab at the Ioffe Institute. His investigations of injection properties of semiconductors and his contributions to the development of [lasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser "Laser"), [solar cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_cell "Solar cell"), [LEDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LED "LED"), and [epitaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epitaxy "Epitaxy") processes led to the creation of modern heterojunction physics and electronics. The development of semiconductor heterojunctions revolutionized semiconductor design, and had a range of immediate commercial applications—including LEDs, [barcode readers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcode_reader "Barcode reader"), and [CDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD "CD"). [Hermann Grimmeiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Grimmeiss "Hermann Grimmeiss") of the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, which awards Nobel Prizes, said: "Without Alferov, it would not be possible to transfer all the information from satellites down to the Earth or to have so many telephone lines between cities."

Alferov had an almost messianic conception of heterostructures, writing: "Many scientists have contributed to this remarkable progress, which not only determines in large measure the future prospects of solid state physics but in a certain sense affects the future of human society as well."

## Scientific administration

In 1987, Alferov became the fifth director of the Ioffe Institute. In 1989, Alferov gained the administrative position of chairman of the Leningrad Scientific Center, now referred to as the St. Petersburg Scientific Center. In the Leningrad region, this scientific center is an overarching organization comprising 70 institutions, organizations, enterprises, and scientific societies.

Alferov worked to foster relationships between early educational institutions and scientific research institutions to train the next generation of scientists, citing [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great "Peter the Great")'s vision for the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") to be organized with a scientific research core in close contact with a gymnasium (secondary school). In 1987, Alferov and colleagues at the Ioffe Institute established a secondary school in Saint Petersburg, the School of Physics and Technology, under the umbrella of the Ioffe charter. In 1997 Alferov founded the Research and Education Center at the Ioffe Institute and in 2002, this center officially became the [Saint Petersburg Academic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Academic_University "Saint Petersburg Academic University") after gaining a charter to award masters and PhD degrees.

In the 2000s, through his role in academic administration and in parliament, Alferov advocated for and worked to advance Russia's [nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanotechnology "Nanotechnology") sector. The primary research charter of the [Saint Petersburg Academic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Academic_University "Saint Petersburg Academic University"), which Alferov founded, was the development of nanotechnology. Alferov provided a consistent voice in parliament in favor of increased scientific funding. In 2006, Prime Minister [Mikhail Fradkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Fradkov "Mikhail Fradkov") announced the creation of a federal agency, [Rosnanotekh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusnano "Rusnano"), to pursue nanotechnology applications.

## Political career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Vladimir_Putin_12_October_2000-2.jpg/250px-Vladimir_Putin_12_October_2000-2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Vladimir_Putin_12_October_2000-2.jpg)

Alferov meeting President [Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin "Vladimir Putin") at the [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin "Kremlin"), 2000.

Alferov was elected to the Russian Parliament, the [State Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Duma "State Duma"), in [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Russian_legislative_election "1995 Russian legislative election") as a deputy for the political party [Our Home – Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Home_–_Russia "Our Home – Russia"), generally considered to be supportive of the policies of President [Boris Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin "Boris Yeltsin"). In [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Russian_legislative_election "1999 Russian legislative election"), he was elected again, this time on the list of the [Communist Party of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Russian_Federation "Communist Party of the Russian Federation"). He was re-elected in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Russian_legislative_election "2003 Russian legislative election") and again in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Russian_legislative_election "2007 Russian legislative election"), when he was placed second on the party's federal electoral list behind [Gennady Zyuganov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennady_Zyuganov "Gennady Zyuganov") and ahead of [Nikolai Kharitonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Kharitonov "Nikolai Kharitonov"), even though he was not a member of the party.

## Non-profit service

Alferov served on the advisory council of [CRDF Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRDF_Global "CRDF Global").

## Personal life

His wife was named Tamara Darskaya. Together they had two children; a son, Ivan, and a daughter, Olga.

Alferov was an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism "Atheism") and expressed objections to religious education. He was one of the signers of the [open letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academicians'_letter "Academicians' letter") to President [Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin "Vladimir Putin") from members of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") against [clericalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clericalism "Clericalism") of Russia.

## Death

Since November 2018, Alferov suffered from [hypertensive emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertensive_emergency "Hypertensive emergency"). He died on 1 March 2019 in [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg "Saint Petersburg") at the age of 88.

## In popular culture

The song «World That Was Made By Alferov» by **Leningrad Nights** from the fourth album *Physics* (2025) is dedicated to Zhores Alferov.
