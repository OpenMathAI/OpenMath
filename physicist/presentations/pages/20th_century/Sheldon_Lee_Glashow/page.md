**Sheldon Lee Glashow** ([US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English "American English"): [/ˈɡlæʃoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"), [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English "British English"): [/ˈɡlæʃaʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); born December 5, 1932) is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") [theoretical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics") [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). He shared the 1979 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Abdus Salam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdus_Salam "Abdus Salam") and [Steven Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Weinberg "Steven Weinberg") "for their contributions to the theory of the [unified weak and electromagnetic interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroweak_force "Electroweak force") between elementary particles, including, inter alia, the prediction of the [weak neutral current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_neutral_current "Weak neutral current")". He is the [Metcalf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metcalf_Center_for_Science_and_Engineering "Metcalf Center for Science and Engineering") Professor of Mathematics and Physics at [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University "Boston University"), and a [Eugene Higgins Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Higgins "Eugene Higgins") of Physics, emeritus, at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). Glashow is a member of the board of sponsors for the *[Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_the_Atomic_Scientists "Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists")*.

## Birth and education

Sheldon Glashow was born on December 5, 1932, in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), to [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews "Ashkenazi Jews") immigrants from [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire"), Bella (née Rubin) and Lewis Gluchovsky, a plumber. He graduated from [Bronx High School of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_High_School_of_Science "Bronx High School of Science") in 1950. Glashow was in the same graduating class as [Steven Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Weinberg "Steven Weinberg"), whose own research, independent of Glashow's, would result in Glashow, Weinberg, and [Abdus Salam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdus_Salam "Abdus Salam") sharing the 1979 Nobel Prize in Physics (see below). Glashow received a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") in 1954 and a PhD degree in physics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") in 1959 under Nobel-laureate physicist [Julian Schwinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Schwinger "Julian Schwinger"). Afterwards, Glashow became a NSF fellow at [NORDITA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NORDITA "NORDITA") and met [Murray Gell-Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Gell-Mann "Murray Gell-Mann"), who convinced him to become a research fellow at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology"). Glashow then became an assistant professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") before joining the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") where he was an associate professor from 1962 to 1966. He joined the Harvard physics department as a professor in 1966, and was named Eugene Higgins Professor of Physics in 1979; he became emeritus in 2000. Glashow has been a visiting scientist at [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN"), and professor at [Aix-Marseille University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-Marseille_University "Aix-Marseille University"), [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT "MIT"), [Brookhaven Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_Laboratory "Brookhaven Laboratory"), [Texas A&M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M "Texas A&M"), the [University of Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Houston "University of Houston"), and [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University "Boston University").

## Research

In 1961, Glashow extended [electroweak unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroweak_unification "Electroweak unification") models due to [Schwinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Schwinger "Julian Schwinger") by including a short range [neutral current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_current "Neutral current"), the [Z0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W_and_Z_bosons#Z_boson "W and Z bosons"). The resulting symmetry structure that Glashow proposed, [SU(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU(2) "SU(2)") × [U(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U(1) "U(1)"), forms the basis of the accepted theory of the [electroweak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroweak "Electroweak") interactions. For this discovery, Glashow along with [Steven Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Weinberg "Steven Weinberg") and [Abdus Salam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdus_Salam "Abdus Salam"), was awarded the 1979 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics").

In collaboration with [James Bjorken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bjorken "James Bjorken"), Glashow was the first to predict a fourth quark, the [charm quark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charm_quark "Charm quark"), in 1964. This was at a time when 4 [leptons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leptons "Leptons") had been discovered but only 3 quarks proposed. The development of their work in 1970, the [GIM mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIM_mechanism "GIM mechanism") showed that the two quark pairs: (d.s), (u,c), would largely cancel out flavor changing neutral currents, which had been observed experimentally at far lower levels than theoretically predicted on the basis of 3 quarks only. The prediction of the charm quark also removed a technical disaster for any quantum field theory with unequal numbers of quarks and leptons — an [anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomaly_(physics) "Anomaly (physics)") — where classical field theory symmetries fail to carry over into the quantum theory.

In 1973, Glashow and [Howard Georgi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Georgi "Howard Georgi") proposed the first [Grand Unified Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Unified_Theory "Grand Unified Theory"). They discovered how to fit the gauge forces in the [Standard Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Model "Standard Model") into an [SU(5) Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_unitary_group "Special unitary group"), and the quarks and leptons into two simple representations. Their theory qualitatively predicted the general pattern of [coupling constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling_constant "Coupling constant") running, with plausible assumptions, it gave rough mass ratio values between third generation leptons and quarks, and it was the first indication that the law of [Baryon number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baryon_number "Baryon number") is inexact, that the [proton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") is unstable. This work was the foundation for all future unifying work.

Glashow shared the 1977 [J. Robert Oppenheimer Memorial Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer_Memorial_Prize "J. Robert Oppenheimer Memorial Prize") with [Feza Gürsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feza_Gürsey "Feza Gürsey").

## Criticism of superstring theory

Glashow is a skeptic of [superstring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstring "Superstring") theory due to its lack of experimentally testable predictions. He had campaigned to keep string theorists out of the [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard "Harvard") physics department, though the campaign failed. About ten minutes into "String's the Thing", the second episode of *[The Elegant Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elegant_Universe "The Elegant Universe")* TV series, he describes superstring theory as a discipline distinct from physics, saying "...you may call it a tumor, if you will...".

## Personal life

Glashow is married to Joan Shirley Alexander. They have four children. [Lynn Margulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_Margulis "Lynn Margulis"), who was married to [Carl Sagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sagan "Carl Sagan"), was Joan's sister. [Daniel Kleitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kleitman "Daniel Kleitman"), who was another doctoral student of [Julian Schwinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Schwinger "Julian Schwinger"), is also his brother-in-law, through Joan's other sister, Sharon.

In 2003, Glashow was one of 22 Nobel Laureates who signed the [Humanist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism_and_Its_Aspirations "Humanism and Its Aspirations"). Glashow has described himself as a "practising [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist")" and a Democrat.

Glashow is one of the 20 American recipients of the Nobel Prize in Physics to sign a letter addressed to President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") in May 2008, urging him to "reverse the damage done to basic science research in the Fiscal Year 2008 Omnibus Appropriations Bill" by requesting additional emergency funding for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")’s [Office of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Science "Office of Science"), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), and the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology").
