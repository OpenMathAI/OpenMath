**John William Strutt, 3rd Baron Rayleigh** ([/ˈreɪli/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*RAY-lee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); 12 November 1842 – 30 June 1919), was a British [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and [hereditary peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditary_peer "Hereditary peer") who received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1904 for his discovery of [argon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argon "Argon").

In 1871, Rayleigh published the first theoretical treatment of the elastic scattering of light by particles much smaller than the light's wavelength, a phenomenon now known as [Rayleigh scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_scattering "Rayleigh scattering"), which notably explains why the sky is blue. He studied and described transverse [surface waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_wave "Surface wave") in solids, now known as [Rayleigh waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_wave "Rayleigh wave"). He contributed extensively to [fluid dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_dynamics "Fluid dynamics"), with concepts such as the [Rayleigh number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_number "Rayleigh number") (a [dimensionless number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_number "Dimensionless number") associated with [natural convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_convection "Natural convection")), [Rayleigh flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_flow "Rayleigh flow"), the [Rayleigh–Taylor instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh–Taylor_instability "Rayleigh–Taylor instability"), and Rayleigh's criterion for the stability of [Taylor–Couette flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor–Couette_flow "Taylor–Couette flow").

Rayleigh also formulated the circulation theory of [aerodynamic lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_(force) "Lift (force)"). In [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics "Optics"), he proposed a well-known criterion for [angular resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_resolution "Angular resolution"). His derivation of the [Rayleigh–Jeans law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh–Jeans_law "Rayleigh–Jeans law") for classical [black-body radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-body_radiation "Black-body radiation") later played an important role in the birth of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") (see [ultraviolet catastrophe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet_catastrophe "Ultraviolet catastrophe")). His textbook, *The Theory of Sound* (1877), is still used today by [acousticians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustician "Acoustician") and engineers. He introduced the [Rayleigh test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_test "Rayleigh test") for circular non-uniformity, which the [Rayleigh plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh_plot "Raleigh plot") visualises.

Rayleigh served as President of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") from 1905 to 1908, and as Chancellor of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") from 1908 until his death in 1919.

## Biography

John William Strutt was born on 12 November 1842 at Langford Grove, Maypole Road in [Maldon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldon "Maldon"), England, the son of John James Strutt, 2nd Baron Rayleigh, and Clara Elizabeth La Touche. In his early years, he suffered from frailty and poor health.

Strutt attended [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College "Eton College") and [Harrow School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow_School "Harrow School") (each for only a short period), before entering [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"), in 1861, where he studied mathematics. In 1865, he received his [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.A. "B.A.") ([Senior Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Wrangler "Senior Wrangler") and [Smith's Prizeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize "Smith's Prize")), and was elected a Fellow of Trinity College the following year. He obtained an [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.A. "M.A.") in 1868.

In 1871, Strutt resigned from his fellowship to marry Evelyn Balfour, the daughter of [James Maitland Balfour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Maitland_Balfour "James Maitland Balfour"), with whom he had three sons. In 1873, on the death of his father, he inherited the [Barony of Rayleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron_Rayleigh "Baron Rayleigh").

In 1879, Rayleigh was appointed [Cavendish Professor of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Professor_of_Physics "Cavendish Professor of Physics") at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"). In 1883, he first described [dynamic soaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_soaring "Dynamic soaring") by seabirds in *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")*. The following year, he resigned as Cavendish Professor to continue his experimental work at [Terling Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terling_Place "Terling Place").

From 1887 to 1905, Rayleigh was Professor of Natural Philosophy at the [Royal Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institution "Royal Institution"). During this period, he carefully and precisely measured [atomic mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_mass "Atomic mass") of oxygen and hydrogen, and by 1892 he moved on to [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen "Nitrogen"). By isolating nitrogen in different ways he discovered a small but significant difference, which indicated a heavier inert gas present in the air besides nitrogen. [William Ramsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ramsay "William Ramsay") joined this research topic, and in August 1894 they discovered [argon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argon "Argon"). In 1904, Rayleigh and Ramsay were awarded the Nobel Prizes in [Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") and [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry"), respectively; both for research related to the discovery of argon.

Around 1900, Rayleigh developed the *duplex* (combination of two) theory of human sound localisation using two [binaural cues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binaural_beats "Binaural beats"), [interaural phase difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaural_time_difference "Interaural time difference") (IPD) and [interaural level difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaural_level_difference "Interaural level difference") (ILD) (based on analysis of a spherical head with no external [pinnae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinna_(anatomy) "Pinna (anatomy)")). The theory posits that we use two primary cues for sound lateralisation, using the difference in the phases of [sinusoidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine_wave "Sine wave") components of the sound and the difference in amplitude (level) between the two ears.

Caricature of Lord Rayleigh in the London magazine *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(British_magazine) "Vanity Fair (British magazine)")*, 1899.

Rayleigh received the degree of *Doctor mathematicae ([honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa "Honoris causa"))* from the [Royal Frederick University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Frederick_University "Royal Frederick University") on 6 September 1902, when they celebrated the centennial of the birth of mathematician [Niels Abel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Abel "Niels Abel").

From 1905 to 1908, Rayleigh served as [President of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Royal_Society "President of the Royal Society"). From time to time, he participated in the [House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords "House of Lords"); however, he spoke up only if politics attempted to become involved in science.

During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War"), Rayleigh was President of the [Advisory Committee for Aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advisory_Committee_for_Aeronautics "Advisory Committee for Aeronautics"), which was located at the [National Physical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Physical_Laboratory_(United_Kingdom) "National Physical Laboratory (United Kingdom)") and chaired by [Richard Glazebrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Glazebrook "Richard Glazebrook").

In 1919, Rayleigh served as President of the [Society for Psychical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Psychical_Research "Society for Psychical Research"). As an advocate that simplicity and theory be part of the scientific method, he argued for the [principle of similitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_similitude "Principle of similitude").

Rayleigh died on 30 June 1919 in [Witham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witham "Witham") at the age of 76, and was buried in the graveyard of All Saints' Church in [Terling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terling "Terling"). His son [Robert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Strutt,_4th_Baron_Rayleigh "Robert Strutt, 4th Baron Rayleigh"), a physicist, succeeded him as Baron Rayleigh.

## Religious views

Rayleigh was an [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican "Anglican"). Though he did not write about the [relationship between science and religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relationship_between_science_and_religion "Relationship between science and religion"), he retained a personal interest in spiritual matters. When his scientific papers were to be published in a collection by the [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press "Cambridge University Press"), Strutt wanted to include a quotation from the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible "Bible"), but he was discouraged from doing so, as he later reported:

> When I was bringing out my *Scientific Papers* I proposed a motto from the [Psalms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalms "Psalms"), *"The Works of the Lord are great, sought out of all them that have pleasure therein."* The Secretary to the Press suggested with many apologies that the reader might suppose that I was the Lord.

Still, he had his wish and the quotation was printed in the five-volume collection of scientific papers. In a letter to a family member, he wrote about his rejection of [materialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materialism "Materialism") and spoke of [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ "Jesus Christ") as a moral teacher:

> I have never thought the materialist view possible, and I look to a power beyond what we see, and to a life in which we may at least hope to take part. What is more, I think that Christ and indeed other spiritually gifted men see further and truer than I do, and I wish to follow them as far as I can.

— Rayleigh (1910)

Rayleigh held an interest in [parapsychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parapsychology "Parapsychology") and was an early member of the [Society for Psychical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Psychical_Research "Society for Psychical Research") (SPR). He was not convinced of [spiritualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritualism_(beliefs) "Spiritualism (beliefs)") but remained open to the possibility of [supernatural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernatural "Supernatural") phenomena. Rayleigh was the president of the SPR in 1919. He gave a presidential address in the year of his death but did not come to any definite conclusions.

## Commemoration

[A lunar crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_(lunar_crater) "Rayleigh (lunar crater)"), as well as [a Martian crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_(Martian_crater) "Rayleigh (Martian crater)"), are named in his honour. The asteroid [22740 Rayleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22740_Rayleigh "22740 Rayleigh") was named after him on 1 June 2007. The [rayl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayl "Rayl"), a unit of [specific acoustic impedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_acoustic_impedance "Specific acoustic impedance"), is also named for him.

Sir [William Ramsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ramsay "William Ramsay"), his co-worker in the investigation to discover argon, described Rayleigh as "the greatest man alive" while speaking to Lady Ramsay during his last illness.

[H. M. Hyndman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hyndman "Henry Hyndman") said of Rayleigh that "no man ever showed less consciousness of great genius".

In honour of Lord Rayleigh, the [Institute of Acoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Acoustics_(United_Kingdom) "Institute of Acoustics (United Kingdom)") sponsors the [Rayleigh Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_Medal "Rayleigh Medal") (established in 1970) and the [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics "Institute of Physics") sponsors the [John William Strutt, Lord Rayleigh Medal and Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_William_Strutt,_Lord_Rayleigh_Medal_and_Prize "John William Strutt, Lord Rayleigh Medal and Prize") (established in 2008).

Many of the papers that he wrote on [lubrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubrication "Lubrication") are now recognized as early classical contributions to the field of [tribology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribology "Tribology"). For these contributions, he was named as one of the 23 "Men of Tribology" by [Duncan Dowson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Dowson "Duncan Dowson").

There is a memorial to him by [Derwent Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derwent_Wood "Derwent Wood") in St Andrew's Chapel at [Westminster Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Abbey "Westminster Abbey").

The [National Physical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Physical_Laboratory_(United_Kingdom) "National Physical Laboratory (United Kingdom)") (NPL) awards annualy the Rayleigh Award for "the authors of the most outstanding NPL's paper published in the previous calendar year."
