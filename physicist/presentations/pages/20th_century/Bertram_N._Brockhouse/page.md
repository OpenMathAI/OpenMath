**Bertram Neville Brockhouse**, [CC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Canada "Order of Canada") [FRSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Canada "Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (July 15, 1918 – October 13, 2003) was a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians "Canadians") [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). He was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") (1994, shared with [Clifford Shull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Shull "Clifford Shull")) "for pioneering contributions to the development of [neutron scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_scattering "Neutron scattering") techniques for studies of condensed matter", in particular "for the development of [neutron spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_spectroscopy "Neutron spectroscopy")".

## Early life and education

Brockhouse was born in [Lethbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethbridge "Lethbridge"), [Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta "Alberta"), to a family of English descent. He was a graduate of the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia "University of British Columbia") ([BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts"), 1947) and the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto "University of Toronto") ([MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts"), 1948; [Ph.D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy"), 1950).

## Career and research

From 1950 to 1962, Brockhouse carried out research at [Atomic Energy of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_Energy_of_Canada "Atomic Energy of Canada")'s [Chalk River Nuclear Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk_River_Nuclear_Laboratory "Chalk River Nuclear Laboratory"). Here he was joined by [P. K. Iyengar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._K._Iyengar "P. K. Iyengar"), who is treated as the father of India's nuclear program.

In 1962, he became a professor at [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University "McMaster University") in Canada, where he remained until his retirement in 1984.

Brockhouse died on October 13, 2003, in [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario "Hamilton, Ontario"), aged 85.

## Awards and honours

Brockhouse was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1965 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1965"). In 1982, Brockhouse was made an Officer of the [Order of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Canada "Order of Canada") and was promoted to Companion in 1995.

Brockhouse shared the 1994 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with American [Clifford Shull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Shull "Clifford Shull") of MIT for developing [neutron scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_scattering "Neutron scattering") techniques for studying condensed matter.

In October 2005, as part of the 75th anniversary of McMaster University's establishment in [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario "Hamilton, Ontario"), a street on the university campus (University Avenue) was renamed to Brockhouse Way in honour of Brockhouse. The town of [Deep River, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_River,_Ontario "Deep River, Ontario"), has also named a street in his honour.

The Nobel Prize that Bertram Brockhouse won (shared with Clifford Shull) in 1994 was awarded after the longest-ever waiting time (counting from the time when the award-winning research had been carried out-[1958-1994 36 years] ).

In 1999 the Division of Condensed Matter and Materials Physics (DCMMP) and the [Canadian Association of Physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Association_of_Physicists "Canadian Association of Physicists") (CAP) created a medal in honour of Brockhouse. The medal is called the Brockhouse Medal and is awarded to recognize and encourage outstanding experimental or theoretical contributions to condensed matter and materials physics. This medal is awarded annually on the basis of outstanding experimental or theoretical contributions to condensed matter physics. An eligible candidate must have performed their research primarily with a Canadian Institution.
