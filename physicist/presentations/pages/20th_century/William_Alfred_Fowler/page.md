**William Alfred Fowler** (August 9, 1911 – March 14, 1995) was an American [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysicist "Astrophysicist"). He shared the 1983 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") "for his theoretical and experimental studies of the nuclear reactions of importance in the  [formation of the chemical elements in the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_nucleosynthesis "Stellar nucleosynthesis")." He is known for his theoretical and experimental research into nuclear reactions within stars and the energy elements produced in the process. With  [Margaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Burbidge "Margaret Burbidge") and [Geoffrey Burbidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Burbidge "Geoffrey Burbidge") and [Fred Hoyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Hoyle "Fred Hoyle"), he authored the influential B2FH paper, *[Synthesis of the Elements in Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesis_of_the_Elements_in_Stars "Synthesis of the Elements in Stars")*.

## Early life

On 9 August 1911, Fowler was born in [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh "Pittsburgh"). Fowler's parents were John MacLeod Fowler and Jennie Summers Watson. Fowler was the eldest of his siblings, Arthur and Nelda.

The family moved to [Lima, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima,_Ohio "Lima, Ohio"), a steam railroad town, when Fowler was two years old. Growing up near the [Pennsylvania Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Railroad "Pennsylvania Railroad") yard influenced Fowler's interest in locomotives. In 1973, he travelled to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") just to observe the steam engine that powered the [Trans-Siberian Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Siberian_Railway "Trans-Siberian Railway") plying the nearly 2,500-kilometre (1,600 mi) route that connects [Khabarovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabarovsk "Khabarovsk") and [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow").

## Education

In 1933, Fowler graduated from the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University "Ohio State University"), where he was a member of the [Tau Kappa Epsilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tau_Kappa_Epsilon "Tau Kappa Epsilon") fraternity. In 1936, Fowler received a Ph.D. in [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics "Nuclear physics") from the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") in [Pasadena, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena,_California "Pasadena, California").

## Career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Fowler-william_a.jpg/250px-Fowler-william_a.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Fowler-william_a.jpg)

Fowler's Los Alamos badge

In 1936, Fowler became a research fellow at Caltech. He was elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1938. In 1939, Fowler became an assistant professor at Caltech.

Although an experimental nuclear physicist, Fowler's most famous paper was his collaboration with Margaret and Geoffrey Burbidge, *[Synthesis of the Elements in Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesis_of_the_Elements_in_Stars "Synthesis of the Elements in Stars")*. Significantly, [Margaret Burbidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Burbidge "Margaret Burbidge") was first author, her husband [Geoffrey Burbidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Burbidge "Geoffrey Burbidge") second, Fowler third, and Cambridge cosmologist [Fred Hoyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Hoyle "Fred Hoyle") fourth. That 1957 paper in Reviews of Modern Physics categorized most nuclear processes for origin of all but the lightest [chemical elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element "Chemical element") in stars. It is widely known as the [B2FH paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B2FH_paper "B2FH paper"). Though the theory of Stellar Nucleosynthesis established in the paper was later cited by the Nobel Committee as the reason for Fowler's 1983 Nobel in Physics, neither any of the Burbidges nor Hoyle shared in the award.

In 1942, Fowler became an associate professor at Caltech. In 1946, Fowler became a Professor at Caltech. Fowler, along with [Lee A. DuBridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Alvin_DuBridge "Lee Alvin DuBridge"), [Max Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Mason "Max Mason"), [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling "Linus Pauling"), and [Bruce H. Sage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Hornbrook_Sage "Bruce Hornbrook Sage"), was awarded the [Medal for Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_for_Merit "Medal for Merit") in 1948 by President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman").

Fowler succeeded [Charles Lauritsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Christian_Lauritsen "Charles Christian Lauritsen") as director of the [W. K. Kellogg Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._K._Kellogg_Radiation_Laboratory "W. K. Kellogg Radiation Laboratory") at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology"), and was himself later succeeded by [Steven E. Koonin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_E._Koonin "Steven E. Koonin"). Fowler was awarded the National Medal of Science by President [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford "Gerald Ford").

Fowler was Guggenheim Fellow at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge "St John's College, Cambridge") in 1962–63. He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1962, won the [Henry Norris Russell Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Norris_Russell_Lectureship "Henry Norris Russell Lectureship") of the [American Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Astronomical_Society "American Astronomical Society") in 1963, elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1965, won the [Vetlesen Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vetlesen_Prize "Vetlesen Prize") in 1973, the [Eddington Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddington_Medal "Eddington Medal") in 1978, the [Bruce Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Medal "Bruce Medal") of the [Astronomical Society of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_Society_of_the_Pacific "Astronomical Society of the Pacific") in 1979, and the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1983 (shared with [Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subrahmanyan_Chandrasekhar "Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar")) for his theoretical and experimental studies of the [nuclear reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reaction "Nuclear reaction") of importance in the formation of the chemical elements in the universe .

Fowler's doctoral students at Caltech included [Donald D. Clayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_D._Clayton "Donald D. Clayton") and [George M. Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_M._Fuller "George M. Fuller").

## Personal life

A lifelong fan of [steam locomotives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_locomotive "Steam locomotive"), Fowler owned several working models of various sizes.

Fowler's first wife was Adriane Fay (née Olmsted) Fowler (1912-1988). They had two daughters, Mary Emily and Martha.

In December 1989, Fowler married Mary Dutcher, an artist, in Pasadena, California.
On 11 March 1995, Fowler died from kidney failure in [Pasadena, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena,_California "Pasadena, California"). He was 83.
