**Norman Foster Ramsey Jr.** (August 27, 1915 – November 4, 2011) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who was awarded the 1989 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for the invention of the separated oscillatory field method (see [Ramsey interferometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_interferometry "Ramsey interferometry")), which had important applications in the construction of [atomic clocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_clock "Atomic clock"). A [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") for most of his career, Ramsey also held several posts with such government and international agencies as [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") and the [United States Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Atomic_Energy_Commission "United States Atomic Energy Commission"). Among his other accomplishments are helping to found the [United States Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")'s [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory") and [Fermilab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermilab "Fermilab").

## Early life

Norman Foster Ramsey Jr. was born in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), on August 27, 1915, to Minna Bauer Ramsey and Norman Foster Ramsey. His mother was the daughter of German immigrants and an instructor at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas "University of Kansas"). His father, who was of Scottish descent, was a 1905 graduate of the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy "United States Military Academy") at [West Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Point "West Point") and an officer in the [Ordnance Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Department "Ordnance Department") who rose to the rank of [brigadier general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_General_(United_States) "Brigadier General (United States)") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), commanding the [Rock Island Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Island_Arsenal "Rock Island Arsenal"). He was raised as an [Army brat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_brat_(U.S._subculture) "Military brat (U.S. subculture)"), frequently moving from post to post, and lived in France for a time when his father was Liaison Officer with the *Direction d'Artillerie* and Assistant [Military Attaché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Attaché "Military Attaché"). This allowed him to skip a couple of grades along the way, so that he graduated from [Leavenworth High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leavenworth_High_School "Leavenworth High School") in [Leavenworth, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leavenworth,_Kansas "Leavenworth, Kansas"), at the age of 15.

Ramsey's parents hoped that he would go to West Point, but at 15, he was too young to be admitted. He was awarded a scholarship to the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas "University of Kansas"), but in 1930 his father was posted to [Governors Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governors_Island "Governors Island"), New York. Ramsey therefore entered [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in 1931 and began studying [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering "Engineering"). He became interested in [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") and switched to this as his [academic major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_major "Academic major"). By the time he received his [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") from Columbia in 1935, he had become interested in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics"). Columbia awarded him a [Kellett Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellett_Fellowship "Kellett Fellowship") to [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University "Cambridge University"), where he studied physics at [Cavendish Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Laboratory "Cavendish Laboratory") under [Lord Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Rutherford "Lord Rutherford") and [Maurice Goldhaber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Goldhaber "Maurice Goldhaber"), and encountered notable physicists, including [Edward Appleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Victor_Appleton "Edward Victor Appleton"), [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born "Max Born"), [Edward Bullard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bullard "Edward Bullard"), [James Chadwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Chadwick "James Chadwick"), [John Cockcroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cockcroft "John Cockcroft"), [Paul Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac"), [Arthur Eddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Eddington "Arthur Eddington"), [Ralph Fowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Howard_Fowler "Ralph Howard Fowler"), [Mark Oliphant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Oliphant "Mark Oliphant") and [J. J. Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Thomson "J. J. Thomson"). At Cambridge, he took the [tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripos "Tripos") in order to study [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"), which had not been covered at Columbia, resulting in being awarded a second BA degree by Cambridge.

A term paper Ramsey wrote for Goldhaber on [magnetic moments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_moment "Magnetic moment") caused him to read recent papers on the subject by [Isidor Isaac Rabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidor_Isaac_Rabi "Isidor Isaac Rabi"), and this stimulated an interest in [molecular beams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_beam "Molecular beam") and in doing research for a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") under Rabi at Columbia. Soon after Ramsey arrived at Columbia, Rabi invented [molecular-beam resonance spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance"), for which he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1944. Ramsey was part of Rabi's team that also included [Jerome Kellogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Kellogg?action=edit&redlink=1 "Jerome Kellogg"), [Polykarp Kusch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polykarp_Kusch "Polykarp Kusch"), [Sidney Millman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Millman?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sidney Millman") and [Jerrold Zacharias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerrold_Zacharias "Jerrold Zacharias"). Ramsey worked with them on the first experiments making use of the new technique and shared with Rabi and Zacharias in the discovery that the [deuteron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuteron "Deuteron") was a magnetic [quadrupole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrupole "Quadrupole"). This meant that the [atomic nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus "Atomic nucleus") was not spherical, as had been thought. He received his PhD in physics from Columbia in 1940 and became a fellow at the [Carnegie Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Institution "Carnegie Institution") in Washington, D.C., where he studied [neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron "Neutron")–[proton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") and proton–[helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium "Helium") [scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering "Scattering").

## World War II

### MIT Radiation Laboratory

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/P-61_from_above.jpg/250px-P-61_from_above.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:P-61_from_above.jpg)

The Northrop P-61 Black Widow [night fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_fighter "Night fighter") was specifically designed to take advantage of the new radar.

In 1940, he married Elinor Jameson of [Brooklyn, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn,_New_York "Brooklyn, New York"), and accepted a teaching position at the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign "University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign"). The two expected to spend the rest of their lives there, but World War II intervened. In September 1940 the British [Tizard Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tizard_Mission "Tizard Mission") brought a number of new technologies to the United States, including a [cavity magnetron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavity_magnetron "Cavity magnetron"), a high-powered device that generates [microwaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave "Microwave") using the interaction of a stream of [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") with a [magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field "Magnetic field"), which promised to revolutionize [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar"). [Alfred Lee Loomis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Lee_Loomis "Alfred Lee Loomis") of the [National Defense Research Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Research_Committee "National Defense Research Committee") established the [Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_Laboratory "Radiation Laboratory") at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") to develop this technology. Ramsey was one of the scientists recruited by Rabi for this work.

Initially, Ramsey was in Rabi's magnetron group. When Rabi became a division head, Ramsey became the group leader. The role of the group was to develop the magnetron to permit a reduction in [wavelength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength "Wavelength") from 150 centimetres (59 in) to 10 centimetres (3.9 in), and then to 3 centimetres (1.2 in) or [X band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_band "X band"). Microwave radar promised to be small, lighter and more efficient than older types. Ramsey's group started with the design produced by Oliphant's team in Britain and attempted to improve it. The Radiation Laboratory produced the designs, which were prototyped by [Raytheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raytheon "Raytheon"), and then tested by the laboratory. In June 1941, Ramsey travelled to Britain, where he met with Oliphant, and the two exchanged ideas. He brought back some British components, which were incorporated into the final design. A [night fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_fighter "Night fighter") aircraft, the [Northrop P-61 Black Widow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_P-61_Black_Widow "Northrop P-61 Black Widow"), was designed around the new radar. Ramsey returned to Washington in late 1942 as an adviser on the use of the new 3 cm microwave radar sets that were now coming into service, working for [Edward L. Bowles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_L._Bowles "Edward L. Bowles") in the office of the [Secretary of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_War "Secretary of War"), [Henry L. Stimson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_L._Stimson "Henry L. Stimson").

### Manhattan Project

In 1943, Ramsey was approached by [Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Oppenheimer "Robert Oppenheimer") and [Robert Bacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bacher "Robert Bacher"), who asked him to join the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project"). Ramsey agreed to do so, but the intervention of the project director, Brigadier General [Leslie R. Groves Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_R._Groves_Jr. "Leslie R. Groves Jr."), was necessary in order to prise him away from the Secretary of War's office. A compromise was agreed to, whereby Ramsey remained on the payroll of the Secretary of War and was merely seconded to the Manhattan Project. In October 1943, Group E-7 of the Ordnance Division was created at the [Los Alamos Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_Laboratory "Los Alamos Laboratory") with Ramsey as group leader, with the task of integrating the design and delivery of the [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon") being built by the laboratory.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Ramsey_signs_the_Fat_man.jpg/250px-Ramsey_signs_the_Fat_man.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ramsey_signs_the_Fat_man.jpg)

Ramsey signs the [Fat Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat_Man "Fat Man") used at Nagasaki.

The first thing he had to do was determine the characteristics of the aircraft that would be used. There were only two [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II") aircraft large enough: the British [Avro Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Lancaster "Avro Lancaster") and the US [Boeing B-29 Superfortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-29_Superfortress "Boeing B-29 Superfortress"). The United States Army Air Forces (USAAF) wanted to use the B-29 if at all possible, even though it required substantial modification. Ramsey supervised the test drop program, which began at [Dahlgren, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Surface_Warfare_Center_Dahlgren_Division "Naval Surface Warfare Center Dahlgren Division"), in August 1943, before moving to [Muroc Dry Lake, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwards_Air_Force_Base "Edwards Air Force Base"), in March 1944. Mock-ups of [Thin Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thin_Man_nuclear_bomb "Thin Man nuclear bomb") and [Fat Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat_Man "Fat Man") bombs were dropped and tracked by an [SCR-584](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCR-584 "SCR-584") ground-based radar set of the kind that Ramsey had helped develop at the Radiation laboratory. Numerous problems were discovered with the bombs and the aircraft modifications, and corrected.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Ramsey-norman_f.jpg/250px-Ramsey-norman_f.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ramsey-norman_f.jpg)

Ramsey's Los Alamos badge

Plans for the delivery of the weapons in combat were assigned to the Weapons Committee, which was chaired by Ramsey and answerable to [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_(United_States_O-6) "Captain (United States O-6)") [William S. Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Parsons "William S. Parsons"). Ramsey drew up tables of organization and equipment for the [Project Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Alberta "Project Alberta") detachment that would accompany the USAAF's [509th Composite Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/509th_Composite_Group "509th Composite Group") to [Tinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinian "Tinian"). Ramsey briefed the 509th's commander, Lieutenant Colonel [Paul W. Tibbets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_W._Tibbets "Paul W. Tibbets"), on the nature of the mission when the latter assumed command of the 509th. Ramsey went to Tinian with the Project Alberta detachment as Parsons's scientific and technical deputy. He was involved in the assembly of the Fat Man bomb and relayed Parsons's message indicating the success of the [bombing of Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Hiroshima "Bombing of Hiroshima") to Groves in Washington, D.C.

## Research

At the end of the war, Ramsey returned to Columbia as a professor and research scientist. Rabi and Ramsey picked up where they had left off before the war with their molecular-beam experiments. Ramsey and his first graduate student, [William Nierenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Nierenberg "William Nierenberg"), measured various nuclear [magnetic dipole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_dipole "Magnetic dipole") and [electric quadrupole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_quadrupole "Electric quadrupole") moments. With Rabi, he helped establish the [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory") on [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island "Long Island"). In 1946, he became the first head of the Physics Department there. His time there was brief, for in 1947, he joined the physics faculty at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), where he would remain for the next 40 years, except for brief visiting professorships at [Middlebury College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlebury_College "Middlebury College"), [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University "Oxford University"), [Mt. Holyoke College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt._Holyoke_College "Mt. Holyoke College") and the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia"). During the 1950s, he was the first science adviser to [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") and initiated a series of fellowships, grants and summer school programs to train European scientists.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e1/Cyclo_ramsey_daven.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cyclo_ramsey_daven.jpg)

The Harvard cyclotron during construction in 1948. Shown are Ramsey (left) and [Lee Davenport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Davenport "Lee Davenport") (right).

Ramsey's research in the immediate post-war years looked at measuring fundamental properties of atoms and molecules by use of molecular beams. On moving to Harvard, his objective was to carry out accurate molecular-beam magnetic-resonance experiments, based on the techniques developed by Rabi. However, the accuracy of the measurements depended on the uniformity of the magnetic field, and Ramsey found that it was difficult to create sufficiently uniform magnetic fields. He developed the separated oscillatory field method in 1949 as a means of achieving the accuracy he wanted.

Ramsey and his PhD student [Daniel Kleppner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kleppner "Daniel Kleppner") developed the atomic-hydrogen [maser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maser "Maser"), looking to increase the accuracy with which the [hyperfine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperfine_structure "Hyperfine structure") separations of atomic [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen "Hydrogen"), [deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium "Deuterium") and [tritium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium "Tritium") could be measured, as well as to investigate how much the hyperfine structure was affected by external magnetic and electric fields. He also participated in developing an extremely stable clock based on a [hydrogen maser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_maser "Hydrogen maser"). From 1967 until 2019, [the second has been defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_revision_of_the_SI "2019 revision of the SI") based on 9,192,631,770 Hz hyperfine transition of a [cesium-133](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesium-133 "Cesium-133") atom; the [atomic clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_clock "Atomic clock") which is used to set this standard is an application of Ramsey's work. He was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1989 "for the invention of the separated oscillatory fields method and its use in the hydrogen maser and other atomic clocks". The Prize was shared with [Hans Georg Dehmelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Georg_Dehmelt "Hans Georg Dehmelt") and [Wolfgang Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Paul "Wolfgang Paul").

In collaboration with the [Institut Laue–Langevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Laue–Langevin "Institut Laue–Langevin"), Ramsey also worked on applying similar methods to beams of neutrons, measuring the [neutron magnetic moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_magnetic_moment "Neutron magnetic moment") and finding a limit to its [electric dipole moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_electric_dipole_moment "Neutron electric dipole moment"). As president of the [Universities Research Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities_Research_Association "Universities Research Association") during the 1960s he was involved in the design and construction of the [Fermilab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermilab "Fermilab") in [Batavia, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Illinois "Batavia, Illinois"). He also headed a 1982 [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council "United States National Research Council") committee that concluded that, contrary to the findings of the [House of Representatives Select Committee on Assassinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_Select_Committee_on_Assassinations "House of Representatives Select Committee on Assassinations"), [acoustic evidence did not indicate the presence of a second gunman's involvement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy_assassination_Dictabelt_recording "John F. Kennedy assassination Dictabelt recording") in the [assassination of President John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_President_John_F._Kennedy "Assassination of President John F. Kennedy").

## Later life

Ramsey eventually became the [Eugene Higgins professor of physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Higgins_professor_of_physics?action=edit&redlink=1 "Eugene Higgins professor of physics") at Harvard and retired in 1986. However, he remained active in physics, spending a year as a research fellow at the [Joint Institute for Laboratory Astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Institute_for_Laboratory_Astrophysics "Joint Institute for Laboratory Astrophysics") (JILA) at the [University of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado "University of Colorado"). He also continued visiting professorships at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"), [Williams College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_College "Williams College") and the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan"). In addition to the Nobel Prize in Physics, Ramsey received a number of awards, including the [Ernest Orlando Lawrence Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Orlando_Lawrence_Award "Ernest Orlando Lawrence Award") in 1960, [Davisson–Germer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davisson–Germer_Prize "Davisson–Germer Prize") in 1974, the [IEEE Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Medal_of_Honor "IEEE Medal of Honor") in 1984, the [Rabi Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._I._Rabi_Award "I. I. Rabi Award") in 1985, the [Rumford Premium Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumford_Prize "Rumford Prize") in 1985, the Compton Medal in 1986, and the [Oersted Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oersted_Medal "Oersted Medal") and the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in 1988. In 1990, Ramsey received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement"). He was an elected member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"), and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"). In 2004, he signed a letter along with 47 other [Nobel laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate "Nobel laureate") endorsing [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry "John Kerry") for President of the United States as someone who would "restore science to its appropriate place in government".

His first wife, Elinor, died in 1983, after which he married Ellie Welch of Brookline, Massachusetts. Ramsey died on November 4, 2011. He was survived by his wife Ellie, his four daughters from his first marriage, and his stepdaughter and stepson from his second marriage.
