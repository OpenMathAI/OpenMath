**Felix Bloch** (23 October 1905 – 10 September 1983) was a Swiss–American theoretical [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared the 1952 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Edward Mills Purcell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Mills_Purcell "Edward Mills Purcell") "for their development of new methods for nuclear magnetic precision measurements and discoveries in connection therewith."

He was the first [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") Nobel laureate.

Bloch made fundamental theoretical contributions to the understanding of [ferromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism "Ferromagnetism") and [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") behavior in [crystal lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bravais_lattice "Bravais lattice"). He is also considered one of the developers of [nuclear magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance").

## Education

Felix Bloch was born on 23 October 1905 in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich "Zurich"), Switzerland, to [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") parents, Gustav Bloch and Agnes Mayer. Gustav was financially unable to attend university and worked as a wholesale grain dealer in Zurich. Gustav moved to Zurich from [Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravia "Moravia") in 1890 to become a Swiss citizen. Their first child was a girl born in 1902, while Felix was born three years later.

Bloch entered public elementary school at the age of six and is said to have been teased, in part because he "spoke [Swiss German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_German "Swiss German") with a somewhat different accent than most members of the class". He received support from his older sister during much of this time, but she died at the age of 12, devastating Felix, who is said to have lived a "depressed and isolated life" in the following years. Bloch learned to play the piano by the age of 8 and was drawn to arithmetic for its "clarity and beauty". Bloch graduated from elementary school at twelve and enrolled in the Cantonal Gymnasium in Zurich for secondary school in 1918. He was placed on a six-year curriculum here to prepare him for university. He continued his curriculum through 1924, even through his study of engineering and physics in other schools, though it was limited to mathematics and languages after the first three years.

After these first three years at the Gymnasium, at the age of 15, Bloch began to study at the [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich"). Although he initially studied engineering, he soon changed to physics. During this time, he attended lectures and seminars given by [Peter Debye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Debye "Peter Debye") and [Hermann Weyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Weyl "Hermann Weyl") at the ETH Zurich and [Erwin Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger "Erwin Schrödinger") at the neighboring [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich"). A fellow student in these seminars was [John von Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann "John von Neumann").

Bloch graduated in 1927, and was encouraged by Debye to go to the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig "University of Leipzig") to study under [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg "Werner Heisenberg"). Bloch became Heisenberg's first graduate student, and gained his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in 1928. His thesis established the [quantum theory of solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"), using waves to describe [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") in periodic lattices.

## Career and research

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Felix_Bloch_1950s.jpg/330px-Felix_Bloch_1950s.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Felix_Bloch_1950s.jpg)

Felix Bloch in the lab, 1950s

Bloch remained in European academia, working on [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity") with [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli") in Zurich; with [Hans Kramers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Kramers "Hans Kramers") and [Adriaan Fokker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriaan_Fokker "Adriaan Fokker") in the Netherlands; with Heisenberg on [ferromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism "Ferromagnetism"), where he developed a description of boundaries between [magnetic domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_domain "Magnetic domain"), now known as [Bloch walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_wall_(magnetism)#Bloch_wall "Domain wall (magnetism)"), and theoretically proposed a concept of [spin waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_wave "Spin wave"), excitations of [magnetic structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_structure "Magnetic structure"); with [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr "Niels Bohr") in Copenhagen, where he worked on a theoretical description of the stopping of [charged particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charged_particle "Charged particle") traveling through matter; and with [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi") in Rome.

In 1932, Bloch returned to Leipzig to assume a position as *[Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent "Privatdozent")* (lecturer). In 1933, immediately after [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") came to power, Bloch left Germany out of fear of [anti-Jewish persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Jewish_persecution "Anti-Jewish persecution"), returning to Zurich before traveling to Paris to lecture at the [Institut Henri Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Henri_Poincaré "Institut Henri Poincaré").

In 1934, the chairman of the Physics Department of [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") invited Bloch to join the faculty. Bloch accepted the offer and emigrated to the United States. In the fall of 1938, Bloch began working with the 37 inch [cyclotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotron "Cyclotron") at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), to determine the magnetic moment of the neutron. Bloch went on to become the first professor of theoretical physics at Stanford. He became a [naturalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized "Naturalized") U.S. citizen in 1939.

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Bloch briefly worked on the [atomic bomb project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project") at [Los Alamos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory "Los Alamos National Laboratory"). Disliking the military atmosphere of the laboratory and uninterested in the theoretical work there, Bloch left to join the [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar") project at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University").

After the war, Bloch concentrated on investigations into nuclear induction and [nuclear magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance"), which are the underlying principles of [MRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging "Magnetic resonance imaging"). In 1946, he proposed the [Bloch equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloch_equations "Bloch equations"), which determine the time evolution of nuclear magnetization. Along with [Edward Purcell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Mills_Purcell "Edward Mills Purcell"), Bloch was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1952 for his work on nuclear magnetic induction.

When [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN") was being set up in the early 1950s, its founders were searching for someone of stature and international prestige to head the fledgling international laboratory, and in 1954 Professor Bloch became CERN's first director-general, at the time when construction was getting under way on the present [Meyrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meyrin "Meyrin") site and plans for the first machines were being drawn up. After leaving CERN, Bloch returned to Stanford University, where in 1961 he was made Max Stein Professor of Physics. He retired from Stanford in 1971.

## Family

On 14 March 1940, Bloch married Lore Clara Misch (1911–1996), a fellow physicist working on [X-ray crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography "X-ray crystallography"), whom he had met at an [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") meeting. They had four children, twins George Jacob Bloch and Daniel Arthur Bloch (born 15 January 1941), son Frank Samuel Bloch (born 16 January 1945), and daughter Ruth Hedy Bloch (born 15 September 1949).

Bloch died on 10 September 1983 in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich "Zurich") at the age of 77. In 2025, Bloch's family donated his [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") to [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN").
