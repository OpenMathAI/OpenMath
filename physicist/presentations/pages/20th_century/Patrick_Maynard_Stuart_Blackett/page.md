**Patrick Maynard Stuart Blackett, Baron Blackett of Chelsea** (18 November 1897 – 13 July 1974), was a British experimental [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and [life peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peer "Life peer") who received the 1948 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"). In 1925, he was the first person to prove that [radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity "Radioactivity") could cause the [nuclear transmutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_transmutation "Nuclear transmutation") of one chemical element to another. He also made major contributions to the [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II") war effort in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), advising on military strategy and developing [operational research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_research "Operational research").

In the war's aftermath, Blackett continued his scientific work, but also became outspoken on political matters. He advocated for restraints on the military use of atomic energy. He was a proponent for [Third World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_World "Third World") development and for reducing the [gap between rich and poor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gap_between_rich_and_poor "Gap between rich and poor"). In the 1950s and '60s, he was a key advisor to the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") on science and technology policy. By the time of his death in 1974, Blackett had become controversial to the point that the *[Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")* obituary referred to him as the "Radical Nobel-Prize Winning Physicist."

## Early life and education

Patrick Maynard Stuart Blackett was born on 18 November 1897 in [Kensington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington "Kensington"), [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London"), the son of Arthur Stuart Blackett, a stockbroker, and Caroline Maynard. His younger sister was [Marion Milner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Milner "Marion Milner"), a noted psychoanalyst. His paternal grandfather, the Rev. Henry Blackett, brother of Australian architect [Edmund Blacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Blacket "Edmund Blacket"), was for many years Vicar of [Croydon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croydon "Croydon"). His maternal grandfather, Charles Maynard, was an officer in the [Royal Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Artillery "Royal Artillery") at the time of the [Indian Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Mutiny "Indian Mutiny"). The Blackett family lived successively in Kensington, [Kenley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenley "Kenley"), [Woking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woking "Woking") and [Guildford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guildford "Guildford"), and [Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey "Surrey"), where he went to [preparatory school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparatory_school_(UK) "Preparatory school (UK)"). His main hobbies were [model aeroplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_aeroplanes "Model aeroplanes") and [crystal radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_radio "Crystal radio"). When he interviewed for entrance to the [Royal Naval College, Osborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_College,_Osborne "Royal Naval College, Osborne"), Isle of Wight, [Charles Rolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Rolls "Charles Rolls") had just completed his cross-channel flight the previous day and Patrick, who had tracked the flight on his crystal set, was able to expound lengthily on the subject. He was accepted and spent two years there before moving on to [Dartmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britannia_Royal_Naval_College "Britannia Royal Naval College") where he was "usually head of his class."

In August 1914 at the outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), Blackett was assigned to active service as a [midshipman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midshipman "Midshipman"). He was transferred to the [Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde "Cape Verde") Islands on [HMS *Carnarvon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Carnarvon_(1903) "HMS Carnarvon (1903)") and was present at the [Battle of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Falkland_Islands "Battle of the Falkland Islands"). He was then transferred to [HMS *Barham*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Barham_(04) "HMS Barham (04)") and saw much action at the [Battle of Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jutland "Battle of Jutland"). While on the *Barham*, Blackett was co-inventor of a gunnery device on which the [Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty "British Admiralty") took out a patent. In 1916, he applied to join the [RNAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNAS "RNAS") but his application was refused. In October of that year, he became a sub-lieutenant on HMS *P17* on [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover "Dover") patrol, and in July 1917 he was posted to [HMS *Sturgeon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Sturgeon "HMS Sturgeon") in the [Harwich Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harwich_Force "Harwich Force") under [Admiral Tyrwhitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Tyrwhitt "Reginald Tyrwhitt"). Blackett was concerned by the poor quality of British gunnery in the Harwich Force when compared with that of the enemy. In May 1918, he was promoted to lieutenant, but by then had decided to leave the Navy. He started to read science textbooks as he planned his post-war career.

In January 1919, the Admiralty sent the officers whose training had been interrupted by the war to the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") for a course of general duties. On his first night at [Magdalene College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalene_College,_Cambridge "Magdalene College, Cambridge"), he met [Kingsley Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsley_Martin "Kingsley Martin") and [Geoffrey Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Webb "Geoffrey Webb"), later recalling that he had never before, in his naval training, heard intellectual conversation. Blackett was impressed by the prestigious [Cavendish Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Laboratory "Cavendish Laboratory"), and left the Navy to study mathematics and physics at Cambridge.

## Career and research

After graduating from Magdalene College in 1921, Blackett spent ten years working in the [Cavendish Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Laboratory "Cavendish Laboratory") as an experimental physicist with [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford "Ernest Rutherford"), and in 1923 became a Fellow of [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge "King's College, Cambridge"), a position he held until 1933.

Rutherford had discovered that the nucleus of the [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen "Nitrogen") atom could be disintegrated by firing fast [alpha particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_particles "Alpha particles") into nitrogen. He asked Blackett to use a [cloud chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_chamber "Cloud chamber") to find visible tracks of this disintegration, and by 1925, Blackett had taken 23,000 photographs showing 415,000 tracks of ionized particles. Eight of these were forked, and this showed that the nitrogen atom-alpha particle combination had formed an atom of [fluorine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorine "Fluorine"), which then disintegrated into an isotope of [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen "Oxygen") 17 and a proton. Blackett published the results of his experiments in 1925. He thus became the first person to deliberately transmute one [element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element "Chemical element") into another.

During his time at Cambridge, Blackett was the head tutor of the young American graduate student, [J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer "J. Robert Oppenheimer"). The latter's desire to study [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics") rather than focus on lab work brought him into conflict with Blackett. The commonly told story of Oppenheimer's retaliatory attempt to poison Blackett is likely fictional and has been directly disputed by Oppenheimer's grandson. Additionally, Cambridge has no record of any attempted poisoning.

Blackett spent one year (1924–1925) in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen "Göttingen"), Germany, working with [James Franck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Franck "James Franck") on [atomic spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_spectra "Atomic spectra"). In 1932, Blackett partnered with [Giuseppe Occhialini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Occhialini "Giuseppe Occhialini") to devise a system of [Geiger counters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geiger_counter "Geiger counter") which took photographs only when a [cosmic ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray "Cosmic ray") particle traversed the chamber. They found 500 tracks of high energy cosmic ray particles in 700 automatic exposures. In 1933, Blackett discovered fourteen tracks which confirmed the existence of the [positron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron "Positron") and revealed the now instantly recognisable opposing spiral traces of positron/electron [pair production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_production "Pair production"). He and Occhialini published their findings in a landmark 1933 paper in *[Proceedings of the Royal Society A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_Royal_Society "Proceedings of the Royal Society")*. This work, combined with his research on [annihilation radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annihilation_radiation "Annihilation radiation"), made Blackett a leading expert in the new theory of [antimatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimatter "Antimatter").

In 1933, Blackett moved to [Birkbeck, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkbeck,_University_of_London "Birkbeck, University of London"), as Professor of Physics, and stayed for four years. In 1937, he went to the [Victoria University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester "Victoria University of Manchester"), where he was elected to the [Langworthy Professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langworthy_Professor "Langworthy Professor") and created a major international research laboratory. The Blackett Memorial Hall and Blackett Lecture Theatre at the University of Manchester were subsequently named after him.

In 1947, Blackett introduced [a theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackett_effect "Blackett effect") to account for the [Earth's magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_magnetic_field "Earth's magnetic field") as a function of its rotation, with the hope that it would unify both the [electromagnetic force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_force "Electromagnetic force") and the force of [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity "Gravity"). He spent a number of years developing high-quality [magnetometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetometer "Magnetometer") to test his theory, but eventually found it to be without merit. However, his work on the subject led him into [geophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysics "Geophysics"), where he later helped process data relating to [paleomagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleomagnetism "Paleomagnetism"), and also provided strong evidence for [continental drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_drift "Continental drift").

Blackett ca. 1950

In 1953, Blackett was appointed head of the Physics Department at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London "Imperial College London"), and retired from there in July 1963. The Physics department building of Imperial College, the [Blackett Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackett_Laboratory "Blackett Laboratory"), is named in his honour.

In 1957, Blackett gave the presidential address ("Technology and World Advancement") to the [British Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Science_Association "British Science Association") meeting in Dublin In 1965, he delivered the MacMillan Memorial Lecture to the [Institution of Engineers and Shipbuilders in Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institution_of_Engineers_and_Shipbuilders_in_Scotland "Institution of Engineers and Shipbuilders in Scotland"). He chose the subject "Continental Drift".

### World War II and operational research

In 1935, Blackett was invited to join the [Aeronautical Research Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautical_Research_Committee "Aeronautical Research Committee") chaired by [Sir Henry Tizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Tizard "Henry Tizard"). The committee was effective in advocating for the early installation of [Radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar") for [air defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aircraft_warfare "Anti-aircraft warfare"). At the beginning of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Blackett served on various committees and spent time at the [Royal Aircraft Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Aircraft_Establishment "Royal Aircraft Establishment") (RAE) [Farnborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnborough_Airfield "Farnborough Airfield"), where he made a major contribution to the design of the [Mark XIV bomb sight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_XIV_bomb_sight "Mark XIV bomb sight"), which allowed bombs to be released without a level bombing run beforehand. In 1940–41, he served on the [MAUD Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAUD_Committee "MAUD Committee") which concluded that an [atomic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bomb "Atomic bomb") was feasible. He disagreed with the committee's conclusion that Britain could produce an atomic bomb by 1943, and he recommended that the project should be discussed with the Americans.

In August 1940, Blackett became scientific adviser to Lieutenant General [Sir Frederick Pile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Frederick_Pile "Sir Frederick Pile"), Commander in Chief of [Anti-Aircraft Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Aircraft_Command "Anti-Aircraft Command") and thus began the work that resulted in the field of study known as [operational research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_research "Operational research") (OR). He was director of Operational Research with the [Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty "British Admiralty") from 1942 to 1945, and his work with [E. J. Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_James_Williams "Evan James Williams") improved the survival odds of [convoys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convoy "Convoy"), presented counter-intuitive but correct recommendations for the armour-plating of aircraft, and achieved many other successes. His aim, he said, was to base military strategy on numbers, not "gusts of emotions." During the war he criticised the assumptions in [Lord Cherwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Cherwell "Lord Cherwell")'s [dehousing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehousing "Dehousing") paper and sided with Tizard who argued that fewer resources should go to [RAF Bomber Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Bomber_Command "RAF Bomber Command") for the [area bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_bombing "Area bombing") offensive and more to the other armed forces. Blackett's studies had shown the ineffectiveness of the area bombing strategies, as opposed to the importance of fighting off the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") [U-boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-boats "U-boats"), which were heavily affecting the war effort with their [sinkings of merchant ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic "Battle of the Atlantic"). In this opinion, he chafed against the existing military authority and was cut out of various circles of communications. However, after the war, the Allied [Strategic Bombing Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Bombing_Survey "Strategic Bombing Survey") proved Blackett correct.

### Politics

While an undergraduate, Blackett befriended [Kingsley Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsley_Martin "Kingsley Martin"), the future editor of the *[New Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Statesman "New Statesman")*; their talks on politics contributed to Blackett's move to the left. He later identified himself as a socialist, and often campaigned on behalf of the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)"). In the aftermath of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Blackett became known for his radical political opinions, which included a belief that Britain ought not to develop atomic weapons. His biographer [Mary Jo Nye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Jo_Nye "Mary Jo Nye") noted:

> Blackett became the first person to openly argue that the United States had used the atomic bomb in Japan "not so much as the last military act of the Second World War, as the first act of the cold diplomatic war with Russia." Outraged Americans characterized Blackett's statements and his opposition to their development of atomic weapons as a Stalinist apology full of political prejudices. [George Orwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Orwell "George Orwell") in 1949 included Blackett on [a blacklist of thirty-eight crypto-communists or fellow-travellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orwell's_list "Orwell's list") that Orwell drew up for the British [Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign,_Commonwealth_and_Development_Office "Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office").

It was subsequently revealed that Blackett was under [MI5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MI5 "MI5") investigation during this time. As a result of his political controversies, he was considered too far left for the [post-war Labour Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Government_1945–1951 "Labour Government 1945–1951") to employ, and he returned to academic life.

Blackett's internationalism found expression in his strong support for India. In 1947 he met [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru "Jawaharlal Nehru"), who sought the scientist's advice on the research and development needs of the [Indian armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_armed_forces "Indian armed forces"). For the next 20 years, Blackett was a frequent visitor and advisor to India on military and civil science. These visits deepened his concern for the underprivileged and the poor. He was convinced that their problems could be overcome by applying science and technology. He used his prestige in the scientific community to try to persuade fellow scientists that one of their first duties should be to help ensure a decent life for all mankind. Before underdevelopment became a popular issue, Blackett proposed in a 1957 presidential address to the [British Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Science_Association "British Science Association") that his country should devote 1% of its national income to the [economic improvement of the Third World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_aid "Development aid"), and he was later one of the prime movers in the founding of the Overseas Development Institute.

During the 13 years when the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") was out of office, Blackett was the senior member of a group of scientists who met regularly to discuss scientific and technological policy. This group grew in influence when [Harold Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Wilson "Harold Wilson") assumed leadership of the Party. Blackett's suggestions directly led to the creation of the [Ministry of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Technology "Minister of Technology") as soon as the [Wilson government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Government_1964–1970 "Labour Government 1964–1970") was formed, and he insisted that a top priority should be revival of Britain's [computer industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_industry "Computer industry"). Blackett did not enter open politics, but worked for a year as a civil servant. He remained deputy chairman of the Minister's Advisory Council throughout the administration's life, and was also personal scientific adviser to the Minister.

### Influence in fiction

- Blackett's theories of planetary magnetism and gravity were taken up by the science fiction author [James Blish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Blish "James Blish"), who cited the [Blackett effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackett_effect "Blackett effect") as the theoretical "basis" behind his "[spindizzy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spindizzy_(Cities_in_Flight) "Spindizzy (Cities in Flight)")" [antigravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigravity "Antigravity") drive.
- In his close friend [C. P. Snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._P._Snow "C. P. Snow")'s [novel sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novel_sequence "Novel sequence") *[Strangers and Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangers_and_Brothers "Strangers and Brothers")* (1940–1974), aspects of Blackett's personality are drawn upon for the left-wing physicist Francis Getliffe.
- Blackett and his dictum, "You can't run a war on gusts of emotion", appear in the 'alternative' WWII novel, *[Gravity's Rainbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity's_Rainbow "Gravity's Rainbow")*.

## Personal life

Blackett married Costanza Bayon (1899–1986) in March 1924. They had a daughter, Giovanna (born 1926) and a son, Nicolas (born 1928).

Blackett was an agnostic or atheist.

Blackett died on 13 July 1974 in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") at the age of 76. His ashes are buried at the [Kensal Green Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensal_Green_Cemetery "Kensal Green Cemetery") in London.

[Bernard Lovell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lovell "Bernard Lovell") wrote of Blackett: "Those who worked with Blackett in the laboratory were dominated by his immensely powerful personality, and those who knew him elsewhere soon discovered that the public image thinly veiled a sensitive and humane spirit."

[Edward Bullard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bullard "Edward Bullard") said that he was the most versatile and best loved physicist of his generation and that his achievement was also without rival: "he was wonderfully intelligent, charming, fun to be with, dignified and handsome."

## Commemoration

The [Blackett Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackett_Laboratory "Blackett Laboratory") is part of [Imperial College Faculty of Natural Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_Faculty_of_Natural_Sciences "Imperial College Faculty of Natural Sciences") and has housed the Physics Department since its completion in 1961.

[Blackett crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackett_(crater) "Blackett (crater)") on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon") is named after him.

In 2016, the house that Blackett lived in from 1953 to 1969 (48 Paultons Square, Chelsea, London) received an [English Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Heritage "English Heritage") [blue plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_plaque "Blue plaque").

In July 2022, the [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy "Royal Navy") named an experimental ship after Blackett in honour of his service to the Royal Navy and to the country; [XV *Patrick Blackett*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XV_Patrick_Blackett "XV Patrick Blackett") will be used by the Royal Navy to experiment with autonomous technologies.

The [Patrick M. S. Blackett Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_M._S._Blackett_Institute_(Erice)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Patrick M. S. Blackett Institute (Erice)") in [Erice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erice "Erice"), [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily "Sicily"), a teaching and conference venue of the [Ettore Majorana Foundation and Centre for Scientific Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettore_Majorana_Foundation_and_Centre_for_Scientific_Culture "Ettore Majorana Foundation and Centre for Scientific Culture").

## In popular culture

Blackett was portrayed by [James D'Arcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_D'Arcy "James D'Arcy") in the 2023 film *[Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_(film) "Oppenheimer (film)")*.
