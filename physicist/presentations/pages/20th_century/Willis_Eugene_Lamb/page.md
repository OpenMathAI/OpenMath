**Willis Eugene Lamb Jr.** (July 12, 1913 – May 15, 2008) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who was able to precisely determine a surprising shift in [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") energies in a [hydrogen atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_atom "Hydrogen atom"), known as the [Lamb shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_shift "Lamb shift"). For this work, Lamb shared the 1955 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Polykarp Kusch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polykarp_Kusch "Polykarp Kusch"). He was a professor at the [University of Arizona College of Optical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona_College_of_Optical_Sciences "University of Arizona College of Optical Sciences").

## Education and career

Willis Eugene Lamb Jr. was born on July 12, 1913 in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), California, the son of Willis Eugene Lamb Sr., a telephone engineer, and Marie Helen Metcalf. First admitted in 1930, he obtained a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in Chemistry from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), in 1934. For theoretical work on scattering of neutrons by a crystal, guided by [J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer "J. Robert Oppenheimer"), Lamb received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in Physics in 1938. Because of limited computational methods available at the time, this research narrowly missed revealing the [Mössbauer Effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mössbauer_Effect "Mössbauer Effect"), 19 years before its recognition by [Rudolf Mössbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Mössbauer "Rudolf Mössbauer"). He worked on nuclear theory, laser physics, and verifying quantum mechanics.

In 1938, Lamb became Instructor in Physics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), where he was promoted to Professor of Physics in 1948. In 1951, he was appointed Professor of Physics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"). From 1956 to 1962, he was [Wykeham Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wykeham_Professor "Wykeham Professor") of Physics at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford"), where he was also a Fellow of [New College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College,_Oxford "New College, Oxford"). In 1962, he was appointed Henry Ford II Professor of Physics at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"). In 1974, he became Professor of Physics and Optical Science at the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona "University of Arizona"), where he remained until his retirement in 2003.

## Quantum physics

In addition to his crucial and famous contribution to [quantum electrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_electrodynamics "Quantum electrodynamics") via the [Lamb shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_shift "Lamb shift"), in the latter part of his career he paid increasing attention to the field of [quantum measurements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurement_in_quantum_mechanics "Measurement in quantum mechanics"). In one of his writings Lamb stated that "most people who use quantum mechanics have little need to know much about the interpretation of the subject." Lamb was also openly critical of many of the [interpretational trends on quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretations_of_quantum_mechanics "Interpretations of quantum mechanics") and of the use of the term [photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon "Photon").

## Personal life and death

In 1939, Lamb married [Ursula Schäfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_Lamb "Ursula Lamb") (1914–1996), a German student who became a distinguished historian of Latin America (and assumed his last name).

Lamb died on May 15, 2008, at the age of 94, due to complications of a gallstone disorder.

Lamb is remembered as a "rare theorist turned experimentalist" by D. Kaiser.
