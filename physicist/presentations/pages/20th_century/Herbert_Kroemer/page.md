**Herbert Kroemer** (German: [[ˈhɛʁbɛʁt ˈkʁøːmɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Herbert_Kroemer.ogg "File:De-Herbert Kroemer.ogg"); August 25, 1928 – March 8, 2024) was a German–American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who, along with [Zhores Alferov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhores_Alferov "Zhores Alferov"), received the 2000 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") "for developing semiconductor [heterostructures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterostructure "Heterostructure") used in high-speed- and opto-electronics." His research into [transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor "Transistor") was a stepping stone to the later development of [mobile phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone "Mobile phone") technologies.

## Education

Herbert Kroemer was born on August 25, 1928, in [Weimar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar "Weimar"), [State of Thuringia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Thuringia_(1920–1952) "State of Thuringia (1920–1952)"). His father was a civil servant, while his mother was a housewife; neither of them had a high school education. Kroemer excelled in physics at school, letting him advance faster than his peers in the subject.

Kroemer received his *[Diplom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplom "Diplom")* in 1951, and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in Theoretical Physics the following year, both from the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"). His doctoral thesis was on [hot electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_electron "Hot electron") effects in the then-new [transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor "Transistor").

## Career and research

From 1952 to 1968, Kroemer worked in a number of research laboratories in Germany and the United States. In 1968, he was appointed Professor of Electrical Engineering at the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder "University of Colorado Boulder"). He joined the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara "University of California, Santa Barbara"), in 1976, focusing its semiconductor research program on the emerging compound semiconductor technology rather than on mainstream [silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon "Silicon") technology.

Kroemer always preferred to work on problems that are ahead of mainstream technology, inventing the [drift transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drift-field_transistor "Drift-field transistor") in the 1950s and being the first to point out that advantages could be gained in various semiconductor devices by incorporating [heterojunctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterojunctions "Heterojunctions"). Most notably, though, in 1963 he proposed the concept of the [double-heterostructure laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_well_laser "Quantum well laser"), which is now a central concept in the field of semiconductor lasers. Kroemer became an early pioneer in [molecular beam epitaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_beam_epitaxy "Molecular beam epitaxy"), concentrating on applying the technology to untried new materials.

[Charles Kittel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Kittel "Charles Kittel") had published the successful *Thermal Physics* in 1969, and enlisted Kroemer to edit it for a second edition, which appeared in 1980. Kroemer is also the author of the textbook *Quantum Mechanics for Engineering, Materials Science and Applied Physics*.

Kroemer retired from UC Santa Barbara in 2012.

## Personal life

Kroemer was an atheist. He became a [naturalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized "Naturalized") U.S. citizen in 2003. He died on March 8, 2024, in [Santa Barbara, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara,_California "Santa Barbara, California"), at the age of 95.
