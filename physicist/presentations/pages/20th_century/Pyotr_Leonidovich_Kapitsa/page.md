**Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsa** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Пётр Леонидович Капица; [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language "Romanian language"): *Petre Capița*; 9 July [[O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates "Old Style and New Style dates") 26 June] 1894 – 8 April 1984), also known as **Peter Kapitza**, was a Russian and Soviet [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"), whose research focused on low-temperature physics. He was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1978.

## Biography

Kapitsa was born on 9 July [[O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates "Old Style and New Style dates") 26 June] 1894, in [Kronstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronstadt "Kronstadt"), Russia, to the [Bessarabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia "Bessarabia") Leonid Petrovich Kapitsa ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language "Romanian language"): *Leonid Petrovici Capița*), a [military engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_engineering-technical_university "Military engineering-technical university") who constructed fortifications, and to the [Volhynian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia "Volhynia") Olga Ieronimovna Kapitsa, from the Polish noble (*[szlachta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szlachta "Szlachta")*) Stebnicki family. Besides Russian, the Kapitsa family also spoke Romanian.

Kapitsa, standing leftmost with Professor [Abram Ioffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abram_Ioffe "Abram Ioffe") (seated fourth from left) in 1915

Kapitsa's studies were interrupted by the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War"), in which he served as an ambulance driver for two years on the [Polish Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_I) "Eastern Front (World War I)"). He graduated from the [Petrograd Polytechnical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrograd_Polytechnical_Institute "Petrograd Polytechnical Institute") in 1918. His wife and two children died in the flu epidemic of 1918–1919. He subsequently studied in Britain, working for over ten years with [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford "Ernest Rutherford") in the [Cavendish Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Laboratory "Cavendish Laboratory") at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), and founding the influential [Kapitza club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitza_club "Kapitza club"). He was the first director (1930–1934) of the [Mond Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Museums_Site "New Museums Site") in Cambridge.

In the 1920s, he originated techniques for creating ultrastrong [magnetic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field "Magnetic field") by injecting high [current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_current "Electrical current") for brief periods into specially constructed air-core [electromagnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnet "Electromagnet"). In 1928 he discovered the linear relation between resistivity and magnetic field strength in various metals under very strong magnetic fields.

In 1934, Kapitsa returned to Russia to visit his parents but the Soviet Union prevented him from travelling back to Great Britain.

As his equipment for high-magnetic field research remained in Cambridge (although later Ernest Rutherford negotiated with the British government the possibility of shipping it to the Soviet Union), he changed the direction of his research to the study of low temperature phenomena, beginning with a critical analysis of the existing methods for achieving low temperatures. In 1934 he developed new and original apparatus (based on the [adiabatic principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adiabatic_principle "Adiabatic principle")) for making significant quantities of [liquid helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_helium "Liquid helium").

Kapitsa participated in formation of the [Institute for Physical Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Physical_Problems "Institute for Physical Problems"), in part using equipment which the Soviet government bought from the Mond Laboratory in Cambridge (with the assistance of Rutherford, once it was clear that Kapitsa would not be permitted to return).

In Russia, Kapitsa began a series of experiments to study [liquid helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_helium "Liquid helium"). This research culminated with the 1937 discovery of [superfluidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluidity "Superfluidity") (another expression of the [state of matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose–Einstein_condensate "Bose–Einstein condensate") that gives rise to [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity")). Beginning with a letter to the editor of *Science* on 8 January 1938 where he reported the absence of measurable viscosity in liquid helium-4 cooled below 1.8 K, Kapitza documented the properties of helium-4 superfluid in a series of papers. This was the body of work for which he was later awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics, "basic inventions and discoveries in the area of low-temperature physics".

In 1939, he developed a new method for liquefaction of air with a low-pressure cycle using a special high-efficiency expansion turbine. Consequently, during World War II he was assigned to head the Department of Oxygen Industry attached to the [USSR Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Council_of_Ministers "USSR Council of Ministers"), where he developed his low-pressure expansion techniques for industrial purposes. He invented high power microwave generators (1950–1955) and discovered a new kind of continuous high pressure plasma discharge with electron temperatures over 1,000,000 K.

In November 1945, Kapitsa quarreled with [Lavrentiy Beria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavrentiy_Beria "Lavrentiy Beria"), head of the [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD "NKVD") and in charge of the [Soviet atomic bomb project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_atomic_bomb_project "Soviet atomic bomb project"), writing to [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin "Joseph Stalin") about Beria's ignorance of physics and his arrogance. Stalin backed Kapitsa, telling Beria he had to cooperate with the scientists. Kapitsa refused to meet Beria: "If you want to speak to me, then come to the Institute." Stalin offered to meet Kapitsa, but this never happened.

Immediately after the war, a group of prominent Soviet scientists (including Kapitsa in particular) lobbied the government to create a new technical university, the [Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Institute_of_Physics_and_Technology "Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology"). Kapitsa taught there for many years. From 1957, he was also a member of the presidium of the [Soviet Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union "Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union") and at his death in 1984 was the only presidium member who was not also a member of the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union "Communist Party of the Soviet Union").

In 1966, Kapitsa was allowed to visit Cambridge to receive the [Rutherford Medal and Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_Medal_and_Prize "Rutherford Medal and Prize"). While dining at his old college, [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"), he found he did not have the required [gown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_dress_in_the_United_Kingdom#Gown "Academic dress in the United Kingdom"). He asked to borrow one, but a college servant asked him when he last dined at [high table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_table "High table"), "Thirty-two years" replied Kapitza. Within moments the servant returned, not with any gown, but Kapitsa's own.

Kapitsa (left) and [Nikolay Semyonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Semyonov "Nikolay Semyonov"), the physics and chemistry Nobel laureates (portrait by [Boris Kustodiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Kustodiev "Boris Kustodiev"), 1921).

In 1978, Kapitsa won the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") "for his basic inventions and discoveries in the area of [low-temperature physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryogenics "Cryogenics")" and was also cited for his long term role as a leader in the development of this area. He shared the prize with [Arno Allan Penzias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arno_Allan_Penzias "Arno Allan Penzias") and [Robert Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Woodrow_Wilson "Robert Woodrow Wilson"), who won for discovering the [cosmic microwave background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_microwave_background "Cosmic microwave background").

*[Kapitsa resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interfacial_thermal_resistance "Interfacial thermal resistance")* is the thermal resistance (which causes a temperature discontinuity) at the interface between [liquid helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_helium "Liquid helium") and a solid. The *[Kapitsa–Dirac effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitsa–Dirac_effect "Kapitsa–Dirac effect")* is a [quantum mechanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") effect consisting of the diffraction of electrons by a [standing wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_wave "Standing wave") of light. In [fluid dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_dynamics "Fluid dynamics"), the *[Kapitza number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapitza_number "Kapitza number")* is a dimensionless number characterizing the flow of thin films of fluid down an incline.

## Personal life

Pyotr Kapitsa had the nickname "Centaurus". This arose when once [Artem Alikhanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artem_Alikhanian "Artem Alikhanian") asked Kapitsas' student [Shalnikov](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Шальников,%20Александр%20Иосифович "ru:Шальников, Александр Иосифович") "is your supervisor a human or a beast?" to which Shalnikov responded that he is a Centaurus, i.e. he can be human but also he can get angry and hit you with hooves like a horse. Kapitsa was married in 1927 to Anna Alekseyevna Krylova (1903–1996), daughter of applied mathematician [Aleksey Krylov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksey_Krylov "Aleksey Krylov"). They had two sons, Sergey and Andrey. [Sergey Kapitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Kapitsa "Sergey Kapitsa") (1928–2012) was a physicist and demographer. Kapitsa was also the host of the popular and long-running Russian scientific TV show *Evident, but Incredible*. [Andrey Kapitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Kapitsa "Andrey Kapitsa") (1931–2011) was a geographer. He was credited with the discovery and naming of [Lake Vostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Vostok "Lake Vostok"), the largest [subglacial lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subglacial_lake "Subglacial lake") in [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica"), which lies 4,000 meters below the continent's [ice cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_cap "Ice cap").

Kapitsa had the ear of people high up in the Soviet government, due to the usefulness to industry of his discoveries, regularly writing letters on matters of science policy. In particular, he saved both [Vladimir Fock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Fock "Vladimir Fock") and [Lev Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Landau "Lev Landau") from [Stalin's purges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge "Great Purge") of the 1930s, telling [Vyacheslav Molotov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Molotov "Vyacheslav Molotov") that Landau was the only one who would be able to solve an important physics puzzle of the time.

Kapitsa died on 8 April 1984 in Moscow, at the age 89.

## Honors and awards

A [minor planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_planet "Minor planet"), [3437 Kapitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3437_Kapitsa "3437 Kapitsa"), discovered by Soviet astronomer [Lyudmila Karachkina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyudmila_Karachkina "Lyudmila Karachkina") in 1982, is named after him. He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1929 "List of fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1929"). In 1958 he was elected a Member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina").
