**Hersz Georges Charpak** (French: [[ʒɔʁʒ ʃaʁpak]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 1 August 1924 – 29 September 2010) was a Polish-born French [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1992 for his invention of the [multiwire proportional chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiwire_proportional_chamber "Multiwire proportional chamber").

## Life

Georges Charpak was born on 1 August 1924 to [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") parents, Chana (Szapiro) and Maurice Charpak, in the village of Dąbrowica in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic "Second Polish Republic") (now [Dubrovytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrovytsia "Dubrovytsia") in [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine")). Charpak's family moved from [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland") to [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") when he was seven years old, beginning his study of mathematics in 1941 at the [Lycée Saint-Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Saint-Louis "Lycée Saint-Louis"). The actor and film director [André Charpak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Charpak "André Charpak") was his younger brother.

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") Charpak served in the [resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance "French Resistance") and was imprisoned by [Vichy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France "Vichy France") authorities in 1943. In 1944 he was deported to the [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazis "Nazis") [concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_camp "Concentration camp") at [Dachau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp "Dachau concentration camp"), where he remained until the camp was liberated in 1945.

After *[classes préparatoires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classe_préparatoire_aux_grandes_écoles "Classe préparatoire aux grandes écoles")* studies at [Lycée Saint-Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Saint-Louis "Lycée Saint-Louis") in Paris and later at [Lycée Joffre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel_of_Montpellier "Citadel of Montpellier") in Montpellier, he joined in 1945 the Paris-based [École des Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_nationale_supérieure_des_mines_de_Paris "École nationale supérieure des mines de Paris"), one of the most prestigious engineering schools in France. The following year he became a naturalized French citizen. He graduated in 1948, earning the French degree of *Civil Engineer of Mines* (*Ingénieur Civil des Mines* equivalent to a [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree "Master's degree")) becoming a pupil in the laboratory of [Frédéric Joliot-Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frédéric_Joliot-Curie "Frédéric Joliot-Curie") at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France") during 1949, the year after Curie had directed construction of the first *atomic pile* within France. While at the Collège, Charpak secured a research position for the [National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique "Centre national de la recherche scientifique") (CNRS). He received his PhD in 1954 in [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics "Nuclear physics") at the Collège de France, receiving the qualification after having written a thesis on the subject of very-low-energy radiation due to disintegration of nuclei (Charpak & Suzor).

He remained politically engaged: in 1972, together with [Daniele Amati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniele_Amati "Daniele Amati"), he launched a petition against the Vietnam War. Several years later, he initiated the [Juri Orlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Orlov "Yuri Orlov") Committee to protest the imprisonment of the human rights activist in the former Soviet Union.

Charpak married Dominique Vidal in 1953. They had three children. The pediatrician [Nathalie Charpak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathalie_Charpak "Nathalie Charpak") (born 1955) is his daughter. Charpak died on 29 September 2010, in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), at the age of 86.

## Scientific achievements

In 1959, Charpak joined the staff of [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN") ([European Organization for Nuclear Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN")) in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva "Geneva"), where he invented and developed the [multiwire proportional chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiwire_proportional_chamber "Multiwire proportional chamber"). The chamber was patented and quickly superseded the old bubble chambers, allowing for better data processing. This new creation had been made public during 1968. Charpak was later to become a joint inventor with Nlolc and Policarpo of the scintillation drift chamber during the latter parts of the 1970s. He retired from CERN in 1991.

In 1980, Georges Charpak became professor-in-residence at École supérieure de physique et de chimie industrielles in Paris ([ESPCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPCI "ESPCI")) and held the Joliot-Curie Chair there in 1984. This is where he developed and demonstrated the powerful applications of the particle detectors he invented, most notably for enabling better health diagnostics.
He was the co-founder of a number of start-up in the biolab arena, including Molecular Engines Laboratories, Biospace Instruments and SuperSonic Imagine – together with [Mathias Fink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathias_Fink "Mathias Fink"). He was elected to the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences") on 20 May 1985.

Georges Charpak received the [High Energy Particle Physics Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Energy_and_Particle_Physics_Prize "High Energy and Particle Physics Prize") of the [European Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Physical_Society "European Physical Society") in 1989 (the first edition of the prize) "for the development of detectors: [multiwire proportional chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiwire_proportional_chambers "Multiwire proportional chambers"), drift chambers and several other gaseous detectors, and their applications in other fields".

Georges Charpak was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1992 "for his invention and development of [particle detectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_detector "Particle detector"), in particular the [multiwire proportional chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiwire_proportional_chamber "Multiwire proportional chamber")", with affiliations to both École supérieure de physique et de chimie industrielles ([ESPCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPCI "ESPCI")) and CERN. This was the last time a single person was awarded the Physics prize, as of 2025. In 1999, Charpak received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement"). In the French village [Saint-Genis-Pouilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Genis-Pouilly "Saint-Genis-Pouilly")—located directly opposite the CERN "campus", there is a street named in his honor.
