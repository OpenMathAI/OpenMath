**Percy Williams Bridgman** (April 21, 1882 – August 20, 1961) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1946 for his work on the physics of [high pressures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_pressure "High pressure"). He also wrote extensively on the [scientific method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method "Scientific method") and on other aspects of the [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science "Philosophy of science"). The [Bridgman effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgman_effect "Bridgman effect"), the [Bridgman–Stockbarger technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgman–Stockbarger_technique "Bridgman–Stockbarger technique"), and the high-pressure mineral [bridgmanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicate_perovskite "Silicate perovskite") are named after him.

## Early life and education

Percy Williams Bridgman was born on April 21, 1882, in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts"), and grew up in nearby [Auburndale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburndale,_Massachusetts "Auburndale, Massachusetts"). His parents were both born in New England. His father, Raymond Landon Bridgman, was "profoundly religious and idealistic" and worked as a newspaper reporter assigned to state politics. His mother, Mary Ann Maria Williams, was described as "more conventional, sprightly, and competitive."

Bridgman attended both elementary and high school in Auburndale, where he excelled at competitions in the classroom, on the playground, and while playing chess. Described as both shy and proud, his home life consisted of family music, card games, and domestic and garden chores. The family was deeply religious; reading the Bible each morning and attending a [Congregational Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregational_church "Congregational church"). However, Bridgman later became an atheist.

In 1900, Bridgman entered [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), where he received his [A.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.B. "A.B.") in 1904, his [A.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") in 1905, and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in 1908.

## Career and research

From 1910 until his retirement, Bridgman taught at Harvard University, becoming a full professor in 1919. In 1905, he began investigating the properties of matter under [high pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_pressure "High pressure"). A machinery malfunction led him to modify his pressure apparatus; the result was a new sealing device enabling him to create pressures eventually exceeding 100,000 [kgf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilogram-force "Kilogram-force")/cm2 (10 [GPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigapascal "Gigapascal"); 100,000 [atmospheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere_(unit) "Atmosphere (unit)")). This was a huge improvement over previous machinery, which could achieve pressures of only 3,000 kgf/cm2 (0.3 GPa). This new apparatus led to an abundance of new findings, including a study of the compressibility, electric and [thermal conductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conductivity "Thermal conductivity"), [tensile strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensile_strength "Tensile strength") and [viscosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity "Viscosity") of more than 100 different compounds. Bridgman is also known for his studies of electrical conduction in metals and properties of crystals. He developed the [Bridgman seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgman_seal "Bridgman seal") and is the eponym for [Bridgman's thermodynamic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgman's_thermodynamic_equations "Bridgman's thermodynamic equations"), which were used to further his research.

Bridgman made many improvements to his high-pressure apparatus over the years, and unsuccessfully attempted the [synthesis of diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_diamond "Synthetic diamond") many times. The [high-pressure torsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-pressure_torsion "High-pressure torsion") apparatus developed by Bridgman significantly contributed to the development of [severe plastic deformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_plastic_deformation "Severe plastic deformation") field decades later.

His [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science "Philosophy of science") book *[The Logic of Modern Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Logic_of_Modern_Physics "The Logic of Modern Physics")* (1927) advocated [operationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operationalism "Operationalism") and coined the term [operational definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_definition "Operational definition"). In 1938 he participated in the International Committee composed to organise the International Congresses for the Unity of Science. He was also one of the 11 signatories to the [Russell–Einstein Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell–Einstein_Manifesto "Russell–Einstein Manifesto").

[J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer "J. Robert Oppenheimer"), the director of the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project"), was an undergraduate student of Bridgman's. Of his teaching abilities, Oppenheimer said that, "I found Bridgman a wonderful teacher because he never really was quite reconciled to things being the way they were and he always thought them out."

## Personal life and death

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6a/Percy_Bridgman_with_wife_and_Gustaf_VI_Adolf_of_Sweden_1946.jpg/250px-Percy_Bridgman_with_wife_and_Gustaf_VI_Adolf_of_Sweden_1946.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Percy_Bridgman_with_wife_and_Gustaf_VI_Adolf_of_Sweden_1946.jpg)

Bridgman with wife and [Gustaf VI Adolf of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustaf_VI_Adolf_of_Sweden "Gustaf VI Adolf of Sweden") in Stockholm in 1946

Bridgman married Olive Ware (1882-1972), of [Hartford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Connecticut "Hartford, Connecticut"), in 1912. Ware's father, [Edmund Asa Ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Asa_Ware "Edmund Asa Ware"), was the founder and first president of [Atlanta University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_University "Atlanta University"). The couple had two children and were married for nearly 50 years, living most of that time in Cambridge. The family also had a summer home in [Randolph, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph,_New_Hampshire "Randolph, New Hampshire"), where Bridgman was known as a skilled mountain climber.

Bridgman was a "penetrating analytical thinker" with a "fertile mechanical imagination" and exceptional manual dexterity. He was a skilled plumber and carpenter, known to shun the assistance of professionals in these matters. He was also fond of music and played the piano, and took pride in his flower and vegetable gardens.

Bridgman committed suicide by gunshot after suffering from [metastatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metastasis "Metastasis") [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer") for some time. His suicide note was a mere two sentences; "It isn't decent for society to make a man do this thing himself. Probably this is the last day I will be able to do it myself." Bridgman's words have been quoted by many in the [assisted suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_suicide "Assisted suicide") debate.

## Commemoration

The [Percy W. Bridgman House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_W._Bridgman_House "Percy W. Bridgman House"), in Massachusetts, is a U.S. [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark "National Historic Landmark") designated in 1975.

In 2014, the Commission on New Minerals, Nomenclature and Classification of the [International Mineralogical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mineralogical_Association "International Mineralogical Association") approved the name [bridgmanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicate_perovskite "Silicate perovskite") for [perovskite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perovskite "Perovskite")-structured (Mg,Fe)SiO3, the Earth's most abundant mineral, in honor of his high-pressure research.
