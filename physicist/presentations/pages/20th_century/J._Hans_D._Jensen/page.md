**Johannes Hans Daniel Jensen** (German: [[ˈhans ˈjɛnzn̩]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Hans_Jensen.ogg "File:De-Hans Jensen.ogg"); 25 June 1907 – 11 February 1973) was a German theoretical [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Jensen worked on the [German nuclear energy project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nuclear_energy_project "German nuclear energy project"), known as the Uranium Club, where he contributed to the separation of [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium "Uranium") [isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotopes "Isotopes"). After the war, Jensen was a professor at the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg "University of Heidelberg"). He was a visiting professor at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison"), the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study"), [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University "Indiana University"), and the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology").

Jensen was awarded the 1963 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") along with [Eugene Wigner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Wigner "Eugene Wigner") and [Maria Goeppert Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Goeppert_Mayer "Maria Goeppert Mayer"), sharing one half of the Prize with the latter "for their discoveries concerning [nuclear shell structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_shell_model "Nuclear shell model")."

## Education

Johannes Hans Daniel Jensen was born on 25 June 1907 in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg "Hamburg"), Germany. From 1926 to 1931, Jensen studied physics, mathematics, physical chemistry, and philosophy at the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg "University of Freiburg") and the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg "University of Hamburg"). He received his *[Doctor rerum naturalium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_rerum_naturalium "Doctor rerum naturalium")* in 1932 and his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation "Habilitation") in 1936, both from the University of Hamburg.

## Career

In 1937, Jensen became a [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent "Docent") at the University of Hamburg, and began working with [Paul Harteck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Harteck "Paul Harteck"), Director of the University's Physical Chemistry Department and advisor to the *[Heereswaffenamt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heereswaffenamt "Heereswaffenamt")* (HWA – Army Ordnance Office) on explosives. Harteck and his teaching assistant, [Wilhelm Groth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Groth "Wilhelm Groth"), made contact with the *[Reichskriegsministerium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskriegsministerium "Reichskriegsministerium")* (RKM – Reich Ministry of War) on 24 April 1939 to tell them of potential military applications of [nuclear chain reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_chain_reaction "Nuclear chain reaction"). Military control of the [German nuclear energy project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nuclear_energy_project "German nuclear energy project"), also known as the *Uranverein* (Uranium Club), began on 1 September 1939, the day that Nazi Germany initiated World War II by invading Poland. Harteck, one of the principals in the *Uranverein*, brought Jensen into the project. Jensen's main thrust was on double [centrifuges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrifuge "Centrifuge") for [separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope_separation "Isotope separation") of [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium "Uranium") isotopes (see the section below citing internal reports of the *Uranverein*). Harteck and Jensen developed a double centrifuge based on a rocking process (*Schaukelverfahren*) to facilitate the separation effect.

In 1941, Jensen was appointed Professor of Theoretical Physics at Technische Hochschule Hannover (now [Leibniz University Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_University_Hannover "Leibniz University Hannover")). In 1949, he became a professor at the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg "University of Heidelberg"), where he remained until his retirement in 1969.

Jensen was a guest professor at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison") (1951), the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") (1952), [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") (1952), [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University "Indiana University") (1953), [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") (1953), [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota"), Twin Cities (1956), and [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego") (1961).

In 1963, Jensen shared half of the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Maria Goeppert Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Goeppert_Mayer "Maria Goeppert Mayer") for their [model of nuclear shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_shell_model "Nuclear shell model"); the other half of the Prize was awarded to [Eugene Wigner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Wigner "Eugene Wigner") for unrelated work in nuclear and particle physics.

Jensen died on 11 February 1973 in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg") at the age of 65.

## Party memberships

[Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") took power on 30 January 1933. On 7 April of that year the [Law for the Restoration of the Professional Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_for_the_Restoration_of_the_Professional_Civil_Service "Law for the Restoration of the Professional Civil Service") was enacted; this law, and its subsequent related ordinances, politicized the education system in Germany. Other factors enforcing the politicization of education were *[Nationalsozialistische Deutsche Arbeiterpartei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party "Nazi Party")* (NSDAP – National Socialist German Workers Party) organizations in academia and the rise of the *[Deutsche Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Physik "Deutsche Physik")* (Aryan Physics) movement, which was [anti-Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Semitic "Anti-Semitic") and had a bias against [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics"), especially including [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"). The Party organizations were the *Nationalsozialistischer Deutscher Studentenbund* (NSDStB – National Socialist German Student League) founded in 1926, the *Nationalsozialistischer Lehrerbund* (NSLB, [National Socialist Teachers League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Socialist_Teachers_League "National Socialist Teachers League")) founded in 1927, and the *Nationalsozialistischer Deutscher Dozentenbund* (NSDDB – [National Socialist German University Lecturers League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Socialist_German_University_Lecturers_League "National Socialist German University Lecturers League")) founded in 1933. While membership in the NSDDB was not mandatory, it was tactically advantageous, if not unavoidable, as the district leaders had a decisive role in the acceptance of a *[Habilitationsschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitationsschrift "Habilitationsschrift")*, which was a prerequisite to attaining the rank of *[Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent "Privatdozent")* necessary to becoming a university lecturer.

While all German universities were politicized, not all were as strict in carrying out this end as was the University of Hamburg, where Jensen received his doctorate and *Habilitationsschrift*. Upon his 1936 *habilitation* he had been a member of NSDDB for three years, the NSLB for two years, and a candidate for membership in NSDAP, which he received the next year. The university leader of NSLB had made it clear that active participation was expected from Jensen, and that is what they got.

After [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") the [denazification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denazification "Denazification") process began. When Jensen faced the proceedings, he turned to [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg "Werner Heisenberg"), a prominent member of the *Uranverein*, for a testament to his character—a document known as a *[Persilschein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persilschein "Persilschein")* (whitewash certificate). Heisenberg was a particularly powerful writer of these documents; as he had never been a member of NSDAP, he had publicly clashed with NSDAP and the *[Schutzstaffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel "Schutzstaffel")* (SS), and was appointed by the British occupation authorities to the chair for theoretical physics and the directorship of the [Max Planck Institute for Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Physics "Max Planck Institute for Physics") then in Göttingen. Heisenberg wrote the document and convinced the authorities that Jensen had joined the Party organizations only to avoid unnecessary difficulties in academia.

## Honors

Honors conferred upon Jensen include:

- 1947: Honorary professor at the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg "University of Hamburg")
- 1963: [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") – jointly with [Maria Goeppert Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Goeppert_Mayer "Maria Goeppert Mayer") "for their discoveries concerning [nuclear shell structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_shell_model "Nuclear shell model")"
- 1964: Honorary doctorate from the [University of Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hannover "University of Hannover")
- 1969: Honorary citizen of [Fort Lauderdale, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale,_Florida "Fort Lauderdale, Florida")

## Internal reports

The following reports were published in *[Kernphysikalische Forschungsberichte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernphysikalische_Forschungsberichte "Kernphysikalische Forschungsberichte")* (*Research Reports in Nuclear Physics*), an internal publication of the German *[Uranverein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nuclear_energy_project "German nuclear energy project")*. The reports were classified Top Secret, they had very limited distribution, and the authors were not allowed to keep copies. The reports were confiscated under the Allied [Operation Alsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Alsos "Operation Alsos") and sent to the [United States Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Atomic_Energy_Commission "United States Atomic Energy Commission") for evaluation. In 1971 the reports were declassified and returned to Germany. The reports are available at the [Karlsruhe Nuclear Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forschungszentrum_Karlsruhe "Forschungszentrum Karlsruhe") and the [American Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Physics "American Institute of Physics").

- [Paul Harteck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Harteck "Paul Harteck"), Johannes Jensen, [Friedrich Knauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Knauer_(chemist) "Friedrich Knauer (chemist)"), and [Hans Suess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Suess "Hans Suess") *Über die Bremsung, die Diffusion und den Einfang von Neutronen in fester Kohlensäure und über ihren Einfang in Uran* G-36 (19 August 1940)
- Paul Harteck and Johannes Jesnsen *Der Thermodiffusionseffekt im Zusammenspiel mit der Konvektion durch mechanisch bewegte Wände und Vergleich mit der Thermosiphonwirkung* G-89 (18 February 1941)
- Johannes Jensen *Über die Ultrazentrifugenmethode zur Trennung der Uranisotope* G-95 (December 1941)
- Paul Harteck and Johannes Jensen *Gerechnung des Trenneffektes und der Ausbeute verschiedner Zentrifugenanordnungen zur Erhöhung des Wirkungsgrades einer einselnen Zentrifuge* G-158 (February 1943)
- Paul Harteck, Johannes Jensen, and Albert Suhr *Über den Zusammenhang zwischen Ausbeute und Trennschärfe bei der Niederdruckkolonne* G-159
