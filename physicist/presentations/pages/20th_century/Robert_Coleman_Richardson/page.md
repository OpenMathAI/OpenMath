**Robert Coleman Richardson** (June 26, 1937 – February 19, 2013) was an American [experimental physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics") whose area of research included [sub-millikelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orders_of_magnitude_(temperature) "Orders of magnitude (temperature)") [temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperature "Temperature") studies of [helium-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium-3 "Helium-3"). Richardson, along with [David Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lee_(physicist) "David Lee (physicist)"), as senior researchers, and then graduate student [Douglas Osheroff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Osheroff "Douglas Osheroff"), shared the 1996 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for their 1972 discovery of the property of [superfluidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluid "Superfluid") in helium-3 atoms in the [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") Laboratory of Atomic and Solid State Physics.

Richardson was born in [Washington D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_D.C. "Washington D.C.") He went to high school at [Washington-Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington-Lee_High_School "Washington-Lee High School") in [Arlington, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Virginia "Arlington, Virginia"). He later described Washington-Lee's biology and physics courses as "very old-fashioned" for the time. "The idea of 'advanced placement' had not yet been invented," he wrote in his Nobel Prize autobiography. He took his first calculus course when he was a sophomore in college.

Richardson attended [Virginia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Tech "Virginia Tech") and received a B.S. in 1958 and a M.S. in 1960. He received his PhD from [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University") in 1966.

## Background

At the time of his death, he was the Floyd Newman Professor of Physics at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"), although he no longer operated a laboratory. From 1998 to 2007 he served as Cornell's vice provost for research, and from 2007 to 2009 was senior science adviser to the president and provost. His past experimental work focused on using [Nuclear Magnetic Resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Magnetic_Resonance "Nuclear Magnetic Resonance") to study the quantum properties of liquids and solids at extremely low temperatures.

Richardson was an [Eagle Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Scout_(Boy_Scouts_of_America) "Eagle Scout (Boy Scouts of America)"), and mentioned the Scouting activities of his youth in the biography he submitted to the Nobel Foundation at the time of his award.

Richardson claimed that he did not believe in an [anthropomorphic God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_god "Personal god"), but it is unclear what specific beliefs he held.

## Personal life

Richardson was born to Lois Price Richardson, who attained a Master’s Degree of her own before she met and married Richardson’s father, Robert Franklin Richardson, a [telephone engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_engineering "Telecommunications engineering"). He married Betty Marilyn McCarthy, a fellow physics PhD student from Duke, on 29 Sep 1962 at the Immaculate Conception Catholic church in [Durham, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina "Durham, North Carolina"). Together they had two children, Jennifer and Pamela; and four grandchildren Jade, Vanessa, Oliver, and Quinn.
