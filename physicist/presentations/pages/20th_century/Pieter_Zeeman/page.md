**Pieter Zeeman** (25 May 1865 – 9 October 1943) was a Dutch experimental [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared the 1902 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Hendrik Lorentz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Lorentz "Hendrik Lorentz") for their discovery and theoretical explanation of the [Zeeman effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeeman_effect "Zeeman effect").

## Education

Pieter Zeeman was born on 25 May 1865 in [Zonnemaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zonnemaire "Zonnemaire"), Netherlands, the son of the Reverend Catharinus Forandinus Zeeman, a minister of the [Dutch Reformed Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Reformed_Church "Dutch Reformed Church"), and Willemina Worst.

Zeeman became interested in physics at an early age. In 1883, the [aurora borealis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_(astronomy) "Aurora (astronomy)") happened to be visible in the Netherlands; Zeeman, then a student at the high school in [Zierikzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zierikzee "Zierikzee"), made a drawing and description of the phenomenon and submitted it to *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")*, where it was published. The editor praised "the careful observations of Professor Zeeman from his observatory in Zonnemaire."

After finishing high school in 1883, Zeeman went to [Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft "Delft") for supplementary education in [classical languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_language "Classical language"), then a requirement for admission to university. He stayed at the home of Dr J. W. Lely, co-principal of the [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)") and brother of [Cornelis Lely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelis_Lely "Cornelis Lely"), who was responsible for the concept and realization of the [Zuiderzee Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuiderzee_Works "Zuiderzee Works"). While in Delft, he first met [Heike Kamerlingh Onnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heike_Kamerlingh_Onnes "Heike Kamerlingh Onnes"), who would become his doctoral advisor.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Zeeman%2C_Pieter.jpg/250px-Zeeman%2C_Pieter.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Zeeman,_Pieter.jpg)

Portrait of Pieter Zeeman by [Jan Veth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Veth "Jan Veth"), 1925.

After passing the qualification exams in 1885, Zeeman entered [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University "Leiden University") to study physics under Kamerlingh Onnes and [Hendrik Lorentz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Lorentz "Hendrik Lorentz"). In 1890, he became Lorentz's assistant, which allowed him to participate in a research programme on the [Kerr effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magneto-optic_Kerr_effect "Magneto-optic Kerr effect"). In 1893, he submitted his doctoral thesis on the Kerr effect, the reflection of [polarized light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polarized_light "Polarized light") on a magnetized surface. After receiving his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D."), he went for half a year to [Friedrich Kohlrausch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Kohlrausch_(physicist) "Friedrich Kohlrausch (physicist)")'s institute at the [University of Strassburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strassburg "University of Strassburg"). In 1895, he returned to Leiden to become a *[Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent "Privatdozent")* in mathematics and physics.

## Zeeman effect

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/ZeemanEffect.GIF/250px-ZeemanEffect.GIF?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ZeemanEffect.GIF)

A photo Zeeman took of the [Zeeman effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeeman_effect "Zeeman effect").

In 1896, shortly before moving from Leiden to Amsterdam, Zeeman measured the splitting of [spectral lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_line "Spectral line") by a strong [magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field "Magnetic field"), a discovery now known as the [Zeeman effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeeman_effect "Zeeman effect"). This research involved an investigation of the effect of magnetic fields on a light source. He discovered that a spectral line is split into several components in the presence of a magnetic field. [Hendrik Lorentz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Lorentz "Hendrik Lorentz") first heard about Zeeman's observations on Saturday 31 October 1896 at the meeting of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences"), where these results were communicated by [Heike Kamerlingh Onnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heike_Kamerlingh_Onnes "Heike Kamerlingh Onnes"). The next Monday, Lorentz called Zeeman into his office and presented him with an explanation of his observations, based on Lorentz's theory of [electromagnetic radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_radiation "Electromagnetic radiation"). In 1902, Zeeman and Lorentz were jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for their respective experimental and theoretical work on the Zeeman effect.

The importance of Zeeman's discovery soon became apparent; it confirmed Lorentz's prediction about the polarization of light emitted in the presence of a magnetic field. Thanks to Zeeman's work, it became clear that the [oscillating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillating "Oscillating") particles, according to Lorentz were the source of light emission, were negatively charged, and were a thousandfold lighter than the [hydrogen atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_atom "Hydrogen atom"). This conclusion was reached well before [J. J. Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Thomson "J. J. Thomson")'s discovery of the [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron"). The Zeeman effect thus became an important tool for elucidating the [structure of the atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom#Structure "Atom").

## Career in Amsterdam

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/EinsteinZeemanEhrenfest.jpg/330px-EinsteinZeemanEhrenfest.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:EinsteinZeemanEhrenfest.jpg)

[Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") visiting Pieter Zeeman *(left)* in Amsterdam, accompanied by [Paul Ehrenfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrenfest "Paul Ehrenfest") (circa 1920).

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/1929_Pieter_Zeeman.jpg/250px-1929_Pieter_Zeeman.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1929_Pieter_Zeeman.jpg)

1929 [Autochrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autochrome_Lumière "Autochrome Lumière") by [Georges Chevalier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Chevalier_(photographer) "Georges Chevalier (photographer)").

Shortly after his discovery, Zeeman was offered a position as a lecturer at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam "University of Amsterdam"), where he started to work in the autumn of 1896. This was followed by his promotion to Professor of Physics in 1900. In 1908, he succeeded [Johannes van der Waals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_van_der_Waals "Johannes van der Waals") as full professor and Director of the Physics Institute. He retired as a professor in 1935.

In 1918, Zeeman published "Some experiments on gravitation: The ratio of mass to weight for crystals and radioactive substances" in the *Proceedings of the Koninklijke Nederlandse Akademie van Wetenschappen*, experimentally confirming the [equivalence principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalence_principle "Equivalence principle") with regard to gravitational and inertial mass.

A new laboratory built in Amsterdam in 1923 was renamed the Zeeman Laboratory in 1940. This new facility allowed Zeeman to pursue a refined investigation of the [Zeeman effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeeman_effect "Zeeman effect"). For the remainder of his career he remained interested in research in [magneto-optic effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magneto-optic_effect "Magneto-optic effect"). He also investigated the propagation of light in moving media. This subject became the focus of a renewed interest because of [special relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_relativity "Special relativity"), and enjoyed a keen interest from Lorentz and [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein"). Later in his career, he became interested in [mass spectrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometry "Mass spectrometry").

## Personal life and death

In 1895, Zeeman married Johanna Elisabeth Lebret (1873–1962), with whom he had three daughters and one son.

Zeeman died on 9 October 1943 in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam "Amsterdam") at the age of 78, and was buried in [Haarlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haarlem "Haarlem").
