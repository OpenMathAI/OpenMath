**Yang Chen-Ning** ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters "Simplified Chinese characters"): 杨振宁; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters "Traditional Chinese characters"): 楊振寧; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): *Yáng Zhènníng*; October 1, 1922 – October 18, 2025) also known as **C.N. Yang** and **Franklin Yang**, was a Chinese-American [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics") who made significant contributions to [statistical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics"), [integrable systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_systems "Integrable systems"), [gauge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_theory "Gauge theory"), [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics") and [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics "Condensed matter physics").

Yang is known for his collaboration with [Robert Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mills_(physicist) "Robert Mills (physicist)") in 1954 in developing non-abelian [gauge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_theory "Gauge theory"), widely known as the [Yang–Mills theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang–Mills_theory "Yang–Mills theory"), which describes the [nuclear forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_force "Nuclear force") in the [Standard Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Model "Standard Model") of [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics").

Yang and [Tsung-Dao Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsung-Dao_Lee "Tsung-Dao Lee") received the 1957 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for their work on parity non-conservation of the [weak interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_interaction "Weak interaction"), which was confirmed by the [Wu experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_experiment "Wu experiment") in 1956. The two proposed that the conservation of [parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_(physics) "Parity (physics)"), a physical law observed to hold in all other physical processes, is violated in [weak nuclear reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_interaction "Weak interaction") – those nuclear processes that result in the emission of [beta particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_particle "Beta particle").

## Early life and education

Yang was born in [Hefei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hefei "Hefei"), Anhui, China, on October 1, 1922. His mother was Luo Meng-hua and his father, [Ko-Chuen Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Ko-Chuen?action=edit&redlink=1 "Yang Ko-Chuen") (楊克純; 1896–1973), was a mathematician.

Yang attended elementary school and high school in [Beiping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beiping "Beiping") (now [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing "Beijing")), and in the autumn of 1937 his family moved to [Hefei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hefei "Hefei") after the [Japanese invaded China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War#1937:_Full-scale_invasion_of_China "Second Sino-Japanese War"). In 1938 they moved to [Kunming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunming "Kunming"), Yunnan, where [National Southwestern Associated University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Southwestern_Associated_University "National Southwestern Associated University") was located. In the same year, as a second-year student, Yang passed the entrance examination and studied at National Southwestern Associated University. He received a Bachelor of Science in 1942, with his thesis on the application of [group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_theory "Group theory") to molecular spectra, under the supervision of [Ta-You Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Ta-You "Wu Ta-You").

Yang continued to study graduate courses there for two years under the supervision of [Wang Zhuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Zhuxi "Wang Zhuxi") (J.S. Wang), working on statistical mechanics. In 1944, he received a Master of Science from [National Tsing Hua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Tsing_Hua_University "National Tsing Hua University"), which had moved to Kunming during the Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945). Yang was then awarded a scholarship from the [Boxer Indemnity Scholarship Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_Indemnity_Scholarship_Program "Boxer Indemnity Scholarship Program"), set up by the United States government using part of the money China had been forced to pay following the [Boxer Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_Rebellion "Boxer Rebellion"). His departure for the United States was delayed for one year, during which time he taught in a middle school as a teacher and studied field theory.

Yang entered the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") in January 1946 and studied with [Edward Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Teller "Edward Teller"). He received a Doctor of Philosophy in 1948.

## Career

Yang remained at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") for a year as an assistant to [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi"). In 1949 he was invited to do his research at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey "Princeton, New Jersey"), New Jersey, where he began a period of fruitful collaboration with [Tsung-Dao Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsung-Dao_Lee "Tsung-Dao Lee"). Lee and Yang published 32 papers together. He was made a permanent member of the Institute in 1952, and full professor in 1955. In 1963, [Princeton University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University_Press "Princeton University Press") published his textbook, *Elementary Particles*. In 1965 he moved to [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University "Stony Brook University"), where he was named the Albert Einstein Professor of Physics and the first director of the newly founded Institute for Theoretical Physics. Today this institute is known as the [C. N. Yang Institute for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._N._Yang_Institute_for_Theoretical_Physics "C. N. Yang Institute for Theoretical Physics"). Yang retired from Stony Brook University in 1999.

Yang visited the Chinese mainland in 1971 for the first time after the thaw in China–US relations, and subsequently worked to help the Chinese physics community rebuild the research atmosphere, which later eroded due to political movements during the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution "Cultural Revolution"). After retiring from Stony Brook, he returned to Beijing as an honorary director of [Tsinghua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsinghua_University "Tsinghua University"), where he was the first Huang Jibei-Lu Kaiqun Professor at the [Center for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Advanced_Study,_Tsinghua_University "Center for Advanced Study, Tsinghua University") (CASTU). He was also one of the two [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize") Founding Members and was a Distinguished Professor-at-Large at the [Chinese University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_University_of_Hong_Kong "Chinese University of Hong Kong").

Yang helped to establish the Theoretical Physics Division at the [Chern Institute of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern_Institute_of_Mathematics "Chern Institute of Mathematics") in 1986 at the request of [Shiing-Shen Chern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiing-Shen_Chern "Shiing-Shen Chern") who was serving as the inaugural director of the Institute at the time.

## Personal life and death

Yang married Tu Chih-li ([traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters "Traditional Chinese characters"): 杜致禮; [simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters "Simplified Chinese characters"): 杜致礼; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): *Dù Zhìlǐ*), a teacher, in 1950; they had two sons and a daughter together. His father-in-law was the [Kuomintang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuomintang "Kuomintang") general [Du Yuming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Yuming "Du Yuming"). Tu died in October 2003. In January 2005, Yang married Weng Fan ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters "Traditional Chinese characters"): 翁帆; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): *Wēng Fān*), a university student. They met in 1995 at a physics seminar; the couple reestablished contact in February 2004 when Yang moved to China to become affiliated with [Tsinghua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsinghua_University "Tsinghua University"). Yang called Weng, who was 54 years his junior, his "final blessing from God".

Yang obtained U.S. citizenship during his research within the country. According to the state-run Xinhua News Agency, Yang said the decision was painful as his father never forgave him for that. According to Xinhua and other mainstream Chinese media, he formally renounced his American citizenship on April 1, 2015. He acknowledged that while the U.S. was a beautiful country that gave him good opportunities to study science, China since his youth had offered the best secondary and undergraduate institutions, though the US had the top graduate studies. However, circumstances changed in favor of China's growth by the turn of the century.

His son Guangnuo was a computer scientist. His second son Guangyu is an astronomer, and his daughter Youli is a doctor.

Yang [turned 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenarian "Centenarian") on October 1, 2022, and died in Beijing on October 18, 2025, at the age of 103. The day after the announcement of his death, people gathered and waited in line at Tsinghua University to pay tributes to Yang.

## Views on the CEPC

Yang is known for having opposed the construction of the [Circular Electron Positron Collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_Electron_Positron_Collider "Circular Electron Positron Collider") (CEPC), a 100 km circumference particle collider in China that would study the [Higgs boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_boson "Higgs boson"). He catalogued the project as "guess" work and without guaranteed results. Yang said that "even if they see something with the machine, it's not going to benefit the life of Chinese people any sooner."

## Academic achievements

Yang worked on [statistical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics"), [condensed matter theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_theory "Condensed matter theory"), [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics") and [gauge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_theory "Gauge theory")/[quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field_theory "Quantum field theory").

Various Nobel Prizes in Physics are based on Yang's work. At least 10 Nobel laureates in Physics cited Yang's work during their Nobel speech, this includes: [Steven Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Weinberg "Steven Weinberg") (1979), [Sheldon Glashow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Glashow "Sheldon Glashow") (1979), [Martinus J. G. Veltman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinus_J._G._Veltman "Martinus J. G. Veltman") (1999), [Gerard 't Hooft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_'t_Hooft "Gerard 't Hooft") (1999), [David Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gross "David Gross") (1999), [Yoichiro Nambu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoichiro_Nambu "Yoichiro Nambu") (2008), [Makoto Kobayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makoto_Kobayashi "Makoto Kobayashi") (2008), [Toshihide Maskawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshihide_Maskawa "Toshihide Maskawa") (2008), [François Englert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Englert "François Englert") (2013) and [Peter Higgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Higgs "Peter Higgs") (2013).

### Early contributions

His first two papers (1944, 1945) were the result of his master thesis on statistical physics, supervised by [J. S. Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Zhuxi "Wang Zhuxi").

At the University of Chicago, Yang first spent twenty months working in an accelerator lab, but found he was not good at [experimental physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics") and switched back to theory. His doctoral thesis was about an atomic beam apparatus for measuring the [nuclear quadrupole resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_quadrupole_resonance "Nuclear quadrupole resonance") of sodium.

Later, Yang worked on particle phenomenology; a well-known work was the Fermi–Yang model of 1949, treating the [pion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pion "Pion") as a bound nucleon–antinucleon pair.

### Main contributions

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Yang.jpg/250px-Yang.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Yang.jpg)

Yang in 1957

Yang is well known for his 1953 collaboration with [Robert Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mills_(physicist) "Robert Mills (physicist)") in developing [non-abelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-abelian_group "Non-abelian group") [gauge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_theory "Gauge theory"), widely known as the [Yang–Mills theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang–Mills_theory "Yang–Mills theory"). The idea was generally conceived by Yang while the novice scientist Mills assisted him as Mills explained:

> During the academic year 1953–54, Yang was a visitor to [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory") ... I was at Brookhaven also ... and was assigned to the same office as Yang. Yang, who demonstrated on a number of occasions his generosity to physicists beginning their careers, told me about his idea of generalizing gauge invariance and we discussed it at some length ... I was able to contribute something to the discussions, especially with regard to the quantization procedures, and to a small degree in working out the formalism; however, the key ideas were Yang's.

[*The Scientist*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scientist_(magazine) "The Scientist (magazine)") called Yang–Mills theory:

> The foundation for the current understanding of how subatomic particles interact is a contribution which has restructured modern physics and mathematics.

In 1956, he and T. D. Lee analyzed a problem known as the τ–θ puzzle, in which a particle called θ decayed into two [pions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pion "Pion") and a particle τ into three pions, the two decays with different [parity symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_(physics) "Parity (physics)"). However the two particles were not distinguishable. Lee and Yang proposed that, in the [weak interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_interaction "Weak interaction"), [parity symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_symmetry "Parity symmetry") was not conserved and that it was the same particle (now known as the [kaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaon "Kaon") K+). To test if weak interaction conserved parity, Lee contacted [Chien-Shiung Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chien-Shiung_Wu "Chien-Shiung Wu")'s team at the National Bureau of Standards in Washington. [Wu experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_experiment "Wu experiment") experimentally verified the theory and the results were announced early in a press conference. The results were also confirmed by two other independent experiments by [Valentine Telegdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine_Telegdi "Valentine Telegdi") and [Jerome Isaac Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Isaac_Friedman "Jerome Isaac Friedman") at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") and by [Richard Garwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Garwin "Richard Garwin") and [Leon M. Lederman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_M._Lederman "Leon M. Lederman") at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"). All three experiments published their results in early 1957. That year, Yang and Lee received the 1957 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for this parity violation theory, which brought revolutionary change to the field of particle physics.

In the 1970s, Yang worked on the topological properties of gauge theory, collaborating with [Tai Tsun Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Tsun_Wu "Tai Tsun Wu") to elucidate the [Wu–Yang monopole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu–Yang_monopole "Wu–Yang monopole"), a type of [magnetic monopole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_monopole "Magnetic monopole"). Unlike the [Dirac monopole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_monopole "Dirac monopole"), it has no singular [Dirac string](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_string "Dirac string"). Their 1975 paper, known as the [Wu–Yang dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu–Yang_dictionary "Wu–Yang dictionary"), helped bridge gaps between physics and differential geometry.

On Yang's retirement from SUNY in 1999, [Freeman Dyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeman_Dyson "Freeman Dyson") called Yang "the pre-eminent stylist" of 20th-century physics alongside [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") and [Paul Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac"), citing how Yang "turns his least important calculations into miniature works of art, and turns his deeper speculations into masterpieces." In 2009, Dyson wrote:

> Yang took [[Hermann] Weyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Weyl "Hermann Weyl")'s place as the leading bird among my generation of physicists ... With non-Abelian gauge fields generating nontrivial Lie algebras, the possible forms of interaction between fields become unique, so that symmetry dictates interaction. This idea is Yang's greatest contribution to physics. It is a contribution of a bird flying high over the rain forest of little problems in which most of us spend our lives.

### 13 seminal contributions

In 2012, there were celebrations for Yang's 90th birthday. The [Chinese University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_University_of_Hong_Kong "Chinese University of Hong Kong") hosted a scientific conference and dinner banquet to announce the creation of the CN Yang archive. Physicist [Kenneth Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Young_(physicist) "Kenneth Young (physicist)") opened the ceremony. Yang also received a black cube from Tsinghua University with 13 of his seminal contributions engraved on the faces of the cube. On the cube is also written "Congratulations on Professor Chen Ning Yang's 90th birthday" in Chinese. The cube also includes an ancient Chinese poem used by Yang in his 2013 *Selected Papers and Commentaries*; it reads:

> A piece of literature
> Is meant for the millennium
> But its ups and downs are known
> Already in the author's heart

— [Du Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Fu "Du Fu")

The 13 contributions engraved on the cube are:

- Statistical mechanics:
  - [Phase transitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_transition "Phase transition") (1952): for his works with Lee on the [Lee–Yang theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee–Yang_theory "Lee–Yang theory") and the [Lee–Yang theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee–Yang_theorem "Lee–Yang theorem"), which describe the liquid-gas phase transition based on microscopic properties.
  - [Bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson "Boson") (1957): for [Lee–Huang–Yang correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee–Huang–Yang_correction "Lee–Huang–Yang correction") for a [weakly interacting Bose gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_interacting_Bose_gas "Weakly interacting Bose gas"), in collaboration with Lee and [Kerson Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerson_Huang "Kerson Huang"). It explains the stability of [Bose–Einstein condensates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose–Einstein_condensate "Bose–Einstein condensate") and [superfluids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluidity "Superfluidity").
  - [Yang–Baxter equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang–Baxter_equation "Yang–Baxter equation") (1967): consistent condition for a one-dimensional factorized scattering many-body system, which resurrected the interest in the [Bethe ansatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethe_ansatz "Bethe ansatz") and allowed to better description of 1D fermions.
  - Finite temperature (1969): for the Yang–Yang thermodynamics formalism for 1D bosons, in collaboration with his brother [Chen-Ping Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen-Ping_Yang?action=edit&redlink=1 "Chen-Ping Yang") (1930–2018). The predictions of this formalism where demonstrated in 2008 using [ultracold atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultracold_atom "Ultracold atom").
- Condensed matter physics:
  - [Flux quantization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_flux_quantum "Magnetic flux quantum") (1961): for [Byers–Yang theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byers–Yang_theorem "Byers–Yang theorem") in [superconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity"), in collaboration with [Nina Byers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Byers "Nina Byers"). They showed that the magnetic flux trapped in a superconducting ring is given by half of the usual [magnetic flux quantum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_flux_quantum "Magnetic flux quantum").
  - [ODLRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-diagonal_long-range_order "Off-diagonal long-range order") (1962): for his development of the concept of [off-diagonal long-range order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-diagonal_long-range_order "Off-diagonal long-range order") (ODLRO) that characterizes [macroscopic quantum phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroscopic_quantum_phenomena "Macroscopic quantum phenomena") like superconductivity and superfluidity.
- Particle physics:
  - Parity non-conservation (1956): for predicting the violation of [parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_(physics) "Parity (physics)") symmetry with Lee, for which they were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1957.
  - [T, C, and P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPT_symmetry "CPT symmetry") (1957): work on explaining the relation between parity (P), [charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-symmetry "C-symmetry") (C) and [time-reversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-symmetry "T-symmetry") (T) symmetries, now known as [CPT symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPT_symmetry "CPT symmetry"). Done in collaboration with Lee and [Reinhard Oehme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Oehme "Reinhard Oehme").
  - Neutrino experiment (1960): for the Lee and Yang paper that proposed to study the weak interaction with high energy neutrinos. This work promoted various neutrino experiments.
  - CP nonconservation (1964): for his paper on [CP violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP_violation "CP violation"), in collaboration with T. T. Wu.
- Field theory:
  - Gauge theory (1954): for [Yang–Mills theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang–Mills_theory "Yang–Mills theory"), in collaboration with [Robert Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mills_(physicist) "Robert Mills (physicist)").
  - Integral formalism (1974): for giving meaning to the nonintegral phase factor in gauge theories.
  - Fiber bundle (1975): for the [Wu–Yang dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu–Yang_dictionary "Wu–Yang dictionary"), in collaboration with T. T. Wu.

He was awarde [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") (1958), [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University") (1992), and the [Chinese University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_University_of_Hong_Kong "Chinese University of Hong Kong") (1997).

### Awards and places named after him

Yang was the first president of the Association of Asia Pacific Physical Societies (AAPPS) when it was established in 1989. In 1997, the AAPPS created the C. N. Yang Award in his honor to highlight young researchers.

In 1998, after his retirement, the Institute of Theoretical Physics of [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University "Stony Brook University") was renamed [C. N. Yang Institute for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._N._Yang_Institute_for_Theoretical_Physics "C. N. Yang Institute for Theoretical Physics"). The C. N. Yang Hall, a residence hall and activity center at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University "Stony Brook University"), was dedicated in 2010.
