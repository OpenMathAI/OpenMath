**Robert Hofstadter** (February 5, 1915 – November 17, 1990) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). He was the joint winner of the 1961 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") (together with [Rudolf Mössbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Mössbauer "Rudolf Mössbauer")) "for his pioneering studies of [electron scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_scattering "Electron scattering") in [atomic nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nuclei "Atomic nuclei") and for his consequent discoveries concerning the structure of [nucleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleon "Nucleon")".

## Biography

Hofstadter was born in New York City on February 5, 1915, to [Polish Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Jewish "Polish Jewish") immigrants Louis Hofstadter, a salesman, and Henrietta, née Koenigsberg. He attended elementary and high schools in New York City and entered [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York "City College of New York"), graduating with a B.S. degree *[magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_cum_laude "Magna cum laude")* in 1935 at the age of 20, and was awarded the Kenyon Prize in Mathematics and Physics. He also received a Charles A. Coffin Foundation Fellowship from the [General Electric Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_Company "General Electric Company"), which enabled him to attend graduate school at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), where he earned his M.S. and Ph.D. degrees at the age of 23. His doctoral dissertation was titled "Infra-red absorption by light and heavy formic and acetic acids." He did his post-doctoral research at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") and was an assistant professor at Princeton before joining [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"). Hofstadter taught at Stanford from 1950 to 1985.

In 1942 he married Nancy Givan (1920–2007), a native of [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore"). They had three children: Laura, Molly, and [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize "Pulitzer Prize")-winner [Douglas Hofstadter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Hofstadter "Douglas Hofstadter").

## Opus

### Thallium-activated sodium iodide gamma ray detector

In 1948 Hofstadter filed a patent on this for the detection of [ionizing radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionizing_radiation "Ionizing radiation") by this crystal. These [thallium-activated sodium iodide detectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scintillation_counter#Detection_materials "Scintillation counter") are widely used for gamma ray detection to this day.

### Coining of the fermi (unit) and 1961 Nobel Lecture

Robert Hofstadter coined the term [fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi_(unit) "Fermi (unit)"), symbol **fm**,
in honor of the Italian [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi") (1901–1954), one of the founders of nuclear physics, in Hofstadter's 1956 paper published in the *[Reviews of Modern Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reviews_of_Modern_Physics "Reviews of Modern Physics")* journal, "Electron Scattering and Nuclear Structure". The term is widely used by nuclear and [particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics") physicists. When Hofstadter was awarded the 1961 Nobel Prize in Physics, it subsequently appeared in the text of his 1961 Nobel Lecture, "The electron-scattering method and its application to the structure of nuclei and nucleons" (December 11, 1961).

### Compton Gamma Ray Observatory and EGRET Telescope

In his last few years, Hofstadter became interested in [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics "Astrophysics") and applied his knowledge of [scintillators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scintillator "Scintillator") to the design of the [EGRET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energetic_Gamma_Ray_Experiment_Telescope "Energetic Gamma Ray Experiment Telescope") [gamma-ray telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma-ray_telescope "Gamma-ray telescope") of the [Compton Gamma Ray Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_Gamma_Ray_Observatory "Compton Gamma Ray Observatory") named for fellow Nobel Laureate in Physics (1927), [Arthur Holly Compton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Holly_Compton "Arthur Holly Compton"). [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University")'s Department of Physics credits Hofstadter with being "one of the principal scientists who developed the Compton Observatory."

## Awards and honors

- 1958, elected to [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences").
- 1961 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), joint winner with [Rudolf Mössbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Mössbauer "Rudolf Mössbauer"), "for his [Hofstadter's] pioneering studies of [electron scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_scattering "Electron scattering") in [atomic nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nuclei "Atomic nuclei") and for his consequent discoveries concerning the structure of [nucleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleon "Nucleon")."
- [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") has an annual lecture series named after Hofstadter, the [Robert Hofstadter Memorial Lectures](https://web.archive.org/web/20140228130505/https://physics.stanford.edu/people/faculty/robert-hofstadter), which consists of two lectures each year, one oriented toward the general public and the other oriented toward scientists.
- 1970, elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences").
- 1984 Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").
- 1986, elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society").
