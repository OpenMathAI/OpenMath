**Wolfgang Ernst Pauli** (25 April 1900 – 15 December 1958) was an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria "Austria") theoretical [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and a pioneer of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"). In 1945, after having been nominated by [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein"), Pauli received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") "for the discovery of the Exclusion Principle, also called the [Pauli Principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_exclusion_principle "Pauli exclusion principle")". The discovery involved [spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(physics) "Spin (physics)") theory, which is the basis of a theory of the [structure of matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter#Structure "Matter").

To preserve the [conservation of energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_energy "Conservation of energy") in [beta decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_decay "Beta decay"), Pauli proposed the existence of a small [neutral particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_particle "Neutral particle"), dubbed the "[neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino "Neutrino")" by [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi"), in 1930. Neutrinos were [first detected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowan–Reines_neutrino_experiment "Cowan–Reines neutrino experiment") in 1956.

## Biography

Wolfgang Ernst Pauli was born on 25 April 1900 in [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna "Vienna"), the son of Wolfgang Josef Pauli (né Pascheles) and Bertha Camilla Schütz. His sister was [Hertha Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertha_Pauli "Hertha Pauli"), a writer and actress. His middle name was given in honor of his [godfather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godparent "Godparent"), physicist [Ernst Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Mach "Ernst Mach"). His paternal grandparents were from prominent [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") families of [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague "Prague"); his great-grandfather was the Jewish publisher [Wolf Pascheles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Pascheles "Wolf Pascheles"). Bertha was raised in her mother's [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic "Roman Catholic") faith; her father was Jewish writer [Friedrich Schütz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Schütz "Friedrich Schütz"). Pauli was also raised as a Roman Catholic.

Pauli attended the Döblinger-Gymnasium in Vienna, graduating with distinction in 1918. Two months later, he published his first paper, on [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein")'s theory of [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity"). He studied under [Arnold Sommerfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Sommerfeld "Arnold Sommerfeld") at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich "LMU Munich"), where he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in 1921 with a thesis on the quantum theory of [ionized diatomic hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihydrogen_cation "Dihydrogen cation") (H+
2).

Sommerfeld asked Pauli to review the [theory of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_relativity "Theory of relativity") for the *[Encyclopedia of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein's_Encyclopedia_of_Mathematical_Sciences "Klein's Encyclopedia of Mathematical Sciences")*. Two months after receiving his doctorate, Pauli completed the article, which came to 237 pages. Einstein praised it and published it as a [monograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph "Monograph"). It remains a standard reference on the subject.

Pauli spent a year at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen") as an assistant to [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born "Max Born"), and the following year at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/43/Wolfgang_Pauli_young.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wolfgang_Pauli_young.jpg)

Wolfgang Pauli lecturing (1929)

From 1923 to 1928, Pauli was a lecturer at the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg "University of Hamburg"). During this period, he was instrumental in the development of the modern theory of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"). In particular, he formulated the [exclusion principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_exclusion_principle "Pauli exclusion principle") and the theory of nonrelativistic [spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(physics) "Spin (physics)").

In 1928, Pauli was appointed Professor of Theoretical Physics at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich") in Switzerland. He was awarded the [Lorentz Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_Medal "Lorentz Medal") in 1930. He held visiting professorships at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan") in 1931 and the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey "Princeton, New Jersey") in 1935.

The [German annexation of Austria in 1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss "Anschluss") made Pauli a German citizen, which became a problem for him in 1939 when [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") broke out. In 1940, he tried in vain to obtain Swiss citizenship, which would have allowed him to remain at the ETH. The same year, he moved to the United States and was appointed Professor of Theoretical Physics at the Institute for Advanced Study. In 1946, he became a [naturalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalization "Naturalization") U.S. citizen and returned to Zurich, where he remained for the rest of his life. In 1949, he was granted Swiss citizenship.

## Carl Jung

At the end of 1930, immediately after his divorce and his mother's death, Pauli experienced a personal crisis. In January 1932, he consulted psychiatrist and psychotherapist [Carl Jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Jung "Carl Jung"), who also lived near [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich "Zurich"). Jung immediately began interpreting Pauli's deeply [archetypal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archetypal "Archetypal") dreams and Pauli became a collaborator of Jung's. He soon began to critique the [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology "Epistemology") of Jung's theory scientifically, and this contributed to a certain clarification of Jung's ideas, especially about [synchronicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronicity "Synchronicity"). A great many of these discussions are documented in the Pauli/Jung letters, today published as *Atom and Archetype*. Jung's elaborate analysis of more than 400 of Pauli's dreams is documented in *[Psychology and Alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology_and_Alchemy "Psychology and Alchemy")*. In 1933 Pauli published the second part of his book on physics, *Handbuch der Physik*, which was considered the definitive book on the new field of quantum physics. [Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Oppenheimer "Robert Oppenheimer") called it "the only adult introduction to quantum mechanics."

## Research

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Wolfgang_Pauli_1953.png/250px-Wolfgang_Pauli_1953.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wolfgang_Pauli_1953.png)

Wolfgang Pauli 1953

Pauli made many important contributions as a physicist, primarily in the field of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"). He seldom published papers, preferring lengthy correspondences with colleagues such as [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr "Niels Bohr") from the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen") in Denmark and [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg "Werner Heisenberg"), with whom he had close friendships. Many of his ideas and results were never published and appeared only in his letters, which were often copied and circulated by their recipients. In 1921 Pauli worked with Bohr to create the [Aufbau Principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aufbau_Principle "Aufbau Principle"), which described building up electrons in shells based on the German word for building up, as Bohr was also fluent in German.

Pauli proposed in 1924 a new quantum degree of freedom (or [quantum number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_number "Quantum number")) with two possible values, to resolve inconsistencies between observed molecular spectra and the developing theory of quantum mechanics. He formulated the Pauli exclusion principle, perhaps his most important work, which stated that no two electrons could exist in the same quantum state, identified by four quantum numbers including his new two-valued degree of freedom. The idea of spin originated with [Ralph Kronig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Kronig "Ralph Kronig"). A year later, [George Uhlenbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Uhlenbeck "George Uhlenbeck") and [Samuel Goudsmit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Goudsmit "Samuel Goudsmit") identified Pauli's new degree of freedom as [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") [spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(physics) "Spin (physics)"), in which Pauli for a very long time wrongly refused to believe.

In 1926, shortly after Heisenberg published the [matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_mechanics "Matrix mechanics") of modern [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"), Pauli used it to derive the observed [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum "Spectrum") of the [hydrogen atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_atom "Hydrogen atom"). This result was important in securing credibility for Heisenberg's theory.

Pauli introduced the 2×2 [Pauli matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_matrices "Pauli matrices") as a basis of spin operators, thus solving the nonrelativistic theory of spin. This work, including the [Pauli equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_equation "Pauli equation"), is sometimes said to have influenced [Paul Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac") in his creation of the [Dirac equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_equation "Dirac equation") for the [relativistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativistic_particle "Relativistic particle") electron, though Dirac said that he invented these same matrices himself independently at the time. Dirac invented similar but larger (4x4) spin matrices for use in his relativistic treatment of [fermionic spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion "Fermion").

In 1930, Pauli considered the problem of [beta decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_decay "Beta decay"). In a letter of 4 December to [Lise Meitner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lise_Meitner "Lise Meitner") *et al.*, beginning, "[Dear radioactive ladies and gentlemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_neutrino#Pauli's_letter "Electron neutrino")", he proposed the existence of a hitherto unobserved neutral particle with a small mass, no greater than 1% the mass of a proton, to explain the continuous spectrum of beta decay. In 1934, [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi") incorporated the particle, which he called a [neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino "Neutrino"), "little neutral one" in Fermi's native Italian, into his theory of beta decay. The neutrino was first confirmed experimentally in 1956 by [Frederick Reines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Reines "Frederick Reines") and [Clyde Cowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_Cowan "Clyde Cowan"), two and a half years before Pauli's death. On receiving the news, he replied by telegram: "Thanks for message. Everything comes to him who knows how to wait. Pauli."

In 1940, Pauli re-derived the [spin-statistics theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin-statistics_theorem "Spin-statistics theorem"), a critical result of quantum field theory that states that particles with half-integer spin are [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion "Fermion"), while particles with integer spin are [bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson "Boson").

In 1949, he published a paper on [Pauli–Villars regularization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli–Villars_regularization "Pauli–Villars regularization"): regularization is the term for techniques that modify infinite mathematical integrals to make them finite during calculations, so that one can identify whether the intrinsically infinite quantities in the theory (mass, charge, wavefunction) form a finite and hence calculable set that can be redefined in terms of their experimental values, which criterion is termed [renormalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renormalization "Renormalization"), and which removes infinities from [quantum field theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field_theory "Quantum field theory"), but also importantly allows the calculation of higher-order corrections in perturbation theory.

Pauli made repeated criticisms of the [modern synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_synthesis_(20th_century) "Modern synthesis (20th century)") of [evolutionary biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_biology "Evolutionary biology"), and his contemporary admirers point to modes of [epigenetic inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics "Epigenetics") as supporting his arguments.

[Paul Drude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Drude "Paul Drude") in 1900 proposed the first theoretical model for a [classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_mechanics "Classical mechanics") [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") moving through a metallic solid. Drude's classical model was also augmented by Pauli and other physicists. Pauli realized that the free electrons in metal must obey the [Fermi–Dirac statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi–Dirac_statistics "Fermi–Dirac statistics"). Using this idea, he developed the theory of [paramagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramagnetism "Paramagnetism") in 1926. Pauli said, "Festkörperphysik ist eine Schmutzphysik"—solid-state physics is the physics of dirt.

## Personality and friendships

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Wolfgang_Pauli.jpg/250px-Wolfgang_Pauli.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wolfgang_Pauli.jpg)

Wolfgang Pauli, c. 1924

The [Pauli effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_effect "Pauli effect") was named after his anecdotal bizarre ability to break experimental equipment simply by being in its vicinity. Pauli was aware of his reputation and was delighted whenever the Pauli effect manifested. These strange occurrences were in line with his controversial investigations into the legitimacy of [parapsychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parapsychology "Parapsychology"), particularly his collaboration with [C. G. Jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._G._Jung "C. G. Jung") on [synchronicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronicity "Synchronicity"). [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born "Max Born") considered Pauli "only comparable to Einstein himself... perhaps even greater". Einstein declared Pauli his "spiritual heir".

Pauli was famously a perfectionist. This extended not just to his own work, but also to that of his colleagues. As a result, he became known in the physics community as the "conscience of physics", the critic to whom his colleagues were accountable. He could be scathing in his dismissal of any theory he found lacking, often labelling it *ganz falsch*, "utterly wrong".

But this was not his most severe criticism, which he reserved for theories or theses so unclearly presented as to be untestable or unevaluatable and thus not properly belonging within the realm of science, even though posing as such. They were worse than wrong because they could not be proved wrong. Famously, he once said of such an unclear paper: "It is [not even wrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Not_even_wrong "Not even wrong")!"

His supposed remark when meeting another leading physicist, [Paul Ehrenfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrenfest "Paul Ehrenfest"), illustrates this notion of an arrogant Pauli. The two met at a conference for the first time. Ehrenfest was familiar with Pauli's papers and quite impressed with them. After a few minutes of conversation, Ehrenfest remarked, "I think I like your Encyclopedia article [on relativity theory] better than I like you," to which Pauli retorted, "That's strange. With me, regarding you, it is just the opposite." The two became very good friends from then on.

A somewhat warmer picture emerges from this story, which appears in the article on Dirac:

> Werner Heisenberg [in *Physics and Beyond*, 1971] recollects a friendly conversation among young participants at the 1927 [Solvay Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvay_Conference "Solvay Conference"), about Einstein and [Planck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck "Max Planck")'s views on religion. Wolfgang Pauli, Heisenberg, and Dirac took part in it. Dirac's contribution was a poignant and clear criticism of the political manipulation of religion, that was much appreciated for its lucidity by Bohr, when Heisenberg reported it to him later. Among other things, Dirac said: "I cannot understand why we idle discussing religion. If we are honest – and as scientists honesty is our precise duty – we cannot help but admit that any religion is a pack of false statements, deprived of any real foundation. The very idea of God is a product of human imagination. [ ... ] I do not recognize any religious myth, at least because they contradict one another. [ ... ]" Heisenberg's view was tolerant. Pauli had kept silent, after some initial remarks. But when finally he was asked for his opinion, jokingly he said: "Well, I'd say that also our friend Dirac has got a religion and the first commandment of this religion is 'God does not exist and Paul Dirac is his prophet'". Everybody burst into laughter, including Dirac.

Many of Pauli's ideas and results were never published and appeared only in his letters, which were often copied and circulated by their recipients. Pauli may have been unconcerned that much of his work thus went uncredited, but when it came to Heisenberg's world-renowned 1958 lecture at Göttingen on their joint work on a unified field theory, and the press release calling Pauli a mere "assistant to Professor Heisenberg", Pauli became offended, denouncing Heisenberg's physics prowess. The deterioration of their relationship resulted in Heisenberg ignoring Pauli's funeral, and writing in his autobiography that Pauli's criticisms were overwrought, though ultimately the field theory was proved untenable, validating Pauli's criticisms.

[George Gamow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow "George Gamow") wrote that "it is just as difficult to find the branch of modern physics in which the Pauli Principle is not used as to find a man as gifted, amiable, and amusing as Wolfgang Pauli was."

## Personal life and death

In 1929, Pauli married Käthe Margarethe Deppner, a cabaret dancer. The marriage was unhappy, ending in divorce after less than a year. He remarried in 1934 to Franziska "Franca" Bertram. She donated Pauli's personal archives, including his [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal"), to [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN") in 1960 and 1971.

In 1958, Pauli was awarded the [Max Planck Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Medal "Max Planck Medal"). The same year, he fell ill with [pancreatic cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancreatic_cancer "Pancreatic cancer"). When his last assistant, [Charles Enz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Enz "Charles Enz"), visited him at the Rotkreuz hospital in Zurich, Pauli asked him, "Did you see the room number?" It was 137. Throughout his life, Pauli had been preoccupied with the question of why the [fine-structure constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-structure_constant "Fine-structure constant"), a [dimensionless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensional_analysis "Dimensional analysis") fundamental constant, has a value nearly equal to 1/137. Pauli died in that room on 15 December 1958.

## Philosophy and religion

In his discussions with [Carl Jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Jung "Carl Jung"), Pauli developed an ontological theory that has been dubbed the "Pauli–Jung Conjecture" and has been seen as a kind of [dual-aspect theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-aspect_theory "Double-aspect theory"). The theory holds that there is "a psychophysically neutral reality" and that mental and physical aspects are derivative of this reality. Pauli thought that elements of quantum physics pointed to a deeper reality that might explain the mind/matter gap and wrote, "we must postulate a cosmic order of nature beyond our control to which *both* the outward material objects *and* the inward images are subject."

Pauli and Jung held that this reality was governed by common principles ("[archetypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archetype "Archetype")") that appear as psychological phenomena or as physical events. They also held that [synchronicities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronicity "Synchronicity") might reveal some of this underlying reality's workings.

Pauli is considered to have been a [deist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deism "Deism") and a [mystic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysticism "Mysticism"). In *No Time to Be Brief: A Scientific Biography of Wolfgang Pauli*, he is quoted as writing to science historian [Shmuel Sambursky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shmuel_Sambursky "Shmuel Sambursky"), "In opposition to the monotheist religions – but in unison with the mysticism of all peoples, including the Jewish mysticism – I believe that the ultimate reality is not personal."

## Recognition
