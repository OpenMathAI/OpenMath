**Henry Way Kendall** (December 9, 1926 – February 15, 1999) was an American particle physicist who won the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1990 jointly with [Jerome Isaac Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Isaac_Friedman "Jerome Isaac Friedman") and [Richard E. Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_E._Taylor "Richard E. Taylor") "for their pioneering investigations concerning [deep inelastic scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_inelastic_scattering "Deep inelastic scattering") of [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") on [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") and bound [neutrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron "Neutron"), which have been of essential importance for the development of the [quark model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark_model "Quark model") in particle physics."

## Biography

Kendall was born in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston") to Evelyn Way and [Henry P. Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_P._Kendall "Henry P. Kendall"), an industrialist. Kendall grew up in [Sharon, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon,_Massachusetts "Sharon, Massachusetts") and attended [Deerfield Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deerfield_Academy "Deerfield Academy"). He enrolled in the [U.S. Merchant Marine Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Merchant_Marine_Academy "United States Merchant Marine Academy") in 1945, and served on a troop transport on the North Atlantic in the winter of 1945 – 1946.

In 1946, he enrolled at [Amherst College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst_College "Amherst College") where he majored in mathematics, graduating in 1950. While at Amherst, he operated a [diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_diving "Underwater diving") and [marine salvage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_salvage "Marine salvage") company during two summers. He co-authored two books, one on shallow water diving and the other on [underwater photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_photography "Underwater photography").

He did graduate research at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"), involving an experimental study of [positronium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positronium "Positronium"), and he obtained his PhD in 1955. He then spent the next two years as a postdoctoral fellow at [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory"). He then spent five years in [Robert Hofstadter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hofstadter "Robert Hofstadter")'s research group at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") in the late 1950s and early 1960s, where he worked with [Jerome Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Isaac_Friedman "Jerome Isaac Friedman") and [Richard Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_E._Taylor "Richard E. Taylor"), studying the structure of [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") and [neutrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron "Neutron"), using the university's 300 feet long [linear electron accelerator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_particle_accelerator "Linear particle accelerator"). He developed a close working relationship with [Wolfgang K. H. Panofsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_K._H._Panofsky "Wolfgang K. H. Panofsky") at Stanford.

Kendall joined the faculty of the [MIT Physics Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Physics_Department "MIT Physics Department") in 1961, where he remained until his death in 1999. He was named Julius A. Stratton Professor of Physics in 1991.

In the late 1960s and early 1970s, Kendall worked in collaboration with researchers at the [Stanford Linear Accelerator Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Linear_Accelerator_Center "Stanford Linear Accelerator Center") (SLAC) including Friedman and Taylor. These experiments involved scattering high-energy beams of [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") from [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") and [deuterons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuteron "Deuteron") and heavier [nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus "Atomic nucleus"). At lower energies, it had already been found that the electrons would only be scattered through low angles, consistent with the idea that the [nucleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleon "Nucleon") had no internal structure. However, the SLAC-MIT experiments showed that higher energy electrons could be scattered through much higher angles, with the loss of some energy. These deep inelastic scattering results provided the first experimental evidence that the protons and neutrons were made up of point-like particles, later identified to be the [up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up_quark "Up quark") and [down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_quark "Down quark") [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark "Quark") that had previously been proposed on theoretical grounds. The experiments also provided the first evidence for the existence of [gluons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluon "Gluon").

Kendall was not only a very accomplished physicist, but also a very skilled mountaineer and photographer. He did extensive rock climbing in [Yosemite Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yosemite_Valley "Yosemite Valley"), followed by expeditions to the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes "Andes"), [Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya "Himalaya") and [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica"), photographing his experiences with large format cameras. He was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1982. On April 7, 2012, the [American Alpine Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Alpine_Club "American Alpine Club") inducted Kendall into its Hall of Mountaineering Excellence at an award ceremony in [Golden, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden,_Colorado "Golden, Colorado").

## Service activities

Kendall was one of the founding members of the [Union of Concerned Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Concerned_Scientists "Union of Concerned Scientists") (UCS) in 1969. He served as chairman of the board of the UCS from 1974 until his death in 1999. His public policy interests included avoiding [nuclear war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_warfare "Nuclear warfare"), the [Strategic Defense Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defense_Initiative "Strategic Defense Initiative"), the [B2 bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-2_Spirit "B-2 Spirit"), [nuclear reactor safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_safety "Nuclear safety") and [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming "Global warming").

He was also a member of the [JASON Defense Advisory Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JASON_Defense_Advisory_Group "JASON Defense Advisory Group").

## Death

Kendall died while diving the cave at the [Edward Ball Wakulla Springs State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Ball_Wakulla_Springs_State_Park "Edward Ball Wakulla Springs State Park"), Florida as a part of the Wakulla 2 Project. He bypassed two pre-dive [checklists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checklist "Checklist") for his [Cis-Lunar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cis-Lunar "Cis-Lunar") MK-5P Mixed Gas [rebreather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebreather "Rebreather") and entered the spring basin without his [dive buddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_diving "Buddy diving") from the [National Geographic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_Society "National Geographic Society"). Kendall missed turning on the [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen "Oxygen") supply to his rebreather and [lost consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconsciousness "Unconsciousness") and drowned. The [autopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopsy "Autopsy") revealed a physiological issue that led to his disregarding the protocols.
