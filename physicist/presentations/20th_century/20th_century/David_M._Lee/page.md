**David Morris Lee** (born January 20, 1931) is an American physicist who shared the 1996 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Robert C. Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Coleman_Richardson "Robert Coleman Richardson") and [Douglas Osheroff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_D._Osheroff "Douglas D. Osheroff") "for their discovery of [superfluidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluidity "Superfluidity") in [helium-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium-3 "Helium-3")." Lee is professor emeritus of physics at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") and distinguished professor of physics at [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University "Texas A&M University").

## Personal life

Lee was born and raised in [Rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye_(town),_New_York "Rye (town), New York"), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(state) "New York (state)"). His parents, Annette (Franks), a teacher, and Marvin Lee, an electrical engineer, were children of [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") immigrants from [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England "England") and [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania "Lithuania"). He graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") in 1952 and then joined the [U.S. Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army "U.S. Army") for 22 months. After being discharged from the army, he obtained a master's degree from the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut "University of Connecticut"). In 1955 Lee entered the Ph.D. program at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") where he worked under [Henry A. Fairbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_A._Fairbank?action=edit&redlink=1 "Henry A. Fairbank") in the low-temperature physics group, doing experimental research on liquid 3He.

After graduating from Yale in 1959, Lee took a job at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"), where he was responsible for setting up the new [Laboratory of Atomic and Solid State Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Atomic_and_Solid_State_Physics?action=edit&redlink=1 "Laboratory of Atomic and Solid State Physics"). Shortly after arriving at Cornell he met his future wife, Dana, then a PhD student in another department; the couple went on to have two sons.

Lee moved his laboratory from Cornell to [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University "Texas A&M University") on November 16, 2009.

## Work

The work that led to Lee's Nobel Prize was performed in the early 1970s. Lee, together with [Robert C. Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Coleman_Richardson "Robert Coleman Richardson") and graduate student, [Doug Osheroff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_D._Osheroff "Douglas D. Osheroff") used a [Pomeranchuk cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranchuk_cooler "Pomeranchuk cooler") to investigate the behaviour of [3He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium-3 "Helium-3") at temperatures within a few thousandths of a degree of absolute zero. They discovered unexpected effects in their measurements, which they eventually explained as phase transitions to a superfluid phase of 3He. Lee, Richardson and Osheroff were jointly awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1996 for this discovery.

Lee's research also covered a number of other topics in low-temperature physics, particularly relating to liquid, solid and superfluid helium (4He, 3He and mixtures of the two). Particular discoveries include the antiferromagnetic ordering in solid helium-3, [nuclear spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_spin "Nuclear spin") waves in spin polarized atomic hydrogen gas with [Jack H. Freed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_H._Freed "Jack H. Freed"), and the tri-critical point on the phase separation curve of liquid 4He-3He, in collaboration with his Cornell colleague [John Reppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Reppy "John Reppy"). His former research group at Cornell currently studies impurity-helium solids.

As well as the Nobel Prize, other prizes won by Lee include the 1976 [Sir Francis Simon Memorial Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Memorial_Prize "Simon Memorial Prize") of the British [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics "Institute of Physics") and the 1981 [Oliver Buckley Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Buckley_Prize "Oliver Buckley Prize") of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") along with Doug Osheroff and Robert Richardson for their superfluid 3He work. In 1997, Lee received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").

Lee is a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences").

Lee is currently teaching physics at [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University "Texas A&M University") and continuing his (formerly Cornell-based) research program there as well.

Lee is one of the 20 American recipients of the Nobel Prize in Physics to sign a letter addressed to President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") in May 2008, urging him to "reverse the damage done to basic science research in the Fiscal Year 2008 Omnibus Appropriations Bill" by requesting additional emergency funding for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")’s [Office of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Science "Office of Science"), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), and the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology").
