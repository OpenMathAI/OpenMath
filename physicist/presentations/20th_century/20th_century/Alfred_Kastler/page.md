**Alfred Kastler** (French: [[kastlɛʁ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 3 May 1902 – 7 January 1984) was a German-born French [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and [Nobel laureate in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"). He is known for the development of [optical pumping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_pumping "Optical pumping").

## Biography

Kastler was born in [Guebwiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guebwiller "Guebwiller") ([Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace-Lorraine "Alsace-Lorraine"), at the time part of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire")), and became a French citizen when Alsace reverted to France at the end of World War I. He attended the [Lycée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée "Lycée") Bartholdi in [Colmar, Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colmar,_Alsace "Colmar, Alsace"), and then [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure "École Normale Supérieure") in Paris in 1921. After his studies, he began teaching [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") at the Lycée of [Mulhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulhouse "Mulhouse") in 1926, and then taught at the [University of Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bordeaux "University of Bordeaux"), where he was a university professor until 1941. [Georges Bruhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Bruhat "Georges Bruhat") asked him to come back to the École Normale Supérieure, where he finally obtained a chair in 1952.

Collaborating with [Jean Brossel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Brossel "Jean Brossel"), he researched [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"), the interaction between [light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light "Light") and [atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom "Atom"), and [spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy "Spectroscopy"). Kastler, working on combination of [optical resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_resonator "Optical resonator") and [magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance"), developed the technique of "[optical pumping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_pumping "Optical pumping")". Those works led to the completion of the theory of [lasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser "Laser") and [masers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maser "Maser").

In 1962, he received the first C.E.K Mees Medal from the [Optical Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optica_(society) "Optica (society)"), and he was elected an Honorary member of the Society. The following year, he was elected a Fellow.

He won the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1966 "for the discovery and development of optical methods for studying Hertzian resonances in atoms".

He was president of the board of the Institut d'optique théorique et appliquée and served as the first chairman of the [non-governmental organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organization "Non-governmental organization") (NGO) [Action Against Hunger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_Against_Hunger "Action Against Hunger").

Kastler also wrote poetry (in [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language "German language")). In 1971 he published *Europe, ma patrie: Deutsche Lieder eines französischen Europäers* (i.e. *Europe, my fatherland: German songs of a French European*).

In 1976, Kastler was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society").

In 1978 he became foreign member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences").

In 1979, Kastler was awarded the [Wilhelm Exner Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Exner_Medal "Wilhelm Exner Medal").

## Laboratoire Kastler-Brossel

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Alfred_Kastler_French_physicist.jpg/330px-Alfred_Kastler_French_physicist.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Alfred_Kastler_French_physicist.jpg)

Kastler ca. 1967

Professor Kastler spent most of his research career at the [Ecole Normale Supérieure in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_(Paris) "École normale supérieure (Paris)") where he started after the war a small research group on spectroscopy with his student, [Jean Brossel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Brossel "Jean Brossel").

Over the forty years that followed, this group trained many young physicists, including Nobel laureates [Claude Cohen Tannoudji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Cohen_Tannoudji "Claude Cohen Tannoudji") and [Serge Haroche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serge_Haroche "Serge Haroche"), and had a significant impact on the development of [atomic physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_physics "Atomic physics") in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France"). The Laboratoire de Spectroscopie hertzienne was renamed [Laboratoire Kastler-Brossel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratoire_Kastler-Brossel "Laboratoire Kastler-Brossel") in 1994. It has part of its laboratories in [Université Pierre et Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Pierre_et_Marie_Curie "Université Pierre et Marie Curie") but mainly at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure "École Normale Supérieure").

## Global policy

He was one of the signatories of the agreement to convene a convention for drafting a [world constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_constitution "World constitution"). As a result, for the first time in human history, a [World Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Constituent_Assembly "World Constituent Assembly") convened to draft and adopt the [Constitution for the Federation of Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_for_the_Federation_of_Earth "Constitution for the Federation of Earth").

## Personal life

In December 1924, Kastler married Elise Cosset, a teacher. They had three children, [Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kastler "Daniel Kastler"), Claude-Yves, and Mireille. His sons became teachers, and Mireille became a doctor.

## Death

Professor Kastler died on 7 January 1984, in [Bandol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandol "Bandol"), France.
