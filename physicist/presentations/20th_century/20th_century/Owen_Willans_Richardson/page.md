**Sir Owen Willans Richardson** (26 April 1879 – 15 February 1959) was a British [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who received the 1928 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for his work on [thermionic emission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermionic_emission "Thermionic emission") and for the discovery of [Richardson's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richardson's_law "Richardson's law").

## Biography

Owen Willans Richardson was born on 26 April 1879 in [Dewsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewsbury "Dewsbury"), England, the only son of Joshua Henry Richardson and Charlotte Maria Willans. Richardson was educated at [Batley Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batley_Grammar_School "Batley Grammar School"), before entering [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"), in 1897, where he gained [First Class Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Class_Honours "First Class Honours") in Natural Science in 1900 and was elected a Fellow in 1902. He obtained a [D.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.Sc. "D.Sc.") from [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London") in 1904.

In 1906, Richardson was appointed Professor of Physics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in the United States, a position he held until 1913. The following year, he returned to England to become Wheatstone Professor of Physics at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London "King's College London"), where he was later made Director of Research in 1924. He retired from King's College in 1944.

Richardson died on 15 February 1959 in [Alton, Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alton,_Hampshire "Alton, Hampshire"), at the age of 79. He is buried in [Brookwood Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookwood_Cemetery "Brookwood Cemetery") (Plot 8) in [Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey "Surrey").

## Research

In 1900, Richardson began researching the [emission of electricity from hot bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermionic_emission "Thermionic emission") in the [Cavendish Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Laboratory "Cavendish Laboratory") at Cambridge. The following year, he demonstrated that the current from a heated wire seemed to depend exponentially on the temperature of the wire with a mathematical form similar to the [Arrhenius equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrhenius_equation "Arrhenius equation"). Richardson also researched the [photoelectric effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoelectric_effect "Photoelectric effect"), the [gyromagnetic effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyromagnetic_ratio "Gyromagnetic ratio"), the emission of electrons by chemical reactions, soft [X-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray "X-ray"), and the spectrum of hydrogen.

## Family

In 1906, Richardson married Lilian Maud Wilson, the sister of his Cavendish colleague, [Harold Albert Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Albert_Wilson "Harold Albert Wilson"). They had two sons and a daughter.

Richardson had two sisters: Elizabeth Mary Dixon Richardson, who married the prominent mathematician [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen "Oswald Veblen"); and Charlotte Sara Richardson, who married the American physicist (and 1937 Nobel laureate in Physics) [Clinton Davisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Davisson "Clinton Davisson"), who was Richardson's Ph.D. student at Princeton. After Lilian's death in 1945, he was remarried in 1948 to Henriette Rupp, a physicist, previously married to [Emil Rupp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Rupp "Emil Rupp").

Richardson had a son, Harold Owen Richardson, who specialised in nuclear physics and was also the chairman of the Physics Department at Bedford College, London University, and later on became emeritus professor at London University.
