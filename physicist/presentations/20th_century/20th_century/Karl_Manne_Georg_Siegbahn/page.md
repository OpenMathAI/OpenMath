**Karl Manne Georg Siegbahn** (Swedish: [[ˈmanːɛ ˈsiːgbɑːn]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Swedish "Help:IPA/Swedish"); 3 December 1886 – 26 September 1978) was a Swedish [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1924 "for his discoveries and research in the field of [X-ray spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_spectroscopy "X-ray spectroscopy")."

## Education and career

Karl Manne Georg Siegbahn was born on 3 December 1886 in [Örebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Örebro "Örebro"), Sweden, the son of Nils Reinhold Georg Siegbahn, a [station master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_master "Station master"), and Emma Sofia Mathilda Zetterberg.

Siegbahn graduated in Stockholm 1906 and began his studies at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University "Lund University") the same year. During his education he was secretarial assistant to [Johannes Rydberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Rydberg "Johannes Rydberg"). In 1908, he studied at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"). He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") from Lund in 1911 with a thesis titled *Magnetische feldmessung* (*Magnetic field measurements*). He became acting professor for Rydberg when his (Rydberg's) health was failing, and succeeded him as full professor in 1920 following his death. In 1923, he left Lund to become Professor of Physics at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University").

In 1937, Siegbahn was appointed Research Professor of Experimental Physics at the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences"). In 1988, this was renamed the Manne Siegbahn Institute (MSI). The institute research groups have been reorganized since, but the name lives on in the Manne Siegbahn Laboratory hosted by [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University "Stockholm University").

## X-ray spectroscopy

Siegbahn began his studies of [X-ray spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_spectroscopy "X-ray spectroscopy") in 1914. Initially, he used the same type of [spectrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrometer "Spectrometer") as [Henry Moseley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Moseley "Henry Moseley") had done for finding the relationship between the frequency of [characteristic X-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_X-ray "Characteristic X-ray") some [elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element "Chemical element") and their place at the [periodic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_system "Periodic system"). Shortly thereafter he developed improved experimental apparatus which allowed him to make very accurate measurements of the X-ray wavelengths produced by atoms of different elements. Also, he found that several of the [spectral lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_line "Spectral line") that Moseley had discovered consisted of more components. By studying these components and improving the spectrometer, Siegbahn got an almost complete understanding of the [electron shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_shell "Electron shell"). He developed a convention for naming the different spectral lines that are characteristic to elements in X-ray spectroscopy, the [Siegbahn notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegbahn_notation "Siegbahn notation"). Siegbahn's precision measurements drove many developments in [quantum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") and [atomic physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_physics "Atomic physics").

## Family

Siegbahn married Karin Högbom in 1914. They had two children: [Bo Siegbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bo_Siegbahn "Bo Siegbahn") (1915–2008), a diplomat and politician, and [Kai Siegbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Siegbahn "Kai Siegbahn") (1918–2007), a physicist who received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1981 for his contribution to the development of [X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_photoelectron_spectroscopy "X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy").
