**Owen Chamberlain** (July 10, 1920 – February 28, 2006) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared with [Emilio Segrè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Segrè "Emilio Segrè") the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for the discovery of the [antiproton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiproton "Antiproton"), a [sub-atomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_atomic_particle "Sub atomic particle") [antiparticle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiparticle "Antiparticle").

## Biography

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Chamberlain-owen.jpg/250px-Chamberlain-owen.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Chamberlain-owen.jpg)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Chamberlain-babette_c.jpg/250px-Chamberlain-babette_c.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Chamberlain-babette_c.jpg)

Owen and Babette Chamberlain Los Alamos badges

Born in [San Francisco, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco,_California "San Francisco, California"), Chamberlain graduated from [Germantown Friends School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germantown_Friends_School "Germantown Friends School") in Philadelphia in 1937. He studied physics at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College "Dartmouth College"), where he was a member of Alpha Theta chapter of [Theta Chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theta_Chi "Theta Chi") fraternity, and at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"). He remained in school until the start of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), and joined the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project") in 1942, where he worked with Segrè, both at Berkeley and in [Los Alamos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos,_New_Mexico "Los Alamos, New Mexico"), [New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico "New Mexico"). He married Beatrice Babette Copper (d. 1988) in 1943, with whom he had four children.

In 1946, after the war, Chamberlain continued with his doctoral studies at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") under physicist [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi"). Fermi acted as an important guide and mentor for Chamberlain, encouraging him to leave behind [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics") for [experimental physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics "Experimental physics"), for which Chamberlain had a particular aptitude. Chamberlain received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") from the University of Chicago in 1949.

In 1948, having completed his experimental work, Chamberlain returned to Berkeley as a member of its faculty. There he, Segrè, and other physicists investigated proton-proton scattering. In 1955, a series of proton scattering experiments at Berkeley's [Bevatron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bevatron "Bevatron") led to the discovery of the anti-proton, a particle like a proton but negatively charged. Chamberlain's later research work included the [time projection chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_projection_chamber "Time projection chamber") (TPC), and work at the [Stanford Linear Accelerator Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Linear_Accelerator_Center "Stanford Linear Accelerator Center") (SLAC).

Chamberlain was politically active on issues of peace and social justice, and outspoken against the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"). He was a member of Scientists for Sakharov, Orlov, and Shcharansky, three physicists of the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") imprisoned for their political beliefs. In the 1980s, he helped found the [nuclear freeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_freeze "Nuclear freeze") movement. In 2003 he was one of 22 Nobel Laureates who signed the [Humanist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism_and_Its_Aspirations "Humanism and Its Aspirations").

After the death of his first wife in 1988, Chamberlain married artist June Steingart Greenfield, who died in 1991. His third wife, Senta Pugh-Chamberlain (née Gaiser) was the widow of physicist Howell Pugh.

Chamberlain was diagnosed with [Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease") in 1985, and retired from teaching in 1989. He died of complications from the disease on February 28, 2006, in Berkeley at the age of 85. He was survived by his third wife, his four children from his first marriage, and two step-daughters from his third marriage.

Chamberlain plays a central role in [Jacob M. Appel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_M._Appel "Jacob M. Appel")'s [Sherwood Anderson Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwood_Anderson_Foundation "Sherwood Anderson Foundation")-winning short story, "Measures of Sorrow".
