**Antony Hewish** (11 May 1924 – 13 September 2021) was a British [radio astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_astronomy "Radio astronomy") who won the [Nobel Prize for Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Physics "Nobel Prize for Physics") in 1974 (together with fellow radio-astronomer [Martin Ryle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Ryle "Martin Ryle")) for his role in the discovery of [pulsars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsar "Pulsar"). He was also awarded the [Eddington Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddington_Medal "Eddington Medal") of the [Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society "Royal Astronomical Society") in 1969.

## Early life and education

Hewish attended [King's College, Taunton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Taunton "King's College, Taunton"). His [undergraduate degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate_degree "Undergraduate degree"), at [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge "Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge"), was interrupted by the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"). He was assigned to war service at the [Royal Aircraft Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Aircraft_Establishment "Royal Aircraft Establishment"), and at the [Telecommunications Research Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_Research_Establishment "Telecommunications Research Establishment") where he worked with [Martin Ryle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Ryle "Martin Ryle"). Returning to the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") in 1946, Hewish completed his undergraduate degree and became a [postgraduate student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate_student "Postgraduate student") in [Ryle's research team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Astrophysics_Group "Cavendish Astrophysics Group") at the [Cavendish Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Laboratory "Cavendish Laboratory"). For his [PhD thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD_thesis "PhD thesis"), awarded in 1952, Hewish made practical and theoretical advances in the observation and exploitation of the [scintillations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interplanetary_scintillation "Interplanetary scintillation") of [astronomical radio sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_radio_source "Astronomical radio source"), due to foreground [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_(physics) "Plasma (physics)").

## Career and research

Hewish proposed the construction of a large [phased array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phased_array "Phased array") radio telescope, which could be used to perform a [survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_survey "Astronomical survey") at high [time resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-domain_astronomy "Time-domain astronomy"), primarily for studying [interplanetary scintillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interplanetary_scintillation "Interplanetary scintillation"). In 1965 he secured funding to construct his design, the [Interplanetary Scintillation Array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interplanetary_Scintillation_Array "Interplanetary Scintillation Array"), at the [Mullard Radio Astronomy Observatory (MRAO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullard_Radio_Astronomy_Observatory "Mullard Radio Astronomy Observatory") outside [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge"). It was completed in 1967. One of Hewish's [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") students, [Jocelyn Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jocelyn_Bell_Burnell "Jocelyn Bell Burnell") (later known as Jocelyn Bell Burnell), helped to build the array and was assigned to analyse its output. Bell soon discovered a radio source which was ultimately recognised as the first [pulsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsar "Pulsar"). Hewish initially thought that the signal might be [radio frequency interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency_interference "Radio frequency interference"), but it remained at a constant [right ascension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_ascension "Right ascension"), which is unlikely for a terrestrial source. The [scientific paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_paper "Scientific paper") announcing the discovery had five authors, Hewish's name being [listed first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_author "First author"), Bell's second.

Hewish and Ryle were awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1974 for work on the development of radio aperture synthesis and for Hewish's decisive role in the discovery of pulsars. The exclusion of Bell from the Nobel prize was controversial (see [Nobel Prize in Physics controversies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics_controversies#1974:_Ryle_and_Hewish "Nobel Prize in Physics controversies")). Fellow Cambridge astronomer [Fred Hoyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Hoyle "Fred Hoyle") argued that Bell should have received a share of the prize, although Bell herself stated "it would demean Nobel Prizes if they were awarded to research students, except in very exceptional cases, and I do not believe this is one of them". [Michael Rowan-Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Rowan-Robinson "Michael Rowan-Robinson") later wrote that "Hewish was undoubtedly the major player in the work that led to the discovery, inventing the scintillation technique in 1952, leading the team that built the array and made the discovery, and providing the interpretation".

Hewish at a conference in 1976

Hewish was professor of radio astronomy in the [Cavendish Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Laboratory "Cavendish Laboratory") from 1971 to 1989 and head of the [MRAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullard_Radio_Astronomy_Observatory "Mullard Radio Astronomy Observatory") from 1982 to 1988. He developed an association with the [Royal Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institution "Royal Institution") in London when it was directed by Sir [Lawrence Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Bragg "Lawrence Bragg"). In 1965 he was invited to co-deliver the [Royal Institution Christmas Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institution_Christmas_Lectures "Royal Institution Christmas Lectures") on "Exploration of the Universe". He subsequently gave several Friday Evening Discourses and was made a Professor of the Royal Institution in 1977. Hewish was a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow "Fellow") of [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge "Churchill College, Cambridge"). He was also a member of the Advisory Council for the [Campaign for Science and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_Science_and_Engineering "Campaign for Science and Engineering").

### Awards and honours

Hewish had honorary degrees from six universities, including Manchester, Exeter and Cambridge, was a foreign member of the [Belgian Royal Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Royal_Academies_for_Science_and_the_Arts_of_Belgium "The Royal Academies for Science and the Arts of Belgium"), [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") and the [Indian National Science Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Science_Academy "Indian National Science Academy"). The [National Portrait Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Portrait_Gallery,_London "National Portrait Gallery, London") holds multiple portraits of him in its permanent collection. Other awards and honours include:

- Elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1968 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1968")
- [Eddington Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddington_Medal "Eddington Medal"), [Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society "Royal Astronomical Society") (1969)
- Dellinger Gold Medal, [International Union of Radio Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_of_Radio_Science "International Union of Radio Science") (1972)
- [Albert A. Michelson Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_A._Michelson_Medal "Albert A. Michelson Medal"), [Franklin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Institute "Franklin Institute") (1973, jointly with [Jocelyn Bell Burnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jocelyn_Bell_Burnell "Jocelyn Bell Burnell"))
- [Fernand Holweck Medal and Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernand_Holweck_Medal_and_Prize "Fernand Holweck Medal and Prize") (1974)
- Nobel Prize for Physics (jointly) (1974)
- [Hughes Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Medal "Hughes Medal"), [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") (1976)
- Elected a [Fellow of the Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Institute_of_Physics "Fellow of the Institute of Physics") (FInstP) in 1998

## Personal life

Hewish married Marjorie Elizabeth Catherine Richards in 1950. They had a son, a physicist, and a daughter, a language teacher. Hewish died on 13 September 2021, aged 97. Following his death, Hewish's [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") and other prizes were donated to [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge "Churchill College, Cambridge") by his family.

### Religious views

Hewish argued that religion and science are complementary. In the foreword to *[Questions of Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Questions_of_Truth "Questions of Truth")*, Hewish writes, "The ghostly presence of [virtual particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_particle "Virtual particle") defies rational common sense and is non-intuitive for those unacquainted with physics. Religious belief in God, and Christian belief ... may seem strange to common-sense thinking. But when the most elementary physical things behave in this way, we should be prepared to accept that the deepest aspects of our existence go beyond our common-sense understanding."
