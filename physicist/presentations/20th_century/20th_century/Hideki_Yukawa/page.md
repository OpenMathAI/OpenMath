**Hideki Yukawa** ([Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): 湯川 秀樹; [né](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") **Ogawa**; 23 January 1907 – 8 September 1981) was a Japanese theoretical [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1949 "for his prediction of the existence of [mesons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meson "Meson") on the basis of theoretical work on [nuclear forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_force "Nuclear force")."

## Early life and education

Hideki Ogawa was born on 23 January 1907 in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo"), Japan, the third son of Takuji Ogawa, and grew up in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto "Kyoto"), where his father became Professor of Geology at [Kyoto Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Imperial_University "Kyoto Imperial University"). He read the Confucian *[Doctrine of the Mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine_of_the_Mean "Doctrine of the Mean")*, and later [Lao-Tzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao-Tzu "Lao-Tzu") and [Chuang-Tzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_Zhou "Zhuang Zhou"). His father, for a time, considered sending him to technical college rather than university since he was "not as outstanding a student as his older brothers." However, when his father broached the idea with his middle school principal, the principal praised his "high potential" in mathematics and offered to adopt Ogawa himself in order to keep him on a scholarly career. At that, his father relented.

Ogawa decided against becoming a mathematician when his high school teacher marked his exam answer as incorrect when Ogawa proved a theorem but in a different manner than the teacher expected. He decided against a career in experimental physics in college when he demonstrated clumsiness in glassblowing, a requirement for experiments in [spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy "Spectroscopy").

In 1929, Ogawa graduated from Kyoto Imperial University, where he was a lecturer from 1932 to 1939. During this period, he was interested in [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics"), particularly in the theory of [elementary particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle "Elementary particle"). In 1933, he became Lecturer and Assistant Professor of Physics at [Osaka Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Imperial_University "Osaka Imperial University").

## Career and research

> Physics is a science that has made rapid progress in the twentieth century ... I desire, as I did in the past, to be a traveler in a strange land and a colonist in a new country. (from the foreword to his autobiography)

In 1935, Yukawa published his theory of [mesons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meson "Meson"), which explained the interaction between [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") and [neutrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron "Neutron") at Osaka Imperial University, and was a major influence on research into elementary particles.

In 1938, Yukawa received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") from Osaka Imperial University for his predictions regarding the existence of mesons and his theoretical work on the nature of [nuclear forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_force "Nuclear force"). These research achievements were the reason he was later awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics").

In 1939, Yukawa was appointed Professor of Theoretical Physics at [Kyoto Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Imperial_University "Kyoto Imperial University"). In 1948, he became a visiting professor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study"), and at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") the following year.

In 1949, Yukawa received the Nobel Prize in Physics, after the discovery by [Cecil Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Powell "Cecil Powell"), [Giuseppe Occhialini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Occhialini "Giuseppe Occhialini"), and [César Lattes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/César_Lattes "César Lattes") of Yukawa's predicted [pi meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pion "Pion") in 1947. Yukawa also worked on the theory of [K-capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-capture "K-capture"), in which a low energy electron is absorbed by the nucleus, after its initial prediction by [G. C. Wick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._C._Wick "G. C. Wick").

> [Once I had published my seminal 1934 paper on particle interaction] I felt like a traveler who rests himself at a small tea shop at the top of a mountain slope. At that time I was not thinking about whether there were any more mountains ahead. [conclusion of his autobiography]

In 1946, Yukawa founded the journal *[Progress of Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_of_Theoretical_Physics "Progress of Theoretical Physics")*, and published the books *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics* (1946) and *Introduction to the Theory of Elementary Particles* (1948).

In 1953, Yukawa became the first Director of the Research Institute for Fundamental Physics (now the [Yukawa Institute for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukawa_Institute_for_Theoretical_Physics "Yukawa Institute for Theoretical Physics")), a position he held until his retirement in 1970.

## Activism

In 1955, Yukawa joined ten other leading scientists and intellectuals in signing the [Russell–Einstein Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell–Einstein_Manifesto "Russell–Einstein Manifesto"), calling for [nuclear disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_disarmament "Nuclear disarmament").

Yukawa was one of the signatories of the agreement to convene a convention for drafting a [world constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_constitution "World constitution"); subsequently, a [World Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Constituent_Assembly "World Constituent Assembly") convened to draft and adopt a [Constitution for the Federation of Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_for_the_Federation_of_Earth "Constitution for the Federation of Earth").

## Personal life and death

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Hideki_Yukawa_with_family_1949.jpg/330px-Hideki_Yukawa_with_family_1949.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hideki_Yukawa_with_family_1949.jpg)

Yukawa with his family, 1949.

In 1932, he married Sumi Yukawa ([スミ](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/湯川スミ "ja:湯川スミ")). In accordance with Japanese customs (see [Mukoyōshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukoyōshi "Mukoyōshi")), since he came from a family with many sons—but his father-in-law, Genyo, had none—he was adopted by Genyo and changed his family name from Ogawa to Yukawa. The couple had two sons, Takaaki and Harumi. Takaaki Yukawa (1934–1971) also became a theoretical physicist, earning a Ph.D. from [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin "Trinity College Dublin") in 1965 under [John Lighton Synge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lighton_Synge "John Lighton Synge").

Owing to increasing infirmity, in his final years Yukawa appeared in public in a wheelchair. He died of [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia "Pneumonia") and [heart failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_failure "Heart failure") on 8 September 1981 at his home in [Sakyo-ku, Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakyo-ku,_Kyoto "Sakyo-ku, Kyoto"), at the age of 74. His tomb is in [Higashiyama-ku, Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higashiyama-ku,_Kyoto "Higashiyama-ku, Kyoto").

Solo violinist [Diana Yukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_Yukawa "Diana Yukawa") (ダイアナ湯川) is a close relative of Hideki Yukawa.
