**Polykarp Kusch** (German: [[ˈpoːliˌkaʁp ˈkuʃ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German"); January 26, 1911 – March 20, 1993) was a German–American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared the 1955 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Willis Eugene Lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_Eugene_Lamb "Willis Eugene Lamb") for his accurate determination that the [electron magnetic moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_magnetic_moment "Electron magnetic moment") was greater than its theoretical value, thus leading to reconsideration of and innovations in [quantum electrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_electrodynamics "Quantum electrodynamics").

## Biography

Polykarp Kusch was born on January 26, 1911, in [Blankenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blankenburg_(Harz) "Blankenburg (Harz)"), Germany, the son of John Mathias Kusch, a [Lutheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran "Lutheran")
missionary, and Henrietta van der Haas. In 1912, Kusch and his family emigrated to the United States, where he [naturalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized "Naturalized") as a U.S. citizen in 1922.

After graduating from grade school in the Midwest, Kusch attended Case Institute of Technology (CIT) in Cleveland, Ohio (now [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University "Case Western Reserve University")), where he majored in physics. After graduating from CIT with a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S. "B.S.") in 1931, Kusch entered the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois "University of Illinois"), from which he received his [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.S. "M.S.") in 1933. He continued his education at the same alma mater, studying under [Francis Wheeler Loomis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Wheeler_Loomis "Francis Wheeler Loomis"). For his work in the field of optical molecular spectroscopy, he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in 1936.

In 1937, Kusch moved to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), where he spent much of his career as a professor and served as its [provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provost_(education) "Provost (education)") for one year in 1970–1971. He worked on molecular beam resonance studies under [I. I. Rabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidor_Isaac_Rabi "Isidor Isaac Rabi"), then discovered the electron [anomalous magnetic moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomalous_magnetic_moment "Anomalous magnetic moment"). Many measurements of magnetic moments and hyperfine structure followed. He expanded into [chemical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_physics "Chemical physics") and continued to publish research on [molecular beams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_beams "Molecular beams"). In 1972, he left Columbia to become a professor at the [University of Texas at Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Dallas "University of Texas at Dallas"), remaining there until his retirement in 1982.

## Personal life

In 1935, Kusch married Edith Starr McRoberts, with whom he had three daughters. Edith died in 1959, and the following year he married Betty Pezzoni. They had two daughters. Kusch House, a residential dormitory for undergraduate students at Case Western Reserve University in Cleveland, Ohio on the South Campus, is named after Kusch. It is located on Carlton Road in Cleveland Heights. The University of Texas at Dallas has a Polykarp Kusch Auditorium with a plaque.

Kusch died on March 20, 1993, in [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas "Dallas") at the age of 82.
