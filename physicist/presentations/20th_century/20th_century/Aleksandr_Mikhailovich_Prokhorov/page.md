**Alexander Mikhailovich Prokhorov** (born **Alexander Michael Prochoroff**; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Александр Михайлович Прохоров; 11 July 1916 – 8 January 2002) was an Australian-born Soviet [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and researcher whose work focused on [quantum electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_electronics "Quantum electronics"). His most famous and well-known works were on optics and electromagnetic research. He was jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1964 with [Charles Hard Townes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hard_Townes "Charles Hard Townes") and [Nikolay Basov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Basov "Nikolay Basov") for his fundamental work that led to the development of the [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser "Laser") and the [maser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maser "Maser").

## Early life

Alexander Michael Prochoroff was born on 11 July 1916 in [Atherton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atherton,_Queensland "Atherton, Queensland"), Australia, to Russian parents who had emigrated from Russia to escape repression by the [Tsarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire") regime. As a child, he attended Butchers Creek State School. The family returned to Russia in 1923, after the culmination of the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution "October Revolution") and the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War "Russian Civil War").

In 1934, Prokhorov entered the [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University "Saint Petersburg State University") to study physics. He was a member of the [Komsomol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komsomol "Komsomol") from 1930 to 1944, the youth wing of the [CPSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPSU "CPSU"). Prokhorov graduated with honors in 1939 and moved to Moscow to work at the [Lebedev Physical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebedev_Physical_Institute "Lebedev Physical Institute"), in the oscillations laboratory headed by academician [N. D. Papaleksi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Papaleksi "Nikolai Papaleksi"). His research there was devoted to propagation of radio waves in the [ionosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionosphere "Ionosphere").

At the onset of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), in June 1941, he joined the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army "Red Army"). Prokhorov fought in the infantry, was wounded twice in battles, and was awarded three medals, including the [Medal For Courage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_"For_Courage"_(Russia) "Medal \"For Courage\" (Russia)") in 1946. He was demobilized in 1944, a year before the end of WWII and eventual victory of the Allies.

Prokhorov returned to the Lebedev Institute where, in 1946, he defended his [Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences "Candidate of Sciences") thesis titled *Theory of Stabilization of Frequency of a Tube Oscillator in the Theory of a Small Parameter*.

## Research

In 1947, Prokhorov started working on coherent radiation emitted by electrons orbiting in a cyclic [particle accelerator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator "Particle accelerator") called a [synchrotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchrotron "Synchrotron"). He demonstrated that the emission is mostly concentrated in the [microwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave "Microwave") spectral range. His results became the basis of his [Doctor of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Sciences "Doctor of Sciences") thesis titled *Coherent Radiation of Electrons in the Synchrotron Accelerator*, defended in 1951. By 1950, Prokhorov was assistant chief of the oscillation laboratory. Around that time, he formed a group of young scientists to work on radiospectroscopy of molecular rotations and vibrations, and later on [quantum electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_electronics "Quantum electronics"). The group focused on a special class of molecules which have three (non-degenerate) moments of inertia. The research was conducted both on experiment and theory. In 1954, Prokhorov became head of the laboratory. Together with [Nikolay Basov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Basov "Nikolay Basov") he developed theoretical grounds for creation of a [molecular oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_harmonic_oscillator "Quantum harmonic oscillator") and constructed such a device based on [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia "Ammonia"). They also proposed a method for the production of population inversion using inhomogeneous electric and magnetic fields. Their results were first presented at a national conference in 1952, but not published until 1954–1955.

In 1955, Prokhorov started his research in the field of [electron paramagnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_paramagnetic_resonance "Electron paramagnetic resonance") (EPR). He focused on relaxation times of ions of the [iron group elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_8_element "Group 8 element") in a lattice of [aluminium oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium_oxide "Aluminium oxide"), but also investigated other, "non-optical", topics, such as magnetic phase transitions in [DPPH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPPH "DPPH"). In 1957, while studying [ruby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby "Ruby"), a [chromium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromium "Chromium")-doped variation of aluminium oxide, he came upon the idea of using this material as an active medium of a [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser "Laser"). As a new type of laser resonator, he proposed, in 1958, an "open type" cavity design, which is widely used today. In 1963, together with A. S. Selivanenko, he suggested a laser using two-quantum transitions. For his pioneering work on lasers and masers, in 1964, he received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") shared with [Nikolay Basov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Basov "Nikolay Basov") and [Charles Hard Townes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hard_Townes "Charles Hard Townes").

## Posts and awards

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/Gustaf_VI_Adolf_and_Alexander_Prokhorov_1964.jpg/250px-Gustaf_VI_Adolf_and_Alexander_Prokhorov_1964.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gustaf_VI_Adolf_and_Alexander_Prokhorov_1964.jpg)

Prokhorov with King [Gustaf VI Adolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustaf_VI_Adolf "Gustaf VI Adolf") and wife of Townes at the Nobel Prize banquet in 1964

In 1959, Prokhorov became a professor at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University") – the most prestigious university in the Soviet Union; the same year, he was awarded the [Lenin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin_Prize "Lenin Prize"). In 1960, he became a member of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") and elected Academician in 1966. In 1967, he was awarded his first [Order of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin "Order of Lenin") (he received five of them during life, in 1967, 1969, 1975, 1981 and 1986). In 1968, he became vice-director of the Lebedev Institute and in 1971 took the position of Head of Laboratory of another prestigious Soviet institution, the [Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Institute_of_Physics_and_Technology "Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology"). In the same year, he was elected a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"). In 1983 he was elected a Member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina"). Between 1982 and 1998, Prokhorov served as acting director of the General Physics Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences, and after 1998 as honorary director. After his death in 2002, the institute was renamed the [A. M. Prokhorov General Physics Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._M._Prokhorov_General_Physics_Institute?action=edit&redlink=1 "A. M. Prokhorov General Physics Institute") of the Russian Academy of Sciences. Prokhorov was a Member and one of the Honorary Presidents of the International Academy of Science, Munich and supported 1993 the foundation and development of the Russian Section of International Academy of Science, Moscow.

In 1969, Prokhorov became a [Hero of Socialist Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_Socialist_Labour "Hero of Socialist Labour"), the highest degree of distinction in the Soviet Union for achievements in national economy and culture. He received the second such award in 1986. Starting in 1969, he was the chief editor of the [Great Soviet Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Soviet_Encyclopedia "Great Soviet Encyclopedia"). He was awarded the [Frederic Ives Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_Ives_Medal "Frederic Ives Medal"), the highest distinction of the [Optical Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_Society_of_America "Optical Society of America") (OSA), in 2000 and became an Honorary OSA Member in 2001. The same year, he was awarded the [Demidov Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demidov_Prize "Demidov Prize") by the government of the Russian Federation.

He died on 8 January 2002 at [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow") and was buried at [Novodevichy Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novodevichy_Cemetery "Novodevichy Cemetery") in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow").

## Politics

Prokhorov became a member of the [Communist Party of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union "Communist Party of the Soviet Union") in 1950. In 1983, together with three other academicians – [Andrey Tychonoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Nikolayevich_Tychonoff "Andrey Nikolayevich Tychonoff"), [Anatoly Dorodnitsyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Dorodnitsyn "Anatoly Dorodnitsyn") and Georgy Skryabin – he signed the famous open letter called "when they lose honor and conscience" (Когда теряют честь и совесть), denouncing [Andrey Sakharov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Sakharov "Andrey Sakharov")'s article in the *[Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs "Foreign Affairs")*.

## Family

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Nicolay_Basov_and_Aleksandr_Prokhorov_with_wives_1964.jpg/250px-Nicolay_Basov_and_Aleksandr_Prokhorov_with_wives_1964.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nicolay_Basov_and_Aleksandr_Prokhorov_with_wives_1964.jpg)

Basov and Prokhorov with their wives in Stockholm in 1964

Both of Prokhorov's parents died during World War II. Prokhorov married [geographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographer "Geographer") Galina Shelepina in 1941, and they had a son, Kiril, born in 1945. Following his father, Kiril Prokhorov became a physicist in the field of optics and is currently leading a laser-related laboratory at the A. M. Prokhorov General Physics Institute.
