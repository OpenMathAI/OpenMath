**Arthur Leonard Schawlow** (May 5, 1921 – April 28, 1999) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who, along with [Charles Townes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Townes "Charles Townes"), developed the theoretical basis for [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser "Laser") science. His central insight was the use of two mirrors as the resonant cavity to take [maser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maser "Maser") action from microwaves to visible wavelengths. He shared the 1981 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Nicolaas Bloembergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaas_Bloembergen "Nicolaas Bloembergen") and [Kai Siegbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Siegbahn "Kai Siegbahn") for his work using lasers to determine [atomic energy levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_energy_level "Atomic energy level") with great precision.

## Biography

Arthur Leonard Schawlow was born on May 5, 1921, in [Mount Vernon, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon,_New_York "Mount Vernon, New York"), to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") father from [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga "Riga") and a Canadian mother. Schawlow was raised in his mother's Protestant faith. When Arthur was three years old, they moved to [Toronto, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto "Toronto"), Canada.

At the age of 16, Schawlow completed high school at [Vaughan Road Collegiate Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaughan_Road_Collegiate_Institute "Vaughan Road Collegiate Institute"), and received a scholarship in science at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto "University of Toronto") ([Victoria College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University,_Toronto "Victoria University, Toronto")). He obtained his [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.A. "B.A.") in 1941 and his [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.A. "M.A.") the following year. He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in 1949 under Professor Malcolm F. Crawford. Schawlow then took a [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher "Postdoctoral researcher") position with [Charles Townes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Townes "Charles Townes") in the Physics Department of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in the fall of 1949.

Schawlow went on to accept a position at [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Telephone_Laboratories "Bell Telephone Laboratories") in late 1951. He left in 1961 to become Professor of Physics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"), where he remained until his retirement in 1991.

Although his research focused on [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics "Optics"), in particular lasers and their use in [spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy "Spectroscopy"), Schawlow also pursued investigations in the areas of [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity") and [nuclear resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance"). Schawlow shared the 1981 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Nicolaas Bloembergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaas_Bloembergen "Nicolaas Bloembergen") and [Kai Siegbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Siegbahn "Kai Siegbahn") for their contributions to the development of laser spectroscopy.

Schawlow co-authored the widely used text *Microwave Spectroscopy* (1955) with Charles Townes. Schawlow and Townes were the first to publish the theory of laser design and operation in their seminal 1958 paper on "optical masers", although [Gordon Gould](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Gould "Gordon Gould") is often credited with the "invention" of the laser, due to his unpublished work that predated Schawlow and Townes by a few months. The first working laser was made in 1960 by [Theodore Maiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Harold_Maiman "Theodore Harold Maiman").

## Science and religion

Schawlow participated in [science and religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_religion "Science and religion") discussions. Regarding [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God "God"), he stated, "I find a need for God in the universe and in my own life."

## Personal life

In 1951, Schawlow married Aurelia Townes, the younger sister of Charles Townes. They had three children: Arthur Jr., Helen, and Edith. Arthur Jr. is [autistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autism "Autism"), with very little speech ability. Aurelia died in 1991 following a vehicle accident.

Schawlow and Professor [Robert Hofstadter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hofstadter "Robert Hofstadter") at Stanford, who also had an autistic child, teamed up to help each other find solutions to the condition. Arthur Jr. was put in a special center for autistic individuals, and later, Schawlow put together an institution to care for people with autism in [Paradise, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise,_California "Paradise, California"). It was later named the Arthur Schawlow Center in 1999, shortly before his death. Schawlow was a promoter of the controversial method of [facilitated communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facilitated_communication "Facilitated communication") with patients of autism.

Schawlow considered himself to be an orthodox Protestant Christian, and attended a Methodist church.
Arthur Schawlow was an intense fan and collector of traditional American [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") recordings, as well as a supporter of instrumental groups performing this type of music.

Schawlow died of [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia") on April 28, 1999, in [Palo Alto, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto,_California "Palo Alto, California"), at the age of 77.

## Commemoration

In 1991, the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society") established the [Arthur L. Schawlow Prize in Laser Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_L._Schawlow_Prize_in_Laser_Science "Arthur L. Schawlow Prize in Laser Science"). The Prize is awarded annually to "candidates who have made outstanding contributions to basic research using lasers."
