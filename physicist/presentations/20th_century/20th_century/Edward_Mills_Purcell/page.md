**Edward Mills Purcell** (August 30, 1912 – March 7, 1997) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared the 1952 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Felix Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Bloch "Felix Bloch") for his independent discovery (published 1946) of [nuclear magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance") (NMR) in liquids and in solids. NMR has become widely used to study the [molecular structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule") of pure materials and the composition of mixtures. Friends and colleagues knew him as **Ed Purcell**.

## Biography

Edward Mills Purcell was born on August 30, 1912, in [Taylorville, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylorville,_Illinois "Taylorville, Illinois"), the son of Edward A. Purcell and Mary Elizabeth Mills. Purcell received his [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S. "B.S.") in Electrical Engineering from [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University "Purdue University") in 1933, followed by his [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.A. "M.A.") (1935) and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") (1938) in Physics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). He was a member of the Alpha Xi chapter of the [Phi Kappa Sigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Kappa_Sigma "Phi Kappa Sigma") fraternity while at Purdue.

After spending the years of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") working at the [MIT Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Laboratory "MIT Radiation Laboratory") on the development of microwave radar, Purcell returned to Harvard to do research. In December 1945, he discovered [nuclear magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance") (NMR) with his colleagues [Robert Pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pound "Robert Pound") and Henry Torrey. NMR provides scientists with an elegant and precise way of determining chemical structure and properties of materials, and is widely used in physics and chemistry. It also is the basis of [magnetic resonance imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging "Magnetic resonance imaging") (MRI), one of the most important medical advances of the 20th century. For his discovery of NMR, Purcell shared the 1952 Nobel Prize in physics with [Felix Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Bloch "Felix Bloch") of [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University").

Purcell also made contributions to [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy") as the first to detect radio emissions from neutral galactic hydrogen (the famous [21 cm line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_line "Hydrogen line") due to [hyperfine splitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperfine_splitting "Hyperfine splitting")), affording the first views of the spiral arms of the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way "Milky Way"). This observation helped launch the field of [radio astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_astronomy "Radio astronomy"), and measurements of the 21 cm line are still an important technique in modern astronomy. He has also made seminal contributions to [solid state physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_physics "Solid-state physics"), with studies of spin-echo relaxation, nuclear magnetic relaxation, and negative spin temperature (important in the development of the laser). With [Norman F. Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_F._Ramsey "Norman F. Ramsey"), he was the first to question the [CP symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP-violation "CP-violation") of particle physics.

Purcell was the recipient of many awards for his scientific, educational, and civic work. He served as science advisor to Presidents [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower "Dwight D. Eisenhower"), [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy"), and [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson"). He was president of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society"), and a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"), the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences"), and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"). He was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in 1979, and the [Jansky Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Radio_Astronomy_Observatory#Jansky_Prize "National Radio Astronomy Observatory") before the [National Radio Astronomy Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Radio_Astronomy_Observatory "National Radio Astronomy Observatory"). Purcell was also inducted into his Fraternity's ([Phi Kappa Sigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Kappa_Sigma "Phi Kappa Sigma")) Hall of Fame as the first Phi Kap ever to receive a Nobel Prize.

Purcell was the author of the innovative introductory text *[Electricity and Magnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_and_Magnetism_(book) "Electricity and Magnetism (book)")*. The book, a [Sputnik-era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_race "Space race") project funded by an NSF grant, was influential for its [use of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativistic_electromagnetism "Relativistic electromagnetism") in the presentation of the subject at this level. The 1965 edition, now freely available due to a condition of the federal grant, was originally published as a volume of the *[Berkeley Physics Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Physics_Course "Berkeley Physics Course")*. The book is also in print as a commercial third edition, as Purcell and Morin. Purcell is also remembered by biologists for his famous lecture "Life at Low [Reynolds Number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reynolds_number "Reynolds number")", in which he explained forces and effects dominating in limiting flow regimes (often at the micro scale). He also emphasized the time-reversibility of low Reynolds number flows with a principle referred to as the [Scallop theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scallop_theorem "Scallop theorem").

Purcell died on March 7, 1997, in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts"), aged 84.
