**Nikolay Gennadiyevich Basov** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Николай Геннадиевич Басов; 14 December 1922 – 1 July 2001) was a Soviet [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and educator. For his fundamental work in the field of [quantum electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_electronics "Quantum electronics") that led to the development of [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser "Laser") and [maser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maser "Maser"), Basov shared the 1964 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Alexander Prokhorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Prokhorov "Alexander Prokhorov") and [Charles Hard Townes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hard_Townes "Charles Hard Townes").

## Early life and education

Nikolay Gennadiyevich Basov was born on 14 December 1922 in [Usman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usman_(town) "Usman (town)"), Russia, the son of Gennady Fedorovich Basov and Zinaida Andreevna Molchanova. Basov finished high school in 1941 in [Voronezh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh "Voronezh"), and was later called for military service at the [Kuibyshev Military Medical Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuibyshev_Military_Medical_Academy "Kuibyshev Military Medical Academy"). In 1943, he left the academy and served in the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army "Red Army") participating in the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War") with the [1st Ukrainian Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Ukrainian_Front "1st Ukrainian Front").

In 1945, Basov entered the [Moscow Engineering Physics Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Engineering_Physics_Institute "Moscow Engineering Physics Institute") (MEPhI). In 1950, he began his postgraduate studies at the MEPhI, although he worked on his [Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences "Candidate of Sciences") thesis at the [Lebedev Physical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebedev_Physical_Institute "Lebedev Physical Institute") (LPI)—under the supervision of [Mikhail Leontovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Leontovich "Mikhail Leontovich") and [Alexander Prokhorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Prokhorov "Alexander Prokhorov")—receiving his degree in 1953. Basov defended his [Doctor of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Sciences "Doctor of Sciences") thesis, *A Molecular Oscillator*, in 1956.

In 1950, Basov married Ksenia Tikhonovna, who was also a physicist. They had two sons, Gennady (born 1954) and Dmitry (born 1963).

## Career and research

Basov was Director of the LPI from 1973 to 1988. He was elected a [corresponding member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corresponding_member "Corresponding member") of the [USSR Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Academy_of_Sciences "USSR Academy of Sciences") ([Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") since 1991) in 1962 and [Full Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academician "Academician") of the Academy in 1966. In 1967, he was elected a Member of the Presidium of the Academy (1967—1990), and since 1990 he was the councillor of the Presidium of the USSR Academy of Sciences. In 1971 he was elected a Member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina"). He was Honorary President and Member of the International Academy of Science, Munich. He was the head of the laboratory of quantum radiophysics at the LPI until his death in 2001.

In the early 1950s Basov and Prokhorov developed theoretical grounds for creation of a molecular oscillator and constructed such an oscillator based on [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia "Ammonia"). Later this oscillator became known as [maser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maser "Maser"). They also proposed a method for the production of [population inversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_inversion "Population inversion") using inhomogeneous electric and magnetic fields. Their results were presented at a national conference in 1952 and published in 1954. Basov then proceeded to the development of laser, an analogous generator of coherent light. In 1955 he designed a [three-level laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-level_laser "Three-level laser"), and in 1959 suggested constructing a [semiconductor laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_laser "Semiconductor laser"), which he built with collaborators in 1963. Basov with co-workers proposed [Disk laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disk_laser "Disk laser") in 1966 and realized experimentally the thin disk [active mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_mirror "Active mirror") semiconductor lasers.
He developed with colleaguaes the first nonlinear theory of [coherent addition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_addition "Coherent addition") of laser sets.
N.G.Basov encouraged the researchers in [nonlinear optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_optics "Nonlinear optics") in [Lebedev Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebedev_Institute "Lebedev Institute") who discovered the [optical phase conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_phase_conjugation "Optical phase conjugation").
Together with [Lebedev Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebedev_Institute "Lebedev Institute") researchers he realized the robust method of the phase-locking of laser arrays via [optical phase conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_phase_conjugation "Optical phase conjugation") in [Stimulated Brillouin scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulated_Brillouin_scattering "Stimulated Brillouin scattering").

Basov's contributions to the development of the laser and maser, which won him the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in 1964, also led to new missile defense initiatives.

Basov died on 1 July 2001 in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow") at the age of 78, and was buried in [Novodevichy Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novodevichy_Cemetery "Novodevichy Cemetery").

## Politics

He entered politics in 1951 and became a member of parliament (the [Soviet of the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_of_the_Union "Soviet of the Union") of the [Supreme Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Soviet_of_the_Soviet_Union "Supreme Soviet of the Soviet Union")) in 1974. Following [U.S. President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._President "U.S. President") [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan")'s speech on [SDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defense_Initiative "Strategic Defense Initiative") in 1983, Basov signed a letter along with other Soviet scientists condemning the initiative, which was published in the New York Times. In 1985 he declared the Soviet Union was capable of matching SDI proposals made by the U.S.
