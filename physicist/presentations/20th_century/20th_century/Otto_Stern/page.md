**Otto Stern** (German: [[ˈɔto ˈʃtɛʁn]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Otto_Stern.ogg "File:De-Otto Stern.ogg"); 17 February 1888 – 17 August 1969) was a German–American experimental [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). He is the second most nominated person for a [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize"), with 82 nominations during the years 1925–1945. In 1943, he received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") "for his contribution to the development of the [molecular ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_beam "Molecular beam") method and his discovery of the [magnetic moment of the proton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_magnetic_moment "Proton magnetic moment")."

## Biography

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Stern-plakette.jpg/250px-Stern-plakette.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Stern-plakette.jpg)

Plaque on the wall of what are now the physics institutes of Hamburg University, commemorating Stern's tenure.

Otto Stern was born on 17 February 1888 in Sohrau (now [Żory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Żory "Żory"), Poland), Germany, into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family. In 1892, he moved with his parents to Breslau (now [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław "Wrocław"), Poland). He studied in [Freiburg im Breisgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg_im_Breisgau "Freiburg im Breisgau"), [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich"), and Breslau.

In 1912, Stern received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in Physical Chemistry from the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau "University of Breslau"), with a thesis on the kinetic theory of osmotic pressure in concentrated solutions. He then followed [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") to [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University "Charles University") in Prague, and in 1913 to [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich"), where he became a *[Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent "Privatdozent")* in Physical Chemistry. The following year, he moved to the [University of Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Frankfurt_am_Main "University of Frankfurt am Main") as a *Privatdozent* in Theoretical Physics. In 1921, he was appointed Associate Professor of Theoretical Physics at the [University of Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rostock "University of Rostock"), and then Professor of Physical Chemistry and director of the laboratory at the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg "University of Hamburg") in 1923.

In 1930, Stern received an [LL.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LL.D. "LL.D.") from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") (UC Berkeley), where he was a frequent visiting professor during the 1930s, becoming close colleagues with members of the Berkeley faculty, including chemistry dean [Gilbert Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_N._Lewis "Gilbert N. Lewis"), whom Stern would nominate for the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in Chemistry in 1933. After resigning from his post at the University of Hamburg in 1933 because of the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism")' *[Machtergreifung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung "Machtergreifung")* (seizure of power), he found refuge in the city of [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh "Pittsburgh"), becoming Professor of Physics at the [Carnegie Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Institute_of_Technology "Carnegie Institute of Technology").

As an experimental physicist, Stern contributed to the discovery of [spin quantization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(physics) "Spin (physics)") in the [Stern–Gerlach experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stern–Gerlach_experiment "Stern–Gerlach experiment") with [Walther Gerlach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Gerlach "Walther Gerlach") in February 1922 at the [Physikalischer Verein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physikalischer_Verein?action=edit&redlink=1 "Physikalischer Verein") in [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main "Frankfurt am Main"). With his life-long collaborator [Immanuel Estermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Estermann "Immanuel Estermann"), he demonstrated of the wave nature of [atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom "Atom") and [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule"); measurement of [atomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom "Atom") [magnetic moments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_moment "Magnetic moment"); discovery of the [proton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton")'s [magnetic moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_moment "Magnetic moment"); and development of the [molecular beam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_beam "Molecular beam") method which is utilized for the technique of [molecular beam epitaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_beam_epitaxy "Molecular beam epitaxy").

After Stern retired from the Carnegie Institute of Technology in 1945, he moved to [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California "Berkeley, California"). He was a regular visitor to the physics colloquium at UC Berkeley. He died of a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack "Heart attack") on 17 August 1969 in Berkeley, at the age of 81.

His niece was the crystallographer [Lieselotte Templeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieselotte_Templeton "Lieselotte Templeton").
