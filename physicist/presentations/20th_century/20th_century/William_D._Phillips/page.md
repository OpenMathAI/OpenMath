**William Daniel Phillips** (born November 5, 1948) is an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). He shared the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1997 with [Steven Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Chu "Steven Chu") and [Claude Cohen-Tannoudji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Cohen-Tannoudji "Claude Cohen-Tannoudji").

## Biography

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Nobel_Prize_laureate_Bill_Phillips_greets_his_college_physics_professor%2C_Dr._Wilfred_Norris%2C_at_Juniata_College.jpg/250px-Nobel_Prize_laureate_Bill_Phillips_greets_his_college_physics_professor%2C_Dr._Wilfred_Norris%2C_at_Juniata_College.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nobel_Prize_laureate_Bill_Phillips_greets_his_college_physics_professor,_Dr._Wilfred_Norris,_at_Juniata_College.jpg)

Bill Phillips greets his college physics professor, Dr. Wilfred Norris, in 2025—55 years after graduation.

Phillips was born on November 5, 1948, in [Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkes-Barre,_Pennsylvania "Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania"), to William Cornelius Phillips of [Juniata, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniata,_Pennsylvania?action=edit&redlink=1 "Juniata, Pennsylvania"), and Mary Catherine Savino of [Ripacandida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripacandida "Ripacandida"), Italy. He is of Italian and Albanian descent on his mother's side and of [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_people "Welsh people") descent on his father's side. His parents moved to [Camp Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Hill,_Pennsylvania "Camp Hill, Pennsylvania") (near [Harrisburg, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg,_Pennsylvania "Harrisburg, Pennsylvania")) in 1959, where he attended [high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Hill_High_School "Camp Hill High School") and graduated valedictorian of his class in 1966. He graduated from [Juniata College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniata_College "Juniata College") in 1970 *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude "Summa cum laude")*. After that he received his physics doctorate from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"). In 1978, he joined [National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIST "NIST") (currently NIST).

In 1996, he received the [Albert A. Michelson Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_A._Michelson_Medal "Albert A. Michelson Medal") from the [Franklin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Institute "Franklin Institute").

Phillips's doctoral thesis concerned the magnetic moment of the proton in H2O. He later did some work with [Bose–Einstein condensates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose–Einstein_condensate "Bose–Einstein condensate"). In 1997 he won the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") together with [Claude Cohen-Tannoudji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Cohen-Tannoudji "Claude Cohen-Tannoudji") and [Steven Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Chu "Steven Chu") for his contributions to [laser cooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_cooling "Laser cooling"), a technique to slow the movement of gaseous atoms in order to better study them, at the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology"), and especially for his invention of the [Zeeman slower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeeman_slower "Zeeman slower").

Phillips is also a professor of physics, which is part of the [University of Maryland College of Computer, Mathematical, and Natural Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland_College_of_Computer,_Mathematical,_and_Natural_Sciences "University of Maryland College of Computer, Mathematical, and Natural Sciences") at [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park "University of Maryland, College Park").

Phillips is one of the 20 American recipients of the Nobel Prize in Physics to sign a letter addressed to President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") in May 2008, urging him to "reverse the damage done to basic science research in the Fiscal Year 2008 Omnibus Appropriations Bill" by requesting additional emergency funding for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")'s [Office of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Science "Office of Science"), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), and the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology").

He was one of the 35 Nobel laureates who signed a letter urging President [Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama "Obama") to provide a stable $15 billion per year support for clean energy research, technology and demonstration.

He is one of three well-known scientists and [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist "Methodist") laity who have involved themselves in the [religion and science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_and_science "Religion and science") dialogue. The other two scientists and fellow Methodists are chemist [Charles Coulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Coulson "Charles Coulson") and 1981 Nobel laureate [Arthur Leonard Schawlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Leonard_Schawlow "Arthur Leonard Schawlow").

In October 2010, Phillips participated in the [USA Science and Engineering Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Science_and_Engineering_Festival "USA Science and Engineering Festival")'s lunch with a laureate program where middle and high school students got to engage in an informal conversation with a Nobel Prize-winning scientist over a brown-bag lunch. Phillips is also a member of the USA Science and Engineering Festival's advisory board.

Phillips has served as a member, and later as an associated member, on the Commission on Symbols, Units, Nomenclature, Atomic Masses and Fundamental Constants (C2) of the [International Union of Pure and Applied Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_of_Pure_and_Applied_Physics "International Union of Pure and Applied Physics") from 2011 to 2024.

## Awards and honors

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/The_Phillips_Lobby_at_Juniata_College_in_Huntingdon%2C_Pennsylvania%2C_honors_physicist_Bill_Phillips.jpg/250px-The_Phillips_Lobby_at_Juniata_College_in_Huntingdon%2C_Pennsylvania%2C_honors_physicist_Bill_Phillips.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Phillips_Lobby_at_Juniata_College_in_Huntingdon,_Pennsylvania,_honors_physicist_Bill_Phillips.jpg)

Phillips has been awarded numerous honors.

- Outstanding Young Scientist Award of the Maryland Academy of Sciences, 1982
- Silver Medal of the Department of Commerce, 1983
- [Samuel Wesley Stratton Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Wesley_Stratton_Award "Samuel Wesley Stratton Award") of the National Bureau of Standards, 1987
- [Arthur S. Flemming Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_S._Flemming_Award "Arthur S. Flemming Award") of the Washington Downtown Jaycees, 1988
- Gold Medal of the Dept. of Commerce, 1993
- Elected Fellow of The Optical Society, 1994
- Election to American Academy of Arts and Sciences 1995
- Election as a NIST Fellow, 1995
- Michelson Medal of the Franklin Institute 1996
- Election to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") 1997
- [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") 1997
- [Arthur L. Schawlow Prize in Laser Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_L._Schawlow_Prize_in_Laser_Science "Arthur L. Schawlow Prize in Laser Science") (APS) 1998
- Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Achievement "American Academy of Achievement") 1999
- Richtmeyer Award of the Am. Assoc. of Physics Teachers 2000
- Election to the European Academy of Arts, Sciences and Humanities (titular member), 2000
- Appointed an Academician of the Pontifical Academy of Sciences 2004
- Elected Honorary Member of [The Optical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Optical_Society "The Optical Society") in 2004
- [Presidential Rank Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Rank_Award "Presidential Rank Award"), 2005
- Service to America Award – Career Achievement 2006
- Honorary Liveryman of the [Worshipful Company of Scientific Instrument Makers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worshipful_Company_of_Scientific_Instrument_Makers "Worshipful Company of Scientific Instrument Makers")
