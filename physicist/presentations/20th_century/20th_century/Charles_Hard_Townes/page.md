**Charles Hard Townes** (July 28, 1915 – January 27, 2015) was an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). Townes worked on the theory and application of the [maser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maser "Maser"), for which he obtained the fundamental patent, and other work in [quantum electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_optics#Quantum_electronics "Quantum optics") associated with both maser and [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser "Laser") devices. He shared the 1964 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Nikolay Basov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Basov "Nikolay Basov") and [Alexander Prokhorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Prokhorov "Alexander Prokhorov"). Townes was an adviser to the U.S. Government, meeting every President from [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman") (1945) to [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") (1999).

Townes directed the U.S. government's Science and Technology Advisory Committee for the [Apollo lunar landing program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program "Apollo program"). After becoming a professor of the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), in 1967, he began an astrophysical program that produced several important discoveries, for example, the [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole "Black hole") at [the center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Center "Galactic Center") of the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way "Milky Way") galaxy.

Townes was religious, and believed that [science and religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_religion "Science and religion") are converging to provide a greater understanding of the nature and purpose of the universe.

## Early life and education

Charles Hard Townes was born on July 28, 1915, in [Greenville, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenville,_South_Carolina "Greenville, South Carolina"), the son of Henry Keith Townes Sr. (1876–1958), an attorney, and Ellen Sumter Hard (1881–1980). His brother, [Henry Keith Townes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Keith_Townes "Henry Keith Townes") Jr., (1913–1990), was a renowned entomologist who was a world authority on [Ichneumon wasps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichneumon_wasp "Ichneumon wasp"). Townes had German, Scottish, English, Welsh, Huguenot French, and Scotch Irish ancestry,

Charles earned a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S. "B.S.") in Physics and a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.A. "B.A.") in Modern Languages from [Furman University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furman_University "Furman University"), where he graduated in 1935. Townes completed work for an [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.A. "M.A.") in Physics at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University") in 1937, and then began graduate school at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology"), from which he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in 1939 with a thesis on [isotope separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope_separation "Isotope separation") and [nuclear spins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_spin "Nuclear spin"). During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), he worked on radar bombing systems at [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Telephone_Laboratories "Bell Telephone Laboratories").

## Career and research

In 1950, Townes was appointed professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"). He served as executive director of the Columbia Radiation Laboratory from 1950 to 1952. He was Chairman of the Physics Department from 1952 to 1955.

In 1951, Townes conceived a new way to create intense, precise beams of [coherent radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_radiation "Coherent radiation"), for which he invented the acronym *[maser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maser "Maser")* (for **M**icrowave **A**mplification by **S**timulated **E**mission of **R**adiation). When the same principle was applied to higher frequencies, the term *laser* was used (the word "light" substituting for the word "microwave").

During 1953, Townes, [James P. Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_P._Gordon "James P. Gordon"), and [Herbert J. Zeiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_J._Zeiger "Herbert J. Zeiger") built the first ammonia maser at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"). This device used stimulated emission in a stream of energized [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia "Ammonia") molecules to produce amplification of microwaves at a frequency of about 24.0 [gigahertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz#SI_multiples "Hertz").

From 1959 to 1961, Townes was on leave of absence from Columbia University to serve as vice president and director of research of the [Institute for Defense Analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses "Institute for Defense Analyses") in Washington, D.C., a nonprofit organization, which advised the U.S. government and was operated by eleven universities. Between 1961 and 1967, he served as both Provost and Professor of Physics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"). Then, during 1967, he was appointed Professor of Physics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), where he remained for almost 50 years; his status was as professor emeritus by the time of his death during 2015. Between 1966 and 1970, he was chairman of the [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") Science Advisory Committee for the [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program "Apollo program") lunar landing program.

For his creation of the maser, Townes along with [Nikolay Basov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Basov "Nikolay Basov") and [Alexander Prokhorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Prokhorov "Alexander Prokhorov") received the 1964 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"). Townes also developed the use of masers and lasers for [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy"), was part of a team that first discovered complex molecules in space, and determined the mass of the [supermassive black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermassive_black_hole "Supermassive black hole") at the center of the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way "Milky Way") galaxy.

During 2002–2003, Townes served as a Karl Schwarzschild Lecturer in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") and the Birla Lecturer and Schroedinger Lecturer in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India").

Townes is one of the 20 American recipients of the Nobel Prize in Physics to sign a letter addressed to President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") in May 2008, urging him to "reverse the damage done to basic science research in the Fiscal Year 2008 Omnibus Appropriations Bill" by requesting additional emergency funding for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")'s [Office of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Science "Office of Science"), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), and the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology").

## Astrophysics

### Galactic Center

The [Galactic Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Center "Galactic Center") of the Milky Way had long puzzled astronomers, and thick dust obscures the view of it in visible light. During the mid to late 1970s, Townes together with Eric Wollman, John Lacy, Thomas Geballe and Fred Baas studied [Sagittarius A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagittarius_A "Sagittarius A"), the [H II region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H_II_region "H II region") at the Galactic Center, at infrared wavelengths. They observed ionized [neon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon "Neon") gas swirling around the center at such velocities that the mass at the very center must be approximately equal to that of 3 million suns. Such a large mass in such a small space implied that the central object (the radio source [Sagittarius A\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagittarius_A* "Sagittarius A*")) contains a supermassive [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole "Black hole"). Sagittarius A\* was one of the first black holes detected; subsequently its mass has been more accurately determined to be 4.3 million solar masses.

### Shapes and sizes of stars

His last major technological creation was the [Infrared Spatial Interferometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_Spatial_Interferometer "Infrared Spatial Interferometer") with Walt Fitelson, Ed Wishnow and others. The project combined three mobile infrared detectors aligned by lasers that study the same star. If each telescope is 10 meters from the other, it creates an impression of a 30-meter lens. Observations of [Betelgeuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betelgeuse "Betelgeuse"), a red giant in the shoulder of the constellation [Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_(constellation) "Orion (constellation)"), found that it is increasing and decreasing in size at the rate of 1% per year, 15% over 15 years. ISI produces extremely high [angular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_resolution "Angular resolution") and [spatial resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_resolution "Spatial resolution"). The technology is also playing an important role in the search for extraterrestrial life in collaborations with [Dan Werthimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Werthimer "Dan Werthimer") of [Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_for_Extraterrestrial_Intelligence "Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence") (SETI).

## Personal life and legacy

Townes married Frances H. Brown, an activist for the homeless, during 1941. They lived in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California "Berkeley, California") and had four daughters: Linda Rosenwein, Ellen Anderson, Carla Kessler, and Holly Townes. Frances died in 2018 aged 101.

Several places are named after Townes. For example, in his hometown of Greenville, the [Charles Townes Center for the Gifted and Talented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Townes_Center_Center_for_the_Gifted_and_Talented?action=edit&redlink=1 "Charles Townes Center Center for the Gifted and Talented") was named after him, as well as the Charles H. Townes Center at [Furman University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furman_University "Furman University").

### Science and religion

A religious man and a member of the [United Church of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Christ "United Church of Christ"), Townes believed that "science and religion [are] quite parallel, much more similar than most people think and that in the long run, they must converge". He wrote in a statement after winning the [Templeton Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Templeton_Prize "Templeton Prize") during 2005: "Science tries to understand what our universe is like and how it works, including us humans. Religion is aimed at understanding the purpose and meaning of our universe, including our own lives. If the universe has a purpose or meaning, this must be reflected in its structure and functioning, and hence in science."

Townes's opinions concerning science and religion were expounded in his essays "The Convergence of Science and Religion", "Logic and Uncertainties in Science and Religion", and his book *Making Waves*. Townes felt that the beauty of nature is "obviously God-made" and that God created the universe for humans to emerge and flourish. He prayed every day and ultimately felt that religion is more important than science because it addresses the most important long-range question: the meaning and purpose of our lives. Townes's belief in the convergence of science and religion is based on claimed similarities:

1. Faith. Townes argued that the scientist has faith much like a religious person does, allowing him/her to work for years for an uncertain result.
2. Revelation. Townes claimed that many important scientific discoveries, like his invention of the maser/laser, occurred as a "flash" much more akin to religious revelation than interpreting data.
3. Proof. During this century the mathematician [Kurt Gödel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Gödel "Kurt Gödel") discovered that there can be no absolute proof in a scientific sense. Every proof requires a set of assumptions, and there is no way to check whether those assumptions are self-consistent because other assumptions would be required.
4. Uncertainty. Townes believed that we should be open-minded to a better understanding of science and religion in the future. This will require us to modify our theories, but not abandon them. For example, at the start of the 20th century physics was largely deterministic. But when scientists began studying the quantum mechanics, they realized that indeterminism and chance play a role in our universe. Both classical physics and quantum mechanics are correct and work well within their own bailiwick, and continue to be taught to students. Similarly, Townes believes that growth of religious understanding will modify, but not make us abandon, our classic religious beliefs.

### Death

Townes had steadily been active at the UCB campus, visiting and working regularly in the physics department or at the Space Sciences Laboratory past his 99th birthday and only a few months before his death. Townes' health began to decline, and he died on route to the hospital in [Oakland, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California "Oakland, California"), on January 27, 2015, at the age of 99. [Reinhard Genzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Genzel "Reinhard Genzel"), a professor of physics at Berkeley, said of Townes: "He was one of the most important experimental physicists of the last century. ... His strength was his curiosity and his unshakable optimism, based on his deep Christian spirituality."
