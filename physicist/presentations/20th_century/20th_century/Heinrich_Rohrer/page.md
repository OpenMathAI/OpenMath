**Heinrich Rohrer** (6 June 1933 – 16 May 2013) was a Swiss [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared half of the 1986 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Gerd Binnig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerd_Binnig "Gerd Binnig") for the design of the [scanning tunneling microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scanning_tunneling_microscope "Scanning tunneling microscope") (STM). The other half of the Prize was awarded to [Ernst Ruska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Ruska "Ernst Ruska"). The [Heinrich Rohrer Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Rohrer_Medal "Heinrich Rohrer Medal") is presented triennially by the Surface Science Society of Japan with [IBM Research – Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Research_–_Zurich "IBM Research – Zurich"), Swiss Embassy in Japan, and Ms. Rohrer in his memory. The medal is not to be confused with the Heinrich Rohrer Award presented at the Nano Seoul 2020 conference.

## Biography

Rohrer was born in [Buchs, St. Gallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchs,_St._Gallen "Buchs, St. Gallen") half an hour after his twin sister. He enjoyed a carefree country childhood until the family moved to [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich "Zürich") in 1949. He enrolled in the [Swiss Federal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich") (ETH) in 1951, where he was student of [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli") and [Paul Scherrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Scherrer "Paul Scherrer"). His PhD thesis was supervised by P. Grassmann who worked on [cryogenic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryogenic_engineering "Cryogenic engineering"). Rohrer measured the length changes of superconductors at the magnetic-field-induced superconducting transition, a project begun by Jørgen Lykke Olsen. In the course of his research, he found that he had to do most of his research at night after the city was asleep because his measurements were so sensitive to vibration.

His studies were interrupted by his military service in the Swiss mountain infantry. In 1961, he married Rose-Marie Egger. Their honeymoon trip to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") included a stint doing research on thermal conductivity of type-II superconductors and metals with Bernie Serin at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University "Rutgers University") in [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey "New Jersey").

In 1963, he joined the [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM "IBM") Research Laboratory in [Rüschlikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rüschlikon "Rüschlikon") under the direction of [Ambros Speiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambros_Speiser "Ambros Speiser"). The first couple of years at IBM, he studied Kondo systems with magnetoresistance in pulsed magnetic fields. He then began studying magnetic phase diagrams, which eventually brought him into the field of critical phenomena.

In 1974, he spent a [sabbatical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbatical "Sabbatical") year at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara "University of California, Santa Barbara") studying [nuclear magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance "Nuclear magnetic resonance") with Vince Jaccarino and Alan King.

Until 1982 he worked on the scanning tunneling microscope. He was appointed [IBM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Fellow "IBM Fellow") in 1986, and led the physics department of the research lab from 1986 until 1988. Rohrer was elected as an honourable member of the [Swiss Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Physical_Society "Swiss Physical Society") in 1990 and an honorary academician of [Academia Sinica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Sinica "Academia Sinica") in 2008.

### Death

Rohrer died of natural causes on 16 May 2013 at his home in [Wollerau, Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollerau,_Switzerland "Wollerau, Switzerland"), aged 79.
