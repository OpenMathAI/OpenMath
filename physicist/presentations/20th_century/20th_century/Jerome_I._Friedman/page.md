**Jerome Isaac Friedman** (born March 28, 1930) is an American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"). He is institute professor and professor of physics, emeritus, at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"). He won the 1990 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") along with [Henry Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_W._Kendall "Henry W. Kendall") and [Richard Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_E._Taylor "Richard E. Taylor"), "for their pioneering investigations concerning [deep inelastic scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_inelastic_scattering "Deep inelastic scattering") of [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") on [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") and bound [neutrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron "Neutron"), which have been of essential importance for the development of the [quark model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark_model "Quark model") in particle physics.", work which showed an internal structure for [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protons "Protons") later known to be [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarks "Quarks"). Friedman sits on the board of sponsors of the *[Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_the_Atomic_Scientists "Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists")*.

## Life and career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/OIST_BOG_Members_After_Meeting_Prime_Minister_Shinzo_Abe_4_October_2013_20131004.jpg/250px-OIST_BOG_Members_After_Meeting_Prime_Minister_Shinzo_Abe_4_October_2013_20131004.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:OIST_BOG_Members_After_Meeting_Prime_Minister_Shinzo_Abe_4_October_2013_20131004.jpg)

Murray"), [Tim Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Hunt "Tim Hunt"), [Torsten Wiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsten_Wiesel "Torsten Wiesel"), [Kōji Omi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kōji_Omi "Kōji Omi"), [Akito Arima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akito_Arima "Akito Arima"), [Jonathan M. Born in [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois "Chicago, Illinois") to Lillian (*née* Warsaw) and Selig Friedman, a sewing machine salesman, Friedman's Jewish parents emigrated to the U.S. from Russia. Jerome Friedman excelled in art but became interested in physics after reading a book on [relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_relativity "Theory of relativity") written by [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein"). He turned down a scholarship to the [Art Institute of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Institute_of_Chicago "Art Institute of Chicago") in order to study physics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"). Whilst there he worked under [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi"), and eventually received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in physics in 1956. In 1960, he joined the physics faculty of the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology").

In 1968–69, commuting between MIT and California, he conducted experiments with [Henry W. Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_W._Kendall "Henry W. Kendall") and [Richard E. Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_E._Taylor "Richard E. Taylor") at the [Stanford Linear Accelerator Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Linear_Accelerator_Center "Stanford Linear Accelerator Center") which gave the first experimental evidence that [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton "Proton") had an internal structure, later known to be [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark "Quark"). For this, Friedman, Kendall and Taylor shared the 1990 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"). He is an [institute professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_professor "Institute professor") at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"). Friedman is also a member of the board of sponsors of the *[Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_the_Atomic_Scientists "Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists")*.

In 2003, he was one of 22 Nobel laureates who signed the [Humanist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism_and_Its_Aspirations "Humanism and Its Aspirations"). He is an atheist.

Friedman is one of the 20 American recipients of the Nobel Prize in Physics to sign a letter addressed to President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") in May 2008, urging him to "reverse the damage done to basic science research in the Fiscal Year 2008 Omnibus Appropriations Bill" by requesting additional emergency funding for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")’s [Office of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Science "Office of Science"), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), and the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology").
