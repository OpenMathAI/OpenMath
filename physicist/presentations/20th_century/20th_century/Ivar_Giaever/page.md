**Ivar Giaever** (April 5, 1929 – June 20, 2025) was a Norwegian–American experimental [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared the 1973 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Leo Esaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Esaki "Leo Esaki") and [Brian Josephson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Josephson "Brian Josephson"). One half of the prize was jointly awarded to Esaki and Giaever "for their experimental discoveries regarding [tunneling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_tunneling "Quantum tunneling") phenomena in [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor") and [superconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductor "Superconductor"), respectively."

## Biography

Ivar Giaever was born on April 5, 1929, in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen "Bergen"), Norway, the second of three children of John A. Giaever, a pharmacist. Ivar grew up in [Toten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toten "Toten"), where he attended elementary school, before moving to [Hamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamar "Hamar") to receive his secondary education. In 1948, he entered the [Norwegian Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Institute_of_Technology "Norwegian Institute of Technology"), graduating in 1952 with an [M.Eng.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.Eng. "M.Eng.") in Mechanical Engineering. The following year, he completed his military service in the [Norwegian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Army "Norwegian Army"), before going on to work for the [Norwegian Patent Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Patent_Office "Norwegian Patent Office") as a patent examiner.

In 1954, Giaever emigrated to Canada, where he joined the Advanced Engineering Program of [Canadian General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_General_Electric "Canadian General Electric"). He then moved to the United States in 1956, joining the [General Electric Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_Research_Laboratory "General Electric Research Laboratory") in 1958. While working for General Electric, Giaever studied physics at [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute "Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute") (RPI), where he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in 1964 with a thesis titled *The Conductivity and the Hall Effect in Binary Alloys*. In 1988, he left General Electric to become an Institute Professor at RPI. The same year, he also became a professor at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo "University of Oslo"), sponsored by [Statoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statoil "Statoil").

Giaever died on June 20, 2025, in [Schenectady, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schenectady,_New_York "Schenectady, New York"), at the age of 96. He is buried in the cemetery of [Hoff Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoff_Church "Hoff Church") in [Østre Toten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Østre_Toten "Østre Toten"), Norway.

## Research

From 1958 to 1969, Giaever worked on [thin films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thin_film "Thin film"), [tunneling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_tunnelling "Quantum tunnelling"), and [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity"). In 1960, following from [Leo Esaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Esaki "Leo Esaki")'s discovery of tunneling in [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor "Semiconductor") in 1957, Giaever showed that tunneling also took place in [superconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductor "Superconductor"), demonstrating tunneling through a very thin layer of [oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxide "Oxide") surrounded on both sides by metal in a superconducting or normal state. His experiments demonstrated the existence of an [energy gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_gap "Energy gap") in superconductors, one of the most important predictions of the [BCS theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCS_theory "BCS theory") of superconductivity, which had been developed in 1957. Giaever's experimental demonstration of tunneling in superconductors stimulated the theoretical physicist [Brian Josephson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Josephson "Brian Josephson") to work on the phenomenon, leading to his prediction of the [Josephson effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_effect "Josephson effect") in 1962. Esaki and Giaever shared half of the 1973 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"), and Josephson received the other half.

Giaever's research later in his career was mainly in the field of [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics"). In 1969, he studied biophysics for a year at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") in England, through a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship "Guggenheim Fellowship"). He continued to work in this area after he returned to the United States in 1970, founding the company Applied BioPhysics, Inc., in 1993.

## Activism

Giaever was a [climate change denier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denier "Climate change denier"), who fueled doubt on [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change"), for example calling it a "new religion." However, he had presented no strong evidence to support this position. On September 13, 2011, he resigned from the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society "American Physical Society"), after the organization called the evidence of damaging global warming "incontrovertible."

Giaever was a science advisor to the [Heartland Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartland_Institute "Heartland Institute"), an American conservative and libertarian [think tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_tank "Think tank") that denies climate change.

Giaever co-signed a letter from over 70 Nobel laureate scientists to the [Louisiana State Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_Legislature "Louisiana State Legislature") supporting the repeal of the anti-evolution [Louisiana Science Education Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Science_Education_Act "Louisiana Science Education Act").

## Personal life

In 1952, Giaever married his childhood sweetheart, Inger Skramstad, who died on September 12, 2023, at the age of 94. They had four children.

In 1964, Giaever became a [naturalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized "Naturalized") U.S. citizen.

Giaever was an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist").
