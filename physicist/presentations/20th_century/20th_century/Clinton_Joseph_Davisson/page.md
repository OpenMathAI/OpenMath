**Clinton Joseph Davisson** (October 22, 1881 – February 1, 1958) was an American experimental [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared the 1937 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [George Paget Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Paget_Thomson "George Paget Thomson") "for their experimental discovery of the [diffraction of electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_diffraction "Electron diffraction") by crystals."

## Biography

Clinton Joseph Davisson was born on October 22, 1881, in [Bloomington, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Illinois "Bloomington, Illinois"), the son of Joseph Davisson, an [artisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artisan "Artisan") from [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio "Ohio"), and Mary Calvert, a schoolteacher from [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania").

Davisson graduated from [Bloomington High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington_High_School_(Bloomington,_Illinois) "Bloomington High School (Bloomington, Illinois)") in 1902, and entered the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") on scholarship. Upon the recommendation of [Robert Millikan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Millikan "Robert Millikan"), he was hired by [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in 1905 as an instructor in physics. He completed the requirements for his [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S. "B.S.") from Chicago in 1908, mainly by working in the summers. While teaching at Princeton, he did doctoral research under [Owen Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Richardson "Owen Richardson"), receiving his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in 1911 with a thesis titled *On The Thermal Emission of Positive Ions From Alkaline Earth Salts*.

After graduating, Davisson became an instructor in the Department of Physics at the Carnegie Institute of Technology (now [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University "Carnegie Mellon University")). In 1917, when the U.S. entered [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), he took up war-related research in the Engineering Department of the [Western Electric Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Electric_Company "Western Electric Company"). Davisson continued to work at Western Electric until 1925, when he joined the Technical Staff of the newly-founded [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Telephone_Laboratories "Bell Telephone Laboratories"). He retired from Bell Labs in 1946. The following year, he accepted a visiting professorship at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia").

Davisson died on February 1, 1958, in [Charlottesville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia "Charlottesville, Virginia"), at the age of 76. [Davisson crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davisson_(crater) "Davisson (crater)") on the [far side of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_side_of_the_Moon "Far side of the Moon") was named after him in 1970.

## Davisson–Germer experiment

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Davisson_and_Germer.jpg/250px-Davisson_and_Germer.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Davisson_and_Germer.jpg)

Davisson *(left)* and [Lester Germer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Germer "Lester Germer"), 1927

[Diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffraction "Diffraction") is a characteristic effect when a wave is incident upon an aperture or a [grating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffraction_grating "Diffraction grating"), and is closely associated with the meaning of wave motion itself. In the 19th century, diffraction was well-established for light and for ripples on the surfaces of fluids.

In 1927, Davisson and his Bell Labs colleague, [Lester Germer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Germer "Lester Germer"), performed an experiment showing that [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") were diffracted at the surface of a crystal of nickel. This work confirmed the [de Broglie hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Broglie_hypothesis "De Broglie hypothesis") that particles of matter have a wave-like nature, which is a central tenet of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"). ## Family

While doing his graduate work at Princeton, Davisson met his future wife, Charlotte Sara Richardson, who was visiting her brother (and his doctoral advisor), Professor [Owen Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Richardson "Owen Richardson"). Charlotte was the sister-in-law of [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen "Oswald Veblen"), a prominent mathematician. Clinton and Charlotte (d. 1984) married in 1911 and had four children: Owen; James; [Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Davisson "Richard Davisson"), who became a physicist; and Elizabeth.
