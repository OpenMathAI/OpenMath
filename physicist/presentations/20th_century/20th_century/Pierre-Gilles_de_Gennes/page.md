**Pierre-Gilles de Gennes** (French: [[pjɛʁ ʒil də ʒɛn]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 24 October 1932 – 18 May 2007) was a French physicist and the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") laureate in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") in 1991.

## Education and early life

He was born in Paris, France, and was [home-schooled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeschooling "Homeschooling") to the age of 12. By the age of 13, he had adopted adult reading habits and was visiting museums. Later, de Gennes studied at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure "École Normale Supérieure"). After leaving the *École* in 1955, he became a research engineer at the [Saclay center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEA_Saclay "CEA Saclay") of the [Commissariat à l'Énergie Atomique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissariat_à_l'Énergie_Atomique "Commissariat à l'Énergie Atomique"), working mainly on [neutron scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_scattering "Neutron scattering") and magnetism, with advice from [Anatole Abragam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatole_Abragam "Anatole Abragam") and [Jacques Friedel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Friedel "Jacques Friedel"). He defended his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in 1957 at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris").

## Career and research

In 1959, he was a [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") visitor with [Charles Kittel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Kittel "Charles Kittel") at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), and then spent 27 months in the [French Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Navy "French Navy"). In 1961, he was assistant professor in [Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_11_University "Paris-Sud 11 University") and soon started the Orsay group on [superconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductors "Superconductors"). In 1968, he switched to studying [liquid crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_crystal "Liquid crystal").

In 1971, he became professor at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France"), and participated in STRASACOL (a joint action of [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg "Strasbourg"), Saclay and *Collège de France*) on polymer physics. From 1980 on, he became interested in interfacial problems: the dynamics of wetting and [adhesion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhesion "Adhesion").

He worked on [granular materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granular_material "Granular material") and on the nature of memory objects in the brain.

## Awards and honours

Awarded the [Fernand Holweck Medal and Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernand_Holweck_Medal_and_Prize "Fernand Holweck Medal and Prize") in 1968.

He was awarded the [Harvey Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Prize "Harvey Prize"), [Lorentz Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_Medal "Lorentz Medal") and [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize "Wolf Prize") in 1988 and 1990. In 1991, he received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"). He was then director of the [École Supérieure de Physique et de Chimie Industrielles de la Ville de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Supérieure_de_Physique_et_de_Chimie_Industrielles_de_la_Ville_de_Paris "École Supérieure de Physique et de Chimie Industrielles de la Ville de Paris") (ESPCI), a post he held from 1976 until his retirement in 2002.

P.G. de Gennes has also received the [F.A. Cotton Medal for Excellence in Chemical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Albert_Cotton#F.A._Cotton_Medal_for_Excellence_in_Chemical_Research "F. Albert Cotton") of the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society") in 1997, the [Holweck Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holweck_Prize "Holweck Prize") from the joint French and British Physical Society; the Ampere Prize, French Academy of Science; the gold medal from the French CNRS; the Matteuci Medal, Italian Academy; the [Harvey Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Prize "Harvey Prize"), Israel; and polymer awards from both APS and ACS.

He was awarded the above-mentioned Nobel Prize for discovering that "methods developed for studying order phenomena in simple systems can be generalized to more complex forms of matter, in particular to liquid crystals and polymers".

The [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry "Royal Society of Chemistry") awards the [De Gennes Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Gennes_Prize "De Gennes Prize") biennially, in his honour. He was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1984 "List of fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1984"). He was awarded [A. Cemal Eringen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._Cemal_Eringen "A. Cemal Eringen") [Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eringen_Medal "Eringen Medal") in 1998.

## Personal life

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Pierre-Gilles_de_Gennes.jpg/250px-Pierre-Gilles_de_Gennes.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pierre-Gilles_de_Gennes.jpg)

De Gennes in his office at [ESPCI Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPCI_Paris "ESPCI Paris"), 1988

He married Anne-Marie Rouet (born in 1933) in June 1954. They remained married until his death and had three children together: Christian (born 9 December 1954), Dominique (born 6 May 1956) and Marie-Christine (born 11 January 1958).

He also had four children with physicist [Françoise Brochard-Wyart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Françoise_Brochard-Wyart "Françoise Brochard-Wyart") (born in 1944) who was one of his former doctoral students and then colleague and co-author. The children are: [Claire Wyart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claire_Wyart "Claire Wyart") (born 16 February 1977), [Matthieu Wyart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthieu_Wyart "Matthieu Wyart") (born 24 May 1978), Olivier Wyart (born 3 August 1984) and Marc de Gennes (born 16 January 1991).

Professors [John Goodby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Goodby "John Goodby") and [George Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_William_Gray "George William Gray") noted in an obituary: "Pierre was a man of great charm and humour, capable of making others believe they, too, were wise. We will remember him as an inspirational lecturer and teacher, an authority on Shakespeare, an expert skier who attended conference lectures appropriately attired with skis to hand, and, robed in red, at the Bordeaux liquid crystal conference in 1978, took great delight in being inaugurated as a Vignoble de St Émilion."

In 2003 he was one of 22 Nobel Laureates who signed the [Humanist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism_and_Its_Aspirations "Humanism and Its Aspirations").

On 22 May 2007, his death was made public as official messages and tributes poured in.
