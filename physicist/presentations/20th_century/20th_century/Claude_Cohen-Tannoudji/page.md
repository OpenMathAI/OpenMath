**Claude Cohen-Tannoudji** (French pronunciation: [[klod kɔɛn tanudʒi]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 1 April 1933) is a French [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") at the [École normale supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_(Paris) "École normale supérieure (Paris)") in Paris. He is known for his experiments in [laser cooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_cooling "Laser cooling"). He was the first to show that it is possible to cool far beyond the limit expected by [sub-Doppler cooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Doppler_cooling "Sub-Doppler cooling"), below the [recoil temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recoil_temperature "Recoil temperature").

He shared the 1997 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Steven Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Chu "Steven Chu") and [William Daniel Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Daniel_Phillips "William Daniel Phillips") for research in methods of laser cooling and [trapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_trap_(atoms) "Magnetic trap (atoms)") atoms.

## Early life

Cohen-Tannoudji was born in [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine,_Algeria "Constantine, Algeria"), [French Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Algeria "French Algeria"), to Algerian [Sephardic Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jewish "Sephardic Jewish") parents Abraham Cohen-Tannoudji and Sarah Sebbah. When describing his origins Cohen-Tannoudji said: "My family, originally from [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier "Tangier"), settled in Tunisia and then in Algeria in the 16th century after having fled Spain during the Inquisition. In fact, our name, Cohen-Tannoudji, means simply the Cohen family from Tangiers. The Algerian Jews obtained the French citizenship in 1870 after Algeria became a French colony in 1830."

After finishing secondary school in [Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers "Algiers") in 1953, Cohen-Tannoudji left for [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") to attend the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure "École normale supérieure"). His professors included [Henri Cartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Cartan "Henri Cartan"), [Laurent Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Schwartz "Laurent Schwartz"), and [Alfred Kastler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Kastler "Alfred Kastler").

In 1958 he married Jacqueline Veyrat, a high school teacher, with whom he has three children. His studies were interrupted when he was [conscripted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription "Conscription") into the army, in which he served for 28 months (longer than usual because of the [Algerian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War "Algerian War")). In 1960 he resumed working toward his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate "Doctorate"), which he obtained from the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure "École normale supérieure") under the supervision of [Alfred Kastler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Kastler "Alfred Kastler") and [Jean Brossel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Brossel "Jean Brossel") at the end of 1962.

## Career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Paris_de_la_Recherche_-_Claude_Cohen-Tannoudji_7.jpg/250px-Paris_de_la_Recherche_-_Claude_Cohen-Tannoudji_7.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Paris_de_la_Recherche_-_Claude_Cohen-Tannoudji_7.jpg)

Claude Cohen-Tannoudji in 2010

After his dissertation, he started teaching [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris"). From 1964-67, he was an associate professor at the university and from 1967-1973 he was a full professor. His lecture notes were the basis of the popular textbook, *[Quantum Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Mechanics_(book) "Quantum Mechanics (book)")* ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language"): *Mécanique quantique*), which he wrote with his colleagues [Bernard Diu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Diu?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bernard Diu") and [Franck Laloë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franck_Laloë "Franck Laloë"). He also continued his research work on [atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom "Atom")-[photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon "Photon") interactions, and his research team developed the model of the *[dressed atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_dressed_state "Light dressed state")*.

In 1973, he became a professor at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France"). In the early 1980s, he started to lecture on radiative forces on atoms in [laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser "Laser") light fields. He also formed a laboratory there with [Alain Aspect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Aspect "Alain Aspect"), [Christophe Salomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophe_Salomon "Christophe Salomon"), and [Jean Dalibard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Dalibard "Jean Dalibard") to study laser cooling and trapping. He even took a statistical approach to laser cooling with the use of [stable distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_distributions "Stable distributions").

In 1976, he took sabbatical leave from the Collège de France, and lectured at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") and [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"). At Harvard, he was a Loeb Lecturer for two weeks, and at MIT, he was a visiting professor.

His work eventually led to the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in physics in 1997 "for the development of methods to cool and trap atoms with laser light", shared with [Steven Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Chu "Steven Chu") and [William Daniel Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Daniel_Phillips "William Daniel Phillips"). Cohen-Tannoudji was the first physics Nobel prize winner born in an Arab country.

In 2015, Cohen-Tannoudji signed the *[Mainau Declaration 2015 on Climate Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainau_Declaration "Mainau Declaration")* on the final day of the 65th [Lindau Nobel Laureate Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindau_Nobel_Laureate_Meetings "Lindau Nobel Laureate Meetings"). The declaration was signed by a total of 76 Nobel Laureates and handed to then-President of the French Republic, [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Hollande "François Hollande"), as part of the successful [COP21 climate summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Climate_Change_Conference "Paris Climate Change Conference") in Paris.
