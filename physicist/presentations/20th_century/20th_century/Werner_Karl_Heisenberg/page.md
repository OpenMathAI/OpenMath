**Werner Karl Heisenberg** ([/ˈhaɪ.zən.bɜːrɡ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); German: [[ˈvɛʁnɐ ˈhaɪzn̩bɛʁk]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Werner_Heisenberg.ogg "File:De-Werner Heisenberg.ogg"); 5 December 1901 – 1 February 1976) was a German theoretical [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"), one of the main pioneers of the theory of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") and a principal scientist in the [German nuclear program during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nuclear_program_during_World_War_II "German nuclear program during World War II").

Heisenberg published his [*Umdeutung* paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umdeutung_paper "Umdeutung paper") in 1925, a major reinterpretation of [old quantum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_quantum_theory "Old quantum theory"). In the subsequent series of papers with [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born "Max Born") and [Pascual Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascual_Jordan "Pascual Jordan"), during the same year, his [matrix formulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_mechanics "Matrix mechanics") of quantum mechanics was substantially elaborated. He is known for the [uncertainty principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncertainty_principle "Uncertainty principle"), which he published in 1927. He received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1932 "for the creation of quantum mechanics".

Heisenberg also made contributions to the theories of the [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_dynamics "Fluid dynamics") of [turbulent flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbulent_flow "Turbulent flow"), the [atomic nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus "Atomic nucleus"), [ferromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism "Ferromagnetism"), [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_rays "Cosmic rays"), and [subatomic particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_particles "Subatomic particles"). He introduced the concept of a [wave function collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_function_collapse "Wave function collapse"). He was also instrumental in planning the first West German [nuclear reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactor "Nuclear reactor") at the [Kernforschungszentrum Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernforschungszentrum_Karlsruhe "Kernforschungszentrum Karlsruhe"), together with one in [Garching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garching "Garching") at the [Technische Hochschule München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule_München "Technische Hochschule München"), in 1957 (both were [research reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_reactor "Research reactor")).

Following World War II, Heisenberg was appointed Director of the [Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Wilhelm_Institute_for_Physics "Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics"), which soon thereafter was renamed the [Max Planck Institute for Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Physics "Max Planck Institute for Physics"). He was director until it was moved to [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich") in 1958. He was Director of the [Max Planck Institute for Physics and Astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Physics_and_Astrophysics "Max Planck Institute for Physics and Astrophysics") from 1960 to 1970.

Heisenberg was also President of the [German Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Forschungsgemeinschaft "Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft"), Chairman of the Commission for Atomic Physics, Chairman of the Nuclear Physics Working Group, and President of the [Alexander von Humboldt Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation "Alexander von Humboldt Foundation").

## Early life and education

### Early years

Werner Karl Heisenberg was born in [Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Würzburg "Würzburg"), Germany, the son of [Kaspar Ernst August Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaspar_Ernst_August_Heisenberg "Kaspar Ernst August Heisenberg") and Annie Wecklein. His father was a secondary school teacher of [classical languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_language "Classical language") who became Germany's only *[ordentlicher Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordentlicher_Professor "Ordentlicher Professor")* (ordinarius professor) of medieval and [modern Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Greek "Modern Greek") studies in the university system.

Heisenberg was raised and lived as a [Lutheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran "Lutheran") Christian. In his late teenage years, Heisenberg read Plato's *[Timaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timaeus_(dialogue) "Timaeus (dialogue)")* while hiking in the [Bavarian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Alps "Bavarian Alps"). He recounted philosophical conversations with his fellow students and teachers about understanding the [atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom "Atom") while receiving his scientific training at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München"), the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"), and the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen"). Heisenberg later stated that "My mind was formed by studying philosophy, Plato and that sort of thing" and that "Modern physics has definitely decided in favor of Plato. In fact the smallest units of matter are not physical objects in the ordinary sense; they are forms, ideas which can be expressed unambiguously only in mathematical language".

In 1919, Heisenberg arrived in Munich as a member of the *[Freikorps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freikorps "Freikorps")* to fight the [Bavarian Soviet Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Soviet_Republic "Bavarian Soviet Republic") established a year earlier. Five decades later he recalled those days as youthful fun, like "playing cops and robbers and so on; it was nothing serious at all"; his duties were restricted to "seizing bicycles or typewriters from 'red' administrative buildings", and guarding suspected "red" prisoners.

### University studies

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/c/c3/Heisenberg%2CWerner_1924_G%C3%B6ttingen_-_adjusted.jpeg/250px-Heisenberg%2CWerner_1924_G%C3%B6ttingen_-_adjusted.jpeg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Heisenberg,Werner_1924_Göttingen_-_adjusted.jpeg)

Heisenberg in 1924

From 1920 to 1923, he studied physics and mathematics at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München") under [Arnold Sommerfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Sommerfeld "Arnold Sommerfeld") and [Wilhelm Wien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Wien "Wilhelm Wien") and at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen") with [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born "Max Born") and [James Franck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Franck "James Franck") and mathematics with [David Hilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hilbert "David Hilbert"). He received his doctorate in 1923 at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München under Sommerfeld.

In June 1922, Sommerfeld took Heisenberg to Göttingen to attend the [Bohr Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr_Festival "Bohr Festival"), because Sommerfeld had a sincere interest in his students and knew of Heisenberg's interest in [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr "Niels Bohr")'s theories on [atomic physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_physics "Atomic physics"). At the event, Bohr was a guest lecturer and gave a series of comprehensive lectures on quantum atomic physics and Heisenberg met Bohr for the first time, which had a lasting effect on him.

Heisenberg's [doctoral thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis "Thesis"), the topic of which was suggested by Sommerfeld, was on [turbulence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbulence "Turbulence"); the thesis discussed both the stability of [laminar flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laminar_flow "Laminar flow") and the nature of [turbulent flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbulence "Turbulence"). The problem of stability was investigated by the use of the [Orr–Sommerfeld equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orr–Sommerfeld_equation "Orr–Sommerfeld equation"), a fourth-order [linear differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_differential_equation "Linear differential equation") for small disturbances from laminar flow. He briefly returned to this topic after World War II.

At Göttingen, under Born, he completed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation "Habilitation") in 1924 with a *Habilitationsschrift* (habilitation thesis) on the anomalous [Zeeman effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeeman_effect "Zeeman effect").

In his youth, he was a member and Scoutleader of the *Neupfadfinder*, a [German Scout association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scouting_in_Germany "Scouting in Germany") and part of the [German Youth Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Youth_Movement "German Youth Movement"). In August 1923, Robert Honsell and Heisenberg organized a trip to Finland with a Scout group of this association from Munich.

### Personal life

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Group_conversion_at_Copenhagen_Conference_in_1937_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Group_conversion_at_Copenhagen_Conference_in_1937_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Group_conversion_at_Copenhagen_Conference_in_1937_(cropped).jpg)

Elisabeth Heisenberg (left) conversing with [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr "Niels Bohr") and Werner Heisenberg, 1937

Heisenberg enjoyed [classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music "Classical music") and was an accomplished pianist; playing for others was a prominent part of his social life. During the late 1920s and early 1930s he would often play music and dance at the Berlin home of his aristocratic student [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_von_Weizsäcker "Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker"), during which time he carried on a courtship with Carl's high-school-age sister Adelheid, which led to him being unwelcome at their home for a time.
Years later, his interest in music also led to meeting his future wife. In January 1937, Heisenberg met Elisabeth Schumacher (1914–1998) at a private music recital. Schumacher was the daughter of a well-known Berlin economics professor, and her brother was the economist [E. F. Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._F._Schumacher "E. F. Schumacher"), author of *[Small Is Beautiful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Is_Beautiful "Small Is Beautiful")*. Heisenberg and Schumacher were married on 29 April. Fraternal twins Maria and Wolfgang were born in January 1938, whereupon [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli") congratulated Heisenberg on his "pair creation"—a wordplay on a process from elementary particle physics, [pair production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_production "Pair production"). They had five more children over the next 12 years: Barbara, Christine, [Jochen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jochen_Heisenberg "Jochen Heisenberg"), [Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Heisenberg "Martin Heisenberg") and Verena. In 1939 he bought a summer home for his family in [Urfeld am Walchensee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urfeld_am_Walchensee "Urfeld am Walchensee"), in southern Germany.

One of Heisenberg's sons, [Martin Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Heisenberg "Martin Heisenberg"), became a [neurobiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurobiologist "Neurobiologist") at the [University of Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Würzburg "University of Würzburg"), while another son, [Jochen Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jochen_Heisenberg "Jochen Heisenberg"), became a physics professor at the [University of New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Hampshire "University of New Hampshire").

## Academic career

### Göttingen, Copenhagen and Leipzig

From 1924 to 1927, Heisenberg was a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent "Privatdozent") at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen "Göttingen"), meaning he was qualified to teach and examine independently, without having a chair. From 17 September 1924 to 1 May 1925, under an International Education Board [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation "Rockefeller Foundation") fellowship, Heisenberg went to do research with [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr "Niels Bohr"), director of the Institute of Theoretical Physics at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen"). On 7 June, after weeks of failing to alleviate a severe bout of [hay fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hay_fever "Hay fever") with aspirin and cocaine, Heisenberg retreated to the pollen-free [North Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea "North Sea") island of [Helgoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heligoland "Heligoland") to focus on quantum mechanics. His seminal paper, "[Über quantentheoretische Umdeutung kinematischer und mechanischer Beziehungen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Über_quantentheoretische_Umdeutung_kinematischer_und_mechanischer_Beziehungen "Über quantentheoretische Umdeutung kinematischer und mechanischer Beziehungen")" ("Quantum theoretical re-interpretation of kinematic and mechanical relations") also called the *Umdeutung* (reinterpretation) paper, was published in September 1925. He returned to Göttingen and, with [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born "Max Born") and [Pascual Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascual_Jordan "Pascual Jordan") over a period of about six months, developed the [matrix mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_mechanics "Matrix mechanics") formulation of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"). On 1 May 1926, Heisenberg began his appointment as a university lecturer and assistant to Bohr in Copenhagen. It was in Copenhagen, in 1927, that Heisenberg developed his [uncertainty principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncertainty_principle "Uncertainty principle"), while working on the mathematical foundations of quantum mechanics. On 23 February, Heisenberg wrote a letter to fellow physicist [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli"), in which he first described his new principle. In his paper on the principle, Heisenberg used the word "*Ungenauigkeit*" (imprecision), not uncertainty, to describe it.

In 1927, Heisenberg was appointed *ordentlicher Professor* (professor ordinarius) of theoretical physics and head of the department of physics at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University "Leipzig University"); he gave his inaugural lecture there on 1 February 1928. In his first paper published from Leipzig, Heisenberg used the [Pauli exclusion principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_exclusion_principle "Pauli exclusion principle") to solve the mystery of [ferromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism "Ferromagnetism").

At 25 years old, Heisenberg gained the title of the youngest full-time professor in Germany and professorial chair of the Institute for Theoretical Physics at the University of Leipzig. He gave lectures that were attended by physicists like [Edward Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Teller "Edward Teller") and [Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Oppenheimer "Robert Oppenheimer"), who would later work on the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project") for the United States.

During Heisenberg's tenure at Leipzig, the high quality of the doctoral students and [post-graduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate_education "Postgraduate education") and research associates who studied and worked with him is clear from the acclaim that many later earned. Mulliken"), [Rudolf Peierls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Peierls "Rudolf Peierls"), [George Placzek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Placzek "George Placzek"), [Isidor Isaac Rabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidor_Isaac_Rabi "Isidor Isaac Rabi"), [Fritz Sauter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Sauter "Fritz Sauter"), [John C. Slater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Slater "John C. Slater"), [Edward Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Teller "Edward Teller"), [John Hasbrouck van Vleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hasbrouck_van_Vleck "John Hasbrouck van Vleck"), [Victor Frederick Weisskopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Frederick_Weisskopf "Victor Frederick Weisskopf"), [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_von_Weizsäcker "Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker"), [Gregor Wentzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Wentzel "Gregor Wentzel"), and [Clarence Zener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Zener "Clarence Zener").

In early 1929, Heisenberg and Pauli submitted the first of two papers laying the foundation for relativistic [quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field_theory "Quantum field theory"). Also in 1929, Heisenberg went on a lecture tour of China, Japan, India, and the United States. In the spring of 1929, he was a visiting lecturer at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"), where he lectured on quantum mechanics. On 19 August 1929 Heisenberg arrived together with [Paul Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac") with the [Graf Zeppelin LZ 127](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LZ_127 "LZ 127") at its first round-the-world flight at [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo"). In 1928, the British [mathematical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Physics "Mathematical Physics") [Paul Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac") had derived his [relativistic wave equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_equation "Dirac equation") of quantum mechanics, which implied the existence of positive electrons, later to be named [positrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron "Positron"). In 1932, from a [cloud chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_chamber "Cloud chamber") photograph of [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray "Cosmic ray"), the American physicist [Carl David Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_David_Anderson "Carl David Anderson") identified a track as having been made by a [positron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron "Positron"). In mid-1933, Heisenberg presented his theory of the positron. His thinking on Dirac's theory and further development of the theory were set forth in two papers. The first, "Bemerkungen zur Diracschen Theorie des Positrons" ("Remarks on Dirac's theory of the positron") was published in 1934, and the second, "Folgerungen aus der Diracschen Theorie des Positrons" ("Consequences of Dirac's Theory of the Positron"), was published in 1936. In these papers Heisenberg was the first to reinterpret the [Dirac equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_equation "Dirac equation") as a "classical" [field equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_equation "Field equation") for any point particle of [spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(physics) "Spin (physics)") ħ/2, itself subject to quantization conditions involving anti-[commutators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutator "Commutator"). Thus reinterpreting it as a (quantum) field equation accurately describing electrons, Heisenberg put matter on the same footing as [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism "Electromagnetism"): as being described by relativistic quantum field equations which allowed the possibility of particle creation and destruction. ([Hermann Weyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Weyl "Hermann Weyl") had already described this in a 1929 letter to [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein").)

### Matrix mechanics and the Nobel Prize

Heisenberg's [*Umdeutung* paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umdeutung_paper "Umdeutung paper") that established modern quantum mechanics has puzzled physicists and historians. His methods assume that the reader is familiar with [Kramers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Kramers "Hans Kramers")-Heisenberg transition probability calculations. The main new idea, [non-commuting matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_multiplication#Properties_of_matrix_multiplication "Matrix multiplication"), is justified only by a rejection of unobservable quantities. It introduces the non-[commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity "Commutativity") multiplication of [matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_(mathematics) "Matrix (mathematics)") by physical reasoning, based on the [correspondence principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correspondence_principle "Correspondence principle"), despite the fact that Heisenberg was not then familiar with the mathematical theory of matrices. The path leading to these results has been reconstructed by MacKinnon, and the detailed calculations are worked out by Aitchison and coauthors.

In Copenhagen, Heisenberg and [Hans Kramers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Kramers "Hans Kramers") collaborated on a paper on dispersion, or the scattering from atoms of radiation whose wavelength is larger than the atoms. They showed that the successful formula Kramers had developed earlier could not be based on Bohr orbits, because the transition frequencies are based on level spacings which are not constant. The frequencies which occur in the [Fourier transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_transform "Fourier transform") of the classical [sharp series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_series "Sharp series") orbits, by contrast, are equally spaced. But these results could be explained by a semi-classical [virtual state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_state "Virtual state") model: the incoming radiation excites the valence, or outer, electron to a virtual state from which it decays. In a subsequent paper, Heisenberg showed that this virtual oscillator model could also explain the polarization of fluorescent radiation.

These two successes, and the continuing failure of the [Bohr–Sommerfeld model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr_model "Bohr model") to explain the outstanding problem of the anomalous Zeeman effect, led Heisenberg to use the virtual oscillator model to try to calculate spectral frequencies. The method proved too difficult to immediately apply to realistic problems, so Heisenberg turned to a simpler example, the [anharmonic oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anharmonic_oscillator "Anharmonic oscillator").

The dipole oscillator consists of a [simple harmonic oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_harmonic_oscillator "Simple harmonic oscillator"), which is thought of as a [charged particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charged_particle "Charged particle") on a spring, perturbed by an external force, like an external charge. The motion of the oscillating charge can be expressed as a [Fourier series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_series "Fourier series") in the frequency of the oscillator. Heisenberg solved for the quantum behavior by two different methods. First, he treated the system with the virtual oscillator method, calculating the transitions between the levels that would be produced by the external source.

He then solved the same problem by treating the anharmonic potential term as a perturbation to the harmonic oscillator and using the [perturbation methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perturbation_theory "Perturbation theory") that he and Born had developed. Both methods led to the same results for the first and the very complicated second-order correction terms. This suggested that behind the very complicated calculations lay a consistent scheme.

So Heisenberg set out to formulate these results without any explicit dependence on the virtual oscillator model. To do this, he replaced the Fourier expansions for the spatial coordinates with matrices, matrices which corresponded to the transition coefficients in the virtual oscillator method. He justified this replacement by an appeal to Bohr's correspondence principle and the Pauli doctrine that quantum mechanics must be limited to observables.

On 9 July, Heisenberg gave Born this paper to review and submit for publication. When Born read the paper, he recognized the formulation as one which could be transcribed and extended to the systematic language of matrices, which he had learned from his study under [Jakob Rosanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Rosanes "Jakob Rosanes") at [Breslau University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breslau_University "Breslau University"). Born, with the help of his assistant and former student [Pascual Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascual_Jordan "Pascual Jordan"), began immediately to make the transcription and extension, and they submitted their results for publication; the paper was received for publication just 60 days after Heisenberg's paper. A follow-on paper was submitted for publication before the end of the year by all three authors.

Up until this time, matrices were seldom used by physicists; they were considered to belong to the realm of [pure mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_mathematics "Pure mathematics"). [Gustav Mie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Mie "Gustav Mie") had used them in a paper on electrodynamics in 1912 and Born had used them in his work on the lattice theory of crystals in 1921. While matrices were used in these cases, the algebra of matrices with their multiplication did not enter the picture as they did in the matrix formulation of quantum mechanics.

In 1928, Albert Einstein nominated Heisenberg, Born, and Jordan for the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"). The announcement of the Nobel Prize in Physics for 1932 was delayed until November 1933. It was at that time announced that Heisenberg had won the Prize for 1932 "for the creation of quantum mechanics, the application of which has, [inter alia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Latin_phrases:_I#inter_alia "List of Latin phrases: I"), led to the discovery of the [allotropic forms of hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_isomers_of_hydrogen "Spin isomers of hydrogen")".

### Interpretation of quantum theory

The development of quantum mechanics, and the apparently contradictory implications in regard to what is "real" had profound philosophical implications, including what scientific observations truly mean. In contrast to Albert Einstein and [Louis de Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Broglie "Louis de Broglie"), who were realists who believed that particles had an objectively true momentum and position at all times (even if both could not be measured), Heisenberg was an anti-realist, arguing that direct knowledge of what is "real" was beyond the scope of science. In his book *The Physicist's Conception of Nature*, Heisenberg argued that ultimately one only can speak of the *knowledge* (numbers in tables) which describes something about particles but they can never have any "true" access to the particles themselves:

> We can no longer speak of the behaviour of the particle independently of the process of observation. As a final consequence, the natural laws formulated mathematically in quantum theory no longer deal with the elementary particles themselves but with our knowledge of them. Nor is it any longer possible to ask whether or not these particles exist in space and time objectively ...
> When we speak of the picture of nature in the exact science of our age, we do not mean a picture of nature so much as a *picture of our relationships with nature*. ...Science no longer confronts nature as an objective observer, but sees itself as an actor in this interplay between man and nature. The scientific method of analysing, explaining and classifying has become conscious of its limitations, which arise out of the fact that by its intervention science alters and refashions the object of investigation. In other words, method and object can no longer be separated.

### *SS* investigation

Shortly after the discovery of the [neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron "Neutron") by [James Chadwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Chadwick "James Chadwick") in 1932, Heisenberg submitted the first of three papers on his [neutron-proton model of the nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron#Proton_–_neutron_model_of_the_nucleus "Neutron"). After [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") came to power in 1933, Heisenberg was attacked in the press as a "White Jew" (i.e. an [Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryan_race "Aryan race") who acts like a Jew). Supporters of *[Deutsche Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Physik "Deutsche Physik")*, or German Physics (also known as Aryan Physics), launched vicious attacks against leading theoretical physicists, including Arnold Sommerfeld and Heisenberg. From the early 1930s the [anti-Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Semitic "Anti-Semitic") and anti-theoretical physics movement *Deutsche Physik* had concerned itself with quantum mechanics and the [theory of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_relativity "Theory of relativity"). As applied in the university environment, political factors took priority over scholarly ability, even though its two most prominent supporters were the [Nobel Laureates in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") [Philipp Lenard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Lenard "Philipp Lenard") and [Johannes Stark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Stark "Johannes Stark").

There had been many failed attempts to have Heisenberg appointed as a professor at a number of German universities. His attempt to be appointed as successor to Arnold Sommerfeld failed because of opposition by the *Deutsche Physik* movement. On 1 April 1935, the eminent theoretical physicist Sommerfeld, Heisenberg's doctoral advisor at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München"), achieved [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus "Emeritus") status. Sommerfeld stayed in his chair during the selection process for his successor, which took until 1 December 1939. The process was lengthy due to academic and political differences between the Faculty's selection at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München and that of the [Reichserziehungsministerium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichserziehungsministerium "Reichserziehungsministerium") (Reich Education Ministry) and the supporters of *Deutsche Physik*.

In 1935, the Faculty at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München drew up a list of candidates to replace Sommerfeld as ordinarius professor of theoretical physics and head of the Institute for Theoretical Physics at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München. The three candidates had all been former students of Sommerfeld: Heisenberg, who had received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics"); [Peter Debye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Debye "Peter Debye"), who had received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1936; and [Richard Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Becker_(physicist) "Richard Becker (physicist)"). The Faculty at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München was firmly behind these candidates, with Heisenberg as their first choice. Supporters of *Deutsche Physik* and elements in the REM had their own list of candidates, and the battle dragged on for over four years. During this time, Heisenberg came under vicious attack by the *Deutsche Physik* supporters. One attack was published in *[Das Schwarze Korps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Schwarze_Korps "Das Schwarze Korps")*, the newspaper of the *[SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS "SS")*, headed by [Heinrich Himmler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Himmler "Heinrich Himmler"). In this, Heisenberg was called a "White Jew" who should be made to "disappear". These attacks were taken seriously, as Jews were violently attacked and incarcerated. Heisenberg fought back with an editorial and a letter to Himmler, in an attempt to resolve the matter and regain his honour.

At one point, Heisenberg's mother visited Himmler's mother. The two women knew each other, as Heisenberg's maternal grandfather and Himmler's father were rectors and members of a Bavarian hiking club. Eventually, Himmler settled the Heisenberg affair by sending two letters, one to SS [Gruppenführer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruppenführer "Gruppenführer") [Reinhard Heydrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Heydrich "Reinhard Heydrich") and one to Heisenberg, both on 21 July 1938. In the letter to Heydrich, Himmler said Germany could not afford to lose or silence Heisenberg, as he would be useful for teaching a generation of scientists. To Heisenberg, Himmler said the letter came on the recommendation of his family and he cautioned Heisenberg to make a distinction between professional physics research results and the personal and political attitudes of the involved scientists.

[Wilhelm Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Müller_(physicist) "Wilhelm Müller (physicist)") replaced Sommerfeld at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München. Müller was not a theoretical physicist, had not published in a physics journal and was not a member of the [Deutsche Physikalische Gesellschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Physikalische_Gesellschaft "Deutsche Physikalische Gesellschaft") (German Physical Society). His appointment was considered a travesty and detrimental to educating theoretical physicists.

The three investigators who led the SS investigation of Heisenberg had training in physics. Heisenberg had participated in the doctoral examination of one of them at the [Universität Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig "University of Leipzig"). The most influential of the three was [Johannes Juilfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Juilfs "Johannes Juilfs"). During their investigation, they became supporters of Heisenberg as well as his position against the ideological policies of the *Deutsche Physik* movement in theoretical physics and academe.

## German nuclear weapons program

### Pre-war work on physics

In mid-1936, Heisenberg presented his theory of [cosmic-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray "Cosmic ray") showers in two papers. Four more papers appeared in the next two years.

In December 1938, the German chemists [Otto Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn "Otto Hahn") and [Fritz Strassmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Strassmann "Fritz Strassmann") sent a manuscript to *[The Natural Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Naturwissenschaften "Die Naturwissenschaften")* reporting they had detected the element [barium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barium "Barium") after bombarding [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium "Uranium") with neutrons, leading Hahn to conclude that a *bursting* of the uranium nucleus had occurred; simultaneously, Hahn communicated these results to his friend [Lise Meitner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lise_Meitner "Lise Meitner"), who had in July of that year fled, first to the Netherlands, then to Sweden. Meitner, and her nephew [Otto Robert Frisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Robert_Frisch "Otto Robert Frisch"), correctly interpreted Hahn's and Strassmann's results as being [nuclear fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fission "Nuclear fission"). Frisch confirmed this experimentally on 13 January 1939.

In June and July 1939, Heisenberg traveled to the United States visiting [Samuel Abraham Goudsmit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Abraham_Goudsmit "Samuel Abraham Goudsmit") at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan") in [Ann Arbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor,_Michigan "Ann Arbor, Michigan"). However, Heisenberg refused an invitation to emigrate to the United States. He did not see Goudsmit again until six years later, when Goudsmit was the chief scientific advisor to the American [Operation Alsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Alsos "Operation Alsos") at the close of World War II.

### Membership in the Uranverein

The [German nuclear weapons program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nuclear_weapons_program "German nuclear weapons program"), known as *Uranverein*, was formed on 1 September 1939, the day [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") began in Europe. The *Heereswaffenamt* (HWA, Army Ordnance Office) had squeezed the *[Reichsforschungsrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsforschungsrat "Reichsforschungsrat")* (RFR, Reich Research Council) out of the *[Reichserziehungsministerium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichserziehungsministerium "Reichserziehungsministerium")* (REM, Reich Ministry of Education) and started the formal German nuclear energy project under military auspices. The project had its first meeting on 16 September 1939. The meeting was organized by [Kurt Diebner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Diebner "Kurt Diebner"), advisor to the HWA, and held in Berlin. The invitees included [Walther Bothe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Bothe "Walther Bothe"), [Siegfried Flügge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegfried_Flügge "Siegfried Flügge"), [Hans Geiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Geiger "Hans Geiger"), [Otto Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn "Otto Hahn"), [Paul Harteck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Harteck "Paul Harteck"), [Gerhard Hoffmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Hoffmann_(physicist) "Gerhard Hoffmann (physicist)"), [Josef Mattauch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Mattauch "Josef Mattauch") and [Georg Stetter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Stetter "Georg Stetter"). A second meeting was held soon thereafter and included Heisenberg, [Klaus Clusius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Clusius "Klaus Clusius"), [Robert Döpel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Döpel "Robert Döpel") and [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_von_Weizsäcker "Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker"). The *Kaiser-Wilhelm Institut für Physik* (KWIP, Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics) in [Berlin-Dahlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlem_(Berlin) "Dahlem (Berlin)"), was placed under HWA authority, with Diebner as the administrative director, and the military control of the nuclear research commenced. During the period when Diebner administered the KWIP under the HWA program, considerable personal and professional animosity developed between Diebner and Heisenberg's inner circle, which included [Karl Wirtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Wirtz "Karl Wirtz") and [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_von_Weizsäcker "Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/UFission.gif/330px-UFission.gif?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:UFission.gif)

A visual representation of an induced nuclear fission event where a slow-moving neutron is absorbed by the nucleus of a uranium-235 atom, which fissions into two fast-moving lighter elements (fission products) and additional neutrons. Most of the energy released is in the form of the kinetic velocities of the fission products and the neutrons.

At a scientific conference on 26–28 February 1942 at the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics, called by the Army Weapons Office, Heisenberg presented a lecture to Reich officials on energy acquisition from nuclear fission. The lecture, entitled "Die theoretischen Grundlagen für die Energiegewinnung aus der Uranspaltung" ("The theoretical basis for energy generation from uranium fission") was, as Heisenberg wrote after the Second World War in a letter to [Samuel Goudsmit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Goudsmit "Samuel Goudsmit"), "adapted to the intelligence level of a Reich Minister". Heisenberg lectured on the enormous energy potential of nuclear fission, stating that 250 million electron volts could be released through the fission of an atomic nucleus. Heisenberg stressed that pure U-235 had to be obtained to achieve a chain reaction. He explored various ways of obtaining isotope 235
92U in its pure form, including uranium enrichment and an alternative layered method of normal uranium and a moderator in a machine. This machine, he noted, could be used in practical ways to fuel vehicles, ships and submarines. Heisenberg stressed the importance of the Army Weapons Office's financial and material support for this scientific endeavour.

A second scientific conference followed. Lectures were heard on problems of modern physics with decisive importance for the national defense and economy. The conference was attended by [Bernhard Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhard_Rust "Bernhard Rust"), the Reich Minister of Science, Education and National Culture. At the conference, Reich Minister Rust decided to take the nuclear project away from the Kaiser Wilhelm Society, and give it to the Reich Research Council.

In April 1942 the army returned the Physics Institute to the Kaiser Wilhelm Society, naming Heisenberg as Director at the Institute. [Peter Debye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Debye "Peter Debye") was still director of the institute, but had gone on leave to the United States after he had refused to become a German citizen when the HWA took administrative control of the KWIP. Heisenberg still also had his department of physics at the University of Leipzig where work had been done for the *Uranverein* by [Robert Döpel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Döpel "Robert Döpel") and his wife [Klara Döpel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klara_Döpel "Klara Döpel").

On 4 June 1942, Heisenberg was summoned to report to [Albert Speer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Speer "Albert Speer"), Germany's Minister of Armaments, on the prospects for converting the Uranverein's research toward developing [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon"). During the meeting, Heisenberg told Speer that a bomb could not be built before 1945, because it would require significant monetary resources and number of personnel.

After the Uranverein project was placed under the leadership of the Reich Research Council, it focused on [nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power "Nuclear power") production and thus maintained its *kriegswichtig* (importance for the war) status; funding therefore continued from the military. The nuclear power project was broken down into the following main areas: [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium "Uranium") and [heavy water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_water "Heavy water") production, uranium [isotope separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope_separation "Isotope separation") and the *Uranmaschine* (uranium machine, i.e., [nuclear reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactor "Nuclear reactor")). The project was then essentially split up between a number of institutes, where the directors dominated the research and set their own research agendas. The point in 1942, when the army relinquished its control of the German nuclear weapons program, was the zenith of the project relative to the number of personnel. About 70 scientists worked for the program, with about 40 devoting more than half their time to nuclear fission research. After 1942, the number of scientists working on applied nuclear fission diminished dramatically. Many of the scientists not working with the main institutes stopped working on nuclear fission and devoted their efforts to more pressing war-related work.

In September 1942, Heisenberg submitted his first paper of a three-part series on the scattering matrix, or [S-matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-matrix "S-matrix"), in elementary [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics"). The first two papers were published in 1943 and the third in 1944. The S-matrix described only the states of incident particles in a collision process, the states of those emerging from the collision, and stable [bound states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bound_state "Bound state"); there would be no reference to the intervening states. This was the same precedent as he followed in 1925 in what turned out to be the foundation of the matrix formulation of quantum mechanics through only the use of observables.

In February 1943, Heisenberg was appointed to the Chair for Theoretical Physics at the *Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität* (today, the [Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin")). In April, his election to the *Preußische Akademie der Wissenschaften* ([Prussian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences "Prussian Academy of Sciences")) was approved. That same month, he moved his family to their retreat in [Urfeld](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urfeld%20am%20Walchensee "de:Urfeld am Walchensee") as Allied bombing increased in Berlin. In the summer, he dispatched the first of his staff at the *Kaiser-Wilhelm Institut für Physik* to [Hechingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hechingen "Hechingen") and its neighboring town of [Haigerloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haigerloch "Haigerloch"), on the edge of the [Black Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Forest "Black Forest"), for the same reasons. From 18–26 October, he travelled to [German-occupied Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-occupied_Netherlands "German-occupied Netherlands"). In December 1943, Heisenberg visited [German-occupied Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Poland_(1939–1945) "History of Poland (1939–1945)").

From 24 January to 4 February 1944, Heisenberg travelled to occupied Copenhagen, after the German army confiscated [Bohr's Institute of Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr's_Institute_of_Theoretical_Physics "Bohr's Institute of Theoretical Physics"). He made a short return trip in April. In December, Heisenberg lectured in [neutral Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_during_the_World_Wars#World_War_II "Switzerland during the World Wars"). The United States [Office of Strategic Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Strategic_Services "Office of Strategic Services") sent agent [Moe Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moe_Berg "Moe Berg") to attend the lecture carrying a pistol, with orders to shoot Heisenberg if his lecture indicated that Germany was close to completing an atomic bomb.

In January 1945, Heisenberg, with most of the rest of his staff, moved from the *Kaiser-Wilhelm Institut für Physik* to the facilities in the Black Forest.

## Post-Second World War

### 1945: Alsos Mission

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Haigerloch-nuclear-reactor_ArM.JPG/250px-Haigerloch-nuclear-reactor_ArM.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Haigerloch-nuclear-reactor_ArM.JPG)

Replica of the German experimental nuclear reactor captured and dismantled at Haigerloch

The Alsos Mission was an Allied effort to determine whether the Germans had an atomic bomb program and to exploit German atomic-related facilities, research, material resources, and scientific personnel for the benefit of the US. Personnel on this operation generally swept into areas that had just come under control of the Allied military forces, but sometimes they operated in areas still under control by German forces. Berlin had been a location of many German scientific research facilities. To limit casualties and loss of equipment, many of these facilities were dispersed to other locations in the latter years of the war. The *Kaiser-Wilhelm-Institut für Physik* (KWIP, Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics) had been bombed so it had mostly been moved in 1943 and 1944 to [Hechingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hechingen "Hechingen") and its neighbouring town of [Haigerloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haigerloch "Haigerloch"), on the edge of the [Black Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Forest "Black Forest"), which eventually became included in the French occupation zone. This allowed the American task force of the Alsos Mission to take into custody a large number of German scientists associated with nuclear research.

On 30 March, the Alsos Mission reached [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg"), where important scientists were captured including [Walther Bothe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Bothe "Walther Bothe"), [Richard Kuhn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kuhn "Richard Kuhn"), [Philipp Lenard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Lenard "Philipp Lenard"), and [Wolfgang Gentner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Gentner "Wolfgang Gentner"). Their interrogation revealed that Otto Hahn was at his laboratory in Tailfingen, while Heisenberg and [Max von Laue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_von_Laue "Max von Laue") were at Heisenberg's laboratory in [Hechingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hechingen "Hechingen"), and that the experimental natural uranium reactor that Heisenberg's team had built in Berlin had been moved to Haigerloch. Thereafter, the main focus of the Alsos Mission was on these nuclear facilities in the [Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Württemberg "Württemberg") area. Heisenberg was smuggled out from Urfeld, on 3 May 1945, in an alpine operation in territory still under control by elite German forces. He was taken to Heidelberg, where, on 5 May, he met Goudsmit for the first time since the Ann Arbor visit in 1939. Germany surrendered just two days later. Heisenberg would not see his family again for eight months, as he was moved across France and Belgium and flown to England on 3 July 1945.

### 1945: Reaction to Hiroshima

Nine of the prominent German scientists who published reports in *[Nuclear Physics Research Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernphysikalische_Forschungsberichte "Kernphysikalische Forschungsberichte")* as members of the *Uranverein* were captured by Operation Alsos and incarcerated in England under [Operation Epsilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Epsilon "Operation Epsilon"). Ten German scientists, including Heisenberg, were held at Farm Hall at [Godmanchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godmanchester "Godmanchester") in England. The facility had been a [safe house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_house "Safe house") of the British foreign intelligence [MI6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MI6 "MI6"). During their detention, their conversations were recorded. Conversations thought to be of intelligence value were transcribed and translated into English. The transcripts were released in 1992. On 6 August 1945, the scientists at Farm Hall learned from media reports that the US had dropped an atomic bomb in [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima "Hiroshima"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"). At first, there was disbelief that a bomb had been built and dropped. In the weeks that followed, the German scientists discussed how the United States might have built the bomb.

The [Farm Hall transcripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm_Hall_transcripts "Farm Hall transcripts") reveal that Heisenberg, along with other physicists interned at Farm Hall including Otto Hahn and [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_von_Weizsäcker "Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker"), were glad the Allies had won World War II. Heisenberg told other scientists that he had never contemplated a bomb, only an atomic pile to produce energy. The morality of creating a bomb for the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party "Nazi Party") was also discussed. Only a few of the scientists expressed horror at the prospect of nuclear weapons, and Heisenberg was cautious in discussing the matter. On the failure of the German nuclear weapons program to build an atomic bomb, Heisenberg remarked, "We wouldn't have had the moral courage to recommend to the government in the spring of 1942 that they should employ 120,000 men just for building the thing up."

When in 1992 the transcripts were declassified, German physicist [Manfred Popp](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred%20Popp "de:Manfred Popp") analyzed the transcripts, as well as the documentation of Uranverein. When the German scientists heard about the Hiroshima bomb, Heisenberg admitted that he had never calculated the critical mass of an atomic bomb before. When he subsequently attempted to calculate the mass, he made serious calculation errors. [Edward Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Teller "Edward Teller") and [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe "Hans Bethe") saw the transcript, and drew the conclusion that Heisenberg had done it for the first time as he made similar errors as they had. Only a week later Heisenberg gave a lecture about the physics of the bomb. He correctly recognized many essential aspects, including the efficiency of the bomb, although he still underestimated it. For Popp, this is proof that Heisenberg did not spend time on a nuclear weapon during the war; on the contrary, he avoided even thinking about it.

## Post-war research career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Werner_Heisenberg_bust.jpg/250px-Werner_Heisenberg_bust.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Werner_Heisenberg_bust.jpg)

Bust of Heisenberg in his old age, on display at the [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society "Max Planck Society") campus in [Garching bei München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garching_bei_München "Garching bei München")

### Executive positions at German research institutions

On 3 January 1946, the ten Operation Epsilon detainees were transported to [Alswede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alswede "Alswede") in Germany. Heisenberg settled in Göttingen, which was in the British zone of [Allied-occupied Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany "Allied-occupied Germany"). Heisenberg immediately began to promote scientific research in Germany. Following the [Kaiser Wilhelm Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Wilhelm_Society "Kaiser Wilhelm Society")'s dissolution by the [Allied Control Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Control_Council "Allied Control Council") and the establishment of the [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society "Max Planck Society") in the British zone, Heisenberg became the director of the [Max Planck Institute for Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Physics "Max Planck Institute for Physics"). [Max von Laue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_von_Laue "Max von Laue") was appointed vice director, while [Karl Wirtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Wirtz "Karl Wirtz"), [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_von_Weizsäcker "Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker") and [Ludwig Biermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Biermann "Ludwig Biermann") joined to help Heisenberg establish the institute. [Heinz Billing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Billing "Heinz Billing") joined in 1950 to promote the development of electronic [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing "Computing"). The core research focus of the institute was [cosmic radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_radiation "Cosmic radiation"). The institute held a colloquium every Saturday morning.

Heisenberg together with [Hermann Rein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Rein?action=edit&redlink=1 "Hermann Rein") was instrumental in the establishment of the [Forschungsrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forschungsrat?action=edit&redlink=1 "Forschungsrat") (research council). Heisenberg envisaged this council to promote the dialogue between the newly founded [Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Germany "Federal Republic of Germany") and the scientific community, based in Germany. Heisenberg was appointed president of the *Forschungsrat*. In 1951, the organization was fused with the [Notgemeinschaft der Deutschen Wissenschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notgemeinschaft_der_Deutschen_Wissenschaft "Notgemeinschaft der Deutschen Wissenschaft") (Emergency Association of German Science) and that same year renamed the [Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Forschungsgemeinschaft "Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft") (German Research Foundation). Following the merger, Heisenberg was appointed to the presidium.

In 1958, the [Max-Planck-Institut für Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Physics "Max Planck Institute for Physics") was moved to Munich, expanded, and renamed [Max-Planck-Institut für Physik und Astrophysik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Astrophysics "Max Planck Institute for Astrophysics") (MPIFA). In the interim, Heisenberg and the astrophysicist [Ludwig Biermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Biermann "Ludwig Biermann") were co-directors of MPIFA. Heisenberg also became an *ordentlicher Professor* (ordinarius professor) at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München"). Heisenberg was the sole director of MPIFA from 1960 to 1970. Heisenberg resigned his directorship of the MPIFA on 31 December 1970.

### Promotion of international scientific cooperation

In 1951, Heisenberg agreed to become the scientific representative of the [Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Germany "Federal Republic of Germany") at the [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") conference, with the aim of establishing a European laboratory for nuclear physics. Heisenberg's aim was to build a large [particle accelerator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator "Particle accelerator"), drawing on the resources and technical skills of scientists across the [Western Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bloc "Western Bloc"). On 1 July 1953 Heisenberg signed the convention that established [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN") on behalf of the Federal Republic of Germany. Although he was asked to become CERN's founding scientific director, he declined. Instead, he was appointed chair of CERN's science policy committee and went on to determine the scientific program at CERN.

In December 1953, Heisenberg became the president of the [Alexander von Humboldt Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation "Alexander von Humboldt Foundation"). During his tenure as president 550 Humboldt scholars from 78 nations received scientific research grants. Heisenberg resigned as president shortly before his death.

### Research interests

In 1946, the German scientist [Heinz Pose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Pose "Heinz Pose"), head of Laboratory V in [Obninsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obninsk "Obninsk"), wrote a letter to Heisenberg inviting him to work in the USSR. The letter lauded the working conditions in the USSR and the available resources, as well as the favorable attitude of the Soviets towards German scientists. A courier hand delivered the recruitment letter, dated 18 July 1946, to Heisenberg; Heisenberg politely declined. In 1947, Heisenberg presented lectures in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge"), [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh "Edinburgh") and [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol "Bristol"). Heisenberg contributed to the understanding of the phenomenon of [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity") with a paper in 1947 and two papers in 1948, one of them with [Max von Laue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_von_Laue "Max von Laue").

In the period shortly after World War II, Heisenberg briefly returned to the subject of his doctoral thesis, turbulence. Three papers were published in 1948 and one in 1950. In the post-war period Heisenberg continued his interests in cosmic-ray showers with considerations on multiple production of [mesons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meson "Meson"). He published three papers in 1949, two in 1952, and one in 1955.

In late 1955 to early 1956, Heisenberg gave the [Gifford Lectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gifford_Lectures "Gifford Lectures") at [St Andrews University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews "University of St Andrews"), in Scotland, on the [intellectual history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_history "Intellectual history") of physics. The lectures were later published as *Physics and Philosophy: The Revolution in Modern Science*. During 1956 and 1957, Heisenberg was the chairman of the *Arbeitskreis Kernphysik* (Nuclear Physics Working Group) of the *Fachkommission II "Forschung und Nachwuchs"* (Commission II "Research and Growth") of the *Deutsche Atomkommission* (DAtK, German Atomic Energy Commission). Other members of the Nuclear Physics Working Group in both 1956 and 1957 were: [Walther Bothe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Bothe "Walther Bothe"), [Hans Kopfermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Kopfermann "Hans Kopfermann") (vice-chairman), [Fritz Bopp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Bopp "Fritz Bopp"), [Wolfgang Gentner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Gentner "Wolfgang Gentner"), [Otto Haxel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Haxel "Otto Haxel"), [Willibald Jentschke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willibald_Jentschke "Willibald Jentschke"), [Heinz Maier-Leibnitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Maier-Leibnitz "Heinz Maier-Leibnitz"), [Josef Mattauch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Mattauch "Josef Mattauch"), [Wolfgang Riezler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Riezler?action=edit&redlink=1 "Wolfgang Riezler"), [Wilhelm Walcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Walcher "Wilhelm Walcher") and [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_von_Weizsäcker "Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker"). [Wolfgang Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Paul "Wolfgang Paul") was also a member of the group during 1957.

In 1957, Heisenberg was a signatory of the [Göttinger Manifest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttinger_Manifest "Göttinger Manifest"), taking a public stand against the [Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Germany "Federal Republic of Germany") arming itself with [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons "Nuclear weapons"). Heisenberg, like [Pascual Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascual_Jordan "Pascual Jordan"), thought politicians would ignore this statement by nuclear scientists. But Heisenberg believed that the Göttinger Manifest would "influence public opinion" which politicians would have to take into account. He wrote to [Walther Gerlach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Gerlach "Walther Gerlach"): "We will probably have to keep coming back to this question in public for a long time because of the danger that public opinion will slacken." In 1961 Heisenberg signed the [Memorandum of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorandum_of_Tübingen "Memorandum of Tübingen") alongside a group of scientists who had been brought together by [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_von_Weizsäcker "Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker") and [Ludwig Raiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Raiser "Ludwig Raiser"). A public discussion between scientists and politicians ensued. As prominent politicians, authors and socialites joined the debate on nuclear weapons, the signatories of the memorandum took a stand against "the full-time intellectual nonconformists".

From 1957 onwards, Heisenberg was interested in [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics "Plasma physics") and the process of [nuclear fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion "Nuclear fusion"). He also collaborated with the International Institute of Atomic Physics in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva "Geneva"). He was a member of the Institute's scientific policy committee, and for several years was the Committee's chair. He was one of the eight signatories of the [Memorandum of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorandum_of_Tübingen "Memorandum of Tübingen") which called for the recognition of the [Oder–Neiße line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder–Neisse_line "Oder–Neisse line") as the official border between [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") and [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland") and spoke against a possible nuclear armament of [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany "West Germany").

In 1973, Heisenberg gave a lecture at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") on the historical development of the concepts of [quantum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"). On 24 March 1973 Heisenberg gave a speech before the Catholic Academy of Bavaria, accepting the Romano Guardini Prize. An English translation of his speech was published under the title "Scientific and Religious Truth", a quotation from which appears in a later section of this article.

## Philosophy and worldview

Heisenberg admired [Eastern philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_philosophy "Eastern philosophy") and saw parallels between it and quantum mechanics, describing himself as in "complete agreement" with the book *[The Tao of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tao_of_Physics "The Tao of Physics")*. Heisenberg even went as far to state that after conversations with [Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore "Rabindranath Tagore") about [Indian philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_philosophy "Indian philosophy") "some of the ideas that seemed so crazy suddenly made much more sense". Regarding the [laws of nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_law "Scientific law") he remarked that "the concept of 'the law of nature' cannot be completely objective, the word 'law' being a purely human principle".

Regarding the philosophy of [Ludwig Wittgenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Wittgenstein "Ludwig Wittgenstein"), Heisenberg disliked *[Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tractatus_Logico-Philosophicus "Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus")* but he liked "very much the later ideas of Wittgenstein and his philosophy about language."

Heisenberg, a devout Christian, wrote: "We can console ourselves that the good Lord God would know the position of the [subatomic] particles, thus He would let the causality principle continue to have validity", in his last letter to Albert Einstein. Einstein continued to maintain that quantum physics must be incomplete because it implies that the universe is indeterminate at a fundamental level.

In lectures given in the 1950s and later published as *Physics and Philosophy*, Heisenberg contended that scientific advances were leading to cultural conflicts. He stated that modern physics is "part of a general historical process that tends toward a unification and a widening of our present world".

When Heisenberg accepted the [Romano Guardini Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romano_Guardini_Prize?action=edit&redlink=1 "Romano Guardini Prize") in 1974, he gave a speech, which he later published under the title *Scientific and Religious Truth*. He mused:

> In the history of science, ever since the famous [trial of Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_affair "Galileo affair"), it has repeatedly been claimed that scientific truth cannot be reconciled with the religious interpretation of the world. Although I am now convinced that scientific truth is unassailable in its own field, I have never found it possible to dismiss the content of religious thinking as simply part of an outmoded phase in the consciousness of mankind, a part we shall have to give up from now on. Thus in the course of my life I have repeatedly been compelled to ponder on the relationship of these two regions of thought, for I have never been able to doubt the reality of that to which they point.

— Heisenberg 1974, 213

Heisenberg referred to nature as "God's second book" (the first being the Bible) and believed that "Physics is reflection on the divine
ideas of Creation; therefore physics is divine service". This was because "God created the world in accordance with his ideas of creation" and humans can understand the world because "Man was created as the spiritual image of God".

## Political stance

Heisenberg never participated in explicit [National Socialist propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_in_Nazi_Germany "Propaganda in Nazi Germany"). However, he fully supported Nazi Germany's project of [European "renewal"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Order_(Nazism) "New Order (Nazism)"), which corresponded with his German-[imperialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism "Imperialism") convictions. The Dutch physicist [Hendrik Casimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Casimir "Hendrik Casimir") recalled hearing from Heisenberg in 1943 that German [world domination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_domination "World domination") was a historical necessity due to the weakness of Western [liberal democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy "Liberal democracy") and the alternative of [Soviet Communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_communism_in_the_Soviet_Union "History of communism in the Soviet Union"). According to the British-German physicist [Rudolf Peierls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Peierls "Rudolf Peierls"), while visiting England in 1947 Heisenberg told a colleague who had been forced to emigrate from Germany that after another fifty years in power the Nazis "would have become quite decent". The Austrian-Swedish physicist [Lise Meitner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lise_Meitner "Lise Meitner") quoted Heisenberg's 1948 reply to being confronted with German atrocities: "Unfortunately, every spiritual upheaval has always been accompanied by great cruelty".

Heisenberg, who did not leave Germany during the Nazi rule, was also unwilling to emigrate after the war. Responding to an offer of permanent [endowed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_endowment "Financial endowment") employment at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") in 1951 conveyed by [Gregory Breit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Breit "Gregory Breit"), he stated he would have considered it only if [World War III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_III "World War III") had broken out and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") had occupied Göttingen.

## Autobiography and death

In his late sixties, Heisenberg penned his autobiography for the mass market. In 1969 the book was published in Germany, in early 1971 it was published in English and in the years thereafter in a string of other languages. Heisenberg initiated the project in 1966, when his public lectures increasingly turned to the subjects of philosophy and religion. Heisenberg had sent the manuscript for a textbook on the [unified field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_field_theory "Unified field theory") to Hirzel Verlag and [John Wiley & Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wiley_&_Sons "John Wiley & Sons") for publication. This manuscript, he wrote to one of his publishers, was the preparatory work for his autobiography. He structured his autobiography in themes, covering: 1) The goal of exact science, 2) The problematic of language in atomic physics, 3) Abstraction in mathematics and science, 4) The divisibility of matter or Kant's antinomy, 5) The basic symmetry and its substantiation, and 6) Science and religion.

Heisenberg wrote his memoirs as a chain of conversations, covering the course of his life. The book became a popular success, but was regarded as troublesome by historians of science. In the preface Heisenberg wrote that he had abridged historical events, to make them more concise. At the time of publication, it was reviewed by [Paul Forman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Forman_(historian) "Paul Forman (historian)") in the journal *Science* with the comment "Now here is a memoir in the form of rationally reconstructed dialogue. And the dialogue as Galileo well knew, is itself a most insidious literary device: lively, entertaining, and especially suited for insinuating opinions while yet evading responsibility for them." Few scientific memoirs had been published, but [Konrad Lorenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Lorenz "Konrad Lorenz") and [Adolf Portmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Portmann "Adolf Portmann") had penned popular books that conveyed scholarship to a wide audience. Heisenberg worked on his autobiography and published it with the [Piper Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_Verlag "Piper Verlag") in Munich. Heisenberg initially proposed the title *Gespräche im Umkreis der Atomphysik* (*Conversations on Atomic Physics*). The autobiography was published eventually under the title *Der Teil und das Ganze* (*The Part and the Whole*). The 1971 English translation was published under the title *[Physics and Beyond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_and_Beyond "Physics and Beyond"): Encounters and Conversations*.

[![A gravestone surrounded by vegetation. On it are the four names and dates, with August and Annie at the top on either side of a large cross. Below the cross Werner and Elisabeth are listed.](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Waldfriedhof_Grabstelle_Werner_Heisenberg.jpg/250px-Waldfriedhof_Grabstelle_Werner_Heisenberg.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Waldfriedhof_Grabstelle_Werner_Heisenberg.jpg)

The grave of the Heisenberg family in [Munich Waldfriedhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Waldfriedhof "Munich Waldfriedhof"), including August Heisenberg (1869–1930), Annie Heisenberg (1879–1945), Werner Heisenberg (1901–1976), and Elisabeth Heisenberg (1914–1998)

Heisenberg died of kidney cancer at his home, on 1 February 1976. The next evening, his colleagues and friends walked in remembrance from the Institute of Physics to his home, lit a candle and placed it in front of his door. Heisenberg is buried in [Munich Waldfriedhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Waldfriedhof "Munich Waldfriedhof").

In 1980 his widow, [Elisabeth Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_Heisenberg?action=edit&redlink=1 "Elisabeth Heisenberg"), published *Das politische Leben eines Unpolitischen* (*The Political Life of an Apolitical Person*), in which she characterized Heisenberg as "first and foremost, a spontaneous person, thereafter a brilliant scientist, next a highly talented artist, and only in the fourth place, from a sense of duty, homo politicus".

- [Romano Guardini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romano_Guardini "Romano Guardini") Prize

## Research reports on nuclear physics

The following reports were published in *[Kernphysikalische Forschungsberichte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernphysikalische_Forschungsberichte "Kernphysikalische Forschungsberichte")* (*Research Reports in Nuclear Physics*), an internal publication of the German *[Uranverein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nuclear_energy_project "German nuclear energy project")*. The reports were classified [Top Secret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Secret "Top Secret"), they had very limited distribution, and the authors were not allowed to keep copies. The reports were confiscated under the Allied [Operation Alsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Alsos "Operation Alsos") and sent to the [United States Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Atomic_Energy_Commission "United States Atomic Energy Commission") for evaluation. In 1971, the reports were declassified and returned to Germany. The reports are available at the [Karlsruhe Nuclear Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forschungszentrum_Karlsruhe "Forschungszentrum Karlsruhe") and the [American Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Physics "American Institute of Physics").

## In popular culture

Heisenberg's surname is used as the primary [alias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_identity "Secret identity") for [Walter White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_White_(Breaking_Bad) "Walter White (Breaking Bad)") (played by [Bryan Cranston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Cranston "Bryan Cranston")), the lead character in [AMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_(TV_channel) "AMC (TV channel)")'s crime drama series *[Breaking Bad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_Bad "Breaking Bad")*, throughout White's transformation from a high-school chemistry teacher into a [meth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methamphetamine "Methamphetamine") cook and a drug kingpin. In the spin-off prequel series *[Better Call Saul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_Call_Saul "Better Call Saul")*, a German character named Werner Ziegler directs the construction of the meth lab belonging to antagonist [Gus Fring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Fring "Gus Fring") that Walt cooks in for much of *Breaking Bad*.

Heisenberg was the target of an assassination by spy [Moe Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moe_Berg "Moe Berg") in the film *[The Catcher Was a Spy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Catcher_Was_a_Spy_(film) "The Catcher Was a Spy (film)")*, based on real events. Heisenberg is also credited with building the atomic bomb used by the Axis in the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video "Amazon Prime Video") [TV series adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_in_the_High_Castle_(TV_series) "The Man in the High Castle (TV series)") of the novel *[The Man in the High Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_in_the_High_Castle "The Man in the High Castle")* by [Philip K. Dick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_K._Dick "Philip K. Dick"). Atomic bombs in this universe are referred to as Heisenberg Devices.

The 2015 TV film *Kampen om Tungtvannet* (*The Heavy Water War: Stopping Hitler's Atomic Bomb*) directed by Per-Olav Sørensen, extensively features Werner Heisenberg and his career, including his nuclear research under the Nazis.

[Daniel Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Craig "Daniel Craig") portrayed Heisenberg in the 2002 film *[Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_(2002_film) "Copenhagen (2002 film)"),* an adaptation of [Michael Frayn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Frayn "Michael Frayn")'s [play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_(play) "Copenhagen (play)"). [Matthias Schweighöfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Schweighöfer "Matthias Schweighöfer") portrayed Heisenberg in the 2023 [biopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biopic "Biopic") *[Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_(film) "Oppenheimer (film)")*.

Heisenberg is the namesake of *[Resident Evil Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_Evil_Village "Resident Evil Village")* secondary antagonist Karl Heisenberg. Heisenberg's research on ferromagnetism served as inspiration for the character's magnetic abilities.

In the television series *[Star Trek: The Next Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek:_The_Next_Generation "Star Trek: The Next Generation")*, the "Heisenberg compensator" is an essential component of [transporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transporter_(Star_Trek) "Transporter (Star Trek)") technology to ensure the integrity of transported matter. The compensator counteracts effects of the applied characteristics identified in Heisenberg's uncertainty principle. To accurately isolate matter prior to its entry into the transporter buffer, all particles must be located, their velocity observed, and tracked; the compensators allow this to happen.
