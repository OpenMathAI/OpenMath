**Walther Wilhelm Georg Bothe** (German: [[ˈvaltɐ ˈboːtə]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Walther_Bothe.ogg "File:De-Walther Bothe.ogg"); 8 January 1891 – 8 February 1957) was a German experimental [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who shared the 1954 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born "Max Born") "for the [coincidence method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coincidence_method "Coincidence method") and his discoveries made therewith."

Bothe served in the military during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") from 1914, and he was a prisoner of war of the Russians, returning to Germany in 1920. Upon his return to the laboratory, he developed and applied [coincidence circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coincidence_circuit "Coincidence circuit") to the study of [nuclear reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reaction "Nuclear reaction"), such as the [Compton effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_effect "Compton effect"), [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_rays "Cosmic rays"), and the [wave–particle duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave–particle_duality "Wave–particle duality") of radiation.

In 1930, Bothe became Full Professor and Director of the Physics Department at the [University of Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen "University of Giessen"). In 1932, he became Director of the Physical and Radiological Institute at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University "Heidelberg University"); he was driven out of this position by elements of the *[Deutsche Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Physik "Deutsche Physik")* movement. To preclude his emigration from Germany, he was appointed Director of the Physics Institute of the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Medical Research at Heidelberg University. There, he built the first operational [cyclotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotron "Cyclotron") in Germany. Furthermore, he became a principal in the German nuclear energy project, also known as *[Uranverein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranverein "Uranverein")*, which was started in 1939 under the supervision of the Army Ordnance Office.

In 1946, in addition to his directorship of the Physics Institute at the KWImf, Bothe was reinstated as a professor at Heidelberg University. From 1956 to 1957, he was a member of the Nuclear Physics Working Group in Germany.

In the year after Bothe's death, his Physics Institute at the KWImF was elevated to the status of a new institute under the [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society "Max Planck Society") and it then became the [Max Planck Institute for Nuclear Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Nuclear_Physics "Max Planck Institute for Nuclear Physics"). Its main building was later named Bothe laboratory.

## Education

Walther Wilhelm Georg Bothe was born on 8 January 1891 in [Oranienburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oranienburg "Oranienburg"), Germany. From 1908 to 1912, Bothe studied physics under [Max Planck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck "Max Planck") at the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin "Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin"), receiving his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in 1914.

## Career and research

### Early years

In 1913, Bothe joined the Physikalisch-Technische Reichsanstalt (PTR – now the [Physikalisch-Technische Bundesanstalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physikalisch-Technische_Bundesanstalt "Physikalisch-Technische Bundesanstalt")), where he stayed until 1930. [Hans Geiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Geiger "Hans Geiger") had been appointed Director of the newly-established Laboratory for Radioactivity there in 1912; Bothe was an assistant to Geiger from 1913 to 1920, a scientific member of Geiger's staff from 1920 to 1927, and Director of the Laboratory for Radioactivity from 1927 to 1930—in succession to Geiger.

In May 1914, Bothe volunteered for service in the German cavalry. He was taken prisoner by the Russians and incarcerated in Russia for five years. While there, he learned the Russian language and worked on theoretical physics problems related to his doctoral studies. He returned to Germany in 1920, with a Russian bride.

On his return from Russia, Bothe continued his employment at the PTR under Hans Geiger in the Laboratory for Radioactivity. In 1924, Bothe published on his coincidence method. The [Bothe–Geiger coincidence experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bothe–Geiger_coincidence_experiment "Bothe–Geiger coincidence experiment") studied the [Compton effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compton_effect "Compton effect") and the [wave–particle duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave–particle_duality "Wave–particle duality") of light. Bothe's [coincidence method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coincidence_method "Coincidence method") and his applications of it earned him the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1954.

In 1925, while still at the PTR, Bothe became a *[Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent "Privatdozent")* (unsalaried lecturer) at the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin "Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin"), and, in 1929, he became an *[ausserordentlicher Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ausserordentlicher_Professor "Ausserordentlicher Professor")* (extraordinary professor) there.

In 1927, Bothe began the study of the [transmutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_transmutation "Nuclear transmutation") of light elements through bombardment with [alpha particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_particles "Alpha particles"). From a joint investigation with H. Fränz and [Heinz Pose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Pose "Heinz Pose") in 1928, Bothe and Fränz correlated reaction products of nuclear interactions to nuclear energy levels.

In 1929, in collaboration with [Werner Kolhörster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Kolhörster "Werner Kolhörster") and [Bruno Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Rossi "Bruno Rossi") (who were guests in Bothe's laboratory at the PTR), Bothe began the study of [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_rays "Cosmic rays"). The study of cosmic radiation would be conducted by Bothe for the rest of his life.

In 1930, Bothe became an *[ordentlicher Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordentlicher_Professor "Ordentlicher Professor")* (ordinary professor) and Director of the Physics Department at the [University of Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen "University of Giessen"). The same year, Bothe and his collaborator Herbert Becker bombarded [beryllium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryllium "Beryllium"), [boron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boron "Boron"), and [lithium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium "Lithium") with alpha particles from [polonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonium "Polonium") and observed a new form of penetrating radiation. In 1932, [James Chadwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Chadwick "James Chadwick") identified this radiation as the [neutron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron "Neutron").

### Heidelberg

In 1932, Bothe succeeded [Philipp Lenard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Lenard "Philipp Lenard") as Director of the Physical and Radiological Institute at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University "Heidelberg University"). It was then that [Rudolf Fleischmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Fleischmann "Rudolf Fleischmann") became a teaching assistant to Bothe. When [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") became Chancellor of Germany on 30 January 1933, the concept of *[Deutsche Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Physik "Deutsche Physik")* (Aryan Physics) took on more favor as well as fervor; it was [antisemitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism") and against theoretical physics, especially against modern physics, including [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") and both atomic and nuclear physics. As applied in the university environment, political factors took priority over the historically applied concept of scholarly ability, even though its two most prominent supporters were the Lenard and [Johannes Stark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Stark "Johannes Stark"). Supporters of *Deutsche Physik* launched vicious attacks against leading theoretical physicists. While Lenard was retired from Heidelberg, he still had significant influence there.

In 1934, Lenard had managed to get Bothe relieved of his directorship of the Physical and Radiological Institute, whereupon Bothe was able to become the Director of the Institute for Physics of the Kaiser-Wilhelm-Institut für medizinische Forschung (KWImF – Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Medical Research) in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg"), replacing Karl W. Hauser, who had recently died. [Ludolf von Krehl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludolf_von_Krehl "Ludolf von Krehl"), Director of the KWImF, and [Max Planck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck "Max Planck"), President of the Kaiser-Wilhelm-Gesellschaft (KWG – [Kaiser Wilhelm Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Wilhelm_Society "Kaiser Wilhelm Society")), had offered the directorship to Bothe to ward off the possibility of his emigration. Bothe held the directorship of the Institute for Physics at the KWImF until his death in 1957. While at the KWImF, Bothe held an honorary professorship at Heidelberg, which he held until 1946. Fleischmann went with Bothe and worked with him there until 1941. To his staff, Bothe recruited scientists including [Wolfgang Gentner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Gentner "Wolfgang Gentner"), [Heinz Maier-Leibnitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Maier-Leibnitz "Heinz Maier-Leibnitz")—who had done his doctorate with the Nobel Laureate [James Franck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Franck "James Franck") and was highly recommended by [Robert Pohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pohl "Robert Pohl") and [Georg Joos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Joos "Georg Joos"), and [Arnold Flammersfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Flammersfeld "Arnold Flammersfeld"). Also included on his staff were Peter Jensen and Erwin Fünfer.

In 1938, Bothe and Gentner published on the energy dependence of the nuclear photo-effect. This was the first clear evidence that nuclear absorption spectra are accumulative and continuous, an effect known as the dipolar giant nuclear resonance. This was explained theoretically a decade later by physicists [J. Hans D. Jensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Hans_D._Jensen "J. Hans D. Jensen"), Helmut Steinwedel, Peter Jensen, Michael Goldhaber, and [Edward Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Teller "Edward Teller").

Also in 1938, Maier-Leibnitz built a [cloud chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_chamber "Cloud chamber"). Images from the cloud chamber were used by Bothe, Gentner, and Maier-Leibnitz to publish, in 1940, the *Atlas of Typical Cloud Chamber Images*, which became a standard reference for identifying scattered particles.

### First German cyclotron

By the end of 1937, the rapid successes Bothe and Gentner had with the building and research uses of a [Van de Graaff generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_de_Graaff_generator "Van de Graaff generator") had led them to consider building a [cyclotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotron "Cyclotron"). By November, a report had already been sent to the President of the KWG, and Bothe began securing funds from the Helmholtz Society, the Baden Ministry of Culture, [IG Farben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IG_Farben "IG Farben"), the KWG, and various other research oriented agencies. Initial promises led to ordering a magnet from [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens "Siemens") in September 1938, however, further financing then became problematic. In these times, Gentner continued his research on the nuclear photoeffect, with the aid of the Van de Graaff generator, which had been upgraded to produce energies just under 1 MeV. When his line of research was completed with the 7Li (p, gamma) and the 11B (p, gamma) reactions, and on the nuclear isomer 80Br, Gentner devoted his full effort to the building of the planned cyclotron.

To facilitate the construction of the cyclotron, at the end of 1938 and into 1939, with the help of a fellowship from the Helmholtz Society, Gentner was sent to Radiation Laboratory of the University of California (now the [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Laboratory "Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory")) in Berkeley, California. As a result of the visit, Gentner formed a cooperative relationship with [Emilio G. Segrè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_G._Segrè "Emilio G. Segrè") and [Donald Cooksey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Cooksey "Donald Cooksey").

After the armistice between France and Germany in the summer of 1940, Bothe and Gentner received orders to inspect the cyclotron [Frédéric Joliot-Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frédéric_Joliot-Curie "Frédéric Joliot-Curie") had built in Paris. While it had been built, it was not yet operational. In September 1940, Gentner received orders to form a group to put the cyclotron into operation. Hermann Dänzer from the [University of Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Frankfurt_am_Main "University of Frankfurt am Main") participated in this effort. While in Paris, Gentner was able to free both Frédéric Joliot-Curie and [Paul Langevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Langevin "Paul Langevin"), who had been arrested and detained. At the end of the winter of 1941/1942, the cyclotron was operational with a 7-MeV beam of [deuterons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterons "Deuterons"). [Uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium "Uranium") and [thorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorium "Thorium") were irradiated with the beam, and the byproducts were sent to [Otto Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn "Otto Hahn") at the *Kaiser-Wilhelm Institut für Chemie* (KWIC – Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Chemistry), in Berlin. In mid-1942, Gentner's successor in Paris, was [Wolfgang Riezler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Riezler?action=edit&redlink=1 "Wolfgang Riezler") from [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn "Bonn").

It was during 1941 that Bothe had acquired all the necessary funding to complete construction of the cyclotron. The magnet was delivered in March 1943, and the first beam of deuteron was emitted in December. The inauguration ceremony for the cyclotron was held on 2 June 1944. While there had been other cyclotrons under construction, Bothe's was the first operational cyclotron in Germany.

### Uranium Club

The German nuclear energy project, also known as *[Uranverein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranverein "Uranverein")* (Uranium Club), began in the spring of 1939 under the auspices of the [Reichsforschungsrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsforschungsrat "Reichsforschungsrat") (RFR – Reich Research Council) of the [Reichserziehungsministerium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichserziehungsministerium "Reichserziehungsministerium") (REM – Reich Ministry of Education). By 1 September, the [Heereswaffenamt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heereswaffenamt "Heereswaffenamt") (HWA – Army Ordnance Office) squeezed out the RFR and took over the effort. Under the control of the HWA, *Uranverein* had its first meeting on 16 September. The meeting was organized by [Kurt Diebner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Diebner "Kurt Diebner"), advisor to the HWA, and held in Berlin. The invitees included Bothe, [Siegfried Flügge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegfried_Flügge "Siegfried Flügge"), [Hans Geiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Geiger "Hans Geiger"), [Otto Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn "Otto Hahn"), [Paul Harteck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Harteck "Paul Harteck"), [Gerhard Hoffmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Hoffmann_(physicist) "Gerhard Hoffmann (physicist)"), [Josef Mattauch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Mattauch "Josef Mattauch"), and [Georg Stetter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Stetter "Georg Stetter"). A second meeting was held soon thereafter and included [Klaus Clusius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Clusius "Klaus Clusius"), [Robert Döpel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Döpel "Robert Döpel"), [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg "Werner Heisenberg"), and [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_von_Weizsäcker "Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker"). With Bothe being one of the principals, [Wolfgang Gentner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Gentner "Wolfgang Gentner"), [Arnold Flammersfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Flammersfeld "Arnold Flammersfeld"), [Rudolf Fleischmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Fleischmann "Rudolf Fleischmann"), Erwin Fünfer, and Peter Jensen were soon drawn into work for the *Uranverein*. Their research was published in *[Kernphysikalische Forschungsberichte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernphysikalische_Forschungsberichte "Kernphysikalische Forschungsberichte")* (*Research Reports in Nuclear Physics*); see below the section *Internal Reports*.

For *Uranverein*, Bothe—and up to 6 members from his staff by 1942—worked on the experimental determination of atomic constants, the energy distribution of fission fragments, and [nuclear cross sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_cross_section "Nuclear cross section"). His erroneous experimental results on the absorption of neutrons in graphite were central in the German decision to favor [heavy water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_water "Heavy water") as a [neutron moderator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_moderator "Neutron moderator"). His value was too high; one conjecture being that this was due to air between the graphite pieces with the nitrogen having high neutron absorption. However the experimental setup involved a sphere of Siemens electro-graphite submerged in water, no air being present. The error in fast neutron cross-section was due to impurities in the Siemens product: "even the Siemens electro-Graphite contained Barium and Cadmium, both ravenous neutron-absorbers." In any event, there were so few staff or groups that they could not repeat experiments to check results, although in fact a separate group at Gottingen, led by Wilhelm Hanle, determined the cause of Bothe's error: "Hanle's own measurements would show that carbon, properly prepared, would in fact work perfectly well as a moderator, but at a cost of production in industrial quantities ruled prohibitive by [German] Army Ordnance".

By late 1941, it was apparent that the nuclear energy project would not make a decisive contribution to ending the war effort in the near term. HWA control of *Uranverein* was relinquished to the RFR in July 1942. The nuclear energy project thereafter maintained its *kriegswichtig* (important for the war) designation and funding continued from the military. However, the German nuclear power project was then broken down into the following main areas: uranium and heavy water production, uranium isotope separation, and the *Uranmaschine* (uranium machine; i.e., [nuclear reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reactor "Nuclear reactor")). Also, the project was then essentially split up between nine institutes, where the directors dominated the research and set their own research agendas. Bothe's Institute for Physics was one of the nine institutes. The other eight institutes or facilities were: the Institute for Physical Chemistry at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München"), the HWA *Versuchsstelle* (testing station) in Gottow, the KWIC, the Physical Chemistry Department of the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg "University of Hamburg"), the *Kaiser-Wilhelm-Institut für Physik* (Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics), the Second Experimental Physics Institute at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"), the *Auergesellschaft*, and the Second Physical Institute at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna "University of Vienna").

### Post-WWII

From 1946 to 1957, in addition to his position at the KWImF, Bothe was an *ordentlicher Professor* at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University "Heidelberg University").

At the end of World War II, the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II") had seized the cyclotron at Heidelberg. In 1949, its control was returned to Bothe.

During 1956 and 1957, Bothe was a member of the *Arbeitskreis Kernphysik* (Nuclear Physics Working Group) of the *Fachkommission II "Forschung und Nachwuchs"* (Commission II "Research and Growth") of the *Deutschen Atomkommission* (German Atomic Energy Commission). Other members of the Nuclear Physics Working Group in both 1956 and 1957 were: [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg "Werner Heisenberg") (chairman), [Hans Kopfermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Kopfermann "Hans Kopfermann") (vice-chairman), [Fritz Bopp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Bopp "Fritz Bopp"), [Wolfgang Gentner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Gentner "Wolfgang Gentner"), [Otto Haxel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Haxel "Otto Haxel"), [Willibald Jentschke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willibald_Jentschke "Willibald Jentschke"), [Heinz Maier-Leibnitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Maier-Leibnitz "Heinz Maier-Leibnitz"), [Josef Mattauch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Mattauch "Josef Mattauch"), [Wolfgang Riezler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Riezler?action=edit&redlink=1 "Wolfgang Riezler"), [Wilhelm Walcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Walcher "Wilhelm Walcher"), and [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_von_Weizsäcker "Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker"). [Wolfgang Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Paul "Wolfgang Paul") was also a member of the group during 1957.

At the end of 1957, Gentner was in negotiations with [Otto Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn "Otto Hahn"), President of the Max-Planck-Gesellschaft (MPG – [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society "Max Planck Society"); successor of the [Kaiser Wilhelm Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Wilhelm_Society "Kaiser Wilhelm Society")), and with the Senate of the MPG to establish a new institute under their auspices. Essentially, Bothe's Institute for Physics at the Max-Planck-Institut für medizinische Forschung ([Max Planck Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Medical_Research "Max Planck Institute for Medical Research")), in Heidelberg, was to be spun off to become a full fledged institute of the MPG. The decision to proceed was made in May 1958. Gentner was named the director of the Max-Planck-Institut für Kernphysik (MPIK – [Max Planck Institute for Nuclear Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Nuclear_Physics "Max Planck Institute for Nuclear Physics")) on 1 October, and he also received the position as an *ordentlicher Professor* at Heidelberg University. Bothe had not lived to see the final establishment of the MPIK, as he had died in February of that year.

Bothe was a German patriot who did not give excuses for his work with *Uranverein*. However, his impatience with Nazi policies in Germany brought him under suspicion and investigation by the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo "Gestapo").

## Personal life

As a result of his incarceration in Russia during World War I as a prisoner of war, Bothe met Barbara Below, whom he married in 1920; they had two children. She preceded him in death by some years.

Bothe was an accomplished painter and musician; he played the piano.

## Works

### Internal reports

The following reports were published in *[Kernphysikalische Forschungsberichte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernphysikalische_Forschungsberichte "Kernphysikalische Forschungsberichte")* (*Research Reports in Nuclear Physics*), an internal publication of the German *[Uranverein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nuclear_energy_project "German nuclear energy project")*. The reports were classified Top Secret, they had very limited distribution, and the authors were not allowed to keep copies. The reports were confiscated under the Allied [Operation Alsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Alsos "Operation Alsos") and sent to the [United States Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Atomic_Energy_Commission "United States Atomic Energy Commission") for evaluation. In 1971, the reports were declassified and returned to Germany. The reports are available at the [Karlsruhe Nuclear Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forschungszentrum_Karlsruhe "Forschungszentrum Karlsruhe") and the [American Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Physics "American Institute of Physics").
