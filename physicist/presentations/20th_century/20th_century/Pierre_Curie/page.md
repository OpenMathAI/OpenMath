**Pierre Curie** (15 May 1859 – 19 April 1906) was a French [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") and [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist"), and a pioneer in [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography "Crystallography") and [magnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetism "Magnetism"). He shared one half of the 1903 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with his wife, [Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie "Marie Curie"), for their work on [radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity "Radioactivity"). With their win, the Curies became the [first married couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_couples_awarded_the_Nobel_Prize "List of couples awarded the Nobel Prize") to win a [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize"), launching the [Curie family legacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize#Family_laureates "Nobel Prize") of five Nobel Prizes.

## Biography

Pierre Curie was born on 15 May 1859 in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), where his father was a general practitioner. After being homeschooled, Curie entered the Faculty of Sciences at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris"), where he earned his [licentiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licentiate "Licentiate") in Physics in 1878. He stayed at the University working as a laboratory demonstrator until 1882, when he joined the faculty at [ESPCI Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPCI_Paris "ESPCI Paris").

In 1895, Curie received his [D.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.Sc. "D.Sc.") from the University of Paris, where he was subsequently appointed Professor of Physics. The submission material for his doctorate consisted of his research on [magnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetism "Magnetism"). In 1900, he was promoted to Professor in the Faculty of Sciences, and became Titular Professor in 1904.

## Research

### Piezoelectricity

In 1880, Pierre and his older brother, [Jacques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Curie "Jacques Curie"), demonstrated that an [electric potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_potential "Electric potential") was generated when crystals were compressed, i.e. [piezoelectricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezoelectricity "Piezoelectricity"). To aid this work, they invented the piezoelectric quartz [electrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrometer "Electrometer"). In 1881, they demonstrated the reverse effect; that crystals could be made to deform when subject to an [electric field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_field "Electric field"). Almost all [digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_electronics "Digital electronics") [electronic circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_circuit "Electronic circuit") now rely on this in the form of [crystal oscillators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_oscillator "Crystal oscillator"). In subsequent work on magnetism, he defined the Curie scale. This work also involved delicate equipment – balances, electrometers, etc.

### Magnetism

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Curie1895These.jpg/250px-Curie1895These.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Curie1895These.jpg)

*Propriétés magnétiques des corps à diverses temperatures*
(Curie's dissertation, 1895)

Before his famous doctoral studies on magnetism, Curie designed and perfected an extremely sensitive [torsion balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_balance "Torsion balance") for measuring magnetic coefficients. Variations on this equipment were commonly used by future workers in that area. He studied [ferromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism "Ferromagnetism"), [paramagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramagnetism "Paramagnetism"), and [diamagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamagnetism "Diamagnetism") for his doctoral thesis, and discovered the effect of temperature on paramagnetism which is now known as [Curie's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curie's_law "Curie's law"). The material constant in Curie's law is known as the [Curie constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curie_constant "Curie constant"). He also discovered that ferromagnetic substances exhibited a [critical temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_temperature "Critical temperature") transition, above which the substances lost their ferromagnetic behavior. This is now known as the [Curie temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curie_temperature "Curie temperature"). The Curie temperature is used to study [plate tectonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics "Plate tectonics"), treat [hypothermia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothermia "Hypothermia"), measure [caffeine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caffeine "Caffeine"), and to understand extraterrestrial [magnetic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field "Magnetic field"). The [curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curie_(unit) "Curie (unit)") is a unit of measurement (3.7 × 1010 decays per second or 37 [gigabecquerels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Becquerel "Becquerel")) used to describe the intensity of a sample of radioactive material and was named after Marie and Pierre Curie by the Radiology Congress in 1910.

### Curie's principle

Curie formulated what is now known as the [Curie Dissymmetry Principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curie_Dissymmetry_Principle "Curie Dissymmetry Principle"): a physical effect cannot have a dissymmetry absent from its efficient [cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality_(physics) "Causality (physics)"). For example, a random mixture of sand in zero gravity has no [dissymmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissymmetry "Dissymmetry") (it is [isotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotropic "Isotropic")). Introduce a [gravitational field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_field "Gravitational field"), and there is a dissymmetry because of the direction of the field. Then the sand grains can 'self-sort' with the density increasing with depth. But this new arrangement, with the directional arrangement of sand grains, actually reflects the dissymmetry of the gravitational field that causes the separation.

### Radioactivity

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie.jpg/250px-Pierre_and_Marie_Curie.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pierre_and_Marie_Curie.jpg)

Pierre and [Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie "Marie Curie") in the laboratory.

Curie worked with [his wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie "Marie Curie") in isolating [polonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonium "Polonium") and [radium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radium "Radium"). They were the first to use the term *[radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity "Radioactivity")*, and were pioneers in its study. Their work, including Marie Curie's celebrated doctoral work, made use of a sensitive piezoelectric electrometer constructed by Pierre and his brother [Jacques Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Curie "Jacques Curie"). Curie's 26 December 1898 publication with his wife and M. G. Bémont for their discovery of radium and polonium was honored by a Citation for Chemical Breakthrough Award from the Division of History of Chemistry of the American Chemical Society presented to the [ESPCI ParisTech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPCI_ParisTech "ESPCI ParisTech") (officially the École supérieure de physique et de Chimie industrielles de la Ville de Paris) in 2015. In 1903, to honor the Curies' work, the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") invited Pierre to present their research. Marie was not permitted to give the lecture, so [Lord Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kelvin "Lord Kelvin") sat beside her while Pierre spoke on their research. After this, Kelvin held a luncheon for Pierre. While in London, Pierre and Marie were awarded the [Davy Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davy_Medal "Davy Medal") of the Royal Society. In 1903, Pierre and Marie Curie, as well as [Henri Becquerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Becquerel "Henri Becquerel"), were awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for their research on radioactivity.

Curie and one of his students, Albert Laborde, made the first discovery of [nuclear energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_binding_energy "Nuclear binding energy"), by identifying the continuous emission of heat from radium particles. Curie also investigated the radiation emissions of radioactive substances, and through the use of magnetic fields was able to show that some of the emissions were positively charged, some were negative and some were neutral. These correspond to [alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_particle "Alpha particle"), [beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_particle "Beta particle"), and [gamma radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_radiation "Gamma radiation").

### Spiritualism

In the late nineteenth century, Curie was investigating the mysteries of ordinary [magnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetism "Magnetism") when he became aware of the [spiritualist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritualism_(movement) "Spiritualism (movement)") experiments of other French scientists, such as [Charles Richet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Richet "Charles Richet") and [Camille Flammarion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Flammarion "Camille Flammarion"). He initially thought the systematic investigation into the paranormal could help with some unanswered questions about magnetism. He wrote to Marie, then his fiancée: "I must admit that those spiritual phenomena intensely interest me. I think they are questions that deal with physics." Pierre Curie's notebooks from this period show he read many books on spiritualism. He did not attend séances such as those of [Eusapia Palladino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusapia_Palladino "Eusapia Palladino") in Paris in June 1905 as a mere spectator, and his goal certainly was not to communicate with spirits. He saw the séances as scientific experiments, tried to monitor different parameters, and took detailed notes of every observation. Curie considered himself an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism "Atheism").

## Family

Pierre Curie's grandfather, Paul Curie (1799–1853), a doctor of medicine, was a committed Malthusian humanist and married Augustine Hofer, daughter of Jean Hofer and great-granddaughter of Jean-Henri Dollfus, great industrialists from [Mulhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulhouse "Mulhouse") in the second half of the 18th century and the first part of the 19th century.
Through this paternal grandmother, Pierre Curie is also a direct descendant of the Basel scientist and mathematician [Jean Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Bernoulli "Johann Bernoulli") (1667–1748), as is [Pierre-Gilles de Gennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Gilles_de_Gennes "Pierre-Gilles de Gennes"), winner of the 1991 Nobel Prize in Physics.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Pierre_Curie_et_Marie_Sklodowska_Curie_1895.jpg/250px-Pierre_Curie_et_Marie_Sklodowska_Curie_1895.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pierre_Curie_et_Marie_Sklodowska_Curie_1895.jpg)

Pierre and [Marie Skłodowska-Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Skłodowska-Curie "Marie Skłodowska-Curie"), 1895.

Curie was introduced to [Maria Skłodowska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie "Marie Curie") by their friend, physicist [Józef Wierusz-Kowalski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Wierusz-Kowalski "Józef Wierusz-Kowalski"). Curie took her into his laboratory as his student. His admiration for her grew when he realised that she would not inhibit his research. He began to regard Skłodowska as his muse. She refused his initial proposal, but finally agreed to marry him on 26 July 1895.

> It would be a beautiful thing, a thing I dare not hope if we could spend our life near each other, hypnotized by our dreams: your patriotic dream, our humanitarian dream, and our scientific dream. [Pierre Curie to Maria Skłodowska]

The Curies had a happy marriage.

Pierre and Marie Curie's daughter, [Irène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irène_Joliot-Curie "Irène Joliot-Curie"), and their son-in-law, [Frédéric Joliot-Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frédéric_Joliot-Curie "Frédéric Joliot-Curie"), were also physicists involved in the study of [radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity "Radioactivity"), and each also received Nobel prizes for their work. The Curies' other daughter, [Ève](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ève_Curie "Ève Curie"), wrote a noted biography of her mother. She was the only member of the Curie family to not become a physicist. Ève married [Henry Richardson Labouisse Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Richardson_Labouisse_Jr. "Henry Richardson Labouisse Jr."), who received a Nobel Peace Prize on behalf of [UNICEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF "UNICEF") in 1965. Pierre and Marie Curie's granddaughter, [Hélène Langevin-Joliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hélène_Langevin-Joliot "Hélène Langevin-Joliot"), is a professor of nuclear physics at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris"), and their grandson, [Pierre Joliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Joliot "Pierre Joliot"), who was named after Pierre Curie, is a noted biochemist.

## Death

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Panth%C3%A9on_Pierre_et_Marie_Curie.JPG/250px-Panth%C3%A9on_Pierre_et_Marie_Curie.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Panthéon_Pierre_et_Marie_Curie.JPG)

Tombs of Marie (above) and Pierre Curie at [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris")' [Panthéon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthéon "Panthéon").

On 19 April 1906, while crossing the busy [Rue Dauphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rue_Dauphine "Rue Dauphine") in the rain at the Quai de Conti, Curie slipped and fell under a heavy horse-drawn cart; one of the wheels ran over his head, fracturing his skull and killing him instantly.

Both the Curies experienced [radium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radium "Radium") burns, both accidentally and voluntarily, and were exposed to extensive doses of radiation while conducting their research. They experienced [radiation sickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_sickness "Radiation sickness") and Marie Curie died from radiation-induced [aplastic anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aplastic_anemia "Aplastic anemia") in 1934. Even now, all their papers from the 1890s, even her cookbooks, are radioactive. Their laboratory books are kept in special lead boxes and people who want to see them have to wear [protective clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment "Personal protective equipment"). Most of these items can be found at the [Bibliothèque nationale de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliothèque_nationale_de_France "Bibliothèque nationale de France"). Had Pierre Curie not died in an accident, he would most likely have eventually died of the effects of radiation, as did his wife; their daughter, [Irène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irène_Joliot-Curie "Irène Joliot-Curie"); and her husband, [Frédéric Joliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frédéric_Joliot-Curie "Frédéric Joliot-Curie").

In April 1995, Pierre and Marie Curie were moved from their original resting place, a family cemetery, and enshrined in the [crypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypt "Crypt") of the [Panthéon in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthéon,_Paris "Panthéon, Paris").
