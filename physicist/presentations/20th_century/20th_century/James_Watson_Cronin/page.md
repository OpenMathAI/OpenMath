**James Watson Cronin** (September 29, 1931 – August 25, 2016) was an American [particle physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics").

Cronin and co-researcher [Val Logsdon Fitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Logsdon_Fitch "Val Logsdon Fitch") were awarded the 1980 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for a 1964 experiment that proved that certain subatomic reactions do not adhere to fundamental symmetry principles. Specifically, they proved, by examining the decay of [kaons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaon "Kaon"), that a reaction run in reverse does not merely retrace the path of the original reaction, which showed that the interactions of [subatomic particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_particle "Subatomic particle") are not invariant under time reversal. Thus the phenomenon of [CP violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP_violation "CP violation") was discovered.

Cronin received the [Ernest Orlando Lawrence Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Orlando_Lawrence_Award "Ernest Orlando Lawrence Award") in 1976 for major experimental contributions to particle physics including fundamental work on weak interactions culminating in the discovery of asymmetry under time reversal. In 1999, he was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science").

Cronin was [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor "Professor") [Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus "Emeritus") at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") winning the prestigious [Quantrell Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantrell_Award "Quantrell Award") and a spokesperson emeritus for the [Auger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Auger_Observatory "Pierre Auger Observatory") project. He was a member of the Board of Sponsors of the [Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_the_Atomic_Scientists "Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists").

## Early life and education

James Cronin was born in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") on September 29, 1931. His father, James Farley Cronin, was a graduate student of classical languages at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"). After his father had obtained his doctorate the family first moved to Alabama, and later in 1939 to [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas "Dallas"), [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas"), where his father became a professor of Latin and Greek at [Southern Methodist University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Methodist_University "Southern Methodist University"). After high school Cronin stayed in Dallas and obtained an undergraduate degree at SMU in physics and mathematics in 1951. He is of Irish descent, with his Irish ancestors immigrating from County Cork, Ireland.

For graduate school Cronin moved back to Illinois to attend the University of Chicago. His teachers there included [Nobel Prize laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_laureate "Nobel Prize laureate") [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi"), [Maria Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Mayer "Maria Mayer"), [Murray Gell-Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Gell-Mann "Murray Gell-Mann") and [Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subrahmanyan_Chandrasekhar "Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar"). He wrote his thesis on experimental nuclear physics under supervision of [Samuel K. Allison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_K._Allison "Samuel K. Allison").

## Research and career

After obtaining his doctorate in 1955, Cronin joined the group of [Rodney L. Cool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_L._Cool "Rodney L. Cool") and [Oreste Piccioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oreste_Piccioni "Oreste Piccioni") at [Brookhaven National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory"), where the new [Cosmotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmotron "Cosmotron") [particle accelerator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator "Particle accelerator") had just been completed. There he started to study [parity violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_violation "Parity violation") in the decay of [hyperon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperon "Hyperon") particles. During that time he also met [Val Fitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Fitch "Val Fitch"), who brought him to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in Fall 1958. After Cosmotron underwent magnet failure, Cronin and the Brookhaven group moved to [Bevatron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bevatron "Bevatron") at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") during the first half of 1958. Cronin and Fitch studied the decays of neutral [K mesons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_meson "K meson"), in which they discovered [CP violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP_violation "CP violation") in 1964. This discovery earned the duo the 1980 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics").

After the discovery, Cronin spent a year in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") at the [Centre d'Études Nucléaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEA_Saclay "CEA Saclay") at [Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saclay "Saclay"). After returning to Princeton he continued studying the neutral CP violating decay modes of the long-lived neutral K meson. In 1971, he moved back to the University of Chicago to become a full professor. This was attractive for him because of a new 400 GeV particle accelerator being built at nearby [Fermilab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermilab "Fermilab"). He received the [Quantrell Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantrell_Award "Quantrell Award").

When he moved to Chicago, he began a long series of experiments on particle production at high transverse momentum. With physicist Pierre Piroue and colleagues we learned about many things. These are summarized in Physical Review D, vol 19, page 764 (1977). Following these experiments Cronin took a sabbatical at [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN "CERN") in 1982–83, where he performed an experiment to measure of the lifetime of the [neutral pion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pion "Pion") (Physics Letters vol 158 B page 81, 1985). He then switched to the study of cosmic rays. The first was a series of measurements looking for point sources of cosmic rays. No sources were found. A summary of the measurements was published in Physical Review D vol 55 page 1714 (1997). In 1998 he joined the faculty at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah "University of Utah") on a half-time basis to work on [ultra-high-energy cosmic ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-high-energy_cosmic_ray "Ultra-high-energy cosmic ray") physics and to jumpstart the [Pierre Auger Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Auger_Observatory "Pierre Auger Observatory") project. His appointment was to last five years, but he left after a year to continue gathering international support for the Observatory with [Alan Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Andrew_Watson "Alan Andrew Watson") and Murat Boratav.

Cronin is one of the 20 American recipients of the Nobel Prize in Physics to sign a letter addressed to President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") in May 2008, urging him to "reverse the damage done to basic science research in the Fiscal Year 2008 Omnibus Appropriations Bill" by requesting additional emergency funding for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")'s [Office of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Science "Office of Science"), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), and the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology").

## Personal life

While in graduate school he also met his wife, Annette Martin, whom he married in 1954. She was the Director of Special Events at the University of Chicago. They have three children: two daughters, Cathryn (b. 1955) and Emily (b. 1959), and a son, Daniel (b. 1971). In June 2005 Annette Martin died of complications of Parkinson's disease. She was 71.

In November 2006 he married Carol Champlin.

In May 2011 his daughter Cathryn Cranston died of leukemia at age 54.

Cronin died on August 25, 2016, at the age of 84.
