**Heike Kamerlingh Onnes** (Dutch: [[ˈɦɛikə ˈkaːmərlɪŋ ˈɔnəs]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Dutch "Help:IPA/Dutch"); 21 September 1853 – 21 February 1926) was a Dutch experimental [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who became the first to [liquefy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquefaction_of_gases "Liquefaction of gases") [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium "Helium"), cooling it to near 1.5 [kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin "Kelvin") (K). For this work, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1913.

In 1911, using [liquid helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_helium "Liquid helium") to investigate the electrical conductivity of solid [mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_(element) "Mercury (element)"), Kamerlingh Onnes found that its [electrical resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance "Electrical resistance") vanishes at 4.2 K, i.e. [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity").

## Biography

Heike Kamerlingh Onnes was born on 21 September 1853 in [Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groningen "Groningen"), Netherlands, the son of Harm Kamerlingh Onnes, a brickworks owner, and Anna Gerdina Coers of [Arnhem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem "Arnhem"). In 1870, Kamerlingh Onnes attended the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen "University of Groningen"), where he earned his [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.Sc. "B.Sc.") the following year. From 1871 to 1873, he studied under [Robert Bunsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bunsen "Robert Bunsen") and [Gustav Kirchhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Kirchhoff "Gustav Kirchhoff") at the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg "University of Heidelberg"). He then returned to Groningen, obtaining an [M.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.Sc. "M.Sc.") in 1878. The following year, he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") with a thesis on [Earth's rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_rotation "Earth's rotation").

In 1878, Kamerlingh Onnes became an assistant to [Johannes Bosscha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Bosscha "Johannes Bosscha"), the Director of the [Delft Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology "Delft University of Technology"), for whom he substituted as a lecturer in 1881 and 1882. In 1882, he was appointed Professor of Experimental Physics and Meteorology at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University "Leiden University"). In 1904, he founded a large [cryogenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryogenics "Cryogenics") laboratory, and invited other researchers to the location, which made him highly regarded in the scientific community. The laboratory is now known as the Kamerlingh Onnes Laboratory.

Kamerlingh Onnes died on 21 February 1926 in [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden "Leiden") at the age of 72.

## Research

### Liquefaction of helium

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Leiden_-_Kamerlingh_Onnes_Building_-_Commemorative_plaque.jpg/250px-Leiden_-_Kamerlingh_Onnes_Building_-_Commemorative_plaque.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Leiden_-_Kamerlingh_Onnes_Building_-_Commemorative_plaque.jpg)

Commemorative plaque on the Kamerlingh Onnes Building in [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden "Leiden").

On 10 July 1908, Kamerlingh Onnes was the first to liquefy [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium "Helium"), using several pre-cooling stages and the [Hampson–Linde cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampson–Linde_cycle "Hampson–Linde cycle") based on the [Joule–Thomson effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule–Thomson_effect "Joule–Thomson effect"). This way he lowered the temperature to the [boiling point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiling_point "Boiling point") of helium (−269 °C, 4.2 K). By reducing the pressure of the liquid helium, he achieved a temperature near 1.5 K; this was the [lowest temperature recorded on Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowest_temperature_recorded_on_Earth "Lowest temperature recorded on Earth") at the time. The equipment employed is at the [Museum Boerhaave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Boerhaave "Museum Boerhaave") in Leiden.

Kamerlingh Onnes received widespread recognition for this work, including the 1913 [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") "for his investigations on the properties of matter at low temperatures which led, inter alia, to the production of [liquid helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_helium "Liquid helium")."

For further research on low-temperature, Kamerlingh Onnes needed large amounts of helium. This he obtained in 1911 from [Welsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Auer_von_Welsbach "Carl Auer von Welsbach")'s company, which processed [thorianite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorianite "Thorianite") to produce [thorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorium "Thorium") for [gas mantles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_mantle "Gas mantle"). Helium is produced as a side product. Previously, he obtained helium from processing [monazite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monazite "Monazite"), and used the processed monazite (which still contained thorium) to trade for the helium. On Earth, helium is usually found in coexistence with radioactive material, since it is a product of [radioactive decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_decay "Radioactive decay").

### Discovery of superconductivity

In 1911, Kamerlingh Onnes measured the electrical conductivity of pure metals ([mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_(element) "Mercury (element)"), and later [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin "Tin") and [lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead "Lead")) at very low temperatures. Some scientists, such as [Lord Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kelvin "Lord Kelvin"), believed that [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") flowing through a [conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_conductor "Electrical conductor") would come to a complete halt or, in other words, metal resistivity would become infinitely large at absolute zero. Others, including Kamerlingh Onnes, felt that a conductor's [electrical resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance "Electrical resistance") would steadily decrease and drop to nil. [Augustus Matthiessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_Matthiessen "Augustus Matthiessen") said that when the temperature decreases, the metal conductivity usually improves or in other words, the [electrical resistivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistivity "Electrical resistivity") usually decreases with a decrease of temperature.

On 8 April 1911, Kamerlingh Onnes found that at 4.2 K the resistance in a solid mercury wire immersed in liquid helium suddenly vanished. He immediately realized the significance of the discovery (as became clear when his notebook was deciphered a century later). He reported that "Mercury has passed into a new state, which on account of its extraordinary electrical properties may be called the superconductive state." He published more articles about the phenomenon, initially referring to it as "supraconductivity," and later adopting the modern-day term "[superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity")." On the same day, he noted that "Just before the lowest temperature [about 1.8 K] was reached, the boiling suddenly stopped and was replaced by evaporation in which liquid visibly shrank" which was the first observation of [superfluidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluidity "Superfluidity") of the surrounding helium bath.

## Family

In 1887, Kamerlingh Onnes married Maria Adriana Wilhelmina Elisabeth Bijleveld, with whom he had one child, named Albert. His brother, Menso Kamerlingh Onnes (1860–1925), was a painter and the father of another painter, [Harm Kamerlingh Onnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harm_Kamerlingh_Onnes "Harm Kamerlingh Onnes"). Their sister, Jenny, married [Floris Verster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floris_Verster "Floris Verster") (1861–1927), also a painter.

## Commemoration

Some of the instruments Kamerlingh Onnes devised for his experiments can be seen at the [Boerhaave Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boerhaave_Museum "Boerhaave Museum") in Leiden. The apparatus he used to first liquefy helium is on display in the lobby of the physics department at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University "Leiden University"), where the low-temperature lab is also named in his honor. His student and successor as director of the lab [Willem Hendrik Keesom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Hendrik_Keesom "Willem Hendrik Keesom") was the first person who was able to solidify helium, in 1926. The former Kamerlingh Onnes laboratory building is currently the Law Faculty at Leiden University and is known as "Kamerlingh Onnes Gebouw" (Kamerlingh Onnes Building), often shortened to "KOG". The current science faculty has a "Kamerlingh Onnes Laboratorium" named after him, as well as a plaque and several machines used by Kamerling Onnes in the main hall of the physics department.

The [Kamerlingh Onnes Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamerlingh_Onnes_Award "Kamerlingh Onnes Award") (1948) and the [Kamerlingh Onnes Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamerlingh_Onnes_Prize "Kamerlingh Onnes Prize") (2000) were established in his honor, recognizing further advances in low-temperature science.

The [Onnes effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollin_film "Rollin film") referring to the creeping of [superfluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluid "Superfluid") helium is named in his honor.

[Kamerlingh Onnes crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamerlingh_Onnes_(crater) "Kamerlingh Onnes (crater)") on the Moon is named after him.

Kamerlingh Onnes is credited with coining the term *[enthalpy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enthalpy "Enthalpy")*.

Kamerlingh Onnes' discovery of superconductivity was named an [IEEE Milestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_IEEE_milestones "List of IEEE milestones") in 2011.
