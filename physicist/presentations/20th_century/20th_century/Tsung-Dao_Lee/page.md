**Tsung-Dao Lee** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 李政道; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): *Lǐ Zhèngdào*; November 24, 1926 – August 4, 2024) was a Chinese-American physicist known for his work on [parity violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_(physics)#Parity_violation "Parity (physics)"), the [Lee–Yang theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee–Yang_theorem "Lee–Yang theorem"), [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics"), relativistic heavy ion (RHIC) physics, nontopological solitons, and [soliton stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soliton_star "Soliton star"). He was a [university professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_professor "University professor") [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus "Emeritus") at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people_in_New_York_City "Chinese people in New York City"), where he taught from 1953 until his retirement in 2012.

In 1957, at the age of 30, Lee won the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") with [Chen Ning Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Ning_Yang "Chen Ning Yang") for their work on the violation of the [parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_(physics)#Parity_violation "Parity (physics)") law in weak interactions, which [Chien-Shiung Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chien-Shiung_Wu "Chien-Shiung Wu") experimentally proved from 1956 to 1957, with her well known [Wu experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_experiment "Wu experiment").

Lee remains the youngest Nobel laureate in the science fields after [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). He is the third-youngest [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate "Nobel laureate") in sciences in history after [William L. Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lawrence_Bragg "William Lawrence Bragg") (who won the prize at 25 with his father [William H. Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Bragg "William Henry Bragg") in 1915) and [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg "Werner Heisenberg") (who won in 1932 also at 30). Lee and Yang were the first [Chinese laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_Nobel_laureates "List of Chinese Nobel laureates"). Since he became a [naturalized American citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_nationality_law "United States nationality law") in 1963, Lee is also the youngest American ever to have won a Nobel Prize.

## Biography

### Family

Lee was born in [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai "Shanghai"), China, with his [ancestral home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_home_(China) "Ancestral home (China)") in nearby [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou "Suzhou"). His father Chun-kang Lee (李駿康; *Lǐ Jùn-kāng*), one of the first graduates of the [University of Nanking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nanking "University of Nanking"), was a [chemical industrialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_industry "Chemical industry") and [merchant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant "Merchant") who was involved in China's early development of modern synthesized [fertilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilizer "Fertilizer"). Lee's grandfather Chong-tan Lee (李仲覃; *Lǐ Zhòng-tán*) was the first Chinese Methodist Episcopal senior pastor of [St. John's Church in Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John's_Church_in_Suzhou?action=edit&redlink=1 "St. John's Church in Suzhou").

Lee had four brothers and one sister. Educator [Robert C. T. Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_C._T._Lee "Robert C. T. Lee") was one of T. D.'s brothers. Lee's mother Chang and brother Robert C. T. moved to [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") in the 1950s.

### Early life

Lee received his secondary education in Shanghai (High School Affiliated to Soochow University, 東吳大學附屬中學) and [Jiangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangxi "Jiangxi") (Jiangxi Joint High School, 江西聯合中學). Due to the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War"), Lee's high school education was interrupted, thus he did not obtain his secondary diploma. Nevertheless, in 1943, Lee directly applied to and was admitted by the National Chekiang University (now [Zhejiang University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang_University "Zhejiang University")). Initially, Lee registered as a student in the Department of Chemical Engineering. Very quickly, Lee's talent was discovered and his interest in physics grew rapidly. Several physics professors, including [Shu Xingbei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_Xingbei "Shu Xingbei") and [Wang Ganchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Ganchang "Wang Ganchang"), largely guided Lee, and he soon transferred into the Department of Physics of [National Che Kiang University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Che_Kiang_University "National Che Kiang University"), where he studied in 1943–1944.

However, again disrupted by a further Japanese invasion, Lee continued at the [National Southwestern Associated University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Southwestern_Associated_University "National Southwestern Associated University") in [Kunming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunming "Kunming") the next year in 1945, where he studied with Professor [Wu Ta-You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Ta-You "Wu Ta-You").

### Life and research in the U.S.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Chien-Shiung_Wu_%281912-1997%29_in_1963_-_Restoration.jpg/250px-Chien-Shiung_Wu_%281912-1997%29_in_1963_-_Restoration.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Chien-Shiung_Wu_(1912-1997)_in_1963_-_Restoration.jpg)

Chien-Shiung Wu, designer of the [Wu experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_experiment "Wu experiment") that violated [parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_(physics) "Parity (physics)")

Professor Wu nominated Lee for a Chinese government fellowship for graduate study in the United States. In 1946, Lee went to the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") and was selected by Professor [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi") to become his PhD student. Lee received his PhD under Fermi in 1950 for his research work *Hydrogen Content of White Dwarf Stars*. Lee served as research associate and lecturer in physics at the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley "University of California at Berkeley") from 1950 to 1951.

In 1953, Lee joined [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), where he remained until retirement. His first work at Columbia was on a solvable model of [quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field_theory "Quantum field theory") better known as the Lee model. Soon, his focus turned to [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics") and the developing puzzle of [K meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_meson "K meson") decays. Lee realized in early 1956 that the key to the puzzle was parity non-conservation. At Lee's suggestion, the first experimental test was on [hyperon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperon "Hyperon") decay by the Steinberger group. At that time, the experimental result gave only an indication of a 2 standard deviation effect of possible [parity violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_violation "Parity violation"). Encouraged by this feasibility study, Lee made a systematic study of possible Time reversal (T), Parity (P), Charge Conjugation (C), and [CP violations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP_violation "CP violation") in [weak interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_interaction "Weak interaction") with collaborators, including [C. N. Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Chen-Ning "Yang Chen-Ning"). After the definitive experimental confirmation by [Chien-Shiung Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chien-Shiung_Wu "Chien-Shiung Wu") and her assistants that showed that parity was not conserved, Lee and Yang were awarded the 1957 Nobel Prize in Physics. Wu was not awarded the Nobel prize, which is considered a [notable controversy in Nobel committee history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_controversies#Physics "Nobel Prize controversies").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Lizhengdao.jpg/250px-Lizhengdao.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lizhengdao.jpg)

Tsung-Dao Lee at a conference in [Tsinghua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsinghua_University "Tsinghua University"), 2006

In the early 1960s, Lee and collaborators initiated the important field of high-energy [neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino "Neutrino") physics. In 1964, Lee, with [Michael Nauenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Nauenberg "Michael Nauenberg"), analyzed the divergences connected with particles of zero rest mass, and described a general method known as the [KLN theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLN_theorem "KLN theorem") for dealing with these divergences, which still plays an important role in contemporary work in [QCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chromodynamics "Quantum chromodynamics"), with its massless, self-interacting gluons. In 1974–75, Lee published several papers on "A New Form of Matter in High Density", which led to the modern field of RHIC physics, now dominating the entire high-energy [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics "Nuclear physics") field.

Besides [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics"), Lee was active in [statistical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics"), [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics "Astrophysics"), [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamics "Hydrodynamics"), many body system, solid state, and lattice QCD. In 1983, Lee wrote a paper entitled, "Can Time Be a Discrete Dynamical Variable?"; which led to a series of publications by Lee and collaborators on the formulation of fundamental physics in terms of difference equations, but with exact invariance under continuous groups of translational and rotational transformations. Beginning in 1975, Lee and collaborators established the field of [non-topological solitons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-topological_soliton "Non-topological soliton"), which led to his work on soliton stars and [black holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_holes "Black holes") throughout the 1980s and 1990s.

From 1997 to 2003, Lee was director of the [RIKEN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riken "Riken")-[BNL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven_National_Laboratory "Brookhaven National Laboratory") Research Center (now director emeritus), which together with other researchers from Columbia, completed a 1 teraflops supercomputer QCDSP for lattice QCD in 1998 and a 10 teraflops [QCDOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QCDOC "QCDOC") machine in 2005. Leading up to 2005, Lee and [Richard M. Friedberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_M._Friedberg "Richard M. Friedberg") developed a new method to solve the [Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_equation "Schrödinger equation"), leading to convergent iterative solutions for the long-standing quantum degenerate double-wall potential and other instanton problems. They also did work on the neutrino mapping matrix.

Lee was one of the 20 American recipients of the Nobel Prize in Physics to sign a letter addressed to President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") in May 2008, urging him to "reverse the damage done to basic science research in the Fiscal Year 2008 Omnibus Appropriations Bill" by requesting additional emergency funding for the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy "United States Department of Energy")'s [Office of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Science "Office of Science"), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation"), and the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology "National Institute of Standards and Technology").

## Educational activities

Soon after the re-establishment of China-American [relations with the PRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-American_relations "Sino-American relations"), Lee and his wife, Jeannette Hui-Chun Chin (秦惠䇹; *Qín Huìjūn*), were able to go to the PRC, where Lee gave a series of lectures and seminars, and organized the [CUSPEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUSPEA "CUSPEA") (China-U.S. Physics Examination and Application).

In 1998, Lee established the Chun-Tsung Endowment (秦惠䇹—李政道中国大学生见习基金) in memory of his wife, who had died three years earlier. The Chun-Tsung scholarships, supervised by the United Board for Christian Higher Education in Asia (New York), are awarded to undergraduates, usually in their 2nd or 3rd year, at six universities, which are [Shanghai Jiaotong University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Jiaotong_University "Shanghai Jiaotong University"), [Fudan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fudan_University "Fudan University"), [Lanzhou University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzhou_University "Lanzhou University"), [Soochow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soochow_University_(Suzhou) "Soochow University (Suzhou)"), [Peking University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_University "Peking University"), and [Tsinghua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsinghua_University "Tsinghua University"). Students selected for such scholarships are named "Chun-Tsung Scholars" (䇹政学者).

## Personal life and death

Lee and Jeannette Hui-Chun Chin (Chinese: 秦惠䇹; pinyin: Qín Huìjūn) married in 1950 and had two sons: James Lee ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 李中清; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): *Lǐ Zhōngqīng*) and [Stephen Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Lee_(chemist) "Stephen Lee (chemist)") ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 李中汉; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): *Lǐ Zhōnghàn*). His wife died in 1996. James donated his father's [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") to [Shanghai Jiao Tong University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Jiao_Tong_University "Shanghai Jiao Tong University") in 2014 where it is on display in the Tsung-Dao Lee Library.

Tsung-Dao Lee died in San Francisco on August 4, 2024, at the age of 97.
