**Charles-Édouard Guillaume** (French: [[ʃaʁl edwaʁ gijom]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 15 February 1861 – 13 June 1938) was a Swiss [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who received the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") in 1920 "for the service he had rendered to precision measurements in physics by his discovery of anomalies in nickel steel [alloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy "Alloy")." In 1919, he gave the fifth [Guthrie Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics_Michael_Faraday_Medal_and_Prize#Guthrie_lecturers "Institute of Physics Michael Faraday Medal and Prize") at the [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics "Institute of Physics") in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") with the title "The Anomaly of the Nickel-Steels."

## Personal life

Charles-Edouard Guillaume was born on 15 February 1861 in [Fleurier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleurier "Fleurier"), Switzerland, into a family of French descent. Guillaume received his early education in [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuchâtel "Neuchâtel"), and obtained his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in Physics from the Federal Polytechnic School in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich "Zurich") (now [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich")) in 1883.

In 1888, Guillaume married A. M. Taufflieb, with whom he had three children.

Guillaume died on 13 June 1938 in [Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sèvres "Sèvres"), Paris, at the age of 77. He was a Christian.

## Scientific career

Guillaume was head of the [International Bureau of Weights and Measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bureau_of_Weights_and_Measures "International Bureau of Weights and Measures"). He also worked with [Kristian Birkeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristian_Birkeland "Kristian Birkeland"), serving at the [Observatoire de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatoire_de_Paris "Observatoire de Paris") – Section de Meudon. He conducted several experiments with [thermostatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermostatics "Thermostatics") measurements at the observatory.

### Nickel–steel alloy

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/1918_Charles_%C3%89douard_Guillaume.jpg/250px-1918_Charles_%C3%89douard_Guillaume.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1918_Charles_Édouard_Guillaume.jpg)

1918 [Autochrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autochrome_Lumière "Autochrome Lumière") by [Auguste Léon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Archives_of_the_Planet "The Archives of the Planet")

Guillaume is known for his discovery of nickel–steel alloys he named [invar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invar "Invar"), [elinvar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elinvar "Elinvar") and [platinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinite?action=edit&redlink=1 "Platinite"), also known as red platinum. Invar has a near-zero [coefficient of thermal expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficient_of_thermal_expansion "Coefficient of thermal expansion"), making it useful in constructing precision instruments whose dimensions need to remain constant in spite of varying temperature. Elinvar has a near-zero thermal coefficient of the [modulus of elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulus_of_elasticity "Modulus of elasticity"), making it useful in constructing instruments with springs that need to be unaffected by varying temperature, such as the [marine chronometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_chronometer "Marine chronometer"). Elinvar is also non-magnetic, which is a secondary useful property for [antimagnetic watches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimagnetic_watch "Antimagnetic watch").

### Space radiation

Guillaume is also known for the *earliest* estimation of the "radiation of the stars” in his 1896 article *"La Température de L'Espace"* ("The Temperature of Space"). This publication made him a pioneer in [plasma cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_cosmology "Plasma cosmology"), the study of conditions far from any particular star. The concept was later known as the [Cosmic microwave background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_microwave_background "Cosmic microwave background"). He was one of the first people in history to estimate the temperature of space, as 5–6 [K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin "Kelvin").

### Horology

As the son of a Swiss [horologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horologist "Horologist"), Guillaume took an interest in [marine chronometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_chronometer "Marine chronometer"). For use as the [compensation balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compensation_balance "Compensation balance") he developed a slight variation of the invar alloy which had a negative quadratic coefficient of expansion. The purpose of doing this was to eliminate the "middle-temperature" error of the balance wheel. The *Guillaume balance* (a type of [balance wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_wheel "Balance wheel")) in [horology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horology "Horology") is named after him.
