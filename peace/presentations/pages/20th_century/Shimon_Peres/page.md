**Shimon Peres** ([/ʃiːˌmoʊn ˈpɛrɛs, -ɛz/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*shee-MOHN PERR-ess, -⁠ez*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): שמעון פרס [[ʃiˌmon ˈpeʁes]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Hebrew "Help:IPA/Hebrew") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:He-Shimon_Peres.ogg "File:He-Shimon Peres.ogg"); born **Szymon Perski**, Polish: [[ˈʂɨmɔn ˈpɛrskʲi]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Polish "Help:IPA/Polish"); 2 August 1923 – 28 September 2016) was an Israeli politician and statesman who served as the [prime minister of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Israel "Prime minister of Israel") from 1984 to 1986 and from 1995 to 1996 and as the [president of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Israel "President of Israel") from 2007 to 2014. He was a member of twelve [cabinets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Israel "Cabinet of Israel") and represented five political parties in a political career spanning 70 years. Peres was elected to the [Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knesset "Knesset") in November 1959 and except for three months out of office in early 2006, served as a member of the Knesset continuously until he was elected president in 2007. Serving in the Knesset for 48 years (with the first uninterrupted stretch lasting more than 46 years), Peres is the longest serving member in the Knesset's history. At the time of his retirement from politics in 2014, he was the world's oldest [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") and was considered the last link to Israel's founding generation, as well as the last Prime Minister to make [aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah "Aliyah") rather than being born on territory that would become Israel.

From a young age, he was renowned for his oratorical brilliance, and was chosen as a [protégé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protégé "Protégé") by [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion "David Ben-Gurion"), Israel's founding father. He began his political career in the late 1940s, holding several diplomatic and military positions during and directly after the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab–Israeli_War "1948 Arab–Israeli War"). His first high-level government position was as deputy director general of defense in 1952 which he attained at the age of 28, and [director general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_General_of_the_Israeli_Ministry_of_Defense "Director General of the Israeli Ministry of Defense") from 1953 until 1959. In 1956, he took part in the historic negotiations on the [Protocol of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_of_Sèvres "Protocol of Sèvres"), which was described by British prime minister [Anthony Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Eden "Anthony Eden") as the "highest form of statesmanship". In 1963, he held negotiations with U.S. president [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy"), which resulted in the sale of [Hawk anti-aircraft missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIM-23_Hawk "MIM-23 Hawk") to Israel, the first sale of U.S. military equipment to Israel. Peres represented [Mapai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapai "Mapai"), [Rafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafi_(political_party) "Rafi (political party)"), the [Alignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alignment_(Israel) "Alignment (Israel)"), [Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Party_(Israel) "Labor Party (Israel)") and [Kadima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadima "Kadima") in the Knesset, and led Alignment and Labor.

Peres first succeeded [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin "Yitzhak Rabin") as [acting prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acting_prime_minister "Acting prime minister") briefly during 1977, before becoming prime minister from 1984 to 1986. As [foreign minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_(Israel) "Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Israel)") under Prime Minister Rabin, Peres engineered the 1994 [Israel–Jordan peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel–Jordan_peace_treaty "Israel–Jordan peace treaty"), and won the 1994 [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") together with Rabin and [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat "Yasser Arafat") for the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords "Oslo Accords") peace talks with the [Palestinian leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization "Palestine Liberation Organization"). In 1996, he founded the [Peres Center for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peres_Center_for_Peace "Peres Center for Peace"), which has the aim of "promot[ing] lasting peace and advancement in the Middle East by fostering tolerance, economic and technological development, cooperation and well-being." Peres died in 2016 at [Sheba Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheba_Medical_Center "Sheba Medical Center") after suffering a stroke.

## Early life

Shimon Peres was born Szymon Perski, on 2 August 1923, in Wiszniew, [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic "Second Polish Republic") (now [Vishnyeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnyeva "Vishnyeva"), Belarus), to Yitzhak (1896–1982) and Sara (1903–1969, née Meltzer) Perski. The family spoke [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"), [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish_language "Yiddish language") and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language") at home, and Peres learned Polish at school. He then learned to speak English and French. His father was a wealthy timber merchant, later branching out into other commodities; his mother was a librarian. Peres had a younger brother, Gershon. He was related to the American film star [Lauren Bacall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_Bacall "Lauren Bacall") (born Betty Joan Perske), and they were described as first cousins, but Peres said, "In 1952 or 1953, I came to New York ... Lauren Bacall called me, said that she wanted to meet, and we did. We sat and talked about where our families came from, and discovered that we were from the same family ... but I'm not exactly sure what our relation is ... It was she who later said that she was my cousin; I didn't say that".

Shimon Peres (standing, third from right) with his family, c. 1930

Peres told Rabbi [Menachem Mendel Schneerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Mendel_Schneerson "Menachem Mendel Schneerson") that he had been born as a result of a blessing his parents had received from a [chassidic rebbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebbe "Rebbe") and that he was proud of it. Peres's grandfather, Rabbi Zvi Meltzer, a grandson of Rabbi [Chaim Volozhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaim_Volozhin "Chaim Volozhin"), had a great impact on his life. In an interview, Peres said: "As a child, I grew up in my grandfather's home. ... I was educated by him. ... My grandfather taught me [Talmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talmud "Talmud"). It was not as easy as it sounds. My home was not an observant one. My parents were not Orthodox but I was [Haredi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredi "Haredi"). At one point, I heard my parents listening to the radio on the Sabbath and I smashed it." When he was a child, Peres was taken by his father to [Radun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radun,_Belarus "Radun, Belarus") to receive a blessing from Rabbi [Yisrael Meir Kagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yisrael_Meir_Kagan "Yisrael Meir Kagan") (known as "the *Chofetz Chaim*"). As a child, Peres would later say, "I did not dream of becoming president of Israel. My dream as a boy was to be a shepherd or a poet of stars." He inherited his love of [French literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_literature "French literature") from his maternal grandfather.

In 1932, Peres's father immigrated to [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine "Mandatory Palestine") and settled in [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv "Tel Aviv"). The family followed him in 1934. He attended [Balfour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasia_Balfour "Gymnasia Balfour") Elementary School and High School, and Geula Gymnasium (High School for Commerce) in [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv "Tel Aviv"). At 15, he transferred to [Ben Shemen agricultural school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shemen_Youth_Village "Ben Shemen Youth Village") and lived on [Kibbutz Geva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geva "Geva") for several years.

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Peres' father enlisted in the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army "British Army") as a sapper in the [Royal Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Engineers "Royal Engineers") and was captured in [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece"), returning to Palestine after spending most of the war as a POW. During his captivity, he hid his Jewish identity and was an inmate at Camp E715, a subcamp for POWs that was part of the [Auschwitz concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp "Auschwitz concentration camp") complex. While imprisoned there he met [Charles Coward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Coward "Charles Coward") and may have helped him save Jewish Auschwitz inmates. After a failed escape attempt he was saved from execution by an Australian military chaplain. Peres' mother worked in a military parts factory and his future wife also served in the British Army as a nurse and truck driver. Peres by contrast showed more interest in local pioneering than the war effort, stating at the time that "the army and the war don't interest me." In 1941, he was elected Secretary of [HaNoar HaOved VeHaLomed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaNoar_HaOved_VeHaLomed "HaNoar HaOved VeHaLomed"), a [Labor Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Zionism "Labor Zionism") youth movement. He was one of the founding pioneers of kibbutz [Alumot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alumot "Alumot"), and did agricultural work while writing book reviews and newspaper opinion pieces. He also climbed [Masada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masada "Masada") in 1942.

At age 20, he was elected to the HaNoar HaOved VeHaLomed national secretariat, where he was only one of two [Mapai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapai "Mapai") party supporters, out of the 12 members. Three years later, he took over the movement and won a majority. The head of Mapai, [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion "David Ben-Gurion"), and [Berl Katznelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berl_Katznelson "Berl Katznelson") began to take an interest in him, and appointed him to Mapai's secretariat.

In 1944, Peres led an illicit expedition into the [Negev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev "Negev"), then a closed military zone requiring a permit to enter. The expedition, consisting of a group of teenagers, along with a [Palmach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmach "Palmach") scout, a zoologist, and an archaeologist, had been funded by Ben-Gurion and planned by Palmach head [Yitzhak Sadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Sadeh "Yitzhak Sadeh"), as part of a plan for future Jewish settlement of the area so as to include it in the Jewish state. The group was arrested by a [Bedouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedouin "Bedouin") camel patrol led by a British officer, taken to [Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beersheba "Beersheba") (then a small Arab town) and incarcerated in the local jail. All of the participants were sentenced to two weeks in prison, and as the leader, Peres was also heavily fined. The expedition came across a nest of [bearded vultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bearded_vulture "Bearded vulture"), called *peres* in Hebrew, and from this Peres took his Hebrew name.

All of Peres's relatives who remained in Wiszniew in 1941 were murdered during the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust"), many of them (including Rabbi Meltzer) burned alive in the town's synagogue.

In 1945, Peres married [Sonia Gelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonia_Peres "Sonia Peres"), who preferred to remain outside the public eye. They had three children.

In 1946, Peres and [Moshe Dayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Dayan "Moshe Dayan") were chosen as the two youth delegates in the Mapai delegation to the Zionist Congress in [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel "Basel").

In 1947, Peres joined the [Haganah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haganah "Haganah"), the predecessor of the [Israel Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces "Israel Defense Forces"). [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion "David Ben-Gurion") made him responsible for personnel and arms purchases; he was appointed to head the naval service when Israel received independence in 1948.

Peres was director of the Defense Ministry's delegation in the United States in the early 1950s. While in the U.S. he studied [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language"), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics"), and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy") at [The New School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_School "The New School") and [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University"), and completed a four-month advanced [management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management "Management") course at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University").

## Director General of Defense (1953–1959)

In 1952, Peres was appointed deputy director general of the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_(Israel) "Ministry of Defense (Israel)"), and the following year, he was promoted to [director general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_General_of_the_Israeli_Ministry_of_Defense "Director General of the Israeli Ministry of Defense"). At age 29, he was the youngest person to hold this position. He was involved in arms purchases and establishing strategic alliances that were important for the State of Israel. He was instrumental in establishing close relations with [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Fourth_Republic "French Fourth Republic"), securing massive amounts of quality arms that, in turn, helped to tip the balance of power in the region.

In 1955, he testified against Minister of Defense [Pinhas Lavon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinhas_Lavon "Pinhas Lavon") in what became known as the [Lavon Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavon_Affair "Lavon Affair").

Owing to Peres's mediation, Israel acquired the advanced [Dassault Mirage III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_III "Dassault Mirage III") French jet fighter, established the [Dimona nuclear reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimona_nuclear_reactor "Dimona nuclear reactor") and entered into a tri-national agreement with France and the United Kingdom, positioning Israel in what would become the 1956 [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis "Suez Crisis"). Peres continued as a primary intermediary in the close French-Israeli alliance from the mid-1950s, although from 1958, he was often involved in tense negotiations with [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle "Charles de Gaulle") over the Dimona project.

Peres is considered to have been the architect of Israel's secret [nuclear weapons program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_of_Israel "Nuclear weapons of Israel") in the 1960s, and he stated that, in the 1960s, he recruited [Arnon Milchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnon_Milchan "Arnon Milchan"), an Israeli-American Hollywood film producer, billionaire businessman, and secret arms dealer and intelligence operative, to work for the Israeli [Bureau of Scientific Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Scientific_Relations "Bureau of Scientific Relations") (LEKEM or LAKAM), a secret intelligence organization tasked with obtaining military technology and science espionage.

### 1956 Suez Crisis

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/King_of_Nepal%2C_Shimon_Peres_and_Ezer_Weizman_1963.jpg/250px-King_of_Nepal%2C_Shimon_Peres_and_Ezer_Weizman_1963.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:King_of_Nepal,_Shimon_Peres_and_Ezer_Weizman_1963.jpg)

Peres (center) with [Ezer Weizman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezer_Weizman "Ezer Weizman") (right) and [King Mahendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahendra_of_Nepal "Mahendra of Nepal") of [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nepal "Kingdom of Nepal") in 1958

From 1954, as director general of the Ministry of Defense, Peres was involved in the planning of the [1956 Suez War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Suez_War "1956 Suez War"), in partnership with France and Britain. Peres was sent by David Ben-Gurion to Paris, where he held secret meetings with the French government. Peres was instrumental in negotiating the Franco-Israeli agreement for a military offensive. In November 1954, Peres visited Paris, where he was received by the French defense minister [Marie-Pierre Kœnig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-Pierre_Kœnig "Marie-Pierre Kœnig"), who told him that France would sell Israel any weapons it wanted to buy. By early 1955, France was shipping large amounts of weapons to Israel. In April 1956, following another visit to Paris by Peres, France agreed to disregard the [Tripartite Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_Declaration_of_1950 "Tripartite Declaration of 1950"), and supply more weapons to Israel. During the same visit, Peres informed the French that Israel had decided upon war with Egypt in 1956. Throughout the 1950s, an extraordinarily close relationship existed between France and Israel, characterised by unprecedented cooperation in the fields of defense and diplomacy. For his work as the architect of this relationship, Peres was awarded the highest order of the French, the [Legion of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honor "Legion of Honor"), as Commander.

At [Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sèvres "Sèvres"), Peres took part in planning alongside [Maurice Bourgès-Maunoury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Bourgès-Maunoury "Maurice Bourgès-Maunoury"), [Christian Pineau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Pineau "Christian Pineau") and Chief of Staff of the French Armed Forces General [Maurice Challe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Challe "Maurice Challe"), and British [Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Affairs "Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs") [Selwyn Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selwyn_Lloyd "Selwyn Lloyd") and his assistant Sir [Patrick Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Dean_(diplomat) "Patrick Dean (diplomat)"). Britain and France enlisted Israeli support for an alliance against Egypt. The parties agreed that Israel would invade the Sinai. Britain and France would then intervene, purportedly to separate the warring Israeli and Egyptian forces, instructing both to withdraw to a distance of 16 kilometres from either side of the canal. The British and French would then argue, according to the plan, that Egypt's control of such an important route was too tenuous, and that it needed be placed under Anglo-French management. The agreement at Sèvres was initially described by British prime minister [Anthony Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Eden "Anthony Eden") as the "highest form of statesmanship". The three allies, especially Israel, were mainly successful in attaining their immediate military objectives. However, the extremely hostile reaction to the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis "Suez Crisis") from both the United States and the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR "USSR") forced them to withdraw, resulting in a failure of Britain and France's political and strategic aims of controlling the Suez Canal.

## Early Knesset career (1959–1974)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Levi_Eshkol_visits_Mamram.jpg/250px-Levi_Eshkol_visits_Mamram.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Levi_Eshkol_visits_Mamram.jpg)

Shimon Peres with [Yitzak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzak_Rabin "Yitzak Rabin") and [Levi Eshkol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Eshkol "Levi Eshkol") in 1964

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_Daily_walk_in_Kibbutz_Sde_Boker.jpg/250px-Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_Daily_walk_in_Kibbutz_Sde_Boker.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_(GPO)_-_Daily_walk_in_Kibbutz_Sde_Boker.jpg)

Peres accompanying [Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben-Gurion "Ben-Gurion") on his daily walk at Kibbutz [Sde Boker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sde_Boker "Sde Boker"). March 1969.

Peres was first elected to the [Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knesset "Knesset") in the [1959 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_Israeli_legislative_election "1959 Israeli legislative election") as a member of the [Mapai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapai "Mapai") party. He was given the role of deputy minister of defense, which he filled until 1965 (holding this position in the [9th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_government_of_Israel "Ninth government of Israel"), [10th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenth_government_of_Israel "Tenth government of Israel"), [11th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_government_of_Israel "Eleventh government of Israel"), and [12th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_government_of_Israel "Twelfth government of Israel") governments of Israel under Prime Ministers David Ben-Gurion and [Levi Eshkol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Eshkol "Levi Eshkol")). In this position, he held negotiations with [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy"), which concluded with the sale of Hawk anti-aircraft missiles to Israel, the first sale of US military equipment to Israel. Peres expressed great appreciation and praise for the American decision to sell Hawk missiles in a speech to the Knesset on 25 June 1963.

Peres resigned from the 12th government in late May 1965, citing the growing rift between Prime Minister Eshkol and former prime minister Ben-Gurion (Peres was aligned with Ben-Gurion). In 1965, Peres and Moshe Dayan were among those that left Mapai with David Ben-Gurion when Ben-Gurion formed a new party, [Rafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafi_(political_party) "Rafi (political party)"). Peres was a co-founder of the Rafi party. The party reconciled with Mapai in 1968, merging to form the [Israeli Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Labor_Party "Israeli Labor Party"). The Labor Party then joined the [Alignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alignment_(Israel) "Alignment (Israel)") (a left-wing alliance).

In 1969, Peres was appointed [minister of immigrant absorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant_Absorption_Minister_of_Israel "Immigrant Absorption Minister of Israel") in the [15th government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifteenth_government_of_Israel "Fifteenth government of Israel") (led by Prime Minister [Golda Meir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golda_Meir "Golda Meir")), and in 1970 (also in the 15th government), he became [minister of transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Minister_of_Israel "Transportation Minister of Israel") and [communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Communications_(Israel) "Ministry of Communications (Israel)"). After this, he served as [minister of information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Minister_of_Israel "Information Minister of Israel") in the Meir-led [16th government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixteenth_government_of_Israel "Sixteenth government of Israel").

## Minister of Defense (1974–1977)

Peres was appointed minister of defense in the [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin "Yitzhak Rabin")-led [17th government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventeenth_government_of_Israel "Seventeenth government of Israel"), after having been Rabin's chief rival for the post of Labor Party leader (and, in effect, the Israeli premiership) in the [1974 Israeli Labor Party leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election "1974 Israeli Labor Party leadership election") that was held after Golda Meir resigned in the aftermath of the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War "Yom Kippur War").

### 1976 Entebbe rescue operation

On 27 June 1976, Peres, as minister of defense, in collaboration with Prime Minister Rabin, handled Israel's response to a coordinated act of [terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism "Terrorism") when 248 Paris-bound travelers on an Air France plane were taken hostage by pro-Palestinian [hijackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_hijacking "Aircraft hijacking") and flown to [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda "Uganda"), Africa, 2,000 miles away.

Peres and Rabin were responsible for approving what became known as the "Operation Entebbe", which took place on 4 July 1976. The rescue boosted the Rabin government's approval rating with the public. The only Israeli soldier that was killed during the successful rescue operation was its commander, 30-year-old Lieutenant Colonel [Jonathan Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Netanyahu "Jonathan Netanyahu"), older brother of [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu "Benjamin Netanyahu").

Before Rabin ultimately approved the rescue mission, he and Peres were at odds on how to proceed. Rabin was open to acquiescing to the terrorists' demands to release forty Palestinian militants if no military option presented itself. Peres, however, felt acquiescing to be a nonstarter, believing it would encourage further terrorism. Rabin initially took steps to begin negotiations with the terrorists, seeing no other option. Peres felt that negotiating with terrorists would, in effect, be a surrender, and thought a rescue operation should be planned.

Peres organized a secret Israel Crisis Committee to come up with a rescue plan. When a plan had been made, he met with commander Netanyahu a number of times. During one of their final private meetings, they both examined maps and went over precise details. Peres later said of Netanyahu's explanation, "My impression was one of exactitude and imagination," saying that Netanyahu seemed confident the operation would succeed with almost no losses. Netanyahu left the meeting understanding that Peres would do everything in his power to see that the operation went smoothly. Peres then went unannounced to [Moshe Dayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Dayan "Moshe Dayan"), the former minister of Defense, interrupting his dinner with friends in a restaurant, to show him the latest plan to get his opinion. Peres told Dayan of the objections that had been raised by Rabin and chief of staff, [Mordechai Gur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordechai_Gur "Mordechai Gur"). Dayan dismissed the objections after reviewing the written details: "Shimon," he said, "this is a plan that I support not one hundred percent but one hundred and fifty percent! There has to be a military operation." Peres later got the approval from Gur, who became fully supportive. Peres then took the plan to Rabin, who had been lukewarm and still didn't like the risks, but he reluctantly approved the plan after Peres answered a number of key questions and Rabin learned that the cabinet had also endorsed it.

Shortly after the mission ended, Rabin recounted, "we called into my office seven of our top commanders...I told our friends in uniform that the honor of the Jewish people, their destinies, are challenged and what we are considering is not just a calculated risk in the military sense, but a comparative risk, which exists between surrender to terror and daring rescue stemming from independence."

After the success of the operation, Peres angled to receive some of the credit and adulation, somewhat competing with Rabin for credit.

### Unsuccessful February 1977 campaign for Labor Party leader

In February 1977, Peres challenged Prime Minister Rabin for the leadership of Labor Party, but lost to Rabin in a narrow 50.72% to 49.28% result in the vote by the party's Central Committee. The leadership election was expected to determine who would lead the party into the [1977 Knesset election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Knesset_election "1977 Knesset election"). This was at moment when Labor was threatened with the prospect of losing its control of government after 28-consecutive years due to the rise of both the [right-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing "Right-wing") [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud "Likud") bloc and the [centrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrism "Centrism") [Democratic Movement for Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Movement_for_Change "Democratic Movement for Change"), which were seen as collectively cutting into the Labor Party's support in the upcoming election. At the time, Rabin and Peres presented little policy difference, with Peres being seen as only slightly to the right of Rabin on domestic matters. Instead of positioning himself in contrast to the incumbent Rabin on policy, Peres instead capitalized off of the political atmosphere and staked his candidacy largely on an argument that the Labor Party needed to satisfy the nation's desire for change by choosing a new leader for itself.

## Unofficial acting premiership (1977)

On 7 April 1977, Prime Minister Rabin announced that, in the wake of a foreign currency scandal involving his wife, he would be stepping down prior to the 1977 Knesset election. Peres made himself a candidate to replace him as the new Labor Party leader. Initially, Foreign Minister [Yigal Allon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yigal_Allon "Yigal Allon") also made himself a candidate. However, Allon and Peres reached an agreement that Peres would appoint Allon to any ministerial position that Allon preferred in exchange from his withdrawal of his candidacy. Following Allon's withdrawal, the Labor Party leadership announced on 10 April 1977 that he had chosen to endorse Peres as the party's new leader. [On 11 April 1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_1977_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election "April 1977 Israeli Labor Party leadership election"), the 815-member Central Committee of the party elected Peres by [acclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acclamation "Acclamation") as the party's new leader.

Rabin ended his active service as prime minister on 22 April 1977, and Peres became Israel's unofficial acting prime minister. The reason why Peres was not officially the holder of this office was that Rabin could not, under Israeli law, resign from his position as prime minister because the government was, at the time, a [caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caretaker_government "Caretaker government").

In his first election as party leader, Peres led Labor Party and the Alignment coalition to its first ever electoral defeat, and the result afforded the first-place Likud party (led by [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin "Menachem Begin")) the ability to form a coalition that excluded the left. When the new Likud-led government was formed on 20 June 1977 Peres' time as the unofficial acting prime minister ended.

## Labor in opposition (1977–1984)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_Menahem_Begin_and_Shimon_Peres.jpg/250px-Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_Menahem_Begin_and_Shimon_Peres.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_(GPO)_-_Menahem_Begin_and_Shimon_Peres.jpg)

Prime Minister [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin "Menachem Begin") and Chairman of the [Alignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alignment_(Israel) "Alignment (Israel)") Shimon Peres at the ceremony held by President [Yitzhak Navon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Navon "Yitzhak Navon") after the inaugural session of the [10th Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Knesset "10th Knesset"), July 1981

Once the Likud-led government took power, Labor and the Alignment bloc entered the Knesset opposition for the first time in its history, and Peres assumed the unofficial role of Knesset opposition leader.

In 1978, Peres was elected vice president of [Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_International "Socialist International"). Through his role within the leadership of this organization, Peres befriended foreign politicians including [Willy Brandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Brandt "Willy Brandt"), [Bruno Kreisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Kreisky "Bruno Kreisky"), members of the [British Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Labour_Party "British Labour Party"), and politicians from parts of Africa and Asia.

After handily winning reelection as Labor Party leader [in 1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election "1980 Israeli Labor Party leadership election") (defeating a challenge from Rabin, who was attempting a comeback to the leadership), Peres led his party to another, narrower, loss in the [1981 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Israeli_legislative_election "1981 Israeli legislative election").

## Grand coalition governments and first premiership (1984–1988)

In the [1984 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Israeli_legislative_election "1984 Israeli legislative election") (Peres' third election as Labor Party leader), the Alignment coalition won more seats than any other party, but the left-wing failed to win the majority of 61 seats needed to form a coalition on their own. Alignment and Likud agreed to an unusual "[rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_government "Rotation government")" arrangement to form a [grand coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition "Grand coalition") unity government during which, for the first twenty-five months, Peres would serve as prime minister and the Likud leader [Yitzhak Shamir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Shamir "Yitzhak Shamir") would be foreign minister, with the two swapping positions thereafter for the second half of the term.

### First premiership (1984–1986)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_ETHIOPIAN_IMMIGRANTS_DEMONSTRATING.jpg/250px-Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_ETHIOPIAN_IMMIGRANTS_DEMONSTRATING.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_(GPO)_-_ETHIOPIAN_IMMIGRANTS_DEMONSTRATING.jpg)

Prime Minister Peres delivers a speech in front of [Ethiopian Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Jews_in_Israel "Ethiopian Jews in Israel") immigrants on 2 October 1985.

Peres was regarded to be a popular prime minister in his two years as premier under the rotation arrangement. During a portion of his premiership, he also held the position of [minister of religious affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Religious_Services "Ministry of Religious Services").

#### Military policy and international relations

Among the most noteworthy moments of his first tenure as prime minister was [Operation Wooden Leg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Wooden_Leg "Operation Wooden Leg") (a long-range Israeli airstrike against the [PLO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLO "PLO") headquarters in Tunisia) and a trip to [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco "Morocco") to confer with [King Hassan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_II_of_Morocco "Hassan II of Morocco").

In 1985, Peres publicly supported the quick pursuit of a military pullback from [Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut "Beirut") to Israel's south Lebanon security belt. A partial Israeli pullback had earlier been approved in 1983 by the Begin-headed Likud-led government that had been in power at that time.

#### 1985 Israel Economic Stabilization Plan

A major domestic policy decision of Peres' first premiership was the implementation of the 1985 Israel Economic Stabilization Plan.

By 1985, Israel's economic fortunes were looking dire, with immense and quickly rising [inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation "Inflation") (Israel was experiencing hyperinflation), a government budget deficit equal to between twelve and fifteen percent of the nation's [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP "GDP") and [national debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_debt "National debt") equal to 220% of the nation's GDP, and Israel's foreign currency reserves were quickly dissipating. With the assistance of the government of the United States, Peres assembled a board of American economist to advise him on the situation. Conditional on him implementing reforms, Peres secured emergency economic assistance from the United States of $750 million (equivalent to 3.5% of the nation's GDP at the time) annually over a two-year period.

Peres was initially hesitant to take the drastic measures that he ultimately would pursue, as they had the strong potential of proving unpopular and came with a risk of potentially creating a drastic increase in unemployment. Peres ultimately was convinced to push through the 1985 Israel Economic Stabilization Plan. Once convinced, he was assertive in pushing for the passage of the program, which was quickly after approved by his cabinet on 1 July 1985. This program had quick success in improving the course of the Israeli economy. By the end of the year, inflation immensely decreased. Additionally, the [shekel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shekel "Shekel") stabilized and the government balanced its budget. While Israel would subsequently slide into a [recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recession "Recession"), the stabilization has been regarded as an important and greatly successful model for addressing economies in crisis, and has been credited with saving the nation's economy.

### Minister of Foreign Affairs (1986–1988)

As per the rotation arrangement, after Peres' two years as prime minister he and Shamir traded places in 1986. Shamir became prime minister of the new [twenty-second government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-second_government_of_Israel "Twenty-second government of Israel") and Peres became foreign minister as well as the [designated acting prime minister of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designated_acting_prime_minister_of_Israel "Designated acting prime minister of Israel").

### Minister of Finance (1988–1990)

In 1988 the Alignment, led by Peres, suffered another [narrow defeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Israeli_legislative_election "1988 Israeli legislative election"). This came despite the fact that polling in 1988 showed Peres to be the most popular politician in the nation. Peres agreed to renew the grand coalition with the Likud, this time conceding the premiership to Shamir for the entire term. In the grand coalition unity government of 1988–90 (the [twenty-third government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-third_government_of_Israel "Twenty-third government of Israel")), Peres served as [minister of finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_Minister_of_Israel "Finance Minister of Israel") and also continued to be the designated acting prime minister of Israel.

## Return to opposition (1990–1992)

### "The dirty trick"

Peres and the Alignment finally left the government in 1990, after "[the dirty trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_dirty_trick_(Israel) "The dirty trick (Israel)")" – a failed bid by Peres to form a narrow government based on a coalition of the Alignment, small leftist factions and [ultra-orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredi_Judaism "Haredi Judaism") parties. Peres' hope had been to create a Labor-led government that would be focused on peace talks with Palestine. Likud had declined proposals by the United States for Israel and Palestine to initiate what would have been the first peace talks between the two sides. Peres' longtime intra-party rival, Yizhak Rabin, had opposed to overthrowing the Likud-led coalition government. Peres succeeded in ending the government twenty-third government with a [vote of no confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_of_no_confidence "Vote of no confidence"). However, Peres was subsequently unable to assemble enough Knesset partners to form a pro-peace talk government. After two months, Shamir managed to form a Likud-led government with right-wing religious parties, establishing what was seen as the most conservative government coalition in the history of Israel up to that point.

### Defeat in the 1992 Labor Party leadership election

Peres led the opposition in the [Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knesset "Knesset") from 1990 until early 1992, when he was defeated by Yitzhak Rabin in [the Israeli Labor Party leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election "1992 Israeli Labor Party leadership election"), the first leadership election held since the party formally merged with the other parties of Alignment, and the first leadership election open to participation by the party's entire membership. Peres remained active in politics, however.

## Rabin government (1992–1995)

After the Labor Party was successful in the [1992 Knesset election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Knesset_election "1992 Knesset election") and Rabin became prime minister again, Peres became foreign minister in the Rabin-led [twenty-fifth government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_government_of_Israel "Twenty-fifth government of Israel"). Peres had previously served as foreign minister from 1986 through 1988.

### Israel–Jordan peace treaty

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_Foreign_Min._Peres_and_King_Hussein.jpg/250px-Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_Foreign_Min._Peres_and_King_Hussein.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_(GPO)_-_Foreign_Min._Peres_and_King_Hussein.jpg)

Shimon Peres (left) with [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin "Yitzhak Rabin") (center) and [King Hussein of Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hussein_of_Jordan "King Hussein of Jordan") (right), prior to signing the [Israel–Jordan peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel–Jordan_peace_treaty "Israel–Jordan peace treaty")

On 26 October 1994, Jordan and Israel signed the [Israel–Jordan peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel–Jordan_peace_treaty "Israel–Jordan peace treaty"), which had been initiated by Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin and Foreign Minister Shimon Peres. The ceremony was held in the Arava valley of Israel, north of [Eilat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilat "Eilat") and near the Jordanian border. Prime Minister Rabin and Prime Minister Abdelsalam al-Majali signed the treaty and the [president of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Israel "President of Israel") [Ezer Weizman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezer_Weizman "Ezer Weizman") shook hands with [King Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_of_Jordan "Hussein of Jordan"). United States president [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") observed, accompanied by United States Secretary of State [Warren Christopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Christopher "Warren Christopher"), as well as the foreign ministers of eleven other nations (including Russia, which had joined the United States as a formal co-sponsor of the peace talks that led to the treaty). The treaty brought an end to 46 years of official war between Israel and [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan "Jordan"). It was only the second full peace agreement that Israel had reached with an Arab nation, after the [Camp David Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_David_Accords "Camp David Accords") signed with [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") in 1978.

### Oslo peace process with Palestine

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Peres_signing_Oslo_I_%281%29.jpg/250px-Peres_signing_Oslo_I_%281%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Peres_signing_Oslo_I_(1).jpg)

Peres signing Oslo I on 13 September 1993

Rabin's 1992 campaign for Labor had primarily been run on the idea of negotiating peace with the Palestinians. This campaign had succeeded as a peaceful resolution to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict was popular among the Israeli public at the time. The twenty-fifth government of Israel was arguably more pro-peace government than any previous Israeli government. It would begin negotiations with the [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat "Yasser Arafat")-led [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization "Palestine Liberation Organization") (PLO).

Peres was involved in secret peace negotiations between Prime Minister Rabin's government and Arafat's PLO organization. These negotiations were held over several months in 1992 and 1993. As part of the negotiations, Peres secretly flew to [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo "Oslo"), Norway on 19 August 1993. The ultimate agreement outlined a peace process between Israel and Palestine, which would include the establishment of an interim Palestinian government within both the [Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip "Gaza Strip") and the [West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank "West Bank"). On 13 September 1993, Peres signed the initial [Oslo I Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_I_Accord "Oslo I Accord") on behalf of the Israeli government in a ceremony at the United States' [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House"), with Rabin in attendance.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_THE_NOBEL_PEACE_PRIZE_LAUREATES_FOR_1994_IN_OSLO..jpg/250px-Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_THE_NOBEL_PEACE_PRIZE_LAUREATES_FOR_1994_IN_OSLO..jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_(GPO)_-_THE_NOBEL_PEACE_PRIZE_LAUREATES_FOR_1994_IN_OSLO..jpg)

[Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin "Yitzhak Rabin"), Shimon Peres and [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat "Yasser Arafat") receiving the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") following the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords "Oslo Accords")

In 1994, in recognition of the Oslo Accords, Peres, Rabin and Arafat were jointly awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"). This was the [second (and most recent) instance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_Peace_Prize_laureates "List of Nobel Peace Prize laureates") in which an Israeli was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize. Then-Prime Minister [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin "Menachem Begin") had previously jointly received the honor with Egyptian president [Anwar Sadat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_Sadat "Anwar Sadat") in 1978. This was also the second time that the award had been given in recognition of middle east peacemaking efforts, with the 1978 award having been the previous instance of this. The awarding of the prize to the three has not been without controversy. After it was decided they would be given the award, [Kåre Kristiansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kåre_Kristiansen "Kåre Kristiansen") resigned from the Nobel Peace Prize committee in protest of Arafat receiving the award, believing Arafat to be, "too tainted by violence, terror and torture". In 2002, a number of members of the Norwegian committee that awards the annual Nobel Peace Prize would state they regretted that Mr. Peres's prize could not be recalled. Because he had not acted to prevent Israel's re-occupation of Palestinian territory, he had not lived up to the ideals he expressed when he accepted the prize, and he was involved in human rights abuses.

Negotiations on further terms continued, with Peres continuing to be an integral player. On 28 September 1995, Rabin and Arafat jointly signed a second major agreement, which has popularly been referred to as "[Oslo II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II "Oslo II")".

## Second premiership (1995–1996)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Channel2_-_Yitzhak_Rabin.webm_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Channel2_-_Yitzhak_Rabin.webm_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Channel2_-_Yitzhak_Rabin.webm_(cropped).jpg)

Peres (far left) and Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin (far right) during the 4 November 1995 peace rally at which Rabin [was assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Yitzhak_Rabin "Assassination of Yitzhak Rabin")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_Peres_and_Clinton.jpg/250px-Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_%28GPO%29_-_Peres_and_Clinton.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Flickr_-_Government_Press_Office_(GPO)_-_Peres_and_Clinton.jpg)

Shimon Peres with U.S. president [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") at the White House, April 1996

While the Oslo peace policies, at the time, enjoyed the support of most Israelis, they also faced intense opposition from extreme members of Israel's right-wing. In response to intense street protests by right-wing opponents of the Oslo peace process, a coalition of left-wing parties and peace groups organized a rally in support of the peace process in [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv "Tel Aviv")'s [Kings Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabin_Square "Rabin Square") on 4 November 1995, which both Prime Minister Rabin and Peres attended. While making his way from the stage to his car after concluding his speech to the gathered crowd of more than 100,000 people, Rabin [was assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Yitzhak_Rabin "Assassination of Yitzhak Rabin") by [Yigal Amir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yigal_Amir "Yigal Amir"), a right-wing Israeli Jew who opposed the peace process.

After Rabin's assassination, Peres was made acting prime minister and acting defense minister of a provisional government. On 14 November 1995, the Labor Party [confirmed Peres as its new leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election "1995 Israeli Labor Party leadership election"), which thereby cleared the last formality before he could be invited by President [Ezer Weizman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezer_Weizman "Ezer Weizman") to form a new government. On 15 November 1995, Peres was invited by to form a new government. On 21 November, Peres signed a coalition agreement between Labor, [Meretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meretz "Meretz") and [Yiud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiud "Yiud") (which had been members of [Rabin's government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_government_of_Israel "Twenty-fifth government of Israel")), which was formally approved by the Knesset the next day, establishing a new government with Peres as prime minister.

Peres' second stint as prime minister ultimately lasted a total of seven months. During this time, he attempted to maintain the momentum of the peace process.

On 10 February 1996, Peres made the widely expected announcement that he would call early elections, moving the elections to late May, five months earlier than they otherwise were to be held. The election would be the first to use a new system in which the prime minister was directly elected in a vote coinciding with the Knesset election. Peres had hoped that early elections would deliver a [mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_(politics) "Mandate (politics)") for his pursuit of a two-state solution. Peres had called the elections early because of promising polls. He was heavily leading in the polls for the prime minister vote at the time the election was called, with polls showing him to have between a twenty and twenty-five percent lead. Additionally, Labor was also leading in polls for the Knesset vote. Despite the promising polls, however, some in Labor had, even at this time, expressed concerns about the ability of Peres to win, given his failure to deliver an outright win for the Labor Party during his earlier stint as party leader. His lead in the polls began to decrease after the [Jaffa Road bus bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa_Road_bus_bombings "Jaffa Road bus bombings") on 25 February 1996. However, even in the last month before the election, he enjoyed a reduced leading margin of around five percent.

On 11 April 1996, Peres initiated [Operation Grapes of Wrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Grapes_of_Wrath "Operation Grapes of Wrath"), which was triggered by [Hezbollah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbollah "Hezbollah") [Katyusha rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyusha_rockets "Katyusha rockets") fired into Israel in response to the killing of two Lebanese by an IDF missile. Israel conducted massive air raids and extensive shelling in southern Lebanon. 106 Lebanese civilians died in the [shelling of Qana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_shelling_of_Qana "1996 shelling of Qana"), when a UN compound was hit in an Israeli shelling.

During his term, Peres promoted the use of the internet in Israel and created the first website of an Israeli prime minister.

## Post-premiership (1996–2007)

### Labor in opposition (1996–1999)

Peres was narrowly defeated by Benjamin Netanyahu in the [1996 Israeli prime ministerial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Israeli_prime_ministerial_election "1996 Israeli prime ministerial election"). Not included in the new government, Labor became an opposition party again. This again placed Peres in the then-unofficial role of Knesset opposition leader.

In 1996, Peres founded the [Peres Center for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peres_Center_for_Peace "Peres Center for Peace"), which has the aim of "promot[ing] lasting peace and advancement in the Middle East by fostering tolerance, economic and technological development, cooperation and well-being."

Peres did not seek re-election as Labor Party leader [in 1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election "1997 Israeli Labor Party leadership election") and was replaced by [Ehud Barak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehud_Barak "Ehud Barak") that year. Barak rebuffed Peres' attempt to secure the position of party president.

### Minister of Regional Cooperation (1999–2001)

In 1999, Ehud Barak [was elected prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Israeli_prime_ministerial_election "1999 Israeli prime ministerial election") and formed a Labor-lead government. He appointed Peres (who was seen as a political rival of Barak) to the minor post of [minister of regional cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Development_Minister_of_Israel "Regional Development Minister of Israel"). The position was vaguely defined, being expected to be tasked with advancing economic and political ties between Israel and the Arab world. The position also did not come with any government funding. Peres accepted the relatively low-ranked position reluctantly. For nearly all of time in this position Peres was sidelined, finding himself disallowed from playing a major role within the government.

On 1 November 2000, amid the [Second Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Intifada "Second Intifada"), Peres met in the Gaza Strip with Arafat on behalf of the Israeli government. The two agreed to terms of a [truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truce "Truce") in the early hours of the following morning.

After the resignation of [Ezer Weizman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezer_Weizman "Ezer Weizman"), Peres ran in the [2000 Israeli presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Israeli_presidential_election "2000 Israeli presidential election"), seeking to be elected by members of the Knesset to a seven-year term as [Israel's president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Israel "President of Israel") (a ceremonial [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") position which usually authorizes the selection of Prime Minister). However, he lost to Likud candidate [Moshe Katsav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Katsav "Moshe Katsav"). Katsav's victory was regarded to be in reaction to the perceived indications that Peres intended to use the presidency to provide his support to the increasingly unpopular peace processes that Barak's government was pursuing. His defeat was considered a significant [upset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upset_(competition) "Upset (competition)"), as he had been regarded as a heavy [front-runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front-runner "Front-runner") to win the Knesset vote. The [editorial board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editorial_board "Editorial board") of the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")* wrote that his defeat appeared to spell the end of Peres' long political career.

There was consideration given later that year to Peres potentially seeking the premiership again. On 20 November 2000, amid polls showing him to be in a virtual-tie with Ariel Sharon, an aide of Peres told the media that he would run in [the 2001 direct election for prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Israeli_prime_ministerial_election "2001 Israeli prime ministerial election"). Peres himself told lawmakers that he intended to run. Despite this, Peres did not become a candidate. In January 2001, there was some talk among Cabinet members that it would be best for Peres to be the candidate of the left. However, this did not happen. In early January 2001, in a joint television appearance with Barak that promoted the government's intent to work towards peace, Peres told the media that his own goal was, "not to become prime minister", but was instead, "to do the best for the state of Israel."

### Minister of Foreign Affairs (2001–2002)

Following Barak's defeat by [Ariel Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Sharon "Ariel Sharon") in the 2001 direct election for prime minister, Peres made yet another comeback. He helped with bringing Labor into a grand coalition unity government with Sharon's Likud (the [Twenty-ninth government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-ninth_government_of_Israel "Twenty-ninth government of Israel")) and secured himself the post of foreign minister. [In 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election "2001 Israeli Labor Party leadership election"), formal leadership of Labor passed to [Binyamin Ben-Eliezer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binyamin_Ben-Eliezer "Binyamin Ben-Eliezer"), and [in 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election "2002 Israeli Labor Party leadership election") it was passed to [Amram Mitzna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amram_Mitzna "Amram Mitzna"). Peres grew to be heavily criticized by many on the left, who accused him of clinging to his position as foreign minister in a government that was not seen as advancing the peace process. Left critics accused Peres of not acting in accordance with his own dovish stance. Peres resigned from his post as foreign minister when Labor left the unity government in advance of the [2003 Knesset election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Knesset_election "2003 Knesset election"). Labor's departure from the unity government placed Labor in the opposition ahead of the 2003 Knesset election.

### Interim Labor Party leader (2003–2005)

After the Labor Party suffered a crushing defeat in the 2003 Knesset election while under the leadership of Mitzna, Peres was made interim leader of the party [on 19 June 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Israeli_Labor_Party_interim_leadership_election "2003 Israeli Labor Party interim leadership election").

#### Vice Prime Minister (2005)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Shimon_Peres_2005.jpg/250px-Shimon_Peres_2005.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Shimon_Peres_2005.jpg)

Peres in 2005

Peres led the Labor Party into a coalition with Sharon once more, reaching an agreement the end of 2004, and entering the party into the [thirtieth government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirtieth_government_of_Israel "Thirtieth government of Israel") in January 2005. This came after the Sharon's support of "disengagement" from Gaza presented a diplomatic program that Labor could support. Sharon made Peres [vice prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_of_the_Prime_Minister_of_Israel "Deputy of the Prime Minister of Israel").

As interim party leader, Peres favored putting off the elections for as long as possible. He claimed that an early election would jeopardize both the September 2005 Gaza withdrawal plan and the standing of the party in a national unity government with Sharon. However, the majority pushed for an earlier date, as younger members of the party, among them [Amir Peretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Peretz "Amir Peretz"), [Ophir Pines-Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophir_Pines-Paz "Ophir Pines-Paz") and [Isaac Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Herzog "Isaac Herzog"), overtook established leaders such as [Binyamin Ben-Eliezer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binyamin_Ben-Eliezer "Binyamin Ben-Eliezer") and [Haim Ramon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haim_Ramon "Haim Ramon") in the party ballot to divide up government portfolios.

### Loss of Labor Party leadership and defection to Kadima

Peres lost a bid for permanent leadership of the Labor Party to [Amir Peretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Peretz "Amir Peretz") in [the November 2005 leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Israeli_Labor_Party_leadership_election "2005 Israeli Labor Party leadership election"), held in advance of the [2006 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Israeli_legislative_election "2006 Israeli legislative election"). Peres received 40% of the vote to Peretz's 42.4%.

Labor withdrew from the unity government on 23 November 2005. On 30 November 2005 Peres announced that he was leaving the Labor Party to support Ariel Sharon and his new [Kadima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadima "Kadima") party. In the immediate aftermath of Sharon's debilitating [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke "Stroke") days later (which left Sharon in a [coma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coma "Coma")), there was speculation that Peres might take over as leader of the Kadima party; most senior Kadima leaders, however, were former members of [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud "Likud") and indicated their support for [Ehud Olmert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehud_Olmert "Ehud Olmert") as Sharon's successor. Labor reportedly tried to woo Peres to rejoin them. However, he announced that he supported Olmert and would remain with Kadima. Peres had previously announced his intention not to run in the [March 2006 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Israeli_legislative_election "2006 Israeli legislative election"), but changed his mind.

Peres resigned from the Knesset on 15 January 2006 due both to [Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Israel "Attorney General of Israel") [Menahem Mazuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menahem_Mazuz "Menahem Mazuz") issuing a decision that ruled Peres and several others could not be appointed to ministerial posts by Prime Minister Olmert and because of a law that, due to him having switched parties, would have prevented him from running for the next Knesset if he remained an incumbent member of the Knesset. By that time, he had served in the Knesset for more than forty-six consecutive years.

Peres was soon elected back to the Knesset in the 2006 election, this time as a member of Kadima. After the new Kadima-led [thirty-first government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-first_government_of_Israel "Thirty-first government of Israel") was formed, Peres was given the role of vice prime minister and [minister for the development of the Negev, Galilee and Regional Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_the_Negev_and_Galilee_Minister_of_Israel "Development of the Negev and Galilee Minister of Israel").

On 13 June 2007, Peres was [elected president of the State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Israeli_presidential_election "2007 Israeli presidential election") by the Knesset. 58 of 120 members of the Knesset voted for him in the first round (whereas 38 voted for [Reuven Rivlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuven_Rivlin "Reuven Rivlin"), and 21 for [Colette Avital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colette_Avital "Colette Avital")). His opponents then backed Peres in the second round and 86 members of the Knesset voted in his favor, while 23 objected. He resigned from his role as a member of the Knesset the same day, having been a member since November 1959 (except for a three-month period in early 2006), the longest serving in Israeli political history. Peres was sworn in as president on 15 July 2007.

> Israel must not only be an asset but a value. A moral, cultural and scientific call for the promotion of man, every man. It must be a good and warm home for Jews who are not Israelis, as well as for Israelis who are not Jews. And it must create equal opportunities for all, without discriminating between religion, nationality, community or sex... I have seen Israel in its most difficult hours and also in moments of achievement and spiritual uplifting. My years place me at an observation point from which can be viewed the scene of our reviving nation, spread out in all its glory... Permit me to remain an optimist. Permit me to be a dreamer of his people. If sometimes the atmosphere is autumnal, and also if today, the day seems suddenly grey, the president Israel has chosen will never tire of encouraging, awakening and reminding - because spring is waiting for us. The spring will definitely come.

— Shimon Peres, President's inaugural address, July 2007

On 20 November 2008, Peres received an honorary knighthood, [Knight Grand Cross of the Order of St Michael and St George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St_Michael_and_St_George "Order of St Michael and St George") from [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II") in [Buckingham Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Palace "Buckingham Palace") in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London").

In June 2011, he was awarded the honorary title of *[sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh "Sheikh")* by Bedouin dignitaries in [Hura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hura "Hura") for his efforts to achieve Middle East peace. Peres thanks his hosts by saying "This visit has been a pleasure. I am deeply impressed by Hura. You have done more for yourselves than anyone else could have". He told the mayor of Hura, [Dr. Muhammad Al-Nabari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Muhammad_Al-Nabari "Dr. Muhammad Al-Nabari"), and members of Hura's governing council, that they were "part of the Negev. It cannot be developed without developing the Bedouin community, so that it may keep its traditions while joining the modern world."

## Post-presidency and death

Peres announced in April 2013 that he would not seek to extend his tenure beyond 2014. His successor, [Reuven Rivlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuven_Rivlin "Reuven Rivlin"), was elected on 10 June 2014 and took office on 24 July 2014.

In July 2016, Peres founded the 'Israel innovation center' in the Arab neighbourhood of [Ajami, Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajami,_Jaffa "Ajami, Jaffa"), aiming to encourage young people from around the world to be inspired by technology.

On 13 September 2016, Peres suffered a severe [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke "Stroke") and was hospitalized at [Sheba Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheba_Medical_Center "Sheba Medical Center"). His condition was reported to be very serious, as he had suffered a massive brain hemorrhage and significant bleeding. Two days later, he was reported as being in a serious but stable condition. However, on 26 September, an examination found irreversible damage to his [brainstem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brainstem "Brainstem"), indicating that it was not possible for him to recover, and the following day, his medical condition deteriorated significantly. He died on 28 September at the age of 93.

### Tributes

On hearing of his death, tributes came from leaders across the world. The president of Russia, [Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin "Vladimir Putin") said: "I was extremely lucky to have met this extraordinary man many times. And every time I admired his courage, patriotism, wisdom, vision and ability." The president of China, [Xi Jinping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_Jinping "Xi Jinping") said: "His death is the loss of an old friend for China." The president of India, [Pranab Mukherjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pranab_Mukherjee "Pranab Mukherjee") said: "Peres would be remembered as a steadfast friend of India." The President of the United States, Barack Obama said: "I will always be grateful that I was able to call Shimon my friend."

Peres was described by *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* as having done "more than anyone to build up his country's formidable military might, then [having] worked as hard to establish a lasting peace with Israel's Arab neighbors."

### Funeral

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1c/%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%A8_%D7%A9%D7%9E%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%9F_%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%A1.jpg/250px-%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%A8_%D7%A9%D7%9E%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%9F_%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%A1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:קבר_שמעון_פרס.jpg)

Peres's grave on the Great Leaders of the Nation section of Mount Herzl

The funeral was held at [Mount Herzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Herzl "Mount Herzl") in Jerusalem on 30 September 2016, with his burial place in the Great Leaders of the Nation section between former Israeli prime ministers [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin "Yitzhak Rabin") and [Yitzhak Shamir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Shamir "Yitzhak Shamir").

About 4,000 mourners and world leaders from 75 countries attended the funeral, with President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") among those who gave a eulogy. Since the funeral for [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela "Nelson Mandela"), this was only the second time Obama traveled overseas for the funeral of a foreign leader. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu also spoke. Among the other delegates in attendance and speaking were former president [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton"). ## Political views

Peres described himself as a "Ben-Gurionist", after his mentor Ben-Gurion. He felt that Jewish sovereignty in the [Land of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Israel "Land of Israel") was a means to a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism "Progressivism") end in which the State of Israel both inspire the world and survive in a region of the world where it was unwelcome.

As a younger man, Peres was once considered a "[hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_hawk "War hawk")". He was a protégé of Ben-Gurion and Dayan and an early supporter of the West Bank settlers during the 1970s. However, after becoming the leader of his party his stance evolved. Subsequently, he was seen as a dove, and a strong supporter of peace through economic cooperation. While still opposed, like all mainstream Israeli leaders in the 1970s and early 1980s, to talks with the [PLO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLO "PLO"), he distanced himself from settlers and spoke of the need for "territorial compromise" over the West Bank and Gaza. For a time he hoped that [King Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_of_Jordan "Hussein of Jordan") of Jordan could be Israel's Arab negotiating partner rather than [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat "Yasser Arafat"). Peres met secretly with Hussein in London in 1987 and reached a framework [agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peres-Hussein_London_Agreement "Peres-Hussein London Agreement") with him, but this was rejected by Israel's then Prime Minister, [Yitzhak Shamir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Shamir "Yitzhak Shamir"). Shortly afterward the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada "First Intifada") erupted, and whatever plausibility King Hussein had as a potential Israeli partner in resolving the fate of the West Bank evaporated. Subsequently, Peres gradually moved closer to support for talks with the PLO, although he avoided making an outright commitment to this policy until 1993.

Peres was perhaps more closely associated with the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords "Oslo Accords") than any other Israeli politician (Rabin included) with the possible exception of his own protégé, [Yossi Beilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yossi_Beilin "Yossi Beilin"). He remained an adamant supporter of the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords "Oslo Accords") and the [Palestinian Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Authority "Palestinian Authority") since their inception despite the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada "First Intifada") and the [al-Aqsa Intifada (Second Intifada)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Intifada "Second Intifada"). However, Peres supported [Ariel Sharon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Sharon "Ariel Sharon")'s military policy of operating the [Israel Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces "Israel Defense Forces") to thwart [suicide bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_bombing "Suicide bombing").

Peres's foreign policy outlook was markedly realist. To placate Turkey, Peres downplayed the [Armenian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide "Armenian genocide"). Peres stated: "We reject attempts to create a similarity between the Holocaust and the Armenian allegations. Nothing similar to the Holocaust occurred. It is a tragedy what the Armenians went through but not a genocide." Although Peres himself did not retract the statement, the Israeli Foreign Ministry later issued a cable to its missions which stated that "The minister absolutely did not say, as the Turkish news agency alleged, 'What the Armenians underwent was a tragedy, not a genocide.'" However, according to Armenian news agencies, the statement released by the Israeli consulate in Los Angeles did not include any mention that Peres had not said that the events were not genocide.

On the issue of the [nuclear program of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_program_of_Iran "Nuclear program of Iran") and the supposed existential threat this poses for Israel, Peres stated, "I am not in favor of a military attack on Iran, but we must quickly and decisively establish a strong, aggressive coalition of nations that will impose painful economic [sanctions on Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_against_Iran "Sanctions against Iran")", adding "Iran's efforts to achieve nuclear weapons should keep the entire world from sleeping soundly." In the same speech, Peres compared [Iranian president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_president "Iranian president") [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Ahmadinejad "Mahmoud Ahmadinejad") and his [call to "wipe Israel off the map"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Ahmadinejad_and_Israel#Wiped_off_the_map"_controversy "Mahmoud Ahmadinejad and Israel") to the genocidal threats to European Jewry made by [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") in the years prior to [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust"). In an interview with Army Radio on 8 May 2006 he remarked that "the president of Iran should remember that Iran can also be wiped off the map." However, after his death it was revealed that Peres had said that he prevented a military strike on Iran's nuclear program that had been ordered by Benjamin Netanyahu and Ehud Barak in 2010.

Peres was a proponent of [Middle East economic integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_economic_integration "Middle East economic integration").

### Technology

Peres is regarded as one of the founders of Israel's technology sector. Through personal meetings with the French government, he established collaboration treaties with France's nuclear industry in 1954. In 1958, he founded the re-organized [RAFAEL Armament Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAFAEL_Armament_Development_Authority "RAFAEL Armament Development Authority"), under the MOD's jurisdiction. From his desk he would control all aspects of Israel's nuclear program (first as director general and after 1959 as deputy minister). In the 1980s, he is credited with having laid the economic foundations for Israel's start-up economy. In later years, he developed an obsessive fascination with [nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanotechnology "Nanotechnology") and [brain research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_research "Brain research"). He believed that brain research would be the key to a better and more peaceful future. He launched his own nanotechnology investment fund in 2003, raising $5 million in the first week. In 2016, he founded the 'Israel innovation center' in the Arab neighbourhood of [Ajami, Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajami,_Jaffa "Ajami, Jaffa"). The center aims to encourage young people from around the world to be inspired by technology. Laying its foundation stone on 21 July 2016, Peres said: "We will prove that innovation has no limits and no barriers. Innovation enables dialogue between nations and between people. It will enable all young people – Jews, Muslims and Christians — to engage in science and technology equally."

## Personal life and family

In May 1945, Peres married Sonya Gelman, whom he had met in the [Ben Shemen Youth Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shemen_Youth_Village "Ben Shemen Youth Village"), where her father served as a carpentry teacher. The couple married after Sonya finished her military service as a truck driver in the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army "British Army") during World War II. Through the years Sonya chose to stay away from the media and keep her privacy and the privacy of her family, despite her husband's extensive political career. Sonya Peres was unable to attend Shimon's 2007 presidential inauguration ceremony because of ill health. With the election of Peres for president, Sonya Peres, who had not wanted her husband to accept the position, announced that she would stay in the couple's apartment in Tel Aviv and not join her husband in Jerusalem. The couple thereafter lived separately. She died on 20 January 2011, aged 87, from [heart failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_failure "Heart failure") at her apartment in [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv "Tel Aviv").

Shimon and Sonya Peres had three children. Their eldest child was a daughter, Dr. [Tsvia ("Tsiki") Walden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsvia_Walden "Tsvia Walden"), who became a linguist and professor at [Beit Berl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Berl "Beit Berl") Academic College. Their middle child was a son, Yoni, who became director of Village Veterinary Center, a veterinary hospital on the campus of [Kfar Hayarok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kfar_Hayarok?action=edit&redlink=1 "Kfar Hayarok") Agricultural School near Tel Aviv. He specializes in the treatment of [guide dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guide_dog "Guide dog"). Their youngest child, Nehemia ("Chemi"), became co-founder and managing general partner of [Pitango Venture Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitango "Pitango"), one of Israel's largest venture capital funds. Chemi Peres is a former helicopter pilot in the IAF.

Peres was a cousin of actress [Lauren Bacall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_Bacall "Lauren Bacall") (born Betty Joan Perske), although the two only discovered their relation to each other in the 1950s. Recalling this, Peres once remarked, "In 1952 or 1953 I came to New York... Lauren Bacall called me, said that she wanted to meet, and we did. We sat and talked about where our families came from, and discovered that we were from the same family".

Peres was a [polyglot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyglot "Polyglot"), speaking [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language "Polish language"), French, English, Russian, [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish_language "Yiddish language"), and [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"). He never lost his Polish accent when speaking in Hebrew.

### Poetry and song-writing

Peres was a lifelong writer of poetry and songs. As a child in [Vishnyeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnyeva "Vishnyeva"), Poland he learned to play the [mandolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandolin "Mandolin"). He wrote his first song when he was 8. He was inspired to write, including during cabinet meetings. Peres was noted to sometimes write [stanzas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanza "Stanza") during Cabinet meetings. As a result of his deep literary interests, he could quote from [Hebrew prophets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevi'im "Nevi'im"), [French literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_literature "French literature"), and [Chinese philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_philosophy "Chinese philosophy") with equal ease. Many of his poems were turned into songs, with the proceedings of the albums going to charity. His songs have been performed by artists including [Andrea Bocelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Bocelli "Andrea Bocelli") and [Liel Kolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liel_Kolet "Liel Kolet"). The most recent of his songs was "Chinese Melody" (recorded in Mandarin with Chinese and Israeli musicians), released in February 2016, which he wrote to celebrate the [Year of the Monkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkey_(zodiac) "Monkey (zodiac)") ([Music Video of 'Chinese Melody'](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ef6LcKQeWNw) on [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube")).

### Use of social media

During his presidency (2007–2014), Shimon Peres was noted for his embrace of social media to communicate with the public, being described as "Israel's first social media president", which included producing comedic videos on his [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") channel such as "Be my Friend for Peace" and "Former Israeli President Shimon Peres Goes Job Hunting". After retirement, he led a viral campaign to encourage children to study mathematics. In one video, he sends his answer to the teacher by throwing a paper plane ([Video: Shimon Peres throws a paper airplane in the name of education](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QJp6SzincgE) on YouTube). According to *The Wall Street Journal*, his presence on platforms such as [Snapchat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snapchat "Snapchat"), allowed him to "pack more punch—and humor—into the causes he championed, especially peaceful coexistence with the Palestinians."

## Places named after Peres

Following his death, it was announced that Israel's Negev nuclear reactor and atomic research center, that had been constructed in 1958, would be named after Peres. Netanyahu stated: "Shimon Peres worked hard to establish this important facility, a facility which has been very important for Israel's security for generations.."

## Overview of offices held

Peres twice officially served as prime minister (Israel's [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government")). His first stint spanned from 13 September 1984 through 20 October 1986, leading the 21st government during the first half of the [11th Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_eleventh_Knesset "List of members of the eleventh Knesset"). His second stint lasted from 4 November 1995 through 18 June 1996 (serving in an acting capacity from 4 November through 22 November 1995; and in permanent capacity thereafter), leading the 25th government as interim prime minister and the 26th government as permanent prime minister during the latter portion of the [13th Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_thirteenth_Knesset "List of members of the thirteenth Knesset"). In addition to these two official stints as prime minister, Peres is also considered to have served as the [de facto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_facto "De facto") acting prime minister from 22 April through 21 June 1977 (with Yitzhak Rabin remaining the [de jure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_jure "De jure") prime minister). Peres served as president (Israel's head of state) from 15 July 2007 through 24 July 2014. Peres was a member of the Knesset (Israel's legislature), first from November 1959 through 15 January 2006 (a record 47-year tenure), and again from March 2006 through 13 June 2007. His overall Knesset tenure of 48 years is the longest tenure in the history of the Knesset.

Peres, four times, served as the leader of the Knesset's opposition. For his first three stints in this role, the opposition leader was an unofficial and honorary role. His final stint in the position came after Knesset formalized the role as an official position. Peres was the unofficial opposition leader from 20 June 1977 through 13 September 1984, during the entirety of the [9th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_ninth_Knesset "List of members of the ninth Knesset") and [10th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_tenth_Knesset "List of members of the tenth Knesset") Knessets. During this stint, he led the opposition to the [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin "Menachem Begin")-led [18th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighteenth_government_of_Israel "Eighteenth government of Israel") and [19th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_government_of_Israel "Nineteenth government of Israel") governments and the Yitzhak Shamir-led [20th government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twentieth_government_of_Israel "Twentieth government of Israel"). His second stint as opposition lasted from 15 March 1990 through 13 July 1992, when in lead the opposition to the Yitzhak Shamir-led [24th government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fourth_government_of_Israel "Twenty-fourth government of Israel") during a portion of the [12th Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_tenth_Knesset "List of members of the tenth Knesset"). Peres' third stint lasted from 18 June 1996 to 1 July 1997, and saw him lead the opposition to the Benjamin Netanyahu-led [24th government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fourth_government_of_Israel "Twenty-fourth government of Israel") during a portion of the [14th Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_fourteenth_Knesset "List of members of the fourteenth Knesset"). Peres' final stint as opposition leader lasted from 25 June 2003 through 10 January 2005, and saw him lead the opposition to the Ariel Sharon-led [30th government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirtieth_government_of_Israel "Thirtieth government of Israel") during a portion of the [sixteenth Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_sixteenth_Knesset "List of members of the sixteenth Knesset").

### Other offices

From 1952 through 1953, Peres was the deputy director general of the Israeli Ministry of Defense. From 1952 through 1959, he was the director general.

Peres served as vice president of Socialist International. He was elected vice president in 1978.
