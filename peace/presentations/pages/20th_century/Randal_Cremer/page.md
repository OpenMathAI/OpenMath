**Sir William Randal Cremer** (18 March 1828 – 22 July 1908) usually known by his middle name "Randal", was a British [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK) "Liberal Party (UK)") [Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom) "Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)"), a [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifist "Pacifist"), and a leading advocate for international arbitration. He was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1903 for his work with the [international arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_arbitration "International arbitration") movement.

## Early life

Cremer was born to a working-class family in the southern English town of [Fareham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fareham "Fareham"). His father was a coachman, who abandoned the family soon after Randal Cremer was born. His mother raised him along with his two sisters, ensuring he received an education at a local [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist "Methodist") school. He augmented his knowledge by attending free lectures, was apprenticed as a builder and became a skilled [carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpenter "Carpenter").

Moving to London 1852, Cremer became active as a union organiser, swiftly becoming a recognized labour leader. Cremer was elected as the Secretary of the [International Workingmen's Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Workingmen's_Association "International Workingmen's Association") in 1865 but resigned two years later in 1867, when the organization decided to make women eligible for membership. Being strongly opposed to women's suffrage, Cremer might have now felt that the organisation was becoming too radical. While heavily involved in campaigning for progressive causes and respected by [Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx "Karl Marx"), Cremer did not agree with a worker-led revolution.

## Role in the international arbitration movement

From as early as his first unsuccessful run for Parliament in 1868, Cremer had advocated the expansion of international arbitration as peaceful alternative to war for the resolution of disputes.

He was elected as Liberal [Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom) "Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)") (MP) for [Haggerston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haggerston_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Haggerston (UK Parliament constituency)") in the [Metropolitan Borough of Shoreditch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Shoreditch "Metropolitan Borough of Shoreditch") from 1885 to 1895, and then from 1900 until his death from [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia "Pneumonia") in 1908.

Using his platform as an MP, Cremer cultivated allies on both continental Europe and across the Atlantic, including [Frédéric Passy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frédéric_Passy "Frédéric Passy"), [William Jennings Bryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Jennings_Bryan "William Jennings Bryan") and [Andrew Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Carnegie "Andrew Carnegie"). Using his network of contacts and his talent for organisation, Cremer did much to create and expand institutions for [international arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_arbitration "International arbitration"), which during his lifetime were successful in peacefully resolving numerous international disputes. This work includes co-founding the [Inter-Parliamentary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Parliamentary_Union "Inter-Parliamentary Union") and the [International Arbitration League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Arbitration_League "International Arbitration League"); gaining acceptance for the 1897 [Olney–Pauncefote Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olney–Pauncefote_Treaty "Olney–Pauncefote Treaty") between the United States and Britain that would have required arbitration of major disputes as the [Essequibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essequibo_(colony) "Essequibo (colony)") territory (the treaty was rejected by the US Senate and never went into effect); and preparing the ground for the [Hague peace conferences of 1899 and 1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Conventions_of_1899_and_1907 "Hague Conventions of 1899 and 1907").

In recognition of his work in the arbitration movement, Cremer won the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"), the first to do so solo, in 1903. Of the £8,000 award he donated £7,000 as an endowment for the [International Arbitration League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Arbitration_League "International Arbitration League").

He also was named a Chevalier of the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") [Légion d'honneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Légion_d'honneur "Légion d'honneur"), won the [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") [Knighthood of Saint Olaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Order_of_St._Olav "Royal Norwegian Order of St. Olav") and was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in 1907.

Randal Cremer Primary School, in Haggerston, is named in his honour.

## Death

Cremer died on 22 July 1908, leaving an estate of £2,241 (£1,803 net). Cremer's [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") was sold at auction at [Sotheby's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotheby's "Sotheby's") in London in November 1985 for $16,750 (equivalent to $50,141 in 2025)
