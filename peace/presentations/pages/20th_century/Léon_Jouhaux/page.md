**Léon Jouhaux** (1 July 1879 – 28 April 1954) was a French [trade union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_union "Trade union") leader who received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1951.

## Biography

Jouhaux was born in [Pantin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantin "Pantin"), [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis "Seine-Saint-Denis"), France. Jouhaux's father worked in a match factory in [Aubervilliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aubervilliers "Aubervilliers"). His secondary schooling ended when his father's earnings were stopped by a strike. He gained employment at the factory at age sixteen and immediately became an important part of the union. In 1900, after serving a brief mandatory military sentence in [French Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Algeria "French Algeria"), Jouhaux joined a [strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_action "Strike action") against the use of the [white phosphorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_phosphorus "White phosphorus") that [blinded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blindness "Blindness") his father, was dismissed, and worked at a succession of jobs until union influence saw him reinstated.

In 1906, he was elected by the local union as a representative to the [General Confederation of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Confederation_of_Labour_(France) "General Confederation of Labour (France)") (CGT), where his abilities saw him quickly rise through the ranks of organized labour. By 1909 he became interim treasurer, and shortly afterwards became secretary-general of the organization, which he held until 1947. His goals as a [trade unionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_union "Trade union") were the familiar ones of the early [labour movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_movement "Labour movement") — the [eight-hour day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-hour_day "Eight-hour day"), the right to [union representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_representative "Union representative") and [collective bargaining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_bargaining "Collective bargaining"), and [paid holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paid_time_off "Paid time off"). During the [Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_(France) "Popular Front (France)"), the [1936 Matignon Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matignon_Accords_(1936) "Matignon Accords (1936)"), to which he was a signatory, awarded many of these rights to French workers.

In the years before [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Jouhaux organised several mass protests, and the organization he led protested against the war. However, once the war started, Jouhaux supported his country and believed that a [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") victory would lead to the destruction of [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy") in Europe. During the war, he was arrested and imprisoned in [Buchenwald concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchenwald_concentration_camp "Buchenwald concentration camp"), later moved to the [Castle Itter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Itter "Castle Itter") before being freed by American and German troops in the 1945 [battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Castle_Itter "Battle of Castle Itter") there.

After the war, Jouhaux split from the CGT to form the social-democrat [Workers' Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Force "Workers' Force") (CGT-FO). In 1951, he was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize").

In an international context, his work was instrumental in the setting up of the [International Labour Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labour_Organization "International Labour Organization") (ILO), and was elected to high positions in international trade union bodies, including the [International Federation of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_Trade_Unions "International Federation of Trade Unions") and its postwar kin the [World Federation of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Federation_of_Trade_Unions "World Federation of Trade Unions") until that body split.

After his death in 1954, Léon Jouhaux was interred in [Le Père Lachaise Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Père_Lachaise_Cemetery "Le Père Lachaise Cemetery") in Paris.

## Legacy

- The *[rue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street "Street") Léon Jouhaux* in [Aix-en-Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-en-Provence "Aix-en-Provence"), [Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenoble "Grenoble"), [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon "Lyon"), [Genas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genas "Genas"), [Villefranche-sur-Saône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villefranche-sur-Saône "Villefranche-sur-Saône") and [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") are named for him.

## Quotation

"I would not go so far as to say that the French trade unions attached greater importance to the struggle for peace than the others did; but they certainly seemed to take it more to heart." Léon Jouhaux
