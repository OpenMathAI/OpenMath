**Philip John Noel-Baker, Baron Noel-Baker** (born **Philip John Baker**; 1 November 1889 – 8 October 1982) was a British politician, diplomat, academic, athlete, and renowned campaigner for [disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disarmament "Disarmament"). He carried the British team flag and won a silver medal for the 1500m run at the [1920 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Summer_Olympics "1920 Summer Olympics") in Antwerp, and received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1959.

Noel-Baker is the only person to have won an Olympic medal and received a Nobel Prize. He was a [Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") [Member of Parliament (UK)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom) "Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)") for 36 years, serving from 1929 to 1931 and again from 1936 to 1970, serving in several ministerial offices and the cabinet. He was created a [life peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peer "Life peer") in 1977.

## Early life and education

Baker was born 1 November 1889 in [Brondesbury Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brondesbury_Park "Brondesbury Park"), London, England, the sixth of seven children of Canadian-born [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker "Quaker") [Allen Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Baker "Allen Baker") and the Scottish-born Elizabeth Balmer Moscrip. His father had moved to England in 1876 to establish a manufacturing business, and served as a [Progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Party_(London) "Progressive Party (London)") member of the [London County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_County_Council "London County Council") from 1895 to 1906, and as a [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK) "Liberal Party (UK)") member of the [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_the_United_Kingdom "House of Commons of the United Kingdom") for [East Finsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Finsbury_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "East Finsbury (UK Parliament constituency)") from 1905 to 1918.

Baker was educated at Quaker [independent schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_school_(UK) "Independent school (UK)"): [Ackworth School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ackworth_School "Ackworth School") in the West Riding of Yorkshire and [Bootham School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bootham_School "Bootham School") in York. He studied in the United States at the Quaker-associated [Haverford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College "Haverford College") in Pennsylvania. Returning to England, he studied at [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge "King's College, Cambridge"), from 1908 to 1912, obtaining a [second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_class_honours "Second class honours") in Part I of the history [tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripos "Tripos") and a [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_honours "First class honours") in Part II economics. In addition to his academic endeavours, he was President of the [Cambridge Union Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Union_Society "Cambridge Union Society") in 1912 and President of the Cambridge University Athletic Club from 1910 to 1912.

## Athletic career

He was a competitor in the Olympic Games as a middle-distance runner, both before and after the First World War, representing [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_GB "Team GB") in the [800 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/800_metres "800 metres") and [1500 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1912_Summer_Olympics_–_Men's_1500_metres "Athletics at the 1912 Summer Olympics – Men's 1500 metres") at the [1912 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_Olympic_Games "1912 Olympic Games") in Stockholm. He reached the final of the 1500 metres, won by his fellow countryman [Arnold Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Jackson_(British_Army_officer) "Arnold Jackson (British Army officer)"). At the [1920 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Summer_Olympics "1920 Summer Olympics") in Antwerp, Baker was captain of the British track team and carried the team's flag. He won his first-round race in the 800 metres, but then concentrated on the 1500 metres, taking the silver medal behind his teammate [Albert Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Hill_(athlete) "Albert Hill (athlete)"). He was captain again at the [1924 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Summer_Olympics "1924 Summer Olympics") in Paris, but did not compete.

## Academic career and military service

Baker's early career was as an academic. Following his graduation from the University of Cambridge in 1912, he was awarded the Whewell Scholarship in international law. In 1914, he was appointed as vice-principal of [Ruskin College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruskin_College,_Oxford "Ruskin College, Oxford"), then an [adult education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_education "Adult education") establishment for working class men which is not part of the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford"). In 1915, he was elected a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_(Oxbridge) "Fellow (Oxbridge)") of King's College, Cambridge, his *[alma mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_mater "Alma mater")*.

During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War"), Baker organised and led the [Friends' Ambulance Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends'_Ambulance_Unit "Friends' Ambulance Unit") attached to the [fighting front in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_(World_War_I) "Western Front (World War I)") (1914–1915), and was then, as a [conscientious objector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objector "Conscientious objector") from 1916, adjutant of the First British Ambulance Unit for Italy, in association with the [British Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Red_Cross "British Red Cross") (1915–1918), for which he received military medals from the UK, France and Italy.

## Political career

After the First World War, Noel-Baker was closely involved in the formation of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations"), serving as assistant to [Lord Robert Cecil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Cecil,_1st_Viscount_Cecil_of_Chelwood "Robert Cecil, 1st Viscount Cecil of Chelwood"), then assistant to Sir [Eric Drummond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Eric_Drummond "James Eric Drummond"), the league's first secretary-general. According to historian [Susan Pedersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Pedersen_(historian) "Susan Pedersen (historian)") "Baker was far to the left of Drummond politically, but he had the kind of formation, connections, and intimate understanding of British officialdom’s rules of the game that made for easy collaboration between the two." Noel-Baker did much of the League's early work on the [mandates system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate "League of Nations mandate").

He became the first [Sir Ernest Cassel Professor of International Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Ernest_Cassel_Professor_of_International_Relations "Sir Ernest Cassel Professor of International Relations") at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics") (which was then part of the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London "University of London")) from 1924 to 1929, and a lecturer at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") from 1933 to 1934. His political career with the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") began in 1924 when he stood, unsuccessfully, for Parliament in the [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)") [safe seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_seat "Safe seat") of [Birmingham Handsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Handsworth "Birmingham Handsworth"). He was elected as the member for [Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Coventry (UK Parliament constituency)") in 1929, and served as [parliamentary private secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_private_secretary "Parliamentary private secretary") to the [Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_(United_Kingdom) "Foreign Secretary (United Kingdom)") [Arthur Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Henderson "Arthur Henderson").

Noel-Baker lost his seat in 1931, but remained Henderson's assistant while Henderson was president of the [World Disarmament Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Disarmament_Conference "World Disarmament Conference") in Geneva in 1932 to 1933. He stood for Parliament again in Coventry in 1935, unsuccessfully, but won the [Derby by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Derby_by-election "1936 Derby by-election") in July 1936 after the sitting [Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Derby (UK Parliament constituency)") Member of Parliament [J. H. Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._H._Thomas "J. H. Thomas") resigned. When that constituency was split in 1950, he transferred to [Derby South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_South "Derby South").

Noel-Baker became a member of the Labour Party's [National Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Executive_Committee_of_the_Labour_Party_(UK) "National Executive Committee of the Labour Party (UK)") in 1937. On 21 June 1938, Noel-Baker, as MP for [Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Derby (UK Parliament constituency)"), in the run up to the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"), spoke at the House of Commons against aerial bombing of German cities based on moral grounds. "The only way to prevent atrocities from the air is to abolish air warfare and national air forces altogether."

In the coalition government during the Second World War he was a parliamentary secretary at the [Ministry of War Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_War_Transport "Ministry of War Transport") from February 1942, and served as [Minister of State for Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_for_Foreign_Affairs_(United_Kingdom) "Minister of State for Foreign Affairs (United Kingdom)") after Labour gained power following the 1945 general election, but had a poor relationship with the Foreign Secretary, [Ernest Bevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Bevin "Ernest Bevin"). Noel-Baker moved to become [Secretary of State for Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Air "Secretary of State for Air") in October 1946, and then became [Secretary of State for Commonwealth Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Commonwealth_Relations "Secretary of State for Commonwealth Relations") in 1947 and joined the cabinet.

Noel-Baker was the minister responsible for organising the [1948 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Summer_Olympics "1948 Summer Olympics") in London. He moved to the [Ministry of Fuel and Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Fuel_and_Power "Ministry of Fuel and Power") in 1950. In the mid-1940s, Noel-Baker served on the British delegation to what became the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"), helping to draft its charter and other rules for operation as a British delegate. He served as [Chairman of the Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Labour_Party "Chairman of the Labour Party") in 1946–47, but lost his place on the National Executive Committee in 1948, his place being taken by [Michael Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Foot "Michael Foot"). An opponent of left-wing [Bevanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bevanite "Bevanite") policies in the 1950s, and an advocate of multilateral nuclear disarmament, rather than a policy of [unilateral disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unilateral_disarmament "Unilateral disarmament"), he received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1959. In 1979, with [Fenner Brockway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenner_Brockway "Fenner Brockway"), he co-founded the World Disarmament Campaign, serving as co-Chair until he died, and was an active supporter of disarmament into the 1980s.

Noel-Baker stood down as the MP for Derby South at the [1970 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_United_Kingdom_general_election "1970 United Kingdom general election"), at which he was succeeded by [Walter Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Johnson_(politician) "Walter Johnson (politician)"). His [life peerage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peer "Life peer") was announced in the [1977 Silver Jubilee and Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Silver_Jubilee_and_Birthday_Honours "1977 Silver Jubilee and Birthday Honours") and, aged 87, he was raised to the [peerage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peerage_of_the_United_Kingdom "Peerage of the United Kingdom") 22 July 1977, as **Baron Noel-Baker**, of the [City of Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Derby "City of Derby"), having declined an appointment as a [Companion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_of_Honour "Companion of Honour") in the 1965 New Year Honours. He was president of the [International Council of Sport Science and Physical Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Council_of_Sport_Science_and_Physical_Education "International Council of Sport Science and Physical Education") from 1960 to 1976. Noel-Baker was an active contributor to [House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords "House of Lords") debates into his nineties, speaking in debates on the ongoing [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War "Falklands War") in the months preceding his death.

A memorial garden, the Philip Noel-Baker Peace Garden, exists within [Elthorne Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_and_open_spaces_in_the_London_Borough_of_Islington "Parks and open spaces in the London Borough of Islington"), a small park in the [London Borough of Islington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Islington "London Borough of Islington").

[Brian Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Harrison_(historian) "Brian Harrison (historian)") recorded an oral history interview with Noel-Baker in April 1977, as part of the Suffrage Interviews project, titled *Oral evidence on the suffragette and suffragist movements: the Brian Harrison interviews.* Noel-Baker discusses the [League of Nations Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_Union "League of Nations Union") and the [Peace Ballot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Ballot "Peace Ballot") of 1934–35, as well as his work with the [United Nations Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Association "United Nations Association") and the work of [Kathleen Courtney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Courtney "Kathleen Courtney").

## Personal life

In June 1915, Philip John Baker married Irene Noel, a field hospital nurse in [East Grinstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Grinstead "East Grinstead"), subsequently adopting the hyphenated name **Noel-Baker** in 1921 by deed poll. His wife was a friend of [Virginia Woolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Woolf "Virginia Woolf"). Their only son, [Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Noel-Baker "Francis Noel-Baker"), also became a Labour MP and served together with his father in the Commons. Their marriage, however was not a success and Noel-Baker's mistress from 1936 was [Megan Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megan_Lloyd_George "Megan Lloyd George"), daughter of the former [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK) "Liberal Party (UK)") leader and Prime Minister [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George "David Lloyd George"), herself a Liberal and later Labour MP. The relationship ended when Irene died in 1956.

He died at home in [Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster "Westminster") on 8 October 1982.
