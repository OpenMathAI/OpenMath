**Eliezer** "**Elie**" **Wiesel** (September 30, 1928 – July 2, 2016) was a Romanian-born American writer, professor, political activist, [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_Peace_Prize_laureates#1980 "List of Nobel Peace Prize laureates"), and [Holocaust survivor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_survivor "Holocaust survivor"). He authored [57 books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Wiesel_bibliography "Elie Wiesel bibliography"), written mostly in French and English, including *[Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_(memoir) "Night (memoir)")*, which is based on his experiences as a Jewish prisoner at [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp "Auschwitz concentration camp") and [Buchenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchenwald_concentration_camp "Buchenwald concentration camp") during [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust").

As a political activist, Wiesel became a regular speaker on the subject of the Holocaust and advocated for justice in numerous causes around the globe, including that of [Soviet Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refusenik "Refusenik") and [Ethiopian Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Israel "Beta Israel"), [South African apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid"), the [Rwandan genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_genocide "Rwandan genocide"), the [Bosnian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_genocide "Bosnian genocide"), the [War in Darfur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Darfur "War in Darfur"), the [Kurdish independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_independence_movement "Kurdish independence movement"), the [Armenian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide "Armenian genocide"), Argentina's [*Desaparecidos*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enforced_disappearance#Argentina "Enforced disappearance"), Nicaragua's [Miskito people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_people "Miskito people"), the [Sri Lankan Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Eelam "Tamil Eelam"), and the [Cambodian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_genocide "Cambodian genocide"). He was also an outspoken advocate for Israel and frequently weighed in to support the country during escalations of the [Arab–Israeli conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab–Israeli_conflict "Arab–Israeli conflict") and throughout the [Iran–Israel proxy conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran–Israel_proxy_conflict "Iran–Israel proxy conflict"). He backed [Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netanyahu "Netanyahu") publicly, including [his controversial 2015 Congress speech on Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu's_2015_address_to_the_United_States_Congress "Benjamin Netanyahu's 2015 address to the United States Congress"), and signed ads praising [Jewish settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_settlement "Israeli settlement"). He said Jerusalem "belongs to the Jewish people" and opposed territorial compromise there. He also hosted direct talks to facilitate the [Israeli–Palestinian peace process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli–Palestinian_peace_process "Israeli–Palestinian peace process").

Wiesel was a professor of the humanities at [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University "Boston University"), which created the Elie Wiesel Center for Jewish Studies in his honor. He received a number of awards, including the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1986. He was a founding board member of the [Human Rights Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Foundation "Human Rights Foundation") and remained active in it throughout his life. Wiesel was one of the main figures who spearheaded the establishment of the [United States Holocaust Memorial Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Holocaust_Memorial_Museum "United States Holocaust Memorial Museum") in 1993.

## Early life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Eli_wiesel_house_in_sighet01.jpg/250px-Eli_wiesel_house_in_sighet01.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Eli_wiesel_house_in_sighet01.jpg)

The house in which Wiesel was born in Sighet

Eliezer Wiesel was born in [Sighet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sighet "Sighet") (now Sighetu Marmației), [Maramureș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maramureș_County "Maramureș County"), in the [Carpathian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains "Carpathian Mountains") of [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania "Kingdom of Romania"). His parents were Sarah Feig and Shlomo Wiesel. At home, the family spoke mainly [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish_language "Yiddish language") in addition to German, [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language "Hungarian language"), and [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language "Romanian language"). Wiesel's mother, Sarah, was the daughter of Dodye Feig, a [Vizhnitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizhnitz_(Hasidic_dynasty) "Vizhnitz (Hasidic dynasty)") [Hasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasidic_Judaism "Hasidic Judaism") and farmer from the nearby village of [Bocskó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocskó "Bocskó"). Dodye was active and trusted within the community.

Wiesel's father, Shlomo, instilled a strong sense of [humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism "Humanism") in his son, encouraging him to learn [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language") and to read literature, whereas his mother encouraged him to study the [Torah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah "Torah"). Wiesel said his father represented reason, while his mother Sarah promoted faith. Wiesel was instructed that his genealogy traced back to [Rabbi Schlomo Yitzhaki (Rashi)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashi "Rashi"), and was a descendant of Rabbi [Yeshayahu ben Abraham Horovitz ha-Levi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshayahu_ben_Abraham_Horovitz_ha-Levi "Yeshayahu ben Abraham Horovitz ha-Levi").

Wiesel had three siblings. His older sisters were Beatrice and Hilda, and a younger sister Tzipora. Beatrice and Hilda survived the war, and were reunited with Wiesel at a French orphanage. They eventually emigrated to North America, with Beatrice moving to [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal"), Quebec, Canada. Tzipora, Shlomo, and Sarah were murdered in the Holocaust.

## Imprisonment during the Holocaust

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Buchenwald_Slave_Laborers_Liberation.jpg/250px-Buchenwald_Slave_Laborers_Liberation.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Buchenwald_Slave_Laborers_Liberation.jpg)

[Buchenwald concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchenwald_concentration_camp "Buchenwald concentration camp"), photo taken April 16, 1945, five days after liberation of the camp. Wiesel is in the second row from the bottom, seventh from the left, next to the bunk post.

In March 1944, Germany [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Margarethe "Operation Margarethe") Hungary, thus extending the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Hungary#The_Holocaust "History of the Jews in Hungary") into [Northern Transylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Transylvania "Northern Transylvania") as well. Wiesel was 15, and he, with his family, along with the rest of the town's Jewish population, was placed in one of the two confinement ghettos set up in Máramarossziget ([Sighet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sighet "Sighet")), the town where he had been born and raised. In May 1944, the Hungarian authorities, under German pressure, began to [deport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deport "Deport") the Jewish community to the [Auschwitz concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp "Auschwitz concentration camp"), where up to 90 percent of the people were murdered on arrival.

Immediately after they were sent to Auschwitz, his mother and his younger sister were murdered in the gas chambers. Wiesel and his father were selected to perform labor so long as they remained able-bodied, after which they were to be murdered in the gas chambers. Wiesel and his father were later deported to the concentration camp at [Buchenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchenwald_concentration_camp "Buchenwald concentration camp"). Until that transfer, he admitted to [Oprah Winfrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oprah_Winfrey "Oprah Winfrey"), his primary motivation for trying to survive Auschwitz was knowing that his father was still alive: "I knew that if I died, he would die." After they were taken to Buchenwald, his father died before the camp was liberated. In *Night*, Wiesel recalled the shame he felt when he heard his father being beaten and was unable to help.

Wiesel was [tattooed with inmate number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_in_Nazi_camps "Identification in Nazi camps") "A-7713" on his left arm. The camp was liberated by the [U.S. Third Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Third_Army "U.S. Third Army") on April 11, 1945, when they were just prepared to be evacuated from Buchenwald.

## March of the Living

The [March of the Living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_the_Living "March of the Living") is an annual educational program that has brought over 300,000 participants from around the world to Poland, where they visit historical sites of the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust"), make a two-mile trek from [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp "Auschwitz concentration camp") to the former extermination site of [Birkenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkenau "Birkenau"). Students learn about the experience through live testimony from survivors. Wiesel served on the Presidium for the first March of the Living in 1988, during its founding year. Wiesel attended the March of the Living in 1990, and again in 2005, during the 60th anniversary of the end of WWII. Wiesel addressed over 18,000 in attendance. It was the biggest event in the program's history .

On the 1990 March of the Living, Elie Wiesel addressed the participants at Auschwitz about his concerns about antisemitism. He stated, "We were convinced that [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism") perished here. Antisemitism did not perish here; its victims perished here." He started to share a story of a young girl, paused, and left the stage. The footage stated Wiesel was simply unable to continue the story. The corroborating article from [Eli Rubenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Rubenstein "Eli Rubenstein"), who was in attendance that day described that even "the world's most eloquent witness to the Holocaust," was not able to convey the story that led to the fate of this young girl.

In 2017, Wiesel's son, [Elisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisha_Wiesel "Elisha Wiesel") participated in the [March of the Living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_the_Living "March of the Living") in memory of his father, honoring his legacy. Since his father's death, he has spoken at the US Holocaust Memorial Museum and Auschwitz, and has begun working on his late father's foundation, the [Elie Wiesel Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Wiesel_Foundation_for_Humanity "Elie Wiesel Foundation for Humanity").

Wiesel is included in the publication *[Witness: Passing the Torch of Holocaust Memory to New Generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witness:_Passing_the_Torch_of_Holocaust_Memory_to_New_Generations "Witness: Passing the Torch of Holocaust Memory to New Generations").* Along with his picture from when he was imprisoned at [Buchenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchenwald_concentration_camp "Buchenwald concentration camp"), he was quoted from the 1990 [March of the Living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_the_Living "March of the Living"):

> "Forever will I see the children who no longer have the strength to cry. Forever will I see the elderly who no longer have the strength to help them. Forever will I see the mothers and the fathers, the grandfathers and grandmothers, the little schoolchildren…their teachers…the righteous and the pious…. From where do we take the tears to cry over them? Who has the strength to cry for them?"

— Elie Wiesel, Witness: Passing the Torch of Holocaust Memory to New Generations, Page 5

## Legacy

In 2026, the tenth anniversary of Wiesel’s death was marked by renewed attention to his legacy and his connection with the March of the Living. The Jerusalem Post and the International March of the Living noted that Wiesel served on the Presidium of the first March of the Living in 1988, attended the program in 1990, and returned in 2005, when he addressed more than 18,000 participants during the 60th anniversary year of the end of the Second World War. In a separate reflection, Canadian-American actress and 1990 March of the Living alumna Tara Strong recalled being present at Auschwitz-Birkenau when Wiesel became unable to continue a story about a young girl he remembered from Auschwitz; according to Strong, members of the Toronto student choir then began singing “Eli, Eli,” after which thousands of participants joined in.

## Post-war career as a writer

### France

After World War II ended and Wiesel was freed, he joined a transport of 1,000 child survivors of Buchenwald to [Ecouis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecouis "Ecouis"), France, where the [Œuvre de secours aux enfants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Œuvre_de_secours_aux_enfants "Œuvre de secours aux enfants") (OSE) had established a rehabilitation center. Wiesel joined a smaller group of 90 to 100 boys from Orthodox homes who wanted [kosher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashrut "Kashrut") facilities and a higher level of religious observance; they were cared for in a home in [Ambloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambloy "Ambloy") under the directorship of [Judith Hemmendinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judith_Hemmendinger "Judith Hemmendinger"). This home was later moved to [Taverny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taverny "Taverny") and operated until 1947.

Afterwards, Wiesel traveled to Paris where he learned French and studied literature, philosophy and psychology at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris"). He heard lectures by philosopher [Martin Buber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Buber "Martin Buber") and existentialist [Jean-Paul Sartre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Paul_Sartre "Jean-Paul Sartre") and he spent his evenings reading works by [Fyodor Dostoyevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Dostoyevsky "Fyodor Dostoyevsky"), [Franz Kafka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Kafka "Franz Kafka"), and [Thomas Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Mann "Thomas Mann").

By the time he was 19, he had begun working as a journalist, writing in French, while also teaching [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language") and working as a choirmaster. He wrote for Israeli and French newspapers, including *Tsien in Kamf* (in [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish_language "Yiddish language")).

In 1946, after learning of the [Irgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irgun "Irgun")'s [bombing of the King David Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_the_King_David_Hotel "Bombing of the King David Hotel") in Jerusalem, Wiesel made an unsuccessful attempt to join the underground Zionist movement. In 1948, he translated articles from Hebrew into Yiddish for Irgun periodicals, but never became a member of the organization. In 1949, he traveled to Israel as a correspondent for the French newspaper *[L'arche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Arche_(magazine) "L'Arche (magazine)")*. He then was hired as Paris correspondent for the Israeli newspaper *[Yedioth Ahronoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yedioth_Ahronoth "Yedioth Ahronoth")*, subsequently becoming its roaming international correspondent.

Excerpt from *Night*

> Never shall I forget that night, the first night in camp, which has turned my life into one long night, seven times cursed and seven times sealed. Never shall I forget that smoke. Never shall I forget the little faces of the children, whose bodies I saw turned into wreaths of smoke beneath a silent blue sky. Never shall I forget those flames which consumed my faith forever. Never shall I forget that nocturnal silence which deprived me, for all eternity, of the desire to live. Never shall I forget those moments which murdered my God and my soul and turned my dreams to dust. Never shall I forget these things, even if I am condemned to live as long as God Himself. Never.

— Elie Wiesel, from [*Night*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_(memoir) "Night (memoir)").

For ten years after the war, Wiesel refused to write about or discuss his experiences during the Holocaust. He began to reconsider his decision after a meeting with the French author [François Mauriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Mauriac "François Mauriac"), the 1952 [Nobel Laureate in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature") who eventually became Wiesel's close friend. Mauriac was a devout Christian who had fought in the [French Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance "French Resistance") during the war. He compared Wiesel to "[Lazarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazarus_of_Bethany "Lazarus of Bethany") rising from the dead", and saw from Wiesel's tormented eyes, "the death of God in the soul of a child". Mauriac persuaded him to begin writing about his harrowing experiences.

Wiesel first wrote the 900-page memoir *Un di velt hot geshvign* (*And the World Remained Silent*) in [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish "Yiddish"), which was published in abridged form in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires"). Wiesel rewrote a shortened version of the manuscript in French, *La Nuit*, in 1955. It was translated into English as *[Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_(memoir) "Night (memoir)")* in 1960. The book sold few copies after its initial publication, but still attracted interest from reviewers, leading to television interviews with Wiesel and meetings with writers such as [Saul Bellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Bellow "Saul Bellow").

As its profile rose, *Night* was eventually translated into 30 languages with ten million copies sold in the United States. At one point film director [Orson Welles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orson_Welles "Orson Welles") wanted to make it into a feature film, but Wiesel refused, feeling that his memoir would lose its meaning if it were told without the silences in between his words. [Oprah Winfrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oprah_Winfrey "Oprah Winfrey") made it a spotlight selection for her book club in 2006.

In 2025, a documentary by filmmaker Oren Rudavsky, *Elie Wiesel: Soul on Fire*, examined the history behind the writing and publication of *Night*. According to *The Times of Israel*, the film traced Wiesel’s postwar years in France, his work as a journalist in Paris, and his decision, about a decade after the Holocaust, to write about his experiences. The article noted that the earliest version of the memoir was an approximately 862-page Yiddish manuscript, *Un di velt hot geshvign* (And the World Remained Silent), which was published in Argentina before Wiesel later shortened and reshaped the work into the French-language La Nuit (1958) and, eventually, the English-language Night (1960). The documentary also discussed the book’s initially limited sales and its later growth in prominence, particularly after increased public interest in Holocaust memory following the 1967 Six-Day War and Oprah Winfrey’s 2006 selection of Night for her book club. The Documentary film premiered across in North America on PBS under their American Masters series on January 22, 2026.

### United States

In 1955, Wiesel moved to New York as foreign correspondent for the Israel daily, *[Yediot Ahronot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yediot_Ahronot "Yediot Ahronot")*. In 1969, he married Austrian Marion Erster Rose, who also translated many of his books. They had one son, [Shlomo Elisha Wiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisha_Wiesel "Elisha Wiesel"), named after Wiesel's father.

Wiesel developed a close relationship with [Menachem Mendel Schneerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Mendel_Schneerson "Menachem Mendel Schneerson"), the Lubavitcher Rebbe, with whom he held extended private conversations and [corresponded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igrot_Kodesh "Igrot Kodesh") about faith after the Holocaust. Wiesel later said that Schneerson convinced him to marry and build a family, after Wiesel had struggled with the idea of rebuilding his personal life following the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust "Holocaust").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fc/Elie_Wiesel_%281987%29_by_Erling_Mandelmann_-_2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Elie_Wiesel_(1987)_by_Erling_Mandelmann_-_2.jpg)

Wiesel in 1987

In the U.S., he eventually wrote over 40 books, most of them non-fiction [Holocaust literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_art_and_literature "The Holocaust in art and literature"), and novels. As an author, he was awarded a number of literary prizes and is considered among the most important in describing the Holocaust from a highly personal perspective. As a result, some historians credited Wiesel with giving the term *[Holocaust](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/holocaust "wikt:holocaust")* its present meaning, although he did not feel that the word adequately described that historical event. In 1975, he co-founded the magazine *[Moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_(magazine) "Moment (magazine)")* with writer [Leonard Fein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Fein "Leonard Fein").

The 1979 book and play *[The Trial of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trial_of_God "The Trial of God")* are said to have been based on his real-life Auschwitz experience of witnessing three Jews who, close to death, conduct a [trial against God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawsuits_against_God "Lawsuits against God"), under the accusation that He has been oppressive towards the Jewish people.

Wiesel also played a role in the initial success of *[The Painted Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Painted_Bird "The Painted Bird")* by [Jerzy Kosinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Kosinski "Jerzy Kosinski") by endorsing it before it became known the book was fiction and, in the sense that it was presented as all Kosinski's true experience, a [hoax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoax "Hoax").

Wiesel published two volumes of memoirs. The first, *All Rivers Run to the Sea*, was published in 1994 and covered his life up to the year 1969. The second, titled *And the Sea is Never Full* and published in 1999, covered the years from 1969 to 1999.

## Political activism

Wiesel and his wife, [Marion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Wiesel "Marion Wiesel"), started the [Elie Wiesel Foundation for Humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Wiesel_Foundation_for_Humanity "Elie Wiesel Foundation for Humanity") in 1986. He served as chairman of the President's Commission on the Holocaust (later renamed the US Holocaust Memorial Council) from 1978 to 1986, spearheading the building of the [United States Holocaust Memorial Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Holocaust_Memorial_Museum "United States Holocaust Memorial Museum") in Washington, D.C. [Sigmund Strochlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmund_Strochlitz "Sigmund Strochlitz") was his close friend and confidant during these years.

The Holocaust Memorial Museum gives the Elie Wiesel Award to "internationally prominent individuals whose actions have advanced the Museum's vision of a world where people confront [hatred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatred "Hatred"), prevent [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide "Genocide"), and promote human [dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dignity "Dignity")". The Foundation had invested its endowment in money manager [Bernard L. Madoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_L._Madoff "Bernard L. Madoff")'s investment [Ponzi scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponzi_scheme "Ponzi scheme"), costing the Foundation $15 million and Wiesel and his wife much of their own personal savings.

### Support for the Israeli government

In 1982, at the request of the Israeli Foreign Ministry, Wiesel agreed to resign from his position as chairman of a [planned international conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Conference_on_the_Holocaust_and_Genocide "International Conference on the Holocaust and Genocide") on the Holocaust and the [Armenian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide "Armenian genocide"). Wiesel then worked with the Foreign Ministry in its attempts to get the conference either canceled or to remove all discussion of the Armenian genocide from it, and to those ends he provided the Foreign Ministry with internal documents on the conference's planning and lobbied fellow academics to not attend the conference.

Wiesel was a co-founder of the Writers and Artists for Peace in the Middle East, a pro-Israel group. In 1984, he signed a letter protesting German arms sales to [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia "Saudi Arabia").

#### On the Arab–Israeli conflict

Wiesel was critical of [Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas "Hamas"); he condemned them for the "use of children [as human shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_of_human_shields_by_Hamas "Use of human shields by Hamas")" during the [2014 Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Gaza_War "2014 Gaza War"), and ran an ad in several large newspapers to express this message. *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")* refused to run the advertisement, saying, "The opinion being expressed is too strong, and too forcefully made, and will cause concern amongst a significant number of *Times* readers."

During his lifetime, Wiesel had deflected questions on the topic of the [Israeli settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_settlement "Israeli settlement"), claiming to abstain from commenting on Israel's internal debates. According to Lebanese-American columnist [Hussein Ibish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Ibish "Hussein Ibish"), despite this position, Wiesel had gone on record as supporting the idea of expanding Jewish settlements into the [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories "Palestinian territories") conquered by Israel during the [Six-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-Day_War "Six-Day War"); such settlements are [considered illegal by the international community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legality_of_Israeli_settlements "Legality of Israeli settlements"). Wiesel often emphasized the [Jewish connection to Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_and_Judaism_in_the_Land_of_Israel "History of the Jews and Judaism in the Land of Israel"), and criticized the [Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Barack_Obama "Presidency of Barack Obama") for pressuring Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Netanyahu "Benjamin Netanyahu") to halt the construction of settlements in [East Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Jerusalem "East Jerusalem"), stating that "[Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem") is above politics. It is [mentioned more than six hundred times in Scripture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_in_Judaism "Jerusalem in Judaism")—and not a single time [in the Koran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_significance_of_Jerusalem#In_Islam "Religious significance of Jerusalem") ... It belongs to the Jewish people and is much more than a city".

### Awards and other activism

Wiesel was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1986 for speaking out against violence, repression, and racism. The [Norwegian Nobel Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Committee "Norwegian Nobel Committee") described Wiesel as "one of the most important spiritual leaders and guides in an age when violence, repression, and racism continue to characterize the world" and called him a "messenger to mankind". It also stressed that Wiesel's commitment originated in the sufferings of the Jewish people but that he expanded it to embrace all repressed peoples and races.

In his acceptance speech he delivered a message "of peace, atonement, and human dignity". He explained his feelings: "Silence encourages the tormentor, never the tormented. Sometimes we must interfere. When human lives are endangered, when human dignity is in jeopardy, national borders and sensitivities become irrelevant."

He received many other prizes and honors for his work, including the [Congressional Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Gold_Medal "Congressional Gold Medal") in 1985, the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom "Presidential Medal of Freedom"), and The International Center in New York's Award of Excellence.
He was also elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Letters "American Academy of Arts and Letters") in 1996.

Wiesel co-founded *[Moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_(magazine) "Moment (magazine)")* magazine with [Leonard Fein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Fein "Leonard Fein") in 1975. They founded the magazine to provide a voice for American Jews. He was also a member of the International Advisory Board of [NGO Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NGO_Monitor "NGO Monitor").

A staunch opponent of the [death penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment "Capital punishment"), Wiesel stated that he thought that even [Adolf Eichmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Eichmann "Adolf Eichmann") should not have been executed. Wiesel advocated clemency in the [Cheshire murder case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire_murders "Cheshire murders"), instead supporting a life sentence of [hard labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_labor "Hard labor") for the perpetrators.

Wiesel, a supporter of immigrant's rights, popularized the slogan "No human being is illegal". stating "you who are so-called illegal aliens should know that no human being is illegal. That is a contradiction in terms Human beings can be beautiful or more beautiful, they can be fat or skinny, they can be right or wrong, but illegal? How can a human being be illegal?"

In April 1999, Wiesel delivered the speech "The Perils of Indifference" in Washington, D.C., criticizing the people and countries who chose to be indifferent while the Holocaust was happening. He defined indifference as being neutral between two sides, which, in this case, amounts to overlooking the victims of the Holocaust. Throughout the speech, he expressed the view that a little bit of attention, either positive or negative, is better than no attention at all.

In 2003, he discovered and publicized the fact that at least 280,000 [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Romania "History of the Jews in Romania") and [Ukrainian Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Ukraine "History of the Jews in Ukraine"), along with other groups, were massacred in Romanian-run [death camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extermination_camp "Extermination camp").

In 2005, he gave a speech at the opening ceremony of the new building of [Yad Vashem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yad_Vashem "Yad Vashem"), the Israeli Holocaust History Museum:

> I know what people say – it is so easy. Those that were there won't agree with that statement. The statement is: it was man's inhumanity to man. NO! It was man's inhumanity to Jews! Jews were not killed because they were human beings. In the eyes of the killers they were not human beings! They were Jews!

In early 2006, Wiesel accompanied Oprah Winfrey as she visited [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp "Auschwitz concentration camp"), a visit which was broadcast as part of *[The Oprah Winfrey Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oprah_Winfrey_Show "The Oprah Winfrey Show")*. The trip was organized by [International March of the Living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_the_Living "March of the Living")'s Vice Chair, *David Machlis.* On November 30, 2006, Wiesel received a [knighthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_honours_system#Knighthood "British honours system") in London in recognition of his work toward raising Holocaust education in the United Kingdom.

In September 2006, he appeared before the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council "UN Security Council") with actor [George Clooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clooney "George Clooney") to call attention to the humanitarian crisis in [Darfur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darfur "Darfur"). When Wiesel died, Clooney wrote, "We had a champion who carried our pain, our guilt, and our responsibility on his shoulders for generations."

In 2007, Wiesel was awarded the [Dayton Literary Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Literary_Peace_Prize "Dayton Literary Peace Prize")'s Lifetime Achievement Award. That same year, the Elie Wiesel Foundation for Humanity issued a letter condemning [Armenian genocide denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide_denial "Armenian genocide denial"), a letter that was signed by 53 Nobel laureates including Wiesel. Wiesel repeatedly called Turkey's 90-year-old campaign to downplay its actions during the [Armenian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide "Armenian genocide") a double killing.

President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush"), joined by the [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Dalai_Lama "14th Dalai Lama") and Wiesel, October 17, 2007, to the ceremony at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., for the presentation of the [Congressional Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Gold_Medal "Congressional Gold Medal") to the Dalai Lama

In 2009, Wiesel criticized the [Vatican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_See "Holy See") for lifting the [excommunication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excommunication "Excommunication") of controversial bishop [Richard Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Williamson "Richard Williamson"), a member of the [Society of Saint Pius X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Saint_Pius_X "Society of Saint Pius X"). The excommunication was later reimposed.

In June 2009, Wiesel accompanied US President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") and German Chancellor [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel "Angela Merkel") as they toured the [Buchenwald concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchenwald_concentration_camp "Buchenwald concentration camp"). Wiesel was an adviser at the [Gatestone Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatestone_Institute "Gatestone Institute"). In 2010, Wiesel accepted a five-year appointment as a Distinguished Presidential Fellow at [Chapman University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapman_University "Chapman University") in [Orange County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County,_California "Orange County, California"). In that role, he made a one-week visit to Chapman annually to meet with students and offer his perspective on subjects ranging from Holocaust history to religion, languages, literature, law and music.

In July 2009, Wiesel announced his support to the minority [Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamils "Tamils") in Sri Lanka. He said that, "Wherever minorities are being persecuted, we must raise our voices to protest ... The Tamil people are being disenfranchised and victimized by the Sri Lanka authorities. This injustice must stop. The Tamil people must be allowed to live in peace and flourish in their homeland."

In 2009, Wiesel returned to Hungary for his first visit since the Holocaust. During this visit, Wiesel participated in a conference at the Upper House Chamber of the [Hungarian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Parliament "Hungarian Parliament"), met Prime Minister [Gordon Bajnai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Bajnai "Gordon Bajnai") and President [László Sólyom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/László_Sólyom "László Sólyom"), and made a speech to the approximately 10,000 participants of an anti-racist gathering held in [Faith Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Church,_Hungary "Faith Church, Hungary"). However, in 2012, he protested against "the whitewashing" of [Hungary's involvement in the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Hungary#The_Holocaust "History of the Jews in Hungary"), and he gave up the Great Cross award he had received from the Hungarian government.

Wiesel was active in trying to prevent Iran from making nuclear weapons, stating that, "The words and actions of the leadership of Iran leave no doubt as to their intentions".

## Teaching

Wiesel held the position of [Andrew Mellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Mellon "Andrew Mellon") Professor of the [Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanities "Humanities") at [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University "Boston University") from 1976, teaching in both its religion and philosophy departments. He became a close friend of the president and chancellor [John Silber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Silber "John Silber"). The university created the Elie Wiesel Center for Jewish Studies in his honor. From 1972 to 1976 Wiesel was a Distinguished Professor at the [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York "City University of New York") and member of the [American Federation of Teachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Teachers "American Federation of Teachers").

In 1982 he served as the first [Henry Luce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Luce "Henry Luce") Visiting Scholar in Humanities and Social Thought at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"). He also co-instructed Winter Term (January) courses at [Eckerd College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckerd_College "Eckerd College"), [St. Petersburg, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg,_Florida "St. Petersburg, Florida"). From 1997 to 1999 he was Ingeborg Rennert Visiting professor of [Judaic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaic_Studies "Judaic Studies") at [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College "Barnard College") of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University").

## Personal life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Elie_Wiesel_and_wife_Marion_2012_Shankbone.JPG/250px-Elie_Wiesel_and_wife_Marion_2012_Shankbone.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Elie_Wiesel_and_wife_Marion_2012_Shankbone.JPG)

Wiesel and wife Marion at the [2012 *Time* 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100")

In 1969 he married [Marion Erster Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Wiesel "Marion Wiesel"), who originally was from Austria and also translated many of his books from French to English. They had one son, [Shlomo Elisha Wiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisha_Wiesel "Elisha Wiesel"), named after Wiesel's father. The family lived in [Greenwich, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich,_Connecticut "Greenwich, Connecticut").

Wiesel was attacked in a San Francisco hotel by 22-year-old [Holocaust denier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_denier "Holocaust denier") Eric Hunt in February 2007, but was not injured. Hunt was arrested the following month and charged with multiple offenses.

In May 2011, Wiesel served as the [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis") commencement speaker.

In February 2012, a member of [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints "The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints") performed a [posthumous baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posthumous_baptism "Posthumous baptism") for [Simon Wiesenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Wiesenthal "Simon Wiesenthal")'s parents without proper authorization. After his own name was submitted for proxy baptism, Wiesel spoke out against the unauthorized practice of posthumously baptizing Jews and asked presidential candidate and Latter-day Saint [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney "Mitt Romney") to denounce it. Romney's campaign declined to comment, directing such questions to church officials.

## Death and aftermath

Wiesel died on the morning of July 2, 2016, at his home in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan"), aged 87. A private funeral service was conducted in his honor at the [Fifth Avenue Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue_Synagogue "Fifth Avenue Synagogue"), and then he was buried at the [Sharon Gardens Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Gardens_Cemetery "Sharon Gardens Cemetery") in [Valhalla, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valhalla,_New_York "Valhalla, New York"), on July 3.

Utah senator [Orrin Hatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orrin_Hatch "Orrin Hatch") paid tribute to Wiesel in a speech on the Senate floor the following week, in which he said that "With Elie's passing, we have lost a beacon of humanity and hope. We have lost a hero of human rights and a luminary of Holocaust literature."

In 2018, antisemitic graffiti were found on the house where Wiesel was born.

Marion Wiesel died on February 2, 2025, at the age of 94.
