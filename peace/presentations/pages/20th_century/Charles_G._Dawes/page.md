**Charles Gates Dawes** (August 27, 1865 – April 23, 1951) was the 30th [vice president of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_president_of_the_United_States "Vice president of the United States") from 1925 to 1929 under President [Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge "Calvin Coolidge"). He was a co-recipient of the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1925 for his work on the [Dawes Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawes_Plan "Dawes Plan") for [World War I reparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I_reparations "World War I reparations"), and a member of the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)").

Born in [Marietta, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marietta,_Ohio "Marietta, Ohio"), Dawes attended [Cincinnati Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Law_School "Cincinnati Law School") before beginning a legal career in [Lincoln, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska "Lincoln, Nebraska"). After serving as a gas plant executive, he managed [William McKinley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_McKinley "William McKinley")'s 1896 presidential campaign in Illinois. After the election, McKinley appointed Dawes as the [Comptroller of the Currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Comptroller_of_the_Currency "Office of the Comptroller of the Currency"). He remained in that position until 1901 before forming the Central Trust Company of Illinois. Dawes served as a general during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") and was the chairman of the general purchasing board for the [American Expeditionary Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Expeditionary_Forces "American Expeditionary Forces"). In 1921, President [Warren G. Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_G._Harding "Warren G. Harding") appointed Dawes as the first director of the [Bureau of the Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Management_and_Budget "Office of Management and Budget"). Dawes served on the Allied Reparations Commission, where he helped formulate the Dawes Plan to aid the struggling [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic") economy.

The [1924 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Republican_National_Convention "1924 Republican National Convention") nominated President [Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge "Calvin Coolidge") without opposition. After former governor of Illinois [Frank O. Lowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_O._Lowden "Frank O. Lowden") declined the vice-presidential nomination, the convention chose Dawes as Coolidge's running mate. The Republican ticket won the [1924 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_United_States_presidential_election "1924 United States presidential election"), and Dawes was sworn in as vice president in 1925. Dawes helped pass the [McNary–Haugen Farm Relief Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McNary–Haugen_Farm_Relief_Bill "McNary–Haugen Farm Relief Bill") in Congress, but President Coolidge vetoed it. Dawes was a candidate for renomination at the [1928 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Republican_National_Convention "1928 Republican National Convention"), but Coolidge's opposition to Dawes helped ensure that [Charles Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Curtis "Charles Curtis") was nominated instead. In 1929, President [Herbert Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover "Herbert Hoover") appointed Dawes to be the [ambassador to the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_ambassador_to_the_United_Kingdom "United States ambassador to the United Kingdom"). Dawes also briefly led the [Reconstruction Finance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_Finance_Corporation "Reconstruction Finance Corporation"), which organized a government response to the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States "Great Depression in the United States"). He resigned from that position in 1932 to return to banking, and died in 1951 of [coronary thrombosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_thrombosis "Coronary thrombosis").

## Early life and family

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Rufus_Dawes_House%2C_4th_Street%2C_Marietta%2C_OH.jpg/250px-Rufus_Dawes_House%2C_4th_Street%2C_Marietta%2C_OH.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rufus_Dawes_House,_4th_Street,_Marietta,_OH.jpg)

Dawes' boyhood home in Marietta, Ohio.

Dawes was born in [Marietta, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marietta,_Ohio "Marietta, Ohio"), in [Washington County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_OH "Washington County, OH"), on August 27, 1865, the son of [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War") General [Rufus Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Dawes "Rufus Dawes") and his wife Mary Beman Gates. Rufus had commanded the 6th Wisconsin Regiment of the [Iron Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Brigade "Iron Brigade") from 1863 to 1864 during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"). His uncle [Ephraim C. Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephraim_C._Dawes "Ephraim C. Dawes") was a major who served under [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant "Ulysses S. Grant") at the [Shiloh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shiloh "Battle of Shiloh") and [Siege of Vicksburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Vicksburg "Siege of Vicksburg"), and was severely wounded at the [Battle of Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dallas "Battle of Dallas"), [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(U.S._state) "Georgia (U.S. state)"), in May 1864.

Dawes's brothers were [Rufus C. Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_C._Dawes "Rufus C. Dawes"), [Beman Gates Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beman_Gates_Dawes "Beman Gates Dawes"), and [Henry May Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_May_Dawes "Henry May Dawes"), all prominent businessmen or politicians. He had two sisters, Mary Frances Dawes Beach, and Betsey Gates Dawes Hoyt.

Dawes was a descendant of [Edward Doty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Doty "Edward Doty"), a passenger on the *[Mayflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayflower "Mayflower")*, [Robert Coe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Coe_(colonist) "Robert Coe (colonist)"), a founder of the [New Haven Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven_Colony "New Haven Colony") and the [New Netherland settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland_settlements "New Netherland settlements"), and [William Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Dawes "William Dawes") who rode with [Paul Revere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Revere "Paul Revere") to warn American colonists of the advancing British army at the outbreak of the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution "American Revolution").

Dawes married [Caro Blymyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caro_Blymyer "Caro Blymyer") on January 24, 1889. They had a son, Rufus Fearing (1890–1912), and a daughter, Carolyn. They later adopted two children, Dana and Virginia.

## Education

He graduated from [Marietta College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marietta_College "Marietta College") in 1884 and [Cincinnati Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Law_School "Cincinnati Law School") in 1886. His fraternity was [Delta Upsilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Upsilon "Delta Upsilon").

## Early business career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Dawes_1.JPG/250px-Dawes_1.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dawes_1.JPG)

From 1909 to 1951, Charles G. Dawes lived in this [house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_G._Dawes_House "Charles G. Dawes House") at 225 Greenwood St. in [Evanston, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanston,_Illinois "Evanston, Illinois"), which was built in 1894 by Robert Sheppard. The house is a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark "National Historic Landmark").

Dawes was admitted to the bar in [Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska "Nebraska"), and he practiced in [Lincoln, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska "Lincoln, Nebraska"), from 1887 to 1894. When Lieutenant [John Pershing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Pershing "John Pershing"), the future army general, was military instructor at the [University of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska–Lincoln "University of Nebraska–Lincoln") he and Dawes met and formed a lifelong friendship. Pershing also received a law degree at Nebraska and proposed leaving the army to go into private practice with Dawes, who cautioned him against giving up the regular army pay for the uncertainty of legal remuneration. Dawes also met [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") Congressman [William Jennings Bryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Jennings_Bryan "William Jennings Bryan"). The two became friends despite their disagreement over [free silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_silver "Free silver") policies.

Dawes relocated from Lincoln to [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") during the [Panic of 1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_of_1893 "Panic of 1893"). In 1894, Dawes acquired interests in several Midwestern gas plants. He became the president of both the La Crosse Gas Light Company in [La Crosse, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Crosse,_Wisconsin "La Crosse, Wisconsin"), and the Northwestern Gas Light and Coke Company in [Evanston, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanston,_Illinois "Evanston, Illinois").

## Interest in music

Dawes was a self-taught [pianist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pianist "Pianist"), [flutist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_concert_flute "Western concert flute") and composer. His composition *Melody in A Major* became a well-known [piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano "Piano") and [violin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin "Violin") piece in 1912. Marie Edwards made a popular arrangement of the work in 1921. Also, in 1921, it was arranged for a small orchestra by [Adolf G. Hoffmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_G._Hoffmann?action=edit&redlink=1 "Adolf G. Hoffmann"). *Melody in A Major* was played at many official functions that Dawes attended.

In 1951, [Carl Sigman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sigman "Carl Sigman") added lyrics to *Melody in A Major*, transforming it into the song "[It's All in the Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_All_in_the_Game_(song) "It's All in the Game (song)")". [Tommy Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Edwards "Tommy Edwards")'s recording of "It's All in the Game" was a number-one hit on the American *[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100")* record chart for six weeks in 1958. Edwards's version of the song became number one on the United Kingdom chart that year.

Since then, it has become a pop standard. Numerous artists have recorded versions, including [Cliff Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff_Richard "Cliff Richard"), [the Four Tops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Four_Tops "The Four Tops"), [Isaac Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Hayes "Isaac Hayes"), [Jackie DeShannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_DeShannon "Jackie DeShannon"), [Van Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Morrison "Van Morrison"), [Nat "King" Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nat_"King"_Cole "Nat \"King\" Cole"), [Brook Benton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brook_Benton "Brook Benton"), and [Keith Jarrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Jarrett "Keith Jarrett").

Dawes is the only U.S. vice president to be credited with a number-one pop hit. Dawes and [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan") (as a writer) are the only persons credited with a number-one pop hit to have also won a Nobel Prize.

Dawes was a brother of [Phi Mu Alpha Sinfonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Mu_Alpha_Sinfonia "Phi Mu Alpha Sinfonia").

## Early political career

Dawes's prominent positions in business caught the attention of Republican party leaders. They asked Dawes to manage the Illinois portion of [William McKinley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_McKinley "William McKinley")'s bid for the [Presidency of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") in [1896](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1896_United_States_presidential_election "1896 United States presidential election"). Following McKinley's election, Dawes was named [Comptroller of the Currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comptroller_of_the_Currency "Comptroller of the Currency"), [United States Department of the Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Treasury "United States Department of the Treasury"). Serving in that position from 1898 to 1901, he collected more than $25 million from banks that had failed during the [Panic of 1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_of_1893 "Panic of 1893") and changed banking practices to try to prevent another panic.

In October 1901, Dawes left the Department of the Treasury to pursue a U.S. Senate seat from Illinois. He thought that, with the help of the McKinley Administration, he could win it. McKinley was assassinated and his successor, President [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt"), preferred Dawes's opponent, [Albert J. Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_J._Hopkins "Albert J. Hopkins"). In 1902, following this unsuccessful attempt at legislative office, Dawes declared that he was done with politics. He organized the Central Trust Company of Illinois, where he served as its president until 1921.

On September 5, 1912, Dawes's 21-year-old son Rufus drowned in [Geneva Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Lake "Geneva Lake"), while on summer break from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"). In his memory, Dawes created [homeless shelters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeless_shelter "Homeless shelter") in both [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") and [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston") and financed the construction of a dormitory at his son's alma mater, the [Lawrenceville School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrenceville_School "Lawrenceville School") in [Lawrenceville, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrenceville,_New_Jersey "Lawrenceville, New Jersey").

## World War I

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Brig._Gen._C._G._Dawes_NARA_-_55209476_%28cropped_2%29.jpg/250px-Brig._Gen._C._G._Dawes_NARA_-_55209476_%28cropped_2%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Brig._Gen._C._G._Dawes_NARA_-_55209476_(cropped_2).jpg)

Dawes in uniform, 1918

Dawes helped support the first [Anglo-French Loan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-French_Financial_Commission "Anglo-French Financial Commission") to the [Entente powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entente_Cordiale "Entente Cordiale") of $500 million. Dawes's support was important because the [House of Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Morgan "House of Morgan") needed public support from a non-Morgan banker. The Morgan banker [Thomas W. Lamont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_W._Lamont "Thomas W. Lamont") said that Dawes's support would "make a position for him in the banking world such as he otherwise could never hope to make". (Loans were seen as possibly violating neutrality, and Wilson was still resisting permitting loans.)

During WWI, Dawes was commissioned as a major on June 11, 1917, in the 17th Engineers. He was subsequently promoted to lieutenant colonel (July 17, 1917), and colonel (January 16, 1918). In October 1918, he was promoted to brigadier general. From August 1917 to August 1919, Dawes served in France during WWI as chairman of the general purchasing board for the American Expeditionary Forces (AEF). His proposal to Gen. Pershing was adopted informed the [Military Board of Allied Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Board_of_Allied_Supply "Military Board of Allied Supply"), on which he served as the American delegate in 1918. When the war ended in November, he became a member of the Liquidation Commission of the [United States War Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_War_Department "United States War Department"). He was decorated with the [Distinguished Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Service_Medal_(U.S._Army) "Distinguished Service Medal (U.S. Army)") and the French [Croix de Guerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croix_de_Guerre "Croix de Guerre") in recognition of his service. He returned to the US aboard the [SS *Leviathan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Leviathan "SS Leviathan") in August 1919. Dawes published a memoir of his World War I service, *A Journal of the Great War*, 1921.

In February 1921, the U.S. Senate held hearings on war expenditures. During heated testimony, Dawes burst out, "Hell and Maria, we weren't trying to keep a set of books over there, we were trying to win a war!" He was later known as "Hell and Maria Dawes" (although he always insisted the expression was "Helen Maria", an exclamation he claimed was common in Nebraska). Dawes resigned from the Army in 1919 and became a member of the [American Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Legion "American Legion").

## 1920s: Financing Europe and the Nobel Peace Prize

He supported [Frank O. Lowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_O._Lowden "Frank O. Lowden") at the [1920 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Republican_National_Convention "1920 Republican National Convention"), but the presidential nomination went to [Warren G. Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_G._Harding "Warren G. Harding"). When the [Bureau of the Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Office_of_Management_and_Budget "United States Office of Management and Budget") was created, he was appointed in 1921 by President Harding as its first director. Secretary of Commerce Herbert Hoover appointed him to the Allied Reparations Commission in 1923. Dawes chaired the group that devised the solution to the European crisis: through the [Dawes Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawes_Plan "Dawes Plan"), American banks loaned large sums of money to Germany. The loans helped Germany's industrial production to recover and the government to make reparation payments to France and Belgium as required by the [Versailles Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles_Treaty "Versailles Treaty"). France and Belgium in turn agreed to withdraw the troops that had been [occupying the Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Ruhr "Occupation of the Ruhr") since January 1923. In 1929 the Reparations Commission under [Owen Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Young "Owen Young") replaced the plan with the more permanent [Young Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Plan "Young Plan"), which reduced the total amount of reparations and called for the removal of [occupying forces from the Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Rhineland "Occupation of the Rhineland"). For his work on the Dawes Plan and the resulting reduction of tensions between France and Germany, Dawes shared the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1925.

## Vice presidency (1925–1929)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Coolidge-Dawes.jpg/250px-Coolidge-Dawes.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Coolidge-Dawes.jpg)

Dawes (right) and [Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge "Calvin Coolidge")

> I should hate to think that the Senate was as tired of me at the beginning of my service as I am of the Senate at the end.

— Charles G. Dawes

At the [1924 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Republican_National_Convention "1924 Republican National Convention"), President [Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge "Calvin Coolidge") was selected almost without opposition to be the Republican presidential nominee. The vice-presidential nominee was more contested. Governor Lowden was nominated, but declined. Coolidge's next choice was Idaho Senator [William Borah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Borah "William Borah"), who also declined the nomination. The Republican National Chairman, William Butler, wanted to nominate then-Commerce Secretary [Herbert Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover "Herbert Hoover"), but he was insufficiently popular. Eventually, the delegates chose Dawes. Coolidge quickly accepted the delegates' choice and felt that Dawes would be loyal to him and make a strong addition to his campaign.

Dawes traveled throughout the country during the campaign, giving speeches to bolster the Republican ticket. On August 22, Dawes would appear at a rally located in [Augusta, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta,_Maine "Augusta, Maine") on the behalf of Republican candidate for Governor [Ralph Owen Brewster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Owen_Brewster "Ralph Owen Brewster"), who was accused by his opponent [William Robinson Pattangall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Robinson_Pattangall "William Robinson Pattangall") of being backed by the [Ku Klux Klan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ku_Klux_Klan "Ku Klux Klan") and having sympathies for them. Dawes, who was challenged by Pattangall to talk on the issue, gave a speech attacking the Klan and its religious and racial prejudice rhetoric (Dawes was however careful on how he talked about race). He frequently attacked [Progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Party_(United_States,_1924–1934) "Progressive Party (United States, 1924–1934)") nominee [Robert M. La Follette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._La_Follette "Robert M. La Follette") as a dangerous radical who sympathized with the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks "Bolsheviks"). The Coolidge-Dawes ticket was elected on November 4, 1924, with more popular votes than the candidates of the Democratic and Progressive parties combined. The inauguration was held on March 4, 1925.

### Speech to Senate

When Dawes took the oath on March 4, he would take action and infamously go on a tangent against the Senate's [filibuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filibuster "Filibuster"). In the speech, Dawes criticized rule XXII, calling it "undemocratic" and noted how it was easily taken advantage of due to its two-thirds voting procedure. Through most of the speech, Dawes pointed at specific senators and repeatedly slammed his fist on a table. [Chief Justice William Howard Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft "William Howard Taft") wrote to his son that the vice president had "made a monkey out of himself." Alongside annoying the entire Senate with a speech that left many shocked, Dawes ended up irritating them again that same day, by having the senators be sworn in one by one (usually they would take the oath in groups). Dawes would end up stealing the thunder from Coolidge that day. With many in the press afterwards making a joke out of Dawes, Coolidge was very upset with how the vice president was starting off his term.

### Nomination of Charles B. Warren

On March 10, the Senate debated the president's nomination of [Charles B. Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_B._Warren "Charles B. Warren") to be [United States Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Attorney_General "United States Attorney General"). In the wake of the [Teapot Dome scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teapot_Dome_scandal "Teapot Dome scandal") and other scandals, Democrats and Progressive Republicans objected to the nomination because of Warren's close association with the [Sugar Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Sugar_Refining_Company "American Sugar Refining Company"). At midday, six speakers were scheduled to address Warren's nomination. Desiring to take a break for a nap, Dawes consulted the majority and minority leaders, who assured him that no vote would be taken that afternoon. After Dawes left the Senate, all but one of the scheduled speakers decided against making formal remarks, and a vote was taken. When it became apparent that the vote would be tied, Republican leaders hastily called Dawes at the Willard Hotel, and he immediately left for the Capitol. The first vote was 40-40, a tie which Dawes could have broken in Warren's favor. While waiting for Dawes to arrive, the only Democratic senator who had voted for Warren switched his vote. The nomination then failed 41-39—the first such rejection of a president's nominee in nearly 60 years. This incident was chronicled in a derisive poem, based on the Longfellow poem "[Paul Revere's Ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Revere's_Ride "Paul Revere's Ride")"; it began with the line, "Come gather round children and hold your applause for the afternoon ride of Charlie Dawes." The choice of poem was based on Charles Dawes being descended from [William Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Dawes "William Dawes"), who rode with Paul Revere.

Dawes and Coolidge became alienated from one another. Dawes declined to attend Cabinet meetings and annoyed Coolidge with his attack on the Senate [filibuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filibuster_in_the_United_States_Senate "Filibuster in the United States Senate"). Dawes championed the [McNary–Haugen Farm Relief Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McNary–Haugen_Farm_Relief_Bill "McNary–Haugen Farm Relief Bill"), which sought to alleviate the [1920s farm crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920s_farm_crisis "1920s farm crisis") by having the government buy surplus farm produce and sell that surplus in foreign markets. Dawes helped ensure the passage of the bill through Congress, but President Coolidge vetoed it.

In 1927, Coolidge announced that he would not seek re-election. Dawes again favored Frank Lowden at the [1928 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Republican_National_Convention "1928 Republican National Convention"), but the convention chose [Herbert Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover "Herbert Hoover"). Rumors circulated about Dawes being chosen as Hoover's running mate. Coolidge made it known that he would consider the renomination of Dawes as vice president to be an insult. [Charles Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Curtis "Charles Curtis") of Kansas, known for his skills in collaboration, was chosen as Hoover's running mate.

## Post-vice presidency (1929–1951)

### Court of St. James's and the RFC

After Dawes completed his term as vice president, he served as the [U.S. Ambassador to the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Ambassador_to_the_United_Kingdom "U.S. Ambassador to the United Kingdom") (known formally as the [Court of St. James's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_St._James's "Court of St. James's")) from 1929 to 1931. Overall, Dawes was an effective ambassador, as [George V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_V "George V")'s son, the future [Edward VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VIII "Edward VIII"), later confirmed in his memoirs. Dawes was rather rough-hewn for some of his duties, disliking presenting American débutantes to the King. On his first visit to the royal court, in deference to American public opinion, he refused to wear the customary Court dress, which then included knee breeches. This episode was said to upset the King, who had been prevented by illness from attending the event.

As the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression "Great Depression") continued to ravage the US, Dawes accepted President [Herbert Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover "Herbert Hoover")'s appeal to leave diplomatic office and head the newly created [Reconstruction Finance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_Finance_Corporation "Reconstruction Finance Corporation") (RFC). After a few months, Dawes resigned from the RFC. As chairman of the failing Central Republic Bank and Trust Company of Chicago, he felt obligated to work for its rescue. Political opponents alleged that, under Dawes's leadership, the RFC had given preferential treatment to his bank. This marked the end of Dawes's career in public service. For the [1932 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_United_States_presidential_election "1932 United States presidential election"), Hoover considered the possibility of adding Dawes to the ticket in place of Curtis, but Dawes declined the potential offer.

Later in 1932, Dawes and associates formed the City National Bank and Trust Co. to take over the deposits of the failed Central Republic Bank and Trust Company. In 1936, Republican congressional leaders informally approached Dawes about the possibility of heading up their presidential ticket at [that year's presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_presidential_election "1936 United States presidential election"), hoping for a candidate associated with the prosperous Coolidge years, but Dawes had no interest in returning to front-line politics; the (ultimately unsuccessful) ticket would instead be headed by [Alf Landon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_Landon "Alf Landon").

## Later life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Grave_of_Charles_Gates_Dawes_%281865%E2%80%931951%29_and_Caro_Dana_Dawes_%281866%E2%80%931957%29_at_Rosehill_Cemetery%2C_Chicago.jpg/250px-Grave_of_Charles_Gates_Dawes_%281865%E2%80%931951%29_and_Caro_Dana_Dawes_%281866%E2%80%931957%29_at_Rosehill_Cemetery%2C_Chicago.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Grave_of_Charles_Gates_Dawes_(1865–1951)_and_Caro_Dana_Dawes_(1866–1957)_at_Rosehill_Cemetery,_Chicago.jpg)

Dawes mausoleum at Rosehill Cemetery

Dawes served for nearly two decades as chairman of the board of City National from 1932 until his death. He died on April 23, 1951, at his Evanston home from [coronary thrombosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_thrombosis "Coronary thrombosis") at the age of 85. He is interred in [Rosehill Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosehill_Cemetery,_Chicago "Rosehill Cemetery, Chicago"), Chicago.

## Personal life

Dawes belonged to several lineage societies and veterans' organizations. He became a First Class Companion of the Illinois Commandery of the [Military Order of the Loyal Legion of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Order_of_the_Loyal_Legion_of_the_United_States "Military Order of the Loyal Legion of the United States") in succession to his father, who died in 1899. He also belonged to the [Sons of the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_the_American_Revolution "Sons of the American Revolution"), [General Society of Colonial Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Society_of_Colonial_Wars "General Society of Colonial Wars"), [American Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Legion "American Legion"), and [Forty and Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forty_and_Eight "Forty and Eight").
Dawes was also a member of the [Ancient and Honorable Artillery Company of Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_and_Honorable_Artillery_Company_of_Massachusetts "Ancient and Honorable Artillery Company of Massachusetts") from 1925 until his death in 1951.

## Honors

- In 1925, Dawes was a co-winner of the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") for his work on WWI reparations.

A Chicago public school located at 3810 W 81st Place is named in his honor, as are an Evanston public school at 440 Dodge Avenue and Evanston's Dawes Park at 1700 Sheridan Road.

Distinguished Service Medal citation:

> The President of the United States of America, authorized by Act of Congress, July 9, 1918, takes pleasure in presenting the Army Distinguished Service Medal to Brigadier General Charles G. Dawes, United States Army, for exceptionally meritorious and distinguished services to the Government of the United States, in a duty of great responsibility during World War I. General Dawes rendered most conspicuous services in the organization of the General Purchasing Board as General Purchasing Agent of the American Expeditionary Forces and as the Representative of the U.S. Army on the Military Board of Allied Supply. His rare abilities, sound business judgment, and aggressive energy were invaluable in securing needed supplies for the Allied armies in Europe. (War Department, General Orders No. 12 (1919))

## Legacy

According to Annette Dunlap, Dawes was:

> a self-made man who valued hard work and thriftiness tempered with Christian generosity. He spent his life promoting solid Republican values of small government with restrained budgets. Franklin Roosevelt's philosophy of big government spending was anathema to him.

In 1944, he bequeathed his lakeshore home in Evanston to [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University") for the Evanston Historical Society (later renamed the Evanston History Center). Dawes lived in the house until his death. The Dawes family continued to occupy it until the death of Mrs. Dawes in 1957. Since then, the Evanston History Center operates out of the house and manages it as a museum. Designated a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark "National Historic Landmark"), the [Charles G. Dawes House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_G._Dawes_House "Charles G. Dawes House") is listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places "National Register of Historic Places").
