**Lars Olof Jonathan Söderblom** (Swedish pronunciation: [[ˈnɑ̌ːtan ˈsø̌ːdɛrblʊm]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Swedish "Help:IPA/Swedish"); 15 January 1866 – 12 July 1931) was a [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedes "Swedes") bishop. He was the [Church of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Sweden "Church of Sweden") [Archbishop of Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Uppsala "Archbishop of Uppsala") from 1914 to 1931, and recipient of the 1930 [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"). He is commemorated in the [Calendar of Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar_of_Saints_(Lutheran) "Calendar of Saints (Lutheran)") of the [Lutheran Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church "Lutheran Church") on 12 July.

## Life and career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Nathan_Soderblom.jpg/250px-Nathan_Soderblom.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nathan_Soderblom.jpg)

Archbishop Nathan Söderblom in 1923.

Söderblom was born in the village of Trönö in [Söderhamn Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Söderhamn_Municipality "Söderhamn Municipality"), [Gävleborg County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gävleborg_County "Gävleborg County"). His father was a parish priest. He enrolled at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University") in 1883. Although not initially convinced what he wanted to study, he eventually decided to follow in his father's footsteps. On returning from a journey to the United States, he was ordained as a priest in 1893. During the years 1892 and 1893, he was first vice president and then president of the [Uppsala Student Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_Student_Union "Uppsala Student Union").

From 1894 to 1901, he had a ministry position at the Swedish Embassy in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), where his congregation included both [Alfred Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Nobel "Alfred Nobel") (1833–1896) and [August Strindberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Strindberg "August Strindberg") (1849–1912). In 1897, he officiated at the memorial service for Nobel. From 1901 to 1914, Söderblom held a chair in the School of Theology at Uppsala University and from 1912 to 1914 was also a professor of [religious studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_studies "Religious studies") at [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University "Leipzig University"). In 1914, he was elected as [Archbishop of Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Uppsala "Archbishop of Uppsala"), the head of the [Lutheran church in Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Sweden "Church of Sweden"). During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War"), he called on all Christian leaders to work for peace and justice while working to alleviate the conditions of prisoners of war and refugees.

He believed that church unity had the specific purpose of presenting the gospel to the world and that the messages of Jesus were relevant to social life. His leadership of the Christian "[Life and Work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_and_Work_(conference) "Life and Work (conference)")" movement in the 1920s has led him to be recognised as one of the principal founders of the [ecumenical movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenism "Ecumenism"). He had begun the movement toward intercommunion between the [Church of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Sweden "Church of Sweden") and the [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England "Church of England") and was a close associate of the English ecumenist [George Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bell_(bishop) "George Bell (bishop)") (1883–1958), Dean of Canterbury, Bishop of Chichester. He was instrumental in chairing the [World Conference of Life and Work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Conference_of_Life_and_Work "World Conference of Life and Work") in Stockholm, in 1925. In 1930 he was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize").
