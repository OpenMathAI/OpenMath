**Alfred Hermann Fried** (German pronunciation: [[ˈʔalfʁeːt ˈhɛʁman ˈfʁiːt]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German"); 11 November 1864 – 4 May 1921) was an Austrian Jewish [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifist "Pacifist"), publicist, journalist, co-founder of the German peace movement, and winner (with [Tobias Asser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_Asser "Tobias Asser")) of the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1911. Fried was also a supporter of [Esperanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanto "Esperanto"). He is the author of an Esperanto textbook and an Esperanto–German and German–Esperanto dictionary, first published in 1903 and republished in 1905.

## Life

Born in Vienna, [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire "Austrian Empire"), Fried left school at the age of 15 and started to work in a bookshop. In 1883, he moved to Berlin, where he opened a bookshop of his own in 1887. Following the publication by [Bertha von Suttner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertha_von_Suttner "Bertha von Suttner") of *[Die Waffen nieder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Waffen_nieder "Die Waffen nieder")!* (*Lay Down Your Arms*) in 1889, he and von Suttner began in 1892 to print a magazine of the same name. In articles published within *Die Waffen nieder!* and its successor, *[Die Friedenswarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Friedenswarte "Die Friedenswarte")* (*The Peace Watch*), he articulated his pacifist philosophy.

In 1892 he was a co-founder of the German Peace Society ([Deutsche Friedensgesellschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Friedensgesellschaft "Deutsche Friedensgesellschaft")). He was one of the fathers of the idea of a modern organisation to assure worldwide peace (the principal idea was developed in the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") and after the Second World War in the [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations")).

Fried was a prominent member of the [Esperanto movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanto_in_Austria "Esperanto in Austria"). In 1903 he published the book *Lehrbuch der internationalen Hilfssprache Esperanto* (*Textbook of the International Auxiliary Language Esperanto*).

In 1909 he collaborated with [Paul Otlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Otlet "Paul Otlet") and [Henri La Fontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_La_Fontaine "Henri La Fontaine") of the [Central Office of International Associations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_International_Associations "Union of International Associations") in the preparation of the *Annuaire de la Vie Internationale*.

In 1911 he received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") together with [Tobias Asser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_Asser "Tobias Asser").

During World War I, Fried moved to neutral Switzerland, and continued to advocate [international peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_peace "World peace"). He died in his hometown, Vienna, in 1921. His ashes are buried at [Feuerhalle Simmering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feuerhalle_Simmering "Feuerhalle Simmering").
