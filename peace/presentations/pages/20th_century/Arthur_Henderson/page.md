**Arthur Henderson** (13 September 1863 – 20 October 1935) was a British [iron moulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_moulder "Iron moulder") and [Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") [politician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politician "Politician"). He was the first Labour [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_Kingdom "Cabinet of the United Kingdom") minister, won the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1934 and, uniquely, served three separate terms as [Leader of the Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Labour_Party_(UK) "Leader of the Labour Party (UK)") in three different decades, and was elected to parliament in five by-elections in different constituencies. He was popular among his colleagues, who called him "Uncle Arthur" in acknowledgement of his integrity, his devotion to the cause and his imperturbability. He was a transitional figure whose policies were, at first, close to those of the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK) "Liberal Party (UK)"). The [trades unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trades_unions "Trades unions") rejected his emphasis on arbitration and conciliation, and thwarted his goal of unifying the Labour Party and the trade unions.

## Early life

Arthur Henderson was born at 10 Paterson Street, [Anderston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderston "Anderston"), [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow "Glasgow"), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland "Scotland"), in 1863, the son of Agnes, a domestic servant, and David Henderson, a textile worker who died when Arthur was ten years old. After his father's death, the Hendersons moved to [Newcastle upon Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne "Newcastle upon Tyne") in the [North-East of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-East_of_England "North-East of England"), where Agnes later married Robert Heath.

Henderson worked at Robert Stephenson and Sons' General Foundry Works from the age of twelve. After finishing his apprenticeship there aged seventeen, he moved to [Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton "Southampton") for a year and then returned to work as an [iron moulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_moulder "Iron moulder") (a type of [foundryman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundryman "Foundryman")) in Newcastle upon Tyne.

Henderson became a [Wesleyan Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_Methodist_Church_(Great_Britain) "Wesleyan Methodist Church (Great Britain)") in 1879, having previously been a [Congregationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregationalist "Congregationalist"), and became a [Methodist local preacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_local_preacher "Methodist local preacher"). After losing his job in 1884, he concentrated on preaching.

## Union leader and Liberal Party activist

In 1892, Henderson entered the complex world of trade union politics when he was elected as a paid organiser for the [Friendly Society of Iron Founders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendly_Society_of_Iron_Founders "Friendly Society of Iron Founders"). He also became a representative on the North East Conciliation Board. Henderson believed that [strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_action "Strike action") caused more harm than they were worth and tried to avoid them whenever he could. For this reason, he opposed the formation of the [General Federation of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Federation_of_Trade_Unions_(UK) "General Federation of Trade Unions (UK)"), as he was convinced that it would lead to more strikes. In 1893 he also became active in politics, becoming a local Councillor on [Newcastle City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_City_Council "Newcastle City Council") for the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK) "Liberal Party (UK)"). In 1896 he served as the [election agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_agent "Election agent") for Sir [Joseph Pease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Joseph_Pease,_1st_Baronet "Sir Joseph Pease, 1st Baronet"), Liberal Party MP for [Barnard Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_Castle_(constituency) "Barnard Castle (constituency)"), County Durham.

## The Labour Representation Committee

In 1900 Henderson was one of the 129 trade union and socialist delegates who passed [Keir Hardie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keir_Hardie "Keir Hardie")'s motion to create the [Labour Representation Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Labour_Party#Labour_Representation_Committee_(1900–1906) "British Labour Party") (LRC). In 1903, Henderson was elected Treasurer of the LRC and was also elected as [Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom) "Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)") (MP) for [Barnard Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_Castle_(constituency) "Barnard Castle (constituency)") at a [by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1903_Barnard_Castle_by-election "1903 Barnard Castle by-election"). From 1903 to 1904, Henderson also served as mayor of [Darlington, County Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlington,_County_Durham "Darlington, County Durham").

## The Labour Party

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/97/Labour_Representation_Committee_leaders_1906.jpg/250px-Labour_Representation_Committee_leaders_1906.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Labour_Representation_Committee_leaders_1906.jpg)

Henderson (on left) in 1906, with other leading figures in the new Labour Party

In 1906, the LRC changed its name to the Labour Party after it won 29 seats at the [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1906_United_Kingdom_general_election "1906 United Kingdom general election"). In 1908, when Hardie resigned as Leader of the Labour Party, Henderson was elected to replace him. He remained Leader until his own resignation two years later, in 1910.

## Cabinet Minister

In 1914 the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War") broke out and [Ramsay MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsay_MacDonald "Ramsay MacDonald") resigned from the [Leadership of the Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Labour_Party_(UK) "Leader of the Labour Party (UK)") in protest. Henderson was elected to replace him. The two became enemies.

In 1915, following Prime Minister [H. H. Asquith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._H._Asquith "H. H. Asquith")'s decision to create a [coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asquith_coalition_ministry "Asquith coalition ministry"), Henderson became the first member of the Labour Party to become a member of the Cabinet, as [President of the Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Board_of_Education "President of the Board of Education"). In this capacity he proposed the foundation of the [Department of Scientific and Industrial Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Scientific_and_Industrial_Research_(United_Kingdom) "Department of Scientific and Industrial Research (United Kingdom)").

In 1916, [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George "David Lloyd George") forced Asquith to resign and replaced him as Prime Minister. Henderson became a member of the small [War Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Cabinet "War Cabinet") with the post of [Minister without Portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_without_portfolio_(United_Kingdom) "Minister without portfolio (United Kingdom)") on 9 December 1916. The other Labour representatives who joined Henderson in Lloyd George's coalition government were [John Hodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hodge_(politician) "John Hodge (politician)"), who became [Minister of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Employment "Secretary of State for Employment"), and [George Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Barnes_(British_politician) "George Barnes (British politician)"), who became [Minister of Pensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Work_and_Pensions "Secretary of State for Work and Pensions"). Henderson resigned on 11 August 1917 after his proposal for an international conference on the war was rejected by the rest of the Cabinet. The [Labour National Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_National_Executive_Committee "Labour National Executive Committee") had rejected the [Second International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_International "Second International")'s request for a meeting of European socialist parties on the war in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm"), but after Henderson convinced it to give provisional support after visiting the [Russian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Republic "Russian Republic") as an envoy and recognizing that the [Russian Provisional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Provisional_Government "Russian Provisional Government") would collapse if the war continued.

In August 1917, three months before the [Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration "Balfour Declaration"), the Labour Party issued a statement in support of a [Jewish state in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_state "Jewish state"). Henderson spoke in favor of a Jewish state.

Henderson turned his attention to building a strong constituency-based support network for the Labour Party. Previously, it had little national organisation, based largely on branches of unions and socialist societies. Working with Ramsay MacDonald and Sidney Webb, Henderson in 1918 established a national network of constituency organisations. They operated separately from trade unions and the National Executive Committee and were open to everyone sympathetic to the party's policies. Secondly, Henderson secured the adoption of a comprehensive statement of party policies, as drafted by [Sidney Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Webb "Sidney Webb"). Entitled "Labour and the New Social Order," it remained the basic Labour platform until 1950. It proclaimed a socialist party whose principles included a guaranteed minimum standard of living for everyone, nationalisation of industry, and heavy taxation of large incomes and of wealth.

## The "Coupon Election" and the 1920s

Henderson lost his seat in the ["Coupon Election" of 14 December 1918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1918_United_Kingdom_general_election "1918 United Kingdom general election"), which had been announced within twenty-four hours of the end of hostilities and which resulted in a landslide victory for a [coalition formed by Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_George_ministry "Lloyd George ministry"). Henderson returned to Parliament in 1919 after winning [a by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919_Widnes_by-election "1919 Widnes by-election") in [Widnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widnes_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Widnes (UK Parliament constituency)"). He then became Labour's [Chief Whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whip_(politics) "Whip (politics)").

[Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin "Vladimir Lenin") held Henderson in very low regard. In a letter to the Soviet Commissar for Foreign Affairs, [Georgy Chicherin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgy_Chicherin "Georgy Chicherin"), written on 10 February 1922 and referring to the [Genoa Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_Conference "Genoa Conference"), Lenin wrote: "Henderson is as stupid as [Kerensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerensky "Kerensky"), and for this reason he is helping us."

Henderson [lost his seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unseating "Unseating") again, at the [general election of 1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_United_Kingdom_general_election "1922 United Kingdom general election"). He returned to Parliament via [another by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_Newcastle-upon-Tyne_East_by-election "1923 Newcastle-upon-Tyne East by-election"), this time representing [Newcastle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne_East_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Newcastle upon Tyne East (UK Parliament constituency)"), but again, he was [unseated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unseated "Unseated") at [the general election of 1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_United_Kingdom_general_election "1923 United Kingdom general election"). He returned to Parliament just two months later after winning [another by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Burnley_by-election "1924 Burnley by-election") in [Burnley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnley_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Burnley (UK Parliament constituency)").

In 1924, Henderson was appointed as [Home Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Secretary "Home Secretary") in [the first-ever Labour government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_MacDonald_ministry "First MacDonald ministry"), led by MacDonald. This government was defeated later the same year and lost [the general election that followed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_United_Kingdom_general_election "1924 United Kingdom general election").

Having been re-elected in 1924, Henderson refused to challenge MacDonald for the party leadership. Worried about factionalism in the Labour Party, he published a pamphlet, *Labour and the Nation*, in which he attempted to clarify the party's goals.

## Foreign Secretary

In 1929, Labour formed [another minority government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_MacDonald_ministry "Second MacDonald ministry") and MacDonald appointed Henderson as [Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_Affairs_(UK) "Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs (UK)"), a position Henderson used to try to reduce the tensions that had been building up in Europe since the end of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War"). Diplomatic relations were re-established with the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") and Henderson guaranteed Britain's full support to the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations").

## The MacDonald "betrayal"

The [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_Kingdom "Great Depression in the United Kingdom") plunged the government into a terminal crisis. The Cabinet agreed that it was essential to maintain the [Gold Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Standard "Gold Standard") and that the [Budget needed to be balanced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balanced_budget "Balanced budget"), but were divided over reducing [unemployment benefits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_benefits "Unemployment benefits") by 10%. At first, Henderson gave strong support to Prime Minister [MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsay_MacDonald "Ramsay MacDonald") throughout the financial and political crisis of August. The financial crisis across Europe was worsening and [Britain's gold reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_reserves_of_the_United_Kingdom "Gold reserves of the United Kingdom") were at high risk. New York banks provided an emergency loan; but additional money was needed and to get it, the budget had to be balanced. MacDonald and Chancellor of the Exchequer [Philip Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Snowden "Philip Snowden") proposed cuts in unemployment benefits. Henderson rejected that solution and became the leader of nearly half the Cabinet. The Labour Cabinet decided to resign. King [George V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_V "George V") implored MacDonald to remain and form an all-party [National Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Government_(United_Kingdom) "National Government (United Kingdom)") that would make the budget cuts. MacDonald agreed on 24 August 1931 and formed an emergency [National Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Government_(1931) "National Government (1931)"), with members from all parties. The new cabinet had four Labour members (now called the "[National Labour Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Labour_Organisation "National Labour Organisation")") who stood with MacDonald, plus four [Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)") and two [Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK) "Liberal Party (UK)"). Labour unions were strongly opposed and the Labour Party officially repudiated the new National government. It expelled MacDonald and his supporters from the party. Henderson cast the only vote against the expulsions. Against his inclinations, Henderson accepted the leadership of the main Labour Party and led it into [the general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_United_Kingdom_general_election "1931 United Kingdom general election") on 27 October against the cross-party National coalition. It was a disastrous result for Labour, which was reduced to a small minority of 52. Yet again Henderson lost his seat, at Burnley. The following year, he relinquished the party leadership.

## Later career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/World_Disarmament_Conference_1932.jpg/250px-World_Disarmament_Conference_1932.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:World_Disarmament_Conference_1932.jpg)

Henderson speaking at the [World Disarmament Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Disarmament_Conference "World Disarmament Conference") on 2 February 1932

Henderson returned to Parliament after winning [a by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_Clay_Cross_by-election "1933 Clay Cross by-election") at [Clay Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Cross_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Clay Cross (UK Parliament constituency)"), achieving the unique feat of being elected five times at [by-elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By-election "By-election") in constituencies where he had not previously been the MP. He holds the [record for the greatest number of comebacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Records_of_members_of_parliament_of_the_United_Kingdom#MPs_who_have_made_more_than_one_comeback "Records of members of parliament of the United Kingdom") from losing a previous seat.

Henderson spent the rest of his life trying to halt the gathering storm of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). He worked with the World League of Peace and chaired the [Geneva Disarmament Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Disarmament_Conference "Geneva Disarmament Conference"), and in 1934 he was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"). Henderson's [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") was stolen in a burglary of the office of the [Lord Mayor of Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mayor_of_Newcastle-upon-Tyne "Lord Mayor of Newcastle-upon-Tyne") on 3 April 2013. A man was subsequently jailed for the theft; the medal has never been recovered.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Arthur_Henderson%2C_Golders_Green_Crematorium.jpg/250px-Arthur_Henderson%2C_Golders_Green_Crematorium.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Arthur_Henderson,_Golders_Green_Crematorium.jpg)

Plaque dedicated to Henderson, his wife and sons at Golders Green Crematorium

Henderson died in 1935, aged 72, and was cremated at [Golders Green Crematorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_Crematorium "Golders Green Crematorium"). All three of Henderson's sons saw military service during the Great War, the eldest, David, being killed in action in 1916 whilst serving as a Captain with the [Middlesex Regiment (Duke of Cambridge's Own)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex_Regiment_(Duke_of_Cambridge's_Own) "Middlesex Regiment (Duke of Cambridge's Own)"). His surviving sons also became Labour politicians: second son [William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henderson,_1st_Baron_Henderson "William Henderson, 1st Baron Henderson") was granted the title of Baron Henderson in 1945, while his third son, [Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Henderson,_Baron_Rowley "Arthur Henderson, Baron Rowley"), was created Baron Rowley in 1966.

The Labour History Archive and Study Centre at the [People's History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_History_Museum "People's History Museum") in [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester "Manchester") holds the papers of Arthur Henderson in their collection, spanning from 1915 to 1935.
