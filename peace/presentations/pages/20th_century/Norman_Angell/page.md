**Sir Ralph Norman Angell** (26 December 1872 – 7 October 1967) was a lecturer, journalist, author and [Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom) "Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)") for the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Labour_Party_(UK) "The Labour Party (UK)"). He was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") for his efforts to promote peace, particularly through writings that argued that modern economic interdependence made war irrational and self-defeating.

Angell was one of the principal founders of the [Union of Democratic Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Democratic_Control "Union of Democratic Control"). He served on the Council of the [Royal Institute of International Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_International_Affairs "Royal Institute of International Affairs"), was an executive for the [World Committee against War and Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Committee_against_War_and_Fascism "World Committee against War and Fascism"), a member of the executive committee of the [League of Nations Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_Union "League of Nations Union"), and the president of the Abyssinia Association. He was knighted in 1931 and awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1933.

Angell is most remembered for his 1910 book *[The Great Illusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Illusion "The Great Illusion")*, the thesis of which is that the [economic integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_integration "Economic integration") of the European countries had grown to such a degree that war between them would be entirely futile, making militarism obsolete. Angell was frequently misunderstood at the time, and afterward, as claiming that a general European war was impossible. Because of this widespread misunderstanding, the advent of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") exposed Angell to scholarly and popular derision.

## Life and career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/High_Street%2C_Holbeach_%28geograph_4099230%29.jpg/250px-High_Street%2C_Holbeach_%28geograph_4099230%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:High_Street,_Holbeach_(geograph_4099230).jpg)

Angell's birthplace on High Street, Holbeach, marked by a blue plaque

Angell was the sixth child born to Thomas Angell Lane and Mary (née Brittain) Lane in [Holbeach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holbeach "Holbeach"), Lincolnshire, England. He was born **Ralph Norman Angell Lane**, but later adopted **Angell** as his sole surname. He attended several schools in England, the [Lycée Alexandre Ribot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Alexandre_Ribot "Lycée Alexandre Ribot") at [Saint-Omer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Omer,_Pas-de-Calais "Saint-Omer, Pas-de-Calais") in France, and the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva "University of Geneva"), while editing an English-language newspaper published in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva "Geneva").

In Geneva, Angell felt that Europe was "hopelessly entangled in insoluble problems". Then, still only 17, he emigrated to the West Coast of the United States, where for several years he worked as a vine planter, an irrigation-ditch digger, a cowboy, a California [homesteader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Act "Homestead Act") (after filing for American citizenship), a mail carrier, a [prospector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospecting "Prospecting"), and then, closer to his natural skills, as a reporter for the *[St. Louis Globe-Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Globe-Democrat "St. Louis Globe-Democrat")* and later the *[San Francisco Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Chronicle "San Francisco Chronicle")*.

Due to family matters he returned to England briefly in 1898, then moved to Paris to work as a sub-editor of the English-language *[Daily Messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Messenger "Daily Messenger")* and then as a staff contributor to the newspaper *Éclair*. Also during this period he acted as French correspondent for some American newspapers, to which he sent dispatches on the progress of the [Dreyfus affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreyfus_affair "Dreyfus affair"). During 1905–12, he became the Paris editor for the *[Daily Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Mail "Daily Mail")*.

He returned to England and, in 1914, co-founded the [Union of Democratic Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Democratic_Control "Union of Democratic Control"). He joined the Labour Party in 1920 and was parliamentary candidate for [Rushcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushcliffe_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Rushcliffe (UK Parliament constituency)") in the [general election of 1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_United_Kingdom_general_election "1922 United Kingdom general election") and for [Rossendale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rossendale_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Rossendale (UK Parliament constituency)") in [1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_United_Kingdom_general_election "1923 United Kingdom general election"). He was MP for [Bradford North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_North "Bradford North") from 1929 to 1931; after the formation of the [National Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Government_(United_Kingdom) "National Government (United Kingdom)"), he announced his decision not to seek reelection on 24 September 1931. In the [1931 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_New_Year_Honours "1931 New Year Honours") he was made a [Knight Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") for public and political services, and in 1933 he received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"). He stood unsuccessfully for the [London University seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_University_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "London University (UK Parliament constituency)") in [1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_United_Kingdom_general_election "1935 United Kingdom general election").

From the mid-1930s, Angell actively campaigned for collective international opposition to the aggressive policies of Germany, Italy, and Japan. He went to the United States in 1940 to lecture in favour of American support for Britain in World War II, and remained there until after the publication of his autobiography in 1951. He later returned to Britain and died at the age of 94 in [Croydon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croydon "Croydon"), Surrey.

He married Beatrice Cuvellier in 1899, but they separated and he lived his last 55 years alone. He purchased [Northey Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northey_Island "Northey Island"), Essex, which is attached to the mainland only at low tide, and lived in the island's sole dwelling.

Angell's [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") was sold at auction at [Sotheby's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotheby's "Sotheby's"), London, in 1983 for £8,000 (equivalent to £27,200 in 2025), being bought by his nephew, Eric Angell Lane. The medal, with its accompanying scroll, is now in the collection of the [Imperial War Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_War_Museum "Imperial War Museum").

## *The Great Illusion*

Angell is most remembered for his 1909 pamphlet *Europe's Optical Illusion,* which was published the next year (and many years thereafter) as the book *[The Great Illusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Illusion "The Great Illusion")*. (The anti-war film *[La Grande Illusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Grande_Illusion "La Grande Illusion")* took its title from his pamphlet.) The book's thesis is that the [economic integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_integration "Economic integration") of the European countries had grown to such a degree that war between them would be entirely futile, making militarism obsolete. This quotation from the "Synopsis" to the popular 1913 edition summarizes his basic argument.

> He establishes this apparent paradox, in so far as the economic problem is concerned, by showing that wealth in the economically civilized world is founded upon credit and commercial contract (these being the outgrowth of an economic interdependence due to the increasing division of labour and greatly developed communication). If credit and commercial contract are tampered with in an attempt at confiscation, the credit-dependent wealth is undermined, and its collapse involves that of the conqueror; so that if conquest is not to be self-injurious it must respect the enemy’s property, in which case it becomes economically futile. Thus the wealth of conquered territory remains in the hands of the population of such territory. When Germany annexed Alsace, no individual German secured a single mark’s worth of Alsatian property as the spoils of war. Conquest in the modern world is a process of multiplying by x, and then obtaining the original figure by dividing by x. For a modern nation to add to its territory no more adds to the wealth of the people of such nation than it would add to the wealth of Londoners if the City of London were to annex the county of Hertford.

During World War I, British historian and polemicist [G. G. Coulton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._G._Coulton "G. G. Coulton") authored a purported refutation of Angell's pamphlet.

## *The Money Game*

Angell was also the designer of *The Money Game*, a visual method of teaching schoolchildren the fundamentals of finance and banking. First published in 1928 by [J. M. Dent & Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._M._Dent_&_Sons "J. M. Dent & Sons"), *The Money Game, How to Play It: A New Instrument of Economic Education* was both a book and a game. The bulk of the book was an essay on money and a discussion of economic theory. It also contained a summary of the game's story and an explanation of the rules.

## Influence

Angell's book *The Press and the Organisation of Society* is cited as a source in [F. R. Leavis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._R._Leavis "F. R. Leavis")' pamphlet *Mass Civilisation and Minority Culture* (1930). [Vera Brittain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Brittain "Vera Brittain") quoted Angell's statement on "the moral obligation to be intelligent" several times in her work.
