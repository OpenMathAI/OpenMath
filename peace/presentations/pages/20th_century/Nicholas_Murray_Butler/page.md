**Nicholas Murray Butler** (April 2, 1862 – December 7, 1947) was an American philosopher, diplomat, and educator. Butler was president of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), president of the [Carnegie Endowment for International Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Endowment_for_International_Peace "Carnegie Endowment for International Peace"), a recipient of the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"), and the late [James S. Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_S._Sherman "James S. Sherman")'s replacement as [William Howard Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft "William Howard Taft")'s running mate in the [1912 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_United_States_presidential_election "1912 United States presidential election"). *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* printed his Christmas greeting to the nation for many years during the 1920s and 1930s.

## Early life and education

Butler, great-grandson of [Morgan John Rhys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_John_Rhys "Morgan John Rhys"), was born in Elizabeth, New Jersey, to Mary Butler and manufacturing worker Henry Butler. He enrolled in [Columbia College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_College_of_Columbia_University "Columbia College of Columbia University") (later Columbia University) and joined the [Peithologian Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peithologian_Society "Peithologian Society"). He earned his bachelor's degree in 1882, his master's degree in 1883, and his doctorate in 1884. Butler's academic and other achievements led [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt") to call him "Nicholas Miraculous". In 1885, Butler studied in Paris and Berlin and became a lifelong friend of future Secretary of State [Elihu Root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elihu_Root "Elihu Root"). Through Root he also met Roosevelt and [William Howard Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft "William Howard Taft"). In the fall of 1885, Butler joined the staff of Columbia's philosophy department.

In 1887, he co-founded with [Grace Hoadley Dodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Hoadley_Dodge "Grace Hoadley Dodge"), and became president of, the New York School for the Training of Teachers, which later affiliated with Columbia University and was renamed [Teachers College, Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachers_College,_Columbia_University "Teachers College, Columbia University"), and from which a co-educational experimental and developmental unit became [Horace Mann School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Mann_School "Horace Mann School"). From 1890 to 1891, Butler was a lecturer at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University") in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore"). Throughout the 1890s, Butler served on the New Jersey Board of Education and helped form the [College Entrance Examination Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Board "College Board"). During the 1890s Butler edited The Great Educators book series for [Charles Scribner's Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Scribner's_Sons "Charles Scribner's Sons").

## Presidency of Columbia University

In 1901, Butler became acting president of Columbia University and, in 1902, formally became president. Among the many dignitaries in attendance at his investiture was President [Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt"). Butler was president of Columbia for 43 years, the longest tenure in the university's history, retiring in 1945. As president, Butler carried out a major expansion of the campus, adding many new buildings, schools, and departments. These additions included [Columbia-Presbyterian Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia-Presbyterian_Medical_Center "Columbia-Presbyterian Medical Center"), the first academic medical center in the world.

In 1919, Butler amended the admissions process to Columbia in order to limit the number of Jewish students; it became the first American institution of higher learning to establish a [quota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_quota "Jewish quota") on the number of Jews admitted. Butler's policy reduced the number of students hailing from New York City from 54% to 23% stemming from what was called at the time "the invasion of the Jewish student". This is one of the reasons why Butler has been called an anti-semite.

In September 1931, Butler told the freshman class at Columbia that totalitarian systems produced "men of far greater intelligence, far stronger character, and far more courage than the system of elections."

In 1937, he was admitted as an honorary member of the New York [Society of the Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_the_Cincinnati "Society of the Cincinnati").

In 1941, the [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize "Pulitzer Prize") fiction jury selected [Ernest Hemingway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Hemingway "Ernest Hemingway")'s *[For Whom the Bell Tolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Whom_the_Bell_Tolls "For Whom the Bell Tolls")*. The Pulitzer Board initially agreed with that judgment, but Butler, *ex officio* head of the Pulitzer board, found the novel offensive and persuaded the board to reverse its determination, so that no novel received the prize that year. Hemingway didn't receive his Pulitzer (for *[The Old Man and the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_Man_and_the_Sea "The Old Man and the Sea")*) until 1952, after Butler's death.

During his lifetime, Columbia named its philosophy library for him; after he died, its main academic library, previously known as South Hall, was renamed [Butler Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butler_Library "Butler Library"). A faculty apartment building on 119th Street and [Morningside Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morningside_Drive_(Manhattan) "Morningside Drive (Manhattan)") was also renamed in Butler's honor, as was a prize in philosophy.

An in-depth look at Butler's time at Columbia University can be found in *[The Goose-Step: A Study of American Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Goose-Step_(book) "The Goose-Step (book)")* by [Upton Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upton_Sinclair "Upton Sinclair").

## Political activity

Butler was a delegate to each [Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_National_Convention "Republican National Convention") from 1888 to 1936; when Vice President [James S. Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_S._Sherman "James S. Sherman") died six days before the [1912 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_United_States_presidential_election "1912 United States presidential election"), Butler was designated to receive the electoral votes that Sherman would have received: the Republican ticket won only 8 electoral votes from [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah "Utah") and [Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermont "Vermont"), finishing third behind the Democrats and the [Progressives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Party_(United_States,_1912) "Progressive Party (United States, 1912)"). Butler [tried to secure the 1916 Republican presidential nomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1916_United_States_presidential_election "1916 United States presidential election") for [Elihu Root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elihu_Root "Elihu Root"). Butler also [sought the nomination for himself in 1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_United_States_presidential_election "1920 United States presidential election"), without success.

Butler believed that [Prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_the_United_States "Prohibition in the United States") was a mistake, with negative effects on the country. He was active in the successful effort for [Repeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repeal_of_Prohibition "Repeal of Prohibition") Prohibition in 1933.

He credited [John W. Burgess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Burgess_(political_scientist) "John Burgess (political scientist)") along with [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton "Alexander Hamilton") for providing the philosophical basis of his Republican principles.

In June 1936, Butler traveled to the Carnegie Endowment Peace Conference in London where, at the meeting, fundamental problems of money and finance were explored.

### Attitude towards Fascism and Nazism

According to historian [Stephen H. Norwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_H._Norwood "Stephen H. Norwood"), Butler failed to "grasp the nature and implications of Nazism... influenced both by his antisemitism, privately expressed, and his economic conservatism and hostility to trade unionism." Butler was a longtime admirer of [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini "Benito Mussolini"). He compared the Italian Fascist leader to [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell "Oliver Cromwell") and, in the 1920s, he noted "the stupendous improvement which [Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism "Fascism") has brought".

In November 1933, months after the [Nazi book burnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_book_burnings "Nazi book burnings") began, he welcomed [Hans Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Luther "Hans Luther"), the German ambassador to the United States, to Columbia and refused to appear with a notable German dissident when the latter visited the university. In 1936, Butler [permanently expelled student Robert Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_activism_at_Columbia_University#1936_protest_against_Nazis "Student activism at Columbia University")— class president of the class of 1938—for leading an anti-Nazi mock [book burning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_book_burnings "Nazi book burnings") protest on campus after Butler accepted an invitation to a celebration at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University "Heidelberg University"). Butler was criticized for his "remarkable silence" and complicity towards [Hitler's regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") until the late 1930s. Butler only unambiguously condemned Nazi Germany after [Kristallnacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristallnacht "Kristallnacht").

## Internationalist

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Buste_van_Nicholas_Murray_Butler_%281862-1947%29_-_Soete%2C_Pierre_de_-_KU_Leuven_Kunstpatrimonium_-_B012.jpg/250px-Buste_van_Nicholas_Murray_Butler_%281862-1947%29_-_Soete%2C_Pierre_de_-_KU_Leuven_Kunstpatrimonium_-_B012.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Buste_van_Nicholas_Murray_Butler_(1862-1947)_-_Soete,_Pierre_de_-_KU_Leuven_Kunstpatrimonium_-_B012.jpg)

Bust of Nicholas Murray Butler in the Reading Room of the University Library of the Catholic University of Leuven. As President of Columbia University, Butler was Chair of the National Committee of the United States for the Restoration of the University of Leuven after the burning of the library during WWI.

From 1907 to 1912, Butler was the chair of the [Lake Mohonk Conference on International Arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mohonk_Conference_on_International_Arbitration "Lake Mohonk Conference on International Arbitration"). Butler was also instrumental in persuading [Andrew Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Carnegie "Andrew Carnegie") to provide the initial $10 million funding for the [Carnegie Endowment for International Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Endowment_for_International_Peace "Carnegie Endowment for International Peace").

Butler became head of international education and communication, founded the European branch of the Endowment headquartered in Paris, and was President of the Endowment from 1925 to 1945. For his work in this field, he received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") for 1931 (shared with [Jane Addams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Addams "Jane Addams")) "[For his promotion] of the [Kellogg-Briand pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg-Briand_pact "Kellogg-Briand pact")" and for his work as the "leader of the more establishment-oriented part of the American peace movement".

In December 1916, Butler, Roosevelt and other philanthropists, including Scottish-born industrialist John C. Moffat, [William Astor Chanler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_A._Chanler "William A. Chanler"), [Joseph Choate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Choate "Joseph Choate"), [Clarence Mackay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Mackay "Clarence Mackay"), [George von Lengerke Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_von_Lengerke_Meyer "George von Lengerke Meyer"), and [John Grier Hibben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Grier_Hibben "John Grier Hibben"), purchased the [Château de Chavaniac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Château_de_Chavaniac "Château de Chavaniac"), birthplace of the [Marquis de Lafayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquis_de_Lafayette "Marquis de Lafayette") in [Auvergne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auvergne_(province) "Auvergne (province)"), to serve as a headquarters for the French Heroes Lafayette Memorial Fund, which was managed by Chanler's ex-wife, Beatrice Ashley Chanler.

Butler was President of the [Pilgrims Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrims_Society "Pilgrims Society"), which promotes Anglo-American friendship. He served as President of the Pilgrims from 1928 to 1946. Butler was president of [The American Academy of Arts and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Letters "The American Academy of Arts and Letters") from 1928 to 1941 and was an early member of the academy.

## Personal life

Butler married Susanna Edwards Schuyler (1863–1903) in 1887 and had one daughter from that marriage. Susanna was the daughter of [Jacob Rutsen Schuyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Rutsen_Schuyler "Jacob Rutsen Schuyler") (1816–1887) and Susannah Haigh Edwards (born 1830). His wife died in 1903 and he married again in 1907 to Kate La Montagne, granddaughter of New York property developer [Thomas E. Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_E._Davis "Thomas E. Davis").

In 1940, Butler completed his autobiography with the publication of the second volume of *Across the Busy Years*.

Butler became almost completely blind in 1945 at age 83. He resigned from the posts he held and died two years later. He is interred at [Cedar Lawn Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Lawn_Cemetery,_Paterson,_New_Jersey "Cedar Lawn Cemetery, Paterson, New Jersey"), in [Paterson, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paterson,_New_Jersey "Paterson, New Jersey").

Butler was not universally liked. In 1939, a former student of Butler, [Rolfe Humphries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolfe_Humphries "Rolfe Humphries"), published in the pages of *[Poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry_(magazine) "Poetry (magazine)")* an effort titled "Draft Ode for a [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa "Phi Beta Kappa") Occasion" that followed a classical format of unrhymed [blank verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blank_verse "Blank verse") in [iambic pentameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iambic_pentameter "Iambic pentameter") with one classical reference per line. The first letters of each line of the resulting [acrostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrostic "Acrostic") spelled out the message: "Nicholas Murray Butler is a horses ass". Upon discovering the "hidden" message, the irate editors ran a formal apology. [Randolph Bourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph_Bourne "Randolph Bourne") lampooned Butler as "Alexander Macintosh Butcher" in "One of our Conquerors", a 1915 essay he published in *[The New Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Republic "The New Republic")*.

Butler wrote and spoke voluminously on all manner of subjects ranging from education to [world peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_peace "World peace"). In *[The American Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Mercury "The American Mercury")*, the critic [Dorothy Dunbar Bromley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Dunbar_Bromley "Dorothy Dunbar Bromley") referred to Butler's pronouncements as "those interminable miasmas of guff".
