**Sir Joseph Austen Chamberlain** [KG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_of_the_Order_of_the_Garter "Knight of the Order of the Garter") (16 October 1863 – 16 March 1937) was a British statesman, [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") winner, son of [Joseph Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Chamberlain "Joseph Chamberlain") and older half-brother of Prime Minister [Neville Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain "Neville Chamberlain"). He served as a [Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom) "Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)") (MP) for 45 years, as [Chancellor of the Exchequer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer "Chancellor of the Exchequer") (twice) and was briefly [Conservative Party leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Conservative_Party_(UK) "Leader of the Conservative Party (UK)") before serving as [Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_(United_Kingdom) "Foreign Secretary (United Kingdom)").

Brought up to be the political heir of his father, whom he physically resembled, he was elected to [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_United_Kingdom "Parliament of the United Kingdom") as a [Liberal Unionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Unionist "Liberal Unionist") at a by-election in 1892. He held office in the [Unionist coalition governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unionist_government,_1895–1905 "Unionist government, 1895–1905") of 1895–1905, remaining in the Cabinet as Chancellor of the Exchequer (1903–05) after his father resigned in 1903 to campaign for [Tariff Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariff_Reform "Tariff Reform"). After his father's disabling stroke in 1906, Austen became the leading tariff reformer in the House of Commons. Late in 1911 he and [Walter Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Long,_1st_Viscount_Long "Walter Long, 1st Viscount Long") were due to compete for the leadership of the Conservative Party (in succession to [Arthur Balfour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Balfour "Arthur Balfour")), but both withdrew in favour of [Bonar Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonar_Law "Bonar Law") rather than risk a party split on a close result.

Chamberlain returned to office in [H. H. Asquith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._H._Asquith "H. H. Asquith")'s wartime [coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asquith_coalition_ministry "Asquith coalition ministry") in May 1915, as [Secretary of State for India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_India "Secretary of State for India"), but resigned to take responsibility for the disastrous [Kut Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Kut "Siege of Kut"). He again returned to office in [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George "David Lloyd George")'s [coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_George_ministry "Lloyd George ministry"), once again serving as Chancellor of the Exchequer. He then served as Conservative Party leader in the Commons (1921–1922), before resigning after the [Carlton Club meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton_Club_meeting "Carlton Club meeting") voted to end the Lloyd George Coalition.

Like many leading coalitionists, he did not hold office in the [Conservative governments of 1922–1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_government,_1922–1924 "Conservative government, 1922–1924"). By now regarded as an elder statesman, he served an important term as Foreign Secretary in [Stanley Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Baldwin "Stanley Baldwin")'s [second government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Baldwin_ministry "Second Baldwin ministry") (1924–1929). He negotiated the [Locarno Treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno_Treaties "Locarno Treaties") (1925), aimed at preventing war between [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_France "Interwar France") and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic"), for which he was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"). Chamberlain last held office as [First Lord of the Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Admiralty "First Lord of the Admiralty") in 1931, but resigned as a result of his actions over the [Invergordon Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invergordon_Mutiny "Invergordon Mutiny"). He was one of the few MPs supporting [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill")'s appeals for rearmament against the German threat in the 1930s and remained an active backbench MP until his death in 1937.

## Early life

Austen Chamberlain was born in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham "Birmingham"), the second child and eldest son of [Joseph Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Chamberlain "Joseph Chamberlain"), then a rising industrialist and political [radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radicalism_(historical) "Radicalism (historical)"), later [Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor "Mayor") of Birmingham and a dominant figure in [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK) "Liberal Party (UK)") and [Unionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)") politics at the end of the 19th century. His father and mother, the former Harriet Kenrick had a baby daughter [Beatrice Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrice_Chamberlain "Beatrice Chamberlain") who would become an educationalist. Harriet [died giving birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_death "Maternal death") to Austen, leaving his father so shaken that for almost 25 years he maintained a distance from his first-born son. In 1868, his father married Harriet's cousin, Florence, and had further children, the oldest of whom, [Neville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain "Neville Chamberlain"), would become [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom "Prime Minister of the United Kingdom") in the year of Austen's death.

Austen was dominated by his elder sister and was therefore sent away to be educated first at [Rugby School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_School "Rugby School") "to release him from her thrall", before passing on to [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"). While at Trinity College, he became a lifelong friend of [F. S. Oliver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._S._Oliver "F. S. Oliver"), a future advocate of [Imperial Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Federation "Imperial Federation") and, after 1909, a prominent member of the [Round Table movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Table_movement "Round Table movement"). Chamberlain made his first political address in 1884 at a meeting of the university's Political Society and was vice-president of the [Cambridge Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Union "Cambridge Union").

It would seem that from an early age his father had intended for politics to be Austen's future path, and with that in mind, he was sent first to France, where he studied at the [Paris Institute of Political Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Institute_of_Political_Studies "Paris Institute of Political Studies") and developed a lasting admiration for the French people and culture. For nine months, he was shown the brilliance of [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") under the [Third Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic "French Third Republic"), and he met and dined with the likes of [Georges Clemenceau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Clemenceau "Georges Clemenceau") and [Alexandre Ribot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Ribot "Alexandre Ribot").

From Paris, Austen was sent to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin") for twelve months, to imbibe the political culture of the other great European power, Germany. Though in his letters home to Beatrice and Neville, he showed an obvious preference for France and the lifestyle he had left behind there, Chamberlain undertook to learn German and learn from his experience in the capital of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire"). Among others, Austen met and dined with the "Iron Chancellor", [Otto von Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Bismarck "Otto von Bismarck"), an experience that was to hold a special place in his heart for the duration of his life.

While attending the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin "University of Berlin"), Austen developed a suspicion of the growing nationalism in Germany based upon his experience of the lecturing style of [Heinrich von Treitschke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_von_Treitschke "Heinrich von Treitschke"), who opened up to him "a new side of the German character – a narrow-minded, proud, intolerant [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia "Prussia") chauvinism", the consequences of which he was later to ponder during the First World War and the crises of the 1930s.

## Early career

### Member of Parliament

Austen returned to England in 1888, lured largely by the prize of a parliamentary constituency. He was first elected to parliament as a member of his father's own [Liberal Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Unionist_Party "Liberal Unionist Party") in 1892, sitting for the seat of [East Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Worcestershire "East Worcestershire"). Owing to the prominence of his father and the alliance between the anti-[Home Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Home_Rule "Irish Home Rule") Liberal Unionists and [Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)"), Chamberlain was returned unopposed on 30 March, and at the first sitting of the new session, he walked up the floor of the house flanked by his father and his uncle, [Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Chamberlain_(MP_for_Islington_West) "Richard Chamberlain (MP for Islington West)").

Owing to the [dissolution of parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Parliament_of_the_United_Kingdom "Dissolution of the Parliament of the United Kingdom") and the [1892 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1892_United_Kingdom_general_election "1892 United Kingdom general election") that August, Chamberlain was unable to make his maiden speech until April 1893, but, when delivered, it was acclaimed by the four-time prime minister [William Ewart Gladstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ewart_Gladstone "William Ewart Gladstone") as "one of the best speeches which has been made". That Chamberlain was speaking against Gladstone's own [Second Home Rule Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Home_Rule_Bill "Second Home Rule Bill") does not seem to have dampened the enthusiasm of the Prime Minister, who responded by publicly congratulating both Austen and his father, Joseph, on such an excellent performance. That was highly significant, given the bad blood existing between Joseph Chamberlain and his former leader.

### Political office

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/Joseph_Austen_Chamberlain%2C_Vanity_Fair%2C_1899-08-03.jpg/250px-Joseph_Austen_Chamberlain%2C_Vanity_Fair%2C_1899-08-03.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Joseph_Austen_Chamberlain,_Vanity_Fair,_1899-08-03.jpg)

Chamberlain caricatured by [Spy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Ward "Leslie Ward") for *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(UK_magazine) "Vanity Fair (UK magazine)")*, 1899.

Appointed a junior Whip of the Liberal Unionists after the general election, Austen's main role was to act as his father's "standard bearer" in matters of policy. Following the Conservative and Unionist landslide win in the [election of 1895](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1895_United_Kingdom_general_election "1895 United Kingdom general election"), Chamberlain was appointed [Civil Lord of the Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Lord_of_the_Admiralty "Civil Lord of the Admiralty"), holding that post until 1900, when he became [Financial Secretary to the Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Secretary_to_the_Treasury "Financial Secretary to the Treasury"). [Lord Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Salisbury "Lord Salisbury") retired as prime minister in July 1902, and the following month Chamberlain was promoted to the position of [Postmaster General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmaster_General_of_the_United_Kingdom "Postmaster General of the United Kingdom") by the new premier, the Conservative [Arthur Balfour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Balfour "Arthur Balfour"), who also designated this a cabinet position, and appointed him to the [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom "Privy Council of the United Kingdom").

In the wake of the struggle between his father and Balfour, Austen Chamberlain became [Chancellor of the Exchequer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer "Chancellor of the Exchequer") in 1903. Austen's appointment was largely a compromise solution to the bitter division of the two Unionist heavyweights, which threatened to split the coalition between supporters of Chamberlain's [Imperial Tariff campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariff_Reform_League "Tariff Reform League") and Balfour's more cautious advocacy of protectionism. While Austen supported his father's programme, his influence within the cabinet was diminished following the departure of the senior Chamberlain to the [backbenches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backbenches "Backbenches"). Facing a resurgent [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK) "Liberal Party (UK)") opposition and the threat of an internal party split, Balfour eventually took the Unionists into opposition in December 1905, and in the ensuing rout in the [election of 1906](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1906_United_Kingdom_general_election "1906 United Kingdom general election"), Austen found himself one of the few surviving Liberal Unionists in the [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_House_of_Commons "British House of Commons").

After his father's stroke and enforced retirement from active politics a few months later, Austen became the effective leader of the tariff reform campaign within the Unionist Party, and thus, he was a contender for the eventual leadership of the party itself.

## Leadership questions

With the Unionists in disarray after electoral defeats at both the [January](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_1910_United_Kingdom_general_election "January 1910 United Kingdom general election") and [December 1910](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_1910_United_Kingdom_general_election "December 1910 United Kingdom general election") elections, Balfour was forced from his position as party leader in November 1911. Chamberlain was one of the leading candidates to succeed as Conservative leader even though he was still technically a member of the Liberal Unionist wing of the coalition (the two parties merged formally only in 1912).

Chamberlain was opposed by Canadian-born [Bonar Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonar_Law "Bonar Law"), [Walter Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Long,_1st_Viscount_Long "Walter Long, 1st Viscount Long") and Irish Unionist [Edward Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Carson "Edward Carson").

Given their standing in the party, only Chamberlain and Long had a realistic chance of success and though Balfour had intended Chamberlain to succeed him, it became clear from an early canvass of the sitting MPs that Long would be elected by a slender margin.

After a short period of internal party campaigning, Chamberlain determined to withdraw from the contest for the good of the still-divided party. He succeeded in persuading Long to withdraw with him in favour of Law, who was subsequently chosen by unanimous vote as a compromise candidate.

Chamberlain's action, while it prevented him from attaining the party leadership and, arguably, the premiership, did a great deal to maintain unity within the Conservative and Liberal Unionist parties at a time of great uncertainty and strain.

## Irish Home Rule

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Sir_%28Joseph%29_Austen_Chamberlain.jpg/250px-Sir_%28Joseph%29_Austen_Chamberlain.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sir_(Joseph)_Austen_Chamberlain.jpg)

Sir Austen Chamberlain, 1908–12, by [Henry Walter Barnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Walter_Barnett "Henry Walter Barnett")

In the last years before the outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), Chamberlain was concerned with one issue above all others: [Home Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Rule "Home Rule") for Ireland. The issue that had prompted his father to leave the Liberal Party in the 1880s now threatened to spill over into outright civil war, with the government of [H. H. Asquith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._H._Asquith "H. H. Asquith") committed to the passage of a [Third Home Rule Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Home_Rule_Bill "Third Home Rule Bill"). Chamberlain was resolutely opposed to the dissolution of the Union with Ireland. To the strain then was added the death of his father in July 1914, only a few days after the assassination of [Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria "Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria"), which began the train of events that led to the war.

## First World War

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Imperial_War_Cabinet.jpg/250px-Imperial_War_Cabinet.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Imperial_War_Cabinet.jpg)

Imperial War Cabinet (1917) Austen is in the second row, fourth from the left

Pressure from the Conservative opposition, in part led by Chamberlain, eventually resulted in the formation of the wartime [coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asquith_coalition "Asquith coalition"), in 1915. Chamberlain joined the cabinet as [Secretary of State for India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_India "Secretary of State for India"). Like other politicians, including [Arthur Balfour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Balfour "Arthur Balfour") and [George Curzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Curzon,_1st_Marquess_Curzon_of_Kedleston "George Curzon, 1st Marquess Curzon of Kedleston"), Chamberlain supported the [invasion of Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_campaign "Mesopotamian campaign") to increase British prestige in the region, thus discouraging a German-inspired Muslim revolt in India.

Chamberlain remained at the [India Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Office "India Office") after [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George "David Lloyd George") succeeded Asquith as prime minister in late 1916, but following the [Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Commissions_(Dardanelles_and_Mesopotamia)_Act_1916 "Special Commissions (Dardanelles and Mesopotamia) Act 1916") into the failure of the Mesopotamian campaign (undertaken by the separately-administered [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army "British Indian Army")) in 1915, including the loss of the British garrison during the [Siege of Kut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Kut "Siege of Kut"), Chamberlain resigned his post in July 1917; as the minister ultimately responsible, the fault lay with him. He was widely acclaimed for such a principled act.

After Lloyd George's Paris speech (12 November 1917) at which he said that "when he saw the appalling casualty lists he wish(ed) it had not been necessary to win so many ["victories"]" there was talk of Chamberlain withdrawing support from the government. Lloyd George survived by claiming that the aim of the new inter-Allied [Supreme War Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_War_Council "Supreme War Council") was purely to "coordinate" policy, not to overrule the British generals, who still enjoyed a good deal of support from Conservatives.

Later, he returned to government and became a member of the [War Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Cabinet "War Cabinet") in April 1918 as [Minister without Portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_without_portfolio_(United_Kingdom) "Minister without portfolio (United Kingdom)"), replacing [Lord Milner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Milner "Lord Milner"), who had become [Secretary of State for War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_War "Secretary of State for War").

Following the victory of the [Lloyd George coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_George_coalition "Lloyd George coalition") in the [1918 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1918_United_Kingdom_general_election "1918 United Kingdom general election"), Chamberlain was again appointed to the position of Chancellor of the Exchequer in January 1919 and immediately faced the huge task of restoring Britain's finances after four years of wartime expenditure.

## Leadership

Citing ill health, Law retired from the leadership of the Conservative branch of the Lloyd George government in the spring of 1921. His seniority and the general dislike of Curzon, his counterpart in the [House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords "House of Lords"), helped Chamberlain to both succeed Law as Leader of the House of Commons and take over in the office of [Lord Privy Seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Privy_Seal "Lord Privy Seal"). He was succeeded at the Exchequer by Sir [Robert Horne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Horne,_1st_Viscount_Horne_of_Slamannan "Robert Horne, 1st Viscount Horne of Slamannan"); it seemed that after ten years of waiting, Austen would again be given the opportunity of succeeding to the premiership.

The Lloyd George coalition was beginning to falter, following numerous scandals and the unsuccessful conclusion of the [Anglo-Irish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Irish_War "Anglo-Irish War"), and it was widely believed that it would not survive until the next general election. He had previously had little regard for Lloyd George, but the opportunity of working closely with the "Welsh Wizard" gave Chamberlain a new insight into his nominal superior in the government (by now, the Conservative Party was by far the larger partner in the government).

It was an unfortunate change of allegiance for Chamberlain, for by late 1921, the Conservative backbenchers were growing more and more restless for an end to the coalition and a return to single-party (Conservative) government. Conservatives in the House of Lords began, in public, to oppose the coalition, disregarding calls for support from Chamberlain. In the country at large, Conservative candidates began to oppose the coalition at by-elections, and discontent spread to the House of Commons.

In the autumn of 1922, Chamberlain faced a backbench revolt, largely led by [Stanley Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Baldwin "Stanley Baldwin"), designed to oust Lloyd George, and when he summoned the [Carlton Club meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton_Club_meeting "Carlton Club meeting") 19 October 1922, of Conservative MPs, a motion was passed there for fighting the forthcoming election as an independent party. Chamberlain resigned the party leadership rather than act against what he believed to be his duty. He was succeeded by Law, whose views and intentions he had predicted the evening before the vote at a private meeting. Law formed a government shortly thereafter, but Chamberlain was not given a post, but it seems that he would not have accepted a position even if he had been offered one.

Austen and Neville Chamberlain, [Iain Duncan Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iain_Duncan_Smith "Iain Duncan Smith") and [Liz Truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Truss "Liz Truss") are the only four Conservative leaders not to lead the party into a general election. Until [William Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hague "William Hague") (1997–2001), Austen had been the only Conservative overall leader in the history of the party not to become prime minister.

## Foreign Secretary

### Appointment

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-R03618%2C_Locarno%2C_Gustav_Stresemann%2C_Chamberlain%2C_Briand.jpg/250px-Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-R03618%2C_Locarno%2C_Gustav_Stresemann%2C_Chamberlain%2C_Briand.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-R03618,_Locarno,_Gustav_Stresemann,_Chamberlain,_Briand.jpg)

With Stresemann (left) and Briand at Locarno

After the second resignation of Law in May 1923 (Law died from throat cancer later that year), Chamberlain was passed over again for the leadership of the party in favour of [Stanley Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Baldwin "Stanley Baldwin"). Baldwin offered Chamberlain the post of Lord Privy Seal, but Chamberlain insisted other former ministers from the Coalition to be included as well; Baldwin refused. However, Chamberlain returned to government when Baldwin formed his [second ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Baldwin_ministry "Second Baldwin ministry") following success in the [election of October 1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_United_Kingdom_general_election "1924 United Kingdom general election"), serving in the important office of [Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_(United_Kingdom) "Foreign Secretary (United Kingdom)") from 1924 to 1929. Chamberlain was largely allowed a free hand by the easy-going Baldwin.

It is as Foreign Secretary that Chamberlain's place in history was finally assured. In a difficult period in international relations, Chamberlain faced not only a split in the *[entente cordiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entente_cordiale "Entente cordiale")* by the French [occupation of the Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Ruhr "Occupation of the Ruhr") but also the controversy over the 1924 [Geneva Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Protocol_(1924) "Geneva Protocol (1924)"), which threatened to dilute British sovereignty over the issue of [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") economic sanctions.

### Locarno Pact

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/1930_Austen_Chamberlain.jpg/250px-1930_Austen_Chamberlain.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1930_Austen_Chamberlain.jpg)

[Autochrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autochrome_Lumière "Autochrome Lumière") portrait by [Georges Chevalier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Chevalier_(photographer) "Georges Chevalier (photographer)"), 1930.

Despite the importance to history of other pressing issues, his reputation chiefly rests on his part in the negotiations over what came to be known as the [Locarno Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno_Pact "Locarno Pact") of 1925. Seeking to maintain the postwar status quo in the West, Chamberlain responded favourably to the approaches of German Foreign Minister [Gustav Stresemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Stresemann "Gustav Stresemann") for a British guarantee of [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic")'s western borders. Besides promoting Franco-German reconciliation, Chamberlain's main motive was to create a situation in which Germany could pursue territorial revisionism in Eastern Europe peacefully.

Chamberlain's understanding was that if Franco-German relations improved, [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_France "Interwar France") would gradually abandon the *[cordon sanitaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordon_sanitaire_(international_relations) "Cordon sanitaire (international relations)")*, the French alliance system in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe "Eastern Europe") between the wars. Once France had abandoned its allies in Eastern Europe as the price of better relations with Germany, [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic "Second Polish Republic") and [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Czechoslovak_Republic "First Czechoslovak Republic") would have no Great Power ally to protect them and would be forced to adjust to German demands. Chamberlain believed that they would peacefully hand over the territories claimed by Germany such as the [Sudetenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudetenland "Sudetenland"), the [Polish Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Corridor "Polish Corridor") and the [Free City of Danzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Danzig "Free City of Danzig"). Promoting territorial revisionism in Eastern Europe in Germany's favour was one of Chamberlain's principal reasons for Locarno.

Together with [Aristide Briand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristide_Briand "Aristide Briand") of France, Chamberlain and Stresemann met at the town of [Locarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno "Locarno") in October 1925 and signed a mutual agreement (together with representatives from [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium "Belgium") and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy "Fascist Italy")) to settle all differences between the nations by arbitration, not war. For his services, Chamberlain was not only awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") but also made a Knight Companion of the [Order of the Garter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Garter "Order of the Garter"). He was the first ordinary Knight Companion of the Garter since Elizabethan times (Sir [Henry Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Lee_(died_1611) "Henry Lee (died 1611)")) to die without having been made a [peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peerage_of_the_United_Kingdom "Peerage of the United Kingdom"). He was the [871st Knight Companion of the Garter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Knights_and_Ladies_of_the_Garter "List of Knights and Ladies of the Garter").

Chamberlain also secured Britain's accession to the [Kellogg-Briand Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg-Briand_Pact "Kellogg-Briand Pact"), which theoretically outlawed war as an instrument of policy. Chamberlain also said that Italian dictator [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini "Benito Mussolini") was "a man with whom business could be done".

### Resignation

After his less satisfactory engagement in issues in the Far East and Egypt, Chamberlain's tenure as Foreign Secretary ended with the defeat of Baldwin's government at the [election of 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_United_Kingdom_general_election "1929 United Kingdom general election"). He went into retirement.

Chamberlain briefly returned to government in 1931 as [First Lord of the Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Admiralty "First Lord of the Admiralty") in [Ramsay MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsay_MacDonald "Ramsay MacDonald")'s first [National Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_National_Government "UK National Government") but soon retired later that year after his actions in regards to the [Invergordon Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invergordon_Mutiny "Invergordon Mutiny"). Many of the officers at the time state that the mutiny at Invergordon was a direct result of the action of the Admiralty under Chamberlain's leadership who accepted the cuts in pay for the crews as a result of the Government's need to reduce expenditure. The First Sea Lord [Frederick Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Field_(Royal_Navy_officer) "Frederick Field (Royal Navy officer)") was ill at the time of the incident and Chamberlain had proceeded without receiving the advice of the First Sea Lord.

## Later life

### Backbencher

Over the next six years as a senior backbencher, he gave strong support to the National Government on domestic issues but was critical on its foreign policy. In 1935, the government faced a parliamentary rebellion over the [Hoare-Laval Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoare-Laval_Pact "Hoare-Laval Pact"); his opposition to the vote of censure is widely believed to have been instrumental in saving the government from defeat on the floor of the House.

Chamberlain was again briefly considered in 1935 for the post of Foreign Secretary but was passed over once the [Abyssinia Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssinia_Crisis "Abyssinia Crisis") was over for being too old for the job. Instead, his advice was sought as to the suitability of his former [Parliamentary Private Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Private_Secretary "Parliamentary Private Secretary"), now Minister for the League of Nations, [Anthony Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Eden "Anthony Eden") for the post.

### Calls for rearmament

From 1934 to 1937, Chamberlain was, with [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill"), [Roger Keyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Keyes "Roger Keyes") and [Leo Amery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Amery "Leo Amery"), the most prominent voice calling for British rearmament in the face of a growing threat from [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany").

However, in 1935, Stanley Baldwin's government produced a [White Paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_relating_to_Defence "Statement relating to Defence") and announced modest rearmament. Although Baldwin would forever be condemned for his failure to rearm sufficiently, the Labour Party opposed the White Paper. [Clement Attlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_Attlee "Clement Attlee") said:

> We believe in a League system in which the whole world would be ranged against an aggressor. If it is shown that someone is proposing to break the peace, let us bring the whole world opinion against her.

Afterwards Austen Chamberlain fully supported Baldwin and severely criticised Attlee's speech with the words:

> If war breaks out, and we become involved in a struggle and if the Honourable Member for [Limehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limehouse_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Limehouse (UK Parliament constituency)") [Clement Attlee] is sitting on the government benches while London is being bombed, do you think he will hold the language he held today? If he does he will be one of the first victims of the war, for he will be strung up by an angry and justifiably angry populace to the nearest lamp post.

In addition to speaking eloquently in Parliament on the matter, he was the chairman of two [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)") parliamentary delegations in late 1936 that met with Prime Minister [Stanley Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Baldwin "Stanley Baldwin") to remonstrate with him about his government's delay in rearming the British defence forces. More respected than Churchill, Chamberlain became something of an icon to young Conservatives, as the last survivor of Victorian high politics.

### Death

Though he never again served in a government, he survived in good health until March 1937, dying just ten weeks before his younger half-brother, Neville, became the first and only member of the Chamberlain dynasty to become prime minister.

Chamberlain died at the age of 73 in his London home, 24 Egerton Terrace, on 16 March 1937. He is buried in [East Finchley Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Finchley_Cemetery "East Finchley Cemetery") in London.

At his death, his estate was valued for probate at £45,044 18/1. This was a relatively modest sum for such a famous public figure. Much of his father's fortune had been lost in a failed attempt by his younger brother Neville to grow [sisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisal "Sisal") in the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies "West Indies") in the early 1890s, and unlike Neville, he never went into business to make money for himself.

His personal and political papers are housed in the Cadbury Research Library at the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham "University of Birmingham").

## Assessments

[Robert Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Blake,_Baron_Blake "Robert Blake, Baron Blake") wrote that Austen Chamberlain "for all his talents was only a thin echo of his formidable father, a mere shadow of that extraordinary figure ... Austen Chamberlain was altogether kinder than his father, more likeable, more honourable, more high-minded and less effective ... he lacked that ultimate hardness without which men seldom reach supreme political power". Blake comments that he often missed his chances because he could be persuaded to back off by the suggestion that he was acting for self-seeking reasons, hence Churchill's quip that "he always played the game, and he always lost it".

David Dutton comments that early assessments of Chamberlain's career compared him unfavourably with his father, who overshadowed his early career, and his brother, who overshadowed his later decades. In his forties, when he was ready to carve out his own identity, he had to act as a surrogate for his disabled father, whom he resembled in appearance (although he was softer and less wiry, both in face and body) and dress (wearing a [monocle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocle "Monocle"), and an [orchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchid "Orchid") in his lapel). Until [William Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hague "William Hague") (1997–2001), he was the only Conservative leader of the twentieth century not to become prime minister. Although this is sometimes attributed to character defects, Dutton argues that he was a major figure in his own right, who only narrowly missed becoming prime minister in 1922 or 1923. Dutton quotes with approval [Leo Amery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Amery "Leo Amery")'s verdict written just after Chamberlain's death: 'He just missed greatness and the highest position, but his was a fine life of honourable public service'.

Chamberlain could speak effectively, but was never a stirring orator. For most of his career he was renowned for rectitude and civic duty. Before the war Chamberlain had been a somewhat reluctant radical, but after 1918 he became more conservative, concerned at the new threat of socialism, and whose dress – he not only wore a monocle and [frock coat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frock_coat "Frock coat") but was one of the last MPs to wear a top hat inside the Commons Chamber – made him appear a relic from a previous generation. Dutton suggests that his "exaggerated sense of his own importance and dignity which compounded an already stiff and unbending personal demeanour", came from having to serve under men – Law and Baldwin – whom he regarded as his juniors.

## Personal life

In 1906, Chamberlain married Ivy Muriel Dundas (died 1941), daughter of Colonel Henry Dundas. She was created a Dame Grand Cross of the [Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire "Order of the British Empire") (GBE) in 1925. The couple had two sons, Joseph and Lawrence, and a daughter, Diane.

During the 1920s, the Chamberlains lived at a house called Twytt's Ghyll in Fir Toll Road, [Mayfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayfield_and_Five_Ashes "Mayfield and Five Ashes"), [East Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Sussex "East Sussex"). The house was sold in 1929. R.C.G. Foster said, "He kept himself quite aloof from the village and was not popular with his neighbours". He had an interest in [rock gardening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_gardening "Rock gardening").
