**Eisaku Satō** (佐藤 栄作, *Satō Eisaku*; 27 March 1901 – 3 June 1975) was a Japanese politician who served as [prime minister of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Japan "Prime minister of Japan") from 1964 to 1972. He was the third [longest-serving and longest-uninterrupted–serving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Japan_by_time_in_office "List of prime ministers of Japan by time in office") Japanese prime minister. Satō is best remembered for securing the return of [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefecture "Okinawa Prefecture") in 1972, and for winning the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1974, which stirred controversy. He was a former elite bureaucrat like his elder brother [Nobusuke Kishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobusuke_Kishi "Nobusuke Kishi") and a member of the Yoshida school like [Hayato Ikeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayato_Ikeda "Hayato Ikeda"). Like his predecessor, he also supported [Keynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keynesianism "Keynesianism") economic policies.

Born in [Yamaguchi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi_Prefecture "Yamaguchi Prefecture"), Satō was a member of the [Satō–Kishi–Abe family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satō–Kishi–Abe_family "Satō–Kishi–Abe family") and the younger brother of prime minister [Nobusuke Kishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobusuke_Kishi "Nobusuke Kishi"). Satō graduated from [Tokyo Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Imperial_University "Tokyo Imperial University") in 1924 and joined the [Ministry of Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Railways_(Japan) "Ministry of Railways (Japan)"). After the war, he entered the [National Diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Diet "National Diet") in 1949 as a member of the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(Japan,_1945) "Liberal Party (Japan, 1945)"), and served in a series of cabinet positions under [Shigeru Yoshida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeru_Yoshida "Shigeru Yoshida"), including posts and telecommunications minister from 1951 to 1952, construction minister from 1952 to 1953, and [chief cabinet secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Cabinet_Secretary_(Japan) "Chief Cabinet Secretary (Japan)") from 1953 to 1954. Satō later joined the [Liberal Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democratic_Party_(Japan) "Liberal Democratic Party (Japan)") and became finance minister from 1958 to 1960 under [Nobusuke Kishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobusuke_Kishi "Nobusuke Kishi") and international trade and industry minister from 1961 to 1962 under [Hayato Ikeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayato_Ikeda "Hayato Ikeda").

In 1964, Satō succeeded Ikeda as LDP president and prime minister. He had the support of Japanese business and finance, and presided over a period of rapid economic growth. In foreign policy, he oversaw the normalization of diplomatic relations with [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea") and maintained close relations with the United States, allowing the [U.S.–Japan Security Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Mutual_Cooperation_and_Security_between_the_United_States_and_Japan "Treaty of Mutual Cooperation and Security between the United States and Japan") to renew in 1970 and arranging for the return of [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefecture "Okinawa Prefecture") to Japan in 1972. In 1967, Satō introduced the "[Three Non-Nuclear Principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Non-Nuclear_Principles "Three Non-Nuclear Principles")" (the non-production, non-possession, and non-introduction of [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon")), and in 1968 signed the [Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Non-Proliferation_of_Nuclear_Weapons "Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons"), for which he won the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"). However, it was later revealed that Satō had made secret agreements with the U.S. to allow violations of the principles. Facing mounting economic problems and falling approval ratings in the early 1970s, Satō resigned in 1972 and was succeeded as prime minister by [Kakuei Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakuei_Tanaka "Kakuei Tanaka"), quickly losing his political influence when his protégé [Takeo Fukuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeo_Fukuda "Takeo Fukuda") did not succeed him.

## Early life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Eisaku_Sat%C5%8D%2C_Shigeru_Yoshida%2C_Saeki_Ozawa_1953.jpg/250px-Eisaku_Sat%C5%8D%2C_Shigeru_Yoshida%2C_Saeki_Ozawa_1953.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Eisaku_Satō,_Shigeru_Yoshida,_Saeki_Ozawa_1953.jpg)

From left Sato (then Minister of Construction), Prime Minister [Shigeru Yoshida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeru_Yoshida "Shigeru Yoshida") and Party chairman Saeki Ozawa (1953)

Satō was born on 27 March 1901, in [Tabuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabuse "Tabuse"), [Yamaguchi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi_Prefecture "Yamaguchi Prefecture"), the third son of businessman Hidesuke Satō and his wife Moyo. His father had worked in the Yamaguchi Prefectural Office, but quit in 1898, and started a [sake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sake "Sake") brewing business in Kishida, Tabuse. The family had a history in sake brewing and had held the right for sake brewing for generations. Satō's great-grandfather was a [samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurai "Samurai") of the [Chōshū Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chōshū_Domain "Chōshū Domain"), with their outsized influence in [Meiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration "Meiji Restoration") era Japan, with more Meiji and [Taisho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taishō "Taishō") prime ministers coming from Yamaguchi than any other prefecture. His two older brothers were [Ichirō Satō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichirō_Satō "Ichirō Satō"), who would become a vice admiral, and [Nobusuke Kishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobusuke_Kishi "Nobusuke Kishi"), who served as prime minister from 1957 to 1960.

Satō studied [German law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_law "German law") at [Tokyo Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Imperial_University "Tokyo Imperial University") and in 1923, passed the senior civil service examinations. Upon graduation the following year, he became a [civil servant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_servant "Civil servant") in the [Ministry of Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Railways_(Japan) "Ministry of Railways (Japan)"). He served as Director of the Osaka Railways Bureau from 1944 to 1946 and [Vice-Minister for Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transport_(Japan) "Ministry of Transport (Japan)") from 1947 to 1948.

## Political career

Satō entered the [National Diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Diet "National Diet") in [1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Japanese_general_election "1949 Japanese general election") as a member of the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_(Japan,_1945) "Liberal Party (Japan, 1945)").

He served as Minister of [Postal Services and Telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Posts_and_Telecommunications_(Japan) "Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications (Japan)") from July 1951 to July 1952. Sato gradually rose through the ranks of Japanese politics, becoming [chief cabinet secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Cabinet_Secretary_(Japan) "Chief Cabinet Secretary (Japan)") to then prime minister [Shigeru Yoshida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeru_Yoshida "Shigeru Yoshida") from January 1953 to July 1954. He later served as minister of construction from October 1952 to February 1953.

After the Liberal Party merged with the [Japan Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(Japan,_1954) "Democratic Party (Japan, 1954)") to form the [Liberal Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democratic_Party_(Japan) "Liberal Democratic Party (Japan)"), Satō served as chairman of the party executive council from December 1957 to June 1958, followed by a post as [minister of finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_(Japan) "Minister of Finance (Japan)") in the cabinet of his brother [Nobusuke Kishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobusuke_Kishi "Nobusuke Kishi") from 1958 to 1960. As minister of finance, Sato requested the US to fund conservatives.

Satō also served in the cabinets of Kishi's successor as prime minister, [Hayato Ikeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayato_Ikeda "Hayato Ikeda"). From July 1961 to July 1962, Satō was [Minister of International Trade and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_International_Trade_and_Industry "Ministry of International Trade and Industry"). From July 1963 to June 1964 he was concurrently head of the [Hokkaidō Development Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaido_Development_Agency "Hokkaido Development Agency") and of the [Science and Technology Agency](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/科学技術庁長官 "ja:科学技術庁長官").

Satō succeeded Ikeda after the latter resigned due to ill health. He would go on to serve the longest stint of any prime minister up until that time, and by the late 1960s he appeared to have single-handed control over the entire Japanese government. He was a popular prime minister due to the growing economy; his foreign policy, which was a balancing act between the interests of the United States and China, was more tenuous, and his grip on domestic politics was challenged by growing opposition to his administration's support for the [American military operations in Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War").

### Foreign policy

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Ferdinand_and_Imelda_Marcos_with_Japanese_Prime_Minister_Eisaku_Sat%C5%8D_and_his_wife.jpg/250px-Ferdinand_and_Imelda_Marcos_with_Japanese_Prime_Minister_Eisaku_Sat%C5%8D_and_his_wife.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ferdinand_and_Imelda_Marcos_with_Japanese_Prime_Minister_Eisaku_Satō_and_his_wife.jpg)

Satō and his wife with [Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos "Ferdinand Marcos") and [Imelda Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imelda_Marcos "Imelda Marcos")

#### China and Taiwan

Satō is the last Prime minister of Japan to visit [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China "Republic of China") during his term. In 1965, Satō approved a US$150 million loan to [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China "Republic of China"). He visited [Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei "Taipei") in September 1967. In 1969, Satō insisted that the defense of Taiwan was necessary for the safety of Japan. Satō followed the United States in most major issues, but Satō opposed the [Nixon visit to China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_visit_to_China "Nixon visit to China"). Satō also bitterly opposed the entry of the PRC into the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") in 1971.

#### South Korea

On 22 June 1965, the Satō government and [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea") under [Park Chung Hee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Chung_Hee "Park Chung Hee") signed the [Treaty on Basic Relations Between Japan and the Republic of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_Basic_Relations_Between_Japan_and_the_Republic_of_Korea "Treaty on Basic Relations Between Japan and the Republic of Korea"), which normalized relations between Japan and South Korea for the first time. Relations with Japan had previously not been officially established since Korea's [decolonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberation_Day_of_Korea "National Liberation Day of Korea") and [division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Korea "Division of Korea") at the end of World War II.

#### Southeast Asia

During Satō's term, Japan participated in the creation of the [Asian Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Development_Bank "Asian Development Bank") in 1966 and held a ministerial level conference on [Southeast Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") economic development. It was the first international conference sponsored by the Japanese government in the postwar period. In 1967, he was also the first Japanese prime minister to visit [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"). He was largely supportive of the [South Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam "South Vietnam") government throughout the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War").

### Okinawan reversion

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/President_Nixon_and_Prime_Minister_Eisaku_Sato_of_Japan_at_San_Clemente_-_NARA_-_194752.jpg/250px-President_Nixon_and_Prime_Minister_Eisaku_Sato_of_Japan_at_San_Clemente_-_NARA_-_194752.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:President_Nixon_and_Prime_Minister_Eisaku_Sato_of_Japan_at_San_Clemente_-_NARA_-_194752.jpg)

Satō negotiated with U.S. president [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon") for the repatriation of [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefecture "Okinawa Prefecture")

Since the end of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"), [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefecture "Okinawa Prefecture") had been occupied by the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"). While visiting the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") in January 1965, Satō openly asked President [Lyndon Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_Johnson "Lyndon Johnson") to return Okinawa to Japan. In August 1965, Satō became the first post-war prime minister of Japan to visit Okinawa.

In 1969, Satō struck a deal with U.S. president [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon") to repatriate [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefecture "Okinawa Prefecture"): this deal was controversial because it allowed the [U.S. forces in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Forces_Japan "United States Forces Japan") to maintain bases in Okinawa after repatriation. Okinawa was formally returned to Japan on 15 May 1972, which also included the [Senkaku Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senkaku_Islands "Senkaku Islands") (also known as the Diaoyu Islands in China and the subject, since 1971, of a Sino-Japanese sovereignty dispute; see [Senkaku Islands dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senkaku_Islands_dispute "Senkaku Islands dispute")).

### Nuclear affairs

In his early years as prime minister, Sato had argued that Japan needed to develop nuclear weapons of its own to match those of China, but the United States government was staunchly opposed to this, and the Johnson administration pressured Japan to sign the [Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Non-Proliferation_Treaty "Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty").

Accordingly, Satō introduced the [Three Non-Nuclear Principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Non-Nuclear_Principles "Three Non-Nuclear Principles") on 11 December 1967, promising the non-production, non-possession, and non-introduction of [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon") inside Japan. Thereafter, Satō shepherded Japan's entry into the [Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Non-Proliferation_Treaty "Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty") and the Diet passed a resolution formally adopting the Non-Nuclear Principles in 1971. For these actions, Satō would receive the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") as a co-recipient in 1974.

However, recent inquiries show that behind the scenes, Satō was more accommodating towards US plans of stationing nuclear weapons on Japanese soil. In December 2008, the Japanese government declassified a document showing that during a visit to the US in January 1965, he was discussing with US officials the possibility of using nuclear weapons against the [People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_China "People's Republic of China"). In December 2009, his son reported that his father agreed in a November 1969 conversation with US President Nixon to allow the stationing of nuclear warheads in [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Prefecture "Okinawa Prefecture") even after it was restored to Japanese sovereignty.

### 1968–1969 University crisis

Overcrowded universities, increasing student radicalization, hopes for an abrogation of the [US-Japan Security Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US-Japan_Security_Treaty?action=edit&redlink=1 "US-Japan Security Treaty") after its initial 10-year term ended in 1970, and growing opposition to Japan's material and ideological support for [America's war in Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") helped precipitate large scale protests at hundreds of Japanese schools and universities in 1968–1969, part of a [worldwide protest cycle in 1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protests_of_1968 "Protests of 1968"). After more than a year of conflict, Satō's administration responded by calling in riot police to forcibly clear the university campuses. Thereafter, Satō allowed the Security Treaty to automatically renew in 1970, dashing the hopes of activist groups who staged [large street protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anpo_protests "Anpo protests") in an attempt to eliminate it.

### Nixon shocks and resignation

The successful resolution of the university crisis, continued robust economic growth, and above all, the 1969 announcement of the United States' commitment to return Okinawa to Japan, made Satō broadly popular with the Japanese public and allowed him to win a then unprecedented third consecutive term as prime minister. However, Satō's popularity soon nosedived, in the aftermath of so-called "[Nixon Shocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_Shock "Nixon Shock")" of 1971. In July 1971, the Japanese government was stunned by Nixon's dramatic announcement of his forthcoming [visit to the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Nixon_visit_to_China "1972 Nixon visit to China"). Many Japanese were chagrined by the failure of the United States to consult in advance with Japan before making such a fundamental change in foreign policy, and the sudden change in America's stance made Satō's staunch adherence to non-relations with China look like he had been played for a fool.

The following month, the government was again surprised to learn that, without prior consultation, Nixon was imposing a 10 percent surcharge on imports, a decision explicitly aimed at hindering Japan's exports to the United States, and was unilaterally suspending the convertibility of dollars into gold, which would eventually lead to the collapse of the [Bretton Woods system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bretton_Woods_system "Bretton Woods system") of fixed currency exchange rates. The resulting decoupling of the yen and the dollar led the yen to soar in value, significantly damaging Japan's international trade and economic outlook.

With his approval ratings plummeting, Satō abandoned plans to run for a fourth term, and resigned from office in 1972. His heir apparent, [Takeo Fukuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeo_Fukuda "Takeo Fukuda"), won the Sato faction's support in the subsequent LDP presidential election, but the more popular [MITI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_International_Trade_and_Industry "Ministry of International Trade and Industry") minister, [Kakuei Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakuei_Tanaka "Kakuei Tanaka"), won the vote, ending the Satō faction's longstanding dominance in Diet politics.

## Later life

Upon leaving the premiership in 1972 to an approval rating of 19% (by April) and a fractured party, Satō moved back to his home in [Setagaya Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setagaya_Ward "Setagaya Ward"), Tokyo, staying out of the eyes of the media but remaining in the Diet. His reputation, however, quickly began to be rehabilitated, starting in November of that year with his awarding of the [Grand Cordon of the Supreme Order of the Chrysanthemum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Cordon_of_the_Supreme_Order_of_the_Chrysanthemum "Grand Cordon of the Supreme Order of the Chrysanthemum"). Satō opened up to the media after the award, with outlets noting his visual image change, with a longer hairstyle reminiscent of the post-presidency of Lyndon B. Johnson.

Satō quickly settled into his life as an elder statesman. In January 1973, Satō and his wife were invited to [President Richard Nixon's second inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Richard_Nixon "Second inauguration of Richard Nixon"). Satō maintained close relations with Nixon, sending him his personal condolences upon his [resignation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resignation_of_Richard_Nixon "Resignation of Richard Nixon"), and Nixon attended his funeral.

Upon returning to Japan, his successor, the initially-popular [Kakuei Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakuei_Tanaka "Kakuei Tanaka"), who had been handed a rebuke with 17 seat losses in the [1972 Japanese general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Japanese_general_election "1972 Japanese general election"), looked to Satō to repair relations within the LDP, especially towards his rival [Takeo Fukuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeo_Fukuda "Takeo Fukuda"). Both men were Satō's protégés, and Satō advised Tanaka in the forming of his post-election cabinet, notably including Fukuda as director-general of the Administrative Agency. Although privately critical towards Tanaka's government, Satō remained in the public eye a unifier within the LDP.

Satō shared the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") with [Seán MacBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seán_MacBride "Seán MacBride") in 1974. He was awarded for representing the Japanese people's will for peace, and for signing the nuclear arms Non-Proliferation Treaty in 1970. He was the first Asian to accept the Nobel Peace Prize. (In 1973, Vietnamese politician [Le Duc Tho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Duc_Tho "Le Duc Tho") had become the first Asian to *win* the prize, but Tho had rejected it.) Satō began working with MacBride shortly after, joining [Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International").

In April 1975, as part of his last foreign visit before his death, Satō was chosen as the LDP representative to attend the [funeral of Chiang Kai-Shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Chiang_Kai-Shek "Death and state funeral of Chiang Kai-Shek"). However, upon protest from the Chinese government of Satō's role as "official envoy of the LDP president", his role was relegated to a "friendship representative".

## Death

On 19 May 1975, Satō attended a dinner in Shikiraku, a restaurant in Tokyo's [Tsukiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsukiji "Tsukiji") district, attended by Fukuda. During the event, he suffered a massive stroke, resulting in a coma. He was held in an emergency unit in the restaurant for four days before being moved to hospital. He died at 12:55 a.m. on 3 June at the [Jikei University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jikei_University_School_of_Medicine "Jikei University School of Medicine") Medical Center, aged 74. After a public funeral, his ashes were buried in the family cemetery at [Tabuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabuse "Tabuse").

Satō was posthumously honored with the Collar of the [Order of the Chrysanthemum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Chrysanthemum "Order of the Chrysanthemum"), the highest honor in the Japanese honors system.

## Personal life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Satou_Family1.jpg/250px-Satou_Family1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Satou_Family1.jpg)

From left – Hiroko, Shinji, Eisaku, Ryūtarō, & Fujie (Matsuoka), 1931

Satō married Hiroko Satō ([佐藤 寛子](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/佐藤寛子%20(首相夫人) "jp:佐藤寛子 (首相夫人)"); 5 January 1907 – 16 April 1987) in 1926 and had two sons, Ryūtarō and [Shinji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinji_Sato_(politician) "Shinji Sato (politician)"). Hiroko's father, Matsusuke Satō, was Eisaku's maternal uncle and the head of main Satō family. After Matsusuke died in 1911, Hiroko, her sister and mother were supported by her maternal uncle, diplomat [Yōsuke Matsuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yōsuke_Matsuoka "Yōsuke Matsuoka"). Their son Shinji followed his father into politics, serving in both houses and as a cabinet minister. Shinji's son-in-law, [Masashi Adachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masashi_Adachi "Masashi Adachi"), currently serves in the [House of Councillors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Councillors_(Japan) "House of Councillors (Japan)"), and formerly worked as an aide for his cousin-in-law, Eisaku's grandnephew, [Shinzo Abe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinzo_Abe "Shinzo Abe").

In a 1969 *Shukan Asahi* interview with novelist [Shūsaku Endō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shūsaku_Endō "Shūsaku Endō"), Hiroko accused Satō of being a [rake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rake_(character) "Rake (character)") and a [wife-beater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_violence "Domestic violence"). His hobbies included golf, fishing, and the [Japanese tea ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_tea_ceremony "Japanese tea ceremony"). [Nobusuke Kishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobusuke_Kishi "Nobusuke Kishi") (his older brother) and [Shinzō Abe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinzō_Abe "Shinzō Abe") (his grandnephew) were also both prime ministers.

## Honours

Satō received the following awards:

- [Golden Pheasant Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Pheasant_Award "Golden Pheasant Award") of the [Scout Association of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scout_Association_of_Japan "Scout Association of Japan") (1970)
- [Grand Cordon of the Order of the Chrysanthemum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Chrysanthemum "Order of the Chrysanthemum") (3 November 1972)
- [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") (11 December 1974)
- [Collar of the Order of the Chrysanthemum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Chrysanthemum "Order of the Chrysanthemum") (3 June 1975; posthumous)
- [Junior First Rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Japanese_court_ranks,_positions_and_hereditary_titles "List of Japanese court ranks, positions and hereditary titles") (3 June 1975)
