**Dag Hjalmar Agne Carl Hammarskjöld** ([/ˈhæmərʃʊld/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*HAM-ər-shuuld*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); Swedish: [[ˈdɑːɡ ˈhâmːarˌɧœld]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Swedish "Help:IPA/Swedish") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sv-Dag_Hammarskj%C3%B6ld.ogg "File:Sv-Dag Hammarskjöld.ogg"); 29 July 1905 – 18 September 1961) was a Swedish economist and diplomat who served as the second [Secretary-General of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-General_of_the_United_Nations "Secretary-General of the United Nations") from April 1953 until his death in [a plane crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Ndola_Transair_Sweden_DC-6_crash "1961 Ndola Transair Sweden DC-6 crash") in September 1961. As of 2026, he remains the youngest person to have held the post, having been 47 years old when he was elected. He was a son of [Hjalmar Hammarskjöld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hjalmar_Hammarskjöld "Hjalmar Hammarskjöld"), who served as [Prime Minister of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Sweden "Prime Minister of Sweden") from 1914 to 1917.

Hammarskjöld's tenure was characterized by efforts to strengthen the newly-formed UN both internally and externally. He led initiatives to improve morale and organizational efficiency while seeking to make the UN more responsive to global issues. He presided over the creation of the first UN peacekeeping forces in [Egypt (the UNEF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Emergency_Force "United Nations Emergency Force") and [the Congo (the ONUC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_the_Congo "United Nations Operation in the Congo") and personally intervened to defuse or resolve diplomatic crises. Hammarskjöld's second term was cut short when he died in a plane crash while en route to [cease-fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cease-fire "Cease-fire") negotiations during the [Congo Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Crisis "Congo Crisis").

Hammarskjöld was and remains well regarded internationally as a capable diplomat and administrator, and his efforts to resolve various global crises led to him being the only [posthumous recipient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posthumous_award "Posthumous award") of the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"). In the Western world, his appointment and tenure were hailed as one of the most notable and successful in UN leadership. U.S. president [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy") called Hammarskjöld "the greatest statesman of our century". In the [Democratic Republic of Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Congo "Democratic Republic of Congo") and broader [Global South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_South "Global South"), however, his legacy is extremely controversial, given his erratic performance in the Congo Crisis, with consequences to this day.

## Early life and education

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/%C3%96stra_Storgatan_91.jpg/250px-%C3%96stra_Storgatan_91.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Östra_Storgatan_91.jpg)

Hammarskjöld's birthplace in [Jönköping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jönköping "Jönköping")

Dag Hammarskjöld was born in [Jönköping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jönköping "Jönköping") to the [noble family Hammarskjöld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammarskjöld_family "Hammarskjöld family") (also spelled *Hammarskiöld* or *Hammarsköld*). He spent most of his childhood in [Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala "Uppsala"). His home there, which he considered his childhood home, was [Uppsala Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_Castle "Uppsala Castle"). He was the fourth and youngest son of [Hjalmar Hammarskjöld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hjalmar_Hammarskjöld "Hjalmar Hammarskjöld"), [Prime Minister of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Sweden "Prime Minister of Sweden") from 1914 to 1917, and [Agnes Hammarskjöld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnes_Hammarskjöld "Agnes Hammarskjöld").

Hammarskjöld studied first at [Katedralskolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katedralskolan,_Uppsala "Katedralskolan, Uppsala") and then at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University"). By January 1930, he had obtained [Licentiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licentiate_(degree) "Licentiate (degree)") of Philosophy and [Master of Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Laws "Master of Laws") degrees. Before he finished his law degree he had already obtained a job as Assistant Secretary of the Unemployment Committee.

## Career

From 1930 to 1934, Hammarskjöld was secretary of a governmental committee on unemployment. During this time he wrote his economics thesis, "Konjunkturspridningen" ("The Spread of the Business Cycle"), and received a doctorate from [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University "Stockholm University"). In 1936, he became a secretary in Sweden's central bank, the [Riksbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sveriges_Riksbank "Sveriges Riksbank"). From 1941 to 1948, he served as chairman of the Riksbank's General Council.

Hammarskjöld quickly developed a successful career as a Swedish public servant. He was state secretary in the [Ministry of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_(Sweden) "Ministry of Finance (Sweden)") (1936–1945), Swedish delegate to the [Organization for European Economic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD") (1947–1953), cabinet secretary for the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_for_Foreign_Affairs_(Sweden) "Ministry for Foreign Affairs (Sweden)") (1949–1951), and [minister without portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_without_portfolio "Minister without portfolio") in [Tage Erlander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tage_Erlander "Tage Erlander")'s government (1951–1953).

He helped coordinate government plans to alleviate the economic problems of the post-[World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") period and was a delegate to the Paris conference that established the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan "Marshall Plan"). In 1950, he became head of the Swedish delegation to [UNISCAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNISCAN?action=edit&redlink=1 "UNISCAN"), a forum to promote economic cooperation between the United Kingdom and the Scandinavian countries. Although Hammarskjöld served in a cabinet dominated by the [Social Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Social_Democratic_Party "Swedish Social Democratic Party"), he never officially joined any political party.

In 1951, Hammarskjöld was vice chairman of the Swedish delegation to the [United Nations General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly "United Nations General Assembly") in Paris. He became the chairman of the Swedish delegation to the [General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Assembly_of_UN "General Assembly of UN") in New York in 1952. On 20 December 1954, he was elected to take his father's vacated seat in the [Swedish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy "Swedish Academy").

## United Nations Secretary-General

### Nomination and election

On 10 November 1952, [Trygve Lie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trygve_Lie "Trygve Lie") announced his resignation as [Secretary-General of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-General_of_the_United_Nations "Secretary-General of the United Nations"). Several months of negotiations ensued between the Western powers and the Soviet Union without reaching an agreement on his successor. On 13 and 19 March 1953, the [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council "Security Council") voted on four candidates. [Lester B. Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson "Lester B. Pearson") of Canada was the only candidate to receive the required majority, but he was vetoed by the Soviet Union. At a consultation of the permanent members on 30 March 1953, [French permanent representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Representative_of_France_to_the_United_Nations "Permanent Representative of France to the United Nations") [Henri Hoppenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Hoppenot "Henri Hoppenot") suggested four candidates, including Hammarskjöld, whom he had met at the [Organisation for European Economic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD "OECD").

The superpowers hoped to seat a Secretary-General who would focus on administrative issues and refrain from participating in political discussion. Hammarskjöld's reputation at the time was, in the words of biographer [Emery Kelèn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emery_Kelèn?action=edit&redlink=1 "Emery Kelèn"), "that of a brilliant economist, an unobtrusive technician, and an aristo-bureaucrat". As a result, there was little to no controversy in his selection; the [Soviet permanent representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Permanent_Representative_to_the_United_Nations "Soviet Permanent Representative to the United Nations"), [Valerian Zorin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerian_Zorin "Valerian Zorin"), found Hammarskjöld "harmless". Zorin declared that he would be voting for Hammarskjöld, surprising the Western powers. The announcement set off a flurry of diplomatic activity. [British Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_of_the_United_Kingdom "Foreign Secretary of the United Kingdom") [Anthony Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Eden "Anthony Eden") was strongly in favor of Hammarskjöld and asked the United States to "take any appropriate action to induce the [Nationalist] Chinese to abstain". (Sweden recognized the [People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_China "People's Republic of China") and faced a potential veto from the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China "Republic of China").) At the [U.S. State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._State_Department "U.S. State Department"), the nomination "came as a complete surprise to everyone here and we started scrambling around to find out who Mr. Hammarskjold was and what his qualifications were". The State Department authorized [Henry Cabot Lodge Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cabot_Lodge_Jr. "Henry Cabot Lodge Jr."), the [US Ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_ambassador_to_the_UN "US ambassador to the UN"), to vote in favor after he told them that Hammarskjöld "may be as good as we can get".

> *Journalist*: "We understand you've been designated Secretary-General of the United Nations."
> *Hammarskjöld*: "This April Fool's Day joke is in extremely bad taste: it's nonsense!"

— Exchange between a Stockholm journalist and Hammarskjöld, 1 April 1953

On 31 March 1953, the [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council "Security Council") voted 10–0–1 to recommend Hammarskjöld to the General Assembly, with an abstention from Nationalist China. The vote was conducted in secret, and Hammarskjöld was unaware his name had been put forward for the position. Shortly after midnight on 1 April 1953, Hammarskjöld was awakened by a telephone call from a journalist with the news, which he dismissed as an [April Fool's Day joke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Fools'_Day "April Fools' Day"). He finally believed the news after the third phone call. The [Swedish mission in New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulate_General_of_Sweden,_New_York_City "Consulate General of Sweden, New York City") confirmed the nomination at 03:00 and a communique from the Security Council was soon thereafter delivered to him. After consulting with the Swedish cabinet and his father, Hammarskjöld decided to accept the nomination. He sent a wire to the Security Council:

> With strong feeling personal insufficiency I hesitate to accept candidature but I do not feel I could refuse to assume the task imposed on me should the [UN General] Assembly follow the recommendation of the Security Council by which I feel deeply honoured.

Later in the day, Hammarskjöld held a press conference at the Swedish Foreign Ministry. According to diplomat [Sverker Åström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sverker_Åström "Sverker Åström"), he displayed an intense interest and knowledge in the affairs of the UN, which he had never shown any indication of before.

The UN General Assembly voted 57–1–1 (the lone vote against came from Nationalist China) on 7 April 1953 to appoint Dag Hammarskjöld as Secretary-General of the United Nations. Hammarskjöld was sworn in as Secretary-General on 10 April 1953. He was unanimously reelected on 26 September 1957 for another term, taking effect on 10 April 1958.

### Tenure

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Dag_Hammarskjold_outside_the_UN_building.jpg/250px-Dag_Hammarskjold_outside_the_UN_building.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dag_Hammarskjold_outside_the_UN_building.jpg)

Hammarskjöld (age 48) outside the [UN headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_headquarters "United Nations headquarters") in New York City, 1953

Immediately following the assumption of the Secretariat, Hammarskjöld attempted to establish a good rapport with his staff. He made a point of visiting as every UN department to shake hands with as many workers as possible, eating in the cafeteria as often as possible, and relinquishing the Secretary-General's private elevator for general use. He began his term by establishing his own secretariat of 4,000 administrators and setting up regulations that defined their responsibilities. He was also actively engaged in smaller projects relating to the UN working environment; for example, he spearheaded the building of a [meditation room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headquarters_of_the_United_Nations_meditation_room "Headquarters of the United Nations meditation room") at the [UN headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_headquarters "UN headquarters"), where people can withdraw into themselves in silence, regardless of their faith, creed, or religion.

During his term, Hammarskjöld tried to improve relations between Israel and the [Arab states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_states "Arab states"), frequently playing the role of a mediator between [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion "David Ben-Gurion") and [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser "Gamal Abdel Nasser"). Other highlights include a 1955 visit to China to negotiate the release of 11 captured US pilots who had served in the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War"), the 1956 establishment of the [United Nations Emergency Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Emergency_Force "United Nations Emergency Force"), and his intervention in the 1956 [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis "Suez Crisis"). He is given credit by some historians for allowing participation of the [Holy See within the UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_See_and_the_United_Nations "Holy See and the United Nations") that year.

In 1960, the newly independent [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Congo_(Léopoldville) "Republic of the Congo (Léopoldville)") asked for UN aid in defusing the [Congo Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Crisis "Congo Crisis"). In July of that year, the UN established its first [peacekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping "Peacekeeping") mission, the [United Nations Operation in the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_the_Congo "United Nations Operation in the Congo") (known by its French abbreviation ONUC), which peaked at nearly 20,000 military personnel primarily from India, Ireland and Sweden and remains among the UN's largest operations in size and scope. ONUC's mandate sought to restore order in [Congo-Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo-Kinshasa "Congo-Kinshasa") and to keep other nations, namely Belgium, out of the crisis. During this time Hammarskjöld made four trips to Congo, but his efforts toward the [decolonisation of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Africa "Decolonisation of Africa") were considered insufficient by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"). Hammarskjöld publicly justified the UN’s refusal to act decisively against the [Katanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Katanga "State of Katanga") secession on the ground of non-intervention, arguing that the issue was an internal Congolese political conflict. However, this position was widely disputed, especially because Katanga’s secession was closely linked to Belgian official and private interests from the beginning. Later UN documents themselves noted the extensive Belgian influence in Katanga’s civilian and security administration. In September 1960, the Soviet government denounced his decision to send a UN emergency force to keep the peace. They demanded his resignation and the replacement of the office of Secretary-General by a three-man directorate with a built-in veto, the "[troika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumvirate "Triumvirate")". The objective was, citing the memoirs of Soviet leader [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev "Nikita Khrushchev"), to "equally represent interests of three groups of countries: capitalist, socialist and recently independent".

Hammarskjöld's refusal to place peacekeepers in the service of [Lumumba's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Lumumba "Patrice Lumumba") constitutionally elected government provoked a strong reaction of disapproval from the Soviets. Declassified documents suggest that Hammarskjöld and senior UN officials were not politically neutral in the Congo crisis. Privately, Hammarskjöld viewed Lumumba as an obstacle, communicated such views to American and British officials, and reportedly supported efforts to weaken or replace him while avoiding open extra-constitutional intervention. UN actions during the conflict also favoured President [Kasavubu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kasa-Vubu "Joseph Kasa-Vubu") against Lumumba, and Hammarskjöld maintained close contact with US and British officials, sharing confidential ONUC information and advising them on Congo policy. The situation would become more scandalous with the assassination of Lumumba by [Tshombe's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moïse_Tshombe "Moïse Tshombe") troops. In February 1961, the UN authorized the Peacekeeping Forces to use military force to prevent civil war. The Blue Helmets' attack on Katanga caused Tshombe to flee to Zambia. Hammarskjöld's erratic attitude in not providing support to Lumumba's government, which had been elected by popular vote, drew severe criticism among non-aligned countries and communist and socialist countries. Hammarskjöld knew that the Belgian Government, allegedly supported by the United States, arranged for the assassination of Patrice Lumumba. In the end, his actions were supported only by the United States and [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium "Belgium").

His final report to the United Nations was some 6,000 words and is considered to be one of his most important. The report was dictated in a single afternoon to his assistant, Hannah Platz.

## Death

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Dag_Hammask%C3%B6lds_gravsten.jpg/250px-Dag_Hammask%C3%B6lds_gravsten.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dag_Hammaskölds_gravsten.jpg)

Hammarskjöld's grave in [Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala "Uppsala")

On 18 September 1961, Hammarskjöld was en route to negotiate a [cease-fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cease-fire "Cease-fire") between [United Nations Operation in the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_the_Congo "United Nations Operation in the Congo") forces and [Katangese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Katanga "State of Katanga") troops under [Moise Tshombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moise_Tshombe "Moise Tshombe"). His [Douglas DC-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-6 "Douglas DC-6") airliner [SE-BDY crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Ndola_United_Nations_DC-6_crash "1961 Ndola United Nations DC-6 crash") near [Ndola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndola "Ndola"), [Northern Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Rhodesia_and_Nyasaland "Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland") (now [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia "Zambia")). Hammarskjöld perished as a result of the crash, as did all of the 15 other passengers. Hammarskjöld's death set off a succession crisis at the United Nations, because there was no line of succession and as a result, the Security Council had to [vote on a successor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection "1961 United Nations Secretary-General selection").

The circumstances of the crash are still unclear. A 1962 Rhodesian inquiry concluded that pilot error was to blame, while a later UN investigation could not determine the cause of the crash. There is evidence suggesting the plane was shot down. A KGB report claimed the CIA was responsible.

The day after the crash, former U.S. President [Harry Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Truman "Harry Truman") commented that Hammarskjöld "was on the point of getting something done when they killed him. Notice that I said 'when they killed him'."

In 1998, documents were discovered that detailed an alleged plot, named Operation Celeste, to assassinate Hammarskjöld. The alleged plot was backed by the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA "CIA"), [MI6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Intelligence_Service "Secret Intelligence Service") and a Belgian mining interest and the assassination was to be carried out by the [South African Institute for Maritime Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Institute_for_Maritime_Research "South African Institute for Maritime Research"), a South African paramilitary organisation. One of the documents stated that CIA director [Allen Dulles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Dulles "Allen Dulles") agreed that "Dag is becoming troublesome ... and should be removed" and pledged local CIA support for the alleged plot. The information was contained in a file that the [South African National Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Intelligence_Agency_(South_Africa) "National Intelligence Agency (South Africa)") turned over to the [South African Truth and Reconciliation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Truth_and_Reconciliation_Commission "South African Truth and Reconciliation Commission") in relation to the 1993 assassination of [Chris Hani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hani "Chris Hani"), leader of the [South African Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Communist_Party "South African Communist Party"). The authenticity of the documents could not be substantiated because they were copies instead of originals. More documents related to the alleged plot were discovered by the South African government in 2016.

In 2011, Göran Björkdahl, a Swedish aid worker whose father worked for the UN in Zambia, wrote that in part, he believed that Hammarskjöld's death was a murder that was committed to benefit mining companies like [Union Minière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Minière_du_Haut_Katanga "Union Minière du Haut Katanga"), after Hammarskjöld had made the UN intervene in the Katanga crisis. Björkdahl based his assertion on interviews with witnesses of the plane crash near the border of the DRC with Zambia and on archival documents.

In 2013, accident investigator Sven Hammarberg was asked by the [International Commission of Jurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Commission_of_Jurists "International Commission of Jurists") to investigate Hammarskjöld's death.

In 2014, newly declassified documents revealed that the American ambassador to the Congo sent a cable to Washington D.C. and in it, he wrote his suspicion that the plane could have been shot down by Belgian mercenary pilot Jan Van Risseghem, commander of the small Katanga Air Force. Van Risseghem died in 2007.

On 16 March 2015, United Nations Secretary-General [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon "Ban Ki-moon") appointed members to an Independent Panel of Experts that was established for the purpose of examining new information that was related to Hammarskjöld's death. The three-member panel was led by [Mohamed Chande Othman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Chande_Othman "Mohamed Chande Othman"), the [Chief Justice of Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Tanzania "Chief Justice of Tanzania"), and it included Kerryn Macaulay (Australia's representative to the [International Civil Aviation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Civil_Aviation_Organization "International Civil Aviation Organization")) and Henrik Larsen (a ballistics expert from the [Danish National Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_National_Police "Danish National Police")). The panel's 99-page report, released 6 July 2015, assigned moderate value to nine new eyewitness accounts and transcripts of radio transmissions. Those accounts suggested that Hammarskjöld's plane was already on fire as it was landing and they also suggested that other jet aircraft and other intelligence agents were nearby.

In 2016, the original documents from the 1998 South African investigation surfaced. Those who were familiar with the investigation cautioned that even if they were authentic, the documents could have initially been authored as part of a disinformation campaign.

In 2019, the documentary film *[Cold Case Hammarskjöld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Case_Hammarskjöld "Cold Case Hammarskjöld")* by Danish filmmaker [Mads Brügger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mads_Brügger "Mads Brügger") claimed that Jan van Risseghem had told a friend that he shot down Hammarskjöld's aircraft. This went against the official stance maintained by van Risseghem's family that he was not involved in the death of Hammarskjöld. According to an interview with van Risseghem's wife, he was in Rhodesia negotiating the purchase of a plane for the Katanga Air Force, with the logbooks proving that he was not flying for Katanga at the time. The documentary crew interviewed colleagues of van Risseghem's for the film, all of whom supported their theory. In an interview with Swedish historian Leif Hellström, van Risseghem claimed that he was not in southern Africa at the time the crash happened, and dismissed the idea of his involvement.

In 2020, journalist [Ravi Somaiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_Somaiya "Ravi Somaiya") published the book *The Golden Thread: The Cold War and the Mysterious Death of Dag Hammarskjöld* where he argues that it was no doubt murder.

A document that was found in France amidst the Fonds [Foccart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Foccart "Jacques Foccart") (National Archives in Pierrefitte) in November 2021 is a death warrant for Hammarskjöld that contained the acronym [OAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_armée_secrète "Organisation armée secrète"), the secret organization that was nestled in the French army at the time of [Algeria's war for independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War "Algerian War"). The document reads: "It is high time to put an end to his harmful intrusion ... this sentence common to justice and fairness to be carried out, as soon as possible". The unsigned document is a facsimile that appeared to be a transcription of an original letter.

Hammarskjöld's 1959 will left his personal archive to the [National Library of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Sweden "National Library of Sweden").

## Personal life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Dag_Hammarskj%C3%B6ld_quote_cropped.jpg/250px-Dag_Hammarskj%C3%B6ld_quote_cropped.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dag_Hammarskjöld_quote_cropped.jpg)

A spiritual quote by Dag Hammarskjöld engraved in the stone wall within the Peace Chapel of the [International Peace Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Peace_Garden "International Peace Garden")

In 1953, soon after his appointment as United Nations Secretary-General, Hammarskjöld was interviewed on radio by [Edward R. Murrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_R._Murrow "Edward R. Murrow"). In the talk, Hammarskjöld declared:

> But the explanation of how a man should live a life of active social service in full harmony with himself as a member of the community of spirit, I found in the writings of those great medieval mystics [[Meister Eckhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meister_Eckhart "Meister Eckhart") and [Jan van Ruysbroek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Ruysbroek_(scholar) "Jan van Ruysbroek (scholar)")] for whom 'self-surrender' had been the way to self-realization, and who in 'singleness of mind' and 'inwardness' had found the strength to say yes to every demand which the needs of their neighbours made them face, and to say yes also to every fate life had in store for them when they followed the call of duty as they understood it.

Hammarskjöld's only book, *[Vägmärken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vägmärken "Vägmärken")* (*Markings*, or more literally *Waymarks*), was published in 1963. A collection of his diary reflections, the book starts in 1925, when he was 20 years old, and ends the month before his death in 1961. This diary was found in his New York house, after his death, along with an undated letter addressed to then Swedish Permanent [Under-Secretary for Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Secretary_for_Foreign_Affairs_(Sweden) "State Secretary for Foreign Affairs (Sweden)"), [Leif Belfrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leif_Belfrage?action=edit&redlink=1 "Leif Belfrage"). In this letter, Hammarskjöld wrote:

> These entries provide the only true 'profile' that can be drawn ... If you find them worth publishing, you have my permission to do so.

The foreword is written by the English poet [W. H. Auden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._H._Auden "W. H. Auden"), a friend of Hammarskjöld.

*Markings* was described by the late theologian Henry P. Van Dusen as "the noblest self-disclosure of spiritual struggle and triumph, perhaps the greatest testament of personal faith written ... in the heat of professional life and amidst the most exacting responsibilities for [world peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_peace "World peace") and order". Hammarskjöld wrote, for example:

> We are not permitted to choose the frame of our destiny. But what we put into it is ours. He who wills adventure will experience it – according to the measure of his courage. He who wills sacrifice will be sacrificed – according to the measure of his purity of heart.

*Markings* is characterised by Hammarskjöld's intermingling of prose and [haiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiku "Haiku") poetry in a manner exemplified by the 17th-century Japanese poet [Basho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsuo_Bashō "Matsuo Bashō") in his *[Narrow Roads to the Deep North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow_Roads_to_the_Deep_North "Narrow Roads to the Deep North")*. In his foreword to *Markings*, W. H. Auden quotes Hammarskjöld as stating:

> In our age, the road to holiness necessarily passes through the world of action.

Hammarskjöld's interest in philosophical and spiritual matters is also proven by the finding of [Martin Buber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Buber "Martin Buber")'s main work *[I and Thou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_and_Thou "I and Thou")*, which he was translating into Swedish, in the wreckage after the plane crash.

The [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_in_America "Evangelical Lutheran Church in America") commemorates the life of Hammarskjöld as a renewer of society on the anniversary of his death, 18 September.

[Brian Urquhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Urquhart "Brian Urquhart")'s biography of Hammarskjöld addressed what [Israel Shenker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Shenker "Israel Shenker") described in his *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* review as "the oft-discussed question of Hammaskjöld's sexuality". Urquhart reports that [Trygve Lie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trygve_Lie "Trygve Lie") spread rumours of Hammarskjöld's homosexuality but, having interviewed Hammarskjöld's close friends, Urquhart concludes that "no one who knew him well or worked closely with him thought he was a homosexual". Shenker infers from Urquhart's work "that Hammarskjöld was an example, not unique in contemporary politics, of an [asexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asexuality "Asexuality"), somewhat [narcissistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcissism "Narcissism") individual" and quoted private papers where Hammarskjöld had written that "the Secretary General of the UN should have an iron constitution and should not be married". Despite Urquhart concluding the rumours were inaccurate, [Larry Kramer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Kramer "Larry Kramer") included Hammarskjöld in the "I belong to a culture" speech in his 1985 play *[The Normal Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Normal_Heart "The Normal Heart")*.

## Legacy

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/United_Nations_Media_Global.11.JPG/250px-United_Nations_Media_Global.11.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:United_Nations_Media_Global.11.JPG)

Memorial at the United Nations Headquarters in New York City

### Honors

- Honorary degrees: [Carleton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_University "Carleton University") in Ottawa (then called Carleton College) awarded its first-ever honorary degree to Hammarskjöld in 1954, when it presented him with a [Legum Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legum_Doctor "Legum Doctor"), *honoris causa*. The university has continued this tradition by conferring an honorary doctorate upon every subsequent Secretary-General of the United Nations. He also held honorary degrees from [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University "Oxford University"), United Kingdom; in the United States from [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard "Harvard"), [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale "Yale"), [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania"), [Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst_College "Amherst College"), [Johns Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University"), the [University of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California "University of California"), and [Ohio University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_University "Ohio University"); in Sweden, Uppsala University; and in Canada from [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University "McGill University") as well as Carleton University, in Ottawa.

### People's views

- [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy"): After Hammarskjöld's death, U.S. president John F. Kennedy regretted that he had opposed the UN policy in the Congo and said: "I realise now that in comparison to him, I am a small man. He was the greatest statesman of our century."
- In 2011, *[The Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Financial_Times "The Financial Times")* wrote that Hammarskjöld has remained the benchmark against which later UN Secretaries-General have been judged.
- His legacy in the third world is extremely controversial, especially due to his statements to the British representative at the UN, [Patrick Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Dean_(diplomat) "Patrick Dean (diplomat)"), that Lumumba was "a communist puppet". For the [Democratic Republic of Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Congo "Democratic Republic of Congo"), its erratic performance in the crisis of the 1960s has had disastrous consequences for the country to this day.

The new currency was introduced in 2015.

## Depictions in music and popular culture

In 1974, the Australian-British composer [Malcolm Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Williamson "Malcolm Williamson"), [Master of the Queen's Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_the_Queen's_Music "Master of the Queen's Music"), wrote his *Hammarskjöld Portrait* for soprano and string orchestra. The text was taken from *[Vägmärken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vägmärken "Vägmärken")*, and the work's first performance took place on 30 July 1974, at a [Royal Albert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall "Royal Albert Hall") [Proms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proms "Proms") Concert, with the soprano [Elisabeth Söderström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_Söderström "Elisabeth Söderström"), and the [BBC Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Symphony_Orchestra "BBC Symphony Orchestra") conducted by [John Pritchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Pritchard_(conductor) "John Pritchard (conductor)").

In 1985, Hammerskjöld was one of the names mentioned in the "I Belong to a Culture" speech in [Larry Kramer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Kramer "Larry Kramer")'s play *[The Normal Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Normal_Heart "The Normal Heart")*, where the protagonist includes him in a list of 24 historical gay figures.

In the 2016 film *[The Siege of Jadotville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Siege_of_Jadotville_(film) "The Siege of Jadotville (film)")*, depicting the events of the [Congo Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Crisis "Congo Crisis"), Hammarskjöld's plane (incorrectly a [DC-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-4 "Douglas DC-4")) is purposely shot down by a [fighter jet only used by American forces at the time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_F-4_Phantom_II "McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II") (it's likely this was for production reasons, just as a DC-4 stood in for the DC-6). Hammarskjöld is played by fellow [Swede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_people "Swedish people"), [Mikael Persbrandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikael_Persbrandt "Mikael Persbrandt").

Also in 2016, the [1961 Ndola Transair Sweden DC-6 crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Ndola_Transair_Sweden_DC-6_crash "1961 Ndola Transair Sweden DC-6 crash") was featured in Canadian TV series *[Mayday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayday_(Canadian_TV_series) "Mayday (Canadian TV series)")* [(S15, E5)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mayday_episodes#Season_15_(2016) "List of Mayday episodes"), "Deadly Mission" and *Air Crash Investigation Special Report* [(S3, E3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mayday_episodes#The_Accident_Files#Season_3_(2020) "List of Mayday episodes"), "VIP on Board". Peter James Howarth portrayed Hammarskjöld.

In 2023, Persbrandt again played the eponymous politician, in the film *[Hammarskjöld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammarskjöld_(film) "Hammarskjöld (film)")*, directed by [Per Fly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Fly "Per Fly"). The film received negative reviews for glossing over its Congo Crisis controversies.

In 2024, Hammarskjöld appears as a central character in the Belgian [Oscar-nominated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar-nominated "Oscar-nominated") documentary *[Soundtrack to a Coup d'Etat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soundtrack_to_a_Coup_d'Etat "Soundtrack to a Coup d'Etat")* directed by [Johan Grimonprez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Grimonprez "Johan Grimonprez"). On an interview for Belgian magazine *Glean* the filmmaker mentions the discoveries made by writer [Ludo De Witte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludo_De_Witte "Ludo De Witte") published in the book *The Assassination of Patrice Lumumba* : "He was able to gather a lot of evidence
from United Nations cables and cables within
Belgium that suggested that Dag Hammarskjold
and the Belgian monarchy were indeed complicit
in the downfall of [Lumumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Lumumba "Patrice Lumumba")."

He is depicted in the 2026 movie *[The Swedish Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Swedish_Connection "The Swedish Connection")*, played by [Christoffer Nordenrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoffer_Nordenrot "Christoffer Nordenrot"), during the events leading up to the [Rescue of the Danish Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_of_the_Danish_Jews "Rescue of the Danish Jews").
