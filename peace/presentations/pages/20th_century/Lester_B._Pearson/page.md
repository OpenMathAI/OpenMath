**Lester Bowles Pearson** (23 April 1897 – 27 December 1972) was a Canadian politician, diplomat, and scholar who served as the 14th [prime minister of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Canada "Prime minister of Canada") from 1963 to 1968. He also served as [leader of the Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Liberal_Party_of_Canada "Leader of the Liberal Party of Canada") from 1958 to 1968 and as [leader of the Official Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Official_Opposition_(Canada) "Leader of the Official Opposition (Canada)") from 1958 to 1963.

Born in [Newtonbrook, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtonbrook "Newtonbrook") (now part of [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto "Toronto")), Pearson pursued a career in the [Department of External Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Affairs_Canada "Global Affairs Canada") and went on to serve as the [Canadian ambassador to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_Canada_to_the_United_States "List of ambassadors of Canada to the United States") from 1944 to 1946. He entered politics in 1948 as [Secretary of State for External Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_(Canada) "Minister of Foreign Affairs (Canada)"), serving in that position until 1957 under [William Lyon Mackenzie King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lyon_Mackenzie_King "William Lyon Mackenzie King") and [Louis St. Laurent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_St._Laurent "Louis St. Laurent"). Pearson was the seventh [president of the United Nations General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly "President of the United Nations General Assembly") from 1952 to 1953. In [1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection "1953 United Nations Secretary-General selection"), he was a candidate to become the [secretary-general of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-General_of_the_United_Nations "Secretary-General of the United Nations"), but was vetoed by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"). In 1957, Pearson won the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") for proposing the [United Nations Emergency Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Emergency_Force "United Nations Emergency Force") to resolve the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis "Suez Crisis"), for which he received worldwide attention. After the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Canada "Liberal Party of Canada") was defeated in the [1957 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Canadian_federal_election "1957 Canadian federal election"), Pearson won the [leadership of the party the following year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Liberal_Party_of_Canada_leadership_election "1958 Liberal Party of Canada leadership election"). He suffered two consecutive defeats by prime minister [John Diefenbaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Diefenbaker "John Diefenbaker") of the [Progressive Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Conservative_Party_of_Canada "Progressive Conservative Party of Canada") in the [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Canadian_federal_election "1958 Canadian federal election") and [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Canadian_federal_election "1962 Canadian federal election") federal elections. He challenged Diefenbaker for a third time in the [1963 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Canadian_federal_election "1963 Canadian federal election"), and won a [minority government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_governments_in_Canada "Minority governments in Canada"). Pearson led the Liberals to a second minority government in [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Canadian_federal_election "1965 Canadian federal election").

During Pearson's tenure as prime minister, he introduced progressive policies including the [Canada Student Loan Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Student_Loan_Program "Canada Student Loan Program"), the [Canada Pension Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Pension_Plan "Canada Pension Plan"), the [Canada Assistance Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Assistance_Plan "Canada Assistance Plan"), the [Canada Labour Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Labour_Code "Canada Labour Code"), and [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_(Canada) "Medicare (Canada)"). He established royal commissions on [bilingualism and biculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_on_Bilingualism_and_Biculturalism "Royal Commission on Bilingualism and Biculturalism") and [the status of women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_on_the_Status_of_Women "Royal Commission on the Status of Women"), oversaw the creation of the [Maple Leaf flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Canada "Flag of Canada") after the [Great Canadian flag debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Canadian_flag_debate "Great Canadian flag debate"), and [unified the Canadian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_the_Canadian_Armed_Forces "Unification of the Canadian Armed Forces"). In 1967, Pearson presided over the [Canadian Centennial celebrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Centennial "Canadian Centennial"). In foreign policy, [Pearson's government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Canadian_Ministry "19th Canadian Ministry") signed the [Auto Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada–United_States_Automotive_Products_Agreement "Canada–United States Automotive Products Agreement") with the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") and kept [Canada out of the Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_and_the_Vietnam_War "Canada and the Vietnam War"). Under his leadership, Canada became the first country in the world to implement a [points-based immigration system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Points-based_immigration_system#Canada "Points-based immigration system"). After five years in power, Pearson resigned as prime minister and retired from politics.

With his government programs and policies, together with his groundbreaking work at the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") and in international diplomacy, which included his role in ending the Suez Crisis, Pearson is considered to be among the most influential Canadians of the 20th century. He [is ranked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_rankings_of_prime_ministers_of_Canada "Historical rankings of prime ministers of Canada") as one of Canada's greatest prime ministers.

## Early life, family, and education

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/Birthplace_of_Lester_B._Pearson_plaque_%28photo_by_Djuradj_Vujcic%29.jpg/250px-Birthplace_of_Lester_B._Pearson_plaque_%28photo_by_Djuradj_Vujcic%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Birthplace_of_Lester_B._Pearson_plaque_(photo_by_Djuradj_Vujcic).jpg)

A memorial plaque on the location of his birthplace

Pearson was born in [Newtonbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtonbrook "Newtonbrook") (now a part of [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto "Toronto")) in the township of [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York,_Toronto "York, Toronto"), Ontario, the son of Annie Sarah (née Bowles) and Edwin Arthur Pearson, a [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodism "Methodism") (later [United Church of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Canada "United Church of Canada")) minister. Lester was the brother of Vaughan Whitier Pearson and Marmaduke "Duke" Pearson. Edwin Arthur was the son of Irish immigrant Marmaduke Louis Pearson and [United Empire Loyalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Empire_Loyalist "United Empire Loyalist") descendant Hester Ann Marsh, while both of his maternal grandparents were children of Irish immigrants. When Pearson was one month old, his family moved to 1984 Yonge Street. Lester Pearson's father moved the young family north of Toronto to [Aurora, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora,_Ontario "Aurora, Ontario"), where he was the minister at Aurora Methodist Church on [Yonge Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonge_Street "Yonge Street"). Lester spent his early years in Aurora and attended the public school on Church Street. The family lived at 39 Catherine Avenue. Pearson was a member of the Aurora Rugby team.

Pearson graduated from Hamilton Collegiate Institute in [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario "Hamilton, Ontario"), in 1913 at the age of 16. Later that same year, he entered [Victoria College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_in_the_University_of_Toronto "Victoria University in the University of Toronto") at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto "University of Toronto"), where he lived in residence in Gate House and shared a room with his brother Duke. He was later elected to the [Pi Gamma Mu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi_Gamma_Mu "Pi Gamma Mu") [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science "Social science") honour society's chapter at the University of Toronto for his outstanding scholastic performance in history and psychology. Just as [Norman Jewison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Jewison "Norman Jewison"), [E. J. Pratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._J._Pratt "E. J. Pratt"), [Northrop Frye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Frye "Northrop Frye") and his student [Margaret Atwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Atwood "Margaret Atwood") would, Pearson participated in the sophomore theatrical tradition of *The Bob Comedy Revue*. After Victoria College, Pearson won a scholarship to study at [St John's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Oxford "St John's College, Oxford"), from 1921 to 1923.

### Sporting interests

At the University of Toronto, Pearson became a noted athlete, excelling in [rugby union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union "Rugby union") and also playing [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball "Basketball"). He later also played for the [Oxford University Ice Hockey Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Ice_Hockey_Club "Oxford University Ice Hockey Club") while on a scholarship at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford"), a team that won the first [Spengler Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spengler_Cup "Spengler Cup") in 1923. Pearson also excelled in [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball "Baseball") and [lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrosse "Lacrosse") as a youth. His baseball talents as an [infielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infielder "Infielder") were strong enough for a summer of semi-pro play with the [Guelph Maple Leafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelph_Maple_Leafs "Guelph Maple Leafs") of the Ontario [Intercounty Baseball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercounty_Baseball_League "Intercounty Baseball League"). Pearson toured North America with a combined Oxford and Cambridge Universities lacrosse team in 1923. After he joined the University of Toronto's History Department as an instructor, he helped to coach the U of T's football and [ice hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey "Ice hockey") teams. He played [golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf "Golf") and [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis") to high standards as an adult.

## First World War

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Pearson_in_World_War_One.jpg/250px-Pearson_in_World_War_One.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pearson_in_World_War_One.jpg)

Pearson serving with the Canadian Army Medical Corps in World War I in [Salonika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaloniki "Thessaloniki")

During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), Pearson volunteered for service as a medical orderly with the University of Toronto hospital unit. In 1915, he entered overseas service with the [Canadian Army Medical Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Army_Medical_Corps "Canadian Army Medical Corps") as a stretcher-bearer with the rank of [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_(rank) "Private (rank)"), and was subsequently promoted to [corporal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporal "Corporal"). During this period of service, he spent nearly two years in southern Europe, being shipped to [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt") and thereafter served on the [Salonika front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salonika_front "Salonika front"). He also served alongside the [Serbian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_campaign "Serbian campaign") as a medical orderly. On 2 August 1917, Pearson was commissioned a temporary lieutenant. The [Royal Canadian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Force "Royal Canadian Air Force") did not exist at that time, so Pearson transferred to Britain's [Royal Flying Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Flying_Corps "Royal Flying Corps"), where he served as a [flying officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_officer "Flying officer"). As a pilot, he received the nickname of "Mike", given to him by a flight instructor who felt that "Lester" was too mild a name for an airman: "That’s a sissy’s name. You’re Mike," the instructor said. Thereafter, Pearson would use the name "Lester" on official documents and in public life, but was always addressed as Mike by friends and family.

Pearson learned to fly at an air training school in [Hendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendon "Hendon"), England. He survived an airplane crash during his first flight. In 1918, Pearson was hit by a bus in London during a citywide [blackout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackout_(wartime) "Blackout (wartime)") and was sent home to recuperate, being demobilised on 11 April 1919.

## Inter-war years

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Ice_hockey_1922.jpg/250px-Ice_hockey_1922.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ice_hockey_1922.jpg)

Ice hockey in Europe; Oxford University vs. [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland"), 1922. Future Canadian prime minister Lester Pearson is at right front. His nickname from the Swiss was "Herr Zig-Zag".

After the war, he returned to school, receiving his [Bachelor of Arts degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts_degree "Bachelor of Arts degree") (BA) from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto "University of Toronto") in 1919. He was able to complete his degree after one more term, under a ruling in force at the time, since he had served in the military during the war. He and his brother Duke then spent a year working in [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario "Hamilton, Ontario"), and in Chicago, in the meat-packing industry at [Armour and Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armour_and_Company "Armour and Company") (whose president at the time, Frank Edson White, was his uncle through marriage to Lillian Sophia Pearson White), which he did not enjoy.

### Oxford

Upon receiving a scholarship from the [Massey Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massey_Foundation "Massey Foundation"), he studied for two years at [St John's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Oxford "St John's College, Oxford") at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford"), where he received a BA degree with second-class honours in modern history in 1923, and the [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degrees_of_Oxford_University "Degrees of Oxford University") in 1925. After Oxford, he returned to Canada and taught history at the University of Toronto.

### Marriage, family

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/John_Ross_Mclean.jpg/250px-John_Ross_Mclean.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:John_Ross_Mclean.jpg)

Pearson with [John Ross McLean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_McLean_(civil_servant) "Ross McLean (civil servant)"), [Vincent Massey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Massey "Vincent Massey") and [Georges Vanier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Vanier "Georges Vanier") on 1 January 1938 at [Canada House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_House "Canada House"), London

In 1925, he married [Maryon Moody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryon_Moody "Maryon Moody"), from Winnipeg, who had been one of his students at the University of Toronto. Together, they had one son, [Geoffrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Pearson "Geoffrey Pearson"), and one daughter, Patricia. Maryon was confident and outspoken, supporting her husband in all his political endeavours.

### Diplomat, public servant

In 1927, after scoring top marks on the Canadian foreign service entry exam, he then embarked on a career in the [Department of External Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_Canada "Foreign Affairs Canada"). Prime minister [R. B. Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._B._Bennett "R. B. Bennett") was a noted talent spotter. He took note of, and encouraged, the young Pearson in the early 1930s, and appointed Pearson to significant roles on two major government inquiries: the 1931 Royal Commission on Grain Futures, and the 1934 Royal Commission on Price Spreads. Bennett saw that Pearson was recognized with an [OBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OBE "OBE") after he shone in that work, arranged a bonus of CA$1,800, and invited him to a London conference. Pearson was assigned to the [High Commission of Canada in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Commission_of_Canada_in_the_United_Kingdom "High Commission of Canada in the United Kingdom") in 1935.

## World War II and aftermath

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Lester_Bowles_Pearson_presiding_at_a_plenary_session_of_the_founding_conference_of_the_United_Nations_Food_and_Agriculture_Organization.jpg/250px-Lester_Bowles_Pearson_presiding_at_a_plenary_session_of_the_founding_conference_of_the_United_Nations_Food_and_Agriculture_Organization.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lester_Bowles_Pearson_presiding_at_a_plenary_session_of_the_founding_conference_of_the_United_Nations_Food_and_Agriculture_Organization.jpg)

Pearson presiding at a plenary session of the founding conference of the United Nations [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization "Food and Agriculture Organization") in 1945.

Pearson continued to serve at [Canada House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_House "Canada House") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") from 1939 through 1942 as the second-in-command, where he coordinated military supply and refugee problems, serving under high commissioner [Vincent Massey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Massey "Vincent Massey").

Pearson returned to [Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa "Ottawa") for a few months, where he was an assistant under secretary from 1941 through 1942. In June 1942 he was posted to the Canadian embassy in Washington, D.C., as a ministerial counsellor. He served as second-in-command for nearly two years. Promoted minister [plenipotentiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenipotentiary "Plenipotentiary") in 1944, he became the second [Canadian ambassador to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Ambassador_to_the_United_States "Canadian Ambassador to the United States") on 1 January 1945. He remained in this position through September 1946.

Pearson had an important part in founding both the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") and the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty_Organization "North Atlantic Treaty Organization") (NATO).

Pearson nearly became the first [secretary-general of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-General_of_the_United_Nations "Secretary-General of the United Nations") in 1946, but was vetoed by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"). He was also the leading candidate for secretary-general in the [1953 selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection,_1953 "United Nations Secretary-General selection, 1953") of the [Seventh session of the United Nations General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_session_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly "Seventh session of the United Nations General Assembly"), when the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") conducted a vigorous campaign on his behalf. He placed first with 10 out of 11 votes in the [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council"), but the lone negative vote was another Soviet veto. The Security Council instead settled on [Dag Hammarskjöld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dag_Hammarskjöld "Dag Hammarskjöld") of [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden"); all UN secretaries-general would come from neutral countries for the rest of the Cold War.

The Canadian prime minister, [Mackenzie King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackenzie_King "Mackenzie King"), tried to recruit Pearson into his government as the war wound down. Pearson felt honoured by King's approach, but resisted due to his personal dislike of King's poor personal style and political methods. Pearson did not make the move into politics until a few years later, after King had announced his retirement as prime minister.

## Secretary of State for External Affairs (1948–1957)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/StLaurnet_Pearson_and_Churchill.jpg/250px-StLaurnet_Pearson_and_Churchill.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:StLaurnet_Pearson_and_Churchill.jpg)

Prime minister [Louis St. Laurent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_St._Laurent "Louis St. Laurent") (far left) and Pearson (far right) welcome [UK prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom "Prime Minister of the United Kingdom") Sir [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill") and foreign secretary Sir [Anthony Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Eden "Anthony Eden") at [Rockcliffe Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa/Rockcliffe_Airport "Ottawa/Rockcliffe Airport"), Ottawa, on 29 June 1954.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/LevesqueinterviewsPearsoninMoscow.jpg/250px-LevesqueinterviewsPearsoninMoscow.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:LevesqueinterviewsPearsoninMoscow.jpg)

[René Levesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Levesque "René Levesque") interviews Pearson in Moscow, 1955

In 1948, before his retirement, prime minister King appointed Pearson [Secretary of State for External Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_External_Affairs_(Canada) "Secretary of State for External Affairs (Canada)") in the [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Canada "Liberal Party of Canada") government. Shortly afterward, Pearson won a seat in the [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada "House of Commons of Canada"), for the federal riding of [Algoma East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algoma_East "Algoma East") in [Northern Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ontario "Northern Ontario"). Pearson then served as Secretary of State for External Affairs for prime minister [Louis St. Laurent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_St._Laurent "Louis St. Laurent"), until the defeat of the St. Laurent government in 1957.

### Vetoed by the Soviet Union

On 10 November 1952, [Trygve Lie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trygve_Lie "Trygve Lie") announced his resignation as [secretary-general of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-General_of_the_United_Nations "Secretary-General of the United Nations"). Several months of negotiations ensued between the Western powers and the Soviet Union without reaching an agreement on his successor. On 13 and 19 March 1953, the Security Council voted on four candidates. This came one week after [Stalin's death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Joseph_Stalin "Death and state funeral of Joseph Stalin") but before [Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrushchev "Khrushchev")'s rise to power. Pearson was the only candidate to receive the required majority, but he was vetoed by the Soviet Union.

### Role in Suez crisis leads to Nobel Peace Prize

In 1957, for his role in resolving the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis "Suez Crisis") through the United Nations one year earlier, Pearson was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"). The selection committee argued that Pearson had "saved the world", but critics accused him of betraying the motherland and [Canada's ties with the UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada–United_Kingdom_relations "Canada–United Kingdom relations"). Pearson and UN secretary-general [Dag Hammarskjöld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dag_Hammarskjöld "Dag Hammarskjöld") are considered the fathers of the modern concept of [peacekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping "Peacekeeping"). Together, they organized the [United Nations Emergency Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Emergency_Force "United Nations Emergency Force") by way of a five-day fly-around in early November 1956 after the [First emergency special session of the United Nations General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_emergency_special_session_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly "First emergency special session of the United Nations General Assembly"). His Nobel medal was on permanent display in the front lobby of the [Lester B. Pearson Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson_Building "Lester B. Pearson Building"), the headquarters of [Global Affairs Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Affairs_Canada "Global Affairs Canada") in Ottawa until 2017 when the medal was loaned to the [Canadian Museum of History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Museum_of_History "Canadian Museum of History"), to be displayed in the 'Canadian History Hall'.

## Opposition leader (1958–1963)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Lester_Pearson_at_election_campaign_event_%2850540638661%29.jpg/250px-Lester_Pearson_at_election_campaign_event_%2850540638661%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lester_Pearson_at_election_campaign_event_(50540638661).jpg)

Pearson campaigning for [Bruce Beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Beer "Bruce Beer") in [Peel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_(federal_electoral_district) "Peel (federal electoral district)") during the 1962 Federal election

St. Laurent was defeated by the [Progressive Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Conservative_Party_of_Canada "Progressive Conservative Party of Canada") under [John Diefenbaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Diefenbaker "John Diefenbaker") in the [election of 1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Canadian_federal_election "1957 Canadian federal election"). After just a few months as [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Canada) "Leader of the Opposition (Canada)"), St. Laurent retired, and he endorsed Pearson as his successor. Pearson was elected leader of the Liberal Party at its [leadership convention of 1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Liberal_Party_of_Canada_leadership_election "1958 Liberal Party of Canada leadership election"), defeating his chief rival, former cabinet minister [Paul Martin Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Martin_Sr. "Paul Martin Sr.")

At his first parliamentary session as opposition leader, Pearson asked Diefenbaker to give power back to the Liberals without an election, because of a recent economic downturn. This strategy backfired when Diefenbaker showed a classified Liberal document saying that the economy would face a downturn in that year. This contrasted heavily with the Liberal campaign promises of 1957.

Consequently, Pearson's party was routed in the [federal election of 1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Canadian_federal_election "1958 Canadian federal election"). Diefenbaker's Conservatives won the largest majority ever seen in Canada to that point (208 of 265 seats). The Liberals lost over half their seats and were cut down to only 48 seats, the fewest in their history at the time. Furthermore, the election cost the Liberals their stronghold in [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec "Quebec"). This province had voted largely Liberal in federal elections since the [Conscription Crisis of 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_Crisis_of_1917 "Conscription Crisis of 1917"), but Quebec had no [favourite son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favourite_son "Favourite son") leader, as it had had since 1948.

Pearson convened a significant "Thinkers' Conference" at [Kingston, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Ontario "Kingston, Ontario") in 1960. This event developed many of the ideas later implemented when he became the prime minister.

In the [federal election of 1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Canadian_federal_election "1962 Canadian federal election"), the Liberals, led by Pearson, recovered much of what they had lost in their severe defeat four years earlier. Liberal gains and the surprise election of 30 [Social Credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Credit "Social Credit") MPs deprived the Tories of their majority. As a consequence, Diefenbaker now had to preside over a [minority government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_governments_in_Canada "Minority governments in Canada").

Not long after the election, Pearson capitalized on the Conservatives' indecision on accepting [American nuclear warheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NORAD_nuclear_weapons_in_Canada "NORAD nuclear weapons in Canada") on Canadian [BOMARC missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BOMARC_missile "BOMARC missile"). Defence minister [Douglas Harkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Harkness "Douglas Harkness") resigned from Cabinet on 4 February 1963, because of Diefenbaker's opposition to accepting the warheads. On the next day, the government lost two non-confidence motions on the issue, [forcing a national election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Canadian_federal_election "1963 Canadian federal election") for a House only a year old. The Liberals raced out to a large lead in opinion polling, and for a time the only question was how large Pearson's majority would be. However, Pearson was forced off the hustings for a time due to ill health. Additionally, when the [United States Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense") leaked documents detailing the proposed missile defences, the Tories claimed a Liberal government would let Canada be a decoy in the event of a nuclear exchange with the Soviets.

By election day, the Liberals had recovered their momentum and took 129 seats to the Tories' 95. The Liberals won 41 percent of the vote, normally enough for a majority. However, their gains were heavily concentrated in Ontario, Quebec and the Atlantic. Winning only three seats in the Prairies, they were five short of a majority. After six Social Credit MPs from Quebec announced their support for the Liberals, Pearson was able to guarantee stable government to the governor general. Rather than face certain defeat in the Commons, Diefenbaker resigned, allowing Pearson to form a minority government. He was sworn in as prime minister on 22 April, a day before his 66th birthday. While the *créditistes* repudiated this statement days later, Pearson was able to stay in office with the support of the [New Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Democratic_Party "New Democratic Party").

### Domestic policy and events

Pearson campaigned during the 1963 election promising "60 Days of Decision" and supported the [Bomarc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomarc "Bomarc") [surface-to-air missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface-to-air_missile "Surface-to-air missile") program. Pearson never had a majority in the [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada "House of Commons of Canada"), but he brought in many of Canada's major updated social programs, including [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_(Canada) "Medicare (Canada)") (though that credit should be shared with [Tommy Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Douglas "Tommy Douglas"), who as premier of Saskatchewan had introduced the country's first medicare system), the [Canada Pension Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Pension_Plan "Canada Pension Plan"), and [Canada Student Loans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Student_Loans "Canada Student Loans"). Pearson instituted a new national flag, the [Maple Leaf flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Canada "Flag of Canada"), after a national debate known as the [Great Canadian flag debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Canadian_flag_debate "Great Canadian flag debate"). He also instituted the 40-hour work week, two weeks vacation time, and a new [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage "Minimum wage") for workers in federally-regulated areas.

In hopes of winning an outright majority, Pearson called an election for [November 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Canadian_federal_election "1965 Canadian federal election"), three years before it was due. Ultimately, the Liberals were only able to pick up three more seats, leaving them two short of a majority. As in 1963, the Liberals were almost nonexistent in the Prairies, winning only one seat there, that of Veterans Affairs minister [Roger Teillet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Teillet "Roger Teillet").

Pearson also started a number of [royal commissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_commission "Royal commission"), including the [Royal Commission on the Status of Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_on_the_Status_of_Women "Royal Commission on the Status of Women") and the [Royal Commission on Bilingualism and Biculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_on_Bilingualism_and_Biculturalism "Royal Commission on Bilingualism and Biculturalism"). These suggested changes that helped create legal equality for women and brought official [bilingualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilingualism_in_Canada "Bilingualism in Canada") into being. After Pearson's term in office, French was made an [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language "Official language"), and the Canadian government provided services in both English and French. Pearson hoped to be the last unilingual prime minister of Canada, and fluency in both English and French became an unofficial requirement for candidates for prime minister after Pearson left office.

In 1966, the [Canada Assistance Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Assistance_Plan "Canada Assistance Plan") was introduced, under which the federal government supported provincial social assistance costs (including half the cost of various health services for those in need such as eyeglasses, dental services, and prescription drugs) and (as noted by one study) “required the provinces to provide assistance to those in need.”

In 1967, Pearson's government introduced a discrimination-free [points-based system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Points-based_immigration_system "Points-based immigration system") which encouraged immigration to Canada, the first country to do so.

Pearson oversaw [Canada's centennial celebrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Centennial "Canadian Centennial") in 1967 before retiring. The Canadian news agency, *[The Canadian Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canadian_Press "The Canadian Press")*, named him "[Newsmaker of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Newsmaker_of_the_Year "Canadian Newsmaker of the Year")", citing his leadership during the centennial celebrations, which brought the [Centennial Flame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centennial_Flame "Centennial Flame") to [Parliament Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_Hill "Parliament Hill").

Several other reforms were carried out during Pearson's time as prime minister, affecting areas such as social security, housing, and agriculture

### Foreign policy

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/1/1a/Trudeau%2C_Turner%2C_Chretien%2C_and_Pearson.jpg/250px-Trudeau%2C_Turner%2C_Chretien%2C_and_Pearson.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Trudeau,_Turner,_Chretien,_and_Pearson.jpg)

Pearson, and three of his cabinet ministers who later became prime ministers. From left to right, [Pierre Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Trudeau "Pierre Trudeau"), [John Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Turner "John Turner"), [Jean Chrétien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Chrétien "Jean Chrétien"), and Pearson.

On 15 January 1964, Pearson became the first Canadian prime minister to make an official [state visit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_visit "State visit") to France.

In 1967, French president [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle "Charles de Gaulle") made a visit to [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec "Quebec"). A staunch advocate of [Quebec separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_sovereignty_movement "Quebec sovereignty movement"), de Gaulle went so far as to say that his procession in [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal") reminded him of his return to Paris after it was freed from the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism") during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"). President de Gaulle also gave his ["Vive le Québec libre" speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vive_le_Québec_libre_speech "Vive le Québec libre speech") during the visit. Given Canada's efforts in aiding France during both world wars, Pearson was enraged. He rebuked de Gaulle in a speech the following day, remarking that "Canadians do not need to be liberated", and made it clear that de Gaulle was no longer welcome in Canada.

Pearson signed the [Canada–United States Automotive Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada–United_States_Automotive_Products_Agreement "Canada–United States Automotive Products Agreement") (or Auto Pact) in January 1965, and unemployment fell to its lowest rate in over a decade.

While in office, Pearson declined U.S. requests to send Canadian combat troops into the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"). Pearson spoke at [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University "Temple University") in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia") on 2 April 1965 and voiced his support for a pause in the [American bombing of North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rolling_Thunder "Operation Rolling Thunder"), so that a diplomatic solution to the crisis might unfold. To president [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson"), this criticism of [American foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_United_States "Foreign policy of the United States") on American soil was intolerable. Before Pearson had finished his speech, he was invited to [Camp David, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_David,_Maryland "Camp David, Maryland"), to meet with Johnson the next day. Johnson, who was notorious for his personal touch in politics, reportedly grabbed Pearson by the lapels and shouted, "You pissed on my rug!" Text of his Philadelphia speech, however, showed that Pearson in fact supported president Johnson's policy in Vietnam, even stating "The government and great majority of people of my country have supported wholeheartedly the US peacekeeping and peacemaking policies in Vietnam."

After this incident, Johnson and Pearson did have further contacts, including two more meetings together, both times in Canada. Canada's exported raw materials and resources helped fuel and sustain American efforts in the Vietnam War.

### Military

Pearson's government endured significant controversy in Canada's military services throughout the mid-1960s, following the tabling of the [White Paper on Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Paper_on_Defence "White Paper on Defence") in March 1964. This document laid out a plan to merge the [Royal Canadian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Navy "Royal Canadian Navy"), the [Royal Canadian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Force "Royal Canadian Air Force"), and the [Canadian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Army "Canadian Army") to form a single service called the [Canadian Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces "Canadian Forces"). Military unification took effect on 1 February 1968, when *The Canadian Forces Reorganization Act* received royal assent.

### Retirement

After his 14 December 1967 announcement that he was retiring from politics, [a leadership convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Liberal_Party_of_Canada_leadership_election "1968 Liberal Party of Canada leadership election") was held. Pearson's successor was [Pierre Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Trudeau "Pierre Trudeau"), whom Pearson had recruited and made [justice minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Justice_(Canada) "Minister of Justice (Canada)") in his [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Canada "Cabinet of Canada"). Two other cabinet ministers Pearson had recruited, [John Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Turner "John Turner") and [Jean Chrétien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Chrétien "Jean Chrétien"), served as prime ministers following Trudeau's retirement.

## After politics

From 1968 to 1969, Pearson served as chairman of the [Pearson Commission on International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_Commission_on_International_Development "Pearson Commission on International Development"), which was sponsored by the [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank"). Following his retirement, he lectured at [Carleton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_University "Carleton University") in Ottawa while writing his memoirs. From 1970 to 1972, he was the first chairman of the board of governors of the [International Development Research Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Development_Research_Centre "International Development Research Centre"). From 1969 until his death in 1972, he was chancellor of Carleton University.

Pearson had planned to write a three-volume set of memoirs with the title "*Mike: The Memoirs of the Rt. Hon. Lester B. Pearson*". The first volume was published in 1972. The other two volumes were published posthumously in 1973 and 1975, but are tainted with the biases of their [ghostwriters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghostwriters "Ghostwriters").

### Illness and death

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Lester_B_Pearson_Gravestone_WakefieldQC.JPG/250px-Lester_B_Pearson_Gravestone_WakefieldQC.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lester_B_Pearson_Gravestone_WakefieldQC.JPG)

Pearson's gravestone in [Wakefield, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield,_Quebec "Wakefield, Quebec")

In 1970, Pearson underwent surgery to have his right eye removed to remove a tumour in that area.

In November 1972, it was reported that he was admitted to the hospital for further unspecified treatment, but the prognosis was poor. He tried to write at this juncture the story of his prime ministerial career, but his condition, which was already precarious, deteriorated rapidly by Christmas Eve.

On 27 December 1972, it was announced that the cancer had spread to the liver and Pearson had lapsed into a coma. He died at 11:40 pm [ET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Time_Zone "Eastern Time Zone") on 27 December 1972 in his Ottawa home.

Pearson is buried at [Maclaren Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maclaren_Cemetery "Maclaren Cemetery") in [Wakefield, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield,_Quebec "Wakefield, Quebec"), next to his close External Affairs colleagues [H. H. Wrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._H._Wrong "H. H. Wrong") and [Norman Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Robertson "Norman Robertson").

- Elected a Foreign Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1957.
- The [Canadian Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Press "Canadian Press") named Pearson "[Newsmaker of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Newsmaker_of_the_Year "Canadian Newsmaker of the Year")" nine times, a record he held until his successor, Pierre Trudeau, surpassed it in 2000. He was also only one of two prime ministers to have received the honour both before and when prime minister (the other being Brian Mulroney).
- Pearson was inducted into the [Canadian Peace Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Peace_Hall_of_Fame?action=edit&redlink=1 "Canadian Peace Hall of Fame") in 2000.
- The [Pearson Medal of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_Medal_of_Peace "Pearson Medal of Peace"), first awarded in 1979, is an award given out annually by the [United Nations Association in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Association_in_Canada "United Nations Association in Canada") to recognize an individual Canadian's "contribution to international service".
- A plaque, placed by the [Ontario Heritage Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Heritage_Trust "Ontario Heritage Trust"), is on the grounds of Newtonbrook United Church, the successor congregation to the one that owned the manse.
- In a survey by Canadian historians of the first 20 prime ministers through [Jean Chrétien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Chrétien "Jean Chrétien"), Pearson ranked No. 6.
- In a survey by Canadian historians of the Canadian prime ministers who served after World War II, Pearson was ranked first "by a landslide".

### Order of Canada Citation

Pearson was appointed a Companion of the [Order of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Canada "Order of Canada") on 28 June 1968. His citation reads:

> "Former Prime Minister of Canada. For his services to Canada at home and abroad."

### Educational and academic institutions

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/LPB_quote_on_Peacekeeping_Monument.jpg/250px-LPB_quote_on_Peacekeeping_Monument.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:LPB_quote_on_Peacekeeping_Monument.jpg)

Lester B. Pearson quote on the [Peacekeeping Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_Monument "Peacekeeping Monument")

- The [Lester B. Pearson United World College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson_United_World_College "Lester B. Pearson United World College"), opened in 1974 as the second [United World College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_World_College "United World College"), near [Victoria, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_British_Columbia "Victoria, British Columbia").
- The [Pearson Peacekeeping Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_Peacekeeping_Centre "Pearson Peacekeeping Centre"), established in 1994, is an independent not-for-profit institution providing research and training on all aspects of peace operations.
- The [Lester B. Pearson School Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson_School_Board "Lester B. Pearson School Board") is the largest [English-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-language "English-language") [school board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_board "School board") in Quebec. The majority of the schools of the Lester B. Pearson School Board are located on the western half of the island of Montreal, while a few of its schools located off the island.
- [Lester B. Pearson High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson_High_School_(disambiguation) "Lester B. Pearson High School (disambiguation)") lists five so-named schools, in [Burlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson_High_School_(Burlington) "Lester B. Pearson High School (Burlington)"), [Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson_High_School_(Calgary) "Lester B. Pearson High School (Calgary)"), [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson_High_School_(Montreal) "Lester B. Pearson High School (Montreal)"), [Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_English_catholic_schools_in_Ottawa "List of English catholic schools in Ottawa"), and [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson_Collegiate_Institute "Lester B. Pearson Collegiate Institute").
- There are Lester B. Pearson elementary schools in [Ajax, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajax,_Ontario "Ajax, Ontario"); [Aurora, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora,_Ontario "Aurora, Ontario"); [Brampton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brampton,_Ontario "Brampton, Ontario"); [London, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_Ontario "London, Ontario"); [Saskatoon, Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatoon,_Saskatchewan "Saskatoon, Saskatchewan"); [Waterloo, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterloo,_Ontario "Waterloo, Ontario") and [Wesleyville, Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyville,_Newfoundland_and_Labrador "Wesleyville, Newfoundland and Labrador").
- Mike's Place, the Graduate Student Pub at Carleton University was named in 1973 in honour of Lester B. Pearson with permission of his estate.
- The Lester B. Pearson International Student Scholarship is the most prestigious scholarship for international students at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto "University of Toronto"). It covers the tuition, books, incidental fees, and full residence support.

### Civic and civil infrastructure

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Tribute_to_Lester_Bowles_Pearson_%2850218610328%29.jpg/250px-Tribute_to_Lester_Bowles_Pearson_%2850218610328%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tribute_to_Lester_Bowles_Pearson_(50218610328).jpg)

Tribute plaque to Lester Bowles Pearson

- [Toronto Pearson International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Pearson_International_Airport "Toronto Pearson International Airport"), first opened in 1939 and renamed with its current name in 1984, is [Canada's busiest airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada's_busiest_airport "Canada's busiest airport").
- The [Lester B. Pearson Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson_Building "Lester B. Pearson Building"), completed in 1973, is the headquarters for the [Department of Foreign Affairs and International Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Foreign_Affairs_and_International_Trade "Department of Foreign Affairs and International Trade"), a tribute to his service as [external affairs minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_External_Affairs_(Canada) "Secretary of State for External Affairs (Canada)").
- Lester B. Pearson Civic Centre in [Elliot Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_Lake "Elliot Lake"), Ontario was heavily damaged in February 2019.
- Lester B. Pearson Garden for Peace and Understanding, E.J. Pratt Library in the University of Toronto, completed in 2004
- Lester B. Pearson Place, completed in 2006, is a four-storey affordable housing building in Newtonbrook, Toronto, near his place of birth, and adjacent to Newtonbrook United Church.
- Lester B. Pearson Park in St. Catharines, Ontario.
- Pearson Avenue is located near [Highway 407](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Highway_407 "Ontario Highway 407") and [Yonge Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonge_Street "Yonge Street") in [Richmond Hill, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Hill,_Ontario "Richmond Hill, Ontario"), Canada; less than five miles from his place of birth.
- Pearson Way is an arterial access road located in a new subdivision in [Milton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton,_Ontario "Milton, Ontario"); many ex-prime ministers are being honoured in this growing community, including prime ministers [Pierre Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Trudeau "Pierre Trudeau") and [Wilfrid Laurier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfrid_Laurier "Wilfrid Laurier").
- [Pearson Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_Plaza "Pearson Plaza"), a mall being developed in [Elliot Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_Lake "Elliot Lake") to replace the [Algo Centre Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algo_Centre_Mall "Algo Centre Mall").
- Pearson Park, a playground built in 2013 in [Wesleyville, Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyville,_Newfoundland_and_Labrador "Wesleyville, Newfoundland and Labrador").

### Sports

- The award for the best [National Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hockey_League "National Hockey League") player as voted by members of the [National Hockey League Players' Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hockey_League_Players'_Association "National Hockey League Players' Association") (NHLPA) was known as the *Lester B. Pearson Award* from its inception in 1971 to 2010, when its name was changed to the *[Ted Lindsay Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Lindsay_Award "Ted Lindsay Award")* to honour [one of the union's pioneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Lindsay "Ted Lindsay").
- Pearson was inducted into the Sports Hall of Fame at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto "University of Toronto") in 1987.
- Pearson was inducted into the [Canadian Baseball Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Baseball_Hall_of_Fame "Canadian Baseball Hall of Fame") in 1983.
- The [Pearson Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_Cup "Pearson Cup") was a baseball competition between the [Toronto Blue Jays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Blue_Jays "Toronto Blue Jays") and [Montreal Expos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Expos "Montreal Expos"). Pearson also served as Expo's Honorary Club President from 1969 to 1972.
