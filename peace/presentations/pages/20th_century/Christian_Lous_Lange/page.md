**Christian Lous Lange** (17 September 1869 – 11 December 1938) was a Norwegian historian, teacher, and [political scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science "Political science"). He was one of the world's foremost exponents of the theory and practice of [internationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internationalism_(politics) "Internationalism (politics)").

## Early life and education

He was born in [Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger "Stavanger"); his father was a military engineer, and he was the grandson of the historian [Christian C. A. Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_C._A._Lange "Christian C. A. Lange"). He graduated from secondary school in 1887 and proceeded to travel and study history, [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language"), and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language") at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo "University of Oslo"), from which he received the [cand. philol.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cand._philol. "Cand. philol.") degree in 1893. He taught at secondary schools for many years and eventually returned to the University of Oslo to receive a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate "Doctorate").

## Career

In 1899, he had his first official role in the internationalist movement when he was appointed secretary of the committee organizing an [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo "Oslo") conference of the [Inter-Parliamentary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Parliamentary_Union "Inter-Parliamentary Union"), an organization dedicated to fostering discussion between parliamentarians of different countries. The next year, he was made secretary of the [Nobel Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Committee "Norwegian Nobel Committee") of the [Norwegian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Parliament "Norwegian Parliament") and, although he gave up the position in 1909, he would play a key role in the early days of the [Norwegian Nobel Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Institute "Norwegian Nobel Institute"). During this time, he was the Norwegian technical delegate to the [Second Hague Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Hague_Peace_Conference "Second Hague Peace Conference").

Lange rejoined the Inter-Parliamentary Union in 1909 when he was appointed secretary general of the organization, a position he would retain until 1933. As secretary general, he oversaw the Union's move to [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels "Brussels"), sustained it in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo "Oslo") through the challenging times of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), then moved it again to [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva "Geneva") after the war. He remained active in numerous other organizations as well, acting as Norway's delegate or alternate delegate to the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") from its founding until 1938, writing reports and acting as a correspondent for the [Carnegie Endowment for International Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Endowment_for_International_Peace "Carnegie Endowment for International Peace"), and participating in the Dutch-sponsored [Central Organisation for Durable Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Organisation_for_Durable_Peace "Central Organisation for Durable Peace"), among other activities. He was often called upon for his expertise in arbitration and arms control, in particular.

His intellectual support of internationalism was demonstrated by the first of a three-volume historical treatise (*Histoire de l'internationalisme*). The work, published in 1919, was said to contribute to the ideological preparation for the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations"). For this and all his other activities, Lange shared the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") with [Hjalmar Branting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hjalmar_Branting "Hjalmar Branting") in 1921.

## Personal life and death

Lange died at the age of 69, one day after the 17th anniversary of his receipt of the Nobel Prize He was the father of [Carl Viggo Manthey Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Viggo_Manthey_Lange "Carl Viggo Manthey Lange"), [Halvard Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halvard_Lange "Halvard Lange") and [Christian August Manthey Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Lange "August Lange").

His daughter Thora was a history and language teacher from 1938 at [Oslo Cathedral School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Cathedral_School "Oslo Cathedral School"). During the [occupation of Norway by Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Norway_by_Nazi_Germany "Occupation of Norway by Nazi Germany"), she joined the [resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_resistance_movement "Norwegian resistance movement") while her husband went into exile. She was imprisoned at [Grini concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grini_concentration_camp "Grini concentration camp") in September 1943, but fell ill and died at [Ullevål Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ullevål_University_Hospital "Ullevål University Hospital") in December.

Lange was a member of the [Norwegian Association for Women's Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Association_for_Women's_Rights "Norwegian Association for Women's Rights"). Lange's [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") has been on long-term loan from his family to the [Nobel Peace Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Center "Nobel Peace Center") in Oslo since 2005. It is displayed in the centre's Medal Chamber.
