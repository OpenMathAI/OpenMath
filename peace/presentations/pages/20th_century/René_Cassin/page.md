**René Samuel Cassin** (5 October 1887 – 20 February 1976) was a French [jurist](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/jurist "wikt:jurist") known for co-authoring the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights "Universal Declaration of Human Rights") and receiving the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize").

Born in [Bayonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayonne "Bayonne"), Cassin served as a soldier in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War") during which he was seriously wounded. He was of Portuguese-Jewish descent.

On 24 June 1940, during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"), Cassin heeded General [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle "Charles de Gaulle")'s [radio appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal_of_18_June "Appeal of 18 June") and joined him in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London"). Cassin used his legal expertise to help de Gaulle's [Free French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French "Free French").

Between 1944 and 1959, Cassin was a member of the [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_(France) "Council of State (France)").

Seconded to the [UN Commission on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Commission_on_Human_Rights "UN Commission on Human Rights") after the war, he was a major contributor to the 1948 [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights "Universal Declaration of Human Rights"). For that work, he received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Nobel_Peace_Prize "1968 Nobel Peace Prize"). The same year, he was awarded one of the [UN General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_General_Assembly "UN General Assembly")'s [Human Rights Prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Prize_in_the_Field_of_Human_Rights "United Nations Prize in the Field of Human Rights").

## Early life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/ReneCassin.JPG/250px-ReneCassin.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ReneCassin.JPG)

Memorial to Cassin in [Forbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbach "Forbach"), France

Cassin was born in [Bayonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayonne "Bayonne") on 5 October 1887, to a [Sephardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi "Sephardi") [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family. He grew up in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice "Nice"), where he attended the [Lycée Masséna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Masséna?action=edit&redlink=1 "Lycée Masséna"), and graduated with a bachelor's degree at 17. At the [University of Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Provence "University of Provence") he studied political economics, constitutional history, and Roman law and was awarded distinctions in law, a university degree with distinction and the first prize in the competitive examinations in the faculty of law. He was an invited speaker at international peace conferences. In 1914 in Paris, he was awarded his doctorate in juridical science, economics and politics.

## First World War

Cassin served in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War") in 1916 at the [Battle of the Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Meuse "Battle of the Meuse"). In one operation, he led the attack on enemy positions and was gravely injured in the arm, side and stomach by machine gunfire. A medic saved his life, but he received surgical treatment only ten days later at [Antibes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibes "Antibes"). He was awarded the *[Croix de Guerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croix_de_Guerre "Croix de Guerre")* for his actions but was too seriously injured to return to active duty, and he was mustered out as a war invalid.

## Interwar period

He helped to found the Union fédérale, a leftist pacifist organization for veterans.

Cassin also headed many [non-governmental organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organizations "Non-governmental organizations") (NGO) and founded the French Federation of Disabled War Veterans in 1918 and served until 1940 as its president and then as its honorary president.

In 1920, Cassin was appointed professor of law at Lille and in 1929 at Paris, where he continued to teach until 1960. In addition, he taught at the Academy of International Law of The Hague, and at the [Geneva Graduate Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Graduate_Institute "Geneva Graduate Institute"), among other places.

As a French delegate to the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") from 1924 to 1938, Cassin pressed for progress on disarmament and for developing institutions to aid the resolution of international conflicts.

## Second World War

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Comit%C3%A9_national_fran%C3%A7ais.jpg/330px-Comit%C3%A9_national_fran%C3%A7ais.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Comité_national_français.jpg)

René Cassin with the [French National Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Committee "French National Committee") in London.

Refusing the armistice, Cassin embarked on a British ship, the *SS Ettrick*, in [Saint-Jean-de-Luz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Jean-de-Luz "Saint-Jean-de-Luz") on 24 June 1940, and joined General [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle "Charles de Gaulle") in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") to help him continue the war against Germany. Cassin was, therefore, one of the first to join de Gaulle.
De Gaulle needed legal help to draft the statutes of Free France and so Cassin's arrival in London was very welcome.

René Cassin did not speak English but already knew leading academics and political figures like British Foreign Minister [Anthony Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Eden "Anthony Eden").

In April 1941, Cassin made a radio broadcast from London by addressing himself especially to [French Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Jews "French Jews") from a secular viewpoint and reminding them of the full and equal protection that France had always offered to Jews since the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution"). He exhorted them to help pay back that debt by joining the forces of [Free France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_France "Free France"). In May, [Vichy France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France "Vichy France") stripped Cassin of his French citizenship and in 1942 sentenced him to death in absentia.

## Later life and career

After the war, Cassin was assigned to the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") to help draft the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights "Universal Declaration of Human Rights"). Working from a list of rights elaborated by the Canadian scholar and professor of law [John Humphrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Peters_Humphrey "John Peters Humphrey"), Cassin produced a revised draft and expanded the text.

He served on the UN [Human Rights Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Commission_on_Human_Rights "United Nations Commission on Human Rights") and the [Hague Court of Arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Court_of_Arbitration "Permanent Court of Arbitration").

He was also a member (1959–1965) and president (1965–1968) of the [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights "European Court of Human Rights"). The court building is now on *Allée René Cassin*, in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg "Strasbourg").

In 1945, General de Gaulle suggested that Cassin, having done so much for the French people, also do something to help the Jewish people. Cassin became the president of the French-Jewish [Alliance Israelite Universelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Israélite_Universelle "Alliance Israélite Universelle") (AIU) which had been dedicated primarily to educating [Sephardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi "Sephardi") Jews living in the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") according to a modern French curriculum. As president of the AIU, Cassin worked with the [American Jewish Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jewish_Committee "American Jewish Committee") and the [Anglo-Jewish Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Jewish_Association "Anglo-Jewish Association") to found the Consultative Council of Jewish Organisations, a network dedicated to building support for Cassin's platform of human rights from a Jewish perspective while the UN human rights system was in its early stages of development.

In 1947, Cassin created the [French Institute of Administrative Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Institute_of_Administrative_Sciences "French Institute of Administrative Sciences") (IFSA). He was the first president of the association, which organized many conferences to help to develop the French doctrine in [administrative law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_law "Administrative law").

On 10 November 1950, he was photographed at a UN radio, alongside [Karim Azkoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Azkoul "Karim Azkoul"), Georges Day and Herald CL Roy, participating in a roundtable discussion for the use of French-speaking countries. That is perhaps all the more interesting because Azkoul and Cassin differed so strongly in their perspectives concerning the politics of [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism "Zionism").

Cassin died in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") in 1976 and was initially interred at the [Montparnasse Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montparnasse_Cemetery "Montparnasse Cemetery") in Paris. In 1987, his remains were exhumed and enshrined in the [crypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypt "Crypt") of the [Pantheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthéon,_Paris "Panthéon, Paris") in Paris.

## Legacy

In 2001, the [CCJO René Cassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CCJO_René_Cassin "CCJO René Cassin") was founded in Cassin's memory to promote [universal human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_human_rights "Universal human rights") from a Jewish perspective.

The René Cassin Medal is awarded by the CCJO to those who have made an outstanding global contribution to human rights. As the head of the Alliance Israélite in France, Cassin had pursued civil rights for the Jews and was an active Zionist.

A high school in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem") is named after him.

In 2003, the [Basque government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_government "Basque government") created the René Cassin Award "with the goal of publicly acknowledging and rewarding individuals or collectives that, through their personal or professional path, showed a strong commitment to the promotion, defence and divulgation of [Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights "Human Rights")". The award is given on 10 December, which is [International Human Rights Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Human_Rights_Day "International Human Rights Day").

The law campus of [Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_1_Panthéon-Sorbonne_University "Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne University") is named after him.
