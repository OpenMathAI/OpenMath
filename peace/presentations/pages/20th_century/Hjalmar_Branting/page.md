**Karl Hjalmar Branting** (Swedish pronunciation: [[ˈjǎlmar ˈbrânːtɪŋ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Swedish "Help:IPA/Swedish") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sv-Hjalmar_Branting.ogg "File:Sv-Hjalmar Branting.ogg"); 23 November 1860 – 24 February 1925) was a Swedish statesman and diplomat who served as [Prime Minister of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Sweden "Prime Minister of Sweden") on three occasions from 1920 to 1925. From 1907 until his death in 1925, Branting led the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Social_Democratic_Party "Swedish Social Democratic Party") (SAP), playing a major role in advocating [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage "Universal suffrage"), an [eight-hour workday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-hour_day_movement "Eight-hour day movement"), and other [labor rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_rights "Labor rights"). He was also instrumental in foreign policy, including his support for the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations"). In 1921, Branting shared the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") with the Norwegian secretary-general of the [Inter-Parliamentary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Parliamentary_Union "Inter-Parliamentary Union"), [Christian Lous Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Lous_Lange "Christian Lous Lange").

## Biography

Born to the professor [Lars Gabriel Branting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Gabriel_Branting?action=edit&redlink=1 "Lars Gabriel Branting") and the noblewoman and pianist [Emma af Georgii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_af_Georgii?action=edit&redlink=1 "Emma af Georgii"), Branting was educated in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm") and at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University"). He developed a scientific background in mathematical astronomy and was an assistant at the [Stockholm Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Observatory "Stockholm Observatory"), but gave up his devotion to scientific work to become a journalist in 1884 and began editing the newspapers *Tiden* and *Social-Demokraten*. The latter was official media outlet of the [Swedish Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Social_Democratic_Party "Swedish Social Democratic Party"). His decision to publish an article by the more radical socialist [Axel Danielsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axel_Danielsson "Axel Danielsson"), a piece denounced by opponents as insulting to religious sensitivities, resulted in political convictions for [blasphemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasphemy "Blasphemy") and imprisonment for both men. Branting was imprisoned for three months in 1888.

Together with [August Palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Palm "August Palm"), Branting was one of the main organizers of the Swedish Social Democratic Party in 1889. He was its first [Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riksdag "Riksdag") from 1896 and for six years the only one.

In the early years of the 20th century, Branting led the Social Democrats in opposing a war to force [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") to remain [in a personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_between_Sweden_and_Norway "Union between Sweden and Norway") with Sweden. When the [crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_in_1905 "Norway in 1905") came in 1905, he coined the slogan "Hands off Norway, King!" The Social Democrats organized resistance to a call-up of reserves and made preparations for a general strike against a war; historians now acclaim this as a major factor in Norway's peaceful independence. In 1908 Branting established a monthly theoretical political journal entitled *[Tiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiden_(magazine) "Tiden (magazine)")* which is still in publication.

Branting accepted [Eduard Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Bernstein "Eduard Bernstein")'s [revision of Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revisionism_(Marxism) "Revisionism (Marxism)") and became a [reformist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformism "Reformism") [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist "Socialist"), advocating a peaceful transition from [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism "Capitalism") towards [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism "Socialism"). He believed that if workers were given the vote through [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage "Universal suffrage"), socialist legislation could be brought through the [Riksdag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riksdag "Riksdag"). Branting supported the [February Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_Revolution "February Revolution") in Russia in 1917. He supported the moderate [Mensheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menshevik "Menshevik") against Lenin's more extreme [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks "Bolsheviks") and defended the government of [Alexander Kerensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Kerensky "Alexander Kerensky"), whom he even personally visited in [Petrograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrograd "Petrograd"). When the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution "October Revolution") broke out in the winter of that year, Branting condemned the [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik "Bolshevik") seizure of power. 1917 saw a split in the Swedish Social Democratic Party on this question, and the [youth league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Left_(Sweden) "Young Left (Sweden)") and the revolutionary sections of the party broke away and formed the [Social Democratic Left Party of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Party_(Sweden) "Left Party (Sweden)"), initially headed by [Zeth Höglund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeth_Höglund "Zeth Höglund"), which soon reorganized as the [Swedish Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Party_(Sweden) "Left Party (Sweden)"). Höglund later returned to the Social Democratic Party after Branting's death, and wrote a two-volume biography of Branting.

As prime minister, Branting brought Sweden into the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") and was personally active as a delegate within it. When the question of whether [Åland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Åland "Åland") should be handed over to Sweden after the independence of [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland "Finland") from [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic "Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic") was brought up, he let the League decide upon the issue; the islands became an [autonomous region of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Åland "Åland"). He was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1921 for his work in the League of Nations, sharing the prize with the Norwegian [Christian Lous Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Lous_Lange "Christian Lous Lange"). Branting was a supporter of false claims that French colonial troops [were committing mass rapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Horror_on_the_Rhine "Black Horror on the Rhine") during the [occupation of the Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Rhineland "Occupation of the Rhineland"). On 10 May 1920, he declared that as a white man he was outraged that the French would deploy African troops in the Rhineland.

Branting died in Stockholm at the age of 64 on 24 February 1925, four months after being sworn in for a third term as prime minister following the SAP's victory in the [1924 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Swedish_general_election "1924 Swedish general election"). He was succeeded as prime minister by [Rickard Sandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickard_Sandler "Rickard Sandler"); [Per Albin Hansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Albin_Hansson "Per Albin Hansson") became party chairman, later serving as prime minister from 1932 to 1946.

## Monuments and memorials

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Hjalmar_Branting_Denkmal_Stockholm_DSC_0005w.jpg/250px-Hjalmar_Branting_Denkmal_Stockholm_DSC_0005w.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hjalmar_Branting_Denkmal_Stockholm_DSC_0005w.jpg)

Monument to Branting at Stockholm

Branting is commemorated by the [Branting Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branting_Monument "Branting Monument") in Stockholm. Additionally in [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg "Gothenburg"), there is a tram and bus interchange named after Branting ([Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_language "Swedish language"): Hjalmar Brantingsplatsen). Stockholms Plads (Stockholm Square) in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen "Copenhagen") was renamed [Hjalmar Brantings Plads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hjalmar_Brantings_Plads "Hjalmar Brantings Plads") in 1925.
