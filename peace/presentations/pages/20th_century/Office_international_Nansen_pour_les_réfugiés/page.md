The **Nansen International Office for Refugees** (*[French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language"): *Office International Nansen pour les Réfugiés**) was an organization established in 1930 by the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") and named after [Fridtjof Nansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fridtjof_Nansen "Fridtjof Nansen"), soon after his death, which was internationally in charge of [refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee "Refugee") from war areas between 1930 and 1939. It is noted for developing the [Nansen passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nansen_passport "Nansen passport") which allowed [stateless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statelessness "Statelessness") people to travel between countries. It received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1938.

The *Office International Nansen pour les Réfugiés* was established in 1930 by the League of Nations, shortly after the death of its name patron Fridtjof Nansen to continue his successful work in international aid for refugee. It thus continued the organization in Geneva, Switzerland, founded by Nansen in 1921. The League also provided the administrative expenses for the Nansen Office though only for fees charged for the Nansen passport since its revenues for welfare and relief were obtained from private contributions.

The organization was to supply material and political support to refugees. For refugees from the [Third Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Reich "Third Reich") or from the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War")-torn [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_State "Spanish State"), the Nansen aid was not applicable, though many countries refused to accept the refugees.

In spite of such problems, the Nobel Peace Prize was awarded to the organization in 1938 for its work, but due to its dissolution shortly afterwards, the prize money was received by a newly founded refugee organization of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations"). The office was dissolved at the same time as the League dissolved the High Commission (established in 1933) because of the problems surrounding German refugees after the dissolution of the Third Reich.

In 1938, this Office was replaced by the appointment of a High Commissioner for Refugees, a precursor to the [United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_High_Commissioner_for_Refugees "United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees").

## Accomplishments

The Nansen International Office for Refugees was responsible for the adoption of the 1933 Refugee Convention, which was a modest charter of human rights, by fourteen countries. The Nansen Office was instrumental in finding shelter in specially constructed houses in villages in [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria") and [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon") for 40,000 [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians "Armenians"), after the resettlement of another 10,000 in [Yerevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerevan "Yerevan"). The Office also successfully resettled [Saar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Saar_status_referendum "1935 Saar status referendum") refugees in [Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay "Paraguay") after its reintegration into Germany in 1935.

## Nobel Peace Prize

The Nansen International Office for Refugees was awarded the 1938 [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") for its efforts to establish the Nansen passports.
