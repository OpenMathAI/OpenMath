**Fredrik Bajer** (21 April 1837 – 22 January 1922) was a [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark "Denmark") writer, teacher, and [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifism "Pacifism") [politician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politician "Politician") who received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1908 together with [Klas Pontus Arnoldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klas_Pontus_Arnoldson "Klas Pontus Arnoldson").

## Life

He was son of a clergyman born in Næstved in 1837. Bajer served as an officer in the Danish army, fighting in the [1864 war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Schleswig_War "Second Schleswig War") against [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia "Prussia") and [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria "Austria") where he was promoted to the rank of first lieutenant. He was discharged in 1865, and moved to Copenhagen where he became a teacher, translator and writer.

He entered the [Danish Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigsdag "Rigsdag") in 1872 as a member of [Folketinget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Denmark "Parliament of Denmark") and held a seat there for the following 23 years. As a member of parliament, he worked for the use of international arbitration to solve conflicts among nations, and it Bajer's efforts that caused foreign relations became part of the work of the Danish Parliament and that Denmark participated in the [Inter-Parliamentary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Parliamentary_Union "Inter-Parliamentary Union") from the beginning and earned a distinguished position among its members.

He supported the [early women's suffrage organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kvindevalgretsforeningen "Kvindevalgretsforeningen") and many peace organizations, including the [International Peace Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Peace_Bureau "International Peace Bureau"), both inside Denmark and Europe-wide, and he helped guide the passage of a bill to reach arbitration agreements with [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden") and [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway").
