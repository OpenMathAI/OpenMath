**Louis Renault** (21 May 1843 – 8 February 1918) was a French jurist and educator, and the co-winner in 1907 (with [Ernesto Teodoro Moneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Teodoro_Moneta "Ernesto Teodoro Moneta")) of the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize").

Renault was born at [Autun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autun "Autun"). From 1868 to 1873, Renault was professor of [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_law#Roman_law_today "Roman law") and [commercial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_law "Commercial law") at the [University of Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dijon "University of Dijon"). From 1873 until his death, he was professor in the faculty of law at the [Paris Institute of Political Studies (Sciences Po)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences_Po "Sciences Po") and the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris"), where in 1881 he became professor of international law. In 1890, he was appointed [jurisconsult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisconsult "Jurisconsult") of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, a post created for him in which he scrutinized French foreign policy in the light of international law. He served at numerous conferences in this capacity, notably at the two [Hague Conventions (1899 and 1907)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Conventions_(1899_and_1907) "Hague Conventions (1899 and 1907)") and the [London Naval Conference (1908–1909)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Naval_Conference_(1908–1909) "London Naval Conference (1908–1909)").

Renault was prominent as an arbitrator, his more famous cases including the Japanese House Tax case of 1905, the Casa Blanca Case of 1909, the [Sarvarkar Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinayak_Damodar_Savarkar#Case_before_the_Permanent_Court_of_Arbitration "Vinayak Damodar Savarkar") of 1911, the [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage "Carthage") case of 1913, and the [Manouba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manouba "Manouba") case of 1913. Among his writings are articles and monographs on the specialized topics of international law. Together with his friend and colleague C. Lyon-Caen, he produced several works on commercial law, including a [compendium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compendium "Compendium") in two volumes, a treatise in eight volumes, and a manual that ran to many editions.

In 1879, Renault published his Introduction to the Study of International Law and in 1917 First Violations of International Law by Germany, concerning the [invasion of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_(World_War_I)#1914—German_invasion_of_France_and_Belgium "Western Front (World War I)") and of [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg "Luxembourg") in breach of [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany")'s treaty obligations.
