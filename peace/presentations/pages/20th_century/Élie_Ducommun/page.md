**Élie Ducommun** (19 February 1833, [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva "Geneva") – 7 December 1906, [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern "Bern")) was a Swiss [peace activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_activist "Peace activist"). He was a Nobel laureate, awarded the 1902 [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"), which he shared with [Charles Albert Gobat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Albert_Gobat "Charles Albert Gobat").

Born in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva "Geneva"), he worked as a tutor, language teacher, journalist and a translator for the Swiss federal Chancellery (1869–1873).

In 1867 he helped to found the Ligue de la paix et de la liberté ([League of Peace and Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Peace_and_Freedom "League of Peace and Freedom")), though he continued working at other positions, including secretary for the Jura-Simplon Steel Company from 1873 to 1891. That year, he was appointed director of the newly formed [International Peace Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Peace_Bureau "International Peace Bureau"), the first non-governmental international peace organization, based in [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern "Bern"). He refused to accept a salary for the position, stating that he wished to serve in this capacity solely for reasons of idealism. From 1895 he was made the director of *[Correspondance bi-mensuelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correspondance_bi-mensuelle "Correspondance bi-mensuelle")*, journal of the International Peace Office.

His keen organizational skills ensured the group's success. He was awarded in the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1902, and served as director of the organization until his death in 1906.
