**Léon Victor Auguste Bourgeois** (French: [[leɔ̃ buʁʒwa]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 21 May 1851 – 29 September 1925) was a French statesman. His ideas influenced the [Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Party_(France) "Radical Party (France)") regarding a wide range of issues.

He promoted [progressive taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_tax "Progressive tax") such as progressive [income taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_taxes "Income taxes") and [social insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_insurance "Social insurance") schemes, along with [economic equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_(economics) "Equality (economics)"), expanded [educational opportunities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_education "Right to education"), and [cooperative solidarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporatism#corporate_solidarism "Corporatism"). In foreign policy, he called for a strong League of Nations, and the maintenance of peace through [compulsory arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_arbitration "Compulsory arbitration"), controlled [disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disarmament "Disarmament"), [economic sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_sanctions "Economic sanctions"), and perhaps an international military force.

## Biography

Bourgeois was born in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") in to a modest Republican family of a watchmaker of [Burgundian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgundians "Burgundians") descent, and was trained in law, graduating from his university in 1874. After holding a subordinate office (1876) in the department of public works, he became successively prefect of the [Tarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarn_(department) "Tarn (department)") (1882) and the [Haute-Garonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Garonne "Haute-Garonne") (1885), and then returned to Paris to enter the Ministry of the Interior.

He became Prefect of Police in November 1887 at the critical moment of [Jules Grévy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Grévy "Jules Grévy")'s resignation from the presidency. In the following year, he entered the Chamber, being elected deputy for the [Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marne_(department) "Marne (department)"), in opposition to [George Boulanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Boulanger "George Boulanger"), and joined the [Radical Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Left_(France) "Radical Left (France)"). He was undersecretary for Home Affairs in [Charles Floquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Floquet "Charles Floquet")'s ministry of 1888 and resigned with it in 1889, being then returned to the chamber for [Reims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reims "Reims"). In [Pierre Tirard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Tirard "Pierre Tirard")'s ministry, which succeeded, he was [Minister of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_the_Interior_(France) "Minister of the Interior (France)"), and subsequently, on 18 March 1890, [Minister of Public Instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Public_Instruction_(France) "Minister of Public Instruction (France)") in the cabinet of [Charles Louis de Saulces de Freycinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Louis_de_Saulces_de_Freycinet "Charles Louis de Saulces de Freycinet"), a post for which he had qualified himself by the attention he had given to educational matters. In this capacity, he was responsible for some important reforms in secondary education in 1890.

He retained his office in [Émile Loubet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émile_Loubet "Émile Loubet")'s cabinet in 1892, and was [Minister of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Justice_(France) "Minister of Justice (France)") under [Alexandre Ribot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Ribot "Alexandre Ribot") at the end of that year, when the [Panama scandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_scandals "Panama scandals") were making the office one of peculiar difficulty. He energetically pressed the Panama prosecution, so much so that he was accused of having put wrongful pressure on the wife of one of the defendants in order to procure evidence. To meet the charge, he resigned in March 1893 but again took office and retired only with the rest of the Freycinet ministry.

In November 1895, he formed his own cabinet, distinctively radical, which fell as the result of a constitutional crisis arising from the persistent refusal of the Senate to vote supply. He was an eminent Freemason and eight of his cabinet members were Freemasons.

Official portrait of Léon Bourgeois

The Bourgeois ministry seemed to think that popular opinion would enable them to override what they regarded as an unconstitutional action on the part of the upper house. However, the public was indifferent, and the Senate triumphed. The blow damaged Bourgeois's career as an *homme de gouvernement*. As Minister of Public Instruction in the [Brisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Brisson "Henri Brisson") cabinet of 1898, he organized courses for adults in primary education. After the short ministry, he represented his country with dignity and effect at the [Hague Peace Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Conventions_of_1899_and_1907 "Hague Conventions of 1899 and 1907"), and in 1903 was nominated a member of the permanent court of arbitration.

He held somewhat aloof from the political struggles of the [Waldeck-Rousseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Marie_René_Ernest_Waldeck-Rousseau "Pierre Marie René Ernest Waldeck-Rousseau") and [Combes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émile_Combes "Émile Combes") ministries, travelling considerably in foreign countries. In 1902 and 1903, he was elected president of the Chamber. In 1905, he replaced the [duc d'Audiffret-Pasquier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duc_d'Audiffret-Pasquier "Duc d'Audiffret-Pasquier") as senator for the department of [Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marne_(department) "Marne (department)"), and in May 1906, he became [Minister of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_(France) "Minister of Foreign Affairs (France)") in the Sarrien cabinet. He was responsible for the direction of French diplomacy in the conference at [Algeciras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeciras "Algeciras"). He was delegate to both [Hague Conferences held in 1899 and 1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Conventions_(1899_and_1907) "Hague Conventions (1899 and 1907)"). Bourgeois also became delegate to [Paris Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference,_1919 "Paris Peace Conference, 1919") and strongly supported the [Japanese Racial Equality Proposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_Equality_Proposal,_1919 "Racial Equality Proposal, 1919") as "an indisputable principle of justice".

Following [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), he became President of the Assembly of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") and won the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") for his work in 1920.

A social republican, Bourgeois sought a middle ground between socialism and capitalism which he termed "solidarism". He believed that the rich had a social debt to the poor which they should pay by the income tax, thus providing the state with the necessary revenue to finance social measures for those living in poverty. However, the Senate opposed his proposal, and opposition grew until his resignation as prime minister.

During his premiership, a law of December the 27th 1895 related to an existing pension fund was passed that gave workmen the right to claim repayments in case of judicial liquidation or failure.

## Support to the French National Museum of Natural History

Bourgeois is one of the founders of the [Friends of the Natural History Museum Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_the_Natural_History_Museum_Paris "Friends of the Natural History Museum Paris") society. He was the very first president in office from 1907 to 1922.
