**Auguste Marie François Beernaert** (26 July 1829 – 6 October 1912) was the [prime minister of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Belgium "Prime minister of Belgium") from October 1884 to March 1894, and the 1909 [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") laureate.

## Life

Born in [Ostend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostend "Ostend") in the [United Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands "United Kingdom of the Netherlands") 1829, he entered the Faculty of Law at the [Catholic University of Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Leuven_(1834–1968) "Catholic University of Leuven (1834–1968)") at age 17. He finished five years later with greatest distinction.

He was elected to the Chamber of Deputies in 1873, and became minister of public works under [Jules Malou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Malou "Jules Malou"), greatly improving the rail, canal and road systems. After his tenure as prime minister, he represented Belgium at the [Hague conventions of 1899 and 1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Conventions_(1899_and_1907) "Hague Conventions (1899 and 1907)"), presiding at the Hague Peace Conference of 1907. He was also co-winner (with [Paul d'Estournelles de Constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_d'Estournelles_de_Constant "Paul d'Estournelles de Constant")) of the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1909 for his work at the [Permanent Court of Arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Court_of_Arbitration "Permanent Court of Arbitration"). He was chosen as president of the panel established under the rules of that organization in the [Sarvarkar Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinayak_Damodar_Savarkar#Case_before_the_Permanent_Court_of_Arbitration "Vinayak Damodar Savarkar") in 1911. A year later, he died in [Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucerne "Lucerne"), Switzerland. A lawyer by profession, he served as minister of public works. He served as prime minister and [minister of finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_(Belgium) "Minister of Finance (Belgium)") from 1884 to 1894. He held the post of president of the international law of association from 1903 to 1905. He was Belgium's first representative to the Hague peace conferences in 1899 and 1907. In the year 1912 he was hospitalised in Lucerne, where he died of pneumonia.

## Achievements

He was the primary force behind proposals to unify international maritime law. A number of conventions dealing with collision and assistance at sea drawn up in 1910 were soon signed by many nations.
