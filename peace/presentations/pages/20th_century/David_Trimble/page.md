**William David Trimble, Baron Trimble** (15 October 1944 – 25 July 2022) was a Northern Irish statesman and politician who was the inaugural [First Minister of Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Northern_Ireland "First Minister of Northern Ireland") from 1998 to 2002 and leader of the [Ulster Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Unionist_Party "Ulster Unionist Party") (UUP) from 1995 to 2005. He was also [Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom) "Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)") (MP) for [Upper Bann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Bann_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Upper Bann (UK Parliament constituency)") from [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Upper_Bann_by-election "1990 Upper Bann by-election") to [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_United_Kingdom_general_election_in_Northern_Ireland "2005 United Kingdom general election in Northern Ireland") and [Member of the Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Legislative_Assembly_(Northern_Ireland) "Member of the Legislative Assembly (Northern Ireland)") (MLA) for [Upper Bann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Bann_(Assembly_constituency) "Upper Bann (Assembly constituency)") from 1998 to [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Northern_Ireland_Assembly_election "2007 Northern Ireland Assembly election").

Trimble began his career teaching law at [The Queen's University of Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Queen's_University_of_Belfast "The Queen's University of Belfast") in the 1970s, during which time he began to get involved with the paramilitary-linked [Vanguard Progressive Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguard_Progressive_Unionist_Party "Vanguard Progressive Unionist Party") (VPUP). He was elected to the [Northern Ireland Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Constitutional_Convention "Northern Ireland Constitutional Convention") in 1975 and joined the UUP in 1978 after the VPUP disbanded. Remaining at Queen's University, he continued his academic career until being elected as the MP for Upper Bann in 1990. In 1995 he was unexpectedly elected as the leader of the UUP. He was instrumental in the negotiations that led to the [Good Friday Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Friday_Agreement "Good Friday Agreement") in 1998 and (along with [John Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hume "John Hume")) won the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") that year for his efforts. He was later elected to become the first First Minister of Northern Ireland, although his tenure was turbulent and frequently interrupted by disagreements over the timetable for [Provisional Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army "Provisional Irish Republican Army") decommissioning.

Trimble resigned the leadership of the UUP soon after being defeated at the [2005 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_United_Kingdom_general_election "2005 United Kingdom general election"). In June 2006, he accepted a [life peerage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peer "Life peer") in the [House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords "House of Lords"), taking the title of *Baron Trimble*, *of [Lisnagarvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisnagarvey "Lisnagarvey") in the County of [Antrim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Antrim "County Antrim")*. He did not stand again for the Assembly, which finally reconvened in 2007, instead leaving the UUP to join the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)").

## Early life and education

William David Trimble was born in the Wellington Park Nursing Home, [Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfast "Belfast") on 15 October 1944. He was the son of William and Ivy Trimble, lower-middle class [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian "Presbyterian") who lived in [Bangor, County Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor,_County_Down "Bangor, County Down").

He attended [Bangor Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor_Grammar_School "Bangor Grammar School") from 1956 to 1963. He then studied at [Queen's University of Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_of_Belfast "Queen's University of Belfast") (QUB) from 1964 to 1968, where he was awarded the McKane Medal for Jurisprudence. He received a [first class honours degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Class_Honours_(degree) "First Class Honours (degree)") (the first at Queen's in three years), becoming a [Bachelor of Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Laws "Bachelor of Laws") (LL.B).

Trimble's paternal grandfather George was born in [Easkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easkey "Easkey"), [County Sligo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Sligo "County Sligo"), to parents William Trimble and Mary Burns.

## Early career

### Academic career

Trimble qualified as a [barrister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrister "Barrister") in 1969. He began that year as a Queen's University of Belfast lecturer, subsequently becoming Assistant Dean of the law faculty from 1973 to 1975, a senior lecturer in 1977 and head of Commercial and Property Law from 1981 to 1989. He resigned from the university in 1990 when he was elected to Parliament.

In 1983, as he sat in his office at the university, he heard gunshots which turned out to be those of the IRA killers of [Edgar Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Graham "Edgar Graham"), a friend and fellow law lecturer. He was asked to identify the body. In 1994 he was told by the [Royal Ulster Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Ulster_Constabulary "Royal Ulster Constabulary") that he had been targeted for assassination.

### Political career

Trimble became involved with the right-wing, paramilitary-linked [Vanguard Unionist Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguard_Unionist_Progressive_Party "Vanguard Unionist Progressive Party") (known as Vanguard) in the early 1970s. He ran unsuccessfully for the party in the [1973 Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Northern_Ireland_Assembly_election "1973 Northern Ireland Assembly election") for [North Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Down_(Assembly_constituency) "North Down (Assembly constituency)"), coming last. In 1974, he was a legal adviser to the [Ulster Workers' Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Workers'_Council "Ulster Workers' Council") during the successful [UWC strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Workers'_Council_strike "Ulster Workers' Council strike") against the [Sunningdale Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunningdale_Agreement "Sunningdale Agreement").

Trimble was elected to the [Northern Ireland Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Constitutional_Convention "Northern Ireland Constitutional Convention") in 1975 as a Vanguard member for [Belfast South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfast_South_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Belfast South (UK Parliament constituency)") and, for a time, served as the party's joint deputy leader, along with the [Ulster Defence Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Defence_Association "Ulster Defence Association")'s [Glenn Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Barr "Glenn Barr"). The party had been established by [Bill Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Craig_(Northern_Ireland_politician) "William Craig (Northern Ireland politician)") to oppose sharing power with Irish Nationalists and to prevent closer ties with the Republic of Ireland; however, Trimble was one of those to back Craig when the party split over Craig's proposal to allow voluntary power sharing with the SDLP.

Trimble joined the mainstream [Ulster Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Unionist_Party "Ulster Unionist Party") (UUP) in 1978 after Vanguard disbanded and was elected one of the four party secretaries. He served as vice chairman of the [Lagan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagan_Valley "Lagan Valley") Unionist Association from 1983 to 1985 and was named chairman in 1985. He served as chairman of the UUP Legal Committee from 1989 to 1995 and as honorary secretary of the Ulster Unionist Council in 1990–96.

Trimble was elected to Parliament with 58% of the vote in a [by-election in Upper Bann in 1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Upper_Bann_by-election "1990 Upper Bann by-election"). He was one of the few British politicians who urged support for the government of [Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina "Bosnia and Herzegovina") during the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina "War in Bosnia and Herzegovina") in the 1990s.

## Leader of the Ulster Unionist Party

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/President_Bill_Clinton_with_David_Trimble.jpg/250px-President_Bill_Clinton_with_David_Trimble.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:President_Bill_Clinton_with_David_Trimble.jpg)

Trimble with US president [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") in Belfast, 1995

On 8 September 1995, Trimble unexpectedly [won election as leader of the UUP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_1995_Ulster_Unionist_Party_leadership_election "September 1995 Ulster Unionist Party leadership election"), defeating the front-runner [John Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Taylor,_Baron_Kilclooney "John Taylor, Baron Kilclooney") and three other candidates.

Trimble's election as Leader came in the aftermath of his role in the [Drumcree conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drumcree_conflict "Drumcree conflict"), in which he led a controversial 1995 [Orange Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Institution "Orange Institution") Protestant march, amidst Nationalist protest, down the predominantly [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Ireland "Catholic Church in Ireland") [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_nationalism "Irish nationalism") Garvaghy Road in [Portadown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portadown "Portadown"), County Armagh. Trimble and [Democratic Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Unionist_Party "Democratic Unionist Party") (DUP) leader [Ian Paisley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Paisley "Ian Paisley") walked hand-in-hand as the march, banned since 1997, proceeded down the road. Many [Irish Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Catholics "Irish Catholics") viewed it as insensitive, while many Protestants felt that it was a sign that Trimble was defending them.

Shortly after the election, Trimble became the first UUP Leader in 30 years (since [Terence O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terence_O'Neill "Terence O'Neill")) to meet with the [Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach "Taoiseach") in Dublin. In 1997, he became the first unionist leader since the [partition of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Ireland "Partition of Ireland") in 1921 to agree to negotiate with [Sinn Féin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinn_Féin "Sinn Féin").

In the subsequent All-Party negotiations, Trimble led the UUP delegation and sat at the table with Sinn Féin, though in the eight months of the negotiations he never spoke directly to their leader, [Gerry Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Adams "Gerry Adams"). Trimble at first opposed the appointment of former US Senator [George Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_J._Mitchell "George J. Mitchell") as the chairman of the all-party talks, but eventually accepted him. The talks were successful, culminating in the [Good Friday Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Friday_Agreement "Good Friday Agreement") of 10 April 1998, which resulted in power-sharing with Nationalists. Trimble was subsequently seen as instrumental in getting his party to accept the accord. On 22 May 1998, [voters in Northern Ireland approved the agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Northern_Ireland_Good_Friday_Agreement_referendum "1998 Northern Ireland Good Friday Agreement referendum"), with 71 per cent in favour.

Trimble was appointed to the [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_(United_Kingdom) "Privy Council (United Kingdom)") in the [1998 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_New_Year_Honours "1998 New Year Honours").

## First Minister of Northern Ireland

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/David_Trimble_Washington_D.C.jpg/250px-David_Trimble_Washington_D.C.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:David_Trimble_Washington_D.C.jpg)

Trimble in Washington, D.C., 2001

Trimble [was elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Northern_Ireland_Assembly_election "1998 Northern Ireland Assembly election") on 25 June 1998 as a [Member of the Northern Ireland Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Northern_Ireland_Assembly "Member of the Northern Ireland Assembly") for Upper Bann. He was elected [First Minister of Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Northern_Ireland "First Minister of Northern Ireland") on 1 July 1998.

Arguments over the extent of [Provisional Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army "Provisional Irish Republican Army") decommissioning led to repeated disruptions during Trimble's tenure as First Minister. In particular:

- The office of First Minister was suspended from 11 February 2000 to 30 May 2000. During this time, Trimble attempted to reassure party members who were sceptical of the post-Good Friday Agreement institutions, saying, "Unionists won the war... The Agreement gave unionism the opportunity to take control of Northern Ireland's constitutional future and to take a major stake in the government of our country... Does anyone really think [Gerry Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Adams "Gerry Adams") wanted this?".
- Trimble resigned as First Minister on 1 July 2001 due to the continuing impasse arising from the IRA's refusal of his demands that it decommission its arms, as per the commitments all parties had signed up to in section 7 pt. 3 (page 25) of the 1998 Good Friday Agreement but he was re-elected on 5 November 2001
- The Assembly was suspended from 14 October 2002 until 2007 due to accusations of an IRA spy ring being operated there (the so-called [Stormontgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stormontgate "Stormontgate") Affair), which Trimble described as ten times worse than [Watergate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watergate "Watergate").

In 1998, [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair "Tony Blair") announced a new judicial inquiry, the [Bloody Sunday Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloody_Sunday_Inquiry "Bloody Sunday Inquiry"), into the killing of 13 unarmed civil rights marchers in Derry in 1972. A previous investigation, the [Widgery Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widgery_Tribunal "Widgery Tribunal"), into the same event had been discredited. During the debate in the House of Commons, Trimble was one of few dissenting voices. He said: "I am sorry to have to say to the Prime Minister that I think that the hope expressed by the Honourable Member for Foyle [[John Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hume "John Hume")] that this will be part of the healing process is likely to be misplaced. Opening old wounds like this is likely to do more harm than good. The basic facts of the situation are known and not open to dispute." Reporting in 2010, the [Saville Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saville_Inquiry "Saville Inquiry") confirmed that all of the 13 killings and 13 woundings were unjustified.

During a meeting with Blair in 2001, Trimble questioned the impartiality of judge [Sir Brian Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Kerr,_Baron_Kerr_of_Tonaghmore "Brian Kerr, Baron Kerr of Tonaghmore"), later chief justice of Northern Ireland and a [Law Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Lord "Law Lord") appointed to the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_Kingdom "Supreme Court of the United Kingdom"). This evidence and comments made about other public figures contradicts what Trimble's biographer considered a "lack of personal bigotry against Catholics".

## Peerage

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Lord_Trimble.jpg/250px-Lord_Trimble.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lord_Trimble.jpg)

Trimble in 2009

At [the general elections of 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_United_Kingdom_general_election_in_Northern_Ireland "2005 United Kingdom general election in Northern Ireland"), Trimble was defeated in Upper Bann by the DUP's [David Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Simpson_(Northern_Ireland_politician) "David Simpson (Northern Ireland politician)") in his bid for re-election to Parliament in Westminster. The Ulster Unionist Party retained only one seat in Parliament (out of 18 in Northern Ireland) after the 2005 general election, and Trimble resigned the party leadership on 7 May 2005.

On 11 April 2006, it was revealed that Trimble would take a seat in the [House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords "House of Lords") as a working [life peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peer "Life peer"). On 21 May 2006 it was announced that he had chosen the geographical designation Lisnagarvey, the original name for his adopted home town of [Lisburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisburn "Lisburn"). Subsequently, on 2 June 2006, he was created *Baron Trimble*, *of [Lisnagarvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisnagarvey "Lisnagarvey") in the [County of Antrim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Antrim "County Antrim")*. Eight months later, he confirmed that he would be standing down from the [Northern Ireland Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Assembly "Northern Ireland Assembly") at the next election.

Trimble announced on 17 April 2007 that he had decided to join the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK) "Conservative Party (UK)") in order to have greater influence in politics in the United Kingdom. At the same time, however, he stated that he did not intend to campaign against the Ulster Unionist Party and proposed the idea of a future alliance between the Conservatives and the Ulster Unionists, similar to that which had existed prior to 1974 and the fallout of the Sunningdale Agreement. This idea became reality with the formation of the [electoral alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_alliance "Electoral alliance") of [Ulster Conservatives and Unionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Conservatives_and_Unionists "Ulster Conservatives and Unionists") in late 2008. It was reported that if the Conservatives won the [2010 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_general_election "2010 United Kingdom general election"), Trimble would receive a "significant" ministerial role, possibly in the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_Kingdom "Cabinet of the United Kingdom"). In the end, however, Trimble was not offered any governmental or [front bench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Government_frontbench "British Government frontbench") position following the formation of the [Conservative–Liberal Democrat coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron–Clegg_coalition "Cameron–Clegg coalition").

In May 2010, Trimble joined the [Friends of Israel Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_Israel_Initiative "Friends of Israel Initiative"), a non-Jewish international project supporting [Israel's right to exist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel's_right_to_exist "Israel's right to exist"). The initiative, started by former prime minister of Spain [José María Aznar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_María_Aznar "José María Aznar"), also included former United States Ambassador to the United Nations [John R. Bolton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Bolton "John R. Bolton"), British historian [Andrew Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Roberts_(historian) "Andrew Roberts (historian)") and former Peruvian president [Alejandro Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Toledo "Alejandro Toledo"). On 29 January 2013, Trimble and Aznar co-wrote an article in *The Times* condemning [Hezbollah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbollah "Hezbollah") and calling on European governments to list it as a [terrorist organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_organisation "Terrorist organisation").

In 2016, Trimble supported the [Leave side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leave_campaign "Leave campaign") in the [UK referendum on EU membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum "2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum"). He said that if he had ever had any doubts about the issue, "his eight years on the EU Select Committees in the House of Lords – which scrutinise the EU's operations – had convinced him of the need to cut ties with Brussels". He cited a study which found that economic growth in the UK reduced after the decision to enter the Common Market and reduced further when the UK went into the Single Market.

### Turkel Commission of Inquiry

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Gaza_flotilla_raid_map.svg/250px-Gaza_flotilla_raid_map.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gaza_flotilla_raid_map.svg)

Routes of [Gaza-bound flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ships_of_Gaza_flotilla_raid "Ships of Gaza flotilla raid") (green) and [Israeli Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Navy "Israeli Navy") (orange)

On 14 June 2010, Trimble was appointed an observer to the Israeli special independent public [Turkel Commission of Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkel_Commission "Turkel Commission") into the [Gaza flotilla raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid "2010 Gaza flotilla raid").

The Commission investigated whether Israel's actions in preventing the arrival of ships in Gaza were in accordance with [international law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_law "International law"). It focused among other things on the security considerations for imposing a [naval blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_blockade "Naval blockade") on the [Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip "Gaza Strip") and the conformity of the naval blockade with the rules of international law; the conformity of the actions during the raid to principles of international law; and the actions taken by those who organised and participated in the flotilla, and their identities.

On the commission were former [Israeli Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Supreme_Court "Israeli Supreme Court") Justice, [Jacob Turkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Turkel "Jacob Turkel"), and former [Technion University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_University "Technion University") President, [Amos Horev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Horev "Amos Horev"), as well two other members added in July 2010. ([Bar Ilan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Ilan_University "Bar Ilan University") Professor of International Law [Shabtai Rosenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabtai_Rosenne "Shabtai Rosenne") also served on the commission from its establishment until his death on 21 September 2010.) In addition, the commission had two foreign observers, Trimble and former head of the Canadian military's judiciary, [Judge Advocate General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judge_Advocate_General_(Canada) "Judge Advocate General (Canada)"), [Ken Watkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Watkin "Ken Watkin"), who took part in hearings and discussions, but did not vote on the final conclusions. The panel, in January 2011, concluded both Israel's naval blockade of Gaza and the interception of the flotilla "were found to be legally pursuant to the rules of international law".

## Personal life

Trimble married firstly Heather McComb, a work colleague in the Land Registry, on 6 September 1968 at [Donaghadee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaghadee "Donaghadee") Church of Ireland church. Their twin sons were born prematurely and did not survive. In 1976 the couple divorced. Two years later, on 31 August 1978, he married a former student of his, Daphne Elizabeth (née Orr) in Warrenpoint Methodist Church. They had two sons and two daughters (Richard, Victoria, Nicholas and Sarah). Richard Trimble is a maths teacher and head of the sixth form at Graveney School in Tooting, South West London. [Lady Trimble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daphne_Trimble "Daphne Trimble") served as a member of the [Equality Commission for Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_Commission_for_Northern_Ireland "Equality Commission for Northern Ireland") and later the [Northern Ireland Human Rights Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Human_Rights_Commission "Northern Ireland Human Rights Commission"), before standing unsuccessfully in the [UK parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_general_election_in_Northern_Ireland "2010 United Kingdom general election in Northern Ireland") of May 2010 for the [Conservatives and Unionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatives_and_Unionists "Conservatives and Unionists") in [Lagan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagan_Valley_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Lagan Valley (UK Parliament constituency)").

His son Nicholas was active within the Ulster Unionist Party and serving on the [Lisburn and Castlereagh City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisburn_and_Castlereagh_City_Council "Lisburn and Castlereagh City Council") at the time of his father's death. Nicholas Trimble was co-opted in 2016 to replace Alexander Redpath as a Councillor representing Downshire West on Lisburn and Castlereagh City Council.

Trimble admitted in July 2019 that he was "forced" to change his position on [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage "Same-sex marriage") and partnerships after voting against them, because of his lesbian daughter Vicky, who married her girlfriend Rosalind Stephens in [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Scotland "Same-sex marriage in Scotland") in 2017. He told peers in the House of Lords "I cannot change that, and I cannot now go around saying that I am opposed to it because I acquiesced to it. There we are."

In 2021 Trimble was diagnosed with mixed dementia, a combination of Alzheimer's disease and vascular dementia, and in early 2022 with lung cancer. He died of community-acquired pneumonia in the Ulster Hospital, Dundonald, on 25 July 2022, at the age of 77.

## Honours

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/1933_Nobel_Peace_Prize_awarded_to_Norman_Angell.JPG/250px-1933_Nobel_Peace_Prize_awarded_to_Norman_Angell.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1933_Nobel_Peace_Prize_awarded_to_Norman_Angell.JPG)

[Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize")

In October 1998, Trimble and John Hume were awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") for their efforts to find a peaceful solution to the conflict in Northern Ireland. The [Nobel Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Institute "Nobel Institute") noted:

> As the leader of the traditionally predominant party in Northern Ireland, David Trimble showed great political courage when, at a critical stage of the process, he advocated solutions which led to the [Belfast (Good Friday)] peace agreement.

At a ceremony in Paris on 8 December 1999, Trimble was appointed an Officier in the [Légion d'Honneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Légion_d'Honneur "Légion d'Honneur") by the French Government.

In 2002, Trimble was awarded the [Golden Plate Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Plate_Award "Golden Plate Award") of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").
