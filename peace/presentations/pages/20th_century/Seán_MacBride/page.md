**Seán MacBride** (26 January 1904 – 15 January 1988) was an [Irish republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_republicanism "Irish republicanism") activist, politician, and diplomat who served as [Minister for External Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Foreign_Affairs_and_Trade "Minister for Foreign Affairs and Trade") from 1948 to 1951, Leader of [Clann na Poblachta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clann_na_Poblachta "Clann na Poblachta") from 1946 to 1965 and [Chief of Staff of the IRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Irish_Republican_Army_chiefs_of_staff "List of Irish Republican Army chiefs of staff") from 1936 to 1937. He served as a [Teachta Dála](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachta_Dála "Teachta Dála") (TD) from 1947 to 1957.

Rising from a domestic Irish political career, he founded or participated in multiple international organisations of the 20th century, including the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"), the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe "Council of Europe") and [Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International"). He received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1974, the [Lenin Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin_Peace_Prize "Lenin Peace Prize") for 1975–1976 and the UNESCO Silver Medal for Service in 1980.

## Early life

MacBride was born in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") in 1904, the son of [Maud Gonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maud_Gonne "Maud Gonne") and Major [John MacBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_MacBride "John MacBride"). As a founding member of [Inghinidhe na hÉireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inghinidhe_na_hÉireann "Inghinidhe na hÉireann") and [Cumann na mBan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumann_na_mBan "Cumann na mBan"), his mother was to become a prominent figure in [Irish nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_nationalism "Irish nationalism") and [republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_republicanism "Irish republicanism") circles. Later, in the 1930s, she became a devotee of the [social credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_credit "Social credit") ideas of [Major C.H. Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._H._Douglas "C. H. Douglas") and was "noisily anti-Semitic". His father had led the [Irish Transvaal Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Transvaal_Brigade "Irish Transvaal Brigade") against the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire") in the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War "Second Boer War"). Within a year of MacBride's birth, he returned to [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin "Dublin") where, following his participation in the [Easter Rising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Rising "Easter Rising"), John MacBride was court-martialed by the British and executed in May 1916.

MacBride's first language was [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language"), and he retained, or, as some would claim, affected, a French accent in the English language for the rest of his life. MacBride first studied at the Jesuit [Lycée Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague "Lycée Saint-Louis-de-Gonzague"), and remained in Paris until his father's execution in 1916, when he was sent to school at Mount St Benedict's, [Gorey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorey "Gorey"), [County Wexford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Wexford "County Wexford") in Ireland, and briefly at the [Downside School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downside_School "Downside School").

MacBride first became involved in politics during the [1918 Irish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1918_United_Kingdom_general_election_in_Ireland "1918 United Kingdom general election in Ireland") in which he was active for [Sinn Féin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinn_Féin "Sinn Féin"). The following year in 1919, aged 15, he lied about his age to join the [Irish Volunteers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Volunteers "Irish Volunteers"), which fought as part of the [Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Republican_Army_(1919–1922) "Irish Republican Army (1919–1922)"), and took part in the [Irish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_War_of_Independence "Irish War of Independence"). He opposed the 1921 [Anglo-Irish Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Irish_Treaty "Anglo-Irish Treaty") and was imprisoned by the [Irish Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Free_State "Irish Free State") during the [Irish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Civil_War "Irish Civil War").

## Anti-Treaty IRA, cooperation with the Soviets

On his release in 1924, MacBride studied law at [University College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Dublin "University College Dublin") and resumed his IRA activities. He worked briefly for [Éamon de Valera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Éamon_de_Valera "Éamon de Valera") as his personal secretary, travelling with him to [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome") to meet various dignitaries.

In January 1925, on his twenty-first birthday, MacBride married Catalina "Kid" Bulfin, a woman four years his senior who shared his political views. Bulfin was the daughter of the [Irish nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_nationalist "Irish nationalist") publisher and travel-writer [William Bulfin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bulfin "William Bulfin").

Before returning to [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin "Dublin") in 1927, where he became the IRA's Director of Intelligence, MacBride worked as a journalist in Paris and [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London").

According to historians Tom Mahon and James J. Gillogly, recently deciphered IRA messages from the 1920s reveal that the organisation's two main sources of funding were [Clan na Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clan_na_Gael "Clan na Gael") and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"). The messages further reveal that MacBride, before becoming IRA Director of Intelligence, was involved in the [espionage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espionage "Espionage") activities in Great Britain of [GRU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRU_(Soviet_Union) "GRU (Soviet Union)") [spymaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spymaster "Spymaster") [Walter Krivitsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Krivitsky "Walter Krivitsky"), whom ciphered IRA communications referred to only by the code name "James".

In addition to supplying the USSR with detailed information on [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy "Royal Navy") ships and [Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force "Royal Air Force") aeroplane engines, MacBride provided Soviet agents with "a brief specification and a complete drawing" of [ASDIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASDIC "ASDIC"), an early [sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonar "Sonar") system for detecting submarines, at a covert meeting in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam "Amsterdam") during the autumn of 1926. MacBride also assisted the GRU by providing forged passports to Soviet intelligence operatives who were at risk of capture.

In October 1926, MacBride sent a ciphered report from [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") to his IRA superiors about Soviet [counterfeiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterfeiting "Counterfeiting") operations, saying, "Several bad [Bank of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_England "Bank of England") notes have been passed here lately. These are said to emanate from Russia."

Soon after his return to Dublin in 1927, he was arrested and charged with the murder of politician [Kevin O'Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_O'Higgins "Kevin O'Higgins"), who had been assassinated near his home in [Booterstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booterstown "Booterstown"), [County Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Dublin "County Dublin"). At his trial, however, [Cumann na nGaedheal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumann_na_nGaedheal "Cumann na nGaedheal") politician [Bryan Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Cooper_(politician) "Bryan Cooper (politician)") testified as a witness for the defence that, at the time of Kevin O'Higgins' murder, both he and MacBride had been aboard a ferry travelling from Britain to Ireland. MacBride was then charged with being a subversive and interned in [Mountjoy Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountjoy_Prison "Mountjoy Prison").

In September 1927, Krivitsky sent the IRA a message from Amsterdam, demanding to see MacBride immediately, claiming to have a "present" he was anxious to give away, and naming a cafe where the meeting could take place. As MacBride was then imprisoned in Dublin, the IRA's [chief of staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_staff "Chief of staff"), [Moss Twomey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss_Twomey "Moss Twomey"), replied that the request was "rather awkward and impossible to fulfill at the present". In response, Twomey and two other senior IRA members travelled to Amsterdam and met with Krivitsky instead.

In 1929 an Irish section of the [League Against Imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_Against_Imperialism "League Against Imperialism") was formed and MacBride served as its secretary. Aimed at creating a "mass anti-imperialist movement", the organization was founded with the support of the [Comintern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comintern "Comintern").

Towards the end of the 1920s, after many supporters had left to join [Fianna Fáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fianna_Fáil "Fianna Fáil"), some members of the IRA started pushing for a more left-wing agenda. After the [IRA Army Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRA_Army_Council "IRA Army Council") voted down the idea, MacBride launched a new movement, [Saor Éire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saor_Éire "Saor Éire") ("Free Ireland"), in 1931. Although it was a non-military organisation, Saor Éire was declared unlawful along with the IRA, [Cumann na mBan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumann_na_mBan "Cumann na mBan") and nine other bodies. MacBride, meanwhile, became the security services' number-one target.

In 1936, the IRA's [chief of staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_staff "Chief of staff") Moss Twomey was sent to prison for three years; he was replaced by MacBride. At the time, the movement was in a state of disarray, with conflicts between several factions and personalities. [Tom Barry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Barry_(Irish_republican) "Tom Barry (Irish republican)") was appointed chief of staff to head up a military operation against the British, an action with which MacBride did not agree.

In 1937, MacBride was called to the [bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Bar "Irish Bar"). He then resigned from the IRA when the [Constitution of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ireland "Constitution of Ireland") was enacted later that year. As a barrister, MacBride frequently defended IRA prisoners of the state ([Thomas Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Harte_(Irish_republican) "Thomas Harte (Irish republican)") & [Patrick McGrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_McGrath_(Irish_republican) "Patrick McGrath (Irish republican)")). MacBride was unsuccessful in preventing the 1944 [death by hanging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_by_hanging "Death by hanging") of [Charlie Kerins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Kerins "Charlie Kerins"), who had been convicted based on fingerprint evidence of the 1942 ambush and murder of [Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garda_Síochána "Garda Síochána") [Detective Sergeant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detective_Sergeant "Detective Sergeant") [Denis O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_O'Brien_(police_officer) "Denis O'Brien (police officer)"). In 1946, during the inquest into the death of [Seán McCaughey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seán_McCaughey "Seán McCaughey"), MacBride embarrassed the Irish State by forcing them to admit that conditions in [Portlaoise Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portlaoise_Prison "Portlaoise Prison") were inhumane.

## Clann na Poblachta, Minister for External Affairs

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Se%C3%A1n_MacBride_circa_1947.jpg/250px-Se%C3%A1n_MacBride_circa_1947.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Seán_MacBride_circa_1947.jpg)

MacBride circa 1947 when he founded Clann na Poblachta

In 1946, MacBride founded the republican party [Clann na Poblachta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clann_na_Poblachta "Clann na Poblachta"). He hoped it would replace Fianna Fáil as Ireland's major political party. In October 1947, he won the [Dublin County by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Dublin_County_by-election "1947 Dublin County by-election"). On the same day, [Patrick Kinane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Kinane "Patrick Kinane") also won the [Tipperary by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Tipperary_by-election "1947 Tipperary by-election") for Clann na Poblachta.

However, at the [1948 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Irish_general_election "1948 Irish general election") Clann na Poblachta won only ten seats. The party joined [Fine Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_Gael "Fine Gael"), the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(Ireland) "Labour Party (Ireland)"), the [National Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Labour_Party_(Ireland) "National Labour Party (Ireland)"), [Clann na Talmhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clann_na_Talmhan "Clann na Talmhan") and several independents to form the [First Inter-Party Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_13th_Dáil "Government of the 13th Dáil") with Fine Gael [TD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachta_Dála "Teachta Dála") [John A. Costello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Costello "John A. Costello") as [Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach "Taoiseach"). [Richard Mulcahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Mulcahy "Richard Mulcahy") was the [Leader of Fine Gael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_Fine_Gael "Leader of Fine Gael"), but MacBride and many other Irish Republicans had never forgiven Mulcahy for his role in [carrying out 77 executions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executions_during_the_Irish_Civil_War "Executions during the Irish Civil War") under the government of the [Irish Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Free_State "Irish Free State") in the 1920s during the [Irish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Civil_War "Irish Civil War"). To gain the support of Clann na Poblachta, Mulcahy stepped aside in favour of Costello. Two Clann na Poblachta TDs were appointed to cabinet; MacBride became [Minister for External Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Foreign_Affairs_and_Trade "Minister for Foreign Affairs and Trade") while [Noël Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noël_Browne "Noël Browne") became [Minister for Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Health_(Ireland) "Minister for Health (Ireland)").

On his ministerial accession, MacBride sent a [telegram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegram "Telegram") to [Pope Pius XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII "Pope Pius XII") offering:

> ...to repose at the feet of Your Holiness the assurance of our filial loyalty and our devotion to Your August Person, as well as our firm resolve to be guided in all our work by the teaching of Christ and to strive for the attainment of a social order in Ireland based on Christian principles.

At MacBride's suggestion, Costello [nominated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominated_members_of_Seanad_Éireann "Nominated members of Seanad Éireann") the northern Protestant [Denis Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Ireland "Denis Ireland") to [Seanad Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seanad_Éireann "Seanad Éireann"), the first resident of Northern Ireland to be appointed as a member of the [Oireachtas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oireachtas "Oireachtas"). While a Senator (1948–1951), Ireland was Irish representative to the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe "Council of Europe") assisting MacBride in the leading role he was to play in securing acceptance of the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights "European Convention on Human Rights")—signed in Rome on 4 November 1950. In 1950, MacBride was president of the Council of Foreign Ministers of the Council of Europe, and he was vice-president of the [Organisation for European Economic Co-operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development "Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development") (OEEC, later OECD) in 1948–1951. He was responsible for Ireland not joining the [North Atlantic Treaty Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") (NATO).

He was instrumental in passing of [The Republic of Ireland Act 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republic_of_Ireland_Act_1948 "The Republic of Ireland Act 1948"), which repealed the [External Relations Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Authority_(External_Relations)_Act_1936 "Executive Authority (External Relations) Act 1936"), and came into force in 1949. It declared that Ireland [may officially be described](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_the_Irish_state "Names of the Irish state") as the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland "Republic of Ireland") and that the [president of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ireland "President of Ireland") had the executive authority of the state in its external relations.

In 1951, MacBride ordered Noël Browne to resign as a minister over the [Mother and Child Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_and_Child_Scheme "Mother and Child Scheme") after it was attacked by the [Irish Catholic hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Ireland "Catholic Church in Ireland") and the Irish medical establishment. Whatever the merits of the scheme, or of Browne, MacBride concluded in a cabinet memorandum:

> Even if, as Catholics, we were prepared to take the responsibility of disregarding [the Hierarchy's] views, which I do not think we can do, it would be politically impossible to do so ... We are dealing with the considered views of the leaders of the Catholic Church to which the vast majority of our people belong; these views cannot be ignored.

Also in 1951, Clann na Poblachta was reduced to two seats after the general election. MacBride held his seat and was re-elected again in 1954. Opposing the internment of IRA suspects during the [Border Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Campaign_(Irish_Republican_Army) "Border Campaign (Irish Republican Army)") (1956–1962), he contested both the [1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Irish_general_election "1957 Irish general election") and [1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Irish_general_election "1961 Irish general election") general elections but failed to be elected both times. He then retired from politics and continued practising as a barrister.

## International roles and commissions

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Se%C3%A1n_MacBride_1986.jpg/330px-Se%C3%A1n_MacBride_1986.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Seán_MacBride_1986.jpg)

Seán MacBride in 1986

After retiring from politics in Ireland, MacBride assumed various international commissions and roles. He was among a group of lawyers who founded [JUSTICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JUSTICE "JUSTICE")—the UK-based human rights and law reform organisation—initially to monitor the [show trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Show_trials "Show trials") after the [1956 Budapest uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956 "Hungarian Revolution of 1956"). Later it became the UK section of the [International Commission of Jurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Commission_of_Jurists "International Commission of Jurists") for which MacBride, from 1963 to 1971, was Secretary-General. In 1966, his standing on the Commission was unsuccessfully challenged by [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") claims that he had been involved with a [Central Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency") funding operation.

In 1961, MacBride was a co-founder of [Amnesty International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International "Amnesty International"), and he served as its International Chairman until 1975.

In 1963, he had helped draft the constitution of the [Organisation of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity "Organisation of African Unity") (OAU); following consultations on the constitutions of newly independent [Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana "Ghana"), [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia "Zambia") and [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania").

In 1968, MacBride was elected chair and, in 1975, president of the [International Peace Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Peace_Bureau "International Peace Bureau") in Geneva, a position he retained until 1985. He was also involved in the International Prisoners of Conscience Fund.

In the course of the 1970s, he held various positions with the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"). In 1973, he was elected by the [UN General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly "United Nations General Assembly") to the post of High Commissioner for [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia "Namibia"), with the rank of Assistant Secretary-General. It was thought that, represented by a man whose father had volunteered to fight alongside their forefathers, the [Afrikaner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaners "Afrikaners") leadership of [Apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid") [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa") would find the UN's assurances in return for a withdrawal from Namibia more credible.

In 1977, MacBride was appointed president of the International Commission for the Study of Communication Problems, set up by [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO"). In 1980, this produced the controversial [MacBride Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacBride_report "MacBride report") which called for policies to reduce the international dominance of the Western media.

During the 1980s, he initiated the Appeal by Lawyers against Nuclear War which was jointly sponsored by the [International Peace Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Peace_Bureau "International Peace Bureau") and the [International Progress Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Progress_Organization "International Progress Organization"). In close cooperation with [Francis Boyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Boyle "Francis Boyle") and [Hans Köchler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Köchler "Hans Köchler") of the [International Progress Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Progress_Organization "International Progress Organization") he lobbied the General Assembly for a resolution demanding an Advisory Opinion from the [International Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Court_of_Justice "International Court of Justice") on the legality of nuclear arms. The Advisory Opinion on the [*Legality of the Threat or Use of Nuclear Weapons*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advisory_Opinion_of_the_International_Court_of_Justice_of_8_July_1996 "Advisory Opinion of the International Court of Justice of 8 July 1996") was eventually handed down by the ICJ in 1996.

In 1982, MacBride was chairman of the International Commission to enquire into reported violations of International Law by Israel during its [invasion of the Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War "1982 Lebanon War"). The other members were [Richard Falk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Falk "Richard Falk"), [Kader Asmal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kader_Asmal "Kader Asmal"), Brian Bercusson, Géraud de la Pradelle, and [Stefan Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Wild "Stefan Wild"). The commission's report, which concluded that "the [government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Israel "Government of Israel") has committed acts of aggression contrary to international law", was published in 1983 under the title *Israel in Lebanon*.

## Nobel Peace Prize and other international honours

MacBride's work was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") (1974) as a man who "mobilised the conscience of the world in the fight against injustice". He shared the prize with [Eisaku Sato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisaku_Satō "Eisaku Satō"), the former [Japanese Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Japan "Prime Minister of Japan") acknowledged for his commitment to peace and [non-proliferation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Non-Proliferation_of_Nuclear_Weapons "Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons").

Other international honours followed: the [Lenin Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin_Peace_Prize "Lenin Peace Prize") in 1975; the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") in 1978; and the UNESCO Silver Medal for Service (1980). He received the Lenin Peace Prize for his opposition to what MacBride referred to as "this absolutely obscene arms race." He was one of only two to win both the Lenin and Nobel peace prizes, the other being [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling "Linus Pauling").

## Last Irish interventions

In 1977, in the midst of the [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland "Northern Ireland") [Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles "The Troubles"), MacBride, co-operating with [Desmond Boal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Boal "Desmond Boal") ([QC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_counsel "Queens counsel") and former chairman of [Ian Paisley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Paisley "Ian Paisley")'s [Democratic Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Unionist_Party "Democratic Unionist Party")), acted as joint mediator in confidential negotiations between the [Provisional IRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army "Provisional Irish Republican Army") (PIRA) and the [Ulster Volunteer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Volunteer_Force "Ulster Volunteer Force") about a federal settlement for Ireland. Their efforts included a meeting, brokered by West Belfast priest [Des Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Wilson_(Irish_Catholic_priest) "Des Wilson (Irish Catholic priest)"), between Boal and [Gerry Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Adams "Gerry Adams"). Inasmuch as their exchange was "frank", Adams found the discussion "constructive", but it failed to identify common political ground.

In 1984, MacBride was one of four well-known Irish activists, two Catholics and two Protestants, who endorsed a set of [fair-employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Employment "Fair Employment") guidelines for American investment in Northern Ireland, intended to redress a history of sectarian discrimination. The campaign for what became known as the [MacBride Principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacBride_Principles "MacBride Principles") resulted in both British and American legislation.

In the Irish Republic, MacBride edited a 1981 report of a Commission of Enquiry into the Irish Penal System. *Crime and Punishment* broadly condemned the country's prisons, unreformed since independence, for failing to address the task of offender rehabilitation and consequently for its high degree of recidivism.

In 1983, MacBride voted yes to the [Eighth Amendment to the Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland "Eighth Amendment of the Constitution of Ireland"), enshrining the legal ban on abortion, and wrote a newspaper article two days before stating his position. In 1985, he supported the largely female workforce in the [Dunnes Stores Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunnes_Stores_Strike "Dunnes Stores Strike") and attended at least one of their rallies.

## Later life and death

In his later years, MacBride lived in his mother's home, Roebuck House, which served as a meeting place for many years for Irish nationalists, as well as in the Parisian [arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissement "Arrondissement") where he grew up with his mother.

Seán MacBride died in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin "Dublin") on 15 January 1988, eleven days before his 84th birthday. He is buried in [Glasnevin Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasnevin_Cemetery "Glasnevin Cemetery") alongside his mother, and his wife, who died in 1976.

On the occasion of MacBride's death, [Oliver Tambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Tambo "Oliver Tambo") of the [African National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_National_Congress "African National Congress") (ANC), stated "Seán MacBride will always be remembered for the concrete leadership he provided to the liberation movement and people of Namibia and South Africa. Driven by his own personal and political insight arising out of the cause of national freedom in Ireland ... our debt to him can never be repaid."

## Legacy

Streets in [Windhoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windhoek "Windhoek"), [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia "Namibia") and Amsterdam are named after him. The Headquarters of [Amnesty International Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesty_International_Ireland "Amnesty International Ireland") is called 'Seán MacBride House' in his honour. and the [International Peace Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Peace_Bureau "International Peace Bureau") likewise named the 'Seán MacBride Prize' in his name.

A bust of MacBride was unveiled in [Iveagh House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iveagh_House "Iveagh House"), headquarters of the Irish Department of Foreign Affairs in 1995.

## In popular culture

A novel, *The Casting of Mr O'Shaughnessy*, was published in 1995 (revised edition 2002) by [Éamon Delaney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eamon_Delaney "Eamon Delaney") in which the eponymous Mr O'Shaughnessy was, in the author's own words "partly, but quite obviously, based on the career of the colourful Seán MacBride".

## Career summary

- 1946–1965 Leader of Clann na Poblachta
- 1947–1958 Member of [Dáil Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dáil_Éireann "Dáil Éireann")
- 1948–1951 [Minister for External Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Foreign_Affairs_and_Trade "Minister for Foreign Affairs and Trade") of Ireland in Inter-Party Government
- 1948–1951 Vice-president of the Organization for European Economic Cooperation (OEEC)
- 1950 President, Committee of Ministers of Council of Europe
- 1954 Offered but declined, Ministerial office in Irish Government
- 1963–1971 Secretary-General, International Commission of Jurists
- 1966 Consultant to the Pontifical Commission on Justice and Peace
- 1961–1975 Chairman Amnesty International Executive
- 1968–1974 Chairman of the Executive International Peace Bureau
- 1975–1985 President of the Executive International Peace Bureau
- 1968–1974 Chairman Special Committee of International NGOs on Human Rights (Geneva)
- 1973 Vice-chairman, Congress of World Peace Forces (Moscow, October 1973)
- 1973 Vice-president, World Federation of United Nations Associations
- 1973–1977 Elected by the General Assembly of the United Nations to the post of United Nations Commissioner for Namibia with the rank of Assistant Secretary-General of the United Nations
- 1977–1980 Chairman, Commission on International Communication for UNESCO
- 1982 Chairman of the International Commission to enquire into reported violations of International Law by Israel during its invasion of Lebanon
