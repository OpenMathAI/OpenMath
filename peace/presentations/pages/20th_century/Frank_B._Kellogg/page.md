**Frank Billings Kellogg** (December 22, 1856 – December 21, 1937) was an American lawyer, politician, and statesman who served in the [U.S. Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senate "U.S. Senate") and as [U.S. Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Secretary_of_State "U.S. Secretary of State"). He co-authored the [Kellogg–Briand Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg–Briand_Pact "Kellogg–Briand Pact"), for which he was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1929.

## Early life and career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Ambassador_Frank_B._Kellogg%2C_by_Philip_Alexius_de_Laszlo%2C_1925.jpg/250px-Ambassador_Frank_B._Kellogg%2C_by_Philip_Alexius_de_Laszlo%2C_1925.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ambassador_Frank_B._Kellogg,_by_Philip_Alexius_de_Laszlo,_1925.jpg)

Portrait of Kellogg by [Philip de László](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_de_László "Philip de László").

Kellogg was born in [Potsdam, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam,_New_York "Potsdam, New York"), on December 22, 1856, the son of Abigail (Billings) and Asa Farnsworth Kellogg. His family moved to [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota "Minnesota") in 1865.

Kellogg [read law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Read_law "Read law") and began practicing law in [Rochester, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_Minnesota "Rochester, Minnesota"), in 1877. He served as city attorney of Rochester 1878–1881 and county attorney for [Olmsted County, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmsted_County,_Minnesota "Olmsted County, Minnesota"), from 1882 to 1887. He moved to [St. Paul, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul,_Minnesota "St. Paul, Minnesota"), in 1886.

In 1905, Kellogg joined the federal government when [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt") asked Kellogg to prosecute a federal [antitrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_antitrust_law "United States antitrust law") case. In 1906, Kellogg was appointed special counsel to the [Interstate Commerce Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Commerce_Commission "Interstate Commerce Commission") for its investigation of [E. H. Harriman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._H._Harriman "E. H. Harriman"). In 1908, he was appointed to lead the federal prosecution against [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad "Union Pacific Railroad"), under the [Sherman Antitrust Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_Antitrust_Act "Sherman Antitrust Act"). His most important case was *[Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil "Standard Oil") Co. of New Jersey v. United States*, 221 U.S. 1 (1911). Following this successful prosecution, he was elected president of the [American Bar Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bar_Association "American Bar Association") (1912–1913). He was a member of the [World War Foreign Debts Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_Foreign_Debts_Commission_Act "World War Foreign Debts Commission Act").

In 1907, Kellogg was honored as a Compatriot of the Minnesota Society of the [Sons of the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_the_American_Revolution "Sons of the American Revolution").

### United States Senate

In 1916, Kellogg was elected as a [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") to the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") from Minnesota and served from March 4, 1917, to March 3, 1923, in the [65th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/65th_United_States_Congress "65th United States Congress"), [66th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/66th_United_States_Congress "66th United States Congress"), and [67th Congresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/67th_United_States_Congress "67th United States Congress"). During the ratification battle for the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles "Treaty of Versailles"), he was one of the few Republicans who supported ratification. He lost his re-election bid in 1922 and, in 1923, he was a delegate to the Fifth [International Conference of American States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Conference_of_American_States "International Conference of American States") at [Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago "Santiago"), Chile.

### Ambassador to Great Britain

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/TIMEMagazine28Sep1925.jpg/250px-TIMEMagazine28Sep1925.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:TIMEMagazine28Sep1925.jpg)

*[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* cover, September 28, 1925

In 1924, he was appointed by President [Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Calvin_Coolidge "Presidency of Calvin Coolidge") as Ambassador [Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary to Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Ambassador_to_the_United_Kingdom "United States Ambassador to the United Kingdom"), serving from January 14, 1924, to February 10, 1925. He succeeded [George Brinton McClellan Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Brinton_McClellan_Harvey "George Brinton McClellan Harvey") who served under [Warren G. Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_G._Harding "Warren G. Harding") and was succeeded by [Alanson B. Houghton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanson_B._Houghton "Alanson B. Houghton") so that Kellogg could assume the role of Secretary of State.

## Secretary of State

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Frank_B._Kellogg_passport.jpg/250px-Frank_B._Kellogg_passport.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Frank_B._Kellogg_passport.jpg)

1927 hand signed passport by Frank B. Kellogg as Secretary of State

From 1925 to 1929, he served as the [United States Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State "United States Secretary of State") in the Cabinet of President Coolidge. In 1928, he was awarded the [Freedom of the City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_City "Freedom of the City") in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin "Dublin"), Ireland and in 1929 the government of France made him a member of the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour "Legion of Honour").

As Secretary of State, he was responsible for improving [U.S.–Mexican relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico–United_States_relations "Mexico–United States relations") and helping to resolve the long-standing [Tacna–Arica controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacna–Arica_compromise "Tacna–Arica compromise") between Peru and Chile. His most significant accomplishment, however, was the [Kellogg–Briand Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg–Briand_Pact "Kellogg–Briand Pact"), signed in 1928. Proposed by its other namesake, French foreign minister [Aristide Briand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristide_Briand "Aristide Briand"), the treaty intended to provide for "the renunciation of war as an instrument of national policy." Kellogg was awarded the 1929 [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in recognition. (Briand had already won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1926).

He was associate judge of the [Permanent Court of International Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Court_of_International_Justice "Permanent Court of International Justice") from 1930 to 1935.

He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1931.

Kellogg was self-conscious about his lack of academic credentials; he attended a one-room country school and dropped out at age 14. He never attended high school, college or law school. His only advanced training came from clerking in a private lawyer's office. Kellogg grew up in a poor farm in Minnesota, and lacked a commanding presence or the sophistication to deal with the aristocrats who dominated European diplomacy. As Secretary of State, his main focus was Latin America, where he dealt with brutal but unsophisticated strongmen. His staff provided the ideas, and they appreciated that he was always open, candid, and easy to communicate with. He helped end the battle between the Mexican government and the Catholic Church, but failed to resolve the dispute over ownership of the oil reserves. In the Far East, he followed the advice of Nelson Trusler Johnson, the new chief of the Division of Far Eastern Affairs. They favored China and protected it from threats from Japan. They successfully negotiated tariff reform with China, thereby giving enhanced status to the Kuomintang and helping get rid of the [unequal treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unequal_treaty "Unequal treaty"). As for Europe he was primarily interested with expanding the limitations on naval armaments that been established by the [Washington Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Naval_Treaty "Washington Naval Treaty"); he made little progress. Kellogg gained international fame, and the Nobel Peace Prize, with the Kellogg–Briand Pact. It was endorsed by nearly every nation and made starting a war a punishable criminal action. It formed the legal basis for the trial and execution of German and Japanese war leaders after 1945.

## Personal life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Embassy_of_Sierra_Leone.JPG/250px-Embassy_of_Sierra_Leone.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Embassy_of_Sierra_Leone.JPG)

Kellogg's former residence in Washington, D.C.

In 1886, Kellogg married [Clara May Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Cook_Kellogg "Clara Cook Kellogg") (1861–1942), the daughter of George Clinton Cook (1828–1901) and Elizabeth ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Née "Née") Burns) Cook (1838–1908).

In 1880, he became a member of the [Masonic Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonic_Lodge "Masonic Lodge") Rochester No. 21, where he received the degrees of [freemasonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry "Freemasonry") on April 1, April 19, and May 3.

He died from pneumonia, following a stroke, in [St. Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul,_Minnesota "St. Paul, Minnesota") on December 21, 1937, the eve of his 81st birthday. He was buried at the Chapel of St. Joseph of Arimathea in [Washington National Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_National_Cathedral "Washington National Cathedral"), Washington, D.C.

### Legacy

In 1937, he endowed the [Kellogg Foundation for Education in International Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg_Foundation_for_Education_in_International_Relations "Kellogg Foundation for Education in International Relations") at [Carleton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_College "Carleton College"), where he was a trustee. His house in St. Paul, the [Frank B. Kellogg House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_B._Kellogg_House "Frank B. Kellogg House") was listed as a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark "National Historic Landmark") in 1976.

The following were named in his honor:

- Kellogg Boulevard in downtown Saint Paul.
- [Kellogg Middle School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg_Middle_School "Kellogg Middle School") in [Shoreline, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoreline,_Washington "Shoreline, Washington"), and [Rochester, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_Minnesota "Rochester, Minnesota"), as was Frank B. Kellogg High School (closed 1986) in [Little Canada, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Canada,_Minnesota "Little Canada, Minnesota"), which had been a part of Roseville School District 623.
- A [Liberty ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_ship "Liberty ship"), the [SS *Frank B. Kellogg*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Liberty_ships_(A) "List of Liberty ships (A)")

### Papers

Frank B. Kellogg's papers are available for research use at the [Minnesota Historical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Historical_Society "Minnesota Historical Society"). They include correspondence and miscellaneous papers, State Department duplicates, news clippings scrapbooks, awards, floor plans, honorary degrees, maps, memorials and memoranda.
