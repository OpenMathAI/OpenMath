**Adolfo Pérez Esquivel** (born 26 November 1931) is an Argentine activist, community organizer, painter, writer and sculptor. He was the recipient of the 1980 [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") for his opposition to [Argentina's last civil-military dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reorganization_Process "National Reorganization Process") (1976–1983), during which he was detained, tortured, and held without trial for 14 months. He also received, among other distinctions, the [Pacem in Terris Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacem_in_Terris_Award "Pacem in Terris Award").

## Early life

Pérez Esquivel was born in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires") on 26 November 1931 to a Spanish fisherman from [Poio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poio "Poio"), [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_(Spain) "Galicia (Spain)"), who emigrated to Argentina. His mother died when he was three, and despite his poverty, he attended the [Manuel Belgrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Belgrano "Manuel Belgrano") School of Fine Arts and the [National University of La Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_La_Plata "National University of La Plata"), where he was trained as a painter and sculptor. He was appointed professor of architecture and worked with a variety of [sculptural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture "Sculpture") media, and for 25 years taught in all levels from primary to university. Pérez Esquivel began working with popularly based Latin American [Christian pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pacifism "Christian pacifism") groups during the 1960s. He relinquished his teaching post in 1974, when he was chosen as coordinator general for a network of Latin America-based communities promoting [liberation of the poor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Theology "Liberation Theology") through [non-violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-violence "Non-violence").

## Plight for human rights

When systematic repression followed the [March 1976 coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_1976_coup "March 1976 coup"), which brought the dictatorship of General [Jorge Videla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Videla "Jorge Videla") to power, Pérez Esquivel contributed to the formation and financing of the linkages between popularly based organizations to defend [human rights in Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Argentina "Human rights in Argentina") and support the families of the victims of the [Dirty War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirty_War "Dirty War"). The [NGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NGO "NGO") *[Servicio Paz y Justicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servicio_Paz_y_Justicia "Servicio Paz y Justicia")* ("Service, Peace and Justice Foundation", or SERPAJ), which he co-founded in 1974, and served as an instrument for the defense of human rights by promoting an international campaign to denounce the atrocities committed by [the military regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceso_de_Reorganización_Nacional "Proceso de Reorganización Nacional"). SERPAJ is a member of the [International Fellowship of Reconciliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Fellowship_of_Reconciliation "International Fellowship of Reconciliation") (IFOR), which supported Esquivel's work from the beginning.

Pérez Esquivel was detained by the [Brazilian Military Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Military_Police "Brazilian Military Police") in 1975. He was jailed in 1976 in Ecuador, along with Latin American and North American bishops. He was detained in Buenos Aires in 1977 by the [Argentine Federal Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Federal_Police "Argentine Federal Police"), tortured, and held without trial for 14 months; during that, he received, among other distinctions, the [Pope John XXIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII "Pope John XXIII") Peace Memorial.

### Distinctions

Nominated by 1976 Nobel Peace Prize laureates [Mairead Corrigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mairead_Corrigan "Mairead Corrigan") and [Betty Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Williams_(Nobel_laureate) "Betty Williams (Nobel laureate)"), Pérez Esquivel was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") on 10 December 1980, for his efforts in the [defense of human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_defenders "Human rights defenders"). Accepting the prize "in the name of the poorest and smallest of my brothers and sisters," he donated the prize money to charity. He remained active in support of the [Mothers of the Plaza de Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mothers_of_the_Plaza_de_Mayo "Mothers of the Plaza de Mayo") despite ongoing harassment by the dictatorship, and later joined causes in support of the [indigenous peoples of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Argentina "Indigenous peoples of Argentina") and [environmentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism "Environmentalism") and against [austerity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austerity "Austerity") policies and the [Free Trade Area of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Trade_Area_of_the_Americas "Free Trade Area of the Americas").

Pérez Esquivel was awarded the [Pacem in Terris Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacem_in_Terris_Award "Pacem in Terris Award") in 1999.

Pérez Esquivel served as president of the Honorary Council of Service, Latin American Peace and Justice Foundation and of the [International League for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_League_for_Human_Rights "International League for Human Rights") and Liberation of Peoples (based in [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan "Milan")), and as a member of the [Permanent Peoples' Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Peoples'_Tribunal "Permanent Peoples' Tribunal"). He published *Caminando Junto al Pueblo* ("Walking Together with the People", 1995), in which he relates his experiences with nonviolence in Latin America, and he was appointed Professor of Peace and Human Rights Studies at the [University of Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Buenos_Aires "University of Buenos Aires") in 1998. He campaigned during 2010 against the practice on the part of the [Esquel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquel "Esquel") Police Department of training children into [paramilitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramilitary "Paramilitary") squads, an operation that he compared to the creation of [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany")'s [Hitler Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler_Youth "Hitler Youth").

## Recent years

Pérez Esquivel is a permanent lecturer at the [University of Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Buenos_Aires "University of Buenos Aires") [Faculty of Social Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_of_Social_Sciences,_University_of_Buenos_Aires "Faculty of Social Sciences, University of Buenos Aires"), where he directs the seminar "Culture for Peace and Human Rights".

Pérez Esquivel opposed the European intervention in the [2011 Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Libyan_Civil_War "2011 Libyan Civil War") and warned against an intervention in the [Syrian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Civil_War "Syrian Civil War"). In the aftermath of the [death of Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Osama_bin_Laden "Death of Osama bin Laden"), he sent a letter to President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") suggesting the United States killed rather than tried bin Laden because he could have revealed unsettling information about [9/11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11 "9/11"). He argued, "You know that there are people who have investigated the tragic events of 9/11/2001 and [claim there is evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11_conspiracy_theories "9/11 conspiracy theories") that this was a self-coup (self-inflicted attack)." Later, he added, "this event was the perfect excuse to launch a war against Afghanistan and Iraq and now against Libya," and referred to the United States an "axis of evil."

Pérez Esquivel expressed himself regarding the 2013 election of [Archbishop of Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Buenos_Aires "Archbishop of Buenos Aires") Jorge Bergoglio as [Pope Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Francis "Pope Francis"), stating that as [Provincial superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_superior "Provincial superior") of [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit "Jesuit") "he had lacked the sufficient courage shown by other Bishops to support our cause for human rights during the dictatorship." Pérez Esquivel also said that "Bergoglio did what he could given his age at that time." He, however, clarified that the future Pope "was no accomplice and had no links with the dictatorship" and that while "it is said he did not do enough to get two priests out of jail, I know personally that many bishops called on the military junta for the release of prisoners and priests and that these requests were not granted."

In June 2017, he defended the government of [Nicolás Maduro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolás_Maduro "Nicolás Maduro") in Venezuela, saying that the country was undergoing a [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_d'état "Coup d'état") attempt, orchestrated by the United States.

He was hospitalized in January 2022 following a stroke.

## Works of art

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Adolfo_P%C3%A9rez_Esquivel_1983.jpg/250px-Adolfo_P%C3%A9rez_Esquivel_1983.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Adolfo_Pérez_Esquivel_1983.jpg)

Adolfo Pérez Esquivel in 1983

Esquivel's work ranges from exhibitions to murals and monuments, including the 15 station Latin American Via Crucis (including a Lenten cloth "A new sky and a new land") made in 1992 to commemorate the 500th anniversary of the conquest of America; Monument to Refugees, located in the Headquarters of the [UNHCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNHCR "UNHCR") in Switzerland; the Latin American Peoples Mural in the Cathedral of [Riobamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riobamba "Riobamba"), Ecuador, dedicated to [Monsignor Proaño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonidas_Proaño "Leonidas Proaño") and the [indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Indigenous peoples of the Americas"); and a bronze statue in tribute to [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi "Mahatma Gandhi") at Gandhi Square, in [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona "Barcelona").
