**John Raleigh Mott** (May 25, 1865 – January 31, 1955) was an American evangelist and long-serving leader of the Young Men's Christian Association ([YMCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YMCA "YMCA")) and the [World Student Christian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Student_Christian_Federation "World Student Christian Federation") (WSCF). He received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 1946 for his work in establishing and strengthening international Protestant [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") student organizations that worked to [promote peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_movement "Peace movement"). He shared the prize with [Emily Greene Balch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Greene_Balch "Emily Greene Balch"). From 1895 until 1920 Mott was the General Secretary of the WSCF. Intimately involved in the formation of the [World Council of Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Council_of_Churches "World Council of Churches") in 1948, that body elected him as a lifelong honorary President. He helped found the [World Student Christian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Student_Christian_Federation "World Student Christian Federation") in 1895, the [1910 World Missionary Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1910_World_Missionary_Conference "1910 World Missionary Conference") and the [World Council of Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Council_of_Churches "World Council of Churches") in 1948. His best-known book, *The Evangelization of the World in this Generation*, became a missionary slogan in the early 20th century.

## Biography

Mott was born in [Livingston Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livingston_Manor,_New_York "Livingston Manor, New York"), [Sullivan County, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sullivan_County,_New_York "Sullivan County, New York"), on May 25, 1865, and his family moved to [Postville, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postville,_Iowa "Postville, Iowa"), in September of the same year. He attended [Upper Iowa University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Iowa_University "Upper Iowa University"), where he studied history and was an award-winning student debater. He transferred to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"), where he received his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") in 1888. He was influenced by [Arthur Tappan Pierson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Tappan_Pierson "Arthur Tappan Pierson") one of the forces behind the [Student Volunteer Movement for Foreign Missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_Volunteer_Movement "Student Volunteer Movement"), which was founded in 1886.

In 1910, Mott, an [American Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Methodist_Church "United Methodist Church") layperson, presided at the [1910 World Missionary Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1910_World_Missionary_Conference "1910 World Missionary Conference"), which was an important milestone in the modern [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant "Protestant") [missions movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_(Christian) "Mission (Christian)") and some say the modern [ecumenical movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_ecumenism "Christian ecumenism").

Mott and a colleague were offered free passage on the *[Titanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic "Titanic")* in 1912 by a [White Star Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Star_Line "White Star Line") official who was interested in their work, but they declined and took the more humble liner the [SS *Lapland*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Lapland "SS Lapland"). According to a biography by C. Howard Hopkins, in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") the two men heard what happened to the *Titanic*, looked at each other and remarked that, "The Good Lord must have more work for us to do."

After touring Europe and promoting ecumenism, Mott traveled to Asia where, from October 1912 to May 1913, he held a series of 18 regional and national conferences, including in Ceylon, India, Burma, Malaya, China, Korea and Japan.

He also worked with [Robert Hallowell Gardiner III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hallowell_Gardiner_III "Robert Hallowell Gardiner III") to maintain relations with the Russian Orthodox Church and Archbishop Tikhon after the Russian Revolution.

From 1920 until 1928, Mott served as the WSCF Chairperson. For his labors in both missions and ecumenism, as well as for peace, some historians consider him to be "the most widely traveled and universally trusted Christian leader of his time".

## Personal life and legacy

Mott married twice. His first wife was a teacher, Leila Ada White. They married in 1891 and had two sons and two daughters, [Irene Mott Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Mott_Bose "Irene Mott Bose"), a social worker in India, and wife of Indian Supreme Court justice [Vivian Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivian_Bose "Vivian Bose"); [Eleanor Mott Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Mott_Ross?action=edit&redlink=1 "Eleanor Mott Ross") of Poughkeepsie, New York; [John Livingstone Mott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Livingstone_Mott?action=edit&redlink=1 "John Livingstone Mott"), who received the [Kaisar-i‐Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaisar-i-Hind_Medal "Kaisar-i-Hind Medal") silver medal in 1931, for his work with the [YMCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YMCA "YMCA") in India; [Frederick Dodge Mott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Dodge_Mott?action=edit&redlink=1 "Frederick Dodge Mott"), who worked in healthcare planning in Canada, and was Canada's representative to the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization").

After Leila Mott died in 1952, Mott remarried in 1953, to Agnes Peter (1880-1957), a descendant of [Martha Custis Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Washington "Martha Washington"). He died in 1955, in [Orlando, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida "Orlando, Florida"), aged 89 years and was buried in [Washington National Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_National_Cathedral "Washington National Cathedral"). His papers are held at the Yale Divinity School Library.

## Veneration

In 2022, John Raleigh Mott was officially added to the [Episcopal Church liturgical calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church) "Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)") with a feast day on October 3.

The high school of the [Postville Community School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postville_Community_School_District "Postville Community School District") in [Postville, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postville,_Iowa "Postville, Iowa"), is named after him.
