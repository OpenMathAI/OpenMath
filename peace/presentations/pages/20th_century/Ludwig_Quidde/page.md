**Ludwig Quidde** (German pronunciation: [[ˈluːtvɪç ˈkvɪdə]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Ludwig_Quidde.ogg "File:De-Ludwig Quidde.ogg"); 23 March 1858 – 4 March 1941) was a German politician and [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifist "Pacifist") who is mainly remembered today for his acerbic criticism of [German Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Emperor "German Emperor") [Wilhelm II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_II "Wilhelm II"). Quidde's long career spanned four different eras of German history: [Otto von Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Bismarck "Otto von Bismarck") (until 1890), the [Hohenzollern Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire") under Wilhelm II (1888–1918), the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic") (1918–1933); and [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany"). In 1927, Quidde was awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize").

## Early life and career

Born into a wealthy [bourgeois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie "Bourgeoisie") merchant family, Quidde grew up in [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_(city) "Bremen (city)"), read history and got involved in the activities of the [German Peace Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Peace_Society "German Peace Society") (*Deutsche Friedensgesellschaft*). In his younger years, he had already opposed Bismarck's policies. In 1881 he received his PhD at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen").

Ludwig Quidde

The fortune that Quidde inherited allowed him to devote himself entirely to politics. In 1893, he joined the [German People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_People's_Party "German People's Party") (DtVP), which was founded in 1868 and met his [anti-militarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimilitarism "Antimilitarism"), anti-Prussian, democratic and [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifist "Pacifist") expectations.

In 1894, Quidde published a 17-page [pamphlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamphlet "Pamphlet"), *Caligula. Eine Studie über römischen Caesarenwahnsinn* (*Caligula: A Study of Roman Imperial Insanity*). Containing 79 footnotes, the short essay is exclusively about the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire") of the 1st century AD. However, Quidde drew an implicit parallel between the [Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Emperor "Roman Emperor") [Caligula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caligula "Caligula") and Wilhelm II and *de facto* accused both rulers of [megalomania](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/megalomania "wiktionary:megalomania"). The author had insisted on publishing his pamphlet under his real name, which effectively ended his academic career as a historian when a periodical had a short review, which explained the parallels that otherwise might have gone unnoticed. After he had made a derogatory comment on a new medal in honour of [Wilhelm I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_I "Wilhelm I"), German Emperor from 1871 to 1888, he was criminally convicted of [lèse majesté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lèse_majesté "Lèse majesté") and sentenced to three months in [prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prison "Prison"), which he served in [Stadelheim Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadelheim_Prison "Stadelheim Prison").

## Post-war life

After the end of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War"), Quidde, like most other Germans, vehemently opposed the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles "Treaty of Versailles") but for different reasons from German [militarists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militarism "Militarism"), who hated mainly the severe restrictions laid upon the [German armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_armed_forces "German armed forces") and the impending economic disaster that they stages would be caused by payment of the high reparations that the tretaty decreed. He and other German pacifists thought ahead and hoped that US President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson "Woodrow Wilson") would win the day by pointing out that such severe conditions would already sow the seeds of a new war:

> A humiliated and torn German nation condemned to economic misery would be a constant danger to world peace, just as a protected German nation whose inalienable rights and subsistence are safeguarded would be a strong pillar of such world peace.
>
> May those who are in power today think beyond this day and consider the future of mankind. Their responsibility is enormous. Today, an altogether new order can be created for the benefit of all peoples. Short-sighted misuse of that power can ruin everything.

— Quidde, et al., Announcement of the German Peace Society", 15 November 1918

When [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") came to power in 1933, Quidde escaped to [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland") and finally settled down in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva "Geneva") for the rest of his days. He remained an optimist throughout his life. Aged 76, he published his essay "Landfriede und Weltfriede" (1934) while [militarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militarism "Militarism") was again on the rise. He believed that modern technology might serve as a deterrent from war:

> [It is] today's technological development which has turned modern war into a suicidal nightmare and which will put an end to war. This was already predicted by [Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant "Immanuel Kant"), who expected "[perpetual peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_peace "Perpetual peace")" to be established not due to the moral perfection of man but due to modern warfare, which would be so unbearable that mankind would see itself forced to guarantee everlasting peace.

Quidde died in his Swiss exile in 1941, aged 82.
