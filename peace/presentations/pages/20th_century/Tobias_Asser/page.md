**Tobias Michael Carel Asser** (Dutch pronunciation: [[ˈtoːbijɑs miˈɕɛl ˈkaːrəl ˈɑsər]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Dutch "Help:IPA/Dutch"); 28 April 1838 – 29 July 1913) was a Dutch lawyer and [legal scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_scholar "Legal scholar").
In 1911, he won the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") (together with [Alfred Fried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Fried "Alfred Fried")) for his role in the establishment of the [Permanent Court of Arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Court_of_Arbitration "Permanent Court of Arbitration") at the First [Hague Peace Conference in 1899](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Hague_Peace_Conference "First Hague Peace Conference") and for his achievements in establishing the [Hague Conference on Private International Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Conference_on_Private_International_Law "Hague Conference on Private International Law") (HCCH).

## Life

Tobias Michael Carel Asser was born on 28 April 1838 in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam "Amsterdam"), the Netherlands, in a Jewish family. He was the son of Carel Daniel Asser (1813–1885) and grandson of [Carel Asser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carel_Asser?action=edit&redlink=1 "Carel Asser") (1780–1836). He studied law at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam "University of Amsterdam") and [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University "Leiden University") and was a law professor at the University of Amsterdam.

Asser co-founded the *Revue de Droit International et de Législation Comparée* with [John Westlake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Westlake_(law_scholar) "John Westlake (law scholar)") and [Gustave Rolin-Jaequemyns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Rolin-Jaequemyns "Gustave Rolin-Jaequemyns"). He also co-founded the [Institut de Droit International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_de_Droit_International "Institut de Droit International") in 1873. In 1880 he became a member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences").

### The Hague Conference on Private International Law

Asser was a leading legal mind in the area of private international law and firmly believed that sound legal frameworks that govern private cross-border relationships would promote peace and stability. In 1893, Asser initiated the convocation of the First Diplomatic Session of the HCCH, the preeminent global organisation in the area of [private international law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_international_law "Private international law"). The participating States were Austria-Hungary, Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Portugal, Romania, Russia, Spain, and Switzerland. Asser was elected the Session's President, and subsequently re-elected at the Second to Fourth Session which took place in 1894, 1900 and 1904 respectively. Under his leadership, the HCCH developed some multilateral treaties, the Hague Conventions, that unified the rules of private international law in the areas of [Marriage (1902)](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/the-old-conventions/1902-marriage-convention), [Divorce (1902)](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/the-old-conventions/1902-divorce-convention), [Guardianship (1902)](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/the-old-conventions/1902-guardianship-convention), [Civil Procedure (1905)](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/the-old-conventions/1905-civil-procedure-convention), [Effects of Marriage (1905)](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/the-old-conventions/1905-effects-of-marriage-convention), and [Deprivation of Civil Rights (1905)](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/the-old-conventions/1905-deprivation-of-civil-rights-convention).

In 1911, Asser received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"). In his Award Ceremony Speech on 10 December 1911, Chairman of the Nobel Committee [Jørgen Gunnarsson Løvland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jørgen_Løvland "Jørgen Løvland") emphasised specifically Asser's work in the field of private international law, and his achievements in establishing the HCCH, as reasons for receiving the Nobel Peace Prize, describing Asser as "a successor to or reviver of The Netherlands' pioneer work in international law in the seventeenth century", the [Hugo Grotius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Grotius "Hugo Grotius") of his time.

### The Hague Peace Conferences

He was a delegate of the Netherlands to both [Hague Peace Conferences in 1899 and 1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Peace_Conferences "Hague Peace Conferences"), where he urged the principle of compulsory arbitration be introduced in the economic area and contributed to the creation of the [Permanent Court of Arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Court_of_Arbitration "Permanent Court of Arbitration") as the first global mechanism for the peaceful settlement of international disputes.

### The Permanent Court of Arbitration

In 1902, he sat on the first arbitration panel to hear an international controversy brought by two states under the auspice of the [Permanent Court of Arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Court_of_Arbitration "Permanent Court of Arbitration"), which was established as a result of the [Hague Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Peace_Conference "Hague Peace Conference") of 1899 (the [Pious Fund of the Californias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pious_Fund_of_the_Californias "Pious Fund of the Californias") Case). He also took a hand in the establishment of what would become [The Hague Academy of International Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague_Academy_of_International_Law "The Hague Academy of International Law"), though he did not live to see its foundation in 1923.

Asser died on 29 July 1913 in The Hague.

## Namesake

A research institute in the fields of Private and Public International Law, [European Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Law "European Law") and International Commercial Arbitration is named after Tobias Michael Carel Asser. This is the [T.M.C. Asser Instituut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T.M.C._Asser_Instituut "T.M.C. Asser Instituut"), based in [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague "The Hague"), Netherlands.
