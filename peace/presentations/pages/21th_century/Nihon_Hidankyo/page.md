The **Japan Confederation of A- and H-Bomb Sufferers Organizations**, often shortened to **Nihon Hidankyō** (日本被団協, *Nihon Hidankyō*), is a group that represents survivors (known as *[hibakusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibakusha "Hibakusha")*) of the [atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki "Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki"). It was formed in 1956.

Nihon Hidankyō [lobbies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbying "Lobbying") both the Japanese government for improved support of the victims and governments worldwide for the [abolition of nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_disarmament "Nuclear disarmament"). Their activities included recording thousands of witness accounts, issuing resolutions and public appeals, and sending annual delegations to various [international organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organisations "International organisations"), including the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"), to advocate for global nuclear disarmament.

The organisation was awarded the [2024 Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Nobel_Peace_Prize "2024 Nobel Peace Prize") "for its efforts to achieve a world free of nuclear weapons and for demonstrating through witness testimony that nuclear weapons must never be used again".

## History

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/Hibakusha.jpg/250px-Hibakusha.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hibakusha.jpg)

Nihon Hidankyo's secretary general [Terumi Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terumi_Tanaka "Terumi Tanaka") speaking to youth about surviving the atomic bombing of Nagasaki at a UN event in [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna "Vienna") in 2007

Nihon Hidankyo is a nation-wide organisation formed by survivor groups of atomic bomb victims from [Hiroshima and Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki "Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki") in each [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_of_Japan "Prefectures of Japan"). The fallout from [Castle Bravo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Bravo "Castle Bravo"), a thermonuclear weapon test conducted at [Bikini Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bikini_Atoll "Bikini Atoll") by the United States in 1954, caused acute radiation syndrome in residents of neighbouring atolls and 23 crew members of the Japanese fishing vessel *[Daigo Fukuryū Maru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daigo_Fukuryū_Maru "Daigo Fukuryū Maru")*. This led to the formation of the [Japan Council against Atomic and Hydrogen Bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Council_against_Atomic_and_Hydrogen_Bombs "Japan Council against Atomic and Hydrogen Bombs") in Hiroshima the following year. Inspired and supported by this movement, atomic bomb survivors established Nihon Hidankyo on 10 August 1956, at the second annual conference of the council in Nagasaki.

[Terumi Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terumi_Tanaka "Terumi Tanaka"), Shigemitsu Tanaka, and Toshiyuki Mimaki at the [2024 Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Nobel_Peace_Prize "2024 Nobel Peace Prize") Ceremony with [Jørgen Watne Frydnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jørgen_Watne_Frydnes "Jørgen Watne Frydnes")

However, the movement's solidarity was jeopardised when the council became actively involved in the [anti-U.S.-Japan Security Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anpo_protests "Anpo protests") movement alongside the left-leaning [Japan Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Socialist_Party "Japan Socialist Party") in 1959. A large number of supporters withdrew from the council, and with the support of the conservative [Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democratic_Party_(Japan) "Liberal Democratic Party (Japan)"), a new organisation, led by Masatoshi Matsushita, leader of the staunchly anti-communist [Democratic Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialist_Party_(Japan) "Democratic Socialist Party (Japan)"), was established. In 1961, when the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") resumed nuclear tests, the communist wing of the council refused to denounce them, which led to severe internal tension. This led to a further split in the movement, with a Japan Socialist Party-backed group that denounces nuclear tests by any nation breaking away as a new council. These tensions within [anti-nuclear movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-nuclear_movement "Anti-nuclear movement") caused some prefectural Hidankyos to split at the local level as well, such as in Hiroshima, where there are both Socialist Party-backed and Communist Party-backed Hidankyos with the same name. The nationwide organisation itself decided not to align with any political movements in 1965, after they became highly politicised.

## Activities

As of October 2024, Nihon Hidankyo's activities include:

- Advocacy for the abolition of nuclear weapons and demands for state compensations
- [Petitioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petition "Petition") actions towards the Japanese government, the United Nations and other governments
- Elimination and removal of nuclear weapons, establishment of an international [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty "Treaty") for nuclear disarmament, holding of international conferences, enactment of non-nuclear laws and enhancement of hibakusha support measures
- [Raising awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness_raising "Consciousness raising") of the realities of the atomic bombings both domestically and internationally
- Research, study, publication, exhibitions and gatherings on atomic bomb damage
- Consultation and support activities for hibakusha

## Key figures

### Current officials

#### Co-chairs

- [Terumi Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terumi_Tanaka "Terumi Tanaka"): Exposed to radiation 3.2 km away from the Nagasaki hypocentre at the age of 13; assumed office on 14 June 2017
- Shigemitsu Tanaka: Exposed to radiation 6 km away from the Nagasaki hypocentre at the age of 4; assumed office on 14 June 2018
- [Toshiyuki Mimaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshiyuki_Mimaki "Toshiyuki Mimaki"): Exposed to radiation at his home in Hiroshima at the age of 3; assumed office on 9 June 2022

#### Secretary general

- [Sueichi Kido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sueichi_Kido "Sueichi Kido"): Exposed to radiation in Nagasaki at the age of 5; assumed office on 7 June 2017

#### Assistant secretaries general

- Toshiko Hamanaka
- Jiro Hamasumi
- Michiko Kodama
- [Masako Wada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masako_Wada "Masako Wada")

### Former officials

- Ichiro Moritaki: Founding chairperson of Nihon Hidankyo. Founding chairperson of the Hiroshima Prefectural Hidankyo, a committee head of the Atomic Water Association, and the third chairperson of the Japan National Conference on the Prohibition of Atomic and Hydrogen Bombs. Died January 25, 1994.
- [Sumiteru Taniguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumiteru_Taniguchi "Sumiteru Taniguchi"): Severely injured by the Nagasaki bomb 1.8 km away from the hypocentre at the age of 16; co-chairperson until his death on 20 August 2017
- [Takeshi Ito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeshi_Ito_(activist) "Takeshi Ito (activist)"): Born in Hiroshima City. Ito was exposed to the atomic bomb during his third year at the former Hiroshima Prefectural First Middle School; co-chairperson until his death on 3 March 2000.
- [Sunao Tsuboi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunao_Tsuboi "Sunao Tsuboi"): Severely injured by the Hiroshima bomb 1.5 km away from the hypocentre at the age of 20; co-chairperson until his death on 24 October 2021
- Mikiso Iwasa: Severely injured by the Hiroshima bomb at his home 1.2 km away from the hypocentre at the age of 16; co-chairperson until his death on 7 September 2020

## Honors

- 2003: [Seán MacBride Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seán_MacBride_Peace_Prize "Seán MacBride Peace Prize")
- 2010: [Award for Social Activism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_of_Peace "Man of Peace") from the [World Summit of Nobel Peace Laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Summit_of_Nobel_Peace_Laureates "World Summit of Nobel Peace Laureates")
- 2024: [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize")

Before being awarded the Nobel Peace Prize, Nihon Hidankyo was also nominated in 1985, 1994 and 2015 by the Swiss-based [International Peace Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Peace_Bureau "International Peace Bureau").
