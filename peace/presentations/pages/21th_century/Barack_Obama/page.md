**Barack Hussein Obama II** (born August 4, 1961) is an American retired politician who served as the 44th [president of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States") from 2009 to 2017. A member of the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)"), he was the first [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans")  to serve as president. Obama represented [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois "Illinois") in the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") from 2005 to 2008 and served as an [Illinois state senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_state_senator "Illinois state senator") from 1997 to 2004.

Born in [Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu "Honolulu"), Obama graduated from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in 1983 and later worked as a community organizer in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"). In 1988, Obama enrolled at [Harvard Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Law_School "Harvard Law School"), where he was the first Black president of the *[Harvard Law Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Law_Review "Harvard Law Review")*. He became a civil rights attorney and an academic, teaching constitutional law at the [University of Chicago Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago_Law_School "University of Chicago Law School") from 1992 to 2004. In 1996, Obama was elected to [represent the 13th district in the Illinois Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Senate_career_of_Barack_Obama "Illinois Senate career of Barack Obama"). In 2000, [Obama ran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Illinois's_1st_congressional_district_election#Obama's_run "2000 Illinois's 1st congressional district election") for [Illinois's 1st congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois's_1st_congressional_district "Illinois's 1st congressional district"), but came in second to the incumbent Representative, [Bobby Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Rush "Bobby Rush"). Following his loss in the House race, he kept his position he held in the State Senate until 2004, when he [successfully ran for the U.S. Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_Senate_election_in_Illinois "2004 United States Senate election in Illinois"). In the [2008 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election "2008 United States presidential election"), after [a close primary campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries "2008 Democratic Party presidential primaries") against [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton"), he was nominated by the Democratic Party for president. Obama selected [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") as his running mate and defeated [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") nominee [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain "John McCain") and his running mate [Sarah Palin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Palin "Sarah Palin").

Obama was awarded the [2009 Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Nobel_Peace_Prize "2009 Nobel Peace Prize") for efforts in international diplomacy, a decision that drew both praise and criticism. During his first term, his administration responded to the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis "2008 financial crisis") with measures including the [American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Recovery_and_Reinvestment_Act_of_2009 "American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009") to address the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession "Great Recession"); a partial extension of the [Bush tax cuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_tax_cuts "Bush tax cuts"); [legislation to reform health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_reforms_proposed_during_the_Obama_administration "Healthcare reforms proposed during the Obama administration"); and the [Dodd–Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodd–Frank_Wall_Street_Reform_and_Consumer_Protection_Act "Dodd–Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act"), a major financial regulation reform bill. Obama also appointed [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Supreme Court of the United States") justices [Sonia Sotomayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonia_Sotomayor "Sonia Sotomayor") and [Elena Kagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Kagan "Elena Kagan"), the former being the first [Hispanic American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_American "Hispanic American") on the Supreme Court. Obama also oversaw the end of the [Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War "Iraq War") and ordered [Operation Neptune Spear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Neptune_Spear "Operation Neptune Spear"), the raid that killed [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden "Osama bin Laden"), who was the leader of [al-Qaeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda "Al-Qaeda") and was responsible for the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"). He downplayed Bush's [counterinsurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterinsurgency "Counterinsurgency") model by expanding [air strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_strikes "Air strikes") and making extensive use of special forces, while encouraging greater reliance on host-government militaries. Obama also ordered the [2011 military intervention in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_military_intervention_in_Libya "2011 military intervention in Libya") to implement [United Nations Security Council Resolution 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1973 "United Nations Security Council Resolution 1973"), which contributed to the overthrow of [Muammar Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muammar_Gaddafi "Muammar Gaddafi").

Obama defeated Republican opponent [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney "Mitt Romney") and his running mate [Paul Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ryan "Paul Ryan") in the [2012 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election "2012 United States presidential election"). In his second term, Obama advocated for [gun control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_politics_in_the_United_States "Gun politics in the United States") in the wake of the [Sandy Hook Elementary School shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Hook_Elementary_School_shooting "Sandy Hook Elementary School shooting"), took steps to [combat climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_climate_change "Combat climate change") by signing the [Paris Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Agreement "Paris Agreement") on climate change and an executive order to limit [carbon emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_emission "Carbon emission"), and presided over the implementation of the [Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_Care_Act "Affordable Care Act") and other legislation passed during his first term. Obama also initiated [sanctions against Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_Ukrainian_crisis "International sanctions during the Ukrainian crisis") following its [invasion in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_military_intervention_in_Ukraine_(2014–present) "Russian military intervention in Ukraine (2014–present)") and again after [Russian interference in the 2016 U.S. elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_interference_in_the_2016_U.S._elections "Russian interference in the 2016 U.S. elections"). He ordered [military intervention in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American-led_intervention_in_Iraq_(2014–present) "American-led intervention in Iraq (2014–present)") after gains made by [ISIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISIL "ISIL") following the 2011 withdrawal from Iraq, negotiated the [Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Comprehensive_Plan_of_Action "Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action") (a nuclear agreement with Iran), and [normalized relations with Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_thaw "Cuban thaw"). The number of [American soldiers in Afghanistan decreased during Obama's second term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_United_States_troops_from_Afghanistan_(2011–2016) "Withdrawal of United States troops from Afghanistan (2011–2016)"), though U.S. soldiers remained in the country throughout his presidency. He also promoted inclusion for [LGBTQ Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_Americans "LGBTQ Americans"), becoming the first sitting U.S. president to publicly support [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_the_United_States "Same-sex marriage in the United States").

Obama has continued to be politically active since he left office in 2017, campaigning for candidates in American elections, including Biden's [successful presidential bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden_2020_presidential_campaign "Joe Biden 2020 presidential campaign") in the [2020 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election "2020 United States presidential election"). Outside politics, [Obama has published three books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliography_of_Barack_Obama "Bibliography of Barack Obama"): *[Dreams from My Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_from_My_Father "Dreams from My Father")* (1995)*, [The Audacity of Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Audacity_of_Hope "The Audacity of Hope")* (2006), and *[A Promised Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Promised_Land "A Promised Land")* (2020). Historians and political scientists rank Obama highly in [historical rankings of U.S. presidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_rankings_of_presidents_of_the_United_States#Scholar_survey_summary "Historical rankings of presidents of the United States"). As of 2026, he is the [youngest-living former U.S. president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_United_States_by_age "List of presidents of the United States by age").

## Early life and career

[![Photo of a young Obama sitting on grass with his grandfather, mother, and half-sister.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/3/33/Ann_Dunham_with_father_and_children.jpg/250px-Ann_Dunham_with_father_and_children.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ann_Dunham_with_father_and_children.jpg)

Obama (right) with grandfather [Stanley Armour Dunham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Armour_Dunham "Stanley Armour Dunham"), mother [Ann Dunham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Dunham "Ann Dunham"), and half-sister [Maya Soetoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Soetoro "Maya Soetoro"), mid-1970s in [Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu "Honolulu")

Barack Hussein Obama II was born on August 4, 1961, at [Kapiolani Medical Center for Women and Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapiolani_Medical_Center_for_Women_and_Children "Kapiolani Medical Center for Women and Children") in Honolulu, Hawaii. He is the only president born outside the [contiguous United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contiguous_United_States "Contiguous United States"). He was born to an 18-year-old American mother and a 27-year-old Kenyan father. His mother, [Ann Dunham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Dunham "Ann Dunham") (1942–1995), was born in [Wichita, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita,_Kansas "Wichita, Kansas"), and was of English, Welsh, German, Swiss, and Irish descent. In 2007 it was discovered her great-great-grandfather Falmouth Kearney emigrated from the village of [Moneygall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moneygall "Moneygall"), Ireland to the U.S. in 1850. In July 2012, [Ancestry.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestry.com "Ancestry.com") found a strong likelihood that Dunham was descended from [John Punch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Punch_(slave) "John Punch (slave)"), an enslaved African man who lived in the [Colony of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia "Colony of Virginia") during the seventeenth century. Obama has described the ancestors of his grandparents as [Scotch-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch-Irish_Americans "Scotch-Irish Americans") mostly. Obama's father, [Barack Hussein Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama_Sr. "Barack Obama Sr.") (1934–1982), was a married [Luo Kenyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luo_people_of_Kenya_and_Tanzania "Luo people of Kenya and Tanzania") from [Nyang'oma Kogelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyang'oma_Kogelo "Nyang'oma Kogelo"). His last name, Obama, was derived from his Luo descent. Obama's parents met in 1960 in a Russian language class at the [University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawaiʻi_at_Mānoa "University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa"), where his father was a foreign student on a scholarship. The couple married in [Wailuku, Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wailuku,_Hawaii "Wailuku, Hawaii"), on February 2, 1961, six months before Obama was born.

In late August 1961, a few weeks after he was born, Barack and his mother moved to the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington "University of Washington") in [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle"), where they lived for a year. During that time, Barack's father completed his undergraduate degree in economics in Hawaii, graduating in June 1962. He left to attend graduate school on a scholarship at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), where he earned a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") in economics. Obama's parents divorced in March 1964. Obama Sr. returned to [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya") in 1964, where he married for a third time and worked for the Kenyan government as the senior economic analyst in the Ministry of Finance. He visited his son in Hawaii only once, at Christmas 1971, before he was killed in an automobile accident in 1982, when Obama was 21 years old. Recalling his early childhood, Obama said: "That my father looked nothing like the people around me—that he was black as pitch, my mother white as milk—barely registered in my mind." He described his struggles as a young adult to reconcile social perceptions of his multiracial heritage.

In 1963, Dunham met [Lolo Soetoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lolo_Soetoro "Lolo Soetoro") at the [University of Hawaiʻi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawaiʻi "University of Hawaiʻi"); he was an Indonesian [East–West Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East–West_Center "East–West Center") graduate student in geography. The couple married on [Molokai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molokai "Molokai") on March 15, 1965. After two one-year extensions of his [J-1 visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-1_visa "J-1 visa"), Lolo returned to Indonesia in 1966. His wife and stepson followed sixteen months later in 1967. The family initially lived in the Menteng Dalam neighborhood in the [Tebet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tebet,_South_Jakarta "Tebet, South Jakarta") district of [South Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jakarta "South Jakarta"). From 1970, they lived in a wealthier neighborhood in the [Menteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menteng "Menteng") district of [Central Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Jakarta "Central Jakarta").

### Education

[![Scan of Obama's elementary school record, where he is wrongly recorded as Indonesian and Muslim.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Barry_Soetoro_school_record.jpg/250px-Barry_Soetoro_school_record.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Barry_Soetoro_school_record.jpg)

Obama's Indonesian school record in St. Francis of Assisi Catholic Elementary School. He was enrolled as "Barry [Soetoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lolo_Soetoro "Lolo Soetoro")" (no. 1), and wrongly recorded as an Indonesian citizen (no. 3) and a Muslim (no. 4).

When he was six years old, Obama and his mother moved to Indonesia to join his stepfather. From age six to ten, he was registered in school as "Barry" and attended local [Indonesian-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian-language "Indonesian-language") schools: *Sekolah Dasar Katolik Santo Fransiskus Asisi* (St. Francis of Assisi Catholic Elementary School) for two years and [*Sekolah Dasar Negeri Menteng 01*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Elementary_School_Menteng_01 "State Elementary School Menteng 01") (State Elementary School Menteng 01) for one and a half years, supplemented by English-language [Calvert School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvert_School "Calvert School") homeschooling by his mother. As a result of his four years in [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta "Jakarta"), he was able to speak [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language "Indonesian language") fluently as a child. During his time in Indonesia, Obama's stepfather taught him to be resilient and gave him "a pretty hardheaded assessment of how the world works".

In 1971, Obama returned to Honolulu to live with his maternal grandparents, [Madelyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madelyn_Dunham "Madelyn Dunham") and [Stanley Dunham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Armour_Dunham "Stanley Armour Dunham"). He attended [Punahou School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punahou_School "Punahou School")—a private [college preparatory school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_preparatory_school "College preparatory school")—with the aid of a scholarship from fifth grade until he graduated from high school in 1979. In high school, Obama continued to use the nickname "Barry" which he kept until making a visit to Kenya in 1980. Obama lived with his mother and half-sister, [Maya Soetoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Soetoro "Maya Soetoro"), in Hawaii for three years from 1972 to 1975 while his mother was a graduate student in [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology "Anthropology") at the University of Hawaii. Obama chose to stay in Hawaii when his mother and half-sister returned to Indonesia in 1975, so his mother could begin anthropology field work. His mother spent most of the next two decades in Indonesia, divorcing Lolo Soetoro in 1980 and earning a PhD degree in 1992, before dying in 1995 in Hawaii following unsuccessful treatment for [ovarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovarian_cancer "Ovarian cancer") and [uterine cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterine_cancer "Uterine cancer").

Of his years in Honolulu, Obama wrote: "The opportunity that Hawaii offered — to experience a variety of cultures in a climate of mutual respect — became an integral part of my world view, and a basis for the values that I hold most dear." Obama has also written and talked about using [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_(drug) "Alcohol (drug)"), [marijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marijuana "Marijuana"), and [cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine "Cocaine") during his teenage years to "push questions of who I was out of my mind". Obama was also a member of the "Choom Gang" (the slang term for smoking marijuana), a self-named group of friends who spent time together and smoked marijuana.

#### College and research jobs

After graduating from high school in 1979, Obama moved to Los Angeles to attend [Occidental College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occidental_College "Occidental College") on a full scholarship. In February 1981, Obama made his first public speech, calling for Occidental to participate in the [disinvestment from South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disinvestment_from_South_Africa "Disinvestment from South Africa") in response to that nation's policy of [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid"). In mid-1981, Obama traveled to Indonesia to visit his mother and half-sister Maya and visited the families of college friends in [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") for three weeks. Later in 1981, he [transferred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_transfer "College transfer") to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in New York City as a [junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_(education_year) "Junior (education year)"), where he majored in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science "Political science") with a specialty in [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations "International relations") and in [English literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_literature "English literature") and lived off-campus on West 109th Street. He graduated with a Bachelor of Arts degree in 1983 and a 3.7 [GPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grading_(education)#United_States "Grading (education)"). After graduating, Obama worked for about a year at the [Business International Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_International_Corporation "Business International Corporation"), where he was a financial researcher and writer, then as a project coordinator for the [New York Public Interest Research Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Public_Interest_Research_Group "New York Public Interest Research Group") on the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York "City College of New York") campus for three months in 1985.

#### Community organizer and Harvard Law School

| External videos | |
| --- | --- |
| [video icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nuvola_apps_kaboodle.svg) [Derrick Bell threatens to leave Harvard](http://bostonlocaltv.org/catalog/V_UDAMVZGA4JEY06N), April 24, 1990, 11:34, [Boston TV Digital Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WGBH_Educational_Foundation "WGBH Educational Foundation") Student Barack Obama introduces Professor Derrick Bell starting at 6:25. | |

Two years after graduating from Columbia, Obama moved from New York to Chicago when he was hired as director of the [Developing Communities Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_Communities_Project "Developing Communities Project"), a faith-based community organization originally comprising eight Catholic parishes in [Roseland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roseland,_Chicago "Roseland, Chicago"), [West Pullman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pullman "West Pullman"), and [Riverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Chicago "Riverdale, Chicago") on Chicago's [South Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Side,_Chicago "South Side, Chicago"). He worked there as a community organizer from June 1985 to May 1988. He helped set up a job training program, a college preparatory tutoring program, and a tenants' rights organization in [Altgeld Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altgeld_Gardens_Homes_(Chicago,_Illinois) "Altgeld Gardens Homes (Chicago, Illinois)"). Obama also worked as a consultant and instructor for the [Gamaliel Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamaliel_Foundation "Gamaliel Foundation"), a community organizing institute. In mid-1988, he traveled for the first time in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe") for three weeks and then for five weeks in Kenya, where he met many of his [paternal relatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jael_Obama "Jael Obama") for the first time.

Despite being offered a full scholarship to [Northwestern University School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University_School_of_Law "Northwestern University School of Law"), Obama enrolled at [Harvard Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Law_School "Harvard Law School") in the fall of 1988, living in nearby [Somerville, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerville,_Massachusetts "Somerville, Massachusetts"). He was selected as an editor of the *[Harvard Law Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Law_Review "Harvard Law Review")* at the end of his first year, president of the journal in his second year, and research assistant to the constitutional scholar [Laurence Tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Tribe "Laurence Tribe") while at Harvard. During his summers, he returned to Chicago, where he worked as a [summer associate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_attorney#Summer_associates "Associate attorney") at the law firms of [Sidley Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidley_Austin "Sidley Austin") in 1989 and [Hopkins & Sutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopkins_&_Sutter "Hopkins & Sutter") in 1990. Obama's election as the [first black president of the *Harvard Law Review*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African-American_firsts "List of African-American firsts") gained national media attention and led to a publishing contract and advance for a book about race relations, which evolved into a personal memoir. The manuscript was published in mid-1995 as *[Dreams from My Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_from_My_Father "Dreams from My Father")*. Obama graduated from Harvard Law in 1991 with a [Juris Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor "Juris Doctor") *[magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honors "Latin honors")*.

#### University of Chicago Law School

In 1991, Obama accepted a two-year position as Visiting Law and Government Fellow at the [University of Chicago Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago_Law_School "University of Chicago Law School") to work on his first book. He then taught [constitutional law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_law "Constitutional law") at the University of Chicago Law School for twelve years, first as a lecturer from 1992 to 1996, and then as a senior lecturer from 1996 to 2004.

From April to October 1992, Obama directed Illinois's [Project Vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Vote "Project Vote"), a [voter registration campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_registration_campaign "Voter registration campaign") with ten staffers and seven hundred volunteer registrars; it achieved its goal of registering 150,000 of 400,000 unregistered African Americans in the state, leading *[Crain's Chicago Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crain's_Chicago_Business "Crain's Chicago Business")* to name Obama to its 1993 list of "40 under Forty" powers to be.

### Family and personal life

[![Picture of Obama, his wife, and their two daughters smiling at the camera. Obama wears a dress shirt and tie.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Obama_family_portrait_in_the_Green_Room.jpg/250px-Obama_family_portrait_in_the_Green_Room.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Obama_family_portrait_in_the_Green_Room.jpg)

Obama poses in the [Green Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Room_(White_House) "Green Room (White House)") of the White House with wife [Michelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Obama "Michelle Obama") and daughters Sasha and Malia in September 2009.

In a 2006 interview, Obama highlighted the diversity of [his extended family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_of_Barack_Obama "Family of Barack Obama"): "It's like a little mini-United Nations," he said. "I've got relatives who look like [Bernie Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Mac "Bernie Mac"), and I've got relatives who look like [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher "Margaret Thatcher")." Obama has a half-sister with whom he was raised (Maya Soetoro-Ng) and seven other half-siblings from his Kenyan father's family. Obama's mother was survived by her Kansas-born mother, Madelyn Dunham, until her death on November 2, 2008, two days before his election to the presidency. Obama also has roots in Ireland; he met with his Irish cousins in [Moneygall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moneygall "Moneygall") in May 2011. In *[Dreams from My Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_from_My_Father "Dreams from My Father")*, Obama ties his mother's family history to possible Native American ancestors and distant relatives of [Jefferson Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Davis "Jefferson Davis"), [President of the Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Confederate_States_of_America "President of the Confederate States of America") during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"). He also shares distant ancestors in common with [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") and [Dick Cheney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Cheney "Dick Cheney"), among others.

While studying in California, Obama met Alexandra McNear. The pair wrote letters to each other while in a long-distance relationship between 1982 and 1984. The letters were acquired by [Emory University's Rose Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University_Libraries#Stuart_A._Rose_Manuscript,_Archives,_and_Rare_Book_Library_(MARBL) "Emory University Libraries") in 2014 and made public in 2017. Obama wrote of his sense of isolation:

> Caught without a class, a structure, or a tradition to support me, in a sense the choice to take a different path is made for me... The only way to assuage my feelings of isolation are to absorb all the traditions, classes, make them mine, me theirs.

In one of his letters Obama described homosexuality as "an attempt to remove oneself from the present, a refusal perhaps to perpetuate the endless farce of earthly life". Describing his own mind as "androgynous", he wrote "I make love to men daily, but in the imagination."

Obama lived with anthropologist [Sheila Miyoshi Jager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheila_Miyoshi_Jager "Sheila Miyoshi Jager") while he was a community organizer in Chicago in the 1980s. He proposed to her twice, but both Jager and her parents turned him down. The relationship was not made public until May 2017, several months after his presidency had ended.

In June 1989, Obama met [Michelle Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Robinson "Michelle Robinson") when he was employed at [Sidley Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidley_Austin "Sidley Austin"). Robinson was assigned for three months as Obama's adviser at the firm, and she joined him at several group social functions but declined his initial requests to date. They began dating later that summer, became engaged in 1991, and were married on October 3, 1992. After suffering a miscarriage, Michelle underwent [in vitro fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro_fertilization "In vitro fertilization") to conceive their children. The couple's first daughter, Malia Ann, was born in 1998, followed by a second daughter, Natasha ("Sasha"), in 2001. The Obama daughters attended the [University of Chicago Laboratory Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago_Laboratory_Schools "University of Chicago Laboratory Schools"). When they moved to Washington, D.C., in January 2009, the girls started at the [Sidwell Friends School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidwell_Friends_School "Sidwell Friends School"). The Obamas had two [Portuguese Water Dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Water_Dog "Portuguese Water Dog"); the first, a male named [Bo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bo_(dog) "Bo (dog)"), was a gift from Senator [Ted Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kennedy "Ted Kennedy"). In 2013, Bo was joined by [Sunny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunny_(dog) "Sunny (dog)"), a female. Bo died of cancer on May 8, 2021, and Sunny died on September 15, 2026, although the cause of death is currently unknown.

Obama is a supporter of the [Chicago White Sox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_White_Sox "Chicago White Sox"), and he threw out the first pitch at the [2005 ALCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_ALCS "2005 ALCS") when he was still a senator. In 2009, he threw out the ceremonial first pitch at the [All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Major_League_Baseball_All-Star_Game "2009 Major League Baseball All-Star Game") while wearing a White Sox jacket. He is also primarily a [Chicago Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Bears "Chicago Bears") football fan in the [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL "NFL"), but in his childhood and adolescence was a [fan of the Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steeler_Nation "Steeler Nation") and rooted for them ahead of their victory in [Super Bowl XLIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLIII "Super Bowl XLIII") 12 days after he took office as president. In 2011, Obama invited the [1985 Chicago Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Chicago_Bears "1985 Chicago Bears") to the White House; the team had not visited the White House after their [Super Bowl win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XX "Super Bowl XX") in 1986 due to the [Space Shuttle Challenger disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger_disaster "Space Shuttle Challenger disaster"). He plays [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball "Basketball"), a sport he participated in as a member of his high school's varsity team, and he is left-handed.

In 2005, the Obama family applied the proceeds of a book deal and moved from a [Hyde Park, Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Chicago "Hyde Park, Chicago") condominium to a $1.6 million house (equivalent to $2.6 million in 2025) in neighboring [Kenwood, Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenwood,_Chicago "Kenwood, Chicago"). The purchase of an adjacent lot—and sale of part of it to Obama by the wife of developer, campaign donor and friend [Tony Rezko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Rezko "Tony Rezko")—attracted media attention because of Rezko's subsequent indictment and conviction on political corruption charges that were unrelated to Obama.

In December 2007, *[Money Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_Magazine "Money Magazine")* estimated Obama's net worth at $1.3 million (equivalent to $2 million in 2025). Their 2009 tax return showed a household income of $5.5 million—up from about $4.2 million in 2007 and $1.6 million in 2005—mostly from sales of his books. On his 2010 income of $1.7 million, he gave 14 percent to non-profit organizations, including $131,000 to [Fisher House Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_House_Foundation "Fisher House Foundation"), a charity assisting wounded veterans' families, allowing them to reside near where the veteran is receiving medical treatments. Per his 2012 financial disclosure, Obama may be worth as much as $10 million.

### Religious views

[![Obama and his wife standing in a crowded Church, looking forward, with their mouths open mid-sentence while reciting a prayer](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Obamas_at_church_on_Inauguration_Day_2013.jpg/250px-Obamas_at_church_on_Inauguration_Day_2013.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Obamas_at_church_on_Inauguration_Day_2013.jpg)

The Obamas worship at [African Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Methodist_Episcopal_Church "African Methodist Episcopal Church") in Washington, D.C., January 2013

Obama is a [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant "Protestant") Christian whose religious views developed in his adult life. He wrote in *[The Audacity of Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Audacity_of_Hope "The Audacity of Hope")* that he "was not raised in a religious household." He described his mother, raised by non-religious parents, as being detached from religion, yet "in many ways the most spiritually awakened person ... I have ever known", and "a lonely witness for [secular humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular_humanism "Secular humanism")." He described his father as a "confirmed [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist")" by the time his parents met, and his stepfather as "a man who saw religion as not particularly useful." Obama explained how, through working with [black churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_church "Black church") as a [community organizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_organizer "Community organizer") while in his twenties, he came to understand "the power of the African-American religious tradition to spur social change."

In January 2008, Obama told *[Christianity Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_Today "Christianity Today")*: "I am a Christian, and I am a devout Christian. I believe in the [redemptive death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redeemer_(Christianity) "Redeemer (Christianity)") and [resurrection of Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection_of_Jesus_Christ "Resurrection of Jesus Christ"). I believe that faith gives me a path to be cleansed of sin and have eternal life." On September 27, 2010, Obama released a statement commenting on his religious views, saying:

> I'm a Christian by choice. My family didn't—frankly, they weren't folks who went to church every week. And my mother was one of the most spiritual people I knew, but she didn't raise me in the church. So I came to my Christian faith later in life, and it was because the precepts of [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ "Jesus Christ") spoke to me in terms of the kind of life that I would want to lead—being my brothers' and sisters' keeper, [treating others as they would treat me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Rule "Golden Rule").

Obama met [Trinity United Church of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_United_Church_of_Christ "Trinity United Church of Christ") pastor [Jeremiah Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremiah_Wright "Jeremiah Wright") in October 1987 and became a member of Trinity in 1992. During Obama's first presidential campaign in May 2008, he resigned from Trinity after [some of Wright's statements were criticized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremiah_Wright_controversy "Jeremiah Wright controversy"). Since moving to Washington, D.C., in 2009, the Obama family has attended several Protestant churches, including [Shiloh Baptist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiloh_Baptist_Church_(Washington,_D.C.) "Shiloh Baptist Church (Washington, D.C.)") and [St. John's Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John's_Episcopal_Church,_Lafayette_Square "St. John's Episcopal Church, Lafayette Square"), as well as Evergreen Chapel at [Camp David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_David "Camp David"), but the members of the family do not attend church on a regular basis.

In 2016, Obama said that he gets inspiration from a few items that remind him "of all the different people I've met along the way", adding: "I carry these around all the time. I'm not that superstitious, so it's not like I think I necessarily have to have them on me at all times." The items, "a whole bowl full", include rosary beads given to him by [Pope Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Francis "Pope Francis"), a figurine of the Hindu deity [Hanuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman "Hanuman"), a [Coptic cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_cross "Coptic cross") from Ethiopia, a small [Buddha statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha_statue "Buddha statue") given by a monk, and a metal poker chip that used to be the lucky charm of a motorcyclist in Iowa.

## Legal career

From 1994 to 2002, Obama served on the boards of directors of the [Woods Fund of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woods_Fund_of_Chicago "Woods Fund of Chicago")—which in 1985 had been the first foundation to fund the Developing Communities Project—and of the [Joyce Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyce_Foundation "Joyce Foundation"). He served on the board of directors of the [Chicago Annenberg Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Annenberg_Challenge "Chicago Annenberg Challenge") from 1995 to 2002, as founding president and chairman of the board of directors from 1995 to 1999. Obama's law license became inactive in 2007.

## Legislative career

### Illinois Senate (1997–2004)

[![Photo of Obama and others carrying a streetsign that reads "Honorary: Milton Davis Blvd."](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Obamamiltondavis1.jpg/250px-Obamamiltondavis1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Obamamiltondavis1.jpg)

State senator Obama and others celebrate the naming of a street in Chicago after [ShoreBank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ShoreBank "ShoreBank") co-founder Milton Davis in 1998.

Obama was elected to the [Illinois Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Senate "Illinois Senate") in 1996, succeeding Democratic state senator [Alice Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Palmer_(politician) "Alice Palmer (politician)") from [Illinois's 13th District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois's_13th_Senate_district "Illinois's 13th Senate district"), which, at that time, spanned Chicago South Side neighborhoods from Hyde Park–Kenwood south to [South Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Shore,_Chicago "South Shore, Chicago") and west to [Chicago Lawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Lawn "Chicago Lawn"). Once elected, Obama gained bipartisan support for legislation that reformed ethics and health care laws. He sponsored a law that increased [tax credits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_credit "Tax credit") for low-income workers, negotiated [welfare reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_reform "Welfare reform"), and promoted increased subsidies for childcare. In 2001, as co-chairman of the bipartisan Joint Committee on Administrative Rules, Obama supported Republican governor [George Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Ryan "George Ryan")'s [payday loan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payday_loan "Payday loan") regulations and [predatory mortgage lending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predatory_mortgage_lending "Predatory mortgage lending") regulations aimed at averting home [foreclosures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreclosure "Foreclosure").

He was reelected to the Illinois Senate in 1998, defeating Republican Yesse Yehudah in the general election, and was re-elected again in 2002. In 2000, he lost a [Democratic primary race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Illinois's_1st_congressional_district_election "2000 Illinois's 1st congressional district election") for [Illinois's 1st congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois's_1st_congressional_district "Illinois's 1st congressional district") in the [United States House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives") to four-term incumbent [Bobby Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Rush "Bobby Rush") by a margin of two to one.

In January 2003, Obama became chairman of the Illinois Senate's Health and Human Services Committee when Democrats, after a decade in the minority, regained a majority. He sponsored and led unanimous, bipartisan passage of legislation to monitor [racial profiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_profiling "Racial profiling") by requiring police to record the race of drivers they detained, and legislation making Illinois the first state to mandate videotaping of homicide interrogations. During his 2004 general election campaign for the U.S. Senate, police representatives credited Obama for his active engagement with police organizations in enacting [death penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_the_United_States "Capital punishment in the United States") reforms. Obama resigned from the Illinois Senate in November 2004 following his election to the U.S. Senate.

### 2004 U.S. Senate campaign in Illinois

In May 2002, Obama commissioned a poll to assess his prospects in a 2004 U.S. Senate race. He created a campaign committee, began raising funds, and lined up political media consultant [David Axelrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Axelrod "David Axelrod") by August 2002. Obama formally announced his candidacy in January 2003.

Obama was an early opponent of the George W. Bush administration's [2003 invasion of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_invasion_of_Iraq "2003 invasion of Iraq"). On October 2, 2002, the day President Bush and Congress agreed on the [joint resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Resolution "Iraq Resolution") authorizing the [Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War "Iraq War"), Obama addressed the first high-profile Chicago [anti-Iraq War rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protests_against_the_Iraq_War "Protests against the Iraq War"), and spoke out against the war. He addressed another anti-war rally in March 2003 and told the crowd "it's not too late" to stop the war.

Decisions by Republican incumbent [Peter Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Fitzgerald_(politician) "Peter Fitzgerald (politician)") and his Democratic predecessor [Carol Moseley Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Moseley_Braun "Carol Moseley Braun") not to participate in the election resulted in wide-open Democratic and Republican primary contests involving 15 candidates. In the March 2004 primary election, Obama won in an unexpected landslide—which overnight made him a rising star within the national Democratic Party, started speculation about a presidential future, and led to the reissue of his memoir, *Dreams from My Father*. In July 2004, Obama delivered [the keynote address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Democratic_National_Convention_keynote_address "2004 Democratic National Convention keynote address") at the [2004 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Democratic_National_Convention "2004 Democratic National Convention"), seen by nine million viewers. His speech was well received and elevated his status within the Democratic Party.

Obama's expected opponent in the general election, Republican primary winner [Jack Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Ryan_(politician) "Jack Ryan (politician)"), withdrew from the race in June 2004. Six weeks later, [Alan Keyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Keyes "Alan Keyes") accepted the Republican nomination to replace Ryan. In the [November 2004 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_Senate_election_in_Illinois "2004 United States Senate election in Illinois"), Obama won with 70 percent of the vote, the largest margin of victory for a Senate candidate in Illinois history. He took 92 of the state's 102 counties, including several where Democrats traditionally do not perform well.

### U.S. Senate (2005–2008)

[![Photo of Obama smiling with his arms crossed, with the Capitol building and the sky in the background](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/BarackObamaportrait.jpg/250px-BarackObamaportrait.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:BarackObamaportrait.jpg)

Official Senate portrait, 2005

Obama was sworn in as a senator on January 3, 2005, becoming the only Senate member of the [Congressional Black Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Black_Caucus "Congressional Black Caucus"). He introduced two initiatives that bore his name: Lugar–Obama, which expanded the [Nunn–Lugar Cooperative Threat Reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunn–Lugar_Cooperative_Threat_Reduction "Nunn–Lugar Cooperative Threat Reduction") concept to conventional weapons; and the [Federal Funding Accountability and Transparency Act of 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Funding_Accountability_and_Transparency_Act_of_2006 "Federal Funding Accountability and Transparency Act of 2006"), which authorized the establishment of USAspending.gov, a web search engine on federal spending. On June 3, 2008, Senator Obama—along with Senators [Tom Carper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Carper "Tom Carper"), [Tom Coburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Coburn "Tom Coburn"), and [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain "John McCain")—introduced follow-up legislation: Strengthening Transparency and Accountability in Federal Spending Act of 2008. He also [cosponsored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponsor_(legislative) "Sponsor (legislative)") the [Secure America and Orderly Immigration Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_America_and_Orderly_Immigration_Act "Secure America and Orderly Immigration Act").

In December 2006, President Bush signed into law the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo") Relief, Security, and Democracy Promotion Act, marking the first federal legislation to be enacted with Obama as its primary sponsor. In January 2007, Obama and Senator Feingold introduced a corporate jet provision to the [Honest Leadership and Open Government Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honest_Leadership_and_Open_Government_Act "Honest Leadership and Open Government Act"), which was signed into law in September 2007.

Later in 2007, Obama sponsored an amendment to the Defense Authorization Act to add safeguards for personality-disorder military discharges. This amendment passed the full Senate in the spring of 2008. He sponsored the Iran Sanctions Enabling Act supporting divestment of state pension funds from Iran's oil and gas industry, which was never enacted but later incorporated in the [Comprehensive Iran Sanctions, Accountability, and Divestment Act of 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Iran_Sanctions,_Accountability,_and_Divestment_Act_of_2010 "Comprehensive Iran Sanctions, Accountability, and Divestment Act of 2010"); and co-sponsored legislation to reduce risks of nuclear terrorism. Obama also sponsored a Senate amendment to the [State Children's Health Insurance Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Children's_Health_Insurance_Program "State Children's Health Insurance Program"), providing one year of job protection for family members caring for soldiers with combat-related injuries.

Obama held assignments on the Senate Committees for [Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Foreign_Relations "United States Senate Committee on Foreign Relations"), [Environment and Public Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Environment_and_Public_Works "United States Senate Committee on Environment and Public Works"), and [Veterans' Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Veterans'_Affairs "United States Senate Committee on Veterans' Affairs") through December 2006. In January 2007, he left the Environment and Public Works committee and took additional assignments with [Health, Education, Labor and Pensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Health,_Education,_Labor_and_Pensions "United States Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor and Pensions") and [Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Homeland_Security_and_Governmental_Affairs "United States Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs"). He also became Chairman of the Senate's subcommittee on [European Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Foreign_Relations_Subcommittee_on_Europe_and_Regional_Security_Cooperation "United States Senate Foreign Relations Subcommittee on Europe and Regional Security Cooperation"). As a member of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, Obama made official trips to Eastern Europe, the Middle East, Central Asia, and Africa. He met with [Mahmoud Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Abbas "Mahmoud Abbas") before Abbas became [President of the Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Palestinian_National_Authority "President of the Palestinian National Authority") and gave a speech at the [University of Nairobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nairobi "University of Nairobi") in which he condemned corruption within the Kenyan government. Obama [resigned his Senate seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resignation_from_the_United_States_Senate "Resignation from the United States Senate") on November 16, 2008, to focus on his transition period for the presidency.

## Presidency (2009–2017)

### 2008 presidential candidacy

[![Results for the 2008 United States presidential election, depicting Obama winning many states in the Northeast, Midwest, and Pacific West, and Florida, and McCain winning many states in the South and Rocky Mountains.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/24/ElectoralCollege2008.svg/330px-ElectoralCollege2008.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ElectoralCollege2008.svg)

2008 electoral vote results. Obama won 365–173.

On February 10, 2007, Obama announced his candidacy for President of the United States in front of the [Old State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_State_Capitol_State_Historic_Site_(Illinois) "Old State Capitol State Historic Site (Illinois)") building in [Springfield, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Illinois "Springfield, Illinois"). The choice of the announcement site was viewed as symbolic, as it was also where [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln "Abraham Lincoln") delivered his ["House Divided"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln's_House_Divided_Speech "Lincoln's House Divided Speech") speech in 1858. Obama emphasized issues of rapidly ending the Iraq War, increasing [energy independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy_of_the_United_States "Energy policy of the United States"), and [reforming the health care system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_reform_in_the_United_States "Health care reform in the United States").

Numerous candidates entered the [Democratic Party presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries "2008 Democratic Party presidential primaries"). The field narrowed to Obama and Senator [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton") after early contests, with the race remaining close throughout the primary process, but Obama gained a steady lead in pledged [delegates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delegate_(American_politics) "Delegate (American politics)") due to better long-range planning, superior fundraising, dominant organizing in [caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucus "Caucus") states, and better exploitation of delegate allocation rules.
On June 2, 2008, Obama had received enough votes to clinch his nomination. After an initial hesitation to concede, on June 7, Clinton ended her campaign and endorsed Obama. On August 23, 2008, Obama announced his [selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Democratic_Party_vice_presidential_candidate_selection "2008 Democratic Party vice presidential candidate selection") of [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware "Delaware") senator [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") as his vice presidential running mate. Obama selected Biden from a field speculated to include former Indiana governor and senator [Evan Bayh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Bayh "Evan Bayh") and Virginia governor [Tim Kaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Kaine "Tim Kaine"). At the [Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Democratic_National_Convention "2008 Democratic National Convention") in [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver "Denver"), Colorado, Hillary Clinton called for her supporters to endorse Obama, and she and [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") gave convention speeches in his support. Obama delivered his acceptance speech at [Invesco Field at Mile High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invesco_Field_at_Mile_High "Invesco Field at Mile High") stadium to a crowd of about eighty-four thousand; the speech was viewed by over three million people worldwide. During both the primary process and the general election, Obama's campaign set numerous fundraising records, particularly in the quantity of small donations. On June 19, 2008, Obama became the first major-party presidential candidate to turn down [public financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_finance_in_the_United_States#Public_financing_of_campaigns "Campaign finance in the United States") in the general election since the system was created in 1976.

[John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain "John McCain") was nominated as the Republican candidate, and he selected [Sarah Palin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Palin "Sarah Palin") as his running mate. Obama and McCain engaged in three [presidential debates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_election_debates "United States presidential election debates") in September and October 2008. On November 4, Obama won the presidency with 365 [electoral votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_(United_States) "Electoral College (United States)") to 173 received by McCain. Obama won 52.9 percent of the [popular vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election "Election") to McCain's 45.7 percent. He became the first African-American to be elected president. Obama delivered [his victory speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama_election_victory_speech,_2008 "Barack Obama election victory speech, 2008") before hundreds of thousands of supporters in Chicago's [Grant Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Park_(Chicago) "Grant Park (Chicago)"). He is one of three United States senators who moved directly from the U.S. Senate to the White House, the others being [Warren G. Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_G._Harding "Warren G. Harding") and [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy").

### 2012 presidential candidacy

[![Results for the 2012 United States presidential election, depicting Obama winning many states in the Northeast, Midwest, and Pacific West, and Florida, and Romney winning many states in the South and Rocky Mountains.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/ElectoralCollege2012.svg/330px-ElectoralCollege2012.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ElectoralCollege2012.svg)

2012 electoral vote results. Obama won 332–206.

On April 4, 2011, Obama filed election papers with the [Federal Election Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Election_Commission "Federal Election Commission") and then announced his reelection campaign for 2012 in a video titled "It Begins with Us" that he posted on his website. As the incumbent president, he ran virtually unopposed in the [Democratic Party presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries,_2012 "Democratic Party presidential primaries, 2012"), and on April 3, 2012, Obama secured the 2778 convention delegates needed to win the Democratic nomination. At the [Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Democratic_National_Convention "2012 Democratic National Convention") in [Charlotte, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte,_North_Carolina "Charlotte, North Carolina"), Obama and Joe Biden were formally nominated by former president Bill Clinton as the Democratic Party candidates for president and vice president in the general election. Their main opponents were Republicans [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney "Mitt Romney"), the former governor of Massachusetts, and Representative [Paul Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ryan "Paul Ryan") of Wisconsin.

On November 6, 2012, Obama won 332 electoral votes, exceeding the 270 required for him to be reelected as president. With 51.1 percent of the popular vote, Obama became the first Democratic president since [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt") to win the [majority of the popular vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_presidential_elections_by_popular_vote_margin "List of United States presidential elections by popular vote margin") twice. Obama addressed supporters and volunteers at Chicago's [McCormick Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCormick_Place "McCormick Place") after his reelection and said: "Tonight you voted for action, not politics as usual. You elected us to focus on your jobs, not ours. And in the coming weeks and months, I am looking forward to reaching out and working with leaders of both parties."

### First 100 days

[![Photo of Obama raising his left hand for the oath of office](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/US_President_Barack_Obama_taking_his_Oath_of_Office_-_2009Jan20.jpg/250px-US_President_Barack_Obama_taking_his_Oath_of_Office_-_2009Jan20.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:US_President_Barack_Obama_taking_his_Oath_of_Office_-_2009Jan20.jpg)

Obama takes the [oath of office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oath_of_office_of_the_President_of_the_United_States "Oath of office of the President of the United States") administered by [Chief Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_the_United_States "Chief Justice of the United States") [John Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Roberts "John Roberts") at [the Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Capitol "United States Capitol") on January 20, 2009.

The [inauguration of Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inauguration_of_Barack_Obama "First inauguration of Barack Obama") as the 44th president took place on January 20, 2009. In his first few days in office, Obama issued [executive orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order "Executive order") and [presidential memoranda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_memoranda "Presidential memoranda") directing the U.S. military to develop plans to withdraw troops from Iraq. He ordered the closing of the [Guantanamo Bay detention camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_detention_camp "Guantanamo Bay detention camp"), but Congress prevented the closure by refusing to appropriate the required funds and preventing the transfer of any Guantanamo detainees. Obama reduced the secrecy given to presidential records. He also revoked President George W. Bush's restoration of President [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan")'s [Mexico City policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_City_policy "Mexico City policy") which prohibited federal aid to international [family planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning "Family planning") organizations that perform or provide counseling about abortion.

### Domestic policy

The first bill signed into law by Obama was the [Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act of 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilly_Ledbetter_Fair_Pay_Act_of_2009 "Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act of 2009"), relaxing the [statute of limitations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_limitations "Statute of limitations") for equal-pay lawsuits. Five days later, he signed the reauthorization of the State Children's Health Insurance Program to cover an additional four million uninsured children. In March 2009, Obama reversed a Bush-era policy that had limited funding of [embryonic stem cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryonic_stem_cell "Embryonic stem cell") research and pledged to develop "strict guidelines" on the research.

Obama appointed two women to serve on the Supreme Court in the first two years of his presidency. He nominated [Sonia Sotomayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonia_Sotomayor "Sonia Sotomayor") on May 26, 2009, to replace retiring [associate justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_Justice_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Associate Justice of the Supreme Court of the United States") [David Souter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Souter "David Souter"). She was confirmed on August 6, 2009, becoming the first Supreme Court Justice of [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic "Hispanic") descent. Obama nominated [Elena Kagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Kagan "Elena Kagan") on May 10, 2010, to replace retiring Associate Justice [John Paul Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_Stevens "John Paul Stevens"). She was confirmed on August 5, 2010, bringing the number of women sitting simultaneously on the Court to three for the first time in American history.

On March 11, 2009, Obama created the [White House Council on Women and Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Council_on_Women_and_Girls "White House Council on Women and Girls"), which formed part of the [Office of Intergovernmental Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Intergovernmental_Affairs "Office of Intergovernmental Affairs"), having been established by [Executive Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order_(United_States) "Executive order (United States)") [13506](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Executive%20Order%2013506 "s:Executive Order 13506") with a broad mandate to advise him on issues relating to the welfare of American women and girls. The council was chaired by [Senior Advisor to the President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Advisor_to_the_President "Senior Advisor to the President") [Valerie Jarrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerie_Jarrett "Valerie Jarrett"). Obama also established the [White House Task Force to Protect Students from Sexual Assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Task_Force_to_Protect_Students_from_Sexual_Assault "White House Task Force to Protect Students from Sexual Assault") through a government memorandum on January 22, 2014, with a broad mandate to advise him on issues relating to sexual assault on college and university campuses throughout the United States. The co-chairs of the Task Force were Vice President Joe Biden and Jarrett. The Task Force was a development out of the White House Council on Women and Girls and [Office of the Vice President of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Vice_President_of_the_United_States "Office of the Vice President of the United States"), and prior to that the 1994 [Violence Against Women Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_Against_Women_Act "Violence Against Women Act") first drafted by Biden.

In July 2009, Obama launched the [Priority Enforcement Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priority_Enforcement_Program "Priority Enforcement Program"), an immigration enforcement program that had been pioneered by George W. Bush, and the [Secure Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Communities "Secure Communities") fingerprinting and immigration status data-sharing program.

In a [major space policy speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_policy_of_the_Obama_administration "Space policy of the Obama administration") in April 2010, Obama announced a planned change in direction at [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA"), the U.S. space agency. He ended plans for a return of [human spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight "Human spaceflight") to the moon and development of the [Ares I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ares_I "Ares I") rocket, [Ares V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ares_V "Ares V") rocket and [Constellation program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation_program "Constellation program"), in favor of funding [earth science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_science "Earth science") projects, a new rocket type, research and development for an eventual crewed mission to Mars, and ongoing missions to the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station "International Space Station").

[![Photo of Obama smiling at a hospital patient while hugging her friend](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Barack_Obama_visiting_victims_of_2012_Aurora_shooting.jpg/250px-Barack_Obama_visiting_victims_of_2012_Aurora_shooting.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Barack_Obama_visiting_victims_of_2012_Aurora_shooting.jpg)

Obama visits an [Aurora shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Aurora_theater_shooting "2012 Aurora theater shooting") victim at [University of Colorado Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Hospital "University of Colorado Hospital"), 2012

On January 16, 2013, one month after the [Sandy Hook Elementary School shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Hook_Elementary_School_shooting "Sandy Hook Elementary School shooting"), Obama signed 23 executive orders and outlined a series of sweeping proposals regarding [gun control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_politics_in_the_United_States "Gun politics in the United States"). He urged Congress to enact a comprehensive gun control package, which included reauthorizing the [assault weapons ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_Weapons_Ban_of_2013 "Assault Weapons Ban of 2013"), limiting magazine capacity to 10 rounds, mandating [universal background checks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_background_check "Universal background check"), prohibiting armor-piercing ammunition, increasing penalties for straw purchasers, and confirming a Director for the [Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Alcohol,_Tobacco,_Firearms_and_Explosives "Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives") (ATF). On January 5, 2016, Obama announced new executive actions extending background check requirements to more gun sellers. In a 2016 editorial in *The New York Times*, Obama compared the struggle for what he termed "common-sense gun reform" to [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage "Women's suffrage") and other [civil rights movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_rights_movements "Civil rights movements") in American history.

In 2011, Obama signed a four-year renewal of the Patriot Act. Following the [2013 global surveillance disclosures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_global_surveillance_disclosures "2013 global surveillance disclosures") by [whistleblower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistleblower "Whistleblower") [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden "Edward Snowden"), Obama condemned the leak as unpatriotic, but called for increased restrictions on the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency "National Security Agency") (NSA) to address violations of privacy. Obama continued and expanded surveillance programs set up by George W. Bush, while implementing some reforms. He supported legislation that would have limited the NSA's ability to collect phone records in bulk under a single program and supported bringing more transparency to the [Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Intelligence_Surveillance_Court "Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court") (FISC).

#### Racial issues

In his speeches as president, Obama did not make more overt references to race relations than his predecessors, but according to one study, he implemented stronger policy action on behalf of African-Americans than any president since the Nixon era.

Following Obama's election, many pondered the existence of a "[post-racial America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-racial_America "Post-racial America")". However, lingering racial tensions quickly became apparent, and many African-Americans expressed outrage over what they saw as an intense racial animosity directed at Obama. The [acquittal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_of_George_Zimmerman "Trial of George Zimmerman") of [George Zimmerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Zimmerman "George Zimmerman") following the [killing of Trayvon Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Trayvon_Martin "Killing of Trayvon Martin") sparked national outrage, leading to Obama giving a speech in which he said that "Trayvon Martin could have been me 35 years ago." The shooting of [Michael Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_of_Michael_Brown "Shooting of Michael Brown") in [Ferguson, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferguson,_Missouri "Ferguson, Missouri"), [sparked a wave of protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferguson_unrest "Ferguson unrest"). These and other events led to the birth of the [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter "Black Lives Matter") movement, which campaigns against violence and [systemic racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systemic_racism "Systemic racism") toward [black people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people "Black people"). Though Obama entered office reluctant to talk about race, by 2014 he began openly discussing the disadvantages faced by many members of minority groups.

Several incidents during Obama's presidency generated disapproval from the African-American community and with law enforcement, and Obama sought to build trust between law enforcement officials and civil rights activists, with mixed results. Some in law enforcement criticized Obama's condemnation of racial bias after incidents in which police action led to the death of African-American men, while some racial justice activists criticized Obama's expressions of empathy for the police. In a March 2016 Gallup poll, nearly one third of Americans said they worried "a great deal" about race relations, a higher figure than in any previous Gallup poll since 2001.

#### LGBT rights

On October 8, 2009, Obama signed the [Matthew Shepard and James Byrd Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Shepard_and_James_Byrd_Jr._Hate_Crimes_Prevention_Act "Matthew Shepard and James Byrd Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act"), a measure that expanded the [1969 United States federal hate-crime law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hate_crime_laws_in_the_United_States#Federal "Hate crime laws in the United States") to include crimes motivated by a victim's actual or perceived gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, or disability. On October 30, 2009, Obama lifted the ban on travel to the United States by those infected with HIV. The lifting of the ban was celebrated by [Immigration Equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_Equality "Immigration Equality"). On December 22, 2010, Obama signed the [Don't Ask, Don't Tell Repeal Act of 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Ask,_Don't_Tell_Repeal_Act_of_2010 "Don't Ask, Don't Tell Repeal Act of 2010"), which fulfilled a promise made in the 2008 presidential campaign to end the [don't ask, don't tell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_ask,_don't_tell "Don't ask, don't tell") policy of 1993 that had prevented gay and lesbian people from serving openly in the [United States Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces "United States Armed Forces"). In 2016, the Pentagon ended the policy that barred [transgender people from serving openly in the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_personnel_in_the_United_States_military "Transgender personnel in the United States military").

#### Same-sex marriage

As a candidate for the Illinois state senate in 1996, Obama stated he favored legalizing [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_the_United_States "Same-sex marriage in the United States"). During his Senate run in 2004, he said he supported civil unions and domestic partnerships for same-sex partners but opposed same-sex marriages. In 2008, he reaffirmed this position by stating "I believe marriage is between a man and a woman. I am not in favor of gay marriage." On May 9, 2012, shortly after the official launch of his campaign for re-election as president, Obama said his views had evolved, and he publicly affirmed his personal support for the legalization of same-sex marriage, becoming the first sitting U.S. president to do so. During his second [inaugural address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Barack_Obama "Second inauguration of Barack Obama") on January 21, 2013, Obama became the first U.S. president in office to call for full equality for gay Americans, and the first to mention [gay rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_the_United_States "LGBT rights in the United States") or the word "gay" in an inaugural address. In 2013, the Obama administration filed briefs that urged the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States "Supreme Court of the United States") to rule in favor of same-sex couples in the cases of *[Hollingsworth v. Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollingsworth_v._Perry "Hollingsworth v. Perry")* (regarding same-sex marriage) and *[United States v. Windsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v._Windsor "United States v. Windsor")* (regarding the [Defense of Marriage Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_of_Marriage_Act "Defense of Marriage Act")).

#### Economic policy

On February 17, 2009, Obama signed the [American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Recovery_and_Reinvestment_Act_of_2009 "American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009"), a $787 billion (equivalent to $1181 billion in 2025) [economic stimulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_stimulus "Economic stimulus") package aimed at helping the economy recover from the [deepening worldwide recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession "Great Recession"). The act includes increased federal spending for health care, infrastructure, education, various tax breaks and [incentives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_incentive "Tax incentive"), and direct assistance to individuals. In March 2009, Obama's Treasury Secretary, [Timothy Geithner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Geithner "Timothy Geithner"), took further steps to manage the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis "2008 financial crisis"), including introducing the [Public–Private Investment Program for Legacy Assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public–Private_Investment_Program_for_Legacy_Assets "Public–Private Investment Program for Legacy Assets"), which contains provisions for buying up to $2 trillion in depreciated real estate assets.

[![Graph showing large deficit increases in 2008 and 2009, followed by a decline](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/U.S._Total_Deficits_vs._National_Debt_Increases_2001-2010.png/250px-U.S._Total_Deficits_vs._National_Debt_Increases_2001-2010.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:U.S._Total_Deficits_vs._National_Debt_Increases_2001-2010.png)

[Deficit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_budget "United States federal budget") and [debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_debt_of_the_United_States "National debt of the United States") increases, 2001–2016

Obama intervened in the [troubled automotive industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_crisis_of_2008–10 "Automotive industry crisis of 2008–10") in March 2009, renewing loans for [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors "General Motors") (GM) and [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler "Chrysler") to continue operations while reorganizing. Over the following months the White House set terms for both firms' bankruptcies, including the [sale of Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Chapter_11_reorganization "Chrysler Chapter 11 reorganization") to Italian automaker [Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat "Fiat") and a [reorganization of GM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors_Chapter_11_reorganization "General Motors Chapter 11 reorganization") giving the U.S. government a temporary 60 percent equity stake in the company. In June 2009, dissatisfied with the pace of economic stimulus, Obama called on his cabinet to accelerate the investment. He signed into law the [Car Allowance Rebate System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_Allowance_Rebate_System "Car Allowance Rebate System"), known colloquially as "Cash for Clunkers", which temporarily boosted the economy.

The Bush and Obama administrations authorized spending and loan guarantees from the [Federal Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve "Federal Reserve") and the [Department of the Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Treasury "United States Department of the Treasury"). These guarantees totaled about $11.5 trillion, but only $3 trillion had been spent by the end of November 2009. On August 2, 2011, after a lengthy congressional debate over whether to raise the nation's debt limit, Obama signed the bipartisan [Budget Control Act of 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_Control_Act_of_2011 "Budget Control Act of 2011"). The legislation enforced limits on discretionary spending until 2021, established a procedure to increase the debt limit, created a Congressional Joint Select Committee on Deficit Reduction to propose further deficit reduction with a stated goal of achieving at least $1.5 trillion in budgetary savings over 10 years, and established automatic procedures for reducing spending by as much as $1.2 trillion if legislation originating with the new joint select committee did not achieve such savings. By passing the legislation, Congress was able to prevent a [U.S. government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._government "U.S. government") [default](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Default_(finance) "Default (finance)") on its obligations.

The unemployment rate rose in 2009, reaching a peak in October at 10.0 percent and averaging 10.0 percent in the fourth quarter. Following a decrease to 9.7 percent in the first quarter of 2010, the unemployment rate fell to 9.6 percent in the second quarter, where it remained for the rest of the year. Between February and December 2010, employment rose by 0.8 percent, which was less than the average of 1.9 percent experienced during comparable periods in the past four employment recoveries. By November 2012, the unemployment rate fell to 7.7 percent, decreasing to 6.7 percent in the last month of 2013. During 2014, the unemployment rate continued to decline, falling to 6.3 percent in the first quarter. GDP growth returned in the third quarter of 2009, expanding at a rate of 1.6 percent, followed by a 5.0 percent increase in the fourth quarter. Growth continued in 2010, posting an increase of 3.7 percent in the first quarter, with lesser gains throughout the rest of the year. In July 2010, the Federal Reserve noted that economic activity continued to increase, but its pace had slowed, and chairman [Ben Bernanke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Bernanke "Ben Bernanke") said the economic outlook was "unusually uncertain". Overall, the economy expanded at a rate of 2.9 percent in 2010.

The [Congressional Budget Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Budget_Office "Congressional Budget Office") (CBO) and a broad range of economists credit Obama's stimulus plan for economic growth. The CBO released a report stating that the stimulus bill increased employment by 1–2.1 million, while conceding that "it is impossible to determine how many of the reported jobs would have existed in the absence of the stimulus package." Although an April 2010, survey of members of the [National Association for Business Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_for_Business_Economics "National Association for Business Economics") showed an increase in job creation (over a similar January survey) for the first time in two years, 73 percent of 68 respondents believed the stimulus bill has had no impact on employment. The economy of the United States has grown faster than the other original [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO "NATO") members by a wider margin under President Obama than it has anytime since the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). The [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development "Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development") credits the much faster growth in the United States to the stimulus plan of the U.S. and the austerity measures in the European Union.

Within a month of the [2010 midterm elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_midterm_elections "2010 midterm elections"), Obama announced a compromise deal with the congressional Republican leadership that included a temporary, two-year extension of the [2001 and 2003 income tax rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_tax_cuts "Bush tax cuts"), a one-year [payroll tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Insurance_Contributions_Act_tax "Federal Insurance Contributions Act tax") reduction, continuation of unemployment benefits, and a new rate and exemption amount for [estate taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_tax_in_the_United_States "Estate tax in the United States"). The compromise overcame opposition from some in both parties, and the resulting $858 billion (equivalent to $1.3 trillion in 2025) [Tax Relief, Unemployment Insurance Reauthorization, and Job Creation Act of 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_Relief,_Unemployment_Insurance_Reauthorization,_and_Job_Creation_Act_of_2010 "Tax Relief, Unemployment Insurance Reauthorization, and Job Creation Act of 2010") passed with bipartisan majorities in both houses of Congress before Obama signed it on December 17, 2010.

In December 2013, Obama declared that growing [income inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_inequality_in_the_United_States "Income inequality in the United States") is a "defining challenge of our time" and called on Congress to bolster the safety net and raise wages. This came on the heels of the [nationwide strikes of fast-food workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_food_worker_strikes "Fast food worker strikes") and [Pope Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Francis "Pope Francis")' criticism of inequality and [trickle-down economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trickle-down_economics "Trickle-down economics"). Obama urged Congress to ratify a 12-nation free trade pact called the [Trans-Pacific Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Pacific_Partnership "Trans-Pacific Partnership").

#### Environmental policy

[![Photo of Obama listening to a briefing, surrounded by senior staffers](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Obama-venice-la.jpg/250px-Obama-venice-la.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Obama-venice-la.jpg)

Obama at a 2010 briefing on the [BP oil spill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BP_oil_spill "BP oil spill") at the [Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard "United States Coast Guard") Station Venice in [Venice, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice,_Louisiana "Venice, Louisiana")

On April 20, 2010, an explosion destroyed an offshore [drilling rig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drilling_rig "Drilling rig") at the [Macondo Prospect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macondo_Prospect "Macondo Prospect") in the [Gulf of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Mexico "Gulf of Mexico"), causing a [major sustained oil leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepwater_Horizon_oil_spill "Deepwater Horizon oil spill"). Obama visited the Gulf, announced a federal investigation, and formed a bipartisan commission to recommend new safety standards, after a review by [Secretary of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Interior "United States Secretary of the Interior") [Ken Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Salazar "Ken Salazar") and concurrent congressional hearings. He then announced a six-month moratorium on new [deepwater drilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepwater_drilling "Deepwater drilling") permits and leases, pending regulatory review. As multiple efforts by BP failed, some in the media and public expressed confusion and criticism over various aspects of the incident, and stated a desire for more involvement by Obama and the federal government. Prior to the oil spill, on March 31, 2010, Obama ended a ban on oil and gas drilling along the majority of the [East Coast of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States "East Coast of the United States") and along the coast of [northern Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Alaska "Northern Alaska") in an effort to win support for an energy and climate bill and to reduce foreign imports of oil and gas.

In July 2013, Obama expressed reservations and said he "would reject the [Keystone XL pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_XL_pipeline "Keystone XL pipeline") if it increased carbon pollution [or] greenhouse emissions." On February 24, 2015, Obama vetoed a bill that would have authorized the pipeline. It was the third veto of Obama's presidency and his first major veto.

In December 2016, Obama permanently banned new offshore oil and gas drilling in most United States-owned waters in the [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic "Atlantic") and Arctic Oceans using the 1953 Outer Continental Shelf Act.

Obama emphasized the [conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_movement "Conservation movement") of [federal lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_lands "Federal lands") during his term in office. He used his power under the [Antiquities Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquities_Act "Antiquities Act") to create 25 new [national monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_monument_(United_States) "National monument (United States)") during his presidency and expand four others, protecting a total of 553,000,000 acres (224,000,000 ha) of federal lands and waters, more than any other U.S. president.

#### Health care reform

Obama called for [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress") to pass legislation reforming [healthcare in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_the_United_States "Healthcare in the United States"), a key campaign promise and a top legislative goal. He proposed an expansion of health insurance coverage to cover the uninsured, cap premium increases, and allow people to retain their coverage when they leave or change jobs. His proposal was to spend $900 billion over ten years and include a government insurance plan, also known as the [public option](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_option "Public option"), to compete with the corporate insurance sector as a main component to lowering costs and improving quality of health care. It would also make it illegal for insurers to drop sick people or deny them coverage for [pre-existing conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-existing_condition "Pre-existing condition"), and require every American to carry health coverage. The plan also includes medical spending cuts and taxes on insurance companies that offer expensive plans.

[![Graph of maximum out-of-pocket premiums by poverty level, showing single-digit premiums for everyone under 400% of the federal poverty level.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/PPACA_Premium_Chart.jpg/500px-PPACA_Premium_Chart.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:PPACA_Premium_Chart.jpg)

Maximum Out-of-Pocket Premium as Percentage of Family Income and [federal poverty level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_poverty_level "Federal poverty level"), under [Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_Care_Act "Affordable Care Act"), starting in 2014 (source: [CRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Research_Service "Congressional Research Service"))

On July 14, 2009, House Democratic leaders introduced a 1,017-page plan for overhauling the U.S. health care system, which Obama wanted Congress to approve by the end of 2009. After public debate during the congressional summer recess of 2009, Obama delivered [a speech to a joint session of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2009_Barack_Obama_speech_to_a_joint_session_of_Congress "September 2009 Barack Obama speech to a joint session of Congress") on September 9 where he addressed concerns over the proposals. In March 2009, Obama lifted a ban on using federal funds for stem cell research.

On November 7, 2009, a health care bill featuring the public option was passed in the House. On December 24, 2009, the Senate passed its own bill—without a public option—on a party-line vote of 60–39. On March 21, 2010, the [Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_Care_Act "Affordable Care Act") (ACA, colloquially "Obamacare") passed by the Senate in December was passed in the House by a vote of 219 to 212. Obama signed the bill into law on March 23, 2010.

The ACA includes [health-related provisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisions_of_the_Patient_Protection_and_Affordable_Care_Act "Provisions of the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act"), most of which took effect in 2014, including expanding [Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid "Medicaid") eligibility for people making up to 133 percent of the [federal poverty level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_poverty_level "Federal poverty level") (FPL) starting in 2014, subsidizing insurance premiums for people making up to 400 percent of the FPL ($88,000 for family of four in 2010) so their maximum "out-of-pocket" payment for annual premiums will be from 2 percent to 9.5 percent of income, providing incentives for businesses to provide health care benefits, prohibiting denial of coverage and denial of claims based on pre-existing conditions, establishing [health insurance exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance_exchange "Health insurance exchange"), prohibiting annual coverage caps, and support for medical research. According to White House and CBO figures, the maximum share of income that enrollees would have to pay would vary depending on their income relative to the federal poverty level.

[![Graph showing significant decreases in uninsured rates after the creation of Medicare and Medicaid, and after the creation of Obamacare](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Percentage_of_Individuals_in_the_United_States_Without_Health_Insurance%2C_1963-2015.png/250px-Percentage_of_Individuals_in_the_United_States_Without_Health_Insurance%2C_1963-2015.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Percentage_of_Individuals_in_the_United_States_Without_Health_Insurance,_1963-2015.png)

Percentage of Individuals in the United States without Health Insurance, 1963–2015 (source: [JAMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JAMA_(journal) "JAMA (journal)"))

The costs of these provisions are offset by taxes, fees, and cost-saving measures, such as new Medicare taxes for those in high-income [brackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_bracket "Tax bracket"), taxes on [indoor tanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_tanning "Indoor tanning"), cuts to the [Medicare Advantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_Advantage "Medicare Advantage") program in favor of traditional Medicare, and fees on medical devices and pharmaceutical companies; there is also a tax penalty for those who do not obtain health insurance, unless they are exempt due to low income or other reasons. In March 2010, the CBO estimated that the net effect of both laws will be a reduction in the federal deficit by $143 billion over the first decade.

The law faced several legal challenges, primarily based on the argument that an individual mandate requiring Americans to buy health insurance was unconstitutional. On June 28, 2012, the Supreme Court ruled by a 5–4 vote in *[National Federation of Independent Business v. Sebelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Federation_of_Independent_Business_v._Sebelius "National Federation of Independent Business v. Sebelius")* that the mandate was constitutional under the U.S. Congress's taxing authority. In *[Burwell v. Hobby Lobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burwell_v._Hobby_Lobby "Burwell v. Hobby Lobby")* the Court ruled that "closely-held" for-profit corporations could be exempt on religious grounds under the [Religious Freedom Restoration Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Freedom_Restoration_Act "Religious Freedom Restoration Act") from regulations adopted under the ACA that would have required them to pay for insurance that covered certain contraceptives. In June 2015, the Court ruled 6–3 in *[King v. Burwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_v._Burwell "King v. Burwell")* that subsidies to help individuals and families purchase health insurance were authorized for those doing so on both the federal exchange and state exchanges, not only those purchasing plans "established by the State", as the statute reads.

### Foreign policy

[![refer to caption](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/P06409PS-0571_%283594694537%29.jpg/250px-P06409PS-0571_%283594694537%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:P06409PS-0571_(3594694537).jpg)

Obama participates in a roundtable interview in 2009 with among others [Jamal Khashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Khashoggi "Jamal Khashoggi"), [Bambang Harymurti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambang_Harymurti "Bambang Harymurti") and [Nahum Barnea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahum_Barnea "Nahum Barnea").

In February and March 2009, Vice President Joe Biden and [Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State "United States Secretary of State") Hillary Clinton made separate overseas trips to announce a "new era" in U.S. foreign relations with Russia and Europe, using the terms "break" and "[reset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_reset "Russian reset")" to signal major changes from the policies of the preceding administration. Obama attempted to reach out to Arab leaders by granting his first interview to an Arab satellite TV network, [Al Arabiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Arabiya "Al Arabiya"). On March 19, Obama continued his outreach to the Muslim world, releasing a New Year's video message to the people and government of Iran. On June 4, 2009, Obama delivered a speech at [Cairo University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_University "Cairo University") in Egypt calling for "[A New Beginning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_New_Beginning_(speech) "A New Beginning (speech)")" in relations between the Islamic world and the United States and promoting Middle East peace. On June 26, 2009, Obama condemned the Iranian government's actions towards protesters following [Iran's 2009 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Iranian_presidential_election "2009 Iranian presidential election").

In 2011, Obama ordered a drone strike in Yemen which targeted and killed [Anwar al-Awlaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_al-Awlaki "Anwar al-Awlaki"), an American imam suspected of being a leading [Al-Qaeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda "Al-Qaeda") organizer. al-Awlaki became the first [U.S. citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._citizen "U.S. citizen") to be targeted and killed by a [U.S. drone strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_strike "Drone strike"). The Department of Justice released a memo justifying al-Awlaki's death as a lawful act of war, while civil liberties advocates described it as a violation of al-Awlaki's constitutional right to [due process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Due_process "Due process"). The killing led to significant controversy. His [teenage son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulrahman_al-Awlaki "Abdulrahman al-Awlaki") and [young daughter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Nawar_al-Awlaki "Death of Nawar al-Awlaki"), also Americans, were later killed in separate [U.S. military actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raid_on_Yakla "Raid on Yakla"), although they were not targeted specifically.

#### Death of Osama bin Laden

[![Photo of Obama, Biden, and national security staffers in the Situation Room, somberly listening to updates on the bin Laden raid](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Obama_and_Biden_await_updates_on_bin_Laden.jpg/250px-Obama_and_Biden_await_updates_on_bin_Laden.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Obama_and_Biden_await_updates_on_bin_Laden.jpg)

Obama and members of the national security team receive an update on [Operation Neptune's Spear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Neptune's_Spear "Operation Neptune's Spear") in the [White House Situation Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Situation_Room "White House Situation Room") on May 1, 2011.

Starting with information received from Central Intelligence Agency operatives in July 2010, the CIA developed intelligence over the next several months that determined what they believed to be the hideout of [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden "Osama bin Laden"). He was living in seclusion in [a large compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden's_compound_in_Abbottabad "Osama bin Laden's compound in Abbottabad") in [Abbottabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbottabad "Abbottabad"), Pakistan, a suburban area 35 miles (56 km) from [Islamabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad "Islamabad"). CIA head [Leon Panetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Panetta "Leon Panetta") reported this intelligence to President Obama in March 2011. Meeting with his national security advisers over the course of the next six weeks, Obama rejected a plan to bomb the compound, and authorized a "surgical raid" to be conducted by [United States Navy SEALs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_SEALs "United States Navy SEALs"). The operation took place on May 1, 2011, and resulted in the shooting death of bin Laden and the seizure of papers, computer drives and disks from the compound. DNA testing was one of five methods used to positively identify bin Laden's corpse, which was buried at sea several hours later. Within minutes of the President's announcement from Washington, DC, late in the evening on May 1, there were spontaneous celebrations around the country as crowds gathered outside the White House, and at New York City's [Ground Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_site "World Trade Center site") and [Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square "Times Square"). [Reaction to the announcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactions_to_the_death_of_Osama_bin_Laden "Reactions to the death of Osama bin Laden") was positive across party lines, including from former presidents Bill Clinton and George W. Bush.

## Post-presidency (2017–present)

[![refer to caption](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Obama_Macri_October_2017.jpg/250px-Obama_Macri_October_2017.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Obama_Macri_October_2017.jpg)

Obama playing golf with Argentine president [Mauricio Macri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Macri "Mauricio Macri"), October 2017

Obama's presidential run finished on January 20, 2017, upon [the inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump "First inauguration of Donald Trump") of his successor, [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump"). The family moved to a house they rented in [Kalorama, Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheridan-Kalorama_Historic_District "Sheridan-Kalorama Historic District") On March 2, the [John F. Kennedy Presidential Library and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy_Presidential_Library_and_Museum "John F. Kennedy Presidential Library and Museum") awarded the [Profile in Courage Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profile_in_Courage_Award "Profile in Courage Award") to Obama "for his enduring commitment to democratic ideals and elevating the standard of political courage." His first public appearance since leaving the office was a seminar at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") on April 24, where he appealed for a new generation to participate in politics. On September 7, Obama partnered with former presidents Jimmy Carter, George H. W. Bush, Bill Clinton, and George W. Bush to work with [One America Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_America_Appeal "One America Appeal") to help the victims of [Hurricane Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Harvey "Hurricane Harvey") and [Hurricane Irma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Irma "Hurricane Irma") in the [Gulf Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Coast "Gulf Coast") and [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas") communities. From October 31 to November 1, Obama hosted the inaugural summit of the [Obama Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama_Foundation "Obama Foundation"), which he intended to be the central focus of his post-presidency and part of his ambitions for his subsequent activities following his presidency to be more consequential than his time in office.

Barack and Michelle Obama signed a deal on May 22, 2018, to produce docu-series, documentaries and features for [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") under the Obamas' newly formed production company, [Higher Ground Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_Ground_Productions "Higher Ground Productions"). Higher Ground's first film, *[American Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Factory "American Factory")*, won the [Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Documentary_Feature "Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature") in 2020. On October 24, a pipe bomb addressed to Obama was intercepted by the Secret Service. It was one of several pipe-bombs that had been [mailed out to Democratic lawmakers and officials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2018_United_States_mail_bombing_attempts "October 2018 United States mail bombing attempts"). In 2019, Barack and Michelle Obama bought a home on [Martha's Vineyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha's_Vineyard "Martha's Vineyard") from [Wyc Grousbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyc_Grousbeck "Wyc Grousbeck"). On October 29, Obama criticized "[wokeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke "Woke")" and [call-out culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call-out_culture "Call-out culture") at the Obama Foundation's annual summit.

Obama was reluctant to make an endorsement in the [2020 Democratic presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Democratic_presidential_primaries "2020 Democratic presidential primaries") because he wanted to position himself to unify the party, regardless of the nominee. On April 14, 2020, Obama endorsed his former vice president, [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden"), the presumptive nominee, for president in [the presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election "2020 United States presidential election"), stating that he has "all the qualities we need in a president right now." In May, Obama criticized President Trump for [his handling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_of_the_Trump_administration_during_the_COVID-19_pandemic "Communication of the Trump administration during the COVID-19 pandemic") of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_States "COVID-19 pandemic in the United States"), calling his response to the crisis "an absolute chaotic disaster", and stating that the consequences of the [Trump presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_presidency_of_Donald_Trump "First presidency of Donald Trump") have been "our worst impulses unleashed, our proud reputation around the world badly diminished, and our democratic institutions threatened like never before." On November 17, Obama's presidential memoir, *[A Promised Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Promised_Land "A Promised Land")*, was released.

Obama and his wife attended the [inauguration of Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inauguration_of_Joe_Biden "Inauguration of Joe Biden") in January 2021.

In February 2021, Obama and musician [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen "Bruce Springsteen") started a podcast called *[Renegades: Born in the USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renegades:_Born_in_the_USA "Renegades: Born in the USA")* where the two talk about "their backgrounds, music and their 'enduring love of America.'" Later that year, Regina Hicks had signed a deal with [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix"), in a venture with his and Michelle's Higher Ground to develop comedy projects.

On March 4, 2022, Obama won an Audio Publishers Association (APA) Award in the best narration by the author category for the narration of his memoir *A Promised Land*. On April 5, Obama visited the White House for the first time since leaving office, in an event celebrating the 12th annual anniversary of the signing of the Affordable Care Act. In June, it was announced that the Obamas and their podcast production company, Higher Ground, signed a multi-year deal with [Audible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audible_(service) "Audible (service)"). In September, Obama visited the White House to unveil his and Michelle's official White House portraits. Around the same time, he won a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Narrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Narrator "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Narrator") for his narration in the Netflix documentary series *[Our Great National Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Great_National_Parks "Our Great National Parks")*.

In 2022, Obama opposed expanding the Supreme Court beyond the present nine Justices.

In March 2023, Obama traveled to Australia as a part of his speaking tour of the country. During the trip, Obama met with Australian prime minister [Anthony Albanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Albanese "Anthony Albanese") and visited [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne") for the first time.

In October 2023, during the [Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war "Gaza war"), Obama declared that [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel") must dismantle [Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas "Hamas") in the wake of the [October 7 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_7_attacks "October 7 attacks"). Weeks later, Obama warned Israel that its actions could "harden Palestinian attitudes for generations" and weaken international support for Israel; any military strategy that ignored the war's human costs "could ultimately backfire."

Obama's family history, upbringing, and [Ivy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League "Ivy League") education differ markedly from those of African-American politicians who rose to prominence in the 1960s through their involvement in the civil rights movement. ### Job approval

[![refer to adjacent text](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/Gallup_Poll-Approval_Rating-Barack_Obama.svg/330px-Gallup_Poll-Approval_Rating-Barack_Obama.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gallup_Poll-Approval_Rating-Barack_Obama.svg)

Graph of Obama's approval ratings per Gallup

According to the [Gallup Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_Organization "Gallup Organization"), Obama began his presidency with a 68 percent [approval rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approval_rating "Approval rating"), the fifth highest for a president following their swearing in. His ratings remained above the majority level until November 2009 and by August 2010 his approval was in the low 40s, a trend similar to Ronald Reagan's and Bill Clinton's first years in office. Following the [death of Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Osama_bin_Laden "Death of Osama bin Laden") on May 2, 2011, Obama experienced a small poll bounce and steadily maintained 50–53 percent approval for about a month, until his approval numbers dropped back to the low 40s.

His approval rating fell to 38 percent on several occasions in late 2011 before recovering in mid-2012 with polls showing an average approval of 50 percent. After his second inauguration in 2013, Obama's approval ratings remained stable around 52 percent before declining for the rest of the year and eventually bottoming out at 39 percent in December. In polling conducted before the [2014 midterm elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_midterm_elections "2014 midterm elections"), Obama's approval ratings were at their lowest with his disapproval rating reaching a high of 57 percent. His approval rating continued to lag throughout most of 2015 but began to reach the high 40s by the end of the year. According to Gallup, Obama's approval rating reached 50 percent in March 2016, a level unseen since May 2013. In polling conducted January 16–19, 2017, Obama's final approval rating was 59 percent, which placed him on par with [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush "George H. W. Bush") and [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower "Dwight D. Eisenhower"), whose final Gallup ratings also measured in the high 50s.

Obama has maintained relatively positive public perceptions after his presidency. In Gallup's retrospective approval polls of former presidents, Obama garnered a 63 percent approval rating in 2018 and again in 2023, ranking him the fourth most popular president since World War II.

Polls showed strong support for Obama in other countries both before and during his presidency. In a February 2009 poll conducted in Western Europe and the U.S. by [Harris Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_Insights_&_Analytics "Harris Insights & Analytics") for [France 24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_24 "France 24") and the *[International Herald Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Herald_Tribune "International Herald Tribune")*, Obama was rated as the most respected world leader, as well as the most powerful. In a similar poll conducted by Harris in May 2009, Obama was rated as the most popular world leader, as well as the one figure most people would pin their hopes on for pulling the world out of the economic downturn.

On October 9, 2009—only nine months into his first term—the [Norwegian Nobel Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Committee "Norwegian Nobel Committee") announced that Obama had won the [2009 Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Nobel_Peace_Prize "2009 Nobel Peace Prize") "for his extraordinary efforts to strengthen international diplomacy and cooperation between peoples", which drew a mixture of praise and criticism from world leaders and media figures. He became the fourth U.S. president to be awarded the Nobel Peace Prize, and the third to become a Nobel laureate while in office. He himself called it a "call to action" and remarked: "I do not view it as a recognition of my own accomplishments but rather an affirmation of American leadership on behalf of aspirations held by people in all nations".

## Legacy and recognition

Obama has been described as one of the most effective campaigners in American history, his 2008 campaign being particularly highlighted, as well as one of the most talented political orators of the 21st century. Historian [Julian Zelizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Zelizer "Julian Zelizer") credits Obama with "a keen sense of how the institutions of government work and the ways that his team could design policy proposals." Zelizer notes Obama's policy successes included the economic stimulus package which ended the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession_in_the_United_States "Great Recession in the United States") and the [Dodd-Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodd-Frank "Dodd-Frank") financial and consumer protection reforms, as well as the [Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_Care_Act "Affordable Care Act"). Zelizer also notes the Democratic Party lost power and numbers of elected officials during Obama's term, saying that the consensus among historians is that Obama "turned out to be a very effective policymaker but not a tremendously successful party builder." Zelizer calls this the "defining paradox of Obama's presidency".

The [Brookings Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings_Institution "Brookings Institution") noted that Obama passed "only one major legislative achievement (Obamacare)—and a fragile one at that—the legacy of Obama's presidency mainly rests on its tremendous symbolic importance and the fate of a patchwork of executive actions." David W. Wise noted that Obama fell short "in areas many Progressives hold dear", including the continuation of drone strikes, not going after big banks during the Great Recession, and failing to strengthen his coalition before pushing for Obamacare. Wise called Obama's legacy that of "a disappointingly conventional president," despite being the first president since [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan") to win a majority of the popular vote twice.

Obama's most significant accomplishment is generally considered to be the Affordable Care Act (ACA), provisions of which went into effect from 2010 to 2020. Many attempts by Senate Republicans to repeal the ACA, including a "skinny repeal", have thus far failed. However, in 2017, the penalty for violating the individual mandate was repealed effective 2019. Together with the [Health Care and Education Reconciliation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Care_and_Education_Reconciliation_Act "Health Care and Education Reconciliation Act") amendment, it represents the U.S. healthcare system's most significant regulatory overhaul and expansion of coverage since the passage of [Medicare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_(United_States) "Medicare (United States)") and Medicaid in 1965.

Many commentators credit Obama with averting a threatened [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_(economics) "Depression (economics)") and pulling the economy back from the Great Recession. According to the [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Bureau_of_Labor_Statistics "U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics"), the [Obama administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obama_administration "Obama administration") created 11.3 million jobs from the month after his first inauguration to the end of his second term. In 2010, Obama signed into effect the Dodd–Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act. Passed as a response to the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis "2008 financial crisis"), it brought the most significant changes to [financial regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_regulation "Financial regulation") in the United States since the regulatory reform that followed the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression "Great Depression") under Democratic President Franklin D. Roosevelt.

In 2009, Obama signed into law the [National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2010 "National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2010"), which contained in it the Matthew Shepard and James Byrd Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act, the first addition to existing federal hate crime law in the United States since Democratic President Bill Clinton signed into law the Church Arson Prevention Act of 1996. The act expanded [existing federal hate crime laws in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hate_crime_laws_in_the_United_States "Hate crime laws in the United States"), and made it a federal crime to assault people based on sexual orientation, gender identity, or disability.

As president, Obama advanced LGBT rights. In 2010, he signed the [Don't Ask, Don't Tell Repeal Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Ask,_Don't_Tell_Repeal_Act "Don't Ask, Don't Tell Repeal Act"), which brought an end to "don't ask, don't tell" policy in the U.S. armed forces that banned open service from [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT "LGBT") people; the law went into effect the following year. In 2016, his administration brought an end to the ban on [transgender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender "Transgender") people serving openly in the U.S. armed forces. A [Gallup poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_poll "Gallup poll"), taken in the final days of Obama's term, showed that 68 percent of Americans believed the U.S. had made progress on LGBT rights during Obama's eight years in office.

Obama substantially escalated the use of [drone strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_strikes "Drone strikes") against suspected militants and terrorists associated with al-Qaeda and the [Taliban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban "Taliban"). In 2016, the last year of his presidency, the U.S. dropped 26,171 bombs on seven different countries. Obama left about 8,400 U.S. troops in [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan"), 5,262 in Iraq, 503 in Syria, 133 in Pakistan, 106 in Somalia, seven in Yemen, and two in Libya at the end of his presidency.

According to [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center "Pew Research Center") and [United States Bureau of Justice Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bureau_of_Justice_Statistics "United States Bureau of Justice Statistics"), from December 31, 2009, to December 31, 2015, inmates sentenced in U.S. federal custody declined by five percent. This is the largest decline in sentenced inmates in U.S. federal custody of any president since [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter"). By contrast, the federal prison population increased significantly under presidents Ronald Reagan, George H. W. Bush, Bill Clinton, and George W. Bush.

[Human Rights Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Watch "Human Rights Watch") (HRW) called Obama's human rights record "mixed", adding that "he has often treated human rights as a secondary interest—nice to support when the cost was not too high, but nothing like a top priority he championed."

Obama left office in January 2017 with a 60 percent approval rating. He gained 10 spots from the same survey in 2015 from the Brookings Institution that ranked him the 18th-greatest American president. In Gallup's 2018 job approval poll for the past 10 U.S. presidents, he received a 63 percent approval rating.

### Presidential library

The Barack Obama Presidential Center is Obama's [presidential library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_library_system "Presidential library system"). It is hosted by the University of Chicago and located in [Jackson Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_Park_(Chicago) "Jackson Park (Chicago)") on the South Side of Chicago.

### Awards and honors

Obama received the [Norwegian Nobel Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Committee "Norwegian Nobel Committee")'s [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Nobel_Peace_Prize "2009 Nobel Peace Prize") in 2009, [The Shoah Foundation Institute for Visual History and Education's Ambassador of Humanity Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_Shoah_Foundation "USC Shoah Foundation") in 2014, the [John F. Kennedy Profile in Courage Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy_Profile_in_Courage_Award "John F. Kennedy Profile in Courage Award") in 2017, and the [Robert F. Kennedy Center for Justice and Human Rights Ripple of Hope Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Kennedy_Human_Rights "Robert F. Kennedy Human Rights") in 2018. He was named [*TIME* Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")'s [*Time* Person of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year "Time Person of the Year") in 2008 and 2012. He also received two [Grammy Awards for Best Spoken Word Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Audio_Book,_Narration_&_Storytelling_Recording "Grammy Award for Best Audio Book, Narration & Storytelling Recording") for *[Dreams from My Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_from_My_Father "Dreams from My Father")* (2006), and *[The Audacity of Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Audacity_of_Hope "The Audacity of Hope")* (2008) as well as three [Primetime Emmy Awards for Outstanding Narrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Narrator "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Narrator") for *[Our Great National Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Great_National_Parks "Our Great National Parks")* (2022), *[Working: What We Do All Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working:_What_We_Do_All_Day "Working: What We Do All Day")* (2023), and *[Our Oceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Oceans "Our Oceans")* (2025). He also won two [Children's and Family Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_and_Family_Emmy_Awards "Children's and Family Emmy Awards"). In 2024 he became the first and so far only President from the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_of_the_United_States "Democratic Party of the United States") to win the [Sylvanus Thayer Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvanus_Thayer_Award "Sylvanus Thayer Award").
