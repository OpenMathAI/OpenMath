**Dmitry Andreyevich Muratov** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Дмитрий Андреевич Муратов; born 29 October 1961) is a Russian journalist, television presenter and the former [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief "Editor-in-chief") of the Russian newspaper *[Novaya Gazeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Gazeta "Novaya Gazeta")*. He was awarded the [2021 Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nobel_Peace_Prize "2021 Nobel Peace Prize") jointly with [Maria Ressa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Ressa "Maria Ressa") for "their efforts to safeguard freedom of expression, which is a precondition for democracy and lasting peace."

Muratov co-founded the pro-democracy newspaper *Novaya Gazeta* in 1993 with several other journalists. He was the newspaper's editor-in-chief from 1995 to 2017, and again assumed the position in 2019. The newspaper is known for its reporting on sensitive topics such as governmental corruption, human rights violations, electoral fraud, police violence, and other misuses of power. As editor-in-chief he was a vocal advocate for an independent press and published articles by [Anna Politkovskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Politkovskaya "Anna Politkovskaya") that scrutinised the [Putin administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_under_Vladimir_Putin "Russia under Vladimir Putin"). Muratov helped to create "the only truly critical newspaper with national influence in Russia today", according to the [Committee to Protect Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_to_Protect_Journalists "Committee to Protect Journalists"). During the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "Russian invasion of Ukraine"); on 28 March 2022, the newspaper announced that it would suspend its online and print activities after it received a second warning from [Roskomnadzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskomnadzor "Roskomnadzor").

On 1 September 2023, Muratov was declared by the Russian authorities to be a "[foreign agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_foreign_agent_law "Russian foreign agent law")". As of April 2026, Muratov continues to reside in Russia, publishing Youtube videos and a limited circulation magazine to comply with censorship laws and his foreign agent status.

## Early life and education

Dmitry Muratov was born on 29 October 1961 into a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians "Russians") family in the city of [Kuibyshev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara "Samara") (officially known since 1991 by its original name, [Samara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara "Samara")). He studied at the Faculty of Philology at [Kuibyshev (now Samara) State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara_State_University "Samara State University") for five years, where he discovered his interest in journalism. While in college he made contact with local newspapers and held a part-time job in journalism.

From 1983 to 1985, after graduating from university, he served in the [Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Army "Soviet Army") as a communication equipment security specialist.

## Early career

In 1987, Muratov began working as a correspondent for *Volzhsky Komsomolets* newspaper. His superiors were so impressed that by the end of his first year he was appointed to head of the *[Komsomolskaya Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komsomolskaya_Pravda "Komsomolskaya Pravda")* youth department, and later was promoted to editor of news articles. Muratov left Komsomolskaya Pravda in 1992.

## *Novaya Gazeta*

In 1993, Muratov and 50+ other colleagues from *Komsomolskaya Pravda* left to start their own paper titled *[Novaya Gazeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Gazeta "Novaya Gazeta")*. Their goal was to create a publication that was "an honest, independent, and rich" source for the citizens of Russia. The newspaper’s mission is to conduct in-depth investigations into human rights issues, corruption and abuse of power. *Novaya Gazeta*'s newsroom started out with two computers, two rooms, one printer and no salary for the employees. Former Soviet [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Soviet_Union "President of the Soviet Union") [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev "Mikhail Gorbachev") donated some of his Nobel Peace Prize money to pay for salaries and computers for the paper. Muratov helped to create *Novaya Gazeta*, where he was named Deputy Press Editor.

In December 1994 – January 1995, Muratov was a correspondent in the war zone of the [First Chechen War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Chechen_War "First Chechen War"). In 1995, he became the head of the editorial board. He held this position for over 20 years, stepping down in 2017, citing the exhausting nature of running the paper. In 2019, he returned to the position, after the paper’s staff voted for his return.

Muratov often reported on sensitive topics including human rights violations, high-level government corruption, and abuse of power. His political beliefs, such as supporting freedom of press, has led to conflict with fellow journalists and the government.

Muratov interviews [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Russia "President of Russia") [Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev "Dmitry Medvedev") in 2009

In 2004, the newspaper printed seven articles by columnist Georgy Rozhnov, which accused [Sergey Kiriyenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Kiriyenko "Sergey Kiriyenko") of embezzling US$4.8 billion of [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund") funds in 1998 when he was [Prime Minister of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Russia "Prime Minister of Russia"). The newspaper based the accusations on a letter allegedly written to [Colin Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Powell "Colin Powell") and signed by [U.S. Congressmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressman "Congressman") [Philip Crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Crane "Philip Crane"), [Mike Pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pence "Mike Pence"), [Charlie Norwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Norwood "Charlie Norwood"), [Dan Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Burton "Dan Burton") and [Henry Bonilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Bonilla "Henry Bonilla") and posted on the website of the American Defense Council. The newspaper claimed that Kiriyenko had used some of the embezzled funds to purchase real estate in the United States. It was later claimed that the letter was a prank concocted by *[The eXile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_eXile "The eXile")*. In response, Kiriyenko sued *Novaya Gazeta* and Rozhnov for libel, and in passing judgement in favour of Kiriyenko the court ordered *Novaya Gazeta* to retract all publications relating to the accusations and went on to say that the newspaper "is obliged to publish only officially proven information linking Mr Kiriyenko with embezzlement."

After *Novaya Gazeta* published an investigation by journalist Denis Korotkov about Russian businessman [Yevgeny Prigozhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevgeny_Prigozhin "Yevgeny Prigozhin"), in October 2018, Denis Korotkov and the editor-in-chief at *Novaya Gazeta* were the target of threatening deliveries of a severed ram's head and funeral flowers to the paper's offices. The style of the threat resembled others by Kremlin-linked [Yevgeny Prigozhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevgeny_Prigozhin "Yevgeny Prigozhin").

Dmitry Muratov at a ceremony in 2012

In 2016, the newspaper was involved in the publishing of the so-called [Panama Papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Papers "Panama Papers"), confidential documents from a [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama "Panama")-based law firm that had assisted companies and individuals from around the world in hiding their wealth.

His newspaper has been influential on shedding light of the turbulent situations in [Chechnya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechnya "Chechnya") and the [Northern Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Caucasus "Northern Caucasus") in general. *Novaya Gazeta* published reports about [anti-gay purges in Chechnya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-gay_purges_in_Chechnya "Anti-gay purges in Chechnya") in 2017, where three men were allegedly killed, and dozens detained and intimidated. After publication, the Chechen Government denied the existence of persecutions in the Republic.

The newspaper published the report by [Elena Milashina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Milashina "Elena Milashina") and the list of 27 Chechens killed on 26 January 2017. The newspaper also addressed the report and the list to the Russian government's [Investigative Committee of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_Committee_of_Russia "Investigative Committee of Russia"), and asked the committee to investigate the data in the published list. While *Novaya Gazeta* listed the names of 27 Chechens killed, the newspaper suspected that real number might be even more, with up to 56 Chechens killed that night. The newspaper said that the dead Chechens were citizens of the Republic of Chechnya, who were detained by the governmental security service, put in custody inside a secure compound belonging to the traffic police regiment in the City of [Grozny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grozny "Grozny"), and executed on 26 January by gunfire (several men brutally killed by [asphyxiation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asphyxia "Asphyxia")) by state security forces without any legal proceedings.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Dmitry_Muratov_-_Nemtsov_Bridge_2021.jpg/250px-Dmitry_Muratov_-_Nemtsov_Bridge_2021.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dmitry_Muratov_-_Nemtsov_Bridge_2021.jpg)

Muratov visited the [Bolshoy Moskvoretsky Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshoy_Moskvoretsky_Bridge "Bolshoy Moskvoretsky Bridge") in Moscow to honor [Boris Nemtsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Nemtsov "Boris Nemtsov")'s memory on 27 February 2021

During Muratov's time at the *Novaya Gazeta*, six of its journalists had been killed. In 2000, [Igor Domnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Domnikov "Igor Domnikov") was murdered in a [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow") apartment building. In 2001, [Victor Popkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Popkov "Victor Popkov"), a *Novaya Gazeta* contributor, died after being wounded in the crossfire of a gunfight in Chechnya. In 2003, [Yury Shchekochikhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yury_Shchekochikhin "Yury Shchekochikhin") was poisoned after investigating a corruption scandal where high-ranking Russian officials were involved. [Anna Politkovskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Politkovskaya "Anna Politkovskaya") was assassinated in her apartment block in 2006 after spending her career covering Chechnya and the Northern Caucasus. In 2009, [Anastasia Baburova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Baburova "Anastasia Baburova") was shot and killed on the street, while [Natalia Estemirova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Estemirova "Natalia Estemirova") was abducted and murdered.

After the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine") on 24 February 2022, Muratov released dual editions of his newspaper in both Russian and Ukrainian, and said that his newspaper would defy the Russian media watchdog's rules which he stated would lead to a situation in which only Russian government statements could be reported. On 4 March 2022, Russian President [Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin "Vladimir Putin") signed into law a bill introducing [prison sentences of up to 15 years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_2022_war_censorship_laws "Russian 2022 war censorship laws") for those who publish "knowingly false information" about the Russian armed forces and their operations, leading to some media outlets in Russia to stop reporting on Ukraine or shutting their media outlet. It introduced criminal liability for the dissemination of "knowingly false information" about the actions of the [Russian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Armed_Forces "Russian Armed Forces"), with the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation "Russian Federation") deciding what is the truth, "in order to protect the interests of the Russian Federation and its citizens and maintain international peace and security". As of December 2022, more than 4,000 people were prosecuted under "[fake news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news "Fake news")" laws in connection with the war in Ukraine.

On 28 March 2022, the newspaper suspended its print activities after receiving a second warning from [Roskomnadzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskomnadzor "Roskomnadzor"); on 6 April 2022, a foreign version of the paper (*Novaya Gazeta Europa*) was launched from [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga "Riga") in order to avoid censorship.

In April 2022, the second largest Norwegian media group [Amedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amedia "Amedia") announced it was handing over its four printing houses in Russia worth some 4 million Euro to Muratov, as it was ceasing its business activities in Russia. However, in the fall of 2023, five Prime Print printing houses, by decree of [Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin "Vladimir Putin"), were transferred first to the temporary management of the Federal Property Management Agency, and then to the Moscow authorities.

In December 2022, Muratov said that while Russia can "destroy" Ukraine's infrastructure, "it will never be able to conquer it". In March 2023, he predicted that there would never be "normal relations between the people of Russia and Ukraine" because "Ukraine will not be able to come to terms with this tragedy." He also said that "In Russia political repression will continue against all opponents of the regime" and that the only hope he has "lies with the young generation; those people who sees the world as a friend, not as an enemy". He warned that Russian state propaganda "is preparing people to think that [nuclear war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_war "Nuclear war") is not a bad thing." Russian nationalist [Telegram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegram_(software) "Telegram (software)") channels supporting the war in Ukraine accused Muratov of being pro-Ukrainian.

In March 2023, [Oleg Orlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Orlov_(biologist) "Oleg Orlov (biologist)"), co-chair of the Nobel Peace Prize-winning human rights group [Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_(society) "Memorial (society)"), was detained on charges of "repeatedly discrediting the armed forces". At the hearing, he was accompanied by Dmitry Muratov, who said that Orlov was being tried "for observing the [Russian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Russia "Constitution of Russia")" which, he argued, guarantees freedom of expression.

In May 2023, Muratov expressed support for playwrights [Evgenia Berkovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgenia_Berkovich "Evgenia Berkovich") and Svetlana Petriichuk, calling the case against them political persecution.

### Attack on train

On 7 April 2022, Muratov was attacked by an unknown person and covered with red paint mixed with acetone while on a train from Moscow to Samara, supposedly as an act of support for Russian troops; according to U.S. officials, the attack was organized by Russian intelligence services. The attack caused chemical burns to his eyes, led to four surgeries, and necessitated the requirement of a magnifying glass to read.

## Awards and honours

[![Muratov after having received the Four Freedoms Award](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Dmitry_Muratov_Four_Freedoms_Award_2010.jpg/250px-Dmitry_Muratov_Four_Freedoms_Award_2010.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dmitry_Muratov_Four_Freedoms_Award_2010.jpg)

Muratov after having received the [Four Freedoms Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Freedoms_Award "Four Freedoms Award")

Muratov is a decorated journalist who has received numerous awards and honours for his contributions to his craft. He received the [CPJ International Press Freedom Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPJ_International_Press_Freedom_Award "CPJ International Press Freedom Award") in 2007 from the [Committee to Protect Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_to_Protect_Journalists "Committee to Protect Journalists") for his bravery in defending the freedom of the press in the face of danger. On 29 January 2010, he was acknowledged by the French government for his devotion to the freedom of journalists. He was given the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour "Legion of Honour") order; France’s highest civil decoration. Muratov traveled to the Netherlands in May 2010 to receive the [Four Freedoms Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Freedoms_Award "Four Freedoms Award") for the *Novaya Gazeta*. In 2016, Muratov accepted the [Golden Pen of Freedom Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Association_of_Newspapers'_Golden_Pen_of_Freedom_Award "World Association of Newspapers' Golden Pen of Freedom Award") from the [World Association of Newspapers and News Publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Association_of_Newspapers_and_News_Publishers "World Association of Newspapers and News Publishers").

Muratov was awarded the [2021 Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nobel_Peace_Prize "2021 Nobel Peace Prize"), alongside [Maria Ressa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Ressa "Maria Ressa") of [the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Philippines "The Philippines"), "for their efforts to safeguard freedom of expression, which is a precondition for democracy and lasting peace." The Nobel Committee specifically commended *Novaya Gazeta*'s "critical articles on subjects ranging from corruption, police violence, unlawful arrests, electoral fraud and 'troll factories' to the use of Russian military forces both within and outside Russia." The [Nobel Prize Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_Committee "Nobel Prize Committee") was criticized for rewarding Muratov and not jailed Russian opposition leader [Alexei Navalny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Navalny "Alexei Navalny"), as an attempt by the Committee "to keep the maximum distance from the current political process" in Russia. Muratov is a member of the democratic political party [Yabloko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yabloko "Yabloko") that did not support the [Smart Voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Voting "Smart Voting") initiative by Navalny. The Kremlin congratulated Muratov on winning the Nobel prize. Muratov has said that he would have given the prize to Alexei Navalny if it were his choice.

During his acceptance speech, at a time when Russia began a major [military build-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prelude_to_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "Prelude to the Russian invasion of Ukraine") near the [Russia–Ukraine border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia–Ukraine_border "Russia–Ukraine border"), Muratov warned that "In [the] heads of some crazy geopoliticians, a war between Russia and Ukraine is not something impossible any longer."

In an interview with *[Meduza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meduza_(website) "Meduza (website)")*, Muratov commented that his Nobel Prize belongs to all journalists of *Novaya Gazeta* who were killed for conducting their investigations:

> It's not mine. I'm not the right beneficiary, there are real ones. It's just that the Nobel Peace Prize isn't awarded posthumously, it's awarded to living people. Obviously, they decided to award it to someone living, having in mind [Yury Shchekochikhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yury_Shchekochikhin "Yury Shchekochikhin"), [Igor Domnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Domnikov "Igor Domnikov"), [Anna Politkovskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Politkovskaya "Anna Politkovskaya"), [Anastasia Baburova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Baburova "Anastasia Baburova"), [Stanislav Markelov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislav_Markelov "Stanislav Markelov"), and [Natalya Estemirova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalya_Estemirova "Natalya Estemirova").

On 22 March 2022, Muratov decided to sell his Nobel Peace Prize medal to an auction, donating the proceeds to [UNICEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF "UNICEF") for the benefit of refugees from Ukraine. The medal auction was carried out by [Heritage Auctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_Auctions "Heritage Auctions") and was sold for US$103.5 million, the highest price ever recorded for a Nobel medal. Pro-Kremlin activists have reported the auction to Russian authorities, claiming he owes about $16M in taxes from the proceeds. As of April 2026, the tax investigation is ongoing.

In 2024, a documentary using real-time videos of how the staffs of *Novaya Gazeta,* Radio [Echo of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echo_of_Moscow "Echo of Moscow"), and [TV Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Rain "TV Rain") coped with the government's suppression of the freedom of speech, was made. The journalists in the film had to close their media and flee Russia for their own safety. Muratov's *Novaya Gazeta* first opened an office in Latvia, and then in Germany and Paris.
