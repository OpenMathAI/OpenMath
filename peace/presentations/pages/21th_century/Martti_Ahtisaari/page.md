**Martti Oiva Kalevi Ahtisaari** (23 June 1937 – 16 October 2023) was a Finnish politician and diplomat who was the [president of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Finland "President of Finland") from 1994 to 2000. He was Finland's Ambassador to [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania") from 1973 to 1977 and [United Nations Commissioner for Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Commissioner_for_Namibia "United Nations Commissioner for Namibia") from 1977 to 1981. Noted for his international [peace work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_work "Peace work"), he received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize") in 2008.

Ahtisaari was a [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") special envoy for [Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo "Kosovo"), charged with organizing the [Kosovo status process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_status_process "Kosovo status process") negotiations. These negotiations aimed to resolve a long-running dispute in Kosovo, which later [declared its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Kosovo_declaration_of_independence "2008 Kosovo declaration of independence") from [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia "Serbia") in 2008. In October 2008, he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize "for his important efforts, on several continents and over more than three decades, to resolve international conflicts". The Nobel statement said that Ahtisaari had played a prominent role in resolving serious and long-lasting conflicts, including ones in [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia "Namibia"), [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh "Aceh") (Indonesia), Kosovo and Serbia, and [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq").

## Youth and early career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/4-vuotias_Martti_Ahtisaari.jpg/250px-4-vuotias_Martti_Ahtisaari.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:4-vuotias_Martti_Ahtisaari.jpg)

4-year-old Ahtisaari, pictured on his birthday

Martti Ahtisaari was born in [Viipuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viipuri "Viipuri"), Finland (now Vyborg, Russia) on 23 June 1937. His father, Oiva Ahtisaari, whose grandfather Julius Marenius Adolfsen had emigrated with his parents to [Kotka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotka "Kotka"), Finland in 1872 from [Tistedalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tistedalen "Tistedalen") in Southern Norway, took [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_nationality_law "Finnish nationality law") [citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship "Citizenship") in 1929 and [Finnicized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnicization "Finnicization") his surname from Adolfsen in 1935. Oiva was working as a [NCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commissioned_officer "Non-commissioned officer") in the supply troops in Viipuri when Martti was born.

The [Continuation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuation_War "Continuation War") (World War II) took Oiva Ahtisaari to the front as a non-commissioned officer army mechanic, while Martti's mother, Tyyne, moved to [Kuopio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuopio "Kuopio") with her son to escape immediate danger from the war in 1940. Kuopio was where Ahtisaari spent most of his childhood, eventually attending [Kuopion Lyseo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuopion_Lyseon_lukio "Kuopion Lyseon lukio") high school.

In 1952, Martti Ahtisaari moved to [Oulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oulu "Oulu") with his family. There he continued his education in [high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oulun_Lyseon_Lukio "Oulun Lyseon Lukio"), graduating in 1956. He also joined the local [YMCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YMCA "YMCA"). After completing his military service (Ahtisaari held the rank of [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_(Land) "Captain (Land)") in the [Finnish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Army "Finnish Army") Reserve), he began to study at Oulu teachers' college, attending the two-year course which enabled him to qualify as a primary-school teacher in 1959.

In the summer of 1960, Ahtisaari signed the contract for the position of director of the Swedish Agency for International Development physical education boarding school in [Karachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi "Karachi"), Pakistan, after interviewing in Sweden and hearing about the offer announced by the YMCA in April of that year. There, he also trained as a teacher.

He returned to Finland in 1963 and began his studies in the [Helsinki School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalto_University_School_of_Business "Aalto University School of Business") and soon became the Executive Director of the Helsinki International Student Club and Student International Aid, where he made friends with Namibian [Nickey Iyambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickey_Iyambo "Nickey Iyambo"). He also joined the international students' organisation [AIESEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIESEC "AIESEC"). In 1965, he joined the [Ministry for Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_for_Foreign_Affairs_(Finland) "Ministry for Foreign Affairs (Finland)") in its Bureau for International Development Aid, to set up the International Development Assistance Office together with Jaakko Iloniemi, which was a pioneering office, as the Finnish presence in international cooperation in the Third World was non-existent. Ahtisaari remained in that office until 1972, where he served from 1971 as assistant to the director, a position he combined with his presence on the Government's Advisory Committee for Trade and Industry Affairs of Developing Countries.

## Diplomatic career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Martti_Ahtisaari_%281987_UN_photo%29.jpg/250px-Martti_Ahtisaari_%281987_UN_photo%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Martti_Ahtisaari_(1987_UN_photo).jpg)

Ahtisaari as a United Nations Under-Secretary-General, 1987

### In the Namibian independence transition

Ahtisaari began his diplomatic career in 1973 when he became Finland's Ambassador to [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania"), [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia "Zambia"), [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia "Somalia") and [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique "Mozambique"), an office he held until 1977. This new mission allowed him to get closer to East African affairs, monitoring from [Dar es Salaam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_es_Salaam "Dar es Salaam") the independence process of [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia "Namibia") and maintaining close contacts with [South West Africa People's Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWAPO "SWAPO") (SWAPO). In 1977 he was recalled by the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") to succeed [Seán MacBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seán_MacBride "Seán MacBride") as [United Nations Commissioner for Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Commissioner_for_Namibia "United Nations Commissioner for Namibia"), a post he held until 1981, and as representative of Secretary-General [Kurt Waldheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Waldheim "Kurt Waldheim") from 1978.

Following the death of a later UN Commissioner for Namibia, [Bernt Carlsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernt_Carlsson "Bernt Carlsson"), on [Pan Am Flight 103](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am_Flight_103 "Pan Am Flight 103") on 21 December 1988 – on the eve of the signing of the [Tripartite Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_Accord_(Angola) "Tripartite Accord (Angola)") at [UN Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Headquarters "United Nations Headquarters") – Ahtisaari was sent to [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia "Namibia") in April 1989 as the [UN Special Representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Representative_of_the_Secretary-General "Special Representative of the Secretary-General") to head the [United Nations Transition Assistance Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Transition_Assistance_Group "United Nations Transition Assistance Group") (UNTAG). Because of the illegal incursion of [SWAPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWAPO "SWAPO") troops from [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola "Angola"), the South African appointed Administrator-General (AG), [Louis Pienaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Pienaar "Louis Pienaar"), sought Ahtisaari's agreement to the deployment of [SADF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SADF "SADF") troops to stabilize the situation. Ahtisaari took advice from British prime minister [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher "Margaret Thatcher"), who was visiting the region at the time, and approved the SADF deployment. A period of intense fighting ensued when at least 375 SWAPO insurgents were killed. In July 1989, [Glenys Kinnock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenys_Kinnock "Glenys Kinnock") and [Tessa Blackstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessa_Blackstone "Tessa Blackstone") of the [British Council of Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Council_of_Churches "British Council of Churches") visited Namibia and reported: "There is a widespread feeling that too many concessions were made to South African personnel and preferences and that Martti Ahtisaari was not forceful enough in his dealings with the South Africans."

Perhaps because of his reluctance to authorise this [SADF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SADF "SADF") deployment, Ahtisaari was alleged to have been targeted by the [South African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa") [Civil Cooperation Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Cooperation_Bureau "Civil Cooperation Bureau") (CCB). According to a hearing in September 2000 of the South African [Truth and Reconciliation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_and_Reconciliation_Commission_(South_Africa) "Truth and Reconciliation Commission (South Africa)"), two CCB operatives (Kobus le Roux and Ferdinand Barnard) were tasked not to kill Ahtisaari, but to give him "a good hiding". To carry out the assault, Barnard had planned to use the grip handle of a metal saw as a knuckleduster. In the event, Ahtisaari did not attend the meeting at the Keetmanshoop Hotel, where Le Roux and Barnard lay in wait for him, and thus Ahtisaari escaped injury.

After the independence elections of 1989, Ahtisaari and his wife were made honorary Namibian citizens in 1992. South Africa gave him the [O R Tambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Tambo "Oliver Tambo") award for "his outstanding achievement as a diplomat and commitment to the cause of freedom in Africa and peace in the world".

Ahtisaari served as UN undersecretary-general for administration and management from 1987 to 1991 causing mixed feelings inside the organisation during an internal investigation of massive fraud. When Ahtisaari revealed in 1990 that he had secretly lengthened the grace period allowing UN officials to return misappropriated taxpayer money from the original three months to three years, the investigators were furious. The 340 officials found guilty of fraud were able to return money even after their crime had been proven. The harshest punishment was the firing of twenty corrupt officials.

### Other roles

On 31 July 1991, he was appointed Secretary of State at the [Ministry for Foreign Affairs of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_for_Foreign_Affairs_(Finland) "Ministry for Foreign Affairs (Finland)") in the [Esko Aho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esko_Aho "Esko Aho")'s government. After the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War "Gulf War"), Ahtisaari headed a team tasked with reporting to the UN on changes in the situation and humanitarian needs. The report did not meet these expectations and was believed to have eroded American support for Ahtisaari's candidacy for the UN Secretary-General.

Between 1992 and 1993, Ahtisaari chaired the UN Conference on Yugoslavia's Working Group on Bosnia and Herzegovina and became the special assistant to [Cyrus Vance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Vance "Cyrus Vance"), the Special Envoy of the Secretary-General of the United Nations for Croatia.

Finland's ongoing [recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recession "Recession") caused established political figures to lose public support, and the [presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_elections "Presidential elections") were now direct instead of being conducted through an electoral college. The [Social Democratic Party of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Finland "Social Democratic Party of Finland")'s candidate to succeed [Mauno Koivisto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauno_Koivisto "Mauno Koivisto") as President of Finland was decided in a primary between Ahtisaari and former Prime Minister and diplomat [Kalevi Sorsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalevi_Sorsa "Kalevi Sorsa"). Ahtisaari led in the polls against Sorsa, who was already a popular and experienced politician and won the primaries on 16 May 1993 with 61% of the votes.

After the primaries, Ahtisaari returned to his work in Geneva, and did not start his presidential campaign until the end of October. Ahtisaari narrowly won over his second round opponent, [Elisabeth Rehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_Rehn "Elisabeth Rehn") of the [Swedish People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_People's_Party_(Finland) "Swedish People's Party (Finland)"). During the campaign, there were rumours spread by some political opponents of Ahtisaari that he had a drinking problem or that he had knowingly accepted a double salary from the Finnish Foreign Ministry and from the United Nations while trying to negotiate an end to the Bosnian War. Ahtisaari denied both allegations and no firm proof of them has emerged. During the three-week campaign between the two rounds of presidential elections, Ahtisaari was praised by his supporters for being more compassionate towards the many unemployed Finns than Rehn, who as Defence Minister had to officially support the Aho government's strict economic policies. A minor controversy arose during a town hall-style presidential debate in [Lappeenranta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lappeenranta "Lappeenranta"), southeastern Finland when an apparently born-again Christian woman in the audience asked Rehn what her relationship with Jesus was. Rehn replied that she had personally no proof that Jesus had been a historical person. Ahtisaari ducked a precise answer by stating that he trusted the Lutheran confession even on this issue. He was sworn in on 1 March 1994.

His term as president began with a schism within the [Centre Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_Party_(Finland) "Centre Party (Finland)") government led by prime minister [Esko Aho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esko_Aho "Esko Aho"), who did not approve of Ahtisaari's being actively involved in foreign policy. There was also some controversy over Ahtisaari's speaking out on domestic issues such as unemployment. He travelled extensively in Finland and abroad, and was nicknamed "Matka-Mara" (*"Travel-Mara"*, Mara being a common diminutive form of Martti). His monthly travels throughout the country and his meetings with ordinary citizens (the so-called *maakuntamatkat* or "provincial trips") nonetheless greatly enhanced his political popularity. Ahtisaari kept his campaign promise to visit one Finnish historical province every month during his presidency. He also donated some thousands of [Finnish marks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_mark "Finnish mark") per month to the unemployed people's organisations, and a few thousand Finnish marks to the Christian social organisation of the late lay preacher and social worker [Veikko Hursti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veikko_Hursti "Veikko Hursti").

Ahtisaari favoured pluralism and religious tolerance publicly. Privately, he and his wife practised their Christian faith. Contrary to some of his predecessors and his successor as the Finnish President, Ahtisaari ended all of his New Year's speeches by wishing the Finnish people God's blessing.

In January 1998 Ahtisaari was criticized by some NGOs, politicians and notable cultural figures because he awarded Commander of the [Order of the Lion of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Lion_of_Finland "Order of the Lion of Finland") to the Forest Minister of [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") and to the main owner of the Indonesian RGM Company, a parent company of the April Company. The April Company was criticized by non-governmental organisations for destroying [rain forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_forest "Rain forest"), and Indonesia itself was criticized heavily for human rights violations, especially in [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Timor "East Timor"). Ahtisaari's party chairman [Erkki Tuomioja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erkki_Tuomioja "Erkki Tuomioja") said that giving medals was questionable since he feared the act may tarnish the public image of Finnish human rights policy. Students of the arts had demonstrations in Helsinki against the decision to give medals.

President Ahtisaari publicly supported Finland's entry into the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union"), and in a 1994 [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Referendum "Referendum"), 57 percent of Finnish voters were in favour of EU membership. He later stated that if Finland had not voted to join the EU he would have resigned. The promotion of a European collective security system and Nordic cooperation, as well as a security policy without membership of NATO, were central to Ahtisaari's foreign policy.

During Ahtisaari's term as president, [Boris Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin "Boris Yeltsin") and [Bill Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Clinton "Bill Clinton") met in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki "Helsinki"). He also negotiated alongside [Viktor Chernomyrdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Chernomyrdin "Viktor Chernomyrdin") with [Slobodan Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobodan_Milošević "Slobodan Milošević") to end the [fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_War "Kosovo War") in the [Yugoslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia "Federal Republic of Yugoslavia") province of [Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo "Kosovo") in 1999.

Ahtisaari's lack of restrained involvement in public affairs and his pronouncements on domestic and economic policy provoked reservations both in Parliament itself and in the Social Democratic Party of Finland, and led Ahtisaari not to stand for re-election in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Finnish_presidential_election "2000 Finnish presidential election"), which was announced in April 1999, and also alleged that two members of the SDP also ran as candidates. Ahtisaari was the last "strong president", before the [2000 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Finland "Constitution of Finland") reduced the president's powers. He was succeeded by [Tarja Halonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarja_Halonen "Tarja Halonen") on 1 March 2000.

## Post-presidential life

Ahtisaari mediating the [Kosovo crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_War "Kosovo War") with U.S. and Russian defence ministers in 1999

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Martti_Ahtisaari_at_the_World_Economic_Forum.jpg/250px-Martti_Ahtisaari_at_the_World_Economic_Forum.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Martti_Ahtisaari_at_the_World_Economic_Forum.jpg)

Martti Ahtisaari at the [World Economic Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum "World Economic Forum") in 2000

In Finnish politics, Ahtisaari long stressed how important it is for [Finland to join NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland–NATO_relations "Finland–NATO relations"). Ahtisaari argued that Finland should be a full member of NATO and the EU in order "to shrug off once and for all the burden of [Finlandization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finlandization "Finlandization")". He believed politicians should file an application and make Finland a member. He said that the way Finnish politicians avoided expressing their opinions was disturbing. He also noted that the so-called "NATO option" (acquiring membership if Finland were to be threatened) was an illusion, making an analogy to trying to obtain fire insurance when the fire has already started. Finland joined NATO on 4 April 2023, while Ahtisaari was still alive.

After leaving office, Ahtisaari held positions in various international organisations. In 2000, he became Chairman of the Brussels-based [International Crisis Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Crisis_Group "International Crisis Group"), an NGO to which he committed $100,000 in government funding in 1994 one month after becoming elected President of Finland. He remained Chairman Emeritus.

Ahtisaari also founded the independent [Crisis Management Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_Management_Initiative "Crisis Management Initiative") (CMI) with the goal of developing and sustaining peace in troubled areas. On 1 December 2000, Ahtisaari was awarded the J. William Fulbright Prize for International Understanding by the [Fulbright Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Association "Fulbright Association") in recognition of his work as a peacemaker in some of the world's most troubled areas. In May 2017 Ahtisaari suggested as new CMI leader [Alexander Stubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Stubb "Alexander Stubb"), a former [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Finland "Prime Minister of Finland") and future [President of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Finland "President of Finland"), representing [Finnish conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_Finland "Conservatism in Finland") i.e. the [National Coalition Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coalition_Party "National Coalition Party").

In 2000–01, Ahtisaari and [Cyril Ramaphosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Ramaphosa "Cyril Ramaphosa") inspected [IRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army "Provisional Irish Republican Army") weapons dumps for the [Independent International Commission on Decommissioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_International_Commission_on_Decommissioning "Independent International Commission on Decommissioning"), as part of the [Northern Ireland peace process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_peace_process "Northern Ireland peace process").

In 2003 Ahtisaari defended [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush")'s [attack to Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_war "Iraq war"), describing it as humanitarian intervention, which incited criticism from professor of history [Juha Sihvola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juha_Sihvola "Juha Sihvola").

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Perjanjian_damai_helsinki.jpg/250px-Perjanjian_damai_helsinki.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Perjanjian_damai_helsinki.jpg)

Agreement to end [insurgency in Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Aceh "Insurgency in Aceh") signed in Helsinki, 2005

In November 2005, [UN Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Secretary-General "UN Secretary-General") [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan "Kofi Annan") appointed Ahtisaari as Special Envoy for the [Kosovo status process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_status_process "Kosovo status process") which was to determine whether Kosovo, having been administered by the United Nations since 1999, should become independent or remain a province of [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia "Serbia"). In early 2006, Ahtisaari opened the UN Office of the Special Envoy for Kosovo (UNOSEK) in [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna "Vienna"), Austria, from where he conducted the Kosovo status negotiations. Those opposed to Ahtisaari's settlement proposal, which involved an internationally monitored independence for Kosovo, sought to discredit him. Allegations made by Balkan media sources of corruption and improper conduct by Ahtisaari were described by [US State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_State "United States Department of State") spokesman Tom Casey as "spurious", adding that Ahtisaari's plan is the "best solution possible" and has the "full endorsement of the United States". *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* suggested that this criticism of Ahtisaari on the part of the Serbs had led to the "bogging down" of the Kosovo status talks. In November 2008, Serbian media reported Pierre Mirel, director of the EU enlargement commission's western Balkans division as saying: "The EU has accepted that the deployment of [EULEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EULEX "EULEX") has to be approved by the [United Nations Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council "United Nations Security Council"), and that the mission has to be neutral and will not be related to the Ahtisaari plan," Mirel said, following his meeting with Serbia's vice-president [Bozidar Djelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozidar_Djelic "Bozidar Djelic").

In July 2007, however, when the [EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union"), Russia and the United States agreed to find a new format for the talks, Ahtisaari announced that he regarded his mission as over. Since neither the UN nor the troika had asked him to continue mediations in the face of Russia's persistent refusal to support independence for Kosovo, he said he would nonetheless be willing to take on "a role as consultant", if requested. After a period of uncertainty and mounting tension, Kosovo unilaterally declared its independence from Serbia in February 2008.

In his work, he emphasised the importance of the United States in the peace process, stating that "There can be no peace without America."

Ahtisaari was chairman of the [Interpeace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpeace "Interpeace") Governing Council from 2000 to 2009.
Beginning in 2009, Ahtisaari was Chairman Emeritus and a Special Advisor.

Ahtisaari was board director of the [ImagineNations Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ImagineNations_Group "ImagineNations Group").

That same year he received the 2007 [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") [Félix Houphouët-Boigny Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Félix_Houphouët-Boigny_Peace_Prize "Félix Houphouët-Boigny Peace Prize"), for "his lifetime contribution to world peace".

In September 2009 Ahtisaari joined [The Elders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elders_(organization) "The Elders (organization)"), a group of independent global leaders who work together on peace and human rights issues. He travelled to the [Korean Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Peninsula "Korean Peninsula") with fellow Elders [Gro Harlem Brundtland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gro_Harlem_Brundtland "Gro Harlem Brundtland"), [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter") and [Mary Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Robinson "Mary Robinson") in April 2011, and to [South Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudan "South Sudan") with Robinson and Archbishop [Desmond Tutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Tutu "Desmond Tutu") in July 2012.

He was also a member of the board of the [European Council on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council_on_Foreign_Relations "European Council on Foreign Relations").

### Nobel Peace Prize

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Nobel_Peace_Prize_2008_Martti_Ahtisaari.jpg/250px-Nobel_Peace_Prize_2008_Martti_Ahtisaari.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nobel_Peace_Prize_2008_Martti_Ahtisaari.jpg)

Nobel Peace Prize 2008

On 10 October 2008, Ahtisaari was announced as that year's recipient of the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"). Ahtisaari received the prize on 10 December 2008 at [Oslo City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_City_Hall "Oslo City Hall") in Norway. Ahtisaari twice worked to find a solution in [Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo "Kosovo") – first in 1999 and again between 2005 and 2007. The committee said he also worked with others this year to find a peaceful solution to the problems in [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq"). According to the committee, Ahtisaari and his group, [Crisis Management Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_Management_Initiative "Crisis Management Initiative") (CMI), also contributed to resolving other conflicts in [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland "Northern Ireland"), [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), and the [Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_of_Africa "Horn of Africa"). Ahtisaari invited Prime Minister [Matti Vanhanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matti_Vanhanen "Matti Vanhanen"), Foreign Affairs Minister [Alexander Stubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Stubb "Alexander Stubb") and others to his Nobel event, but not President Halonen.

According to the memoir of the former secretary of the [Norwegian Nobel Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Committee "Norwegian Nobel Committee"), [Geir Lundestad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geir_Lundestad "Geir Lundestad"), former Foreign Minister and UN ambassador [Keijo Korhonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keijo_Korhonen_(politician) "Keijo Korhonen (politician)"), who was strongly against awarding the 2008 Nobel Peace Prize to Ahtisaari, wrote a letter to the committee which negatively portrayed Ahtisaari as a person and his merits in international conflict zones.

### Syria conflict

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/The_Elders_%289358747992%29.jpg/250px-The_Elders_%289358747992%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Elders_(9358747992).jpg)

Ahtisaari (first from left) with [William Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hague "William Hague"), [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter"), and [Lakhdar Brahimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakhdar_Brahimi "Lakhdar Brahimi") from The Elders group in London, 24 July 2013

In August 2012, Ahtisaari opined on the sectarian violence in [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_civil_war "Syrian civil war") and was mentioned as a possible replacement as Joint Envoy there to succeed former [Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-General_of_the_United_Nations "Secretary-General of the United Nations") [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan "Kofi Annan"). However, Ahtisaari then told the Finnish state broadcaster [YLE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YLE "YLE") that "he wished the mission would fall on someone else" which it ultimately did in the person of [Lakhdar Brahimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakhdar_Brahimi "Lakhdar Brahimi"), a former [Algerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria "Algeria") foreign minister and longtime U.N. diplomat.

In late 2015, Martti Ahtisaari reiterated charges he already had made in an interview with German broadcaster [Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle "Deutsche Welle") in early 2013 against members of the [UN security council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_security_council "UN security council") on the obstruction of a political solution to the escalating conflict in Syria. Ahtisaari said in an interview in September 2015 that he held talks about Syria with envoys from the five permanent members of the [UN security council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_security_council "UN security council") in February 2012. According to Ahtisaari, [Vitaly Churkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaly_Churkin "Vitaly Churkin"), Russian ambassador to the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations"), laid out three points during a meeting with him, which included not arming the Syrian opposition, commencing talks between Syrian president [Assad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashar_al-Assad "Bashar al-Assad") and the opposition and finding "an elegant way for Assad to step aside". But the US, Britain and France subsequently ignored the proposal. Ahtisaari said in the interview: "Nothing happened because I think all these, and many others, were convinced that Assad would be thrown out of office in a few weeks so there was no need to do anything."

## Personal life, health and death

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Paavo_Lipponen_sek%C3%A4_Eeva_ja_Martti_Ahtisaari_vaalivalvojaisissa.jpg/250px-Paavo_Lipponen_sek%C3%A4_Eeva_ja_Martti_Ahtisaari_vaalivalvojaisissa.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Paavo_Lipponen_sekä_Eeva_ja_Martti_Ahtisaari_vaalivalvojaisissa.jpg)

Ahtisaari with his wife [Eeva Ahtisaari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eeva_Ahtisaari "Eeva Ahtisaari") (second from left), 1994

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Presidentti_Ahtisaaren_valtiolliset_hautajaiset_10.11.2023_-_38.jpg/250px-Presidentti_Ahtisaaren_valtiolliset_hautajaiset_10.11.2023_-_38.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Presidentti_Ahtisaaren_valtiolliset_hautajaiset_10.11.2023_-_38.jpg)

[State funeral of Martti Ahtisaari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funeral_of_Martti_Ahtisaari "State funeral of Martti Ahtisaari")

In 1968, he married [Eeva Irmeli Hyvärinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eeva_Ahtisaari "Eeva Ahtisaari"), who was studying history at the University of Helsinki and whom he met as a child at the Lyceum in Kuopio. They had one son, [Marko Ahtisaari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marko_Ahtisaari "Marko Ahtisaari"), who was born in 1969.

On 24 March 2020, amid the [large-scale outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_coronavirus_pandemic_in_Europe "2020 coronavirus pandemic in Europe") of [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_disease_2019 "Coronavirus disease 2019"), it was announced that Ahtisaari had tested positive for the disease. His spouse, Eeva Ahtisaari, was diagnosed with the same virus on 21 March. Eeva Ahtisaari had attended the [International Women's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Women's_Day "International Women's Day") concert on 8 March at the [Helsinki Music Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Music_Centre "Helsinki Music Centre") while infected. On 14 April 2020 it was announced that Martti and Eeva Ahtisaari were recovering from the coronavirus infection.

On 2 September 2021, it was announced that Ahtisaari had [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease "Alzheimer's disease") and had retired from public life.

Ahtisaari died from complications of Alzheimer's disease in Helsinki, on 16 October 2023, at age 86. [His state funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funeral_of_Martti_Ahtisaari "State funeral of Martti Ahtisaari") was held on 10 November 2023 in [Helsinki Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Cathedral "Helsinki Cathedral") at 1 p.m., after which he was buried at the [Hietaniemi Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hietaniemi_Cemetery "Hietaniemi Cemetery") in Helsinki.
