The **Center for Civil Liberties** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language "Ukrainian language"): Центр Громадянських Свобод, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian "Romanization of Ukrainian"): *Tsentr Hromadyansʹkykh Svobod*) is a [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainia "Ukrainia") [human rights organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_organization "Human rights organization") led by the Ukrainian lawyer [Oleksandra Matviichuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandra_Matviichuk "Oleksandra Matviichuk"). It was founded in 2007, with the purpose of pressuring Ukraine's government to make the country more democratic. The organization was awarded the [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nobel_Peace_Prize "2022 Nobel Peace Prize") [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"), jointly with [Ales Bialiatski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ales_Bialiatski "Ales Bialiatski") and Russian organization [Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_(society) "Memorial (society)").

## History

Center for Civil Liberties was founded in [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv "Kyiv"), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine") on 30 May 2007. The organization is engaged in introducing legislative amendments in an attempt to make Ukraine more democratic and to improve the public control of [law enforcement agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Template:Law_enforcement_agencies_of_Ukraine "Template:Law enforcement agencies of Ukraine") and [the judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Ukraine "Judiciary of Ukraine"). One of the organisation's focuses is the updating of the [Criminal Code of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Code_of_Ukraine "Criminal Code of Ukraine").

At the time of the 2013—2014 [Euromaidan protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan "Euromaidan"), the group started the Euromaidan SOS project to provide legal support to protesters who took part in the Euromaidan protests and to monitor abuses done by then-president [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych "Viktor Yanukovych")'s security forces.

After the [2014 Russian annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation "Annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation") and the start of the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas "War in Donbas") (also in 2014), the organization started documenting political persecution in [Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimea "Crimea") and crimes in the territory controlled by the Russian-backed separatist [Luhansk People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic "Luhansk People's Republic") and [Donetsk People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donetsk_People's_Republic "Donetsk People's Republic"). The organization also started international campaigns for the release of illegally imprisoned people in [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia"), Russian-annexed Crimea and [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas "Donbas").

Following the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine"), the Center for Civil Liberties also started to document [Russian war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crimes_in_the_2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine "War crimes in the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine") committed during the war. The [Norwegian Nobel Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Committee "Norwegian Nobel Committee") said in 2022 that the organization was "playing a pioneering role in holding guilty parties [accountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accountability "Accountability") for their [crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crime "War crime")".

On 7 October 2022, the Center for Civil Liberties was awarded the [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nobel_Peace_Prize "2022 Nobel Peace Prize") [Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize "Nobel Peace Prize"), jointly with [Ales Bialiatski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ales_Bialiatski "Ales Bialiatski") and Russian organization [Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_(society) "Memorial (society)"). This was the first ever Nobel Prize awarded to a Ukrainian citizen or organization. At the time of a 8 October 2022 press conference Center for Civil Liberties head [Oleksandra Matviichuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandra_Matviichuk "Oleksandra Matviichuk") admitted that neither [Ukrainian President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_President "Ukrainian President") [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Zelenskyy "Volodymyr Zelenskyy") nor any other (Ukrainian) government official had congratulated Center for Civil Liberties on winning the Nobel Prize. Matviichuk said that they might have tried but could have been unsuccessful because she and her colleague "were just returning from a business trip."

In November 2022 Oleksandra Matviichuk called for countries to provide Ukraine with weapons to liberate the occupied territories in Ukraine as the best way to stop the long-lasting crimes being committed by the Russian Federation.

In the spring of 2024, the Center for Civil Liberties was labeled as an "[undesirable organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_undesirable_organizations_law "Russian undesirable organizations law")" in Russia.

## Name

According to the organization's statute, the organization's full name is **Centre for Civil Liberties Civil Society Organisation** and the abbreviated denomination is **Centre for Civil Liberties**. On its web site, the organization mostly calls itself **Center for Civil Liberties**.
