**Graeme Bryce Segal** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (born 21 December 1941) is an Australian [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"), and professor at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford").

## Biography

Segal was educated at the [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney "University of Sydney"), where he received his [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSc "BSc") degree in 1961. He went on to receive his [D.Phil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.Phil. "D.Phil.") in 1967 from [St Catherine's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Catherine's_College,_Oxford "St Catherine's College, Oxford"); his thesis, written under the supervision of [Michael Atiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Atiyah "Michael Atiyah"), was titled *Equivariant K-theory*.

His thesis was in the area of equivariant [K-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-theory "K-theory"). The [Atiyah–Segal completion theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiyah–Segal_completion_theorem "Atiyah–Segal completion theorem") in that subject was a major motivation for the [Segal conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segal_conjecture "Segal conjecture"), which he formulated. He has made many other contributions to [homotopy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_theory "Homotopy theory") in the past four decades, including an approach to [infinite loop spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_loop_space "Infinite loop space"). He was also a pioneer of [elliptic cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_cohomology "Elliptic cohomology"), which is related to his interest in [topological quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_quantum_field_theory "Topological quantum field theory").

Segal was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") in 1970 in Nice and in 1990 in Kyoto. He taught at Oxford University from 1964 to 1990, then became the [Lowndean Professor of Astronomy and Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowndean_Professor_of_Astronomy_and_Geometry "Lowndean Professor of Astronomy and Geometry") at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") from 1990 to 1999, and then a Senior Research Fellow of [All Souls College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Souls_College,_Oxford "All Souls College, Oxford") from 1999 to 2009. He was elected the President of the [London Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Mathematical_Society "London Mathematical Society") in 2011.

He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") in 1982 and was awarded the [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal "Sylvester Medal") by the Royal Society in 2010.

At the 2026 [ICM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians"), Segal was awarded the [Chern Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern_Medal "Chern Medal") "for his visionary mathematical insights that have had an enduring influence in a wide range of fields, including topology, mathematical physics, representation theory and category theory."

He is married to writer [Marina Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Warner "Marina Warner"). They live in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London").
