**Yuri Vladimirovich Linnik** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Ю́рий Влади́мирович Ли́нник; January 8, 1915 – June 30, 1972) was a Soviet mathematician active in [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory"), [probability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_theory "Probability theory") and [mathematical statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_statistics "Mathematical statistics").

## Biography

Linnik was born in [Bila Tserkva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bila_Tserkva "Bila Tserkva"), in present-day [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine"). He went to [Saint Petersburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University "Saint Petersburg State University") where his supervisor was Vladimir Tartakovsky, and later worked at that university and the [Steklov Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Institute_of_Mathematics "Steklov Institute of Mathematics"). He was a member of the [Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union "Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union"), as was his father, Vladimir Pavlovich Linnik. He was awarded both [Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize "USSR State Prize") and [Lenin Prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin_Prize "Lenin Prize"). He died in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg "Saint Petersburg").

## Work in number theory

- [Linnik's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linnik's_theorem "Linnik's theorem") in analytic number theory
- The dispersion method (which allowed him to solve the Titchmarsh problem).
- The [large sieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_sieve "Large sieve") (which turned out to be extremely influential).
- An elementary proof of the [Hilbert-Waring theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waring's_problem "Waring's problem"); see also [Schnirelmann density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schnirelmann_density "Schnirelmann density").
- The Linnik ergodic method, see [Linnik (1968)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Linnik#CITEREFLinnik1968), which allowed him to study the distribution properties of the representations of integers by integral ternary [quadratic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_form "Quadratic form").

## Work in probability theory and statistics

### Infinitely divisible distributions

Linnik obtained numerous results concerning [infinitely divisible distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_divisibility_(probability) "Infinite divisibility (probability)"). In particular, he proved the following generalisation of [Cramér's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cramér's_decomposition_theorem "Cramér's decomposition theorem"): any divisor of a convolution of Gaussian and Poisson random variables is also a convolution of Gaussian and Poisson.

He has also coauthored the book [Linnik & Ostrovskii (1977)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Linnik#CITEREFLinnikOstrovskii1977) on the arithmetics of infinitely divisible distributions.

### Central limit theorem

- [Linnik zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linnik_zones?action=edit&redlink=1 "Linnik zones") (zones of asymptotic normality)
- [Information-theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_theory "Information theory") proof of the [central limit theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_limit_theorem "Central limit theorem")

### Statistics

- [Behrens–Fisher problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behrens–Fisher_problem "Behrens–Fisher problem")
