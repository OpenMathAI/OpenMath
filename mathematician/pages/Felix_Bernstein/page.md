**Felix Bernstein** (24 February 1878 – 3 December 1956), was a German [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") known for proving in 1896 the [Schröder–Bernstein theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schröder–Bernstein_theorem "Schröder–Bernstein theorem"), a central result in [set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_theory "Set theory"), and less well known for demonstrating in 1924 the correct [blood group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_type "Blood type") inheritance pattern of multiple [alleles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele "Allele") at one [locus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locus_(genetics) "Locus (genetics)") through [statistical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_analysis "Statistical analysis").

## Life

Felix Bernstein was born in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle,_Saxony-Anhalt "Halle, Saxony-Anhalt") on 24 February 1878 to a Jewish family of academics. His father [Julius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Bernstein "Julius Bernstein") held the Chair of Physiology at the [Martin Luther University of Halle-Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_University_of_Halle-Wittenberg "Martin Luther University of Halle-Wittenberg"), and was the Director of the Physiological Institute at the University of Halle.

While still in [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(Germany) "Gymnasium (Germany)") in Halle, Bernstein heard the university seminar of [Georg Cantor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Cantor "Georg Cantor"), who was a friend of Bernstein's father. From 1896 to 1900, Bernstein studied at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München"), the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle "University of Halle"), the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin "Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin"), and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"). In the early [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic "Weimar Republic"), Bernstein temporarily was Göttingen vice-chairman of the local chapter of [German Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Democratic_Party "German Democratic Party"). In 1933, after [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler "Hitler")'s rise to power, Bernstein was removed from his chair, per §6 of the Nazi [Law for the Restoration of the Professional Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_for_the_Restoration_of_the_Professional_Civil_Service "Law for the Restoration of the Professional Civil Service"), often used against politically unpopular persons. He received the message of his dismissal during a research/lecturing journey (started on Dec. 1st, 1932) to the United States, and he stayed there.

Bernstein was a visiting professor of mathematics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") from 1933 to 1936 and a professor of [biometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometry "Biometry") at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University") from 1936 to 1943. In 1942, he was elected a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science"). In 1948, Bernstein retired from teaching in the US, and returned to Europe. He mainly lived in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome") and [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg "Freiburg"), occasionally visiting Göttingen, where he became [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus "Professor emeritus"). He died in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich "Zurich") on 3 December 1956.
