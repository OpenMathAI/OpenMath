**Stefan Banach** (Polish: [[ˈstɛfan ˈbanax]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Polish "Help:IPA/Polish") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pl-Stefan_Banach.ogg "File:Pl-Stefan Banach.ogg"); 30 March 1892 – 31 August 1945) was a Polish [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who is generally considered one of the 20th century's most important and influential mathematicians. He was one of the founders of modern [functional analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_analysis "Functional analysis"), and an original member of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów_School_of_Mathematics "Lwów School of Mathematics"). His major work was the 1932 book, *Théorie des opérations linéaires* (Theory of Linear Operations), the first monograph on the general theory of functional analysis.

Born in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków") to a family of [Goral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorals "Gorals") descent, Banach showed a keen interest in mathematics and engaged in solving [mathematical problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_problem "Mathematical problem") during school [recess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recess_(break) "Recess (break)"). After completing his secondary education, he befriended [Hugo Steinhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Steinhaus "Hugo Steinhaus"), with whom he established the [Polish Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Mathematical_Society "Polish Mathematical Society") in 1919 and later published the scientific journal *[Studia Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studia_Mathematica "Studia Mathematica")*. In 1920, he received an assistantship at the [Lwów Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów_Polytechnic "Lwów Polytechnic"), subsequently becoming a professor in 1922 and a member of the [Polish Academy of Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Learning "Polish Academy of Learning") in 1924. Banach was also a co-founder of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów_School_of_Mathematics "Lwów School of Mathematics"), a [school of thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_thought "School of thought") comprising some of the most renowned Polish mathematicians of the [interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period "Interwar period") (1918–1939).

Some of the most notable [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical "Mathematical") [concepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept "Concept") that bear Banach's name include [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_space "Banach space"), [Banach algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_algebra "Banach algebra"), [Banach measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_measure "Banach measure"), the [Banach–Tarski paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Tarski_paradox "Banach–Tarski paradox"), the [Hahn–Banach theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hahn–Banach_theorem "Hahn–Banach theorem"), the [Banach–Steinhaus theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Steinhaus_theorem "Banach–Steinhaus theorem"), the [Banach–Mazur game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Mazur_game "Banach–Mazur game"), the [Banach–Alaoglu theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Alaoglu_theorem "Banach–Alaoglu theorem"), [Banach-Saks property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach-Saks_property "Banach-Saks property"), and the [Banach fixed-point theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_fixed-point_theorem "Banach fixed-point theorem").

## Life

### Early life

Stefan Banach was born on 30 March 1892 at St. Lazarus General Hospital in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków"), then part of the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary "Austria-Hungary"), into a [Góral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorals "Gorals") [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic "Roman Catholic") family, and was subsequently baptised by his father. Banach's parents were Stefan Greczek and Katarzyna [Banach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach "Banach"), both natives of the [Podhale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podhale "Podhale") region. Greczek was a soldier in the [Austro-Hungarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army "Austro-Hungarian Army") stationed in Kraków. Little is known about Banach's mother. According to his [baptismal certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism "Baptism"), she was born in [Borówna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borówna "Borówna") and worked as a domestic helper.

Unusually, Stefan's surname was his mother's instead of his father's, though he received his father's given name, Stefan. Military regulations did not permit soldiers of Stefan Greczek's rank to [marry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage "Marriage"); he was a [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_(rank) "Private (rank)") and as the mother was too poor to support the child, the couple decided that he should be reared by family and friends. Stefan spent the first few years of his life with his grandmother, but when she was taken ill, Greczek arranged for his son to be raised by Franciszka Płowa and her niece Maria Puchalska in Kraków. Young Stefan came to regard Franciszka as his foster mother and Maria as his older sister. In his early years Banach was tutored by Juliusz Mien, a French intellectual and friend of the Płowa family, who had emigrated to Poland and supported himself with photography and translations of Polish literature into French. Mien taught Banach French and most likely encouraged him in his early mathematical pursuits.

In 1902, Banach, aged 10, enrolled in Kraków's *IV Gymnasium* (the predecessor of [Jan III Sobieski High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_III_Sobieski_High_School,_Kraków "Jan III Sobieski High School, Kraków")). While the school specialized in the [humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanities "Humanities"), Banach and his best friend [Witold Wiłkosz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Wilkosz "Witold Wilkosz") (also a future mathematician) spent most of their time working on mathematics problems during breaks and after school. Later in life Banach credited Dr. Kamil Kraft, the mathematics and physics teacher at the school, with kindling his interests in mathematics. While Banach was a diligent student he did, on occasion, receive low grades (he failed Greek during his first semester at the school) and later spoke critically of the school's math teachers.

After obtaining his *[matura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matura "Matura")* (high school degree) at age 18 in 1910, Banach moved to [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów "Lwów") (today called [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv "Lviv")) with the intention of studying at the [Lwów Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów_Polytechnic "Lwów Polytechnic"). He initially chose engineering as his field of study since at the time he was convinced that there was nothing new to discover in mathematics. At some point he also attended [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University "Jagiellonian University") in Kraków on a part-time basis. As Banach had to earn money to support his studies it was not until 1914 that he finally, at age 22, passed his high school graduation exams.

When [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") broke out, Banach was excused from military service due to his left-handedness and poor vision. When the [Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army "Imperial Russian Army") opened its offensive toward Lwów, Banach left for Kraków, where he spent the rest of the war. He made his living as a [tutor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutor "Tutor") at the local schools, worked in a bookstore and as a foreman of a road building crew. He attended some lectures at the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University "Jagiellonian University") at that time, including those of the famous Polish mathematicians [Stanisław Zaremba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanisław_Zaremba_(mathematician) "Stanisław Zaremba (mathematician)") and [Kazimierz Żorawski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Żorawski "Kazimierz Żorawski"), but little is known of that period of his life.

### Discovery by Steinhaus

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Otto_Nikodym_Stefan_Banach_Memorial_Bench_Krakow_Poland.jpg/250px-Otto_Nikodym_Stefan_Banach_Memorial_Bench_Krakow_Poland.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Otto_Nikodym_Stefan_Banach_Memorial_Bench_Krakow_Poland.jpg)

[Otto Nikodym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Nikodym "Otto Nikodym") and Stefan Banach Memorial Bench in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków"), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland") (sculpted by Stefan Dousa)

In 1916, in Kraków's *[Planty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planty_Park "Planty Park")* park, Banach encountered Professor [Hugo Steinhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Steinhaus "Hugo Steinhaus"), one of the renowned mathematicians of the time. According to Steinhaus, while he was strolling through the gardens he was surprised to overhear the term *"Lebesgue integral"* ([Lebesgue integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration "Lebesgue integration") was at the time still a fairly new idea in mathematics) and walked over to investigate. As a result, he met Banach, as well as [Otto Nikodym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Nikodym "Otto Nikodym"). Steinhaus became fascinated with the self-taught young mathematician. The encounter resulted in a long-lasting collaboration and friendship. In fact, soon after the encounter Steinhaus invited Banach to solve some problems he had been working on but which had proven difficult. Banach solved them within a week and the two soon published their first joint work (*On the Mean Convergence of Fourier Series*). Steinhaus, Banach and Nikodym, along with several other Kraków mathematicians ([Władysław Ślebodziński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Władysław_Ślebodziński "Władysław Ślebodziński"), [Leon Chwistek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Chwistek "Leon Chwistek"), Alfred Rosenblatt and [Włodzimierz Stożek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Włodzimierz_Stożek "Włodzimierz Stożek")) also established a mathematical society, which eventually became the [Polish Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Mathematical_Society "Polish Mathematical Society"). The society was officially founded on 2 April 1919. It was also through Steinhaus that Banach met his future wife, Łucja Braus.

### Interbellum

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Lwow_-_Kawiarnia_Szkocka.jpg/250px-Lwow_-_Kawiarnia_Szkocka.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lwow_-_Kawiarnia_Szkocka.jpg)

[Scottish Café](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Café "Scottish Café"), meeting place of many famous Lwów mathematicians

Steinhaus introduced Banach to academic circles and substantially accelerated his career. After [Poland regained independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic "Second Polish Republic") in 1918, Banach was given an assistantship at the Lwów Polytechnic. Steinhaus' backing also allowed him to receive a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate "Doctorate") without actually graduating from a university. The doctoral thesis, accepted by [King John II Casimir University of Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_University "Lviv University") in 1920 and published in 1922, included the basic ideas of functional analysis, which was soon to become an entirely new branch of mathematics. In his dissertation, written in 1920, he axiomatically defined what is today called a Banach space. The thesis was widely discussed in academic circles and allowed him in 1922 to become a professor at the Lwów Polytechnic. Initially an assistant to Professor [Antoni Łomnicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Łomnicki "Antoni Łomnicki"), in 1927, Banach received his own chair. In 1924 he was accepted as a member of the [Polish Academy of Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Learning "Polish Academy of Learning"). At the same time, from 1922, Banach also headed the second Chair of Mathematics at [University of Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_University "Lviv University").

Young and talented, Banach gathered around him a large group of mathematicians. The group, meeting in the [Scottish Café](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Café "Scottish Café"), soon gave birth to the "Lwów School of Mathematics". In 1929 the group began publishing its own journal, *[Studia Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studia_Mathematica "Studia Mathematica")*, devoted primarily to Banach's field of study—functional analysis. Around that time, Banach also began working on his best-known work, the first monograph on the general theory of [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_space "Linear space")-[metric space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_space "Metric space"). First published in Polish in 1931, the next year it was also translated into French and gained wider recognition in European academic circles. The book was also the first in a long series of mathematics monographs edited by Banach and his circle. On 17 June 1924, Banach became a [correspondence member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corresponding_member "Corresponding member") of the [Polish Academy of Sciences and Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Learning "Polish Academy of Learning") in Kraków.

### World War II

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Cmentarz-Lyczakowski-Grob_Banacha.jpg/250px-Cmentarz-Lyczakowski-Grob_Banacha.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cmentarz-Lyczakowski-Grob_Banacha.jpg)

Banach's grave, [Lychakiv Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lychakiv_Cemetery "Lychakiv Cemetery"), [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv "Lviv") (*[Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów "Lwów")*, in [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language "Polish language"))

After the [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland "Invasion of Poland") by [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), Lwów came under the control of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") for almost two years. Banach, from 1939 a [corresponding member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corresponding_member "Corresponding member") of the [Academy of Sciences of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_Ukraine "Academy of Sciences of Ukraine"), and on good terms with [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") mathematicians, had to promise to learn Ukrainian to be allowed to keep his chair and continue his academic activities. In 1940 he was appointed by the Soviets as a member of the [Lviv City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_City_Council "Lviv City Council"). After the German takeover of Lwów in 1941 during [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa "Operation Barbarossa"), all universities were closed and Banach, along with many colleagues and his son, was employed as a [lice feeder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeder_of_lice "Feeder of lice") at Professor [Rudolf Weigl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Weigl "Rudolf Weigl")'s [Typhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhus "Typhus") Research Institute. Employment in Weigl's Institute provided many unemployed university professors and their associates protection from random arrest and deportation to [Nazi concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps "Nazi concentration camps").

After the Soviet [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army "Red Army") recaptured [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów "Lwów") in the [Lvov–Sandomierz Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lvov–Sandomierz_Offensive "Lvov–Sandomierz Offensive") of 1944, Banach returned to the university and helped re-establish it after the war years. However, because the Soviets were [deporting Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_population_transfers_(1944–1946) "Polish population transfers (1944–1946)") from annexed formerly Polish [eastern territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kresy "Kresy"), Banach began preparing to leave the city and settle in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków"), Poland, where he had been promised a chair at the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University "Jagiellonian University"). He was also considered a candidate for Minister of Education of Poland. In January 1945, however, he was diagnosed with [lung cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_cancer "Lung cancer") and was permitted to stay in Lwów. He died on 31 August 1945, aged 53. His funeral at the [Lychakiv Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lychakiv_Cemetery "Lychakiv Cemetery") (*Cmentarz Łyczakowski*) was attended by hundreds of people. His chair at Lwow was subsequently taken up by [Wladimir Lewicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wladimir_Lewicki "Wladimir Lewicki").

## Contributions

Banach's dissertation, completed in 1920 and published in 1922, formally axiomatized the concept of a [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_metric_space "Complete metric space") [normed vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_vector_space "Normed vector space") and laid the foundations for the area of [functional analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_analysis "Functional analysis"). In this work Banach called such spaces *"class E-spaces"*, but in his 1932 book, *Théorie des opérations linéaires*, he changed terminology and referred to them as "spaces of type B", which most likely contributed to the subsequent [eponymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym "Eponym") naming of these spaces after him. The theory of what came to be known as [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_space "Banach space") had antecedents in the work of the Hungarian mathematician [Frigyes Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigyes_Riesz "Frigyes Riesz") (published in 1916) and contemporaneous contributions from [Hans Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Hahn_(mathematician) "Hans Hahn (mathematician)") and [Norbert Wiener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Wiener "Norbert Wiener"). For a brief period in fact, complete normed linear spaces were referred to as "Banach–Wiener" spaces in mathematical literature, based on terminology introduced by Wiener himself. However, because Wiener's work on the topic was limited, the established name became just *Banach spaces*.

Likewise, [Banach's fixed point theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_fixed-point_theorem "Banach fixed-point theorem"), based on earlier methods developed by [Charles Émile Picard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Émile_Picard "Charles Émile Picard"), was included in his dissertation, and was later extended by his students (for example in the [Banach–Schauder theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Schauder_theorem "Banach–Schauder theorem")) and other mathematicians (in particular [Brouwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._E._J._Brouwer "L. E. J. Brouwer") and [Poincaré and Birkhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré–Birkhoff_theorem "Poincaré–Birkhoff theorem")). The theorem did not require linearity of the [space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_(mathematics) "Space (mathematics)"), and applied to any complete [Cauchy space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_space "Cauchy space") (in particular to any complete metric space).

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Banach-Tarski_Paradox.svg/500px-Banach-Tarski_Paradox.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Banach-Tarski_Paradox.svg)

Decomposition of a ball into two identical balls – the [Banach–Tarski paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Tarski_paradox "Banach–Tarski paradox")

The [Hahn–Banach theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hahn–Banach_theorem "Hahn–Banach theorem") is one of the fundamental theorems of functional analysis. Further theorems related to Banach are:

- [Banach–Tarski paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Tarski_paradox "Banach–Tarski paradox")
- [Banach–Steinhaus theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_boundedness_principle "Uniform boundedness principle")
- [Banach–Alaoglu theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Alaoglu_theorem "Banach–Alaoglu theorem")
- [Banach–Stone theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Stone_theorem "Banach–Stone theorem")

## Recognition

In 1946, the [Stefan Banach Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Banach_Prize "Stefan Banach Prize") (Polish: *Nagroda im. Stefana Banacha*) was established by the [Polish Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Mathematical_Society "Polish Mathematical Society").
In 1982, the [Polish Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Post "Polish Post") issued a 15-[zloty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zloty "Zloty") commemorative stamp featuring Banach as part of the "Polish Mathematicians" series.

In 1992, the [Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematics_of_the_Polish_Academy_of_Sciences "Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences") established a special [Stefan Banach Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Banach_Medal "Stefan Banach Medal") for outstanding achievements in mathematical sciences. Since 2009, the [International Stefan Banach Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Stefan_Banach_Prize "International Stefan Banach Prize") has been conferred by the Polish Mathematical Society to mathematicians for best doctoral dissertations in the mathematical sciences with the objective to "promote and financially support the most promising young researchers".

Stefan Banach is the patron of a number of schools and streets including in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw "Warsaw"), [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv "Lviv"), [Świdnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Świdnica "Świdnica"), [Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toruń "Toruń") and [Jarosław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarosław "Jarosław").

In 2001, a minor planet [16856 Banach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16856_Banach "16856 Banach"), discovered by [Paul Comba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Comba "Paul Comba") in 1997, was named after him.

In 2012, the [National Bank of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Poland "National Bank of Poland") celebrated the mathematician's achievements by issuing a series of commemorative coins designed by Robert Kotowicz (golden 200-[zloty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_zloty "Polish zloty") coin, silver 10-zloty coin and [Nordic Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Gold "Nordic Gold") 2-zloty coin).

In 2016, a commemorative bench featuring Banach and [Otto Nikodym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Nikodym "Otto Nikodym") was unveiled in Kraków's [Planty Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planty_Park_(Kraków) "Planty Park (Kraków)") on the 100th anniversary of the conversation the two mathematicians held when they first met [Hugo Steinhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Steinhaus "Hugo Steinhaus"), which proved instrumental in the development of his scientific career.

In 2018, at a ceremony held at the [Royal Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Castle,_Warsaw "Royal Castle, Warsaw") in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw "Warsaw") on the 100th anniversary of Poland regaining its independence, Polish President [Andrzej Duda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Duda "Andrzej Duda") posthumously conferred the country's highest distinction, the [Order of the White Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_White_Eagle_(Poland) "Order of the White Eagle (Poland)"), upon 25 distinguished Poles including Banach.

In 2019, Polish [entomologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entomologist "Entomologist") Marcin Kamiński named a newly discovered species of South African [darkling beetle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkling_beetle "Darkling beetle"), *Machleida banachi*, in honor of the mathematician Stefan Banach and his contributions to science.

In 2021, one of the episodes of Polish documentary TV series *Geniusze i marzyciele* (Geniuses and Dreamers) aired on [TVP1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVP1 "TVP1") and TVP Dokument channels was devoted to Stefan Banach.

In 2022, [Google Doodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Doodle "Google Doodle") commemorated the 100th anniversary of Banach receiving his title of professor.
