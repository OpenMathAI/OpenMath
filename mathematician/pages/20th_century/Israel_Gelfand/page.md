**Israel Moiseevich Gelfand**, also written **Israïl Moyseyovich Gel'fand**, or **Izrail M. Gelfand** ([Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish_language "Yiddish language"): ישראל געלפֿאַנד, [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Изра́иль Моисе́евич Гельфа́нд, [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language "Ukrainian language"): Ізраїль Мойсейович Гельфанд; 2 September [[O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates "Old Style and New Style dates") 20 August] 1913 – 5 October 2009) was a prominent Soviet and American [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"), one of the greatest mathematicians of the 20th century, biologist, teacher and organizer of mathematical education. He made significant contributions to many branches of mathematics, including [group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_theory "Group theory"), [representation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_theory "Representation theory") and [functional analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_analysis "Functional analysis"). The recipient of many awards, including the [Order of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin "Order of Lenin") and the first [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize "Wolf Prize"), he was a Foreign [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") and professor at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University") and, after immigrating to the United States shortly before his 76th birthday, at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University "Rutgers University"). Gelfand is also a [1994 MacArthur Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program "MacArthur Fellows Program").

His legacy continues through his students, who include [Endre Szemerédi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endre_Szemerédi "Endre Szemerédi"), [Alexandre Kirillov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Kirillov "Alexandre Kirillov"), [Edward Frenkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Frenkel "Edward Frenkel"), [Joseph Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bernstein "Joseph Bernstein"), [David Kazhdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kazhdan "David Kazhdan"), as well as his own son, Sergei Gelfand.

## Early years

Gelfand was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family in [Okny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okny "Okny"), in the [Kherson Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kherson_Governorate "Kherson Governorate") of the Russian Empire (now in [Odesa Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_Oblast "Odesa Oblast"), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine")). According to his own account, Gelfand was expelled from high school under the [Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR "USSR") because his father had been a mill owner. Bypassing both high school and college, he proceeded to postgraduate study at the age of 19 at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University"), where his advisor was the preeminent mathematician [Andrei Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Kolmogorov "Andrei Kolmogorov"). He received his PhD in 1935.

Gelfand immigrated to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") in 1989.

## Work

Gelfand is known for many developments including:

- the book *Calculus of Variations* (1963), which he co-authored with [Sergei Fomin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Fomin "Sergei Fomin");
- [Gelfand's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_radius#Gelfand's_formula "Spectral radius"), which expresses the spectral radius as a limit of matrix norms.
- the [Gelfand representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfand_representation "Gelfand representation") in [Banach algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_algebra "Banach algebra") theory;
- the [Gelfand–Mazur theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfand–Mazur_theorem "Gelfand–Mazur theorem") in Banach algebra theory;
- the [Gelfand–Naimark theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfand–Naimark_theorem "Gelfand–Naimark theorem");
- the [Gelfand–Naimark–Segal construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfand–Naimark–Segal_construction "Gelfand–Naimark–Segal construction");
- [Gelfand–Shilov spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfand–Shilov_space "Gelfand–Shilov space");
- the [Gelfand–Pettis integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pettis_integral "Pettis integral");
- the [representation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_theory "Representation theory") of the [complex classical Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_group "Classical group");
- contributions to the theory of [Verma modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verma_module "Verma module") in the [representation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_theory "Representation theory") of [semisimple Lie algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_algebra "Semisimple Lie algebra") (with I. N. Bernstein and S. I. Gelfand);
- contributions to [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_(mathematics) "Distribution (mathematics)") theory and measures on infinite-dimensional spaces;
- the first observation of the connection of [automorphic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_form "Automorphic form") with representations (with [Sergei Fomin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Fomin "Sergei Fomin"));
- conjectures about the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiyah–Singer_index_theorem "Atiyah–Singer index theorem");
- [ordinary differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_differential_equation "Ordinary differential equation") (Gelfand–[Levitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Levitan "Boris Levitan") theory);
- work on [calculus of variations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_of_variations "Calculus of variations") and [soliton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soliton "Soliton") theory (Gelfand–Dikii equations);
- contributions to the *[philosophy of cusp forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_cusp_forms "Philosophy of cusp forms")*;
- Gelfand–[Fuchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Fuchs "Dmitry Fuchs") [cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology "Cohomology") of [Lie algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebra "Lie algebra");
- [Gelfand–Kirillov dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfand–Kirillov_dimension "Gelfand–Kirillov dimension");
- [integral geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_geometry "Integral geometry");
- combinatorial definition of the [Pontryagin class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_class "Pontryagin class");
- [Coxeter functors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_functor "Coxeter functor");
- [general hypergeometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_hypergeometric_function "General hypergeometric function");
- Gelfand–[Tsetlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Tsetlin "Michael Tsetlin") patterns;
- Gelfand–Lokutsievski method;
- the [BGG correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BGG_correspondence "BGG correspondence") (with [Joseph Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bernstein "Joseph Bernstein") and [Sergei Gelfand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Gelfand?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sergei Gelfand"));
- and many other results, particularly in the representation theory of [classical groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_group "Classical group").

Gelfand ran a seminar at Moscow State University from 1943 until May 1989 (when it continued at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University "Rutgers University")). Covereing a wide range of topics, the seminar was an important school for many young mathematicians.

## Influence outside mathematics

The [Gelfand–Tsetlin (also spelled Zetlin) basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restricted_representation#Gelfand–Tsetlin_basis "Restricted representation") is a widely used tool in [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics "Theoretical physics") and the result of Gelfand's work on the representation theory of the [unitary group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_group "Unitary group") and [Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_group "Lie group") in general.

Gelfand also published works on biology and medicine. For a long time he took an interest in [cell biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_biology "Cell biology") and organized a research seminar on the subject.

He worked extensively in mathematics education, particularly with correspondence education. In 1994, he was awarded a [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellowship "MacArthur Fellowship") for this work.

## Personal life

Gelfand was married to [Zorya Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorya_Shapiro "Zorya Shapiro"), and their two sons, Sergei and Vladimir, both live in the United States. A third son, Aleksandr, died of [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia"). His first marriage ended in divorce; Gelfand later remarried. With his second wife, Tatiana, they had a daughter, also named Tatiana. The family also includes four grandchildren and three great-grandchildren. Memories about I. Gelfand are collected at a dedicated website handled by his family.

Gelfand was an advocate of [animal rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_rights "Animal rights"). He became a [vegetarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarianism "Vegetarianism") in 1994 and [vegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veganism "Veganism") in 2000.

## Honors and awards

Gelfand held several honorary degrees and was awarded the [Order of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin "Order of Lenin") three times for his research. In 1977 he was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society"). He won the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize "Wolf Prize") in 1978, [Kyoto Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Prize "Kyoto Prize") in 1989 and MacArthur Foundation Fellowship in 1994. He held the presidency of the [Moscow Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Mathematical_Society "Moscow Mathematical Society") between 1968 and 1970, and was elected a foreign member of the [U.S. National Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences"), the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), the [Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy "Royal Irish Academy"), the [American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Society "American Mathematical Society") and the [London Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Mathematical_Society "London Mathematical Society").

In an October 2003 article in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*, written on the occasion of his 90th birthday, Gelfand is described as a scholar who is considered "among the greatest mathematicians of the 20th century", having exerted a tremendous influence on the field both through his own works and those of his students.

## Death

Gelfand died at the [Robert Wood Johnson University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wood_Johnson_University_Hospital "Robert Wood Johnson University Hospital") near his home in [Highland Park, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_New_Jersey "Highland Park, New Jersey"). He was less than five weeks past his 96th birthday. His death was first reported on the blog of his former collaborator [Andrei Zelevinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Zelevinsky "Andrei Zelevinsky") and confirmed a few hours later by an obituary in the Russian online newspaper *Polit.ru*.
