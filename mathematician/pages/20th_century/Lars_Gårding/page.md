**Lars Gårding** (7 March 1919 – 7 July 2014) was a [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"). He made notable contributions to the study of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equation "Partial differential equation") and [partial differential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_operator "Partial differential operator"). He was a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor "Professor") of mathematics at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University "Lund University") in Sweden from 1952 to 1984. Together with [Marcel Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Riesz "Marcel Riesz"), he was a thesis advisor for [Lars Hörmander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Hörmander "Lars Hörmander"). In physics, he is known for postulating the [Gårding–Wightman axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wightman_axioms "Wightman axioms") of [quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field_theory "Quantum field theory").

## Biography

Gårding was born in [Hedemora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedemora "Hedemora"), Sweden but grew up in [Motala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motala "Motala"), where his father was an engineer at the plant. He began to study mathematics in [Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University "Lund University") in 1937, first with the intention of becoming an [actuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuary "Actuary").

His doctorate thesis, which was written under the supervision of [Marcel Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Riesz "Marcel Riesz"), was first on [group representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_representations "Group representations") in 1944, but in the following years he changed his research focus to the theory of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations "Partial differential equations"). He held the professorship of mathematics at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University "Lund University") from 1952 until retirement in 1984.

Gårding's interest was not limited to mathematics, but also in art, literature and music. He played the violin and the piano. He published a book on bird songs and calls in 1987, a result of his interest in birdwatching.

He was elected a member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences") in 1953 and of the [Finnish Society of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Society_of_Sciences_and_Letters "Finnish Society of Sciences and Letters") in 1985.

Gårding died on 7 July 2014, aged 95.
