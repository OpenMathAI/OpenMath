**Lev Semyonovich Pontryagin** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Лев Семёнович Понтрягин, also written **Pontriagin** or **Pontrjagin**, first name sometimes anglicized as **Leon**) (3 September 1908 – 3 May 1988) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"). Completely blind from the age of 14, he made major discoveries in a number of fields of mathematics, including [algebraic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_topology "Algebraic topology"), [differential topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_topology "Differential topology") and [optimal control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_control "Optimal control").

## Early life and career

Pontryagin was born in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow") and lost his eyesight completely due to an unsuccessful [eye surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_surgery "Eye surgery") after a [primus stove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primus_stove "Primus stove") explosion when he was 14. His mother Tatyana Andreyevna, who did not know mathematical symbols, read mathematical books and papers (notably those of [Heinz Hopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Hopf "Heinz Hopf"), [J. H. C. Whitehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._H._C._Whitehead "J. H. C. Whitehead"), and [Hassler Whitney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassler_Whitney "Hassler Whitney")) to him, and later worked as his secretary. His mother used alternative names for math symbols, such as "tails up" for the set-union symbol

In 1925 he entered [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University"), where he was strongly influenced by the lectures of [Pavel Alexandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Alexandrov "Pavel Alexandrov") who would become his doctoral thesis advisor. After graduating in 1929, he obtained a position at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University"). In 1934 he joined the [Steklov Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Institute_of_Mathematics "Steklov Institute of Mathematics") in Moscow. In 1970 he became vice president of the [International Mathematical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Union "International Mathematical Union").

## Work

Pontryagin worked on [duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duality_(mathematics) "Duality (mathematics)") theory for [homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_(mathematics) "Homology (mathematics)") while still a student. He went on to lay foundations for the abstract theory of the [Fourier transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_transform "Fourier transform"), now called [Pontryagin duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_duality "Pontryagin duality"). Using these tools, he was able to solve the case of [Hilbert's fifth problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert's_fifth_problem "Hilbert's fifth problem") for abelian groups in 1934.

In 1935, he was able to compute the homology groups of the classical compact [Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_group "Lie group"), which he would later call his greatest achievement.

With [René Thom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Thom "René Thom"), he is regarded as one of the co-founders of [cobordism theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobordism "Cobordism"), and co-discoverers of the central idea of this theory, that framed cobordism and [stable homotopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_homotopy_theory "Stable homotopy theory") are equivalent. This led to the introduction around 1940 of a theory of certain [characteristic classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_class "Characteristic class"), now called [Pontryagin classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_class "Pontryagin class"), designed to vanish on a [manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold "Manifold") that is a [boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_(topology) "Boundary (topology)").

In 1942 he introduced the cohomology operations now called [Pontryagin squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_cohomology_operation "Pontryagin cohomology operation"). Moreover, in [operator theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_theory "Operator theory") there are specific instances of [Krein spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefinite_inner_product_space "Indefinite inner product space") called [Pontryagin spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefinite_inner_product_space "Indefinite inner product space").

Starting in 1952, he worked in [optimal control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_control "Optimal control") theory. His [maximum principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin's_maximum_principle "Pontryagin's maximum principle") is fundamental to the modern theory of optimization. He also introduced the idea of a [bang–bang principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bang–bang_control "Bang–bang control"), to describe situations where the applied control at each moment is either the maximum positive 'steer', or the maximum negative 'steer'.

Pontryagin authored several influential monographs as well as popular textbooks in mathematics.

Pontryagin's students include [Dmitri Anosov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Anosov "Dmitri Anosov"), [Vladimir Boltyansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Boltyansky "Vladimir Boltyansky"), [Revaz Gamkrelidze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revaz_Gamkrelidze "Revaz Gamkrelidze"), Yevgeny Mishchenko, [Mikhail Postnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Postnikov "Mikhail Postnikov"), [Vladimir Rokhlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Abramovich_Rokhlin "Vladimir Abramovich Rokhlin"), and [Mikhail Zelikin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Zelikin "Mikhail Zelikin").

## Controversy and antisemitism allegations

Pontryagin participated in a few notorious political campaigns in the Soviet Union. In 1930, he and several other young members of the [Moscow Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Mathematical_Society "Moscow Mathematical Society") publicly denounced as counter-revolutionary the Society's head [Dmitri Egorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Egorov "Dmitri Egorov"), who openly supported the Russian Orthodox Church and had recently been arrested. They then proceeded to follow their plan of reorganizing the Society.

Pontryagin was accused of anti-Semitism on several occasions. For example, he attacked [Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson "Nathan Jacobson") for being a "mediocre scientist" representing the "[Zionism movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism "Zionism")", while both men were vice-presidents of the [International Mathematical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Union "International Mathematical Union"). When a prominent Soviet Jewish mathematician, [Grigory Margulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Margulis "Grigory Margulis"), was selected by the [IMU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Union "International Mathematical Union") to receive the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") at the upcoming 1978 [ICM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians"), Pontryagin, who was a member of the executive committee of the IMU at the time, vigorously objected. Although the IMU stood by its decision to award Margulis the Fields Medal, Margulis was denied a Soviet exit visa by the Soviet authorities and was unable to attend the 1978 ICM in person.

Pontryagin rejected charges of antisemitism in an article published in *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_(journal) "Science (journal)")* in 1979. In his memoirs Pontryagin claims that he struggled with [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism "Zionism"), which he considered a form of [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism "Racism").
