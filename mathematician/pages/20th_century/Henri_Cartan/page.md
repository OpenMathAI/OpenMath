**Henri Paul Cartan** (French: [[kaʁtɑ̃]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 8 July 1904 – 13 August 2008) was a French [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who made substantial contributions to [algebraic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_topology "Algebraic topology").

He was the son of the mathematician [Élie Cartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Élie_Cartan "Élie Cartan"), nephew of mathematician [Anna Cartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Cartan "Anna Cartan"), oldest brother of composer [Jean Cartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Cartan?action=edit&redlink=1 "Jean Cartan"), [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") [Louis Cartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Cartan?action=edit&redlink=1 "Louis Cartan") and mathematician [Hélène Cartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hélène_Cartan?action=edit&redlink=1 "Hélène Cartan"), and the son-in-law of physicist [Pierre Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Weiss "Pierre Weiss").

## Life

According to his own words, Henri Cartan was interested in mathematics at a very young age, without being influenced by his family. He moved to [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") with his family after his father's appointment at [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University "Sorbonne University") in 1909 and he attended secondary school at [Lycée Hoche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Hoche "Lycée Hoche") in [Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles,_Yvelines "Versailles, Yvelines").

In 1923 he started studying mathematics at [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure "École Normale Supérieure"), receiving an [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation "Agrégation") in 1926 and a doctorate in 1928. His PhD thesis, entitled *Sur les systèmes de fonctions holomorphes à variétés linéaires lacunaires et leurs applications*, was supervised by [Paul Montel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Montel "Paul Montel").

Cartan taught at [Lycée Malherbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Malherbe "Lycée Malherbe") in [Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caen "Caen") from 1928 to 1929, at the [University of Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lille "University of Lille") from 1929 to 1931 and at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg "University of Strasbourg") from 1931 to 1939. After the [German invasion of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_France_(1940) "German invasion of France (1940)") the university staff was moved to [Clermont Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont_Ferrand "Clermont Ferrand"). He recommended the mathematicians [Marie-Hélène Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-Hélène_Schwartz "Marie-Hélène Schwartz") and [Laurent Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Schwartz "Laurent Schwartz") move to work at the University of Strasbourg in Clermont Ferrand for both their research and for their safety. In 1940 he returned to Paris to work at [Université de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris") and École Normale Supérieure. From 1969 until his retirement in 1975 he was professor at [Paris-Sud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_University "Paris-Sud University").

Cartan died on 13 August 2008 at the age of 104. His funeral took place the following Wednesday on 20 August in [Die, Drome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die,_Drome "Die, Drome").

## Honours and awards

In 1932 Cartan was invited to give a [Cours Peccot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peccot_Lectures "Peccot Lectures") at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France"). In 1950 he was elected president of the [Société mathématique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_mathématique_de_France "Société mathématique de France") and from 1967 to 1970 he was president of the [International Mathematics Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Union "International Mathematical Union"). He was awarded the [Émile Picard Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émile_Picard_Medal "Émile Picard Medal") in 1959, the [CNRS Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS_Gold_Medal "CNRS Gold Medal") in 1976, and the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize "Wolf Prize") in 1980.

He was an invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") in 1932 in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich "Zürich") and a Plenary Speaker at the ICM in 1950 in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts") and in 1958 in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh "Edinburgh").

From 1974 until his death he had been a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences"). ## Political and social activities

During the 70's and the 80's Cartan used his influence to help obtain the release of several dissident mathematicians, including [Leonid Plyushch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Plyushch "Leonid Plyushch"), imprisoned by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), [Jose Luis Massera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Luis_Massera "Jose Luis Massera"), imprisoned between 1975 and 1984 by the [Uruguayan dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic-military_dictatorship_of_Uruguay "Civic-military dictatorship of Uruguay"), and [Sion Assidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sion_Assidon "Sion Assidon"), imprisoned during the [Moroccan Years of Lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Years_of_Lead_(Morocco) "Years of Lead (Morocco)"). For his humanitarian efforts, he received in 1989 the [Heinz R. Pagels Human Rights of Scientists Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_R._Pagels_Human_Rights_of_Scientists_Award "Heinz R. Pagels Human Rights of Scientists Award") from the [New York Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Academy_of_Sciences "New York Academy of Sciences").

Since the 30's Cartan had tight collaborations with many German mathematicians, including [Heinrich Behnke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Behnke "Heinrich Behnke") and [Peter Thullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thullen "Peter Thullen"). Right after [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") he put many efforts to improve the cooperation between French and German mathematicians and restore the flow of exchanges of ideas and students.

Cartan supported the idea of [European Federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Federalism "European Federalism") and from 1974 to 1985 was president of the French section of the [Union of European Federalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_European_Federalists "Union of European Federalists"). At the [1984 European elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_European_elections "1984 European elections") he was the leader of the *Liste pour les États-Unis d'Europe*, which obtained 0.4% of votes and did not elect any candidate.

In 1992 he gave a speech at the first [European Congress of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Congress_of_Mathematics "European Congress of Mathematics") in Paris, remarking the common heritage and future of European countries and praising the first reunion between mathematicians from the two [previously separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain "Iron Curtain") parts of Europe.

## Research

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/Henri_Cartan_celebration.jpg/250px-Henri_Cartan_celebration.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Henri_Cartan_celebration.jpg)

During the "Cartan celebration", at [Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orsay "Orsay") in 1975.

Cartan worked in several fields across [algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra "Algebra"), [geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry "Geometry") and [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis"), focusing primarily on [algebraic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_topology "Algebraic topology") and [homological algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homological_algebra "Homological algebra").

He was a founding member of the [Bourbaki group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Bourbaki "Nicolas Bourbaki") in 1934 and one of its most active participants. After 1945 he started his own [seminar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_mathematical_seminars "French mathematical seminars") in Paris, which deeply influenced [Jean-Pierre Serre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Serre "Jean-Pierre Serre"), [Armand Borel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_Borel "Armand Borel"), [Alexander Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Grothendieck "Alexander Grothendieck") and [Frank Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Adams "Frank Adams"), amongst others of the leading lights of the younger generation. The number of his official students was small, but includes [Joséphine Guidy Wandja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joséphine_Guidy_Wandja "Joséphine Guidy Wandja") (the first African woman to gain a PhD in mathematics), [Adrien Douady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrien_Douady "Adrien Douady"), [Roger Godement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Godement "Roger Godement"), [Max Karoubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Karoubi "Max Karoubi"), [Jean-Louis Koszul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Louis_Koszul "Jean-Louis Koszul"), Jean-Pierre Serre and [René Thom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Thom "René Thom").

Cartan's first research interests, until the 40's, were in the theory of [functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functions_of_several_complex_variables "Functions of several complex variables"), which later gave rise to the theory of [complex varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variety "Complex variety") and [analytic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry_and_analytic_geometry "Algebraic geometry and analytic geometry"). Motivated by the solution to the [Cousin problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cousin_problems "Cousin problems"), he worked on [sheaf cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_cohomology "Sheaf cohomology") and [coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_sheaves "Coherent sheaves") and [proved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_proof "Mathematical proof") two powerful results, [Cartan's theorems A and B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan's_theorems_A_and_B "Cartan's theorems A and B").

Since the 50's he became more interested in [algebraic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_topology "Algebraic topology"). Among his major contributions, he worked on [cohomology operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_operation "Cohomology operation") and [homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_(mathematics) "Homology (mathematics)") of the [Eilenberg–MacLane spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg–MacLane_space "Eilenberg–MacLane space"), he introduced the notion of [Steenrod algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steenrod_algebra "Steenrod algebra"), and, together with Jean-Pierre Serre, developed the method of "killing [homotopy groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_group "Homotopy group")". His 1956 book with [Samuel Eilenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Eilenberg "Samuel Eilenberg") on homological algebra was an important text, treating the subject with a moderate level of abstraction with the help of [category theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_theory "Category theory"). They introduced fundamental concepts, including those of [projective module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_module "Projective module"), [weak dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_dimension "Weak dimension"), and what is now called the [Cartan–Eilenberg resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan–Eilenberg_resolution "Cartan–Eilenberg resolution").

Among his other contributions, in [general topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_topology "General topology") he introduced the notions of [filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filter_(mathematics) "Filter (mathematics)") and [ultrafilter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafilter "Ultrafilter") and in [potential theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_theory "Potential theory") he developed the [fine topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_topology_(potential_theory) "Fine topology (potential theory)") and proved [Cartan's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan's_lemma_(potential_theory) "Cartan's lemma (potential theory)"). The [Cartan model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan_model "Cartan model") for [equivariant cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant_cohomology "Equivariant cohomology") is also named after him.
