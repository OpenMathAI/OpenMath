**Edmund Georg Hermann Landau** (14 February 1877 – 19 February 1938) was a German [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who worked in the fields of [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory") and [complex analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_analysis "Complex analysis").

## Biography

Edmund Landau was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family in Berlin. His father was [Leopold Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Landau "Leopold Landau"), a [gynecologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gynaecology "Gynaecology"), and his mother was Johanna Jacoby. Landau studied mathematics at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin "University of Berlin"), receiving his doctorate in 1899 and his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation "Habilitation") (the post-doctoral qualification required to teach in German universities) in 1901. His doctoral thesis was 14 pages long.

In 1895, his paper on scoring chess tournaments is the earliest use of [eigenvector centrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector_centrality "Eigenvector centrality").

Landau taught at the University of Berlin from 1899 to 1909, after which he held a chair at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"). He married Marianne Ehrlich, the daughter of the Nobel Prize-winning biologist [Paul Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrlich "Paul Ehrlich"), in 1905.

At the [1912 International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_International_Congresses_of_Mathematicians_Plenary_and_Invited_Speakers#1912,_Cambridge "List of International Congresses of Mathematicians Plenary and Invited Speakers") Landau listed four problems in number theory about primes that he said were particularly hard using current mathematical methods. They remain unsolved to this day and are now known as [Landau's problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau's_problems "Landau's problems").

During the 1920s, Landau was instrumental in establishing the Mathematics Institute at the nascent [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem "Hebrew University of Jerusalem"). Intent on eventually settling in Jerusalem, he taught himself Hebrew and delivered a lecture entitled *Solved and unsolved problems in elementary number theory* in Hebrew on 2 April 1925 during the university's groundbreaking ceremonies. He negotiated with the university's president, [Judah Magnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judah_Magnes "Judah Magnes"), regarding a position at the university and the building that was to house the Mathematics Institute.

Landau and his family emigrated to [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine "Mandatory Palestine") in 1927 and he began teaching at the Hebrew University. The family had difficulty adjusting to the primitive living standards then available in Jerusalem. In addition, Landau became a pawn in a struggle for control of the university between Magnes and [Chaim Weizmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaim_Weizmann "Chaim Weizmann") and [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein"). Magnes suggested that Landau be appointed Rector of the university, but Einstein and Weizmann supported [Selig Brodetsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selig_Brodetsky "Selig Brodetsky"). Landau was disgusted by the dispute and decided to return to Göttingen, remaining there until he was forced out by the Nazi regime after the [Machtergreifung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung "Machtergreifung") in 1933, in a boycott organized by [Oswald Teichmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Teichmüller "Oswald Teichmüller"). Thereafter, he lectured only outside Germany. He moved to Berlin in 1934, where he died in early 1938 of natural causes.

In 1903, Landau gave a much simpler proof than was then known of the [prime number theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_number_theorem "Prime number theorem") and later presented the first systematic treatment of [analytic number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_number_theory "Analytic number theory") in the *Handbuch der Lehre von der Verteilung der Primzahlen* (the "*Handbuch*"). He also made important contributions to [complex analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_analysis "Complex analysis").

[G. H. Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._H._Hardy "G. H. Hardy") and [Hans Heilbronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Heilbronn "Hans Heilbronn") wrote that "No one was ever more passionately devoted to mathematics than Landau".

## Works

- *Handbuch der Lehre von der Verteilung der Primzahlen*, Taubner, Leipzig, 1909.
- *Darstellung und Begründung einiger neuerer Ergebnisse der Funktionentheorie*, Springer, 1916.
- *Einführung in die elementare und analytische Theorie der algebraischen Zahlen und Ideale*, 1918.
- *Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie*, 3 Vols, S. Hirzel, Leipzig, 1927.
- *Grundlagen der Analysis, Akademische Verlagsgesellschaft*, Leipzig, 1930.
- *Einführung in die Differential- und Integralrechnung*, P. Noordhoff N. V., Groningen, 1934.
- *Über einige neuere Fortschritte der additiven Zahlentheorie*, Cambridge University Press, London, 1937.
