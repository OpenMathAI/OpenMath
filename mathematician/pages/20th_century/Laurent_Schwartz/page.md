**Laurent-Moïse Schwartz** (French: [[lɔʁɑ̃ mɔiz ʃvaʁts]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 5 March 1915 – 4 July 2002) was a French [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who received the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") in 1950 for pioneering the theory of [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_(mathematics) "Distribution (mathematics)") or generalized functions, giving a well-defined meaning to objects such as the [Dirac delta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_delta_function "Dirac delta function"). For several years he taught at the [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique "École polytechnique").

## Family

Laurent Schwartz came from a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family of [Alsatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace "Alsace") origin, with a strong scientific background: his father was a well-known [surgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgeon "Surgeon"), his uncle [Robert Debré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Debré "Robert Debré") (who contributed to the creation of [UNICEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF "UNICEF")) was a famous [pediatrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pediatrics "Pediatrics"), and his great-uncle-in-law, [Jacques Hadamard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Hadamard "Jacques Hadamard"), was a famous mathematician.

During his training at [Lycée Louis-le-Grand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Louis-le-Grand "Lycée Louis-le-Grand") to enter the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure "École Normale Supérieure"), he fell in love with mathematician [Marie-Hélène Lévy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-Hélène_Schwartz "Marie-Hélène Schwartz"), daughter of the probabilist [Paul Lévy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Lévy_(mathematician) "Paul Lévy (mathematician)") who was then teaching at the [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique "École polytechnique"). They married in 1938. Later they had two children, Marc-André and Claudine Robert, who became a professor of statistics at the [University of Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenoble_Alpes_University "Grenoble Alpes University"). Marie-Hélène was gifted in mathematics as well, contributing to the development of geometry of singular analytic spaces. She also taught at the [University of Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Lille_Nord_de_France "Université Lille Nord de France").

Angelo Guerraggio describes "Mathematics, politics and butterflies" as Schwartz's "three great loves".

## Education

According to his teachers, Schwartz was an exceptional student. He was particularly gifted in Latin, Greek and mathematics. One of his teachers told his parents: "Beware, some will say your son has a gift for languages, but he is only interested in the scientific and mathematical aspect of languages: he should become a mathematician."

In 1934, he was admitted at the École Normale Supérieure, and in 1937 he obtained the [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation "Agrégation") (with rank 2).

## World War II

As a man of [Trotskyist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotskyism "Trotskyism") affinities and [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew "Jew") descent, life was difficult for Schwartz during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). He had to hide and change his identity to avoid being [deported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation "Deportation") after Nazi Germany overran France. He worked for the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg "University of Strasbourg") (which had been relocated to [Clermont-Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand "Clermont-Ferrand") because of the war) under the name of Laurent-Marie Sélimartin (his thesis "Étude des sommes d'exponentielles réelles" being however published in 1943 under his real name in the Publications de l'Institut de Mathématique de l'Université de Clermont-Ferrand, volume 959 of Hermann's Actualités scientifiques et industrielles), while Marie-Hélène used the name Lengé instead of Lévy. Unlike other mathematicians at Clermont-Ferrand such as [Feldbau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Feldbau "Jacques Feldbau"), the couple managed to escape the Nazis.'

## Later career

Schwartz taught mainly at [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique "École Polytechnique"), from 1958 to 1980. At the end of the war, he spent one year in [Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenoble "Grenoble") (1944), then in 1945 joined the University of [Nancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy,_France "Nancy, France") on the advice of [Jean Delsarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Delsarte "Jean Delsarte") and [Jean Dieudonné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Dieudonné "Jean Dieudonné"), where he spent seven years. He was both an influential researcher and teacher, with students such as [Bernard Malgrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Malgrange "Bernard Malgrange"), [Jacques-Louis Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques-Louis_Lions "Jacques-Louis Lions"), [François Bruhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Bruhat "François Bruhat") and [Alexander Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Grothendieck "Alexander Grothendieck"). He joined the science faculty of the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris") in 1952. In 1958 he became a teacher at the [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique "École polytechnique") after having at first refused this position. From 1961 to 1963 the École polytechnique suspended his right to teach, because of his having signed the [Manifesto of the 121](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifesto_of_the_121 "Manifesto of the 121") about the [Algerian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_war "Algerian war"), a gesture not appreciated by Polytechnique's military administration. However, Schwartz had a lasting influence on mathematics at the École polytechnique, having reorganized both teaching and research there. In 1965 he established the *[Centre de mathématiques Laurent-Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_de_mathématiques_Laurent-Schwartz "Centre de mathématiques Laurent-Schwartz")* (CMLS) as its first director.

In 1973 he was elected corresponding member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences"), and was promoted to full membership in 1975.

## Mathematical legacy

At the 1950 [International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") in Cambridge (Massachusetts), Schwartz was a plenary speaker and was awarded the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") for his work on [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_(mathematics) "Distribution (mathematics)"). He was the first French mathematician to receive the Fields medal. Because of his sympathy for [Trotskyism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotskyism "Trotskyism"), Schwartz encountered serious problems trying to enter the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") to receive the medal; however, he was ultimately successful.

The theory of distributions clarified the (then) mysteries of the [Dirac delta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_delta_function "Dirac delta function") and [Heaviside step function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaviside_step_function "Heaviside step function"). It helps to extend the theory of [Fourier transforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_transform "Fourier transform") and is now of critical importance to the theory of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equation "Partial differential equation").

## Popular science

Throughout his life, Schwartz actively worked to promote science and bring it closer to the general audience. Schwartz said: "What are mathematics helpful for? Mathematics are helpful for physics. Physics helps us make fridges. Fridges are made to contain spiny lobsters, and spiny lobsters help mathematicians who eat them and have hence better abilities to do mathematics, which are helpful for physics, which helps us make fridges which..."

## Entomology

His mother, who was passionate about natural science, passed on her taste for [entomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entomology "Entomology") to Laurent. His
personal collection of 20,000 [Lepidoptera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepidoptera "Lepidoptera") specimens, collected during his various travels, was bequeathed to the [Muséum national d'histoire naturelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Natural_History_(France) "National Museum of Natural History (France)"), the [Science Museum of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musée_des_Confluences "Musée des Confluences"), the [Museum of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muséum_de_Toulouse "Muséum de Toulouse") and the Museo de Historia Natural Alcide d'Orbigny in [Cochabamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochabamba "Cochabamba") (Bolivia). Several species discovered by Schwartz bear his name.

## Personal ideology

Apart from his scientific work, Schwartz was a well-known outspoken [intellectual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual "Intellectual"). As a young [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotskyism "Trotskyism") influenced by [Leon Trotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky "Leon Trotsky"), Schwartz opposed the [totalitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarianism "Totalitarianism") of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), particularly under [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin "Joseph Stalin"). Schwartz broke with Trotskyism in 1947 and wrote that his "political culture comes almost entirely from that Trotskyist period".

On his religious views, Schwartz called himself an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism "Atheism").
