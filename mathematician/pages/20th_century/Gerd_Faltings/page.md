**Gerd Faltings** (German: [[ɡɛʁt ˈfaltɪŋs]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Gerd_Faltings.ogg "File:De-Gerd Faltings.ogg"); born 28 July 1954) is a German [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") known for his work in [arithmetic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_geometry "Arithmetic geometry"). He was awarded the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") in 1986 for his [proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_proof "Mathematical proof") of the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture "Mordell conjecture") and several related [conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjecture "Conjecture"). He won the [Abel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Prize "Abel Prize") in 2026 for these achievements.

## Education

Gerd Faltings was born on 28 July 1954 in [Gelsenkirchen-Buer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buer,_Germany "Buer, Germany"), [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany "West Germany"). From 1972 to 1978, Faltings studied [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") and [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster "University of Münster"). Interrupted by 15 months of obligatory military service, he received his [Dr. rer. nat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._rer._nat. "Dr. rer. nat.") in mathematics in 1978.

## Career and research

In 1981, he obtained the *[venia legendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venia_legendi "Venia legendi")* in mathematics from the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster "University of Münster"), where he was an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor "Assistant professor"). He was a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor "Professor") at the [University of Wuppertal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wuppertal "University of Wuppertal") from 1982 to 1984 and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") from 1985 to 1994. In the autumn of 1988 and in the [academic year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_year "Academic year") 1992–1993, he was a [visiting scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_scholar "Visiting scholar") at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study").

He was awarded the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") in 1986 for proving the [Tate conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate_conjecture "Tate conjecture") for [Abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_variety "Abelian variety") over [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_number_field "Algebraic number field"), the [Shafarevich conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faltings'_theorem "Faltings' theorem") for Abelian varieties over number fields and the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture "Mordell conjecture"), which states that any non-singular [projective curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_curve "Projective curve") of genus *g* > 1 defined over a number field *K* contains only finitely many [*K*-rational points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_point "Rational point").

In 1994, as an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "Invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians") he gave a talk *Mumford-Stabilität in der algebraischen Geometrie* (lit. 'Mumford stability in algebraic geometry'). Extending methods of [Paul Vojta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Vojta "Paul Vojta"), he proved the [Mordell–Lang conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell–Lang_conjecture "Mordell–Lang conjecture"), which is a generalization of the Mordell conjecture. Together with [Gisbert Wüstholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gisbert_Wüstholz "Gisbert Wüstholz"), he re-proved [Roth's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roth's_theorem "Roth's theorem"), for which Roth had been awarded the Fields medal in 1958.

He returned to Germany the same year, and from 1994 to 2018, he was a director of the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck-Institut_für_Mathematik "Max-Planck-Institut für Mathematik") in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn "Bonn"). In 1996, he received the [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize "Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize") of the [Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Forschungsgemeinschaft "Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft").

Faltings has been the formal supervisor of over a dozen [PhD students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD_student "PhD student"), including [Shinichi Mochizuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinichi_Mochizuki "Shinichi Mochizuki"), [Wiesława Nizioł](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesława_Nizioł "Wiesława Nizioł"), and [Nikolai Durov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Durov "Nikolai Durov").

On 19 March 2026, the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters "Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters") announced Faltings as the winner of the [Abel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Prize "Abel Prize"), "for introducing powerful tools in [arithmetic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_geometry "Arithmetic geometry") and resolving long-standing [diophantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_equation "Diophantine equation") conjectures of [Mordell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture "Mordell conjecture") and [Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell–Lang_conjecture "Mordell–Lang conjecture")." He is the first German mathematician to have received both the Fields Medal and the Abel Prize.

## Personal life

Faltings married Angelika Tschimmel in 1984; she died in 2011.
