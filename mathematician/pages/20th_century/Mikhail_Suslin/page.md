**Mikhail Yakovlevich Suslin** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Михаи́л Я́ковлевич Су́слин; November 15, 1894 – 21 October 1919, Krasavka) (sometimes transliterated **Souslin**) was a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians "Russians") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who made major contributions to the fields of [general topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_topology "General topology") and [descriptive set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descriptive_set_theory "Descriptive set theory").

## Biography

Mikhail Suslin was born on November 15, 1894, in the village of Krasavka, the only child of poor peasants Yakov Gavrilovich and Matrena Vasil'evna Suslin. From a young age, Suslin showed a keen interest in mathematics and was encouraged to continue his education by his primary school teacher, Vera Andreevna Teplogorskaya-Smirnova. From 1905 to 1913 he attended Balashov boys' grammar school.

In 1913, Suslin enrolled at the [Imperial Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Moscow_University "Imperial Moscow University") and studied under the tutelage of [Nikolai Luzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Luzin "Nikolai Luzin"). He graduated with a degree in mathematics in 1917 and immediately began working at the [Ivanovo-Voznesensk Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivanovo-Voznesensk_Polytechnic_Institute "Ivanovo-Voznesensk Polytechnic Institute").

Suslin died of [typhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhus "Typhus") in the [1919 Moscow epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic_typhus#20th_century "Epidemic typhus") following the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War "Russian Civil War"), at the age of 24.

## Work

His name is associated to [Suslin's problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin's_problem "Suslin's problem"), a question relating to [totally ordered sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered_set "Totally ordered set") that was eventually found to be [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_(mathematical_logic) "Independence (mathematical logic)") of the standard system of set-theoretic axioms, [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC "ZFC").

He contributed greatly to the theory of [analytic sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_set "Analytic set"), sometimes called after him, a kind of a [set of reals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_theory_of_the_real_line "Set theory of the real line") that is definable via [trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_(descriptive_set_theory) "Tree (descriptive set theory)"). ## See also

:   1.  A [Suslin algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_algebra "Suslin algebra") is a Boolean algebra that is complete, atomless, countably distributive, and satisfies the countable chain condition.
:   2.  A [Suslin cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_cardinal "Suslin cardinal") is a cardinal λ such that there exists a set P ⊆ 2ω such that P is λ-Suslin but P is not λ'-Suslin for any λ' < λ.
:   3.  The [Suslin hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_hypothesis "Suslin hypothesis") says that Suslin lines do not exist.
:   4.  A [Suslin line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_line "Suslin line") is a complete dense unbounded totally ordered set satisfying the countable chain condition and not order-isomorphic to the real line.
:   5.  The [Suslin number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_number "Suslin number") is the supremum of the cardinalities of families of disjoint open non-empty sets.
:   6.  The [Suslin operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_operation "Suslin operation"), usually denoted by *A*, is an operation that constructs a set from a Suslin scheme.
:   7.  The [Suslin problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_problem "Suslin problem") asks whether Suslin lines exist.
:   8.  The [Suslin property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_property "Suslin property") states that there is no uncountable family of pairwise disjoint non-empty open subsets.
:   9.  A [Suslin representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_representation "Suslin representation") of a set of reals is a tree whose projection is that set of reals.
:   10.  A [Suslin scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_scheme "Suslin scheme") is a function with domain the finite sequences of positive integers.
:   11.  A [Suslin set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_set "Suslin set") is a set that is the image of a tree under a certain projection.
:   12.  A [Suslin space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_space "Suslin space") is the image of a Polish space under a continuous mapping.
:   13.  A [Suslin subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_subset "Suslin subset") is a subset that is the image of a tree under a certain projection.
:   14.  The [Suslin theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_set "Analytic set") about analytic sets states that a set that is analytic and coanalytic is Borel.
:   15.  A [Suslin tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_tree "Suslin tree") is a tree of height ω1 such that every branch and every antichain is at most countable.
