**Nikolai Nikolayevich Luzin** (also spelled **Lusin**; Russian: Никола́й Никола́евич Лу́зин, IPA: [[nʲɪkɐˈlaj nʲɪkɐˈlajɪvʲɪtɕ ˈluzʲɪn]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Russian "Help:IPA/Russian") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ru-Nikilai_Nikilayevich_Luzin.ogg "File:Ru-Nikilai Nikilayevich Luzin.ogg"); 9 December 1883 – 28 February 1950) was a Soviet and Russian [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") known for his work in [descriptive set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descriptive_set_theory "Descriptive set theory") and aspects of [mathematical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis") with strong connections to [point-set topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-set_topology "Point-set topology"). He was the [eponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym "Eponym") of **Luzitania**, a loose group of young Moscow mathematicians of the first half of the 1920s. They adopted his [set-theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set-theoretic "Set-theoretic") orientation, and went on to apply it in other areas of mathematics.

## Life

He started studying [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") in 1901 at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University"), where his advisor was [Dmitri Egorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Egorov "Dmitri Egorov"). He graduated in 1905.

Luzin underwent great personal turmoil in the years 1905 and 1906, when his materialistic worldview had collapsed and he found himself close to suicide. In 1906 he wrote to [Pavel Florensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Florensky "Pavel Florensky"), a former fellow mathematics student who was now studying theology: *You found me a mere child at the University, knowing nothing. I don't know how it happened, but I cannot be satisfied any more with analytic functions and [Taylor series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_series "Taylor series") ... it happened about a year ago. ... To see the misery of people, to see the torment of life, to wend my way home from a mathematical meeting ... where, shivering in the cold, some women stand waiting in vain for dinner purchased with horror - this is an unbearable sight. It is unbearable, having seen this, to calmly study (in fact to enjoy) science. After that I could not study only mathematics, and I wanted to transfer to the medical school.* The correspondence between the two men continued for many years and Luzin was greatly influenced by Florensky's religious treatise *The Pillar and Foundation of Truth* (1908).

From 1910 to 1914 Luzin studied at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"), where he was influenced by [Edmund Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Landau "Edmund Landau"). He then returned to Moscow and received his Ph.D. degree in 1915. During the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War "Russian Civil War") (1918–1920) Luzin left Moscow for the *Polytechnical Institute Ivanovo-Voznesensk* (now called [Ivanovo State University of Chemistry and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivanovo_State_University_of_Chemistry_and_Technology "Ivanovo State University of Chemistry and Technology")). He returned to Moscow in 1920.

In the 1920s Luzin organized a famous research seminar at Moscow State University. His doctoral students included some of the most famous Soviet mathematicians: [Pavel Alexandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Alexandrov "Pavel Alexandrov"), [Nina Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Bari "Nina Bari"), [Aleksandr Khinchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Khinchin "Aleksandr Khinchin"), [Andrey Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Kolmogorov "Andrey Kolmogorov"), [Aleksandr Kronrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Kronrod "Aleksandr Kronrod"), [Mikhail Lavrentyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Lavrentyev "Mikhail Lavrentyev"), [Alexey Lyapunov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Lyapunov "Alexey Lyapunov"), [Lazar Lyusternik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazar_Lyusternik "Lazar Lyusternik"), [Pyotr Novikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Novikov "Pyotr Novikov"), [Lev Schnirelmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Schnirelmann "Lev Schnirelmann") and [Pavel Urysohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Urysohn "Pavel Urysohn").

On 5 January 1927 Luzin was elected as a corresponding member of the [Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union "Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union") and became a [full member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academician "Academician") of the Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union first at the Department of Philosophy and then at the Department of Pure Mathematics (12 January 1929). In 1929 he was elected as a member of the [Polish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Polish Academy of Arts and Sciences") in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków").

## Research work

Luzin's first significant result was a construction of an almost everywhere divergent [trigonometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_series "Trigonometric series") with monotonic convergence to zero coefficients (1912). This example disproved the [Pierre Fatou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Fatou "Pierre Fatou") conjecture and was unexpected to most mathematicians at that time.

At approximately the same time, he proved what is now called [Lusin's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusin's_theorem "Lusin's theorem") in [real analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_analysis "Real analysis").

His Ph.D. thesis titled *Integral and trigonometric series* (1915) had a large impact on the subsequent development of the metric theory of functions. A set of problems formulated in this thesis for a long time attracted attention from mathematicians. For example, the first problem in the list, on the convergence of the [Fourier series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_series "Fourier series") for a [square-integrable function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square-integrable_function "Square-integrable function"), came to be called Luzin's conjecture and was solved by [Lennart Carleson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lennart_Carleson "Lennart Carleson") in 1966 ([Carleson's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleson's_theorem "Carleson's theorem")).

In the theory of boundary properties of [analytic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_functions "Analytic functions") he proved an important result on the invariance of sets of boundary points under [conformal mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_map "Conformal map") (1919).

Luzin was one of the founders of [descriptive set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descriptive_set_theory "Descriptive set theory"). Together with his student [Mikhail Suslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Suslin "Mikhail Suslin"), he developed the theory of [analytic sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_set "Analytic set").

He also made contributions to [complex analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_analysis "Complex analysis"), the theory of [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equation "Differential equation"), and [numerical methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_analysis "Numerical analysis").

### Letter to Vygodsky

In a letter to M. Ya. Vygodsky dating from 1932, Luzin expresses sympathy with Vygodsky's [infinitesimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal "Infinitesimal") approach to developing calculus. He mocks accusations of [bourgeois decadence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeois_decadence?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bourgeois decadence") against Vygodsky's textbook, and relates his own youthful experience with what he felt were unnecessary formal complications of the traditional development of analysis. Typical is his youthful reaction to his teachers' insistence that the derivative is a limit: "They won't fool me: it's simply the ratio of infinitesimals, nothing else." A recent study notes that Luzin's letter contained remarkable anticipations of modern calculus with infinitesimals.

## Luzin affair of 1936

On 21 November 1930, the declaration of the "initiative group" of the [Moscow Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Mathematical_Society "Moscow Mathematical Society") which consisted of Luzin's former students Lazar Lyusternik and [Lev Schnirelmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Schnirelmann "Lev Schnirelmann") along with [Alexander Gelfond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Gelfond "Alexander Gelfond") and [Lev Pontryagin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Pontryagin "Lev Pontryagin") claimed that "there appeared active counter-revolutionaries among mathematicians". Some of these mathematicians were pointed out, including the advisor of Luzin, [Dmitri Egorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Egorov "Dmitri Egorov"). In September 1930, Egorov was arrested on the basis of his religious beliefs. He then left the position of director of the Moscow Mathematical Society and was replaced by [Ernst Kolman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Kolman "Ernst Kolman"). As a result, Luzin left the Moscow Mathematical Society and Moscow State University. Egorov died on 10 September 1931, after a [hunger strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunger_strike "Hunger strike") initiated in prison. In 1931, Kolman brought the first complaint against Luzin.

In 1936 the [Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge "Great Purge") began. Millions of people were arrested or executed, including leading members of the intelligentsia. In July–August of that year, Luzin was criticized in *[Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravda "Pravda")* in a series of anonymous articles whose authorship later was attributed to Kolman. It was alleged that Luzin published "would-be scientific papers", "felt no shame in declaring the discoveries of his students to be his own achievements", and stood close to the ideology of the "[black hundreds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hundreds "Black Hundreds")", orthodoxy, and [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy "Monarchy") "fascist-type modernized but slightly." One of the complaints was that he published his major results in foreign journals.

The article triggered a special hearing on Luzin's case by the Commission of the Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union, where the allegations were reviewed and formalized. At the hearing, [Alexandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Alexandrov "Pavel Alexandrov"), [Lyusternik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazar_Lyusternik "Lazar Lyusternik"), [Khinchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Khinchin "Aleksandr Khinchin"), [Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Kolmogorov "Andrey Kolmogorov") and some other students of Luzin accused him of plagiarism from [Pyotr Novikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Novikov "Pyotr Novikov") and [Mikhail Suslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Suslin "Mikhail Suslin") and various forms of misconduct, which included denying promotions to Kolmogorov and Khinchin. According to some researchers, Alexandrov and Kolmogorov had been involved in a homosexual relationship in the 1930s, a fact the police used to pressure them into testifying against their former teacher. [Sergei Sobolev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Sobolev "Sergei Sobolev"), [Gleb Krzhizhanovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleb_Krzhizhanovsky "Gleb Krzhizhanovsky") and [Otto Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Schmidt "Otto Schmidt") incriminated Luzin with charges of disloyalty to Soviet power. The methods of political insinuations and slander had been used against the old Muscovite professorship already several years before the article in *Pravda*.

The hearings were completed in five sessions between July 7, 1936, and July 15, 1936, and people testifying, as well as the nature of accusations, changed from one session to another. In the initial session, the accusations were separated into accusations of scientific misconduct, which included plagiarism; accusations of professional misconduct, which mostly involved accusations of nepotism in promotions and reviews; and political accusations, which were the most serious. The initial review on July 7, which most prominently featured Alexandrov and Kolmogorov, concluded in a warning to Luzin regarding plagiarism while stressing the overall importance of his work, cleared him politically, yet recommended to relieve him of administrative duties.

However, this outcome did not seem to satisfy the instigators of the case, so that from the second hearing on, the nature of accusations shifted: now the primary focus was that Luzin published his papers extensively in France rather than in Soviet journals, and his pre-Soviet sympathies were brought to the forefront.

The special hearing of the Commission of the Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union endorsed all accusations of Luzin as an "enemy under the mask of a Soviet citizen." Although the Commission convicted Luzin, he was neither expelled from the academy nor arrested, but his department in the [Steklov Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Institute_of_Mathematics "Steklov Institute of Mathematics") was closed and he lost all his official positions. There has been some speculation about why his punishment was so much milder than that of most other people condemned at that time, but the reason for this does not seem to be known for certain. [Historian of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_mathematics "History of mathematics") [Adolph P. Yushkevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolph_P._Yushkevich "Adolph P. Yushkevich") speculated that at the time, [Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin "Joseph Stalin") was more concerned with the forthcoming [Moscow trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_trials "Moscow trials") of [Lev Kamenev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Kamenev "Lev Kamenev"), [Grigory Zinoviev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Zinoviev "Grigory Zinoviev") and others, whereas the eventual fate of Luzin was of a little interest to him.

The 1936 decision of the Academy of Sciences was not canceled after Stalin's death. The decision was finally [reversed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_rehabilitation "Political rehabilitation") on January 17, 2012.

## Honors

In 1976, Martian crater *[Luzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzin_(crater) "Luzin (crater)")* was named in his honor.
