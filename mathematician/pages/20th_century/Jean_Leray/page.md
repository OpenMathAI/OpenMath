**Jean Leray** (French: [[ləʁɛ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 7 November 1906 – 10 November 1998) was a French [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"), who worked on both [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equation "Partial differential equation") and [algebraic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_topology "Algebraic topology").

## Life and career

He was born in Chantenay-sur-Loire (today part of [Nantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantes "Nantes")). He studied at [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure "École Normale Supérieure") from 1926 to 1929. He received his Ph.D. in 1933. In 1934 Leray published an important paper that founded the study of [weak solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_solution "Weak solution") of the [Navier–Stokes equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier–Stokes_equations "Navier–Stokes equations"). In the same year, he and [Juliusz Schauder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliusz_Schauder "Juliusz Schauder") discovered a topological invariant, now called the [Leray–Schauder degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leray–Schauder_degree "Leray–Schauder degree"), which they applied to prove the existence of solutions for partial differential equations lacking uniqueness.

From 1938 to 1939 he was professor at the [University of Nancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nancy "University of Nancy"). He did not join the [Bourbaki group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbaki_group "Bourbaki group"), although he was close with its founders.

His main work in [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology "Topology") was carried out while he was a [prisoner of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_war "Prisoner of war") in a camp in [Edelbach, Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edelbach,_Allentsteig "Edelbach, Allentsteig") from 1940 to 1945. He concealed his expertise on differential equations, fearing that its connections with [applied mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematics "Applied mathematics") could lead him to be asked to aid the German war effort.

Leray's work of this period proved seminal to the development of [spectral sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_sequence "Spectral sequence") and [sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_(mathematics) "Sheaf (mathematics)"). These were subsequently developed by many others, each separately becoming an important tool in [homological algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homological_algebra "Homological algebra").

He returned to work on partial differential equations from about 1950.

He was professor at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris") from 1945 to 1947, and then at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France") until 1978.

He was awarded the Malaxa Prize ([Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania "Romania"), 1938), the Grand Prix in mathematical sciences ([French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences"), 1940), the [Feltrinelli Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feltrinelli_Prize "Feltrinelli Prize") ([Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei "Accademia dei Lincei"), 1971), the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Mathematics "Wolf Prize in Mathematics") ([Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"), 1979), and the [Lomonosov Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov_Gold_Medal "Lomonosov Gold Medal") ([Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow"), 1988). He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1959 and the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1965.
