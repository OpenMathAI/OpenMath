**Jean-Pierre Serre** (French: [[sɛʁ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 15 September 1926) is a French [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who has made contributions to [algebraic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_topology "Algebraic topology"), [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry") and [algebraic number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_number_theory "Algebraic number theory"). He was awarded the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") in 1954 and the inaugural [Abel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Prize "Abel Prize") in 2003.

## Biography

### Personal life

Born in [Bages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bages,_Pyrénées-Orientales "Bages, Pyrénées-Orientales"), [Pyrénées-Orientales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrénées-Orientales "Pyrénées-Orientales"), to pharmacist parents, Serre was educated at the Lycée de Nîmes. Then he studied at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure "École Normale Supérieure") in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") from 1945 to 1948. He was awarded his doctorate from the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris") in 1951. From 1948 to 1954 he held positions at the [Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_National_de_la_Recherche_Scientifique "Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique") in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"). In 1956 he was elected professor at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France"), a position he held until his retirement in 1994.

His wife, Professor [Josiane Heulot-Serre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josiane_Serre "Josiane Serre"), was a chemist; she also was the director of the Ecole Normale Supérieure de Jeunes Filles. Their daughter is the former French diplomat, historian and writer [Claudine Monteil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudine_Monteil "Claudine Monteil"). The French mathematician [Denis Serre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Serre "Denis Serre") is his nephew. He practices skiing, table tennis, and rock climbing (in [Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontainebleau "Fontainebleau")).

### Career

From a very young age, Serre was an outstanding figure in the school of [Henri Cartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Cartan "Henri Cartan"), working on [algebraic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_topology "Algebraic topology"), [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables "Several complex variables") and then [commutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra "Commutative algebra") and [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry"), where he introduced [sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_(mathematics) "Sheaf (mathematics)") theory and [homological algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homological_algebra "Homological algebra") techniques. Serre's thesis concerned the [Leray–Serre spectral sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_spectral_sequence "Serre spectral sequence") associated to a [fibration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibration "Fibration"). Together with Cartan, Serre established the technique of using [Eilenberg–MacLane spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg–MacLane_space "Eilenberg–MacLane space") for computing [homotopy groups of spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_groups_of_spheres "Homotopy groups of spheres"), which at that time was one of the major problems in topology.

In his speech at the Fields Medal award ceremony in 1954, [Hermann Weyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Weyl "Hermann Weyl") gave high praise to Serre, and also made the point that the award was for the first time awarded to a non-analyst. Serre subsequently changed his research focus.

#### Algebraic geometry

In the 1950s and 1960s, a fruitful collaboration between Serre and the two-years-younger [Alexander Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Grothendieck "Alexander Grothendieck") led to important foundational work, much of it motivated by the [Weil conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_conjectures "Weil conjectures"). Two major foundational papers by Serre were *Faisceaux algébriques cohérents* (FAC, 1955), on [coherent cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_cohomology "Coherent cohomology"), and *Géométrie algébrique et géométrie analytique* ([GAGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAGA "GAGA"), 1956).

Even at an early stage in his work Serre had perceived a need to construct more general and refined [cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology "Cohomology") theories to tackle the Weil conjectures. The problem was that the cohomology of a [coherent sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_sheaf "Coherent sheaf") over a [finite field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_field "Finite field") could not capture as much topology as [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology "Singular cohomology") with integer coefficients. Amongst Serre's early candidate theories of 1954–55 was one based on [Witt vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witt_vector "Witt vector") coefficients.

Around 1958, Serre suggested that isotrivial principal bundles on algebraic varieties—those that become trivial after pullback by a finite [étale map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_map "Étale map")—are important. This acted as one important source of inspiration for Grothendieck to develop [étale topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_topology "Étale topology") and the corresponding theory of [étale cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_cohomology "Étale cohomology"). These tools, developed in full by Grothendieck and collaborators in [Séminaire de géométrie algébrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Séminaire_de_géométrie_algébrique "Séminaire de géométrie algébrique") (SGA) 4 and SGA 5, provided the tools for the eventual proof of the Weil conjectures by [Pierre Deligne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Deligne "Pierre Deligne").

#### Other work

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/SERRE_Jean-Pierre.jpg/250px-SERRE_Jean-Pierre.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:SERRE_Jean-Pierre.jpg)

Serre

From 1959 onward Serre's interests turned towards [group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_theory "Group theory"), [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory"), in particular [Galois representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module "Galois module") and [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_form "Modular form").

Amongst his most original contributions were: his "[Conjecture II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_conjecture_II_(algebra) "Serre's conjecture II (algebra)")" (still open) on Galois cohomology; his use of [group actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_(mathematics) "Group action (mathematics)") on [trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass–Serre_theory "Bass–Serre theory") (with [Hyman Bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyman_Bass "Hyman Bass")); the Borel–Serre compactification; results on the number of points of curves over finite fields; [Galois representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_representation "Galois representation") in [ℓ-adic cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-adic_cohomology "L-adic cohomology") and the proof that these representations have often a "large" image; the concept of [p-adic modular form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_modular_form "P-adic modular form"); and the [Serre conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_conjecture_(number_theory) "Serre conjecture (number theory)") (now a theorem) on mod-*p* representations that made [Fermat's Last Theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat's_Last_Theorem "Fermat's Last Theorem") a connected part of mainstream [arithmetic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_geometry "Arithmetic geometry").

In his paper FAC, Serre asked whether a finitely generated [projective module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_module "Projective module") over a [polynomial ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_ring "Polynomial ring") is [free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_module "Free module"). This question led to a great deal of activity in [commutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra "Commutative algebra"), and was finally answered in the affirmative by [Daniel Quillen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Quillen "Daniel Quillen") and [Andrei Suslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Suslin "Andrei Suslin") independently in 1976. This result is now known as the [Quillen–Suslin theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quillen–Suslin_theorem "Quillen–Suslin theorem").

## Honors and awards

In 1954, Serre, at the age of 27 in 1954, was and still is the youngest person ever to have been awarded the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal"). He went on to win the [Balzan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balzan_Prize "Balzan Prize") in 1985, the [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize "Steele Prize") in 1995, the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Mathematics "Wolf Prize in Mathematics") in 2000, and was the first recipient of the [Abel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Prize "Abel Prize") in 2003. He has been awarded other prizes, such as the Gold Medal of the [French National Scientific Research Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Scientific_Research_Centre "French National Scientific Research Centre") (Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique, CNRS).

He is a foreign member of several scientific Academies (US, Norway, Sweden, Russia, the Royal Society, [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences") (1978), [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"), the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society")) and has received many honorary degrees (from Cambridge, Oxford, Harvard, Oslo and others). In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Society "American Mathematical Society").

Serre has been awarded the highest honors in France as [Grand Cross of the Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour "Legion of Honour") (Grand Croix de la Légion d'Honneur) and [Grand Cross of the Legion of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_national_du_Mérite "Ordre national du Mérite") (Grand Croix de l'Ordre National du Mérite).
