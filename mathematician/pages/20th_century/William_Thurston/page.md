**William Paul Thurston** (October 30, 1946 – August 21, 2012) was an American [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"). He was a pioneer in the field of [low-dimensional topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-dimensional_topology "Low-dimensional topology") and was awarded the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") in 1982 for his contributions to the study of [3-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-manifold "3-manifold").

Thurston was a professor of mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis "University of California, Davis"), and [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"). He was also a director of the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute "Mathematical Sciences Research Institute").

## Early life and education

William Thurston was born in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), to Margaret Thurston ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Martt), a seamstress, and Paul Thurston, an aeronautical engineer. William Thurston suffered from congenital [strabismus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabismus "Strabismus") as a child, causing issues with depth perception. His mother worked with him as a toddler to reconstruct three-dimensional images from two-dimensional ones.

He received his bachelor's degree from [New College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College_of_Florida "New College of Florida") in 1967 as part of its inaugural class. For his undergraduate thesis, he developed an [intuitionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuitionism "Intuitionism") foundation for topology. Taking advantage of New College's liberal attitude towards student living arrangements, Thurston at various times lived in a tent in nearby woods or slept in academic buildings.

Following this, he received a doctorate in mathematics from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), under [Morris Hirsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Hirsch "Morris Hirsch"), with a thesis titled *Foliations of Three-Manifolds which are Circle Bundles* in 1972.

## Career

After completing his Ph.D., Thurston spent a year at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study"), then another year at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") as an assistant professor.

In 1974, Thurston was appointed a full professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"). He returned to [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1991 to be a professor (1991-1996) and was also director of the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute "Mathematical Sciences Research Institute") (MSRI) from 1992 to 1997. He was on the faculty at [UC Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis "University of California, Davis") from 1996 until 2003, when he moved to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University").

Thurston was an early adopter of computing in pure mathematics research. He inspired [Jeffrey Weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Weeks_(mathematician) "Jeffrey Weeks (mathematician)") to develop the [SnapPea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SnapPea "SnapPea") computing program.

During Thurston's directorship at MSRI, the institute introduced several innovative educational programs that have since become standard for research institutes.

His Ph.D. students include [Danny Calegari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Calegari "Danny Calegari"), [Richard Canary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Canary "Richard Canary"), [Benson Farb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benson_Farb "Benson Farb"), [William Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Floyd_(mathematician) "William Floyd (mathematician)"), [David Gabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gabai "David Gabai"), [William Goldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Goldman_(professor) "William Goldman (professor)"), [Richard Kenyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kenyon "Richard Kenyon"), [Steven Kerckhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Kerckhoff "Steven Kerckhoff"), [Yair Minsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yair_Minsky "Yair Minsky"), [Igor Rivin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Rivin "Igor Rivin"), [Oded Schramm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oded_Schramm "Oded Schramm"), [Richard Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schwartz_(mathematician) "Richard Schwartz (mathematician)"), and [Jeffrey Weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Weeks_(mathematician) "Jeffrey Weeks (mathematician)").

## Research

### Foliations

His early work, in the early 1970s, was mainly in [foliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foliation "Foliation") theory. His more significant results include:

- The proof that every [Haefliger structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haefliger_structure "Haefliger structure") on a manifold can be integrated to a foliation (this implies, in particular, that every manifold with zero [Euler characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_characteristic "Euler characteristic") admits a foliation of [codimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codimension "Codimension") one).
- The construction of a continuous family of smooth, codimension-one foliations on the [three-sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-sphere "N-sphere") whose [Godbillon–Vey invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godbillon–Vey_invariant?action=edit&redlink=1 "Godbillon–Vey invariant") (after [Claude Godbillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Godbillon?action=edit&redlink=1 "Claude Godbillon") and Jacques Vey) take every real value.
- With [John N. Mather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_N._Mather "John N. Mather"), he gave a proof that the [cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology "Cohomology") of the group of [homeomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphism "Homeomorphism") of a manifold is the same whether the group is considered with its [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_space "Discrete space") or its [compact-open topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact-open_topology "Compact-open topology").

In fact, Thurston resolved so many outstanding problems in foliation theory in such a short period of time that it led to an exodus from the field, where advisors counselled students against going into foliation theory, because Thurston was "cleaning out the subject" (see "On Proof and Progress in Mathematics", especially section 6).

### The geometrization conjecture

His later work, starting around the mid-1970s, revealed that [hyperbolic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_geometry "Hyperbolic geometry") played a far more important role in the general theory of [3-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-manifold "3-manifold") than was previously realised. Prior to Thurston, there were only a handful of known examples of [hyperbolic 3-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_3-manifold "Hyperbolic 3-manifold") of finite volume, such as the [Seifert–Weber space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifert–Weber_space "Seifert–Weber space"). The independent and distinct approaches of [Robert Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Riley_(mathematician) "Robert Riley (mathematician)") and [Troels Jørgensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troels_Jørgensen "Troels Jørgensen") in the mid-to-late 1970s showed that such examples were less atypical than previously believed; in particular their work showed that the [figure-eight knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure-eight_knot_(mathematics) "Figure-eight knot (mathematics)") [complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_complement "Knot complement") was [hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_link "Hyperbolic link"). This was the first example of a [hyperbolic knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_knot "Hyperbolic knot").

Inspired by their work, Thurston took a different, more explicit means of exhibiting the hyperbolic structure of the [figure-eight knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure-eight_knot "Figure-eight knot") complement. He showed that the figure-eight knot complement could be [decomposed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_decomposition "Manifold decomposition") as the union of two regular ideal hyperbolic tetrahedra whose hyperbolic structures matched up correctly and gave the hyperbolic structure on the figure-eight knot complement. By utilizing [Haken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Haken "Wolfgang Haken")'s [normal surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_surface "Normal surface") techniques, he classified the [incompressible surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incompressible_surface "Incompressible surface") in the knot complement. Together with his analysis of deformations of hyperbolic structures, he concluded that all but 10 [Dehn surgeries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehn_surgery "Dehn surgery") on the figure-eight knot resulted in [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducibility_(mathematics) "Irreducibility (mathematics)"), non-[Haken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haken_manifold "Haken manifold") non-[Seifert-fibered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifert_fiber_space "Seifert fiber space") 3-manifolds. These were the first such examples; previously it had been believed that except for certain Seifert fiber spaces, all irreducible 3-manifolds were Haken. These examples were actually hyperbolic and motivated his next theorem.

Thurston proved that in fact most Dehn fillings on a cusped hyperbolic 3-manifold resulted in hyperbolic 3-manifolds. This is his celebrated [hyperbolic Dehn surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_Dehn_surgery "Hyperbolic Dehn surgery") theorem.

To complete the picture, Thurston proved a [hyperbolization theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolization_theorem "Hyperbolization theorem") for [Haken manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haken_manifold "Haken manifold"). A particularly important corollary is that many knots and links are in fact hyperbolic. Together with his hyperbolic Dehn surgery theorem, this showed that closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds existed in great abundance.

The hyperbolization theorem for Haken manifolds has been called *Thurston's Monster Theorem,* due to the length and difficulty of the proof. Complete proofs were not written up until almost 20 years later. The proof involves a number of deep and original insights which have linked many apparently disparate fields to [3-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-manifold "3-manifold").

Thurston was next led to formulate his [geometrization conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrization_conjecture "Geometrization conjecture"). This gave a conjectural picture of 3-manifolds which indicated that all 3-manifolds admitted a certain kind of geometric decomposition involving eight geometries, now called Thurston model geometries. Hyperbolic geometry is the most prevalent geometry in this picture and also the most complicated. The conjecture was proved by [Grigori Perelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigori_Perelman "Grigori Perelman") in 2002–2003.

### Density conjecture

Thurston and [Dennis Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Sullivan "Dennis Sullivan") generalized [Lipman Bers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipman_Bers "Lipman Bers")' [density conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_theorem_for_Kleinian_groups "Density theorem for Kleinian groups") from singly degenerate Kleinian surface groups to all [finitely generated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_generated_group "Finitely generated group") [Kleinian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleinian_group "Kleinian group") in the late 1970s and early 1980s. The conjecture states that every finitely generated Kleinian group is an algebraic limit of [geometrically finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_finiteness "Geometric finiteness") Kleinian groups, and was independently proven by Ohshika and Namazi–Souto in 2011 and 2012 respectively.

### Orbifold theorem

In his work on hyperbolic Dehn surgery, Thurston realized that [orbifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbifold "Orbifold") structures naturally arose. Such structures had been studied prior to Thurston, but his work, particularly the next theorem, would bring them to prominence. In 1981, he announced the [orbifold theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbifold#3-dimensional_orbifolds "Orbifold"), an extension of his geometrization theorem to the setting of 3-orbifolds. Two teams of mathematicians around 2000 finally finished their efforts to write down a complete proof, based mostly on Thurston's lectures given in the early 1980s in Princeton. His original proof relied partly on [Richard S. Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_S._Hamilton "Richard S. Hamilton")'s work on the [Ricci flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_flow "Ricci flow").

## Awards and honors

In 1976, Thurston and [James Harris Simons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Harris_Simons "James Harris Simons") shared the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen_Prize_in_Geometry "Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry").

Thurston received the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") in 1982 for "revolutioniz[ing] [the] study of topology in 2 and 3 dimensions, showing interplay between analysis, topology, and geometry" and "contribut[ing] [the] idea that a very large class of closed [3-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-manifold "3-manifold") carry a hyperbolic structure."

In 2005, Thurston won the first [American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Society "American Mathematical Society") [Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_L._Doob_Prize "Joseph L. Doob Prize"), for *[Three-dimensional Geometry and Topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_geometry_and_topology_of_three-manifolds "The geometry and topology of three-manifolds")*.
The prize "recognizes an outstanding research book that makes a seminal contribution to the research literature". He was awarded the 2012 [Leroy P. Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leroy_P._Steele_Prize "Leroy P. Steele Prize") by the American Mathematical Society for seminal contribution to research. The citation described his work as having "revolutionized 3-manifold theory".

## Personal life

Thurston met his first wife, Rachel Findley, as members of New College's inaugural class. They had three children: Dylan, Nathaniel, and Emily. Dylan was a [MOSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Olympiad_Program "Mathematical Olympiad Program") participant (1988–90) and is a mathematician at [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College "Boston College"). Thurston had two children with his second wife, Julian Muriel Thurston: Hannah Jade and Liam.

Thurston died on August 21, 2012, in [Rochester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York "Rochester, New York"), of a sinus mucosal [melanoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanoma "Melanoma") that was diagnosed in 2011.
