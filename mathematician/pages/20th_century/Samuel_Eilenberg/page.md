**Samuel Eilenberg** (September 30, 1913 – January 30, 1998) was a [Polish-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Americans "Polish Americans") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who co-founded [category theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_theory "Category theory") (with [Saunders Mac Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saunders_Mac_Lane "Saunders Mac Lane")) and [homological algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homological_algebra "Homological algebra").

## Early life and education

He was born in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw "Warsaw"), Kingdom of Poland to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") family. He spent much of his career as a professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University").

He earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") from [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw "University of Warsaw") in 1936, with thesis *On the Topological Applications of Maps onto a Circle*; his thesis advisors were [Kazimierz Kuratowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Kuratowski "Kazimierz Kuratowski") and [Karol Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk "Karol Borsuk"). He died in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") in January 1998.

## Career

Eilenberg's main body of work was in [algebraic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_topology "Algebraic topology"). He worked on the axiomatic treatment of [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory "Homology theory") with [Norman Steenrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Steenrod "Norman Steenrod") (and the [Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg–Steenrod_axioms "Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms") are named for the pair), and on [homological algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homological_algebra "Homological algebra") with [Saunders Mac Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saunders_Mac_Lane "Saunders Mac Lane"). As a result of this work, Eilenberg and Mac Lane developed the field of [category theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_theory "Category theory"), for which they are now best known.

Eilenberg was a member of [Bourbaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Bourbaki "Nicolas Bourbaki") and, with [Henri Cartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Cartan "Henri Cartan"), wrote the 1956 book *Homological Algebra*.

Later in life he worked mainly in pure category theory, being one of the founders of the field. The [Eilenberg swindle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg_swindle "Eilenberg swindle") (or *telescope*) is a construction applying the [telescoping cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescoping_cancellation "Telescoping cancellation") idea to [projective modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_module "Projective module").

Eilenberg contributed to [automata theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automata_theory "Automata theory") and algebraic automata theory. In particular, he introduced a model of computation called [X-machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-machine "X-machine") and a new prime decomposition algorithm for finite state machines in the vein of [Krohn–Rhodes theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krohn–Rhodes_theory "Krohn–Rhodes theory"). He also identified a natural correspondence between certain [classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_(set_theory) "Class (set theory)") of [regular languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_language "Regular language") called varieties and [pseudovarieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(universal_algebra) "Variety (universal algebra)") of finite monoids, a result which is now known as Eilenberg's theorem.

## Art collection

Eilenberg was also a prominent collector of [Asian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_art "Asian art"). His collection mainly consisted of small sculptures and other artifacts from [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia"), [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") and [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"). In 1991–1992, the [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art "Metropolitan Museum of Art") in New York staged an exhibition from more than 400 items that Eilenberg had donated to the museum, entitled *The Lotus Transcendent: Indian and Southeast Asian Art From the Samuel Eilenberg Collection*. In reciprocity, the Metropolitan Museum of Art donated substantially to the endowment of the Samuel Eilenberg Visiting Professorship in Mathematics at Columbia University.
