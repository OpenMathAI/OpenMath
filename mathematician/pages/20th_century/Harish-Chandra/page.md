**Harish-Chandra** (né Harishchandra) [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (11 October 1923 – 16 October 1983) was an [Indian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian-Americans "Indian-Americans") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist") who did fundamental work in [representation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_theory "Representation theory"), especially [harmonic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_analysis "Harmonic analysis") on [semisimple Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group "Semisimple Lie group").

## Early life

Harish-Chandra was born in [Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur "Kanpur"). He was educated at [B.N.S.D. College, Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNSD_Inter_College "BNSD Inter College") and at the [University of Allahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Allahabad "University of Allahabad"). After receiving his master's degree in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") in 1940, he moved to the [Indian Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Science "Indian Institute of Science"), Bangalore for further studies under [Homi J. Bhabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homi_J._Bhabha "Homi J. Bhabha").

In 1945, he moved to [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), and worked as a research student under [Paul Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac"). While at Cambridge, he attended lectures by [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli "Wolfgang Pauli"), and during one of them, Harish-Chandra pointed out a mistake in Pauli's work. The two became lifelong friends. During this time he became increasingly interested in mathematics. He obtained his PhD, *Infinite Irreducible Representations of the Lorentz* *Group*, at Cambridge in 1947 under Dirac.

## Honors and awards

He was a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") and a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society"). He was the recipient of the [Cole Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cole_Prize "Cole Prize") of the [American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Society "American Mathematical Society"), in 1954. The [Indian National Science Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Science_Academy "Indian National Science Academy") honoured him with the [Srinivasa Ramanujan Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srinivasa_Ramanujan_Medal "Srinivasa Ramanujan Medal") in 1974. In 1981, he received an honorary degree from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University").

The mathematics department of V.S.S.D. College, Kanpur celebrates his birthday every year in different forms, which includes lectures from students and professors from various colleges, institutes and students' visit to Harish-Chandra Research Institute.

The Indian Government named the [Harish-Chandra Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harish-Chandra_Research_Institute "Harish-Chandra Research Institute"), an institute dedicated to Theoretical Physics and Mathematics, after him.

[Robert Langlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Langlands "Robert Langlands") wrote in a biographical article of Harish-Chandra:

> He was considered for the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") in 1958, but a forceful member of the selection committee in whose eyes [Thom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Thom "René Thom") was a [Bourbakist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Bourbaki "Nicolas Bourbaki") was determined not to have two. So Harish-Chandra, whom he also placed on the Bourbaki camp, was set aside.

He was also a recipient of the [Padma Bhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bhushan "Padma Bhushan") in 1977.

## Personal life

In 1952, Harish-Chandra married Lalitha "Lily" Kale (1934–2019). Kale, whose father was an Indian food scientist and whose mother was a Polish Jew, was born in Warsaw but grew up in [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore "Bangalore") after her family fled Poland in 1939 or 1940. They had two daughters, [Premala Chandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premala_Chandra "Premala Chandra") and Devika Chandra; Premala also became a physicist.

## Death

Starting in 1969, Harish-Chandra began to experience [heart attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction "Myocardial infarction"). A second and third heart attack occurred in 1970 and 1982, respectively. From then, his physical capabilities began to decline. A fourth heart attack occurred in 1983, leaving him mostly bedridden and in isolation. On the day after a conference organized for him and mathematician [Armand Borel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_Borel "Armand Borel") took place, Harish-Chandra died from his final heart attack.
