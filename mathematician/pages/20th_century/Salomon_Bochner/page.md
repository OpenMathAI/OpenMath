**Salomon Bochner** (20 August 1899 – 2 May 1982) was a [Galizien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria "Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria")-born [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"), known for work in [mathematical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis"), [probability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_theory "Probability theory") and [differential geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_geometry "Differential geometry").

## Life

He was born into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family in [Podgórze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podgórze "Podgórze") (near [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków")), then Austria-Hungary, now [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland"). Fearful of a Russian invasion in [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_(Central_Europe) "Galicia (Central Europe)") at the beginning of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") in 1914, his family moved to Germany, seeking greater security. Bochner was educated at a [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin") [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)") (secondary school), and then at the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin "Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin"). There, he was a student of [Erhard Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erhard_Schmidt "Erhard Schmidt"), writing a dissertation involving what would later be called the [Bergman kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergman_kernel "Bergman kernel"). Shortly after this, he left the academy to help his family during the [escalating inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation#The_1920s_German_inflation "Hyperinflation"). After returning to mathematical research, he lectured at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München") from 1924 to 1933. His academic career in Germany ended after the [Nazis came to power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung "Machtergreifung") in 1933, and he left for a position at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"). He was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") from 1945 to 1948. He was appointed as Henry Burchard Fine Professor in 1959, retiring in 1968. Although he was seventy years old when he retired from Princeton, Bochner was appointed as Edgar Odell Lovett Professor of Mathematics at [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University "Rice University") and went on to hold this chair until his death in 1982. He became Head of the Department at Rice in 1969 and held this position until 1976. He died in [Houston, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston,_Texas "Houston, Texas"). He was an [Orthodox Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Jew "Orthodox Jew").

## Mathematical work

In 1925, he started work in the area of [almost periodic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_periodic_function "Almost periodic function"), simplifying the approach of [Harald Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Bohr "Harald Bohr") by use of [compactness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactness "Compactness") and [approximate identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximate_identity "Approximate identity") arguments. In 1933, he defined the [Bochner integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochner_integral "Bochner integral"), as it is now called, for vector-valued functions. [Bochner's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochner's_theorem "Bochner's theorem") on [Fourier transforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_transform "Fourier transform") appeared in a 1932 book. His techniques came into their own as [Pontryagin duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_duality "Pontryagin duality") and then the representation theory of [locally compact groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_group "Locally compact group") developed in the following years.

Subsequently, he worked on [multiple Fourier series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_Fourier_series "Multiple Fourier series"), posing the question of the [Bochner–Riesz means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochner–Riesz_mean "Bochner–Riesz mean"). This led to results on how the Fourier transform on [Euclidean space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_space "Euclidean space") behaves under rotations.

In differential geometry, [Bochner's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochner's_formula "Bochner's formula") on [curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curvature "Curvature") was published in 1946. Joint work with [Kentaro Yano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentaro_Yano_(mathematician) "Kentaro Yano (mathematician)") (1912–1993) led to the 1953 book *Curvature and Betti Numbers*. It had consequences for the [Kodaira vanishing theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodaira_vanishing_theory "Kodaira vanishing theory"), [representation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_theory "Representation theory"), and [spin manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_manifold "Spin manifold"). Bochner also worked on functions of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variable "Several complex variable"), resulting in the [Bochner–Martinelli formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochner–Martinelli_formula "Bochner–Martinelli formula") and the book *Several Complex Variables* (with [W. T. Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._T._Martin "W. T. Martin") in 1948).
