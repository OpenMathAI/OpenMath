**Harold Calvin Marston Morse** (March 24, 1892 – June 22, 1977) was an American [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") best known for his work on the *[calculus of variations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_of_variations "Calculus of variations") in the large*, a subject where he introduced the technique of [differential topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_topology "Differential topology") now known as [Morse theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_theory "Morse theory"). The [Morse–Palais lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse–Palais_lemma "Morse–Palais lemma"), one of the key results in Morse theory, is named after him, as is the [Thue–Morse sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thue–Morse_sequence "Thue–Morse sequence"), an infinite binary sequence with many applications.

He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1929, the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1932, and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1936. In 1933 he was awarded the [Bôcher Memorial Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bôcher_Memorial_Prize "Bôcher Memorial Prize") for his work in [mathematical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis"). [J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer "J. Robert Oppenheimer") described Morse as "almost a statesman of mathematics."

## Biography

Morse was born in [Waterville, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterville,_Maine "Waterville, Maine") to Ella Phoebe Marston and Howard Calvin Morse in 1892. He received his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") from [Colby College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colby_College "Colby College") (also in Waterville) in 1914. At [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), he received both his [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree "Master's degree") in 1915 and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in 1917. He wrote his PhD thesis, *Certain Types of Geodesic Motion of a Surface of Negative Curvature*, under the direction of [George David Birkhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_David_Birkhoff "George David Birkhoff").

Morse was married on June 20, 1922 to Celeste Phelps and they had two children, [Meroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meroë_Morse "Meroë Morse") and Dryden. The couple divorced. He later married Louise Jefferys on June 13, 1940. They had five children, Julia, William, Elizabeth, Peter, and Louise.

Morse was a Benjamin Peirce Instructor at Harvard in 1919–1920, after which he served as an assistant professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") from 1920 to 1925 and at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University "Brown University") in 1925–1926. He returned to Harvard in 1926, advancing to professor in 1929, and teaching there until 1935. That year, he accepted a position at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey "Princeton, New Jersey"), where he remained until his retirement in 1962.

Morse spent most of his career on a single subject, now known as Morse theory, a branch of [differential topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_topology "Differential topology") that enables one to analyze the [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_space "Topological space") of a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiable_manifold "Differentiable manifold") by studying [differentiable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiable_function "Differentiable function") on that manifold. Morse originally applied his theory to [geodesics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic "Geodesic") ([critical points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_point_(mathematics) "Critical point (mathematics)") of the [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_mechanics "Hamiltonian mechanics") [functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_(mathematics) "Functional (mathematics)") on [paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_(mathematics) "Path (mathematics)")); these techniques were used in [Raoul Bott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Bott "Raoul Bott")'s proof of his [periodicity theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_periodicity_theorem "Bott periodicity theorem"). Morse theory is a very important subject in modern [mathematical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physics "Mathematical physics"), such as [string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_theory "String theory").

Morse died on June 22, 1977, at his home in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey "Princeton, New Jersey"). His second wife, Louise Jeffreys, died in 2016.
