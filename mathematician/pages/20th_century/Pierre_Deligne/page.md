**Pierre René, Viscount Deligne** (French: [[dəliɲ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 3 October 1944) is a Belgian mathematician. He is best known for work on the [Weil conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_conjectures "Weil conjectures"), leading to a complete proof in 1973. He is the winner of the 1978 [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal"), 1988 [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize "Crafoord Prize"), 2008 [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize "Wolf Prize") and 2013 [Abel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Prize "Abel Prize").

## Early life and education

Deligne was born in [Etterbeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etterbeek "Etterbeek"), attended school at [Athénée Adolphe Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athénée_Adolphe_Max "Athénée Adolphe Max") and studied at the [Université libre de Bruxelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_libre_de_Bruxelles "Université libre de Bruxelles") (ULB), writing a dissertation titled *Théorème de Lefschetz et critères de dégénérescence de suites spectrales* (Theorem of Lefschetz and criteria of degeneration of spectral sequences). He completed his doctorate at the [University of Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris-Sud "University of Paris-Sud") in [Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orsay "Orsay") 1972 under the supervision of [Alexander Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Grothendieck "Alexander Grothendieck"), with a thesis titled *Théorie de Hodge*.

## Career

Starting in 1965, Deligne worked with Grothendieck at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques "Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques") (IHÉS) near Paris, initially on the generalization within [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory "Scheme theory") of [Zariski's main theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski's_main_theorem "Zariski's main theorem"). In 1968, he also worked with [Jean-Pierre Serre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Serre "Jean-Pierre Serre"); their work led to important results on the l-adic representations attached to [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_form "Modular form"), and the conjectural [functional equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_equation "Functional equation") of [L-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-function "L-function"). Deligne also focused on topics in [Hodge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_theory "Hodge theory"). He introduced the concept of weights and tested them on objects in [complex geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_geometry "Complex geometry"). He also collaborated with [David Mumford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Mumford "David Mumford") on a new description of the [moduli spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_space "Moduli space") for curves. Their work came to be seen as an introduction to one form of the theory of [algebraic stacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_stack "Algebraic stack"), and recently has been applied to questions arising from [string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_theory "String theory"). But Deligne's most famous contribution was his proof of the third and last of the [Weil conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_conjectures "Weil conjectures"). This proof completed a programme initiated and largely developed by [Alexander Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Grothendieck "Alexander Grothendieck") lasting for more than a decade. As a corollary he proved the celebrated [Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan–Petersson_conjecture "Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture") for [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_form "Modular form") of weight greater than one; weight one was proved in his work with Serre. Deligne's 1974 paper contains the first proof of the [Weil conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_conjectures "Weil conjectures"). Deligne's contribution was to supply the estimate of the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors "Eigenvalues and eigenvectors") of the [Frobenius endomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_endomorphism "Frobenius endomorphism"), considered the geometric analogue of the [Riemann hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_hypothesis "Riemann hypothesis"). It also led to a proof of the [Lefschetz hyperplane theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Lefschetz_theorem "Hard Lefschetz theorem") and the old and new estimates of the classical exponential sums, among other applications. Deligne's 1980 paper contains a much more general version of the Riemann hypothesis.

From 1970 until 1984, Deligne was a permanent member of the IHÉS staff. During this time he did much important work outside of his work on algebraic geometry. In joint work with [George Lusztig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lusztig "George Lusztig"), Deligne applied [étale cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_cohomology "Étale cohomology") to construct representations of [finite groups of Lie type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_Lie_type "Group of Lie type"); with [Michael Rapoport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Rapoport "Michael Rapoport"), Deligne worked on the moduli spaces from the 'fine' arithmetic point of view, with application to [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_form "Modular form"). He received a [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") in 1978. In 1984, Deligne moved to the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") in Princeton.

### Hodge cycles

In terms of the completion of some of the underlying Grothendieck program of research, he defined [absolute Hodge cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_cycle "Hodge cycle"), as a surrogate for the missing and still largely conjectural theory of [motives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motive_(mathematics) "Motive (mathematics)"). This idea allows one to get around the lack of knowledge of the [Hodge conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_conjecture "Hodge conjecture"), for some applications. The theory of [mixed Hodge structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_structure "Hodge structure"), a powerful tool in algebraic geometry that generalizes classical Hodge theory, was created by applying weight filtration, Hironaka's [resolution of singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolution_of_singularities "Resolution of singularities") and other methods, which he then used to prove the Weil conjectures. He reworked the [Tannakian category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannakian_category "Tannakian category") theory in his 1990 paper for the "Grothendieck Festschrift", employing [Beck's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck's_monadicity_theorem "Beck's monadicity theorem") – the Tannakian category concept being the categorical expression of the linearity of the theory of motives as the ultimate [Weil cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_cohomology_theory "Weil cohomology theory"). All this is part of the *yoga of weights*, uniting [Hodge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_theory "Hodge theory") and the l-adic [Galois representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module "Galois module"). The [Shimura variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimura_variety "Shimura variety") theory is related, by the idea that such varieties should parametrize not just good (arithmetically interesting) families of Hodge structures, but actual motives. This theory is not yet a finished product, and more recent trends have used [K-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-theory "K-theory") approaches.

### Perverse sheaves

With [Alexander Beilinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Beilinson "Alexander Beilinson"), [Joseph Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bernstein "Joseph Bernstein"), and [Ofer Gabber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofer_Gabber "Ofer Gabber"), Deligne made definitive contributions to the theory of [perverse sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perverse_sheaves "Perverse sheaves"). This theory plays an important role in the recent proof of the [fundamental lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_lemma_(Langlands_program) "Fundamental lemma (Langlands program)") by [Ngô Bảo Châu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngô_Bảo_Châu "Ngô Bảo Châu"). It was also used by Deligne himself to greatly clarify the nature of the [Riemann–Hilbert correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann–Hilbert_correspondence "Riemann–Hilbert correspondence"), which extends [Hilbert's twenty-first problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert's_twenty-first_problem "Hilbert's twenty-first problem") to higher dimensions. Prior to Deligne's paper, [Zoghman Mebkhout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoghman_Mebkhout "Zoghman Mebkhout")'s 1980 thesis and the work of [Masaki Kashiwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaki_Kashiwara "Masaki Kashiwara") through [D-modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-modules "D-modules") theory (but published in the 80s) on the problem have appeared.

### Other works

In 1974 at the IHÉS, Deligne's joint paper with [Phillip Griffiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Griffiths "Phillip Griffiths"), [John Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Morgan_(mathematician) "John Morgan (mathematician)") and [Dennis Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Sullivan "Dennis Sullivan") on the real [homotopy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_theory "Homotopy theory") of compact [Kähler manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_manifolds "Kähler manifolds") was a major piece of work in complex differential geometry which settled several important questions of both classical and modern significance. The input from Weil conjectures, Hodge theory, variations of Hodge structures, and many geometric and topological tools were critical to its investigations. His work in complex [singularity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singularity_theory "Singularity theory") generalized [Milnor maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milnor_fibration "Milnor fibration") into an algebraic setting and extended the [Picard-Lefschetz formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard-Lefschetz_formula "Picard-Lefschetz formula") beyond their general format, generating a new method of research in this subject. His paper with [Ken Ribet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Ribet "Ken Ribet") on abelian L-functions and their extensions to [Hilbert modular surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_modular_surface "Hilbert modular surface") and p-adic L-functions form an important part of his work in [arithmetic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_geometry "Arithmetic geometry"). Other important research achievements of Deligne include the notion of cohomological descent, motivic L-functions, mixed sheaves, nearby [vanishing cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanishing_cycle "Vanishing cycle"), central extensions of [reductive groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_group "Reductive group"), geometry and topology of [braid groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braid_group "Braid group"), providing the modern axiomatic definition of Shimura varieties, the work in collaboration with [George Mostow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mostow "George Mostow") on the examples of non-arithmetic lattices and monodromy of [hypergeometric differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_equation "Hypergeometric equation") in two- and three-dimensional complex [hyperbolic spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_space "Hyperbolic space"), etc.

## Awards

He was awarded the [Fields Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medal "Fields Medal") in 1978, the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize "Crafoord Prize") in 1988, the [Balzan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balzan_Prize "Balzan Prize") in 2004, the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize "Wolf Prize") in 2008, and the [Abel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Prize "Abel Prize") in 2013, "for seminal contributions to algebraic geometry and for their transformative impact
on number theory, representation theory, and related fields". He was elected a foreign member of the Academie des Sciences de Paris in 1978.

In 2006 he was ennobled by the Belgian king as [viscount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_nobility#Viscount "Belgian nobility").

In 2009, Deligne was elected a foreign member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences") and a residential member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"). He is a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters "Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters").

Additionally, many different conjectures in mathematics have been called the **Deligne conjecture**:

- *[Deligne's conjecture on Hochschild cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deligne's_conjecture_on_Hochschild_cohomology "Deligne's conjecture on Hochschild cohomology")*.
- The *[Deligne conjecture on special values of L-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deligne_conjecture_on_special_values_of_L-functions "Deligne conjecture on special values of L-functions")* is a formulation of the hope for [algebraicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_number "Algebraic number") of *L*(*n*) where *L* is an [L-function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-function "L-function") and *n* is an integer in some set depending on *L*.
- There is a *Deligne conjecture on 1-motives* arising in the theory of [motives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motive_(algebraic_geometry) "Motive (algebraic geometry)") in [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry").
- There is a *Gross–Deligne conjecture* in the theory of [complex multiplication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_multiplication "Complex multiplication").
- There is a *Deligne conjecture on [monodromy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monodromy "Monodromy")*, also known as the *weight monodromy conjecture*, or purity conjecture for the monodromy filtration.
- There is a *[Deligne conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E7½_(Lie_algebra) "E7½ (Lie algebra)")* in the [representation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_theory "Representation theory") of [exceptional Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exceptional_Lie_group "Exceptional Lie group").
- There is a conjecture named the Deligne–Grothendieck conjecture for the discrete [Riemann–Roch theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann–Roch_theorem "Riemann–Roch theorem") in [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_(algebra) "Characteristic (algebra)") 0.
- There is a conjecture named the Deligne–Milnor conjecture for the differential interpretation of a formula of Milnor for Milnor fibres, as part of the extension of nearby cycles and their Euler numbers.
- The Deligne–Milne conjecture is formulated as part of motives and Tannakian categories.
- There is a *Deligne–Langlands conjecture* of historical importance in relation with the development of the [Langlands philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_philosophy "Langlands philosophy").
- *Deligne's conjecture on the Lefschetz trace formula* (now called Fujiwara's theorem for equivariant correspondences).
