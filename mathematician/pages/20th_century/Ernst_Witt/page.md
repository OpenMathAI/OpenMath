**Ernst Witt** (26 June 1911 – 3 July 1991) was a German [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"), one of the leading [algebraists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra "Algebra") of his time.

## Biography

Witt was born on the island of [Alsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Als_(island) "Als (island)"), then a part of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire"). Shortly after his birth, his parents moved the family to China to work as missionaries, and he did not return to Europe until he was nine.

After his schooling, Witt went to the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg "University of Freiburg") and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"). He joined the [NSDAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party "Nazi Party") (Nazi Party) and was an active party member. Witt was awarded a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") at the University of Göttingen in 1933 with a thesis titled: "Riemann-Roch theorem and zeta-Function in hypercomplexes" (Riemann-Rochscher Satz und Zeta-Funktion im Hyperkomplexen) that was supervised by [Gustav Herglotz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Herglotz "Gustav Herglotz"), with [Emmy Noether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Noether "Emmy Noether") suggesting the topic for the doctorate. He became a qualified lecturer and gave guest lectures in Göttingen and [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg "University of Hamburg"). He was also associated with the team led by [Helmut Hasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Hasse "Helmut Hasse") who led his habilitation. In June 1936, he gave his habilitation lecture.

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") he joined a group of five mathematicians, recruited by [Wilhelm Fenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Fenner "Wilhelm Fenner"), and which included [Georg Aumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Aumann "Georg Aumann"), [Alexander Aigner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Aigner "Alexander Aigner"), [Oswald Teichmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Teichmüller "Oswald Teichmüller"), Johann Friedrich Schultze and their leader professor [Wolfgang Franz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Franz_(mathematician) "Wolfgang Franz (mathematician)"), to form the backbone of the new mathematical research department in the late 1930s that would eventually be called: Section IVc of [Cipher Department of the High Command of the Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cipher_Department_of_the_High_Command_of_the_Wehrmacht "Cipher Department of the High Command of the Wehrmacht") (abbr. OKW/Chi).

From 1937 until 1979, he taught at the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg "University of Hamburg"). He died in Hamburg in 1991, shortly after his 80th birthday.

## Work

Witt's work has been highly influential. His invention of the [Witt vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witt_vector "Witt vector") clarifies and generalizes the structure of the [*p*-adic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_number "P-adic number"). It has become fundamental to [*p*-adic Hodge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_Hodge_theory "P-adic Hodge theory").

Witt was the founder of the theory of [quadratic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_form "Quadratic form") over an arbitrary [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_(mathematics) "Field (mathematics)"). He [proved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_proof "Mathematical proof") several of the key results, including the [Witt cancellation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witt's_theorem "Witt's theorem"). He defined the [Witt ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witt_group#Ring_structure "Witt group") of all quadratic forms over a field, now a central object in the theory.

The [Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt_theorem "Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem") is basic to the study of [Lie algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebra "Lie algebra"). In [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry"), the [Hasse–Witt matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasse–Witt_matrix "Hasse–Witt matrix") of an [algebraic curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_curve "Algebraic curve") over a [finite field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_field "Finite field") determines the cyclic [étale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_morphism "Étale morphism") coverings of degree *p* of a curve in [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_(algebra) "Characteristic (algebra)") *p*.

In the 1970s, Witt claimed that in 1940 he had discovered what would eventually be named the "[Leech lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leech_lattice "Leech lattice")" many years before [John Leech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Leech_(mathematician) "John Leech (mathematician)") discovered it in 1965, but Witt did not publish his discovery and the details of exactly what he did are unclear.
