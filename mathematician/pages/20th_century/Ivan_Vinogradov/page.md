**Ivan Matveevich Vinogradov** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") (Russian: Ива́н Матве́евич Виногра́дов, IPA: [[ɪˈvan mɐtˈvʲejɪvʲɪtɕ vʲɪnɐˈɡradəf]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Russian "Help:IPA/Russian") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ru-Ivan_Matveyevich_Vinogradov.ogg "File:Ru-Ivan Matveyevich Vinogradov.ogg"); 14 September 1891 – 20 March 1983) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR "USSR") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"), who was one of the creators of modern [analytic number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_number_theory "Analytic number theory"), and also a dominant figure in mathematics in the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"). He was born in the [Velikiye Luki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velikiye_Luki "Velikiye Luki") district, [Pskov Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pskov_Oblast "Pskov Oblast"). He graduated from the [University of St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University "Saint Petersburg State University"), where in 1920 he became a Professor. From 1934 he was a Director of the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Institute_of_Mathematics "Steklov Institute of Mathematics"), a position he held for the rest of his life, except for the five-year period (1941–1946) when the institute was directed by Academician [Sergei Sobolev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Sobolev "Sergei Sobolev"). In 1941 he was awarded the [Stalin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize "USSR State Prize"). He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1942. In 1951 he became a foreign member of the Polish Academy of Sciences and Letters in Kraków.

## Mathematical contributions

In [analytic number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_number_theory "Analytic number theory"), *Vinogradov's method* refers to his main problem-solving technique, applied to central questions involving the estimation of [exponential sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_sum "Exponential sum"). In its most basic form, it is used to estimate sums over prime numbers, or [Weyl sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_sum "Weyl sum"). With the help of this method, Vinogradov tackled questions such as the [ternary Goldbach problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternary_Goldbach_problem "Ternary Goldbach problem") in 1937 (using [Vinogradov's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinogradov's_theorem "Vinogradov's theorem")), and the zero-free region for the [Riemann zeta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_zeta_function "Riemann zeta function"). His own use of it was inimitable; in terms of later techniques, it is recognised as a prototype of the [large sieve method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_sieve_method "Large sieve method") in its application of [bilinear forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilinear_form "Bilinear form"), and also as an exploitation of combinatorial structure. In some cases his results resisted improvement for decades.

He also used this technique on the [Dirichlet divisor problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor_summatory_function#Dirichlet's_divisor_problem "Divisor summatory function"), allowing him to estimate the number of integer points under an arbitrary [curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curve "Curve"). This was an improvement on the work of [Georgy Voronoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgy_Voronoy "Georgy Voronoy").

In 1918 Vinogradov proved the [Pólya–Vinogradov inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_residue#The_Pólya–Vinogradov_inequality "Quadratic residue") for character sums.

## Personality and career

Vinogradov served as director of the [Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Institute_of_Mathematics "Steklov Institute of Mathematics") for 49 years. For his long service he was twice awarded the order of The [Hero of the Socialist Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_the_Socialist_Labour "Hero of the Socialist Labour"). The house where he was born was converted into his memorial – a unique honour among Russian [mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"). As the head of a leading mathematical institute, Vinogradov enjoyed significant influence in the [Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") and was regarded as an informal leader of [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") [mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"), not always in a positive way: his anti-Semitic feelings led him to hinder the careers of many prominent Soviet mathematicians.

Although he was always faithful to the official line, he was never a member of the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_USSR "Communist Party of the USSR") and his overall mindset was [nationalistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism "Nationalism") rather than [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism"). This can at least partly be attributed to his origins: his father was a priest of the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church "Russian Orthodox Church"). Vinogradov was enormously strong: in some recollections it is stated that he could lift a chair with a person sitting on it by holding the leg of the chair in his hands. He was never married and was very attached to his [dacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacha "Dacha") in [Abramtsevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abramtsevo "Abramtsevo"), where he spent all his weekends and vacations (together with his sister Nadezhda, also unmarried) enjoying flower [gardening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardening "Gardening"). He had friendly relations with the president of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") [Mstislav Keldysh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mstislav_Keldysh "Mstislav Keldysh") and [Mikhail Lavrentyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Lavrentyev "Mikhail Lavrentyev"), both mathematicians whose careers started in his institute.
