**Wolfgang Krull** (26 August 1899 – 12 April 1971) was a German [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who made fundamental contributions to [commutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra "Commutative algebra"), introducing concepts that are now central to the subject.

Krull was born and went to school in [Baden-Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-Baden "Baden-Baden"). He attended the Universities of [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg "University of Freiburg"), [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rostock "University of Rostock") and finally [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen") from 1919–1921, where he earned his doctorate under [Alfred Loewy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Loewy "Alfred Loewy"). He worked as an instructor and professor at Freiburg, then spent a decade at the [University of Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen "University of Erlangen"). In 1939, Krull moved to become chair at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn "University of Bonn"), where he remained for the rest of his life. Wolfgang Krull was a member of the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party "Nazi Party").

His 35 doctoral students include [Wilfried Brauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfried_Brauer "Wilfried Brauer"), [Karl-Otto Stöhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Otto_Stöhr "Karl-Otto Stöhr") and [Jürgen Neukirch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jürgen_Neukirch "Jürgen Neukirch").
