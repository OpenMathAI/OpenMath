Wang Xiaotong

**Wang Xiaotong** (王孝通) (AD 580–640), also known as Wang Hs'iao-t'ung, was a Chinese mathematician, calendarist, politician, and writer of the early Tang dynasty. He is famous as the author of the *[Jigu Suanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jigu_Suanjing "Jigu Suanjing")* (*Continuation of Ancient Mathematics*) one of the *[Ten Computational Canons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Computational_Canons "Ten Computational Canons")*.

He presented this work to [Li Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Gaozu_of_Tang "Emperor Gaozu of Tang"), the first emperor of the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty "Tang dynasty"), along with a brief biography.

According to this autobiography, he became interested in mathematics at a young age. After a study of the *[Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Chapters_on_the_Mathematical_Art "Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art")* and particularly [Liu Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Hui "Liu Hui")'s commentary on it, Wang became a teacher of mathematics, and later deputy director of the Astronomical Bureau.

It was known that the [Chinese calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_calendar "Chinese calendar") at that time was in need of reform since, although only in operation for a few years, already predictions of [eclipses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse "Eclipse") were getting out of step. In 623, together with [Zu Xiaosun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zu_Xiaosun "Zu Xiaosun"), a Civil Servant, he was assigned to report on problems with the calendar—although only recently adopted, it was already out of step with the eclipses. In fact, Wang did not approach this in a sophisticated way; he proposed to ignore the irregularity of the sun's motion and also the precession of the [equinoxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equinox "Equinox")—both had already been incorporated in calendar calculations by [Zu Chongzhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zu_Chongzhi "Zu Chongzhi") in the fifth century.

## Jigu Suanjing

His major contribution was the *Jigu suanjing* ("Continuation of Ancient Mathematics" 缉古算经), written before year 626. Jigu Suanjing became a text for the [imperial examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_examination "Imperial examination"); it was included as one of the *Ten Computational* Canons when reprinted in 1084.

The book contains 20 problems based mostly on engineering construction of astronomic observation tower, dike, excavation of a canal bed etc. and [right angled triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_angled_triangle "Right angled triangle"), but which in essence deal with the solution of [cubic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_equation "Cubic equation"), the first known Chinese work to deal with them.
