**Erich Hecke** (German: [[ˈhɛkə]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German"); 20 September 1887 – 13 February 1947) was a German [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") known for his work in [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory") and the theory of [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_form "Modular form").

## Biography

Hecke was born in [Buk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buk,_Greater_Poland_Voivodeship "Buk, Greater Poland Voivodeship"), [Province of Posen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Posen "Province of Posen"), [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire") (now [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań "Poznań"), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland")). He obtained his doctorate in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_August_University_of_Göttingen "Georg August University of Göttingen") under the supervision of [David Hilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hilbert "David Hilbert").

[Kurt Reidemeister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Reidemeister "Kurt Reidemeister") and [Heinrich Behnke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Behnke "Heinrich Behnke") were among his students.

In 1933 Hecke signed the *[Loyalty Oath of German Professors to Adolf Hitler and the National Socialist State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vow_of_allegiance_of_the_Professors_of_the_German_Universities_and_High-Schools_to_Adolf_Hitler_and_the_National_Socialistic_State "Vow of allegiance of the Professors of the German Universities and High-Schools to Adolf Hitler and the National Socialistic State")*, but was later known as being opposed to the Nazis.

Hecke died in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen "Copenhagen"), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark "Denmark").

[André Weil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Weil "André Weil"), in the foreword to his text *[Basic Number Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Number_Theory "Basic Number Theory")* says: "To improve upon Hecke, in a treatment along classical lines of the theory of algebraic numbers, would be a futile and impossible task", referring to Hecke's book *Lectures on the Theory of Algebraic Numbers*.

## Research

His early work included establishing the [functional equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_equation_(L-function) "Functional equation (L-function)") for the [Dedekind zeta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind_zeta_function "Dedekind zeta function"), with a proof based on [theta functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theta_function "Theta function"). The method extended to the [L-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-function "L-function") associated to a class of characters now known as [Hecke characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecke_character "Hecke character") or [idele class characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idele_class_character "Idele class character"); such L-functions are now known as Hecke L-functions. He devoted most of his research to the theory of [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_form "Modular form"), creating the general theory of [cusp forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cusp_form "Cusp form") ([holomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_function "Holomorphic function"), for GL(2)), as it is now understood in the classical setting.

## Recognition

He was a Plenary Speaker of the [ICM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") in 1936 in Oslo.
