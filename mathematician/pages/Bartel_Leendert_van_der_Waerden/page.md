**Bartel Leendert van der Waerden** (Dutch: [[ˈbɑrtə(l) ˈleːndərt fɑn dər ˈʋaːrdə(n)]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Dutch "Help:IPA/Dutch"); 2 February 1903 – 12 January 1996) was a [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people "Dutch people") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") and [historian of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_mathematics "History of mathematics").

## Biography

### Education and early career

Van der Waerden learned advanced mathematics at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam "University of Amsterdam") and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"), from 1919 until 1926. He was much influenced by [Emmy Noether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Noether "Emmy Noether") at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen "Göttingen"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany"). Amsterdam awarded him a Ph.D. for a thesis on [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry"), supervised by Hendrick de Vries. Göttingen awarded him the [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation "Habilitation") in 1928. In that year, at the age of 25, he accepted a professorship at the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen "University of Groningen").

At 27, Van der Waerden published his *[Moderne Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderne_Algebra "Moderne Algebra")*, an influential two-volume treatise on [abstract algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_algebra "Abstract algebra"), still cited, and perhaps the first treatise to treat the subject as a comprehensive whole. This work systematized an ample body of research by [Emmy Noether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Noether "Emmy Noether"), [David Hilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hilbert "David Hilbert"), [Richard Dedekind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dedekind "Richard Dedekind"), and [Emil Artin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Artin "Emil Artin"). In the following year, 1931, he was appointed professor at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig "University of Leipzig").

In July 1929, he married Camilla Juliana Anna Rellich, the sister of mathematician [Franz Rellich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Rellich "Franz Rellich"). They had three children.

### Nazi Germany

After the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") [seized power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung "Machtergreifung"), and through [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Van der Waerden remained at Leipzig, and passed up opportunities to leave Nazi Germany for [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") and [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University "Utrecht University"). However, he was critical of the Nazis and refused to give up his Dutch nationality, both of which led to difficulties for him.

### Postwar career

Following the war, Van der Waerden was repatriated to the Netherlands rather than returning to [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig "Leipzig") (then under Soviet control), but struggled to find a position in the Dutch academic system, in part because his time in Germany made his politics suspect and in part due to [Brouwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luitzen_Egbertus_Jan_Brouwer "Luitzen Egbertus Jan Brouwer")'s opposition to Hilbert's school of mathematics. After a year visiting [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University") and two years as a part-time professor, in 1950, Van der Waerden filled the chair in mathematics at the University of Amsterdam. In 1951, he moved to the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich"), where he spent the rest of his career, supervising more than 40 [Ph.D. students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy").

In 1949, Van der Waerden became member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences"), in 1951 this was changed to a foreign membership. In 1973 he received the *[Pour le Mérite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pour_le_Mérite "Pour le Mérite")*.

## Contributions

Van der Waerden is mainly remembered for his work on [abstract algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_algebra "Abstract algebra"). He also wrote on [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry"), [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology "Topology"), [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory"), [geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry "Geometry"), [combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorics "Combinatorics"), [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis"), [probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability "Probability") and [statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics "Statistics"), and [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") (he and [Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg "Heisenberg") had been colleagues at Leipzig). In later years, he turned to the [history of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_mathematics "History of mathematics") and [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science "History of science"). His historical writings include *Ontwakende wetenschap* (1950), which was translated into English as *Science Awakening* (1954), *Sources of Quantum Mechanics* (1967), *Geometry and Algebra in Ancient Civilizations* (1983), and *A History of Algebra* (1985).

Van der Waerden has over 1000 [academic descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_genealogy "Academic genealogy"), most of them through three of his students, [David van Dantzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_van_Dantzig "David van Dantzig") (Ph.D. Groningen 1931), [Herbert Seifert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Seifert "Herbert Seifert") (Ph.D. Leipzig 1932), and Hans Richter (Ph.D. Leipzig 1936, co-advised by [Paul Koebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Koebe "Paul Koebe")).
