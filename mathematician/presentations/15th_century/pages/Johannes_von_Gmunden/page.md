**Johannes von Gmunden** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language "Latin language"): *Johannes de Gamundia*; c. 1380/84 – February 23, 1442) was a German [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer "Astronomer"), [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") and [humanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanist "Humanist").

## Biography

Johannes von Gmunden received the degree of a Master of Arts at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna "University of Vienna") in 1406. From 1408, he was a lecturer at Vienna, lecturing on [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle")'s *[Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_(Aristotle) "Physics (Aristotle)")* (1408) and *[Meteora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorology_(Aristotle) "Meteorology (Aristotle)")* (1409, 1411), [Peter of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_of_Spain_(author) "Peter of Spain (author)") (1410) and *Algorismus de minutiis* (1412). He fell seriously ill in 1412.

In 1415 and 1416 studied theology, completing a [Bachelor of Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Theology "Bachelor of Theology") in 1416. He continued lecturing only in 1419, on *algorismus de integris*. From 1420, Johannes was permitted to restrict his teaching to the specialized field of the mathematics of astronomy, focusing on [Euclid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid "Euclid")'s *[Elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_Elements "Euclid's Elements")* and the *Sphaera materialis* of [John Holywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Holywood "John Holywood"). With the aid of students (Weidler's 1741 *Historia astronomiae* names [Georg Pruneck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Pruneck?action=edit&redlink=1 "Georg Pruneck") of Ruspach, Georg of Neuenburg, Johannes Schinkel and Johannes Feldner) he compiled voluminous [astronomical tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_tables "Astronomical tables"). In 1425, he was elected [canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_(priest) "Canon (priest)") at [St. Stephen's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Stephen's_Cathedral,_Vienna "St. Stephen's Cathedral, Vienna"). [Georg von Peuerbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_von_Peuerbach "Georg von Peuerbach") succeeded him at Vienna University in 1450.

John's origins are somewhat disputed. He was probably born in [Gmunden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmunden "Gmunden"), [Upper Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Austria "Upper Austria"), but there were also suggestions connecting him with [Gmünd, Lower Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmünd,_Lower_Austria "Gmünd, Lower Austria"), or that he was a Swabian from [Schwäbisch Gmünd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwäbisch_Gmünd "Schwäbisch Gmünd") who studied in [Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm "Ulm") in his youth, based on a document written at Ulm in 1404 by one "Johannes Wissbier de Gamundia".
