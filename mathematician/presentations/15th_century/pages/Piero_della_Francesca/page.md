**Piero della Francesca** ([/ˌpjɛəroʊ ˌdɛlə frænˈtʃɛskə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*PYAIR-oh DEL-ə fran-CHESK-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"), [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English "American English") also [/- frɑːnˈ-/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*-⁠ frahn-*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); Italian: [[ˈpjɛːro della franˈtʃeska]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian "Help:IPA/Italian") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:It-Piero_della_Francesca.oga "File:It-Piero della Francesca.oga"); [né](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") **Piero di Benedetto de' Franceschi**; c. 1415 – 12 October 1492) was an [Italian painter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance_painter "Italian Renaissance painter"), [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") and [geometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_geometers "List of geometers") of the [Early Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Renaissance "Early Renaissance"), nowadays chiefly appreciated for his art. His painting is characterized by its serene [humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism "Humanism"), its use of geometric forms and [perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspective_(graphical) "Perspective (graphical)"). His most famous work is the cycle of frescoes *[The History of the True Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_History_of_the_True_Cross "The History of the True Cross")* in the [Basilica of San Francesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Francesco,_Arezzo "Basilica of San Francesco, Arezzo") in the [Tuscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscany "Tuscany") town of [Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arezzo "Arezzo").

## Biography

### Early years

Piero was born Piero di Benedetto in the town of [Borgo Santo Sepolcro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansepolcro "Sansepolcro"), modern-day [Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscany "Tuscany"), to Benedetto de' Franceschi, a tradesman, and Romana di Perino da [Monterchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterchi "Monterchi"), members of the Florentine and Tuscan Franceschi noble family. His father died before his birth, and he was called Piero della Francesca after his mother, who was referred to as "la Francesca" due to her marriage into the Franceschi family (similar to [Lisa Gherardini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_del_Giocondo "Lisa del Giocondo") who was known as "la Gioconda" through her marriage into the Giocondo family). Romana supported his education in mathematics and art.

He was most probably apprenticed to the local painter Antonio di Giovanni d'Anghiari, because in documents about payments it is noted that he was working with Antonio in 1432 and May 1438. He certainly took notice of the work of some of the [Sienese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena "Siena") artists active in San Sepolcro during his youth; e.g. [Sassetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassetta "Sassetta"). In 1439 Piero received, together with [Domenico Veneziano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_Veneziano "Domenico Veneziano"), payments for his work on [frescoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresco "Fresco") for the church of Sant'Egidio in Florence, now lost. In Florence he must have met leading masters like [Fra Angelico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fra_Angelico "Fra Angelico"), [Luca della Robbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_della_Robbia "Luca della Robbia"), [Donatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donatello "Donatello"), and [Brunelleschi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Brunelleschi "Filippo Brunelleschi"). The classicism of [Masaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaccio "Masaccio")'s frescoes and his majestic figures in the [Santa Maria del Carmine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_del_Carmine,_Florence "Santa Maria del Carmine, Florence") were for him an important source of inspiration. Dating of Piero's undocumented work is difficult because his style does not seem to have developed over the years.

### Mature work

Piero returned to his hometown in 1442 and was elected to the City Council of Sansepolcro. Three years later, he received his first commission, to paint the [*Madonna della Misericordia* altarpiece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altarpiece_of_the_church_of_the_Misericordia "Altarpiece of the church of the Misericordia") for the church of the Misericordia in Sansepolcro, which was completed in the early 1460s. In 1449 he executed several frescoes in the [Castello Estense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castello_Estense "Castello Estense") and the church of Sant'Andrea of [Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara "Ferrara"), now also lost. His influence was particularly strong in the later Ferrarese [allegorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegory "Allegory") works of [Cosimo Tura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosimo_Tura "Cosimo Tura").

The *Baptism of Christ*, now in the [National Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery "National Gallery") in London, was completed in about 1450 for the high altar of the church of the Priory of S. Giovanni Battista at Sansepolcro. Other notable works are the frescoes of *[The Resurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Resurrection_(Piero_della_Francesca) "The Resurrection (Piero della Francesca)")* in Sansepolcro, and the *[Madonna del parto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_del_parto "Madonna del parto")* in [Monterchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterchi "Monterchi"), near Sansepolcro.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/Piero_della_Francesca_Malatesta.jpg/250px-Piero_della_Francesca_Malatesta.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Piero_della_Francesca_Malatesta.jpg)

*[Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismondo_Pandolfo_Malatesta "Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta") kneeling before [St. Sigismund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Sigismund "St. Sigismund")* (1451)

Two years later he was in [Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimini "Rimini"), working for the [condottiero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condottieri "Condottieri") [Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismondo_Pandolfo_Malatesta "Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta"). In 1451, during that sojourn, he executed the famous fresco of *St. Sigismund and Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta* in the [Tempio Malatestiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Malatestiano "Tempio Malatestiano"), as well as a [portrait of Sigismondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_of_Sigismondo_Pandolfo_Malatesta "Portrait of Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta"). In Rimini, Piero may have met the famous [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance") mathematician and architect [Leon Battista Alberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Battista_Alberti "Leon Battista Alberti"), who had redesigned the [Tempio Malatestiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempio_Malatestiano "Tempio Malatestiano"), although it is known that Alberti directed the execution of his designs for the church by correspondence with his building supervisor. Thereafter Piero was active in [Ancona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancona "Ancona"), [Pesaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesaro "Pesaro") and [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna "Bologna").

In 1454, he signed a contract for the *Polyptych of Saint Augustine* in the church of Sant'Agostino in Sansepolcro. The central panel of this [polyptych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyptych "Polyptych") is lost, and the four panels of the wings, with representations of [saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint "Saint"), are now scattered around the world. A few years later, summoned by [Pope Nicholas V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_V "Pope Nicholas V"), he moved to Rome, where he executed frescoes in the [Basilica di Santa Maria Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_di_Santa_Maria_Maggiore "Basilica di Santa Maria Maggiore"), of which only fragments remain. Two years later he was again in the Papal capital, painting frescoes in the [Vatican Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Palace "Apostolic Palace"), which have since been destroyed.

### Frescoes in San Francesco at Arezzo

In 1452, Piero della Francesca was called to [Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arezzo "Arezzo") to replace [Bicci di Lorenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicci_di_Lorenzo "Bicci di Lorenzo") in painting the frescoes of the basilica of [San Francesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Francesco,_Arezzo "Basilica of San Francesco, Arezzo"). The work was finished in 1464.
*[The History of the True Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_History_of_the_True_Cross "The History of the True Cross")* cycle of frescoes is generally considered among his masterworks and those of [Renaissance painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_painting "Renaissance painting") in general. The story in these frescoes derives from legendary medieval sources as to how timber relics of the [True Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Cross "True Cross") came to be found. These stories were collected in the *[Golden Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Legend "Golden Legend")* of [Jacopo da Varazze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_da_Varazze "Jacopo da Varazze") (Jacopo da Varagine) of the mid-13th century.

### Piero's activity in Urbino

At some point, [Giovanni Santi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Santi "Giovanni Santi") invited Piero to [Urbino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbino "Urbino"), where Piero "executed several commissions for Duke [Federico da Montefeltro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_da_Montefeltro "Federico da Montefeltro")." *[The Flagellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flagellation_(Piero_della_Francesca) "The Flagellation (Piero della Francesca)")* is generally considered Piero's oldest work in Urbino (c. 1455–1470). It is one of the most famous and controversial pictures of the early Renaissance. As discussed in its own entry, it is marked by an air of geometric sobriety, in addition to presenting a perplexing enigma as to the nature of the three men standing at the foreground.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Piero_della_Francesca_046.jpg/250px-Piero_della_Francesca_046.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Piero_della_Francesca_046.jpg)

The [Montefeltro Altarpiece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montefeltro_Altarpiece "Montefeltro Altarpiece") or the *Brera Madonna*

Another famous work painted in Urbino is the *Double Portrait* of Federico and his wife Battista Sforza, in the [Uffizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uffizi "Uffizi"). The portraits in profile take their inspiration from large bronze medals and stucco roundels with the official portraits of Federico and his wife. Other paintings made in Urbino are the monumental [Montefeltro Altarpiece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brera_Madonna "Brera Madonna") (1474) in the [Brera Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brera_Gallery "Brera Gallery") in Milan and the *[Madonna of Senigallia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_of_Senigallia "Madonna of Senigallia")*.

In Urbino Piero met the painters [Melozzo da Forlì](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melozzo_da_Forlì "Melozzo da Forlì"), [Fra Carnevale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fra_Carnevale "Fra Carnevale"), and the Flemish [Justus van Gent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justus_van_Gent "Justus van Gent"), the mathematician Fra [Luca Pacioli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Pacioli "Luca Pacioli"), the architect [Francesco di Giorgio Martini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_di_Giorgio_Martini "Francesco di Giorgio Martini"), and probably also [Leon Battista Alberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Battista_Alberti "Leon Battista Alberti").

### Later years

In his later years, painters such as [Perugino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perugino "Perugino") and [Luca Signorelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Signorelli "Luca Signorelli") frequently visited his workshop. He completed the treatise *[On Perspective in Painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Prospectiva_pingendi "De Prospectiva pingendi")* in the mid-1470s to 1480s. By 1480, his vision began to deteriorate, but he continued writing treatises such as *Short Book on the Five Regular Solids* in 1485. It is documented that Piero rented a house in Rimini in 1482. Piero made his will in 1487 and he died five years later, on 12 October 1492, in his own house in Sansepolcro. He left his possessions to his family and the Church.

## Criticism and interpretation

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Piero%2C_madonna_col_bambino_e_4_angeli%2C_clark_art_institute.jpg/250px-Piero%2C_madonna_col_bambino_e_4_angeli%2C_clark_art_institute.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Piero,_madonna_col_bambino_e_4_angeli,_clark_art_institute.jpg)

*Virgin and Child Enthroned With Four Angels* by Piero della Francesca, [Clark Art Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Art_Institute "Clark Art Institute")

In a 2013 exhibition, the [Frick Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frick_Collection "Frick Collection") in New York collected seven of the eight paintings of Piero known to exist in the United States. Of the seven paintings in the exhibit, critic [Jerry Saltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Saltz "Jerry Saltz") writing in *New York* magazine singled out Piero's *Virgin and Child Enthroned With Four Angels* for its exemplary qualities.

Saltz wrote, "The Virgin and child are elevated two steps. They are in a world itself apart from this world apart. Mary isn't looking at her child and looks instead at the rose he reaches for. You begin to glean the revelation she is having. The flower represents love, devotion, and beauty. It also symbolizes blood and the crown of thorns Christ will wear. This child who will suffer a horrendous death reaches for his acceptance of fate. Mary does not pull the flower back. You sense an inner agony, noticing her deep-blue robe open to reveal scarlet beneath, symbol of outward passion and pain to come. In the dead-center vertical line of the painting is Christ's right palm that will be nailed to the cross."

By contrast, Walter Kaiser, reviewing the exhibition in *The New York Review of Books*, wrote, "The most splendid picture in the Frick exhibition is the magnificent figure of [Saint Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Augustine_Altarpiece_(Piero_della_Francesca) "Saint Augustine Altarpiece (Piero della Francesca)") from the [Museu Nacional de Arte Antiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Ancient_Art "National Museum of Ancient Art") in Lisbon, a companion to Saint John the Evangelist [owned by the Frick Collection] on the Sant'Agostino altarpiece".

## Work in mathematics and geometry

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Piero_della_Francesca_-_Trattato_d%27Abaco_%281%29.jpg/250px-Piero_della_Francesca_-_Trattato_d%27Abaco_%281%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Piero_della_Francesca_-_Trattato_d'Abaco_(1).jpg)

First page of the *Trattato d'Abaco*

Piero's deep interest in the theoretical study of perspective and his contemplative approach to his paintings are apparent in all his work.
In his youth, Piero was trained in mathematics, which most likely was for mercantilism. Three treatises written by Piero have survived to the present day: *[Trattato d'Abaco (Abacus Treatise)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trattato_d'Abaco_(Abacus_Treatise)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Trattato d'Abaco (Abacus Treatise)")*, *[De quinque corporibus regularibus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_quinque_corporibus_regularibus "De quinque corporibus regularibus")* (*On the Five Regular Solids*) and *[De Prospectiva pingendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Prospectiva_pingendi "De Prospectiva pingendi")* (*On Perspective in painting*). The subjects covered in these writings include [arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic "Arithmetic"), [algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra "Algebra"), [geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry "Geometry") and innovative work in both [solid geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_geometry "Solid geometry") and [perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspective_(graphical) "Perspective (graphical)"). Much of Piero's work was later absorbed into the writing of others, notably [Luca Pacioli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Pacioli "Luca Pacioli"). Piero's work on solid geometry was translated in Pacioli's *[Divina proportione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divina_proportione "Divina proportione")*, a work illustrated by [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci "Leonardo da Vinci"). Biographers of his patron [Federico da Montefeltro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_da_Montefeltro "Federico da Montefeltro") of [Urbino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbino "Urbino") record that he was encouraged to pursue the interest in perspective which was shared by the Duke.

In the late 1450s, Piero copied and illustrated the following works of [Archimedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes "Archimedes"): *[On the Sphere and Cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Sphere_and_Cylinder "On the Sphere and Cylinder")*, *[Measurement of a Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurement_of_a_Circle "Measurement of a Circle")*, *On Conoids and Spheroids*, *[On Spirals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Spirals "On Spirals")*, *[On the Equilibrium of Planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Equilibrium_of_Planes "On the Equilibrium of Planes")*, *[The Quadrature of the Parabola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Quadrature_of_the_Parabola "The Quadrature of the Parabola")*, and *[The Sand Reckoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sand_Reckoner "The Sand Reckoner")*. The [manuscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuscript "Manuscript") consists of 82 folio leaves, is held in the collection of the [Biblioteca Riccardiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Riccardiana "Biblioteca Riccardiana") and is a copy of the translation of the Archimedean corpus made by Italian humanist [Iacopo da San Cassiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iacopo_da_San_Cassiano "Iacopo da San Cassiano").

## Inspirations

[Bohuslav Martinů](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohuslav_Martinů "Bohuslav Martinů") wrote a three movement work for orchestra entitled *[Les Fresques de Piero della Francesca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Fresques_de_Piero_della_Francesca "Les Fresques de Piero della Francesca")*. Dedicated to [Rafael Kubelik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Kubelik "Rafael Kubelik"), it was premiered by Kubelik and the [Vienna Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Philharmonic "Vienna Philharmonic") at the 1956 Salzburg Festival.

Piero's geometrical perfection and the almost magic atmosphere of the light in his painting inspired modern painters like [Giorgio de Chirico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_de_Chirico "Giorgio de Chirico"), [Massimo Campigli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Campigli "Massimo Campigli"), [Felice Casorati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felice_Casorati "Felice Casorati"), and [Balthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balthus "Balthus").
