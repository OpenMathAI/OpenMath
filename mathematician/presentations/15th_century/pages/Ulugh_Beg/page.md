**Mīrzā Muhammad Tarāghāy bin Shāhrukh** ([Chagatay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatay_language "Chagatay language"): میرزا محمد تراغای بن شاهرخ; [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language"): میرزا محمد طارق بن شاهرخ), better known as **Ulugh Beg** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language"): الغ‌بیگ; 22 March 1394 – 27 October 1449), was a [Timurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire "Timurid Empire") [sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan "Sultan"), as well as an [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_astronomy "Islamic astronomy") and [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematics "Islamic mathematics").

Ulugh Beg was notable for his work in astronomy-related mathematics, such as [trigonometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometry "Trigonometry") and [spherical geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_geometry "Spherical geometry"), as well as his general interests in the arts and intellectual activities. It is thought that he spoke five languages: [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic "Arabic"), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language"), [Chaghatai Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_language "Chagatai language"), [Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language "Mongolian language"), and a small amount of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_language "Mandarin language"). During his rule (first as a governor, then outright) the [Timurid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire "Timurid Empire") achieved the cultural peak of the [Timurid Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Renaissance "Timurid Renaissance") through his attention and patronage. [Samarkand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarkand "Samarkand") was captured and given to Ulugh Beg by his father [Shah Rukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Rukh "Shah Rukh").

He built the great [Ulugh Beg Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Beg_Observatory "Ulugh Beg Observatory") in Samarkand between 1424 and 1429. It was considered by scholars to have been one of the finest observatories in the Islamic world at the time and the largest in [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"). Ulugh Beg was subsequently recognized as the most important observational astronomer from the 15th century by many scholars. He also built the [Ulugh Beg Madrasah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Beg_Madrasa,_Samarkand "Ulugh Beg Madrasa, Samarkand") (1417–1420) in Samarkand and [Bukhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukhara "Bukhara"), transforming the cities into cultural centers of learning in [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia").

However, Ulugh Beg's scientific expertise was not matched by his skills in governance. During his short reign, he failed to establish his power and authority. As a result, other rulers, including his family, took advantage of his lack of control, and he was subsequently overthrown and assassinated by his son Abd al Latif around 1449 (852 AH).

## Early life

He was a grandson of the great conqueror and king, [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur "Timur") (Tamerlane) (1336–1405), and the oldest son of [Shah Rukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Rukh_(Timurid_dynasty) "Shah Rukh (Timurid dynasty)"), both of whom came from the Turkicized Mongol [Barlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barlas "Barlas") tribe of [Transoxiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transoxiana "Transoxiana") (now [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan "Uzbekistan")). His mother was a noblewoman named [Gawhar Shad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gawhar_Shad "Gawhar Shad"), daughter of a member of the representative Turkic tribal aristocracy, Ghiyasuddin Tarkhan.

Ulugh Beg was born in [Sultaniyeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultaniyeh "Sultaniyeh") during his grandfather's invasion of [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia "Persia"). He was given the name *[Mīrzā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_(noble) "Mirza (noble)") Muhammad Tāraghay*. Ulugh Beg, the name he was most commonly known by, was not truly a personal name, but rather a [moniker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moniker "Moniker"), which can be loosely translated as "Great Ruler" (compare modern Turkish *[ulu](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ulu "wikt:ulu")*, "great", and *[bey](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bey "wikt:bey")*, "chief") and is the [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages "Turkic languages") equivalent of [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur "Timur")'s Perso-Arabic title *Amīr-e Kabīr*.

As a child he wandered through a substantial part of the Middle East and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") as his grandfather expanded his conquests in those areas. After Timur's death, Shah Rukh moved the empire's capital to [Herat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herat,_Afghanistan "Herat, Afghanistan") (in modern [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan")). Sixteen-year-old Ulugh Beg subsequently became the governor of the former capital of Samarkand in 1409. In 1411, he was named the sovereign ruler of the whole of [Mavarannahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transoxiana "Transoxiana").

## Science

The young ruler set out to turn the city into an intellectual center for the empire. Between 1417 and 1420, he built a *[madrasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasah "Madrasah")* ("university" or "institute") on [Registan Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Registan "Registan") in Samarkand (currently in [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan "Uzbekistan")), and he invited numerous [Islamic astronomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_astronomy "Islamic astronomy") and [mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematics "Islamic mathematics") to study there. The *madrasa* building still survives. Ulugh Beg's most famous pupil in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy") was [Ali Qushchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Qushji "Ali Qushji") (died in 1474). [Qadi Zada al-Rumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadi_Zada_al-Rumi "Qadi Zada al-Rumi") was the most notable teacher at Ulugh Beg's madrasa and [Jamshid al-Kashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamshid_al-Kashi "Jamshid al-Kashi"), an astronomer, later came to join the staff.

### Astronomy

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/A_model_of_Ulug_Beg_obsrvatory_in_the_Uzbek_National_Muzeum.jpg/250px-A_model_of_Ulug_Beg_obsrvatory_in_the_Uzbek_National_Muzeum.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:A_model_of_Ulug_Beg_obsrvatory_in_the_Uzbek_National_Muzeum.jpg)

A model of Ulug Beg observatory in the Uzbek National Muzeum - the central partition hosted the huge [sextant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextant "Sextant").

Astronomy piqued Ulugh Beg's interest when he visited the [Maragheh Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maragheh_observatory "Maragheh observatory") at a young age. This observatory, located in [Maragheh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maragheh "Maragheh"), Iran, is where the well-known astronomer [Nasir al-Din al-Tusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir_al-Din_al-Tusi "Nasir al-Din al-Tusi") practised.

In 1428, Ulugh Beg built an enormous [observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatory "Observatory"), similar to [Tycho Brahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe "Tycho Brahe")'s later [Uraniborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uraniborg "Uraniborg") as well as [Taqi al-Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taqi_al-Din_Muhammad_ibn_Ma'ruf "Taqi al-Din Muhammad ibn Ma'ruf")'s [observatory in Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople_Observatory_of_Taqi_ad-Din "Constantinople Observatory of Taqi ad-Din"). Lacking [telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope "Telescope") to work with, he increased his accuracy by increasing the length of his [sextant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextant_(astronomical) "Sextant (astronomical)"); the so-called *Fakhri* [sextant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextant_(astronomical) "Sextant (astronomical)") had a radius of about 36 meters (118 feet) and the [optical separability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_resolution "Angular resolution") of 180" (seconds of arc). The [Fakhri sextant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhri_sextant?action=edit&redlink=1 "Fakhri sextant") was the largest instrument at the observatory in Samarkand (an image of the sextant is on the side of this article). There were many other astronomical instruments located at the observatory, but the Fakhri sextant is the most well-known instrument there. The purpose of the Fakhri sextant was to measure the transit altitudes of the stars. This was a measurement of the maximum altitude above the horizon of the stars. It was only possible to use this device to measure the declination of celestial objects. The image, which can be found in this article, shows the remaining portion of the instrument, which consists of the underground, lower portion of the instrument that was not destroyed. The observatory built by Ulugh Beg was the most pervasive and well-known observatory throughout the Islamic world.

With the instruments located in the observatory in Samarkand, Ulugh Beg composed a star catalogue consisting of 1018 stars, which is eleven fewer stars than are present in the star catalogue of [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy "Ptolemy"). Ulugh Beg utilized dimensions from [al-Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Sufi "Al Sufi") and based his star catalogue on a new analysis that was autonomous from the data used by Ptolemy. Throughout his life as an astronomer, Ulugh Beg came to realize that there were multiple mistakes in the work and subsequent data of Ptolemy that had been in use for many years.

Using it, he compiled the 1437 *[Zij-i-Sultani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zij-i-Sultani "Zij-i-Sultani")* of 994 [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star "Star"), generally considered the greatest [star catalogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_catalogue "Star catalogue") between those of [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy "Ptolemy") and [Tycho Brahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe "Tycho Brahe"), a work that stands alongside [Abd al-Rahman al-Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Rahman_al-Sufi "Abd al-Rahman al-Sufi")'s *[Book of Fixed Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Fixed_Stars "Book of Fixed Stars")*. The serious errors which he found in previous Arabian star catalogues (many of which had simply updated Ptolemy's work, adding the effect of [precession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precession_of_the_equinoxes "Precession of the equinoxes") to the longitudes) induced him to redetermine the positions of 992 fixed stars, to which he added 27 stars from [Abd al-Rahman al-Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Rahman_al-Sufi "Abd al-Rahman al-Sufi")'s catalogue *[Book of Fixed Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Fixed_Stars "Book of Fixed Stars")* from the year 964, which were too far south for observation from Samarkand. This catalogue, one of the most original of the Middle Ages, was first edited by [Thomas Hyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hyde "Thomas Hyde") at Oxford in 1665 under the title *Jadāvil-i Mavāzi' S̱avābit*, *sive, Tabulae Long. ac Lat. Stellarum Fixarum ex Observatione Ulugh Beighi* and reprinted in 1767 by [G. Sharpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Sharpe?action=edit&redlink=1 "Gregory Sharpe"). More recent editions are those by [Francis Baily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Baily "Francis Baily") in 1843 in Vol. XIII of the *Memoirs of the Royal Astronomical Society*, and by [Edward Ball Knobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Ball_Knobel "Edward Ball Knobel") in *Ulugh Beg's Catalogue of Stars, Revised from all Persian Manuscripts Existing in Great Britain, with a Vocabulary of Persian and Arabic Words* (1917).

In 1437, Ulugh Beg determined the length of the [sidereal year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidereal_year "Sidereal year") as 365.2570370...d = 365d 6h 10m 8s (an error of +58 seconds). In his measurements over the course of many years he used a 50 m high [gnomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnomon "Gnomon"). This value was improved by 28 seconds in 1525 by [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus "Nicolaus Copernicus"), who appealed to the estimation of [Thabit ibn Qurra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thabit_ibn_Qurra "Thabit ibn Qurra") (826–901), which had an error of +2 seconds. However, Ulugh Beg later measured another more precise value of the [tropical year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_year "Tropical year") as 365d 5h 49m 15s, which has an error of +25 seconds, making it more accurate than Copernicus's estimate which had an error of +30 seconds. Ulugh Beg also determined the Earth's [axial tilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt "Axial tilt") as 23°30'17" in the [sexagesimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexagesimal "Sexagesimal") system of degrees, minutes and seconds of arc, which in decimal notation converts to 23.5047°.

### Mathematics

In [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematics "Islamic mathematics"), Ulugh Beg wrote accurate [trigonometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometry "Trigonometry") tables of [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine_and_cosine "Sine and cosine") and [tangent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent "Tangent") values correct to at least eight decimal places.

## Foreign relations

Once Ulugh Beg became governor of [Samarqand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarkand "Samarkand"), he fostered diplomatic relations with the [Yongle emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle_Emperor "Yongle Emperor") of the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty"). In 1416, Ming envoys [Chen Cheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Cheng_(Ming_dynasty) "Chen Cheng (Ming dynasty)") and Lu An presented [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk "Silk") and silver stuffs to Ulugh Beg on behalf of the Yongle emperor. In 1419, The Timurid sent his own emissaries, Sultan-Shah and Muhammad Bakhshi, to the Ming court. Ulugh Beg's emissaries came across [Ghiyāth al-dīn Naqqāsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghiyāth_al-dīn_Naqqāsh "Ghiyāth al-dīn Naqqāsh") and other envoys representing Shah Rukh, [Prince Baysunghur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baysunghur "Baysunghur"), and other Timurid authorities in Beijing; however, they stayed at separate hostelries. Ghiyāth al-dīn Naqqāsh even saw the Yongle emperor riding a black [horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse "Horse") with white feet which had been gifted by Ulugh Beg.

Coinage of Ulugh Beg, dated AH 852 (1448–49). [Herat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herat "Herat") mint.

Ulugh Beg led two major campaigns against his neighbours. This first one took place in 1425 and was directed against [Moghulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moghulistan "Moghulistan") and its ruler [Shir Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher_Muhammad "Sher Muhammad"). He was victorious but the impact of the campaign was limited and Shir Muhammad remained in power. A year later, [Baraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baraq_(Khan_of_Golden_Horde) "Baraq (Khan of Golden Horde)"), Khan of the [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde "Golden Horde") and former protégé of Ulugh Beg, laid claim to Timurid possessions around the Syr Darya, including the town of [Sighnaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sighnaq "Sighnaq"). In response to that, in 1427 Ulugh Beg, accompanied by his brother [Muhummad Juki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Juki "Muhammad Juki"), marched against Baraq. In a hill close to Sighnaq the Timurid army was surprised by a smaller enemy force but was soundly defeated. The humiliation suffered at the hands of Baraq was to have a lasting effect on Ulugh Beg. His campaign against the Golden Horde would be the last he would undertake against a neighbouring power. The armies he later sent against them would not win any resounding victories and by the end of his reign his territories would be raided by his northern and easterly foes.

In 1439, the [Zhengtong emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Yingzong_of_Ming "Emperor Yingzong of Ming") ordered an artist to produce a painting of a black horse with white feet and a white forehead that had been sent by Ulugh Beg. Six years later, the Ming emperor sent a letter to Ulugh Beg in order to express his gratitude for all the "[tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary_system_of_China "Tributary system of China")" from Samarqand. The emperor sent "vessels made of gold and jade, a spear with a [dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon "Dragon")'s head, a fine horse with saddle, and variegated gold-embroidered silk stuffs" to Ulugh Beg, as well as silk stuffs and garments for the Timurid prince's family.

## War of succession and death

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Tomb_of_Tamerlane.jpg/250px-Tomb_of_Tamerlane.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tomb_of_Tamerlane.jpg)

Ulugh Beg's headstone at the foot of Timur's in the [Gur-e-Amir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur-e-Amir "Gur-e-Amir")

In 1447, upon learning of the death of his father [Shah Rukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Rukh "Shah Rukh"), Ulugh Beg went to [Balkh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkh "Balkh"). Here, he heard that [Ala al-Dawla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ala_al-Dawla_Mirza "Ala al-Dawla Mirza"), the son of his late brother [Baysunghur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baysunghur "Baysunghur"), had claimed the rulership of the [Timurid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire "Timurid Empire") in [Herat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herat "Herat"). Consequently, Ulugh Beg marched against Ala al-Dawla and met him in battle at [Murghab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bala_Murghab "Bala Murghab"). He defeated his nephew and advanced toward Herat, massacring its people in 1448. However, [Abul-Qasim Babur Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul-Qasim_Babur_Mirza "Abul-Qasim Babur Mirza"), Ala al-Dawla's brother, came to the latter's aid and defeated Ulugh Beg.

Ulugh Beg retreated to Balkh where he found that its governor, his oldest son [Abdal-Latif Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdal-Latif_Mirza "Abdal-Latif Mirza"), had [rebelled against him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolt_of_Abdal-Latif_Mirza "Revolt of Abdal-Latif Mirza"). Another civil war ensued. Abdal-Latif recruited troops to meet his father's army on the banks of the [Amu Darya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amu_Darya "Amu Darya") river. However, Ulugh Beg was forced to retreat to Samarkand before any fighting took place, having heard news of turmoil in the city. Abdal-Latif soon reached Samarkand and Ulugh Beg involuntarily surrendered to his son. Abd-al-Latif released his father from custody, allowing him to make [pilgrimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajj "Hajj") to [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca "Mecca"). However, he ensured Ulugh Beg never reached his destination, having him, as well as his brother Abdal-Aziz assassinated in 1449.

Eventually, Ulugh Beg's reputation was rehabilitated by his nephew, [Abdallah Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdallah_Mirza "Abdallah Mirza") (1450–1451), who placed his remains at Timur's feet in the [Gur-e-Amir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur-e-Amir "Gur-e-Amir") in Samarkand, where they were found by [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet "Soviet") archaeologists in 1941.

## Legacy

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/14/1987_CPA_5876.jpg/250px-1987_CPA_5876.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1987_CPA_5876.jpg)

Ulugh Beg and his astronomical observatory scheme, depicted on the 1987 USSR stamp. He was one of Islam's greatest astronomers during the Middle Ages. The stamp says "Uzbek astronomer and mathematician Ulugbek" in Russian.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Ulugh_Beigh_crater_4189_h1.jpg/250px-Ulugh_Beigh_crater_4189_h1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ulugh_Beigh_crater_4189_h1.jpg)

The [Ulugh Beigh crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Beigh_(crater) "Ulugh Beigh (crater)") on the Moon, named for Ulugh Beg.

- The crater, [Ulugh Beigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Beigh_(crater) "Ulugh Beigh (crater)"), on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon"), was named after him by the German astronomer [Johann Heinrich von Mädler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Heinrich_von_Mädler "Johann Heinrich von Mädler") on his 1830 map of the Moon.
- [2439 Ulugbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2439_Ulugbek "2439 Ulugbek"), a [main-belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid_belt "Asteroid belt") [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid "Asteroid") which was discovered on 21 August 1977 by [N. Chernykh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._Chernykh "N. Chernykh") at [Nauchnyj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauchnyj "Nauchnyj"), was named after him.
- The 2017 documentary *The Man Who Unlocked The Universe* is based on his life, with [Lola Karimova-Tillyaeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lola_Karimova-Tillyaeva "Lola Karimova-Tillyaeva") as executive producer.
- The [dinosaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur "Dinosaur") *[Ulughbegsaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulughbegsaurus "Ulughbegsaurus")* was named after him in 2021.

## Exhumation

Soviet anthropologist [Mikhail M. Gerasimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Mikhaylovich_Gerasimov "Mikhail Mikhaylovich Gerasimov") reconstructed the face of Ulugh Beg through the analysis of the remains that were exhumed from his tomb in 1941. Like his grandfather Timurlane, Ulugh Beg is close to the Mongoloid type with slightly Europoid features. His father Shah Rukh had predominantly Caucasoid features, with no obvious Mongoloid feature.
