**Ghiyāth al-Dīn Jamshīd Masʿūd al-Kāshī** (or **al-Kāshānī**) ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language"): غیاث‌الدین جمشید کاشانی *Ghiyās-ud-dīn Jamshīd Kāshānī*; c. 1380 – 22 June 1429) was a Persian [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy_in_the_medieval_Islamic_world "Astronomy in the medieval Islamic world") and [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_in_medieval_Islam "Mathematics in medieval Islam") during the reign of [Tamerlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamerlane "Tamerlane").

Much of al-Kāshī's work was not brought to [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe") and still, even the extant work, remains unpublished in any form.

## Biography

Al-Kashi was born in 1380, in [Kashan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashan "Kashan"), in central Iran, to a [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people "Persian people") family. This region was controlled by [Tamerlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur "Timur"), better known as Timur.

The situation changed for the better when Timur died in 1405, and his son, [Shah Rokh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Rukh_(Timurid_dynasty) "Shah Rukh (Timurid dynasty)"), ascended into power. Shah Rokh and his wife, [Goharshad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goharshad "Goharshad"), a Turkish princess, were very interested in the [sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences "Sciences"), and they encouraged their court to study the various fields in great depth. Consequently, the period of their power became one of many scholarly accomplishments. This was the perfect environment for al-Kashi to begin his career as one of the world's greatest mathematicians.

Eight years after he came into power in 1409, their son, [Ulugh Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Beg "Ulugh Beg"), founded an institute in [Samarkand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarkand "Samarkand") which soon became a prominent university. Students from all over the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East") and beyond, flocked to this academy in the capital city of Ulugh Beg's empire. Consequently, Ulugh Beg gathered many great mathematicians and scientists of the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East"). In 1414, al-Kashi took this opportunity to contribute vast amounts of knowledge to his people. His best work was done in the court of Ulugh Beg.

Al-Kashi was still working on his book, called “Risala al-watar wa’l-jaib” meaning “The Treatise on the Chord and Sine”, when he died, in 1429. Some state that he was murdered and say that Ulugh Beg probably ordered this, whereas others suggest he died a natural death. Regardless, after his death, Ulugh Beg described him as "a remarkable scientist" who "could solve the most difficult problems".

## Astronomy

### *Khaqani Zij*

Al-Kashi produced a *[Zij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zij "Zij")* entitled the *Khaqani Zij*, which was based on [Nasir al-Din al-Tusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir_al-Din_al-Tusi "Nasir al-Din al-Tusi")'s earlier *[Zij-i Ilkhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zij-i_Ilkhani "Zij-i Ilkhani")*. In his *Khaqani Zij*, al-Kashi thanks the [Timurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty "Timurid dynasty") sultan and mathematician-astronomer [Ulugh Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Beg "Ulugh Beg"), who invited al-Kashi to work at his [observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatory "Observatory") (see [Islamic astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_astronomy "Islamic astronomy")) and his [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University "University") (see [Madrasah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasah "Madrasah")) which taught [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalam "Kalam"). Al-Kashi produced [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_functions "Trigonometric functions") tables to four [sexagesimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexagesimal "Sexagesimal") digits (equivalent to eight [decimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal "Decimal") places) of accuracy for each degree and includes differences for each minute. He also produced tables dealing with transformations between [coordinate systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_system "Coordinate system") on the [celestial sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_sphere "Celestial sphere"), such as the transformation from the [ecliptic coordinate system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecliptic_coordinate_system "Ecliptic coordinate system") to the [equatorial coordinate system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_coordinate_system "Equatorial coordinate system").

### Sizes and distances of heavenly bodies

He wrote the book *[Sullam al-sama'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sullam_al-sama' "Sullam al-sama'")* (*Stairway to the Heavens*, 1407) on the resolution of difficulties met by predecessors in the determination of sizes and distances of [heavenly bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavenly_bodies "Heavenly bodies") such as the [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth "Earth"), the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon"), the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun "Sun"), and the [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stars "Stars").

### Astronomical instruments

In 1416, al-Kashi wrote the *Treatise on Astronomical Observational Instruments*, which described a variety of different instruments, including the [triquetrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triquetrum_(astronomy) "Triquetrum (astronomy)") and [armillary sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armillary_sphere "Armillary sphere"), the [equinoctial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equinox "Equinox") armillary and [solsticial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solstice "Solstice") armillary of [Mo'ayyeduddin Urdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo'ayyeduddin_Urdi "Mo'ayyeduddin Urdi"), the [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine "Sine") and [versine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versine "Versine") instrument of Urdi, the [sextant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextant_(astronomical) "Sextant (astronomical)") of [al-Khujandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khujandi "Al-Khujandi"), the Fakhri sextant at the [Samarqand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarqand "Samarqand") observatory, a double quadrant [Azimuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azimuth "Azimuth")-[altitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude "Altitude") instrument he invented, and a small armillary sphere incorporating an [alhidade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhidade "Alhidade") which he invented.

#### Plate of conjunctions

Al-Kashi invented the plate of conjunctions, an [analog computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_computer "Analog computer") instrument used to determine the time of day at which [planetary conjunctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_conjunction "Planetary conjunction") will occur, and for performing [linear interpolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_interpolation "Linear interpolation").

#### Planetary computer

Al-Kashi also invented a mechanical planetary [computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_computer "Analog computer") which he called the Plate of Zones, which could graphically solve a number of planetary problems, including the prediction of the true positions in [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude "Longitude") of the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun "Sun") and [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon"), and the [planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet "Planet") in terms of [elliptical orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptical_orbit "Elliptical orbit"); the [latitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude "Latitude") of the Sun, Moon, and planets; and the [ecliptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecliptic "Ecliptic") of the Sun. The instrument also incorporated an [alhidade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhidade "Alhidade") and [ruler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruler "Ruler").

Al-Kashi made the most accurate [approximation of *π*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximations_of_π "Approximations of π") to date in his *al-Risāla al-muhītīyya* (*Treatise on the Circumference*). He correctly computed [2*π*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi "Pi") to 9 [sexagesimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexagesimal "Sexagesimal") digits in 1424, and he converted this estimate of 2π to 16 [decimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal "Decimal") places of accuracy. This was far more accurate than the estimates earlier given in [Greek mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mathematics "Greek mathematics") (3 decimal places by [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy "Ptolemy"), AD 150), [Chinese mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_mathematics "Chinese mathematics") (7 decimal places by [Zu Chongzhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zu_Chongzhi "Zu Chongzhi"), AD 480) or [Indian mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_mathematics "Indian mathematics") (11 decimal places by [Madhava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhava_of_Sangamagrama "Madhava of Sangamagrama") of [Kerala School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_School_of_Astronomy_and_Mathematics "Kerala School of Astronomy and Mathematics"), *c.* 14th Century). The accuracy of al-Kashi's estimate was not surpassed until [Ludolph van Ceulen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludolph_van_Ceulen "Ludolph van Ceulen") computed 20 decimal places of π 180 years later. Al-Kashi's goal was to compute the circle constant so precisely that the circumference of the largest possible circle (ecliptica) could be computed with the highest desirable precision (the diameter of a hair).

### *Treatise on the Chord and Sine*

In Al-Kashi's *Risālah al-watar waʾl-jaib* (*Treatise on the Chord and Sine*), he computed sin 1° to nearly as much accuracy as his value for π, which was the most accurate approximation of sin 1° in his time and was not surpassed until [Taqi al-Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taqi_ad-Din_Muhammad_ibn_Ma'ruf "Taqi ad-Din Muhammad ibn Ma'ruf") in the sixteenth century. In [algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra "Algebra") and [numerical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_analysis "Numerical analysis"), he developed an [iterative method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iterative_method "Iterative method") for solving [cubic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_equation "Cubic equation"), which was not discovered in Europe until centuries later.

### *The Key to Arithmetic*

#### Law of cosines

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Law_of_cosines_following_al-Kashi.png/330px-Law_of_cosines_following_al-Kashi.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Law_of_cosines_following_al-Kashi.png)

Al-Kashi's version of the law of cosines (case where γ is obtuse), expressed with modern algebraic notation.

Al-Kashi's *Miftāḥ al-ḥisāb* (*Key of Arithmetic*, 1427) explained how to [solve triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solution_of_triangles "Solution of triangles") from various combinations of given data. The method used when two sides and their included angle were given was essentially the same method used by 13th century Persian mathematician [Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir_al-Din_al-Tusi "Nasir al-Din al-Tusi") in his *Kitāb al-Shakl al-qattāʴ* (*Book on the Complete Quadrilateral*, c. 1250), but Al-Kashi presented all of the steps instead of leaving details to the reader:

> **Another case** is when two sides and the angle between them are known and the rest are unknown. We multiply one of the sides by the sine of the [known] angle one time and by the sine of its complement the other time converted and we subtract the second result from the other side if the angle is acute and add it if the angle is obtuse. We then square the result and add to it the square of the first result. We take the square root of the sum to get the remaining side....

— Al-Kāshī's *Miftāḥ al-ḥisāb*,

#### Decimal fractions

In discussing [decimal fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_fractions "Decimal fractions"), [Struik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirk_Jan_Struik "Dirk Jan Struik") states that (p. 7):

> "The introduction of decimal fractions as a common computational practice can be dated back to the [Flemish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_Region "Flemish Region") pamphlet *De Thiende*, published at [Leyden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden "Leiden") in 1585, together with a French translation, *La Disme*, by the Flemish mathematician [Simon Stevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Stevin "Simon Stevin") (1548-1620), then settled in the Northern [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands "Netherlands"). It is true that decimal fractions were used by the [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_mathematics "Chinese mathematics") many centuries before Stevin and that the Persian astronomer Al-Kāshī used both decimal and [sexagesimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexagesimal "Sexagesimal") fractions with great ease in his *Key to arithmetic* (Samarkand, early fifteenth century)."

#### Khayyam's triangle

The Persian mathematician [Al-Karaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Karaji "Al-Karaji") (953–1029) wrote a now-lost book which contained the first description of Pascal's triangle.
In considering [Pascal's triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal's_triangle "Pascal's triangle"), known in Persia as "Khayyam's triangle" (named after [Omar Khayyám](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Khayyám "Omar Khayyám")), Struik notes that (p. 21):

> "The Pascal triangle appears for the first time (so far as we know at present) in a book of 1261 written by [Yang Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Hui "Yang Hui"), one of the mathematicians of the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty "Song dynasty") in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"). The properties of [binomial coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_coefficient "Binomial coefficient") were discussed by the Persian mathematician Jamshid Al-Kāshī in his *Key to arithmetic* of c. 1425. Both in China and Persia the knowledge of these properties may be much older. This knowledge was shared by some of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance") mathematicians, and we see [Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Pascal "Blaise Pascal")'s triangle on the title page of [Peter Apian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Apian "Peter Apian")'s [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language "German language") arithmetic of 1527. After this, we find the triangle and the properties of binomial coefficients in several other authors."

## Biographical film

In 2009, [IRIB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIB "IRIB") produced and broadcast (through Channel 1 of IRIB) a biographical-historical film series on the life and times of Jamshid Al-Kāshi, with the title *[The Ladder of the Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ladder_of_the_Sky "The Ladder of the Sky")* (*Nardebām-e Āsmān*). The series, which consists of 15 parts, with each part being 45 minutes long, is directed by [Mohammad Hossein Latifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Hossein_Latifi?action=edit&redlink=1 "Mohammad Hossein Latifi") and produced by Mohsen Ali-Akbari. In this production, the role of the adult Jamshid Al-Kāshi is played by Vahid Jalilvand.
