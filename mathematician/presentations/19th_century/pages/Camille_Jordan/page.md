**Marie Ennemond Camille Jordan** (French: [[ʒɔʁdɑ̃]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 5 January 1838 – 22 January 1922) was a French mathematician, known both for his foundational work in [group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_theory "Group theory") and for his [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook "Textbook") *[Cours d'analyse de l'École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cours_d'analyse_de_l'École_polytechnique "Cours d'analyse de l'École polytechnique")*.

## Biography

Jordan was born in [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon "Lyon") and educated at the [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique "École polytechnique"). He was an engineer by profession; later in life he taught at the École polytechnique and the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France"), where he had a reputation for eccentric choices of notation.

He is remembered now by name in a number of results:

- The [Jordan curve theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_curve_theorem "Jordan curve theorem"), a topological result required in [complex analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_analysis "Complex analysis")
- The [Jordan normal form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_normal_form "Jordan normal form") and the [Jordan matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_matrix "Jordan matrix") in [linear algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_algebra "Linear algebra")
- In [mathematical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis"), [Jordan measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_measure "Jordan measure") (or *Jordan content*) is an area measure that predates [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory "Measure theory")
- In [group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_theory "Group theory"), the [Jordan–Hölder theorem on composition series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composition_series "Composition series") is a basic result.
- [Jordan's theorem on finite linear groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan's_theorem_on_finite_linear_groups "Jordan's theorem on finite linear groups")

Jordan's work did much to bring [Galois theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_theory "Galois theory") into the mainstream. He also investigated the [Mathieu groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathieu_group "Mathieu group"), the first examples of [sporadic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_group "Sporadic group"). His *Traité des substitutions*, on [permutation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_group "Permutation group"), was published in 1870; this treatise won for Jordan the 1870 [*prix Poncelet*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poncelet_Prize "Poncelet Prize"). He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") in 1920 in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg "Strasbourg").

The [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid "Asteroid") [25593 Camillejordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25593_Camillejordan "25593 Camillejordan") and the Institut Camille Jordan are named in his honour.

Camille Jordan is not to be confused with the [geodesist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesist "Geodesist") [Wilhelm Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Jordan_(geodesist) "Wilhelm Jordan (geodesist)") ([Gauss–Jordan elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_elimination "Gaussian elimination")) or the physicist [Pascual Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascual_Jordan "Pascual Jordan") ([Jordan algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_algebra "Jordan algebra")).
