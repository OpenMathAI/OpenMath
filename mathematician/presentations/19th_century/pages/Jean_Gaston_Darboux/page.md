**Jean-Gaston Darboux** [FAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences") [MIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_France "Institute of France") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London "Royal Society of London") [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh "Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh") (French: [[daʁbu]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 14 August 1842 – 23 February 1917) was a French [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician").

## Life

According to his birth certificate, he was born in [Nîmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nîmes "Nîmes") in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") on 14 August 1842, at 1 am. However, probably due to the midnight birth, Darboux himself usually reported his own birthday as 13 August, *e.g.* in his filled form for Légion d'Honneur.

His parents were François Darboux, businessman of mercery, and Alix Gourdoux. The father died when Gaston was 7. His mother undertook the mercery business with great courage, and insisted that her children receive good education. Gaston had a younger brother, Louis, who taught mathematics at the Lycée Nîmes for almost his entire life.

He studied at the Nîmes Lycée and the [Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier "Montpellier") Lycée before being accepted as the top qualifier at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure "École Normale Supérieure") in 1861, and received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") there in 1866. His thesis, written under the direction of [Michel Chasles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Chasles "Michel Chasles"), was titled *Sur les surfaces orthogonales*. During his studies at the ENS, he also took lectures in [Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University "Sorbonne University") and [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Darboux_as_a_student_of_the_Ecole_Normale.png/250px-Darboux_as_a_student_of_the_Ecole_Normale.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Darboux_as_a_student_of_the_Ecole_Normale.png)

Darboux as a student of the École Normale Supérieure, c. 1865

In 1870, he co-founded the journal *Bulletin des sciences mathématiques et astronomiques*, called "Darboux's Journal" by his contemporary mathematicians. The publishing house was the Henry Gauthier-Villars et Cie Éditeurs, located in Paris.

In 1872, Darboux married the Beauvaisian [milliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milliner "Milliner") Amélie 'Célina' Carbonnier (1848–1911), daughter of Charles Louis Carbonnier, tailor, and Marie Victorine Anastase Hènocq. He and Célina had two children, Jean-*Gaston* (1870–1921), who was born at the time of the [Siege of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Paris_(1870–71) "Siege of Paris (1870–71)") and later became a marine zoologist at the Faculty of Science in Marseille, and Anaïs Berthe *Lucie* (1873–1970).

He participated in the foundation of the [École normale supérieure de jeunes filles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_jeunes_filles "École normale supérieure de jeunes filles") in 1880, an institute that aimed at training female educators and ran parallel to the École Normale Supérieure. Its first director was [Julie Favre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Favre "Julie Favre").

In 1884, Darboux was elected to the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences").

Darboux made several important contributions to [geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry "Geometry") and [mathematical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis") (see, for example, [linear PDEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equation#Linear_PDEs "Differential equation")). He was a [biographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biographer "Biographer") of [Henri Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Poincaré "Henri Poincaré") and he edited the Selected Works of [Joseph Fourier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Fourier "Joseph Fourier").

Among his students were [Émile Borel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émile_Borel "Émile Borel"), [Élie Cartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Élie_Cartan "Élie Cartan"), [Édouard Goursat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Édouard_Goursat "Édouard Goursat"), [Émile Picard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émile_Picard "Émile Picard"), [Gheorghe Țițeica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Țițeica "Gheorghe Țițeica") and [Stanisław Zaremba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanisław_Zaremba_(mathematician) "Stanisław Zaremba (mathematician)").

In 1892 he was awarded honorary membership of the [Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_and_Philosophical_Society "Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society"), and in 1900, he was appointed the academy's permanent secretary of its Mathematics section.

In 1902, he was elected to the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"); in 1916, he received the [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal "Sylvester Medal") from the Society. In 1908, he was a [plenary speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_International_Congresses_of_Mathematicians_Plenary_and_Invited_Speakers "List of International Congresses of Mathematicians Plenary and Invited Speakers") at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome").
He continued to contribute to the French *Bulletin des sciences mathématiques*, even after 1916.
