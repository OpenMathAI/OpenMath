**Ernst Eduard Kummer** (German: [[ˈkʊmɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German"); 29 January 1810 – 14 May 1893) was a German [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"). Skilled in [applied mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematics "Applied mathematics"), Kummer trained German army officers in [ballistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistics "Ballistics"); afterwards, he taught for 10 years in a *[gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)")*, the German equivalent of high school, where he inspired the mathematical career of [Leopold Kronecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Kronecker "Leopold Kronecker").

## Life

Kummer was born in [Sorau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorau "Sorau"), [Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brandenburg "Province of Brandenburg") (then part of [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia "Prussia")). His father, a physician, died in 1813.

He initially pursued Protestant theology but later studied mathematics and was awarded a PhD from the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle "University of Halle") in 1831 for writing a prize-winning mathematical essay (*De cosinuum et sinuum potestatibus secundum cosinus et sinus arcuum multiplicium evolvendis*), which was published a year later.

In 1840, Kummer married Ottilie Mendelssohn, daughter of Nathan Mendelssohn and Henriette Itzig. Ottilie was a cousin of [Felix Mendelssohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Mendelssohn "Felix Mendelssohn") and his sister Rebecca Mendelssohn Bartholdy, the wife of the mathematician [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gustav_Lejeune_Dirichlet "Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet"). His second wife (whom he married soon after the death of Ottilie in 1848), Bertha Cauer, was a maternal cousin of Ottilie. Overall, he had 13 children. His daughter Marie married the mathematician [Hermann Schwarz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Schwarz "Hermann Schwarz"). Kummer retired from teaching and from mathematics in 1890 and died three years later in Berlin.

## Mathematics

Kummer made several contributions to mathematics in different areas; he codified some of the relations between different [hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_series "Hypergeometric series"), known as contiguity relations. The [Kummer surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kummer_surface "Kummer surface") results from taking the quotient of a two-dimensional [abelian variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_variety "Abelian variety") by the cyclic group {1, −1} (an early [orbifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbifold "Orbifold"): it has 16 singular points, and its geometry was intensively studied in the nineteenth century).

Kummer also proved [Fermat's Last Theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat's_Last_Theorem "Fermat's Last Theorem") for a considerable class of prime exponents (see [regular prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_prime "Regular prime"), [ideal class group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_class_group "Ideal class group")). His methods were closer, perhaps, to [*p*-adic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_number "P-adic number") ones than to [ideal theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_ideal "Ring ideal") as understood later, though the term 'ideal' was invented by Kummer. He studied what were later called [Kummer extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kummer_extension "Kummer extension") of [fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_(mathematics) "Field (mathematics)"): that is, extensions generated by adjoining an *n*th root to a field already containing a primitive *n*th [root of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_unity "Root of unity"). This is a significant extension of the theory of quadratic extensions, and the genus theory of [quadratic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_form "Quadratic form") (linked to the 2-torsion of the class group). As such, it is still foundational for [class field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_field_theory "Class field theory").

Kummer further conducted research in [ballistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistics "Ballistics") and, jointly with [William Rowan Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Rowan_Hamilton "William Rowan Hamilton") he investigated
[ray systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_system "Ray system").
