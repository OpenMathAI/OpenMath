**Marius Sophus Lie** ([/liː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*LEE*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); Norwegian: [[liː]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Norwegian "Help:IPA/Norwegian"); 17 December 1842 – 18 February 1899) was a [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"). He largely created the theory of [continuous symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_symmetry "Continuous symmetry") and applied it to the study of [geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry "Geometry") and [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations "Differential equations"). He also made substantial contributions to the development of algebra.

## Life and career

Marius Sophus Lie was born on 17 December 1842 in the small town of [Nordfjordeid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordfjordeid "Nordfjordeid"). He was the youngest of six children born to Lutheran pastor Johann Herman Lie and his wife, who came from a well-known [Trondheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trondheim "Trondheim") family.

He had his primary education in the south-eastern coast of Moss, before attending high school in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo "Oslo") (known then as Christiania). After graduating from high school, his ambition towards a military career was dashed when the army rejected him due to poor eyesight. He then enrolled at the [Royal Frederick University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Christiania "University of Christiania").

Sophus Lie's first mathematical work, *Repräsentation der Imaginären der Plangeometrie* (Representations of the Imaginaries in Plane Geometry), was published in 1869 by the Academy of Sciences in [Christiania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo "Oslo") and also by *[Crelle's Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crelle's_Journal "Crelle's Journal")*. That same year he received a scholarship and travelled to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin"), where he stayed from September to February 1870. There, he met [Felix Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Klein "Felix Klein") and they became close friends. When he left Berlin, Lie travelled to [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), where he was joined by Klein two months later. There, they met [Camille Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Jordan "Camille Jordan") and [Gaston Darboux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Gaston_Darboux "Jean Gaston Darboux"). But on 19 July 1870 the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War "Franco-Prussian War") began and Klein (who was [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia "Kingdom of Prussia")) had to leave France very quickly. Lie left for [Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontainebleau "Fontainebleau") where he was arrested, suspected of being a German spy, garnering him fame in Norway. He was released from prison after a month, thanks to the intervention of Darboux.

Lie obtained his PhD at the Royal Frederick University (in present-day [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo "Oslo")) in 1871 with a thesis entitled *Over en Classe geometriske Transformationer* (On a Class of Geometric Transformations). It would be described by Darboux as "one of the most handsome discoveries of modern Geometry". The next year, the Norwegian Parliament established an extraordinary professorship for him. That same year, Lie visited Klein, who was then at [Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen-Nuremberg "University of Erlangen-Nuremberg") and working on the [Erlangen program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlangen_program "Erlangen program").

In 1872, Lie spent eight months together with [Peter Ludwig Mejdell Sylow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Ludwig_Mejdell_Sylow "Peter Ludwig Mejdell Sylow"), editing and publishing the mathematical works of their countryman, [Niels Henrik Abel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Henrik_Abel "Niels Henrik Abel").

At the end of 1872, Sophus Lie proposed to Anna Birch, then eighteen years old, and they were married in 1874. The couple had three children: Marie (b. 1877), Dagny (b. 1880) and Herman (b. 1884).

From 1876, he co-edited the journal *[Archiv for Mathematik og Naturvidenskab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archiv_for_Mathematik_og_Naturvidenskab "Archiv for Mathematik og Naturvidenskab")*, together with the physician Jacob Worm-Müller, and the biologist [Georg Ossian Sars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Ossian_Sars "Georg Ossian Sars").

In 1884, [Friedrich Engel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Engel_(mathematician) "Friedrich Engel (mathematician)") arrived at Christiania to help him, with the support of [Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Klein "Felix Klein") and [Adolph Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Gustav_Adolph_Mayer "Christian Gustav Adolph Mayer") (who were both professors at [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig "University of Leipzig") by then). Engel would help Lie to write his most important treatise, *Theorie der Transformationsgruppen*, published in Leipzig in three volumes from 1888 to 1893. Decades later, Engel would also be one of the two editors of Lie's collected works.

In 1886, Lie became a professor at Leipzig, replacing Klein, who had moved to [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"). In November 1889, Lie suffered a mental breakdown and had to be hospitalized until June 1890. Subsequently he returned to his post, but over the years his [anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemia "Anemia") progressed to the point where he returned to his homeland. In May 1898 he tendered his resignation, and he left for home in September the same year. He died in 1899 at the age of 56, due to [pernicious anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernicious_anemia "Pernicious anemia"), a disease caused by impaired absorption of [vitamin B12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_B12 "Vitamin B12").

He was made Honorary Member of the [London Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Mathematical_Society "London Mathematical Society") in 1878, Corresponding Member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences") in 1892, honorary member of the [Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_and_Philosophical_Society "Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society") 1894, Foreign Member of the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London "Royal Society of London") in 1895 and foreign associate of the [National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") in 1895.

## Legacy

Lie's principal tool, and one of his greatest achievements, was the discovery that continuous [transformation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_groups "Transformation groups") (now called, after him, [Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_group "Lie group")) could be better understood by "linearizing" them, and studying the corresponding generating [vector fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_field "Vector field") (the so-called [infinitesimal generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_group#The_Lie_algebra_associated_with_a_Lie_group "Lie group")). The generators are subject to a linearized version of the [group law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_law "Group law"), now called the [commutator bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutator_bracket "Commutator bracket"), and have the structure of what is today called a [Lie algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebra "Lie algebra").

[Hermann Weyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Weyl "Hermann Weyl") used Lie's work on group theory in his papers from 1922 and 1923, and Lie groups today play a role in [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"). However, the subject of Lie groups as it is studied today is vastly different from what the research by Sophus Lie was about and "among the 19th century masters, Lie's work is *in detail* certainly the least known today".

Sophus Lie was an eager proponent in the establishment of the [Abel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Prize "Abel Prize"). Inspired by the Nansen fund named after [Fridtjof Nansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fridtjof_Nansen "Fridtjof Nansen"), and the lack of a prize for mathematics in the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize"). He gathered support for the establishment of an award for outstanding work in pure mathematics.

Lie advised many doctoral students who went on to become successful mathematicians. [Élie Cartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Élie_Cartan "Élie Cartan") became widely regarded as one of the greatest mathematicians of the 20th century. [Kazimierz Żorawski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Żorawski "Kazimierz Żorawski")'s work was proved to be of importance to a variety of fields. [Hans Frederick Blichfeldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Frederick_Blichfeldt "Hans Frederick Blichfeldt") made contributions to various fields of mathematics.
