**Leopold Kronecker** (German: [[ˈkʁoːnɛkɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German"); 7 December 1823 – 29 December 1891) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans "Germans") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who worked on [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory"), [abstract algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_algebra "Abstract algebra") and [logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_logic "Mathematical logic"), and criticized [Georg Cantor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Cantor "Georg Cantor")'s work on [set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_theory "Set theory"). [Heinrich Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Martin_Weber "Heinrich Martin Weber") quoted Kronecker
as having said, "**Die ganzen Zahlen hat der liebe Gott gemacht, alles andere ist Menschenwerk**" ("God made the integers, all else is the work of man").
Kronecker was a student and life-long friend of [Ernst Kummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Kummer "Ernst Kummer").

## Biography

Leopold Kronecker was born on 7 December 1823 in [Liegnitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liegnitz "Liegnitz"), [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia "Kingdom of Prussia") (now Legnica, [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland")) in a wealthy [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family. His parents, Isidor and Johanna (née Prausnitzer), took care of their children's education and provided them with private tutoring at home—Leopold's younger brother [Hugo Kronecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Kronecker "Hugo Kronecker") would also follow a scientific path, later becoming a notable [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiologist "Physiologist"). Kronecker then went to the Liegnitz [Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)") where he was interested in a wide range of topics including [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science "Science"), [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History "History") and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy"), while also practicing gymnastics and swimming. At the gymnasium he was taught by [Ernst Kummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Kummer "Ernst Kummer"), who noticed and encouraged the boy's interest in mathematics.

In 1841 Kronecker became a student at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin") where his interest did not immediately focus on mathematics, but rather spread over several subjects including [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy") and philosophy. He spent the summer of 1843 at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn "University of Bonn") studying astronomy and 1843–44 at the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau "University of Breslau") following his former teacher Kummer. Back in Berlin, Kronecker studied mathematics with [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gustav_Lejeune_Dirichlet "Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet") and in 1845 defended his dissertation in [algebraic number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_number_theory "Algebraic number theory") written under Dirichlet's supervision.

After obtaining his degree, Kronecker did not follow his interest in research on an academic career path. He went back to his hometown to manage a large farming estate built up by his mother's uncle, a former banker. In 1848 he married his cousin Fanny Prausnitzer, and the couple had six children. For several years Kronecker focused on business, and although he continued to study mathematics as a hobby and corresponded with Kummer, he published no mathematical results. In 1853 he wrote a memoir on the algebraic solvability of equations extending the work of [Évariste Galois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Évariste_Galois "Évariste Galois") on the [theory of equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_equations "Theory of equations").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1c/KroneckerGrab.jpg/250px-KroneckerGrab.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KroneckerGrab.jpg)

Grave of Kronecker (St Matthäus, Berlin)

Due to his business activity, Kronecker was financially comfortable, and thus he could return to Berlin in 1855 to pursue mathematics as a private scholar. Dirichlet, whose wife [Rebecka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecka_Mendelssohn "Rebecka Mendelssohn") came from the wealthy [Mendelssohn family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendelssohn_family "Mendelssohn family"), had introduced Kronecker to the Berlin elite. He became a close friend of [Karl Weierstrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Weierstrass "Karl Weierstrass"), who had recently joined the university, and his former teacher Kummer who had just taken over Dirichlet's mathematics chair. Over the following years Kronecker published numerous papers resulting from his previous years' independent research. As a result of this published research, he was elected a member of the [Berlin Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences "Prussian Academy of Sciences") in 1861.

Although he held no official university position, Kronecker had the right as a member of the Academy to hold classes at the University of Berlin and he decided to do so, starting in 1862. In 1866, when [Riemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhard_Riemann "Bernhard Riemann") died, Kronecker was offered the mathematics chair at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen") (previously held by [Carl Friedrich Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_Gauss "Carl Friedrich Gauss") and Dirichlet), but he refused, preferring to keep his position at the Academy. Only in 1883, when Kummer retired from the university, was Kronecker invited to succeed him and became an ordinary professor. Kronecker was the supervisor of [Kurt Hensel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Hensel "Kurt Hensel"), [Adolf Kneser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Kneser "Adolf Kneser"), [Mathias Lerch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathias_Lerch "Mathias Lerch"), and [Franz Mertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Mertens "Franz Mertens"), amongst others.

His philosophical view of mathematics put him in conflict with several mathematicians over the years, notably straining his relationship with Weierstrass, who almost decided to leave the university in 1888. Kronecker died on 29 December 1891 in Berlin, several months after the death of his wife. In the last year of his life, he converted to [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"). He is buried in the [Alter St Matthäus Kirchhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alter_St.-Matthäus-Kirchhof "Alter St.-Matthäus-Kirchhof") cemetery in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin")-[Schöneberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schöneberg "Schöneberg"), close to [Gustav Kirchhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Kirchhoff "Gustav Kirchhoff").

## Scientific activity

### Mathematics research

An important part of Kronecker's research focused on [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory") and [algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra "Algebra"). In an 1853 paper on the [theory of equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_equations "Theory of equations") and [Galois theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_theory "Galois theory") he formulated the [Kronecker–Weber theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker–Weber_theorem "Kronecker–Weber theorem"), without however offering a definitive proof (the theorem was proved completely much later by [David Hilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hilbert "David Hilbert")). He also introduced the [structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_generated_abelian_group "Finitely generated abelian group"). Kronecker studied [elliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_functions "Elliptic functions") and conjectured his "[liebster Jugendtraum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker's_Jugendtraum "Kronecker's Jugendtraum")" ("dearest dream of youth"), a generalization that was later put forward by Hilbert in a modified form as his [twelfth problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert's_problems "Hilbert's problems"). In an 1850 paper, *On the Solution of the General Equation of the Fifth Degree*, Kronecker solved the [quintic equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintic_equation "Quintic equation") by applying [group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_theory "Group theory") (though his solution was not in terms of radicals: that was already proven impossible by the [Abel–Ruffini theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel–Ruffini_theorem "Abel–Ruffini theorem")).

In [algebraic number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_number_theory "Algebraic number theory") Kronecker introduced the [theory of divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor_(algebraic_geometry) "Divisor (algebraic geometry)") as an alternative to [Dedekind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dedekind "Richard Dedekind")'s theory of [ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_(ring_theory) "Ideal (ring theory)"), which he did not find acceptable for philosophical reasons. Although the general adoption of Dedekind's approach led Kronecker's theory to be ignored for a long time, his divisors were found useful and were revived by several mathematicians in the 20th century.

Kronecker also contributed to the concept of [continuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_(mathematics) "Continuity (mathematics)"), reconstructing the form of [irrational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_number "Irrational number") in [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_number "Real number"). In [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis"), Kronecker rejected the formulation of a [continuous, nowhere differentiable function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous,_nowhere_differentiable_function "Continuous, nowhere differentiable function") by his colleague, [Karl Weierstrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Weierstrass "Karl Weierstrass").

Also named for Kronecker are the [Kronecker limit formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker_limit_formula "Kronecker limit formula"), [Kronecker's congruence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker's_congruence "Kronecker's congruence"), [Kronecker delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker_delta "Kronecker delta"), [Kronecker comb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker_delta#The_Kronecker_comb "Kronecker delta"), [Kronecker symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker_symbol "Kronecker symbol"), [Kronecker product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker_product "Kronecker product"), [Kronecker's method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker's_method "Kronecker's method") for factorizing polynomials, [Kronecker substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker_substitution "Kronecker substitution"), [Kronecker's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker's_theorem "Kronecker's theorem") in number theory, [Kronecker's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker's_lemma "Kronecker's lemma"), and [Eisenstein–Kronecker numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein–Kronecker_number "Eisenstein–Kronecker number").

### Philosophy of mathematics

Kronecker's [finitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitism "Finitism") made him a forerunner of [intuitionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuitionism "Intuitionism") in [foundations of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundations_of_mathematics "Foundations of mathematics").

## Honors

Kronecker was elected as a member of several academies:

- [Prussian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences "Prussian Academy of Sciences") (1861)
- [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences") (1868)
- [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") (1884).

The [25624 Kronecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25624_Kronecker "25624 Kronecker") asteroid is named after him.
