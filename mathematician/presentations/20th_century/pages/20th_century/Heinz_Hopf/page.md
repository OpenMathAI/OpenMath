**Heinz Hopf** (19 November 1894 – 3 June 1971) was a German [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who worked on the fields of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems "Dynamical systems"), [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology "Topology") and [geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry "Geometry").

## Early life and education

Hopf was born in Gräbschen, [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire") (now [Grabiszyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grabiszyn-Grabiszynek "Grabiszyn-Grabiszynek"), part of [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław "Wrocław"), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland")), the son of Elizabeth (née Kirchner) and Wilhelm Hopf. His father was born Jewish and converted to Protestantism a year after Heinz was born; his mother was from a Protestant family.

Hopf attended [Karl Mittelhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Mittelhaus?action=edit&redlink=1 "Karl Mittelhaus") higher boys' school from 1901 to 1904, and then entered the König-Wilhelm-[Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)") in Breslau. He showed mathematical talent from an early age. In 1913 he entered the [Silesian Friedrich Wilhelm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Friedrich_Wilhelm_University "Silesian Friedrich Wilhelm University") where he attended lectures by [Ernst Steinitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Steinitz "Ernst Steinitz"), [Adolf Kneser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Kneser "Adolf Kneser"), [Max Dehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Dehn "Max Dehn"), [Erhard Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erhard_Schmidt "Erhard Schmidt"), and [Rudolf Sturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Sturm "Rudolf Sturm"). When [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") broke out in 1914, Hopf eagerly enlisted. He was wounded twice and received the iron cross (first class) in 1918.

After the war Hopf continued his mathematical education in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg") (winter 1919/20 and summer 1920) and [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin") (starting in winter 1920/21). He studied under [Ludwig Bieberbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Bieberbach "Ludwig Bieberbach") and received his doctorate in 1925.

## Career

In his dissertation, *Connections between topology and metric of manifolds* ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language "German language"): *Über Zusammenhänge zwischen Topologie und Metrik von Mannigfaltigkeiten*), he proved that any simply connected complete [Riemannian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_manifold "Riemannian manifold") [3-manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-manifold "3-manifold") of constant sectional [curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curvature "Curvature") is globally [isometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometry "Isometry") to [Euclidean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_space "Euclidean space"), [spherical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_geometry "Elliptic geometry"), or [hyperbolic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_space "Hyperbolic space"). He also studied the indices of zeros of [vector fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_field "Vector field") on [hypersurfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersurface "Hypersurface"), and connected their sum to curvature. Some six months later he gave a new proof that the sum of the indices of the zeros of a vector field on a [manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold "Manifold") is independent of the choice of vector field and equal to the [Euler characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_characteristic "Euler characteristic") of the manifold. This theorem is now called the [Poincaré–Hopf theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré–Hopf_theorem "Poincaré–Hopf theorem").

Hopf spent the year after his doctorate at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"), where [David Hilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hilbert "David Hilbert"), [Richard Courant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Courant "Richard Courant"), [Carl Runge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Runge "Carl Runge"), and [Emmy Noether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Noether "Emmy Noether") were working. While there he met [Pavel Alexandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Alexandrov "Pavel Alexandrov") and began a lifelong friendship.

In 1926 Hopf moved back to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin"), where he gave a course in [combinatorial topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_topology "Combinatorial topology"). He spent the academic year 1927/28 at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") on a Rockefeller fellowship with Alexandrov. [Solomon Lefschetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Lefschetz "Solomon Lefschetz"), [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen "Oswald Veblen") and [J. W. Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Waddell_Alexander_II "James Waddell Alexander II") were all at Princeton at the time. In the summer of 1928 Hopf returned to Berlin and began working with [Pavel Alexandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Alexandrov "Pavel Alexandrov"), at the suggestion of Courant, on a book on [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology "Topology"). Three volumes were planned, but only one was finished. It was published in 1935.

In 1929, he declined a job offer from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"). In 1931 Hopf took [Hermann Weyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Weyl "Hermann Weyl")'s position at [ETH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich"), in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich "Zürich"). Hopf received another invitation to Princeton in 1940, but he declined it. Two years later, however, he was forced to file for Swiss citizenship after his property was confiscated by [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany"), his father's conversion to Christianity having failed to convince German authorities that he was an "[Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryan "Aryan")".

In 1946/47 and 1955/56 Hopf visited the United States, staying at Princeton and giving lectures at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University") and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"). He served as president of the [International Mathematical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Union "International Mathematical Union") from 1955 to 1958.

## Personal life

In October 1928 Hopf married Anja von Mickwitz (1891–1967).

## Honors and awards

He received honorary doctorates from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg "University of Freiburg"), the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester "University of Manchester"), the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris "University of Paris"), the [Free University of Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Brussels_(1834–1969) "Free University of Brussels (1834–1969)"), and the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne "University of Lausanne"). In 1949 he was elected a corresponding member of the [Heidelberg Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_Academy_of_Sciences "Heidelberg Academy of Sciences"). He was elected to the United States National Academy of Sciences in 1957, the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1961, and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1963. He was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") (ICM) in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich "Zürich") in 1932 and a Plenary Speaker at the ICM in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts") in 1950.

In memory of Hopf, [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich "ETH Zürich") awards the [Heinz Hopf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Hopf_Prize "Heinz Hopf Prize") for outstanding scientific work in the field of [pure mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_mathematics "Pure mathematics").
