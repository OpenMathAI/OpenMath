**Solomon Lefschetz** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Соломо́н Ле́фшец; 3 September 1884 – 5 October 1972) was a Russian-born American [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who did fundamental work on [algebraic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_topology "Algebraic topology"), its applications to [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry"), and the theory of non-linear [ordinary differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_differential_equation "Ordinary differential equation").

## Life

He was born in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow"), the son of Alexander Lefschetz and his wife Sarah or Vera Lifschitz, both [Russian-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-Jewish "Russian-Jewish") traders who used to travel around Europe and the Middle East (since they held [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire "Ottoman Empire") passports). Thereafter, the family moved to [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") and he was educated there in [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering "Engineering") at the [École Centrale Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Centrale_Paris "École Centrale Paris") but emigrated to the US in 1905.

He was badly injured in an industrial accident in 1907, losing both hands. He moved towards mathematics, receiving a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in algebraic geometry from [Clark University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_University "Clark University") in Worcester, Massachusetts in 1911. He then took positions at the [University of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska–Lincoln "University of Nebraska–Lincoln") and [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas "University of Kansas"), moving to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in 1924, where he was soon given a permanent position. He remained there until 1953.

In the application of topology to algebraic geometry, he followed the work of [Charles Émile Picard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Émile_Picard "Charles Émile Picard"), whom he had heard lecture in Paris at the [École Centrale Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Centrale_Paris "École Centrale Paris"). He proved theorems on the topology of [hyperplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperplane "Hyperplane") sections of [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties "Algebraic varieties"), which provide a basic inductive tool (these are now seen as allied to [Morse theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_theory "Morse theory"), though a [Lefschetz pencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefschetz_pencil "Lefschetz pencil") of hyperplane sections is a more subtle system than a Morse function because hyperplanes intersect each other). The [Picard–Lefschetz formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard–Lefschetz_theory "Picard–Lefschetz theory") in the theory of [vanishing cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanishing_cycle "Vanishing cycle") is a basic tool relating the [degeneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degeneracy_(mathematics) "Degeneracy (mathematics)") of families of varieties with 'loss' of topology, to [monodromy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monodromy "Monodromy"). He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") in 1920 in Strasbourg. His book *L'analysis situs et la géométrie algébrique* from 1924, though opaque foundationally given the current technical state of [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory "Homology theory"), was in the long term very influential (one could say that it was one of the sources for the eventual proof of the [Weil conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_conjectures "Weil conjectures"), through [SGA 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Séminaire_de_Géométrie_Algébrique_du_Bois_Marie#SGA_7 "Séminaire de Géométrie Algébrique du Bois Marie") also for the study of [Picard groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_group "Picard group") of [Zariski surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski_surface "Zariski surface")). In 1924 he was awarded the [Bôcher Memorial Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bôcher_Memorial_Prize "Bôcher Memorial Prize") for his work in [mathematical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis"). He was elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1925 and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1929.

The [Lefschetz fixed-point theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefschetz_fixed-point_theorem "Lefschetz fixed-point theorem"), now a basic result of topology, was developed by him in papers from 1923 to 1927, initially for [manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold "Manifold"). Later, with the rise of [cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory "Cohomology theory") in the 1930s, he contributed to the [intersection number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_number "Intersection number") approach (that is, in cohomological terms, the ring structure) via the [cup product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup_product "Cup product") and duality on manifolds. His work on topology was summed up in his monograph *[Algebraic Topology](http://www.ams.org/online_bks/coll27/)* (1942). From 1944 he worked on [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equation "Differential equation").

He was editor of the *[Annals of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annals_of_Mathematics "Annals of Mathematics")* from 1928 to 1958. During this time, the *Annals* became an increasingly well-known and respected journal, and Lefschetz played an important role in this.

In 1945 he travelled to Mexico for the first time, where he joined the Institute of Mathematics at the [National University of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Mexico "National University of Mexico") as a visiting professor. He visited frequently for long periods, and during 1953–1966 he spent most of his winters in Mexico City. He played an important role in the foundation of mathematics in Mexico, and sent several students back to Princeton. His students included Emilio Lluis, [José Adem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Adem "José Adem"), [Samuel Gitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Gitler_Hammer "Samuel Gitler Hammer"), [Santiago López de Medrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_López_de_Medrano "Santiago López de Medrano"), [Francisco Javier González-Acuña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Javier_González-Acuña "Francisco Javier González-Acuña") and Alberto Verjovsky.

Lefschetz came out of retirement in 1958, because of the launch of [Sputnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik "Sputnik"), to augment the mathematical component of [Glenn L. Martin Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_L._Martin_Company "Glenn L. Martin Company")'s [Research Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies "Research Institute for Advanced Studies") (RIAS) in Baltimore, Maryland. His team became the world's largest group of mathematicians devoted to research in [nonlinear differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_differential_equation "Nonlinear differential equation"). The RIAS mathematics group stimulated the growth of nonlinear differential equations through conferences and publications. He left RIAS in 1964 to form the Lefschetz Center for Dynamical Systems at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University "Brown University"), Providence, Rhode Island.
