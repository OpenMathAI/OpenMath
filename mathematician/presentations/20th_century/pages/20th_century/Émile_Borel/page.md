**Félix Édouard Justin Émile Borel** (French: [[bɔʁɛl]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 7 January 1871 – 3 February 1956) was a French [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") and [politician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politician "Politician"). As a mathematician, he was known for his founding work in the areas of [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory "Measure theory") and [probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability "Probability").

## Biography

Borel was born in [Saint-Affrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Affrique "Saint-Affrique"), [Aveyron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aveyron "Aveyron"), the son of a [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant "Protestant") pastor. He studied at the [Collège Sainte-Barbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_Sainte-Barbe "Collège Sainte-Barbe") and [Lycée Louis-le-Grand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Louis-le-Grand "Lycée Louis-le-Grand") before applying to both the [École normale supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_(Paris) "École normale supérieure (Paris)") and the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique "École Polytechnique"). He qualified in the first position for both and chose to attend the former institution in 1889. That year he also won the [concours général](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concours_général "Concours général"), an annual national mathematics competition. After graduating in 1892, he placed first in the [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation "Agrégation"), a competitive civil service examination leading to the position of professeur agrégé. His thesis, published in 1893, was titled *Sur quelques points de la théorie des fonctions* ("On some points in the theory of functions"). That year, Borel started a four-year stint as a lecturer at the [University of Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lille "University of Lille"), during which time he published 22 research papers. He returned to the École normale supérieure in 1897, and was appointed to the chair of theory of functions, which he held until 1941.

In 1901, Borel married 17-year-old Marguerite, the daughter of colleague [Paul Émile Appel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Émile_Appel "Paul Émile Appel"); she later wrote more than 30 novels under the pseudonym [Camille Marbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Marbo "Camille Marbo"). Émile Borel died in Paris on 3 February 1956.

## Work

Along with [René-Louis Baire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René-Louis_Baire "René-Louis Baire") and [Henri Lebesgue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Lebesgue "Henri Lebesgue"), Émile Borel was among the pioneers of [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_(mathematics) "Measure (mathematics)") and its application to [probability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_theory "Probability theory"). The concept of a [Borel set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_set "Borel set") is named in his honor. One of his books on probability introduced the amusing [thought experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought_experiment "Thought experiment") that entered popular culture under the name [infinite monkey theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_monkey_theorem "Infinite monkey theorem") or the like. He also published a series of papers (1921–1927) that first defined [games of strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_theory "Game theory"). [John von Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann "John von Neumann") objected to this assignment of priority in a letter to *Econometrica* published in 1953 where he asserted that Borel could not have defined games of strategy because he rejected the minimax theorem.

With the development of [statistical hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing "Statistical hypothesis testing") in the early 1900s various tests for [randomness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomness "Randomness") were proposed. Sometimes these were claimed to have some kind of general significance, but mostly they were just viewed as simple practical methods. In 1909, Borel formulated the notion that numbers picked randomly on the basis of their value are [almost always](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_always "Almost always") [normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_number "Normal number"), and with explicit constructions in terms of digits, it is quite straightforward to get numbers that are normal.

In 1913 and 1914 he bridged the gap between [hyperbolic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_geometry "Hyperbolic geometry") and [special relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_relativity "Special relativity") with expository work. For instance, his book *Introduction Géométrique à quelques Théories Physiques* described [hyperbolic rotations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_rotation "Hyperbolic rotation") as transformations that leave a hyperbola [stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_(mathematics)#Invariant_set "Invariant (mathematics)") just as a circle around a rotational center is stable.

In 1915 he was appointed head of the technical cabinet for the newly formed [Directorate of Inventions for National Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorate_of_Inventions_for_National_Defense "Directorate of Inventions for National Defense") which aimed to coordinate French laboratories for the war effort in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War").

In 1922, he founded the [Paris Institute of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Institute_of_Statistics "Paris Institute of Statistics"), the oldest French school for statistics; then in 1928 he co-founded the [Institut Henri Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Henri_Poincaré "Institut Henri Poincaré") in Paris.

## Political career

In the 1920s, 1930s, and 1940s, he was active in politics. From 1924 to 1936, he was a member of the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_(France) "Chamber of Deputies (France)"). In 1925, he was [Minister of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Naval_Ministers_of_France "List of Naval Ministers of France") in the cabinet of fellow mathematician [Paul Painlevé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Painlevé "Paul Painlevé"). During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"), he was a member of the [French Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance "French Resistance").

## Honors

Besides the *Centre Émile Borel* at the [Institut Henri Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Henri_Poincaré "Institut Henri Poincaré") in Paris
and a [crater on the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_(crater) "Borel (crater)"), the following mathematical notions are named after him:

- [Borel algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_algebra "Borel algebra")
- [Borel's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel's_lemma "Borel's lemma")
- [Borel's law of large numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel's_law_of_large_numbers "Borel's law of large numbers")
- [Borel measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_measure "Borel measure")
- [Borel–Kolmogorov paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel–Kolmogorov_paradox "Borel–Kolmogorov paradox")
- [Borel–Cantelli lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel–Cantelli_lemma "Borel–Cantelli lemma")
- [Borel–Carathéodory theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel–Carathéodory_theorem "Borel–Carathéodory theorem")
- [Heine–Borel theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heine–Borel_theorem "Heine–Borel theorem")
- [Borel determinacy theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_determinacy_theorem "Borel determinacy theorem")
- [Borel right process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_right_process "Borel right process")
- [Borel set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_set "Borel set")
- [Borel summation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_summation "Borel summation")
- [Borel distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_distribution "Borel distribution")
- [Borel's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_measure_zero_set "Strong measure zero set") about [strong measure zero sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_measure_zero_set "Strong measure zero set") (not to be confused with [Borel conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_conjecture "Borel conjecture"), named for Armand Borel).

Borel also described a [poker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poker "Poker") model that he coins *La Relance* in his 1938 book *Applications de la théorie des probabilités aux Jeux de Hasard*.

Borel was awarded the [Resistance Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_Medal "Resistance Medal") in 1950.
