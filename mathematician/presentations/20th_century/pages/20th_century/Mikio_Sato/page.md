**Mikio Sato** ([Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): 佐藤 幹夫, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization "Hepburn romanization"): *Satō Mikio*; 18 April 1928 – 9 January 2023) was a Japanese mathematician known for founding the fields of [algebraic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_analysis "Algebraic analysis"), [hyperfunctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperfunction "Hyperfunction"), and holonomic quantum fields. He was a professor at the [Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Institute_for_Mathematical_Sciences "Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences") in Kyoto.

## Biography

Born in Tokyo on 18 April 1928, Sato studied at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo "University of Tokyo"), receiving his BSc in 1952 and PhD under [Shokichi Iyanaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shokichi_Iyanaga "Shokichi Iyanaga") in 1963. He was a professor at [Osaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_University "Osaka University") and the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo "University of Tokyo") before moving to the [Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Institute_for_Mathematical_Sciences "Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences") (RIMS) attached to [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University") in 1970. He was director of RIMS from 1987 to 1991.

His disciples include [Masaki Kashiwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaki_Kashiwara "Masaki Kashiwara"), [Takahiro Kawai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takahiro_Kawai "Takahiro Kawai"), [Tetsuji Miwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetsuji_Miwa "Tetsuji Miwa"), as well as [Michio Jimbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michio_Jimbo "Michio Jimbo"), who have been called the "Sato School".

Sato died at home in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto "Kyoto") on 9 January 2023, aged 94.

## Research

Sato was known for his innovative work in a number of fields, such as [prehomogeneous vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehomogeneous_vector_space "Prehomogeneous vector space") and [Bernstein–Sato polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernstein–Sato_polynomial "Bernstein–Sato polynomial"); and particularly for his hyperfunction theory. This theory initially appeared as an extension of the ideas of [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_(mathematics) "Distribution (mathematics)") theory; it was soon connected to the [local cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_cohomology "Local cohomology") theory of [Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck "Grothendieck"), for which it was an independent realisation in terms of [sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_(mathematics) "Sheaf (mathematics)") theory. Further, it led to the theory of [microfunctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfunction "Microfunction") and [microlocal analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microlocal_analysis "Microlocal analysis") in linear [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equation "Partial differential equation") and [Fourier theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_analysis "Harmonic analysis"), such as for wave fronts, and ultimately to the current developments in [*D*-module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-module "D-module") theory. Part of Sato's hyperfunction theory is the modern theory of [holonomic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holonomic_system "Holonomic system"): PDEs overdetermined to the point of having finite-dimensional spaces of solutions ([algebraic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_analysis "Algebraic analysis")).

In theoretical physics, Sato wrote a series of papers in the 1970s with [Michio Jimbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michio_Jimbo "Michio Jimbo") and [Tetsuji Miwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetsuji_Miwa "Tetsuji Miwa") that developed the theory of holonomic quantum fields. When Sato was awarded the 2002–2003 [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Mathematics "Wolf Prize in Mathematics"), this work was described as "a far-reaching extension of the mathematical formalism underlying the two-dimensional Ising model, and introduced along the way the famous tau functions." Sato also contributed basic work to non-linear [soliton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soliton "Soliton") theory, with the use of [Grassmannians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassmannian "Grassmannian") of infinite dimension.

In [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory"), he and [John Tate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tate_(mathematician) "John Tate (mathematician)") independently posed the [Sato–Tate conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sato–Tate_conjecture "Sato–Tate conjecture") on [*L*-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-function "L-function") around 1960.

[Pierre Schapira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Schapira_(mathematician) "Pierre Schapira (mathematician)") remarked, "Looking back, 40 years later, we realize that Sato's approach to mathematics is not so different from that of Grothendieck, that Sato did have the incredible temerity to treat [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis") as [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry") and was also able to build the algebraic and geometric tools adapted to his problems."

## Awards and honours

Sato received the 1969 [Asahi Prize of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahi_Prize_of_Science "Asahi Prize of Science"), the 1976 [Japan Academy Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy_Prize_(academics) "Japan Academy Prize (academics)"), the 1984 [Person of Cultural Merits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Person_of_Cultural_Merits "Person of Cultural Merits") award of the [Japanese Education Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Education_Ministry "Japanese Education Ministry"), the 1997 [Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schock_Prize "Schock Prize"), and the 2002–2003 [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Mathematics "Wolf Prize in Mathematics").

Sato was a plenary speaker at the 1983 [International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw "Warsaw"). He was elected a foreign member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") in 1993.
