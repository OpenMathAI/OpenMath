**Charles Émile Picard** (French: [[ʃaʁl emil pikaʁ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 24 July 1856 – 11 December 1941) was a French [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"). He was elected the fifteenth member to occupy seat 1 of the [Académie française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_française "Académie française") in 1924.

## Life

He was born in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") on 24 July 1856 and educated there at the [Lycée Henri-IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Henri-IV "Lycée Henri-IV"). He then studied mathematics at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_(Paris) "École normale supérieure (Paris)").

Picard's mathematical papers, textbooks, and many popular writings exhibit an extraordinary range of interests, as well as an impressive mastery of the mathematics of his time. [Picard's little theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard's_little_theorem "Picard's little theorem") states that every nonconstant [entire function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entire_function "Entire function") takes every value in the [complex plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_plane "Complex plane"), with perhaps one exception. [Picard's great theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard's_great_theorem "Picard's great theorem") states that an [analytic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_function "Analytic function") with an [essential singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_singularity "Essential singularity") takes every value infinitely often, with perhaps one exception, in any neighborhood of the singularity. He made important contributions in the theory of [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equation "Differential equation"), including work on [Picard–Vessiot theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard–Vessiot_theory "Picard–Vessiot theory"), [Painlevé transcendents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painlevé_transcendents "Painlevé transcendents") and his introduction of a kind of [symmetry group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_group "Symmetry group") for a [linear differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_differential_equation "Linear differential equation"). He also introduced the [Picard group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_group "Picard group") in the theory of [algebraic surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_surface "Algebraic surface"), which describes the classes of [algebraic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_curve "Algebraic curve") on the surface modulo linear equivalence. In connection with his work on function theory, he was one of the first mathematicians to use the emerging ideas of [algebraic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_topology "Algebraic topology"). In addition to his theoretical work, Picard made contributions to [applied mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematics "Applied mathematics"), including the theories of [telegraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraphy "Telegraphy") and [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_(physics) "Elasticity (physics)"). His collected papers run to four volumes.

[Louis Couturat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Couturat "Louis Couturat") studied [integral calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_calculus "Integral calculus") with Picard in 1891–1892, taking detailed notes of the lectures. These notes were preserved and now are available in three cahiers from [Internet Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Archive "Internet Archive").

Like his contemporary, [Henri Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Poincaré "Henri Poincaré"), Picard was much concerned with the training of mathematics, physics, and engineering students.
He wrote a classic textbook on [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis") and one of the first textbooks on the [theory of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_relativity "Theory of relativity"). Picard's popular writings include biographies of many leading French mathematicians, including his father in law, [Charles Hermite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hermite "Charles Hermite").

Picard was an International Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), an International Member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"), and an International Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society").

## Family

In 1881 he married Marie, the daughter of [Charles Hermite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hermite "Charles Hermite").

## Society

Charles Émile Picard was one of the founder members in 1930 of the [Society of Friends of André-Marie Ampère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Friends_of_André-Marie_Ampère "Society of Friends of André-Marie Ampère") which was created to develop the first science museum in France, the [Ampère Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampère_Museum "Ampère Museum") close to [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon "Lyon").
