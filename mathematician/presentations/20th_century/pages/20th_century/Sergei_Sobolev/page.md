Prof **Sergei Lvovich Sobolev**, [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE "FRSE") ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language "Russian language"): Серге́й Льво́вич Со́болев; 6 October 1908 –  3 January 1989) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") working in [mathematical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis") and [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equation "Partial differential equation").

Sobolev introduced notions that are now fundamental for several areas of mathematics. [Sobolev spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobolev_space "Sobolev space") can be defined by some growth conditions on the [Fourier transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_transform "Fourier transform"). They and their embedding theorems are an important subject in [functional analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_analysis "Functional analysis"). [Generalized functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_function "Generalized function") (later known as [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_(mathematics) "Distribution (mathematics)")) were first introduced by Sobolev in 1935 for [weak solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_solution "Weak solution"), and further developed by [Laurent Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Schwartz "Laurent Schwartz"). Sobolev abstracted the classical notion of [differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative "Derivative"), so expanding the range of application of the technique of Newton and Leibniz. The theory of distributions is considered now as the calculus of the modern epoch.

## Life

He was born in [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg "Saint Petersburg") as the son of Lev Aleksandrovich Sobolev, a lawyer, and his wife, Natalya Georgievna. His city was renamed Petrograd in his youth and then Leningrad in 1924.

Sobolev studied mathematics at [Leningrad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University "Saint Petersburg State University") and graduated in 1929, having studied under Professor [Nikolai Günther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Günther "Nikolai Günther"). After graduation, he worked with [Vladimir Smirnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Smirnov_(mathematician) "Vladimir Smirnov (mathematician)"), whom he considered as his second teacher. He worked in Leningrad from 1932 and at the [Steklov Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Institute_of_Mathematics "Steklov Institute of Mathematics") in Moscow from 1934. He headed the institute in evacuation to [Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan "Kazan") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). He was a [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University "Moscow State University") professor of mathematics from 1935 to 1957 and also a deputy director of the [Institute for Atomic Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurchatov_Institute "Kurchatov Institute") from 1943 to 1957, where he participated in the [Soviet atomic bomb project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_atomic_bomb_project "Soviet atomic bomb project"). In 1958, he led with [Nikolay Brusentsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Brusentsov "Nikolay Brusentsov") the development of the [ternary computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternary_computer "Ternary computer") [Setun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setun "Setun").

In 1956, Sobolev joined a number of scientists in proposing a large-scale scientific and educational initiative for the Eastern parts of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), which resulted in the creation of the Siberian Division of the [Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union "Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union"). He was the founder and first director of the Institute of Mathematics at [Akademgorodok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akademgorodok "Akademgorodok") near [Novosibirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk "Novosibirsk"), which was later to bear his name, and played an important role in the establishment and development of [Novosibirsk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk_State_University "Novosibirsk State University"). In 1962, he called for a reform of the Soviet education system.

He died in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow").

## Family

In 1930 he married Ariadna Dmitrievna.

## Publications

In 1955 he co-wrote *[The Main Features of Cybernetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Main_Features_of_Cybernetics "The Main Features of Cybernetics")* with [Alexey Lyapunov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Lyapunov "Alexey Lyapunov") and [Anatoly Kitov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Kitov "Anatoly Kitov"), which was published in *[Problems of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problems_of_Philosophy_(magazine) "Problems of Philosophy (magazine)")*.
