**Carl Ludwig Siegel** (31 December 1896 – 4 April 1981) was a German mathematician specialising in [analytic number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_number_theory "Analytic number theory"). He is known for, amongst other things, his contributions to the [Thue–Siegel–Roth theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thue–Siegel–Roth_theorem "Thue–Siegel–Roth theorem") in [Diophantine approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_approximation "Diophantine approximation"), Siegel's method, [Siegel's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegel's_lemma "Siegel's lemma") and the [Siegel mass formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith–Minkowski–Siegel_mass_formula "Smith–Minkowski–Siegel mass formula") for quadratic forms. He has been named one of the most important mathematicians of the 20th century.

[André Weil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Weil "André Weil"), without hesitation, named Siegel as the greatest mathematician of the first half of the 20th century. [Atle Selberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atle_Selberg "Atle Selberg") said of Siegel and his work:

> He was in some ways, perhaps, the most impressive mathematician I have met. I would say, in a way, devastatingly so. The things that Siegel tended to do were usually things that seemed impossible. Also after they were done, they still seemed almost impossible.

## Biography

Siegel was born in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin"), where he enrolled at the [Humboldt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University "Humboldt University") in Berlin in 1915 as a student in mathematics, [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy"), and [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics"). Amongst his teachers were [Max Planck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck "Max Planck") and [Ferdinand Georg Frobenius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Georg_Frobenius "Ferdinand Georg Frobenius"), whose influence made the young Siegel abandon astronomy and turn towards number theory instead. His best-known student was [Jürgen Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jürgen_Moser "Jürgen Moser"), one of the founders of [KAM theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAM_theory "KAM theory") ([Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Kolmogorov "Andrey Kolmogorov")–[Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Arnold "Vladimir Arnold")–Moser), which lies at the foundations of [chaos theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_theory "Chaos theory"). Other notable students were [Kurt Mahler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Mahler "Kurt Mahler"), the number theorist, and [Hel Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hel_Braun "Hel Braun") who became one of the few female full professors in mathematics in Germany.

Siegel was an [antimilitarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimilitarist "Antimilitarist"), and in 1917, during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") he was committed to a psychiatric institute as a [conscientious objector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objector "Conscientious objector"). According to his own words, he withstood the experience only because of his support from [Edmund Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Landau "Edmund Landau"), whose father had a clinic in the neighborhood. After the end of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), he enrolled at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"), studying under Landau, who was his doctoral thesis supervisor (PhD in 1920). He stayed in Göttingen as a teaching and research assistant; many of his groundbreaking results were published during this period. In 1922, he was appointed professor at the [Goethe University Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt "Goethe University Frankfurt") as the successor of [Arthur Moritz Schönflies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Moritz_Schönflies "Arthur Moritz Schönflies"). Siegel, who was deeply opposed to Nazism, was a close friend of the [docents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent "Docent") [Ernst Hellinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Hellinger "Ernst Hellinger") and [Max Dehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Dehn "Max Dehn") and used his influence to help them. This attitude prevented Siegel's appointment as a successor to the chair of [Constantin Carathéodory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Carathéodory "Constantin Carathéodory") in Munich. In Frankfurt he took part with Dehn, Hellinger, [Paul Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Epstein "Paul Epstein"), and others in a seminar on the history of mathematics, which was conducted at the highest level. In the seminar they read only original sources. Siegel's reminiscences about the time before World War II are in an essay in his collected works.

In 1936 he was a Plenary Speaker at the [ICM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Mathematicians "International Congress of Mathematicians") in Oslo. In 1938, he returned to [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen "Göttingen") before emigrating in 1940 via [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") to the United States, where he joined the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey "Princeton, New Jersey"), where he had already spent a [sabbatical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbatical "Sabbatical") in 1935. He returned to Göttingen after [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), when he accepted a post as professor in 1951, which he kept until his retirement in 1959. In 1968 he was elected a foreign associate of the U.S. National Academy of Sciences.

## Career

Siegel's work on [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory"), [diophantine equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_equation "Diophantine equation"), and [celestial mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_mechanics "Celestial mechanics") in particular won him numerous honours. In 1978, he was awarded the first [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Mathematics "Wolf Prize in Mathematics"), one of the most prestigious in the field. When the prize committee decided to select the greatest living mathematician, the discussion centered around Siegel and [Israel Gelfand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Gelfand "Israel Gelfand") as the leading candidates. The prize was ultimately split between them.

Siegel's work spans [analytic number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_number_theory "Analytic number theory"); and his [theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theorem "Theorem") on the [finiteness of the integer points of curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finiteness_of_the_integer_points_of_curves "Finiteness of the integer points of curves"), for [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_(mathematics) "Genus (mathematics)") > 0, with the case of genus 1 an earlier paper in his collected works, is historically important as a major general result on diophantine equations, when the field was essentially undeveloped. The methods included those Weil used in his thesis on finite generation of rational points on abelian varieties (a thesis much guided by Siegel) and versions of the famous Thue-Siegel-Roth Theorem on diophantine approximation. has a detailed discussion of the history. He worked on [L-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-function "L-function"), discovering the (presumed illusory) [Siegel zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegel_zero "Siegel zero") phenomenon. His work, derived from the [Hardy–Littlewood circle method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Littlewood_circle_method "Hardy–Littlewood circle method") on [quadratic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_form "Quadratic form"), appeared in the later, [adele group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adele_group "Adele group") theories encompassing the use of [theta-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theta-function "Theta-function"). The [Siegel modular varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegel_modular_variety "Siegel modular variety"), which describe [Siegel modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegel_modular_form "Siegel modular form"), are recognised as part of the [moduli theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_theory "Moduli theory") of [abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties "Abelian varieties"). In all this work the structural implications of analytic methods show through.

In the early 1970s Weil gave a series of seminars on the history of number theory prior to the 20th century and he remarked that Siegel once told him that when the first person discovered the simplest case of [Faulhaber's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faulhaber's_formula "Faulhaber's formula") then, in Siegel's words, "Es gefiel dem lieben Gott." (It pleased the dear Lord.) Siegel was a profound student of the history of mathematics and put his studies to good use in such works as the [Riemann–Siegel formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann–Siegel_formula "Riemann–Siegel formula"), which Siegel found while reading through Riemann's unpublished papers.
