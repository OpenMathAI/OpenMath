Richard Swineshead

**Richard Swineshead** (also **Suisset**, **Suiseth**, etc.; fl. c. 1340 – 1354) was an English mathematician, [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician "Logician"), and [natural philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosopher "Natural philosopher"). He was perhaps the greatest of the [Oxford Calculators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Calculators "Oxford Calculators") of [Merton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College "Merton College"), where he was a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow "Fellow") certainly by 1344 and possibly by 1340. His magnum opus was a series of treatises known as the *Liber calculationum* ("Book of Calculations"), written c. 1350, which earned him the nickname of **The Calculator**.

*Calculator*, 1520

[Robert Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Burton_(scholar) "Robert Burton (scholar)") (d. 1640) wrote in *[The Anatomy of Melancholy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Anatomy_of_Melancholy "The Anatomy of Melancholy")* that "[Scaliger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar_Scaliger "Julius Caesar Scaliger") and [Cardan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerolamo_Cardano "Gerolamo Cardano") admire Suisset the calculator, *qui pene modum excessit humani ingenii* [whose talents were almost superhuman]".

[Gottfried Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Leibniz "Gottfried Leibniz") wrote in a letter of 1714: "Il y a eu autrefois un Suisse, qui avoit mathématisé dans la Scholastique: ses Ouvrages sont peu connus; mais ce que j'en ai vu m'a paru profond et considérable." ("There was once a Suisse, who did mathematics belonging to [scholasticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism "Scholasticism"); his works are little known, but what I have seen of them seemed to me profound and relevant.") Leibniz had a copy of one of Swineshead's treatises made from an edition in the [Bibliothèque du Roi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliothèque_du_Roi "Bibliothèque du Roi") in Paris and credited Swineshead as "the man 'who introduced mathematics into scholastic philosophy.'"
