Immanuel Bonfils

**Immanuel ben Jacob Bonfils** (c. 1300 – 1377) was a [French-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French-Jewish "French-Jewish") mathematician and astronomer in [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval "Medieval") times who flourished from 1340 to 1377, a [rabbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbi "Rabbi") who was a pioneer of exponential calculus and is credited with inventing the system of [decimal fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_fractions "Decimal fractions"). He taught astronomy and mathematics in [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange,_Vaucluse "Orange, Vaucluse") and later lived in [Tarascon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarascon "Tarascon"), both towns in the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire "Holy Roman Empire") that are now part of modern-day [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France"). Bonfils studied the works of [Gersonides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gersonides "Gersonides") (Levi ben Gershom), the father of modern trigonometry, and [Al-Battani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Battani "Al-Battani") and even taught at the academy founded by Gersonides in Orange.

Bonfils preceded any attempt at a European decimal system by 150 years, publishing the treatise *Method of Division by Rabbi Immanuel and Other Topics* ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): דרך חילוק) on the general theory of [decimal fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_fraction "Decimal fraction") around 1350. This was a forerunner to [Simon Stevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Stevin "Simon Stevin"), the first to widely distribute publications on this topic, and employed decimal notation for integers, fractions, and both positive and negative exponents.

While living in Tarascon in 1365, Bonfils published the work for which he would become best known, *Sepher Shesh Kenaphayim* (*Book of Six Wings*) ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): שש כנפים), a manuscript on [eclipses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse "Eclipse") that featured astronomical tables predicting future solar and lunar positions (divided into six parts). The book included data for every important date on the [Jewish calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_calendar "Jewish calendar") and even correction factors necessary for those who lived as far away as [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople "Constantinople"). Breaking the tables into six parts was an allusion to the six wings of the [seraphim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seraphim "Seraphim") as mentioned in the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible "Bible") in [Isaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah "Book of Isaiah") 6:2, earning Bonfils the nickname master of the wings.

For 300 years, Bonfils' calculations which were extensively used by sailors and explorers well into the 17th century. The book was translated from [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew "Hebrew") into [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") in 1406 by [Johannes Lucae e Camerino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Lucae_e_Camerino?action=edit&redlink=1 "Johannes Lucae e Camerino") and into [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") in 1435 by [Michael Chrysokokkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Chrysokokkes?action=edit&redlink=1 "Michael Chrysokokkes"). The book inspired Chemist [George Sarton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Sarton "George Sarton") to publish his own version of *Six Wings* nearly 600 years later. Bonfils translated a number of books from Latin to Hebrew. He also wrote a treatise on the relationship between the diameter and [circumference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumference "Circumference") of a circle and methods of calculating square roots.

## Works

- Bonfils, Immanuel (1365), *The Wings of Eagles*, [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): שש כנפים, in six books. Other name: *Book of Six Wings*, [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): שש כנפים. The main astronomical work of Bonfils.
- Bonfils, Immanuel (c. 1350), *The Invention of the Decimal Fractions and the Application of the Exponential Calculus by Immanuel Bonfils of Tarascon*
- Bonfils, Immanuel (c. 1350), *Method of Division by Rabbi Immanuel and Other Topics*, [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"): דרך חילוק, a course of decimal arithmetics, including decimal fractions.
