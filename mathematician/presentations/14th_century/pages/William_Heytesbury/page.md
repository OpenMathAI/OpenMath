William of Heytesbury

**William of Heytesbury**, or **William Heytesbury**, or **William de Heytisbury**, called in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin")  **Guglielmus Hentisberus** or **Tisberus** (c. 1313 – 1372/1373), was an [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England "Kingdom of England") philosopher and logician, best known as one of the [Oxford Calculators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Calculators "Oxford Calculators") of [Merton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College "Merton College"), [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford"), where he was a fellow.

## Life

Heytesbury had become a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow "Fellow") of Merton by 1330. In his work he applied logical techniques to the problems of [divisibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitely_divisible "Infinitely divisible"), the [continuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuum_(set_theory) "Continuum (set theory)"), and [kinematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinematics "Kinematics"). His *[magnum opus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masterpiece "Masterpiece")* was the *Regulae solvendi sophismata* (*Rules for Solving Sophisms*), written about 1335.

He was [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chancellors_of_the_University_of_Oxford "List of Chancellors of the University of Oxford") of the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") for the year 1371 to 1372.

  - 2. *On knowing and doubting*
