Narayana Pandita (mathematician)

**Nārāyaṇa Paṇḍita** ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language "Sanskrit language"): नारायण पण्डित) (1340–1400) was an Indian [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"). [Plofker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Plofker "Kim Plofker") writes that his texts were the most significant Sanskrit mathematics treatises after those of [Bhaskara II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaskara_II "Bhaskara II"), other than the [Kerala school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_school_of_astronomy_and_mathematics "Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics"). He wrote the *[Ganita Kaumudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganita_Kaumudi "Ganita Kaumudi")* (lit. "Moonlight of mathematics") in 1356 about mathematical operations. The work anticipated many developments in [combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorics "Combinatorics").

## Life and Works

About his life, the most that is known is that:

> His father’s name was Nṛsiṃha or Narasiṃha, and the distribution of the manuscripts of his works suggests that he may have lived and worked in the northern half of India.

Narayana Pandit wrote two works, an arithmetical treatise called *Ganita Kaumudi* and an [algebraic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra "Algebra") treatise called *Bijaganita Vatamsa*. Narayana is also thought to be the author of an elaborate commentary of [Bhaskara II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaskara_II "Bhaskara II")'s [Lilavati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilavati "Lilavati"), titled *Karmapradipika* (or *Karma-Paddhati*). Although the *Karmapradipika* contains little original work, it contains seven different methods for squaring numbers, a contribution that is wholly original to the author, as well as contributions to algebra and [magic squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_square "Magic square").

Narayana's other major works contain a variety of mathematical developments, including a rule to calculate approximate values of square roots, investigations into the second order [indeterminate equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminate_equation "Indeterminate equation") *nq*2 + 1 = *p*2 ([Pell's equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pell's_equation "Pell's equation")), solutions of indeterminate [higher-order equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_of_a_polynomial "Degree of a polynomial"), mathematical operations with [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_(number) "0 (number)"), several [geometrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry "Geometry") rules, methods of integer factorization, and a discussion of magic squares and similar figures. Narayana has also made contributions to the topic of [cyclic quadrilaterals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_quadrilateral "Cyclic quadrilateral").
Narayana is also credited with developing a method for [systematic generation of all permutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation#Generation_in_lexicographic_order "Permutation") of a given sequence.

## Narayana's cows sequence

In his *Ganita Kaumudi* Narayana proposed the following problem on a herd of cows and calves:

> A cow produces one calf every year. Beginning in its fourth year, each calf produces one calf at the beginning of each year. How many cows and calves are there altogether after 20 years?

Translated into the modern mathematical language of [recurrence sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurrence_relation "Recurrence relation"):

:   N*n* = N*n*-1 + N*n*-3 for *n* > 2,

with initial values

:   N0 = N1 = N2 = 1.

The first few terms are 1, 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 13, 19, 28, 41, 60, 88,... (sequence [A000930](https://oeis.org/A000930 "oeis:A000930") in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences "On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences")).
The limit ratio between consecutive terms is the [supergolden ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergolden_ratio "Supergolden ratio").

The recurrence on N*n*-1 + N*n*-*k* puts Narayana's cows and the supergolden ratio as the next in a series of sequences starting with *k* = 1 the [powers of two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powers_of_two "Powers of two") with 2, and *k* = 2 the [Fibonacci sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_sequence "Fibonacci sequence") with the [golden ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_ratio "Golden ratio"), which are used in computing to make [buddy allocators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_memory_allocation "Buddy memory allocation").
