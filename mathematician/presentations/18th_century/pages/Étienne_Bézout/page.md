**Étienne Bézout** (French: [[bezu]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 31 March 1730 – 27 September 1783) was a French [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") who was born in [Nemours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemours "Nemours"), [Seine-et-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-et-Marne "Seine-et-Marne"), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France"), and died in [Avon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avon,_Seine-et-Marne "Avon, Seine-et-Marne") (near [Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontainebleau "Fontainebleau")), France.

## Early life

Étienne Bézout was the second son of Pierre Bézout and Jeanne-Hélène Filz. His family was well-connected politically, and both his father and grandfather had served as district [magistrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistrate "Magistrate"). Bézout, who was deeply influenced by [Leonhard Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonhard_Euler "Leonhard Euler") at an early age, chose to pursue a career in [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"). At the age of 19 (in 1758), Bézout was elected an adjoint in mechanics of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences"). He got married at the age of 24.

## Career

Bézout's marriage at the age of 24 led him to accept the position as a mathematics teacher and examiner of the [Gardes de la Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardes_de_la_Marine "Gardes de la Marine") in 1763, the post being offered by the [Duke of Choiseul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étienne_François_de_Choiseul,_Duke_of_Choiseul "Étienne François de Choiseul, Duke of Choiseul"). One important task that he was given in this role was to compose a textbook specially designed for teaching mathematics to the naval cadets. One of his most famous works came from this task: the *Cours de mathématiques à l'usage des Gardes du Pavillon et de la Marine,* a four volume work which appeared in 1764-1767*.* In 1768, Camus, who was the examiner for the artillery, died. Bézout was appointed to succeed him becoming examiner of the Corps d'Artillerie. Then, he began work on another mathematics textbook and as a result he produced *Cours complet de mathématiques à l'usage de la marine et de l'artillerie*, a six volume work which appeared between 1770 and 1782. In 1768, Bézout was promoted to associé in mechanics at the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences") in 1768 and then further promoted to pensionnaire in 1770. Bézout is also acknowledged for his work on algebra, despite being much occupied with his teaching duties, leaving him with relatively little time for algebra research. He published many papers exploring algebraic equations, [theory of equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_equations "Theory of equations") and [elimination theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_theory "Elimination theory"), like the *Sur plusieurs classes d'équations de tous les degrés qui admettent une solution algébrique,* which examined how a single equation in a single unknown could be "attacked" by writing it as two equations in two unknowns. Many of Bézout's papers regarding [theory of equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_equations "Theory of equations") were gathered and published in possibly his most famous book, the *Théorie générale des équations algébriques*, published at [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") in 1779, which in particular contained much new and valuable matter on the [theory of elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_theory "Elimination theory") and symmetrical functions of the [roots of an equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_an_equation "Root of an equation"): he used [determinants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Determinant "Determinant") in a paper in the *Histoire de l'académie royale*, 1764, but did not treat the general theory.

## Legacy

After his death, a statue was erected in his birth town, [Nemours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemours "Nemours"), to commemorate his achievements.

In 2000, the [minor planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_planet "Minor planet") [17285 Bezout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meanings_of_minor_planet_names:_17001–18000#285 "Meanings of minor planet names: 17001–18000") was named after him.
