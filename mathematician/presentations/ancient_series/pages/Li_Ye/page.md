Li Ye (mathematician)

**Li Ye** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 李冶; [Wade–Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade–Giles "Wade–Giles"): ****Li Yeh****; 1192–1279), born **Li Zhi** (Chinese: 李治), [courtesy name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtesy_name "Courtesy name") **Li Jingzhai** (Chinese: 李敬斋), was a Chinese mathematician, politician, and writer who published and improved the [tian yuan shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tian_yuan_shu "Tian yuan shu") method for solving [polynomial equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_equation "Polynomial equation") of one variable. Along with the 4th-century Chinese astronomer [Yu Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yu_Xi "Yu Xi"), Li Ye proposed the idea of a [spherical Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_Earth "Spherical Earth") instead of a [flat one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_Earth "Flat Earth") before the advances of European science in the 17th century.

## Name

Li Ye was born Li Zhi, but later changed his name to Li Ye to [avoid confusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_taboo "Naming taboo") with the [third Tang emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Gaozong_of_Tang "Emperor Gaozong of Tang") who was also named Li Zhi, removing one stroke from his original name to change the character. His name is also sometimes written as Li Chih or Li Yeh. His literary name was Renqing ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 仁卿; [Wade–Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade–Giles "Wade–Giles"): *Jen-ch’ing*) and his [appellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art-name "Art-name") was Jingzhai ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 敬斋; [Wade–Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade–Giles "Wade–Giles"): *Ching-chai*).

## Life

Li Ye was born in [Daxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daxing_District "Daxing District") (now Beijing). His father was a secretary to an officer in the [Jurchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurchen_people "Jurchen people") army. Li passed the [civil service examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_examination "Imperial examination") in 1230 at the age of 38, and was administrative prefect of Jun prefecture in [Henan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henan "Henan") province until the [Mongol invasion in 1233](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol–Jin_Dynasty_War "Mongol–Jin Dynasty War"). He then lived in poverty in the mountainous [Shanxi province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanxi_province "Shanxi province"). In 1248 he finished his most known work *Ceyuan haijing* (測圓海鏡, *Sea mirror of circle measurements*). Li then returned to [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei "Hebei").

In 1257, [Kublai Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kublai_Khan "Kublai Khan"), grandson of [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan "Genghis Khan"), ordered Li to give advice on science. In 1259, Li completed *Yigu yanduan* (益古演段, *New steps in computation*), also a mathematics text. After becoming [Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_(title) "Khan (title)"), Kublai twice offered Li government positions, but Li was too old and in ill health. In 1264, Li finally accepted a position at the [Hanlin Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanlin_Academy "Hanlin Academy"), writing official histories. However, he had a political fallout and resigned after a few months, again citing ill health. He spent his final years teaching at his home near Feng Lung mountain in Yuan, [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei "Hebei"). Li told his son to burn all of his books except for *Sea mirror of circle measurements*.

## Mathematics

### Ceyuan haijing

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/%E5%9C%86%E5%9F%8E%E5%9B%BE%E5%BC%8F.jpg/330px-%E5%9C%86%E5%9F%8E%E5%9B%BE%E5%BC%8F.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:圆城图式.jpg)

The master figure in *Sea mirror of circle measurements*, that all the problems use. It shows a circular city wall, inscribed in a right triangle and a square.

[Ceyuan haijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceyuan_haijing "Ceyuan haijing") (*Sea mirror of circle measurements*) is a collection of 170 problems, all related to the same example of a circular city wall inscribed in a [right triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_triangle "Right triangle") and a square. They often involve two people who walk on straight lines until they can see each other, meet or reach a tree in a certain spot. The purpose of book was to study intricate geometrical relations with algebra and provide solutions to equations.

Many of the problems are solved by polynomial equations, which are represented using a method called [tian yuan shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tian_yuan_shu "Tian yuan shu"), "coefficient array method" or literally "method of the celestial unknown". The method was known before him in some form. It is a positional system of [rod numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_numeral "Rod numeral") to represent [polynomial equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_equation "Polynomial equation").

For example, 2x2 + 18x − 316 = 0 is represented as

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d6/Polynomial_equation_with_rod_numerals.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Polynomial_equation_with_rod_numerals.png) which is equal to [![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/be/Polynomial_equation_in_tian_yuan_shu_with_arabic_numerals.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Polynomial_equation_in_tian_yuan_shu_with_arabic_numerals.png) in Arabic Numbers.

The 元 (*yuan*) denotes the unknown x, so the numerals on that line mean 18x. The line below is the constant term (-316) and the line above is the coefficient of the quadratic (x2) term. The system accommodates arbitrarily high exponents of the unknown by adding more lines on top and negative exponents by adding lines below the constant term. Decimals can also be represented. Later, the line order was reversed so that the first line is the lowest exponent.

Li does not explain how to solve equations in general, but shows it with the example problems. Most of the equations can be reduced to the second or sometimes third order. It is often assumed that he used methods similar to [Ruffini's rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruffini's_rule "Ruffini's rule") and [Horner scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horner_scheme "Horner scheme").

### Yigu yanduan

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/%E7%9B%8A%E5%8F%A4%E7%AC%AC%E5%85%AB%E9%97%AE.jpg/330px-%E7%9B%8A%E5%8F%A4%E7%AC%AC%E5%85%AB%E9%97%AE.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:益古第八问.jpg)

Problem 8 in Yigu yanduan

[Yigu yanduan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yigu_yanduan "Yigu yanduan") (*New steps in computation*) is a work of more basic mathematics written soon after Li Ye completed Ceyuan haijing, and was probably written to help students who could not understand *Sea mirror of circle measurements*. Yigu yanduan consists of three volumes dedicated to solving geometrical problems on two tracks, through [Tian yuan shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tian_yuan_shu "Tian yuan shu") and geometry. It also contained algebraic problems, but with slightly different notations.

### Astronomy and shape of the earth

The *huntian* (渾天) theory of the [celestial sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_sphere "Celestial sphere") stipulated that the earth was [flat and square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_Earth "Flat Earth"), while the heavens were spherical in shape, along with celestial bodies such as the sun and moon (described by 1st-century AD polymathic scientist and statesman [Zhang Heng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Heng "Zhang Heng") like a [crossbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossbow "Crossbow") bullet and ball, respectively). However, the idea of a flat earth was criticized by the Jin dynasty astronomer [Yu Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yu_Xi "Yu Xi") (fl. 307-345 AD), who suggested a rounded shape as an alternative. In his *Jingzhai gu zhin zhu* (敬齋古今注), Li Ye echoed Yu's idea that the [Earth was spherical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_Earth "Spherical Earth"), similar in shape to the heavens but smaller in size, arguing that it could not be square since that would hinder the movement of the heavens and celestial bodies.

However, the idea of a spherical earth was not accepted in mainstream Chinese science and [cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cartography "Chinese cartography") until the 17th century during the late [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty") and early [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty") periods, with the advent of evidence of European [circumnavigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumnavigation "Circumnavigation") of the globe. The flat Earth theory in Chinese science was finally overturned in the 17th century. [Jesuits in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit_China_Missions "Jesuit China Missions") also introduced the spherical Earth model advanced by [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_astronomy "Ancient Greek astronomy") such as [Philolaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philolaus "Philolaus") and [Eratosthenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eratosthenes "Eratosthenes") and presented in [world maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_map "World map") such as [Matteo Ricci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Ricci "Matteo Ricci")'s *[Kunyu Wanguo Quantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunyu_Wanguo_Quantu "Kunyu Wanguo Quantu")* published in [Ming-dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty "Ming dynasty") China in 1602.
