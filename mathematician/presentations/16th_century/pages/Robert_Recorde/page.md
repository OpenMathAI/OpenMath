**Robert Recorde** (c. 1510 – 1558) was a [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_people "Welsh people") physician and mathematician. He invented the [equals sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equals_sign "Equals sign") (=) and also introduced the pre-existing [plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plus_sign "Plus sign") (+) and [minus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minus_sign "Minus sign") (−) signs to [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") speakers in 1557.

## Biography

Born around 1510, Robert Recorde was the second and last son of Thomas and Rose Recorde of [Tenby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenby "Tenby"), [Pembrokeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembrokeshire "Pembrokeshire"), in [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales "Wales").

Recorde entered the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") about 1525, and was elected a Fellow of [All Souls College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Souls_College,_Oxford "All Souls College, Oxford") there in 1531. Having adopted medicine as a profession, he went to the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") to take the degree of M.D. in 1545. He afterwards returned to Oxford, where he publicly taught mathematics, as he had done prior to going to Cambridge. He invented the "equals" sign, which consists of two horizontal parallel lines, stating that no two things can be more equal. It appears that he afterwards went to London, and acted as physician to King [Edward VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VI "Edward VI") and to Queen [Mary I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_I "Mary I"), to whom some of his books are dedicated. He was also controller of the [Royal Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Mint "Royal Mint") and served as Comptroller of Mines and Monies in Ireland. After being sued for defamation by a political enemy, he was arrested for debt and died in the [King's Bench Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Bench_Prison "King's Bench Prison"), [Southwark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwark "Southwark"), by the middle of June 1558.

## Publications

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/First_Equation_Ever.png/500px-First_Equation_Ever.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:First_Equation_Ever.png)

The first known equation, equivalent to 14*x* + 15 = 71 in modern notation, from *[The Whetstone of Witte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Whetstone_of_Witte "The Whetstone of Witte")*. (The solution is *x* = 4)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2b/Recorde_-_The_Whetstone_of_Witte_-_equals.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Recorde_-_The_Whetstone_of_Witte_-_equals.jpg)

Recorde's introduction of the [equals sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equals_sign "Equals sign") in *The Whetstone of Witte*, "to avoid tedious repetition".

Recorde published several works upon mathematical and medical subjects, chiefly in the form of dialogue between master and scholar, such as the following:

- *[The Grounde of Artes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ground_of_Arts "The Ground of Arts"), teachings the Worke and Practise, of Arithmeticke, both in whole numbers and fractions* (1543), the first English language book on [algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra "Algebra").
- *The Pathway to Knowledge, containing the First Principles of Geometry ... bothe for the use of Instrumentes Geometricall and Astronomicall, and also for Projection of Plattes* (London, 1551)
- *The Castle of Knowledge, containing the Explication of the Sphere both Celestiall and Materiall, etc.* (London, 1556) A book explaining [Ptolemaic astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy "Ptolemy") while mentioning the [Copernican heliocentric model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernican_heliocentrism "Copernican heliocentrism") in passing.
- *[The Whetstone of Witte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Whetstone_of_Witte "The Whetstone of Witte"), whiche is the seconde parte of Arithmeteke: containing thextraction of rootes; the cossike practise, with the rule of equation; and the workes of Surde Nombers* (London, 1557). This was the book in which the [equals sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equals_sign "Equals sign") was introduced within a printed edition. With the publication of this book Recorde is credited with introducing algebra into the Island of Britain with a systematic notation.
- A medical work, *The Urinal of Physick* (1548), frequently reprinted.

Most of those works were written in the form of a [catechism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechism "Catechism").
Several books whose authors are unknown have been attributed to him: *Cosmographiae isagoge*, *De Arte faciendi Horologium* and *De Usu Globorum et de Statu temporum*.
