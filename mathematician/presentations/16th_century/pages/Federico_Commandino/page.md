Federico Commandino

**Federico Commandino** (1509 – 5 September 1575) was an Italian [humanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism "Humanism") and [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician").

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Federico_Commandino.jpg/250px-Federico_Commandino.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Federico_Commandino.jpg)

Federico Commandino

Born in [Urbino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbino "Urbino"), he studied at [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua "Padua") and then at [Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara "Ferrara"), where he received his doctorate in [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine "Medicine") under [Antonio Musa Brassavola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Musa_Brassavola "Antonio Musa Brassavola"). He had numerous patrons throughout his life. Initially, aided by Grassi, the bishop of Viterbo, he then came under the patronage of Pope [Clement VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_VIII "Clement VIII"). In [Urbino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbino "Urbino"), he was sponsored by [Guidobaldo II della Rovere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guidobaldo_II_della_Rovere "Guidobaldo II della Rovere"), but then came to Rome with cardinal [Ranuccio Farnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranuccio_Farnese_(cardinal) "Ranuccio Farnese (cardinal)"). In Rome, he was patronized by [Cardinal Cervini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Marcellus_II "Pope Marcellus II"), who served briefly as Pope. Lured back to Urbino by [Francesco Maria II della Rovere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maria_II_della_Rovere "Francesco Maria II della Rovere"). In Urbino, he putatively met [John Dee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dee "John Dee"), and corresponded with the scholars [Conrad Dasypodius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_Dasypodius "Conrad Dasypodius") (il Dasipodio), [Gerolamo Cardano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerolamo_Cardano "Gerolamo Cardano"), [Francesco Maurolico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maurolico "Francesco Maurolico"), and [Christopher Clavius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Clavius "Christopher Clavius").

He was most famous for his central role as translator of works of ancient mathematicians. In this, his sources were primarily written in Greek and secondarily in Arabic, while his translations were primarily in Latin and secondarily in Italian. He was responsible for the publication of many treatises of [Archimedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes "Archimedes"). He also translated the works of [Aristarchus of Samos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristarchus_of_Samos "Aristarchus of Samos") (*On the sizes and distances of the Sun and the Moon*), [Pappus of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappus_of_Alexandria "Pappus of Alexandria") (*Mathematical collection*), [Hero of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_Alexandria "Hero of Alexandria") (*Pneumatics*), [Ptolemy of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_of_Alexandria "Ptolemy of Alexandria") (*Planisphere* and *Analemma*), [Apollonius of Perga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollonius_of_Perga "Apollonius of Perga") (*Conics*) and [Euclid of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid_of_Alexandria "Euclid of Alexandria") (*Elements*). Among his pupils was [Guidobaldo del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guidobaldo_del_Monte "Guidobaldo del Monte") and [Bernardino Baldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardino_Baldi "Bernardino Baldi"). Commandino maintained a correspondence with the astronomer [Francesco Maurolico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maurolico "Francesco Maurolico"). The proposition known as [Commandino's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandino's_theorem "Commandino's theorem") first appears in his work on centers of gravity.
