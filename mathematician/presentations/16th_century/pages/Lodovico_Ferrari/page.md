**Lodovico de Ferrari** (2 February 1522 – 5 October 1565) was an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians "Italians") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") best known today for solving the [quartic equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_equation "Quartic equation").

## Biography

Born in [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna "Bologna"), Lodovico's grandfather, Bartolomeo Ferrari, was forced out of Milan to Bologna. Lodovico settled in Bologna, and he began his career as the servant of [Gerolamo Cardano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerolamo_Cardano "Gerolamo Cardano"). He was extremely bright, so Cardano started teaching him mathematics. Ferrari aided Cardano on his solutions for [quartic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_equations "Quartic equations") and [cubic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_equation "Cubic equation"), and was mainly responsible for the solution of quartic equations that Cardano published. While still in his teens, Ferrari was able to obtain a prestigious teaching post in Rome after Cardano resigned from it and recommended him. Ferrari retired, when young at 42 years old, and wealthy. He then moved back to his hometown of Bologna, where he lived with his widowed sister Maddalena to take up a professorship of mathematics at the University of Bologna in 1565. Shortly thereafter, he died of white [arsenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenic "Arsenic") poisoning, according to a legend, by his sister.

## Cardano–Tartaglia formula

In 1545 a famous dispute erupted between Ferrari and Cardano's contemporary [Niccolò Fontana Tartaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccolò_Fontana_Tartaglia "Niccolò Fontana Tartaglia"), involving the solution to cubic equations. Widespread stories that Tartaglia devoted the rest of his life to ruining Ferrari's teacher and erstwhile master Cardano, however, appear to be fabricated. Mathematical historians now credit both Cardano and Tartaglia with the formula to solve cubic equations, referring to it as the "[Cardano–Tartaglia formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardano–Tartaglia_formula "Cardano–Tartaglia formula")".
