**Pedro Nunes** (Portuguese: [[ˈpeðɾu ˈnunɨʃ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Portuguese "Help:IPA/Portuguese"); [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin"): *Petrus Nonius*; 1502 – 11 August 1578) was a [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal "Portugal") [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician"), [cosmographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmographer "Cosmographer"), and [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor "Professor"), probably from a [New Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Christian "New Christian") (of [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew "Jew") origin) family.

Considered one of the greatest mathematicians of his time, Nunes is best known for being the first to approach navigation and cartography with mathematical tools. Among other accomplishments, he was the first to propose the idea of a [loxodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loxodrome "Loxodrome") (a [rhumb line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhumb_line "Rhumb line")), and was the inventor of several measuring devices, including the [nonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonius_(device) "Nonius (device)") (from which the [Vernier scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernier_scale "Vernier scale") was derived), named after his Latin surname.

## Life

Little is known about Nunes' early education, life or family background, only that he was born in [Alcácer do Sal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcácer_do_Sal "Alcácer do Sal") in Portugal, his origins are possibly [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") and that his grandchildren spent a few years behind bars after they were accused by the [Portuguese Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Inquisition "Portuguese Inquisition") of professing and secretly practicing Judaism. He studied at the [University of Salamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Salamanca "University of Salamanca"), maybe from 1517 until 1522. He returned to Lisbon c. 1529 and started teaching at the university.

He continued his medical studies but held various [teaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaching "Teaching") posts within the [University of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lisbon "University of Lisbon"), including [Moral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral "Moral"), [Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy"), [Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic "Logic") and [Metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics "Metaphysics"). He obtained his doctorate in [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine "Medicine") in 1532. When, in 1537, the Portuguese University located in [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon "Lisbon") returned to [Coimbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbra "Coimbra"), he moved to the re-founded University of Coimbra to teach mathematics, a post he held until 1562. This was a new post in the University of Coimbra and it may have been established to provide instruction in the technical requirements for navigation: clearly a topic of great importance in [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal "Portugal") at this period, when the control of sea trade was the primary source of Portuguese wealth. Mathematics became an independent post in 1544.

In addition to teaching he was appointed Royal Cosmographer in 1529 and Chief Royal Cosmographer in 1547: a post which he held until his death.

In 1531, King [John III of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_III_of_Portugal "John III of Portugal") charged Nunes with the education of his younger brothers [Luís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luís_of_Portugal,_Duke_of_Beja "Luís of Portugal, Duke of Beja") and [Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry,_King_of_Portugal "Henry, King of Portugal"). Years later Nunes was also charged with the education of the king's grandson, and future king, [Sebastian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_of_Portugal "Sebastian of Portugal").

It is possible that, while at the University of Coimbra, future astronomer [Christopher Clavius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Clavius "Christopher Clavius") attended Pedro Nunes' classes, and was influenced by his works. Clavius, proponent of the [Gregorian Calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_Calendar "Gregorian Calendar"), the greatest figure of the [Collegio Romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_College "Roman College"), the great center of Roman Catholic knowledge of that period, classified Nunes as “supreme mathematical genius". Nunes died in [Coimbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbra "Coimbra").

## Work

Pedro Nunes lived in a transition period, during which science was changing from valuing theoretical knowledge (which defined the main role of a scientist/mathematician as commenting on previous authors), to providing [experimental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experiment "Experiment") data, both as a source of information and as a method of confirming theories. Nunes was, above all, one of the last great commentators, as is shown by his first published work “Tratado da Esfera”, enriched with comments and additions that denote a profound knowledge of the difficult cosmography of the period. He also acknowledged the value of experimentation.

In his *Tratado da sphera* he argued for a common and universal diffusion of knowledge. Accordingly, he not only published works in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin"), at that time science's [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca"), aiming for an audience of European scholars, but also in [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_(language) "Portuguese (language)"), and [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_(language) "Spanish (language)") (*Livro de Algebra*).

### Navigation

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ad/Pedro_Nunes_April_2009-1a.jpg/250px-Pedro_Nunes_April_2009-1a.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pedro_Nunes_April_2009-1a.jpg)

Detail of the [Monument to the Portuguese Discoveries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padrão_dos_Descobrimentos "Padrão dos Descobrimentos") showing the well-known navigators. Left to right: [Jácome de Maiorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehuda_Cresques "Jehuda Cresques") (cosmographer and chart maker), [Pedro Escobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Escobar "Pedro Escobar") (navigator), Pedro Nunes (shown holding an [armillary sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armillary_sphere "Armillary sphere")), [Pero de Alenquer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pero_de_Alenquer "Pero de Alenquer") (navigator), [Gil Eanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gil_Eanes "Gil Eanes") (navigator) and finally [João Gonçalves Zarco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/João_Gonçalves_Zarco "João Gonçalves Zarco") (navigator)

Much of Nunes' work related to [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation "Navigation"). He was the first to understand why a ship maintaining a steady [course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Course_(navigation) "Course (navigation)") would not travel along a [great circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_circle "Great circle"), the shortest path between two points on Earth, but would instead follow a [spiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral "Spiral") course, called a [loxodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loxodrome "Loxodrome"). These lines —also called [rhumb lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhumb_line "Rhumb line")— maintain a fixed angle with the [meridians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_(geography) "Meridian (geography)"). In other words, loxodromic curves are directly related to the construction of the Nunes [connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_connection "Affine connection") —also called navigator connection.

In his *Treaty defending the sea chart*, Nunes argued that a [nautical chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nautical_chart "Nautical chart") should have its [parallels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_of_latitude "Circle of latitude") and meridians shown as straight lines. Yet he was unsure how to solve the problems that this caused: a situation that lasted until [Mercator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardus_Mercator "Gerardus Mercator") developed the [projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map_projection "Map projection") bearing his name. The [Mercator Projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercator_Projection "Mercator Projection") is the system which is still used.

### Geometry

Nunes also solved the problem of finding the day with the shortest [twilight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight "Twilight") duration, for any given position, and its duration. This problem *per se* is not greatly important, yet it shows the geometric genius of Nunes as it was a problem which was independently tackled by [Johann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Bernoulli "Johann Bernoulli") and [Jakob Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Bernoulli "Jakob Bernoulli") more than a century later with less success. They could find a solution to the problem of the [shortest day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortest_day "Shortest day"), but failed to determine its duration, possibly because they got lost in the details of [differential calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_calculus "Differential calculus") which, at that time, had only recently been developed. The achievement also shows that Nunes was a pioneer in solving maxima and minima problems, which became a common requirement only in the next century using differential calculus.

### Cosmology

He was probably the last major mathematician to make relevant improvements to the [ptolemaic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_system "Ptolemaic system") (a [geocentric model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geocentric_model "Geocentric model") describing the relative motion of the Earth and Sun). With time, in a slow and complex process, the geocentric model was replaced by the [heliocentric system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliocentrism "Heliocentrism") proposed by [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus "Nicolaus Copernicus"). Nunes knew Copernicus' work but referred only briefly to it in his published works, with the purpose of correcting some mathematical errors.

Most of Nunes' achievements were possible because of his profound understanding of [spherical trigonometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_trigonometry "Spherical trigonometry") and his ability to transpose [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy "Ptolemy")'s adaptations of [Euclidean geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_geometry "Euclidean geometry") to it.

### Inventions

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Nonio_originale.jpg/250px-Nonio_originale.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nonio_originale.jpg)

Modern reconstruction of a quadrant with [nonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonius_(device) "Nonius (device)")

Nunes worked on several practical nautical problems concerning [course correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Course_(navigation) "Course (navigation)") as well as attempting to develop more accurate devices to determine a ship's position.

He created the [nonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonius_(device) "Nonius (device)") to improve the accuracy of instruments (such as the [quadrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrant_(instrument) "Quadrant (instrument)")). This device consisted of a number of concentric circles traced on the instrument, with each successive circle divided into one fewer division than the adjacent outer circle.Thus, the outermost quadrant would comprise 90° divided into 90 equal parts; the next inner quadrant would have 89 divisions, the next 88, and so on. When an angle was measured, the circle and the division on which the [alidade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alidade "Alidade") fell was noted. A table was then consulted to provide the exact measure.

The nonius was used by [Tycho Brahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe "Tycho Brahe"), who considered it too complex. The method inspired improved systems by [Christopher Clavius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Clavius "Christopher Clavius") and [Jacob Curtius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Kurz_von_Senftenau "Jacob Kurz von Senftenau"). These were eventually improved further by [Pierre Vernier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Vernier "Pierre Vernier") in 1631, which reduced the nonius to the [Vernier scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernier_scale "Vernier scale") that includes two scales, one of them fixed and the other movable. Vernier himself used to say that his invention was a perfected nonius and for a long time it was known as the “nonius”, even in France. In some languages, the Vernier scale is still named after Nunes, for example **nonieskala** in Swedish.

Pedro Nunes also worked on some [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") problems, from a mathematical point of view.

## Influence

Nunes was very influential internationally, e.g. on the work of [John Dee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dee "John Dee") and [Edward Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Wright_(mathematician) "Edward Wright (mathematician)").

## Honours

- One of the best known [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon "Lisbon") public Secondary/High Schools is named after Pedro Nunes, *Escola Secundária de Pedro Nunes* (teaching 7th to 12th grade). It was founded, in 1906, as *Lyceu Central da 3ª Zona Escolar de Lisboa* (*Central Liceum of the 3rd School Area of Lisbon*). Over the years had known several designations: Lyceu Central de Pedro Nunes (1911–1930), Liceu Normal de Lisboa (1930–1937), Liceu Pedro Nunes (1937–1956), Liceu Normal de Pedro Nunes (1956–1978) and Escola Secundária de Pedro Nunes (1978–present), but is still known as Liceu Pedro Nunes. Multiple Portuguese personalities have studied in Pedro Nunes. The current headquarters commemorated its centenary in 2011, after being refurbished and modernized between 2008 and 2010.
- He was featured on 100 [escudos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escudo "Escudo") coins.
- The [Instituto Pedro Nunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Pedro_Nunes "Instituto Pedro Nunes") in [Coimbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbra "Coimbra"), a business incubator and a center of innovation and technology transfer founded by the University of Coimbra, is named after Pedro Nunes.
- Asteroid [5313 Nunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5313_Nunes "5313 Nunes") is named after him
- [TAP Air Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAP_Air_Portugal "TAP Air Portugal") has named an [Airbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus "Airbus") A330-202 airplane after him, registered CS-TOP.

Pedro Nunes translated, commented and expanded some of the major works in his field, and he also published original research.

Printed work:

- *Tratado da sphera com a Theorica do Sol e da Lua* (Treatise on the Sphere with the theory of the Sun and the Moon), (1537). From *[Tractatus de Sphæra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tractatus_de_Sphæra "Tractatus de Sphæra")* by [Johannes de Sacrobosco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_de_Sacrobosco "Johannes de Sacrobosco"), *Theoricae novæ planetarum* by [Georg Purbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Purbach "Georg Purbach") and the *Geography* by [Claudius Ptolemaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy "Ptolemy"). Included original texts: *Tratado em defensam da carta de marear* (Treatise in defense of the nautical chart), (1537); *Tratado sobre certas dúvidas da navegação* (Treatise about some doubts of navigation), (1537)
- *De crepusculis* (About the Twilight), (1542).
- [*De crepusculis*](https://gutenberg.beic.it/webclient/DeliveryManager?pid=13148868) (in Latin). Lisboa: Luiz Rodrigues. 1542.
- *De erratis Orontii Finæi* (About the errors of [Oronce Fine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oronce_Fine "Oronce Fine")), (1546).
- *Petri Nonii Salaciensis Opera*, (1566). Expanded, corrected and reedited as *De arte adque ratione navigandi* in 1573.
- [Livro de algebra en arithmetica y geometria](https://purl.pt/40145) (Book of Algebra in Arithmetics and Geometry), (1567).

Manuscripts:

- [*Manuscrito de Florença*] (Florence manuscript), (written in the period 1537-1540)

Some modern reprints:
