**Franco Modigliani** ([US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English "American English"): [/ˌmoʊdiːlˈjɑːni/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); Italian: [[ˈfɾaŋko modiʎˈʎaːni]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian "Help:IPA/Italian"); 18 June 1918 – 25 September 2003) was an Italian-American [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist") and the recipient of the 1985 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economics "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics"). He was a professor at [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign "University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign"), [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University "Carnegie Mellon University"), and [MIT Sloan School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Sloan_School_of_Management "MIT Sloan School of Management").

## Early life and education

Modigliani was born on 18 June 1918 in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome") to the [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew "Jew") family of a [pediatrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pediatrician "Pediatrician") father and a voluntary [social worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_worker "Social worker") mother.

He entered university at the age of seventeen, enrolling in the faculty of Law at the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome "Sapienza University of Rome"). In his second year at Sapienza, his submission to a nationwide contest in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics") sponsored by the official [student organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioventù_Italiana_del_Littorio "Gioventù Italiana del Littorio") of the state, won first prize and Modigliani received an award from the hand of [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini "Benito Mussolini").
He wrote several essays for the fascist magazine *[Lo Stato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo_Stato "Lo Stato")* where he showed an inclination for the fascist ideological currents critical of liberalism.

Among his early works in Fascist Italy was an article about the organization and management of production in a [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist "Socialist") economy, written in [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language "Italian language") and arguing the case for socialism along lines laid out by earlier [market socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_socialist "Market socialist") like [Abba Lerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abba_Lerner "Abba Lerner") and [Oskar Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Lange "Oskar Lange").

But, that early enthusiasm evaporated soon after the passage of [racial laws in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Racial_Laws "Italian Racial Laws"). In 1938, Modigliani left Italy for [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") together with his then-girlfriend, Serena Calabi, to join her parents there. After briefly returning to [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome") to defend his *[laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea "Laurea")* thesis at the city's university, he obtained his diploma on 22 July 1939, and returned to Paris.

The same year, they all immigrated to the United States and he enrolled at the Graduate Faculty of the [New School for Social Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_School_for_Social_Research "New School for Social Research"). His [Ph.D. dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy"), an elaboration and extension of [John Hicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hicks "John Hicks")' [IS–LM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IS–LM "IS–LM") model, was written under the supervision of [Jacob Marschak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Marschak "Jacob Marschak") and [Abba Lerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abba_Lerner "Abba Lerner"), in 1944, and is considered "ground breaking."

## Career

From 1942 to 1944, Modigliani taught at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") and [Bard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bard_College "Bard College") as an instructor in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics") and [statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics "Statistics"). In 1946, he became a [naturalized citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen "Naturalized citizen") of the United States. In 1948, he joined the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign "University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign") faculty. From 1952 to 1962, he was a member of the [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University "Carnegie Mellon University") faculty.

In 1962, he joined the faculty of [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT "MIT"), as an [Institute Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_Professor "Institute Professor").

## Contributions to economic theory

Modigliani, beginning in the 1950s, was an originator of the [life-cycle hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life-cycle_hypothesis "Life-cycle hypothesis"), which attempts to explain the level of [saving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saving "Saving") in the economy. The hypothesis that [consumers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer "Consumer") aim for a stable level of [consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_(economics) "Consumption (economics)") throughout their lifetime (for example by saving during their working years and then spending during their [retirement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retirement "Retirement")).

The [rational expectations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_expectations "Rational expectations") hypothesis is considered by economists to originate in the paper written by Modigliani and Emile Grunberg in 1954.

When he was a member of the [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University "Carnegie Mellon University") faculty, he formulated in 1958, along with [Merton Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_Miller "Merton Miller"), the [Modigliani–Miller theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modigliani–Miller_theorem "Modigliani–Miller theorem") for [corporate finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_finance "Corporate finance"). The theorem posits that, under certain assumptions, the value of a firm is not affected by whether it is financed by [equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock "Stock") (selling shares) or by [debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt "Debt") (borrowing money), meaning that the [debt-to-equity ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt-to-equity_ratio "Debt-to-equity ratio") is unimportant for private firms.

In the early 1960s, his response, co-authored with [Albert Ando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ando "Albert Ando"), to the 1963 paper of [Milton Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Friedman "Milton Friedman") and [David I. Meiselman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_I._Meiselman "David I. Meiselman"), initiated the so-called "[monetary/fiscal policy debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary/fiscal_policy_debate "Monetary/fiscal policy debate")" among economists, which went on for more than sixty years.

In 1975, Modigliani, in a paper whose co-author was his former student [Lucas Papademos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Papademos "Lucas Papademos"), introduced the concept of the "NIRU", the non-inflationary rate of [unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment "Unemployment"), ostensibly an improvement over the "[natural rate of unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_rate_of_unemployment "Natural rate of unemployment")" concept. The terms refer to a level of unemployment below which [inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation "Inflation") rises.

In 1997, Modigliani and his granddaughter, Leah Modigliani, developed what is now called the "[Modigliani Risk-Adjusted Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modigliani_Risk-Adjusted_Performance "Modigliani Risk-Adjusted Performance")," a measure of the risk-adjusted returns of an [investment portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_portfolio "Investment portfolio") that was derived from the [Sharpe ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpe_ratio "Sharpe ratio"), adjusted for the risk of the portfolio relative to that of a benchmark, e.g. the "market."

## Appointments and awards

In October 1985, Modigliani was awarded the [Nobel prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_prize_in_Economics "Nobel prize in Economics") "for his pioneering analyses of [saving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saving "Saving") and of [financial markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_market "Financial market")."

In 1985, Modigliani received MIT's James R. Killian Faculty Achievement Award. In 1997, he received an *[honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa "Honoris causa")* degree in [Management Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_Engineering "Management Engineering") from the [University of Naples Federico II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II "University of Naples Federico II") in 1997.

Late in his life, Modigliani became a trustee of the [Economists for Peace and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economists_for_Peace_and_Security "Economists for Peace and Security") organization, formerly "Economists Allied for Arms Reduction" and was considered an "influential adviser": in the late 1960s, on a contract with the [Federal Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_System "Federal Reserve System"), he designed the "MIT-Pennsylvania-Social Science Research Council" model, a tool that "guided [monetary policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_policy "Monetary policy") in Washington for many decades."

A collection of Modigliani's papers is housed at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University")'s [Rubenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rubenstein#Duke_University "David Rubenstein") Library.

## Criticism

Modigliani's work on fiscal policy came under criticism from followers of [Post-Keynesian economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Keynesian_economics "Post-Keynesian economics"), who disputed the "Keynesianism" of his viewpoints, pointing out his contribution to the [NAIRU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAIRU "NAIRU") concept, as well as his general stance on [fiscal deficits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_policy "Fiscal policy"). The Modigliani-Miller theorem implies that, for a closed economy, state borrowing is merely deferred [taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax "Tax"), since state spending can be financed only by "[printing money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_policy "Fiscal policy")", taxation, or borrowing, and therefore monetary financing of state spending implies the subsequent imposition of a so-called "inflation tax," which ostensibly has the same effect on [permanent income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_income "Permanent income") as explicit taxation.

Nonetheless, they acknowledged his dissenting voice on the issue of unemployment, in which Modigliani concurred early on with [heterodox economists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterodox_economist "Heterodox economist") that Europe-wide unemployment in the late 20th century was caused by the lack of [demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demand "Demand") induced by [austerity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austerity "Austerity") policies.

## Personal life

In 1939, while they were in Paris, Modigliani married Serena Calabi. They had two children, Andre and Sergio Modigliani.

Modigliani died in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts"), in 2003, while still working at MIT, and teaching until the last months of his life. He was 85. Serena Modigliani-Calabi, active to the end in [progressive politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_politics "Progressive politics"), most notably with the [League of Women Voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Women_Voters "League of Women Voters"), and an outspoken believer in [participatory democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participatory_democracy "Participatory democracy"), died in 2008.
