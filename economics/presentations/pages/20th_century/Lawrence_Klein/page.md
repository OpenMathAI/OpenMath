**Lawrence Robert Klein** (September 14, 1920 – October 20, 2013) was an American [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics"). For his work in creating [computer models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_model "Computer model") to forecast [economic trends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_forecasting "Economic forecasting") in the field of [econometrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometrics "Econometrics") in the Department of Economics at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania"), he was awarded the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") in 1980 specifically "for the creation of econometric models and their application to the analysis of economic fluctuations and economic policies." Due to his efforts, such models have become widespread among [economists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist"). [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") professor [Martin Feldstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Feldstein "Martin Feldstein") told the [Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_Journal "Wall Street Journal") that Klein "was the first to create the statistical models that embodied [Keynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Keynes "John Maynard Keynes") economics," tools still used by the [Federal Reserve Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank "Federal Reserve Bank") and other central banks.

## Life and career

Klein was born in [Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska "Omaha, Nebraska"), the son of Blanche (née Monheit) and Leo Byron Klein. He went on to graduate from [Los Angeles City College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_City_College "Los Angeles City College"), where he learned [calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus "Calculus"); the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), where he began his [computer modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_model "Computer model") and earned a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in Economics in 1942; he earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in Economics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (MIT) in 1944, where he was [Paul Samuelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Samuelson "Paul Samuelson")'s first doctoral student.

### Early model-building

Klein then moved to the [Cowles Commission for Research in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowles_Commission_for_Research_in_Economics "Cowles Commission for Research in Economics"), which was then at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"), now the [Cowles Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowles_Foundation "Cowles Foundation"). There he built a model of the [United States economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_economy "United States economy") to forecast the development of business fluctuations and to study the effects of government economic-political policy. After [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") Klein used his model to correctly predict, against the prevailing expectation, that there would be an economic upturn rather than a [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_(economics) "Depression (economics)") due to increasing consumer demand from returning servicemen. Similarly, he correctly predicted a mild [recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recession "Recession") at the end of the [Korean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War").

Klein briefly joined the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA "Communist Party USA") during the 1940s, which led to trouble years later.

At the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan"), Klein developed enhanced [macroeconomic models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomic_model "Macroeconomic model"), in particular the famous [Klein–Goldberger model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein–Goldberger_model "Klein–Goldberger model") with [Arthur Goldberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Goldberger "Arthur Goldberger"), which was based on foundations laid by [Jan Tinbergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Tinbergen "Jan Tinbergen") of the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands "Netherlands"), later winner of the first economics prize in 1969. Klein differed from Tinbergen in using an alternative [economic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_theory "Economic theory") and a different [statistical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics "Statistics") technique.

### McCarthyism and move to England

In 1954, Klein's brief membership in the Communist Party was made public and he was denied tenure at the University of Michigan, in the wake of the [McCarthy era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarthyism "McCarthyism"). Klein moved to the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford"), and developed an economic model of the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") known as the [Oxford model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_model "Oxford model") with [Sir James Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Ball_(economist) "James Ball (economist)"). Additionally, at the Institute of Statistics, Klein assisted with the creation of the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") Savings Surveys, based upon the Michigan Surveys.

### Return to the U.S.

In 1958 Klein returned to the U.S. to join the Department of Economics at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania"). In 1959 he was awarded the [John Bates Clark Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bates_Clark_Medal "John Bates Clark Medal"), one of the two most prestigious awards in the field of economics. In 1968 he became the [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin "Benjamin Franklin") Professor of Economics and Finance at Penn.

### Brookings-SSRC Project

In the early 1960s Klein became the leader of the major "Brookings-SSRC Project" to construct a detailed [econometric model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_model "Econometric model") to forecast the short-term development of the U.S. economy.

### Wharton

Later in the '60s, Klein constructed the [Wharton Econometric Forecasting Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_Econometric_Forecasting_Model?action=edit&redlink=1 "Wharton Econometric Forecasting Model"). This model, considerably smaller than the Brookings model, achieved a very good reputation for its analysis of business conditions, used to forecast fluctuations including national product, exports, investments, and consumption, and to study the effect on them of changes in taxation, public expenditure, oil price, etc.

In 1969 Klein founded [Wharton Econometric Forecasting Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_Econometric_Forecasting_Associates "Wharton Econometric Forecasting Associates") or WEFA (now [Global Insight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Insight "Global Insight")), launching the econometric forecasting industry in the United States. Among his clients were [General Electric Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_Company "General Electric Company"), [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM "IBM"), and [Bethlehem Steel Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_Steel_Corporation "Bethlehem Steel Corporation"). He was the initiator of, and an active research leader in their [LINK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_LINK "Project LINK") project, a consortium of model builders from many countries, which was also mentioned in his Nobel citation. The aim was to produce the world's first global economic model, linking models of many of the world's countries so that the effect of changes in the economy of one country are reflected in the other. The LINK modeling system was transferred from University of Pennsylvania to the United Nations Secretariat at New York in 1989 and Klein remained as the intellectual leader of the project until 2013 when he died. Project LINK continued at the United Nations until 2020.

Klein served as a thesis advisor for numerous well-known economists including [E. Roy Weintraub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._Roy_Weintraub "E. Roy Weintraub") in the late 1960s.

### Later career

During the [1976 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_United_States_presidential_election "1976 United States presidential election"), Klein coordinated [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter")'s economic task force. He declined an invitation to join Carter's administration. Klein has also been president of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society "Econometric Society"), the International Atlantic Economic Society (1989–1990), and the [American Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Association "American Economic Association") (in 1977).

His Nobel citation concludes that "few, if any, research workers in the empirical field of economic science, have had so many successors and such a large impact as Lawrence Klein". However, [Christopher Sims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Sims "Christopher Sims") has criticized the assumptions underlying large macro-econometric models built by Klein (Sims, 1980). Also, many economists have questioned the suitability of estimation methods employed by large structural models and the usefulness of simple autoregressive models for approximating economic systems.

In his final years, he was constructing short range "current quarter models" that use current economic indicators to get a handle on the rate of economic growth during the current and next quarter. In contrast to earlier efforts to model the economy structurally and to use constant adjustments and judgmental estimates for the exogenous variables, these systems are deliberately automatic and mechanical, simply translating available information into a statistically best estimate of current conditions. This represents a very different tradition from his earlier model building and applications.

After formal retirement and until his death he was engaged in macro econometric model building high-frequency models that project the economy within a short timeframe (e.g., monthly or quarterly). A publication on high frequency model containing regions such as US, [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia "Russia"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India"), [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil "Brazil"), [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico "Mexico"), [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea "Korea") and [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong") was expected in 2008.

Klein was a founding trustee of [Economists for Peace and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economists_for_Peace_and_Security "Economists for Peace and Security"). He was also a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"), and the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences").

He died at the age of 93 in his home on October 20, 2013.
