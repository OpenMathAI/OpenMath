**Reinhard Justus Reginald Selten** (German: [[ˈʁaɪnhaʁt ˈzɛltn̩]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Reinhard_Selten.ogg "File:De-Reinhard Selten.ogg"); 5 October 1930 – 23 August 2016) was a German [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics"), who won the 1994 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") (shared with [John Harsanyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harsanyi "John Harsanyi") and [John Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_Nash "John Forbes Nash")). He is also well known for his work in [bounded rationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_rationality "Bounded rationality") and can be considered one of the founding fathers of [experimental economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_economics "Experimental economics").

## Biography

Selten was born in [Breslau (Wrocław)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław "Wrocław") in [Lower Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lower_Silesia "Province of Lower Silesia"), now in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland"), to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") father, Adolf Selten (a blind bookseller; d. 1942), and [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant "Protestant") mother, Käthe Luther. Reinhard Selten was raised as Protestant.

After a brief family exile in Saxony and Austria, Selten returned to Hesse, Germany, after the war and, in high school, read an article in [Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_(magazine) "Fortune (magazine)") magazine about game theory by the business writer [John D. McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._McDonald_(business_writer)?action=edit&redlink=1 "John D. McDonald (business writer)"). He recalled later, he would occupy his "mind with problems of elementary geometry and algebra" while walking back and forth to school during that time. He studied mathematics at [Goethe University Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt "Goethe University Frankfurt") and obtained his diploma in 1957. He then worked as scientific assistant to Heinz Sauermann until 1967. In 1959, he married with Elisabeth Langreiner. They had no children. In 1961, he also received his doctorate in Frankfurt in mathematics with a thesis on the evaluation of n-person games.

He was a visiting professor at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") and taught from 1969 to 1972 at the [Free University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Berlin "Free University of Berlin") and, from 1972 to 1984, at [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University "Bielefeld University"). He then accepted a professorship at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn "University of Bonn"). There he built the BonnEconLab, a laboratory for experimental economic research, where he was active even after his retirement.

Selten was professor emeritus at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn "University of Bonn"), Germany, and held several honorary doctoral degrees. He had been an [Esperantist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperantist "Esperantist") since 1959 and met his wife through the Esperanto movement. He was a member and co-founder of the [International Academy of Sciences San Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akademio_Internacia_de_la_Sciencoj_San_Marino "Akademio Internacia de la Sciencoj San Marino").

For the [2009 European Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_European_Parliament_election_in_Germany "2009 European Parliament election in Germany"), he was the top candidate for the German wing of [Europe – Democracy – Esperanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_–_Democracy_–_Esperanto "Europe – Democracy – Esperanto").

## Work

For his work in [game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_theory "Game theory"), Selten won the 1994 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") (shared with [John Harsanyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harsanyi "John Harsanyi") and [John Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_Nash "John Forbes Nash")). Selten was Germany's first and, at the time of his death, only Nobel winner for economics. Among many other topics, he is credited with the first study of [aggregative games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggregative_game "Aggregative game").

He is also well known for his work in [bounded rationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_rationality "Bounded rationality"), and can be considered one of the founding fathers of [experimental economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_economics "Experimental economics"). With [Gerd Gigerenzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerd_Gigerenzer "Gerd Gigerenzer") he edited the book *Bounded Rationality: The Adaptive Toolbox* (2001). He developed an example of a game called Selten's Horse because of its extensive form representation. His last work was "Impulse Balance Theory and its Extension by an Additional Criterion".

He was noted for his publishing in non-refereed journals to avoid being forced to make unwanted changes to his work.
