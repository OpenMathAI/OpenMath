**Jan Tinbergen** ([/ˈtɪnbɜːrɡən/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*TIN-bur-gən*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"), Dutch: [[jɑn ˈtɪmbɛrɣə(n)]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Dutch "Help:IPA/Dutch"); 12 April 1903 – 9 June 1994) was a Dutch [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist") who was awarded the first [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") in 1969, which he shared with [Ragnar Frisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragnar_Frisch "Ragnar Frisch") for having developed and applied dynamic models for the analysis of [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic "Economic") [processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process "Business process"). He is widely considered to be one of the most influential economists of the 20th century and one of the founding fathers of [econometrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometrics "Econometrics").

His important contributions to econometrics include the development of the first [macroeconometric models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large-scale_macroeconometric_model "Large-scale macroeconometric model"), the solution of the identification problem, and the understanding of dynamic models. Tinbergen was a founding trustee of [Economists for Peace and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economists_for_Peace_and_Security "Economists for Peace and Security"). In 1945, he founded the [Bureau for Economic Policy Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_for_Economic_Policy_Analysis "Bureau for Economic Policy Analysis") (CPB) and was the agency's first director.

## Biography

Tinbergen was the eldest of five children of Dirk Cornelis Tinbergen and Jeannette van Eek. His brother [Nikolaas "Niko" Tinbergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolaas_Tinbergen "Nikolaas Tinbergen") would also win a Nobel Prize (for [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine"), during 1973) for his work in [ethology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethology "Ethology"), while his youngest brother [Luuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luuk_Tinbergen "Luuk Tinbergen") would become a famous [ornithologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithologist "Ornithologist"). Jan and Nikolaas Tinbergen are the only siblings to have both won Nobel Prizes.

Between 1921 and 1925, Tinbergen studied [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") and [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") at the [University of Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leiden "University of Leiden") under [Paul Ehrenfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrenfest "Paul Ehrenfest"). During those years at Leiden, he had numerous discussions with Ehrenfest, [Kamerlingh Onnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heike_Kamerlingh_Onnes "Heike Kamerlingh Onnes"), [Hendrik Lorentz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Lorentz "Hendrik Lorentz"), [Pieter Zeeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_Zeeman "Pieter Zeeman"), and [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein").

After graduating, Tinbergen fulfilled his community service in the administration of a prison in Rotterdam and at the Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) in The Hague. He then returned to the [University of Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leiden "University of Leiden") and in 1929 defended his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") thesis titled "Minimumproblemen in de natuurkunde en de economie" (Minimisation problems in Physics and Economics). This topic was suggested by Ehrenfest and allowed Tinbergen to combine his interests in mathematics, physics, economics and politics. At that time, CBS established a new department of business surveys and mathematical statistics, and Tinbergen became its first chairman, working at CBS until 1945. Access to the vast CBS data helped Tinbergen in testing his theoretical models. In parallel, starting from 1931, he was professor of statistics at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam "University of Amsterdam"), and in 1933 he was appointed associate professor of mathematics and statistics at The [Netherlands School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_School_of_Economics "Netherlands School of Economics"), Rotterdam, where he stayed until 1973.

From 1929 to 1945 he worked for the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands "Netherlands") statistical office and served as consultant at the [Economic and Financial Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Financial_Organization_of_the_League_of_Nations "Economic and Financial Organization of the League of Nations") of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") (1936–1938). In 1945 he became the first director of the [Netherlands Bureau for Economic Policy Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPB_(Netherlands) "CPB (Netherlands)") and left this position in 1955 to focus on education. He spent one year as a visiting professor at the [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") and then returned to the Dutch Economic Institute (the successor of the Netherlands School of Economics). In parallel, he provided consulting services to international organizations and governments of various developing countries, such as United Arab Republic, Turkey, Venezuela, Surinam, Indonesia and Pakistan.

Tinbergen became a member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1946. He was also a member of the International Academy of Science, Munich. In 1956 he founded the [Econometric Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Institute "Econometric Institute") at the [Erasmus Universiteit Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Universiteit_Rotterdam "Erasmus Universiteit Rotterdam") together with [Henri Theil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Theil "Henri Theil"), who also was his successor in [Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam "Rotterdam"). In 1960, he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association "Fellow of the American Statistical Association") and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"). The [Tinbergen Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinbergen_Institute "Tinbergen Institute") was named in his honour. The [International Institute of Social Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_of_Social_Studies "International Institute of Social Studies") (ISS) awarded its Honorary Fellowship to Jan Tinbergen in 1962. He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1963. In 1968, he received an honorary doctorate from [Sir George Williams University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_George_Williams_University "Sir George Williams University"), which later became [Concordia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_University "Concordia University"). He was elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1974.

## Work

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Jan_Tinbergen_1975.jpg/500px-Jan_Tinbergen_1975.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jan_Tinbergen_1975.jpg)

Minister [Pronk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Pronk "Jan Pronk"), Tinbergen and De Seynes at an economy symposium in The Hague, 23 May 1975

For many, Jan Tinbergen became known for the so-called 'Tinbergen Norm' often discussed long after his death. There is no written work of Tinbergen in which he himself states it formally. It is generally believed to be the principle that, if the ratio between the greatest and least income exceeds 5, it becomes disadvantageous for the societal unit involved. Tinbergen himself discussed some technicalities of a five-to-one income distribution ratio in an article published in 1981. Apart from specifics about a five-to-one ratio, it is true in general that Tinbergen's grand theme was [income distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_distribution "Income distribution") and the search for an optimal social order.

Tinbergen developed the first national comprehensive [macroeconomic model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomic_model "Macroeconomic model"), which he first developed in 1936 for the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands "Netherlands"), and later applied to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom").

In his work on macroeconomic modelling and economic policy making, Tinbergen classified some economic quantities as *targets* and others as *[instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomic_policy_instruments "Macroeconomic policy instruments")*. Targets are those macroeconomic variables the policy maker wishes to influence, whereas instruments are the variables that the policy maker can control directly. Tinbergen emphasized that achieving the desired values of a certain number of targets requires the policy maker to control an equal number of instruments. This is known as the [Tinbergen Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinbergen_Rule?action=edit&redlink=1 "Tinbergen Rule").

Tinbergen was appointed to the Dutch Government commission set up to oversee the design and construction of the [Delta Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Works "Delta Works") in the aftermath of the [North Sea Flood of 1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_flood_of_1953 "North Sea flood of 1953"), the *Deltacommissie* ([English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language"): Delta Commission). As part of the commission's work, he produced a [cost–benefit analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost–benefit_analysis "Cost–benefit analysis") for the Delta Works in 1954, and again in 1960. His work for the commission has been noted as one of the first formal social cost/benefit appraisals of a national infrastructure project.

Tinbergen's classification remains influential today, underlying the theory of monetary policy used by [central banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank "Central bank"). Many central banks today regard the [inflation rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_rate "Inflation rate") as their target; the policy instrument they use to control inflation is the short-term [interest rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate "Interest rate").

Tinbergen's work on macroeconomic models was later continued by [Lawrence Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Klein "Lawrence Klein"), contributing to another [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Economics "Nobel Prize in Economics"). For his cultural contributions, he was given the [Gouden Ganzenveer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouden_Ganzenveer "Gouden Ganzenveer") in 1985.

Tinbergen's econometric modelling lead to a lively debate with several known participants including [J.M. Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.M._Keynes "J.M. Keynes"), [Ragnar Frisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragnar_Frisch "Ragnar Frisch") and [Milton Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Friedman "Milton Friedman"). The debate is sometime referred to as the [Tinbergen debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keynes–Tinbergen_debate "Keynes–Tinbergen debate").
