**Tjalling Charles Koopmans** (August 28, 1910 – February 26, 1985) was a [Dutch-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_American "Dutch American") mathematician and economist. He was the joint winner with [Leonid Kantorovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Kantorovich "Leonid Kantorovich") of the 1975 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") for his work on the theory of the optimum allocation of resources. Koopmans showed that on the basis of certain efficiency criteria, it is possible to make important deductions concerning optimum price systems.

## Biography

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/KoopmansLeuven1967.jpg/500px-KoopmansLeuven1967.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KoopmansLeuven1967.jpg)

Koopmans (1967)

Koopmans was born in ['s-Graveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'s-Graveland "'s-Graveland"), [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands "Netherlands"). He began his university education at the [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University "Utrecht University") at seventeen, specializing in mathematics. Three years later, in 1930, he switched to theoretical physics. In 1933, he met [Jan Tinbergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Tinbergen "Jan Tinbergen"), the winner of the 1969 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics and moved to [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam "Amsterdam") to study [mathematical economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_economics "Mathematical economics") under him. In addition to mathematical economics, Koopmans extended his explorations to [econometrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometrics "Econometrics") and [statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics "Statistics"). In 1936, he graduated from [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University "Leiden University") with a PhD, under the direction of [Hendrik Kramers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Kramers "Hendrik Kramers"). The title of the thesis was "Linear regression analysis of economic time series". He also worked for the [Economic and Financial Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Financial_Organization_of_the_League_of_Nations "Economic and Financial Organization of the League of Nations") of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations").

Koopmans moved to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") in 1940. There, he worked for a while for a government body in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), where he published on the [economics of transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics_of_transportation "Economics of transportation") focusing on optimal routing, then moved to [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") where he joined a research body, the [Cowles Commission for Research in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowles_Foundation "Cowles Foundation"), affiliated with the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"). In 1946, he became a [naturalized citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen "Naturalized citizen") of the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), and in 1948, director of the Cowles Commission. Also in 1948, he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association "Fellow of the American Statistical Association"). In 1950, he became a corresponding member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences"). Rising hostile opposition to the Cowles Commission by the department of economics at University of Chicago during the 1950s led Koopmans to convince the Cowles family to move it to [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") in 1955 (where it was renamed the [Cowles Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowles_Foundation "Cowles Foundation")). He continued to publish, on the [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics") of optimal growth and activity analysis.

Koopmans's early works on the [Hartree-Fock theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartree–Fock_method "Hartree–Fock method") of atomic and molecular structure are associated with the [Koopmans' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koopmans'_theorem "Koopmans' theorem"), which is very well known in [quantum chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chemistry "Quantum chemistry").

Koopmans was awarded his Nobel memorial prize (jointly with Leonid Kantorovich) for his contributions to the field of resource allocation, specifically the theory of optimal use of resources. The work for which the prize was awarded focused on activity analysis, the study of interactions between the inputs and outputs of [production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_(economics) "Production (economics)"), and their relationship to [economic efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_efficiency "Economic efficiency") and [prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prices "Prices"). Finally, the importance of the article by Koopmans (1942) deriving the distribution of the serial correlation coefficient was recognized by [John von Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann "John von Neumann"), and it later influenced the optimal tests for a [unit root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_root "Unit root") by [John Denis Sargan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Denis_Sargan "John Denis Sargan") and [Alok Bhargava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alok_Bhargava "Alok Bhargava") (Sargan and Bhargava, 1983).

### Family and name

Tjalling Charles Koopmans was a son of Sjoerd Koopmans and Wytske van der Zee; his [middle name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_name "Middle name") Charles was probably derived from his [patronymic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronymic "Patronymic") "Sjoerds".

One of Sjoerd Koopmans's sisters, Gatske Koopmans, and her husband Symon van der Meer were the paternal grandparents of Nobel Prize winner [Simon van der Meer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_van_der_Meer "Simon van der Meer"). Tjalling Koopmans and Simon van der Meer were therefore [first cousins once removed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_cousins_once_removed#1stOnceRem "First cousins once removed").

Tjalling had two brothers, one of whom was [theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theologian "Theologian") Rev. Dr [Jan Koopmans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Koopmans "Jan Koopmans"), who in 1940, early during the [German occupation of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_the_Netherlands "German occupation of the Netherlands"), wrote the widely distributed pamphlet "*Bijna te laat*" ("*Almost too late*", 30,000 copies), warning about the future of the Jews under the Nazi regime. In 1945, towards the end of the war, he witnessed an execution of hostages in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam "Amsterdam") from behind a window and was mortally wounded by a stray bullet.

Koopmans married Truus Wanningen in October 1936. The couple had three children – a son, Henry, and two daughters, Anne and Helen.
