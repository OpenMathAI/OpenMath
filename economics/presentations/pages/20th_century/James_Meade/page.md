**James Edward Meade** (23 June 1907 – 22 December 1995) was a British [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist") who made major contributions to the theory of [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade "International trade") and [welfare economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_economics "Welfare economics"). Along with [Richard Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kahn,_Baron_Kahn "Richard Kahn, Baron Kahn"), James Meade helped develop the concept of the [Keynesian multiplier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keynesian_multiplier "Keynesian multiplier") while participating in the [Cambridge circus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Circus_(economics) "Cambridge Circus (economics)"). In the 1930s, he served as specialist adviser on behalf of the British government at the [Economic and Financial Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Financial_Organization_of_the_League_of_Nations "Economic and Financial Organization of the League of Nations") of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations").

## Biography

Born in [Swanage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swanage "Swanage"), Meade was brought up in [Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_Somerset "Bath, Somerset"), and educated at [Lambrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambrook "Lambrook") prep school, [Malvern College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvern_College "Malvern College"), and [Oriel College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriel_College,_Oxford "Oriel College, Oxford"), where he initially read [classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literae_humaniores "Literae humaniores"), before switching (in 1928) to the newly-established course in [philosophy, politics, and economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy,_politics_and_economics "Philosophy, politics and economics"). He was elected a Fellow of [Hertford College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertford_College,_Oxford "Hertford College, Oxford") in 1930, and was a lecturer in economics at Oxford from 1931 to 1937. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), he was recalled to the Economic Section of the Secretariat of the [War Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Cabinet#Churchill_war_ministry "War Cabinet"), which he chaired from 1946 to 1947.

He was appointed [CB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Bath "Order of the Bath") in 1946, and served as President of the [Royal Economic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Economic_Society "Royal Economic Society") from 1964 to 1966. While his work was not confined by political boundaries, he advised the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)") in the 1930s, and was a member of the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_(UK) "Social Democratic Party (UK)") during the 1980s. He once said that he had "my heart to the left, and my brain to the right".

In 1976 he received an Honorary Doctorate from the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath "University of Bath").

Along with the Swedish economist [Bertil Ohlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertil_Ohlin "Bertil Ohlin"), he received the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") in 1977 "for their pathbreaking contribution to the theory of [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade "International trade") and international [capital movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_movement "Capital movement")".

In 1981 he attracted attention as one of the 364 economists who signed a letter to *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")* questioning [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher "Margaret Thatcher")'s economic policies, warning that it would only result in deepening the prevailing [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_(economics) "Depression (economics)").
