**John Charles Harsanyi** ([Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language "Hungarian language"): *Harsányi János Károly*; May 29, 1920 – August 9, 2000) was a Hungarian-American economist who spent most of his career at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"). He was the recipient of the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") in 1994.

Harsanyi is best known for his contributions to the study of [game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_theory "Game theory") and its application to economics, specifically for his developing the highly innovative analysis of games of incomplete information, so-called [Bayesian games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_game "Bayesian game"). He also made important contributions to the use of game theory and economic reasoning in political and moral philosophy (specifically [utilitarian ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utilitarianism "Utilitarianism")) as well as contributing to the study of [equilibrium selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_selection "Equilibrium selection"). For his work, he was a co-recipient along with [John Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_Nash "John Forbes Nash") and [Reinhard Selten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Selten "Reinhard Selten") of the 1994 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences").

He moved to the United States in 1956, and spent most of his life there. According to [György Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/György_Marx "György Marx"), he was one of [The Martians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Martians_(scientists) "The Martians (scientists)").

## Early life

Harsanyi was born on May 29, 1920, in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest "Budapest"), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_(1920–1946) "Kingdom of Hungary (1920–1946)"), the son of Alice Harsányi (née Gombos) and Károly Harsányi, a pharmacy owner. His parents converted from Judaism to Catholicism a year before he was born. He attended high school at the [Lutheran Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasori_Gimnázium "Fasori Gimnázium") in Budapest. In high school, he became one of the best problem solvers of the [KöMaL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KöMaL "KöMaL"), the Mathematical and Physical Monthly for Secondary Schools. Founded in 1893, this periodical is generally credited with a large share of Hungarian students' success in mathematics. He also won the first prize in the Eötvös mathematics competition for high school students.

Although he wanted to study mathematics and philosophy, his father sent him to France in 1939 to enroll in [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering "Chemical engineering") at the [University of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lyon "University of Lyon"). However, because of the start of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Harsanyi returned to Hungary to study pharmacology at the [University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University "Eötvös Loránd University") (today: [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University "Eötvös Loránd University")), earning a diploma in 1944. As a pharmacology student, Harsanyi escaped conscription into the [Royal Hungarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Hungarian_Army "Royal Hungarian Army") which, as a person of [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") [descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_heritage "Cultural heritage"), would have meant forced labor.

However, in 1944 (after the fall of the [Horthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miklós_Horthy "Miklós Horthy") regime and the seizure of power by the [Arrow Cross Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_Cross_Party "Arrow Cross Party")) his military deferment was cancelled and he was compelled to join a forced labor unit on the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II) "Eastern Front (World War II)"). After seven months of forced labor, when the German authorities decided to deport his unit to a [concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_camp "Concentration camp") in Austria, John Harsanyi managed to escape and found sanctuary for the rest of the war in a [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit "Jesuit") house.

## Postwar

After the end of the war, Harsanyi returned to the University of Budapest for graduate studies in philosophy and sociology, earning his PhD in both subjects in 1947. Then a devout [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic"), he simultaneously studied theology, also joining lay ranks of the [Dominican Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order "Dominican Order"). He later abandoned Catholicism, becoming an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist") for the rest of his life. Harsanyi spent the academic year 1947–1948 on the faculty of the Institute of Sociology of the University of Budapest, where he met Anne Klauber, his future wife. He was forced to resign the faculty because of openly expressing his anti-[Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist "Marxist") opinions, while Anne faced increasing peer pressure to leave him for the same reason.

Harsanyi remained in Hungary for the following two years attempting to sell his family's pharmacy without losing it to the authorities. After it became apparent that the communist party would confiscate the pharmacy in 1950, he fled with Anne and her parents by illegally crossing the border into Austria and then going to Australia where Klauber's parents had some friends.

## Australia

The two did not marry until they arrived in Australia because Klauber's immigration papers would need to be changed to reflect her married name. The two arrived with her parents on December 30, 1950, and they looked to marry immediately. Harsanyi and Klauber were married on January 2, 1951. Neither spoke much English and understood little of what they were told to say to each other. Harsanyi later explained to his new wife that she had promised to cook better food than she usually did.

Harsanyi's Hungarian degrees were not recognized in Australia, but they earned him credit at the [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney "University of Sydney") for a master's degree. Harsanyi worked in a factory during the day and studied economics in the evening at the University of Sydney, finishing with a M.A. in 1953. While studying in Sydney, he started publishing research papers in economic journals, including the *Journal of Political Economy* and the *Review of Economic Studies*. The degree allowed him to take a teaching position in 1954 at the [University of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Queensland "University of Queensland") in [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane "Brisbane"). While in Brisbane, Harsanyi's wife became a fashion designer for a small factory.

## Later years

In 1956, Harsanyi received a [Rockefeller scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation "Rockefeller Foundation") that enabled him and Anne to spend the next two years in the United States, at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") and, for a semester, at the [Cowles Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowles_Foundation "Cowles Foundation"). At Stanford Harsanyi wrote a dissertation in [game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_theory "Game theory") under the supervision of [Kenneth Arrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Arrow "Kenneth Arrow"), earning a second PhD in economics in 1959, while Anne earned an MA in psychology. Harsanyi's student visa expired in 1958 and the two returned to Australia.

After working for a short time as a researcher at the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University "Australian National University") in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra "Canberra"), Harsanyi became frustrated with the lack of interest in game theory in Australia. With the help of Kenneth Arrow and [James Tobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Tobin "James Tobin"), he was able to move to the United States, taking a position as professor of economics at the [Wayne State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_State_University "Wayne State University") in Detroit between 1961 and 1963. In 1964, he moved to [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California "Berkeley, California"), California; he remained at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), until retiring in 1990. Shortly after arriving in Berkeley, he and Anne had a child, Tom.

While teaching at Berkeley, Harsanyi did extensive research in game theory. [Harold Kuhn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_W._Kuhn "Harold W. Kuhn"), who had been [John von Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann "John von Neumann")'s student in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") and already had game theory publications encouraged him in this. The work for which he won the 1994 Nobel Prize in economics was a series of articles published in 1967 and 1968 which established what has become the standard framework for analyzing "games of incomplete information", situations in which the various strategic decisionmakers have different information about the parameters of the game. He resolved the problem of how players could make decisions while not knowing what each other knows by modelling the situation with initial moves by Nature using known probabilities to choose the parameters, with some players observing Nature's move but other players just knowing the probabilities and the fact that some players have observed the actual realized values. This relies on assuming that all players know the structure of the game, which means they all have "common priors", knowing the probabilities Nature uses in selecting parameters values, an assumption known as the Harsanyi Doctrine.

From 1966 to 1968, Harsanyi was part of a team of game theorists tasked with advising the United States Arms Control and Disarmament Agency in collaboration with Mathematica, a consulting group from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") led by Harold Kuhn and [Oskar Morgenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Morgenstern "Oskar Morgenstern").

John Harsanyi died on August 9, 2000, from a heart attack in Berkeley, California, after developing [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease "Alzheimer's disease").

## Publications

Harsanyi began researching [utilitarian ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utilitarianism "Utilitarianism") in the mid-fifties at the [University of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Queensland "University of Queensland") in [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane "Brisbane"). This led to two publications explaining why, before understanding moral problems, the difference between people's personal and moral preferences must be distinguished. As he says at the beginning of his essay included in the book edited by A. Sen and B. Williams (see below), he tries to reconcile three traditions of Western moral thinking, those of [Adam Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Smith "Adam Smith"), [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant "Immanuel Kant") and the utilitarians ([Bentham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Bentham "Jeremy Bentham"), [Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stuart_Mill "John Stuart Mill"), [Sidgwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Sidgwick "Henry Sidgwick") and [Edgeworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ysidro_Edgeworth "Francis Ysidro Edgeworth")). He is considered one of the most important exponents of the "[rule utilitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_utilitarianism "Rule utilitarianism")".

After moving to the US on a [Rockefeller Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation "Rockefeller Foundation") where he was supervised by [Kenneth Arrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Arrow "Kenneth Arrow"), Harsanyi was influenced by Nash's publications on game theory and became increasingly interested in the topic.
