**James Tobin** (March 5, 1918 – March 11, 2002) was an American [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist") who served on the [Council of Economic Advisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Economic_Advisers "Council of Economic Advisers") and consulted with the [Board of Governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Governors "Board of Governors") of the [Federal Reserve System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_System "Federal Reserve System"), and taught at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"). He contributed to the development of key ideas in the [Keynesian economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keynesian_economics "Keynesian economics") of his generation and advocated [government intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_intervention "Government intervention") in particular to stabilize output and avoid [recessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recession "Recession"). His academic work included pioneering contributions to the study of [investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_(macroeconomics) "Investment (macroeconomics)"), monetary and [fiscal policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_policy "Fiscal policy") and financial markets. He also proposed an [econometric model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_model "Econometric model") for [censored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censoring_(statistics) "Censoring (statistics)") dependent variables, the well-known [tobit model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobit_model "Tobit model").

Along with fellow [neo-Keynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Keynesian_economics "Neo-Keynesian economics") economist [James Meade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Meade "James Meade") in 1977, Tobin proposed [nominal GDP targeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominal_income_target "Nominal income target") as a [monetary policy rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discretionary_policy "Discretionary policy") in 1980. Tobin received the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") in 1981 for "creative and extensive work on the analysis of financial markets and their relations to expenditure decisions, employment, production and prices."

Outside academia, Tobin was widely known for his suggestion of a [tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax "Tax") on [foreign exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_market "Foreign exchange market") transactions, now known as the "[Tobin tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobin_tax "Tobin tax")." This was designed to reduce [speculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculation "Speculation") in the international [currency markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_market "Currency market"), which he saw as dangerous and unproductive.

## Life and career

### Early life

Tobin was born on March 5, 1918, in [Champaign, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign,_Illinois "Champaign, Illinois"). His father was Louis Michael Tobin (b. 1879), a journalist working at the [University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana–Champaign "University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign") and was credited as the inventor of "Homecoming." His mother, Margaret Edgerton Tobin (b. 1893), was a social worker. Tobin attended the [University Laboratory High School of Urbana, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Laboratory_High_School_of_Urbana,_Illinois "University Laboratory High School of Urbana, Illinois"), a [laboratory school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_school "Laboratory school") on the university's campus.

In 1935, he was admitted to [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") with a national scholarship. During his studies he first read [Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Keynes "John Maynard Keynes")' *[The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_General_Theory_of_Employment,_Interest_and_Money "The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money")*, published in 1936. Tobin graduated *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude "Summa cum laude")* in 1939 with a thesis centered on a critical analysis of Keynes' mechanism for introducing equilibrium [involuntary unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Involuntary_unemployment "Involuntary unemployment"). His first published article, in 1941, was based on this senior thesis.

Tobin earned his master degree at Harvard in 1940. In 1941, he worked for the [Office of Price Administration and Civilian Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Price_Administration_and_Civilian_Supply "Office of Price Administration and Civilian Supply") and the [War Production Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Production_Board "War Production Board"). In 1942 he enlisted in the [US Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Navy "US Navy"), spending the war as an officer on destroyers including (among possibly others) the [USS *Kearny* (DD-432)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Kearny "USS Kearny"). After the war he returned to Harvard, receiving his Ph.D. in 1947 with a thesis on the [consumption function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_function "Consumption function") written under the supervision of [Joseph Schumpeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Schumpeter "Joseph Schumpeter"). In 1947 Tobin was elected a Junior Fellow of Harvard's [Society of Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Fellows "Society of Fellows"), which allowed him the freedom and funding to spend the next three years studying and doing research.

### Academic activity and consultancy

In 1950 Tobin moved to [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"), where he remained for the rest of his career. He joined the [Cowles Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowles_Foundation "Cowles Foundation"), which moved to Yale in 1955, also serving as its president between 1955–1961 and 1964–1965. His main research interest was to provide [microfoundations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfoundations "Microfoundations") to [Keynesian economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keynesian_economics "Keynesian economics"), with a special focus on [monetary economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_economics "Monetary economics"). One of his frequent collaborators was his Yale colleague [William Brainard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Brainard "William Brainard"). In 1957 Tobin was appointed [Sterling Professor of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_Professor "Sterling Professor") at Yale.

Besides teaching and research, Tobin was also strongly involved in the public life, writing on current economic issues and serving as an economic expert and policy consultant. During 1961–62, he served as a member of [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy "John F. Kennedy")'s [Council of Economic Advisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Economic_Advisers "Council of Economic Advisers"), under the chairman [Walter Heller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Heller "Walter Heller"), then acted as a consultant between 1962 and 1968. Here, in close collaboration with [Arthur Okun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Okun "Arthur Okun"), [Robert Solow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Solow "Robert Solow") and [Kenneth Arrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Arrow "Kenneth Arrow"), he helped design the Keynesian economic policy implemented by the Kennedy administration. Tobin also served for several terms as a member of the Board of Governors of [Federal Reserve System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_System "Federal Reserve System") Academic Consultants and as a consultant of the [US Treasury Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Treasury "United States Department of the Treasury").

Tobin was awarded the [John Bates Clark Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bates_Clark_Medal "John Bates Clark Medal") in 1955 and, in 1981, the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Economics "Nobel Prize in Economics"). He was a fellow of several professional associations, holding the position of president of the [American Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Association "American Economic Association") in 1971. He was an elected member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"), and the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences").

In 1972 Tobin, along with fellow Yale economics professor [William Nordhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Nordhaus "William Nordhaus"), published *Is Growth Obsolete?*, an article that introduced the [Measure of Economic Welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_of_Economic_Welfare "Measure of Economic Welfare") as the first model for economic [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability "Sustainability") assessment, and economic [sustainability measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability_measurement "Sustainability measurement").

In 1982–1983, Tobin was Ford Visiting Research Professor of economics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"). In 1988 he formally retired from Yale, but continued to deliver some lectures as [Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus "Professor Emeritus") and continued to write. He died on March 11, 2002, in [New Haven, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut "New Haven, Connecticut").

Tobin was a trustee of [Economists for Peace and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economists_for_Peace_and_Security "Economists for Peace and Security").

### Personal life

James Tobin married Elizabeth Fay Ringo, a former [M.I.T.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") student of Paul Samuelson, on September 14, 1946. They had four children.

### Legacy

In August 2009 in a [roundtable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_table_(discussion) "Round table (discussion)") interview in [Prospect *magazine*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospect_(magazine) "Prospect (magazine)"), [Adair Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adair_Turner "Adair Turner") supported the idea of new global taxes on financial transactions, warning that the "swollen" financial sector paying excessive salaries had grown too big for society. Lord Turner's suggestion that a "[Tobin tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobin_tax "Tobin tax")" – named after James Tobin – should be considered for financial transactions made headlines around the world.

Tobin's [Tobit model of regression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobit_model "Tobit model") with [censored endogenous variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censoring_(statistics) "Censoring (statistics)") (Tobin 1958a) is a standard econometric technique. His ["q" theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobin's_q "Tobin's q") of investment (Tobin 1969), the [Baumol–Tobin model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baumol–Tobin_model "Baumol–Tobin model") of the transactions demand for money (Tobin 1956), and his model of liquidity preference as behavior toward risk (the asset demand for money) (Tobin 1958b) are all staples of economics textbooks.

In his 1958 article Tobin also led the way in showing how to deal with utility maximization under uncertainty with an infinite number of possible states. As Palda explains "One way to get out of the mess of figuring out asset prices using a model of maximizing the expected utility of investing in stocks is to make assumptions about either preferences or the probabilities of the different possible states of the world. Nobelist James Tobin (1958) took this line and discovered that in some cases you do not need to worry about the utility of income in thousands of states, and the attached probabilities, to solve the consumer's choice on how to spread income among states. When preferences contain only a linear and a squared term (a case of diminishing returns) or the probabilities of different stock returns follow a normal distribution (an equation that contains a linear and squared terms as parameters), a simple formulation of a person's investment choices becomes possible. Under Tobin's assumptions we can reformulate the person's decision problem as being one of trading off risk and expected return. Risk, or more precisely the variance of your investment portfolio creates spread in the returns you expect. People are willing to assume more risk only if compensated by a higher level of expected return. One can thus think of a tradeoff people are willing to make between risk and expected return. They invest in risky assets to the point at which their willingness to trade off risk and return is equal to the rate at which they able to trade them off. It is difficult to exaggerate how brilliant is the simplification of the investment problem that flows from these assumptions. Instead of worrying about the investor's optimization problem in potentially millions of possible states of the world, one need only worry about how the investor can trade off risk and return in the stock market."
