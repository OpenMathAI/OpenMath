**Harry Max Markowitz** (August 24, 1927 – June 22, 2023) was an American economist who received the 1989 [John von Neumann Theory Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann_Theory_Prize "John von Neumann Theory Prize") and the 1990 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences").

Markowitz was a professor of finance at the [Rady School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rady_School_of_Management "Rady School of Management") at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego") (UCSD). He is best known for his work in [modern portfolio theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_portfolio_theory "Modern portfolio theory"), studying the effects of asset [risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk "Risk"), [return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_(finance) "Return (finance)"), [correlation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation "Correlation") and [diversification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversification_(finance) "Diversification (finance)") on probable investment portfolio returns.

## Biography

Harry Markowitz was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jews "American Jews") family, the son of Morris and Mildred Markowitz. During high school, Markowitz developed an interest in physics and philosophy, in particular the ideas of [David Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hume "David Hume"), an interest he continued to follow during his undergraduate years at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"). After receiving his [Ph.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Philosophy "Bachelor of Philosophy") in Liberal Arts, Markowitz decided to continue his studies at the University of Chicago, choosing to specialize in economics. There he had the opportunity to study under important economists, including [Milton Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Friedman "Milton Friedman"), [Tjalling Koopmans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tjalling_Koopmans "Tjalling Koopmans"), [Jacob Marschak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Marschak "Jacob Marschak") and [Leonard Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Savage "Leonard Savage"). While still a student, he was invited to become a member of the [Cowles Commission for Research in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowles_Commission_for_Research_in_Economics "Cowles Commission for Research in Economics"), which was in Chicago at the time. He completed his A.M. in Economics from the university in 1950.

Markowitz chose to apply mathematics to the analysis of the [stock market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market "Stock market") as the topic for his dissertation. Jacob Marschak, who was the thesis advisor, encouraged him to pursue the topic, noting that it had also been a favorite interest of [Alfred Cowles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Cowles "Alfred Cowles"), the founder of the Cowles Commission. While researching the then current understanding of stock prices, which at the time consisted in the [present value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Present_value "Present value") model of [John Burr Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Burr_Williams "John Burr Williams"), Markowitz realized that the theory lacks an analysis of the impact of risk. This insight led to the development of his seminal theory of [portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_(finance) "Portfolio (finance)") allocation under uncertainty, published in 1952 by *the [Journal of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Finance "Journal of Finance")*.

In 1952, Harry Markowitz went to work for the [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation "RAND Corporation"), where he met [George Dantzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Dantzig "George Dantzig"). With Dantzig's help, Markowitz continued to research [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_(mathematics) "Optimization (mathematics)") techniques, further developing the [critical line algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_line_method "Critical line method") for the identification of the optimal mean-variance portfolios, relying on what was later named the [Markowitz frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_portfolio_theory "Modern portfolio theory"). It was also in 1952 that Markowitz published *Portfolio Selection*, his first paper dissecting investment portfolio strategy.

In 1954, he received a PhD in economics from the University of Chicago with a thesis on the portfolio theory. The topic was so novel that, while Markowitz was defending his dissertation, Milton Friedman argued his contribution was not economics. During 1955–1956 Markowitz spent a year at the Cowles Foundation, which had moved to [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"), at the invitation of [James Tobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Tobin "James Tobin"). He published the critical line algorithm in a 1956 paper and used this time at the foundation to write a book on portfolio allocation which was published in 1959.

Markowitz won the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") in 1990 while a professor of finance at [Baruch College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_College "Baruch College") of the [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York "City University of New York"). In the preceding year, he received the John von Neumann Theory Prize from the [Operations Research Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_Research_Society_of_America "Operations Research Society of America") (now [Institute for Operations Research and the Management Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences "Institute for Operations Research and the Management Sciences"), [INFORMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INFORMS "INFORMS")) for his contributions in the theory of three fields: portfolio theory; sparse matrix methods; and simulation language programming ([SIMSCRIPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIMSCRIPT "SIMSCRIPT")). Sparse matrix methods are now widely used to solve very large systems of simultaneous equations whose coefficients are mostly zero. SIMSCRIPT has been widely used to program computer simulations of manufacturing, transportation, and computer systems as well as war games. SIMSCRIPT (I) included the [Buddy memory allocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_memory_allocation "Buddy memory allocation") method, which was also developed by Markowitz.
He was elected to the 2002 class of [Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow "Fellow") of the [Institute for Operations Research and the Management Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences "Institute for Operations Research and the Management Sciences").

### CACI

The company that would become [CACI International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CACI_International "CACI International") was founded by Herb Karr and Harry Markowitz on July 17, 1962, as California Analysis Center, Inc. They helped develop [SIMSCRIPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIMSCRIPT "SIMSCRIPT"), the first simulation programming language, at RAND and after it was released to the public domain, CACI was founded to provide support and training for SIMSCRIPT.

In 1968, Markowitz joined Arbitrage Management company founded by [Michael Goodkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Goodkin "Michael Goodkin"). Working with [Paul Samuelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Samuelson "Paul Samuelson") and [Robert Merton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_C._Merton "Robert C. Merton") he created a hedge fund that represents one of the first known attempts at computerized arbitrage trading. He took over as chief executive in 1970. After a successful run as a private hedge fund, AMC was sold to Stuart & Co. in 1971. A year later, Markowitz left the company.

Years later, he was involved with CACI's [SIMSCRIPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIMSCRIPT "SIMSCRIPT") addition of [Object-oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object-oriented_programming "Object-oriented programming") features.

### Post-CACI

Markowitz divided his time between teaching (he was an adjunct professor at the Rady School of Management at the University of California at San Diego, UCSD); video casting lectures; and consulting (out of his Harry Markowitz Company offices). He served on the advisory board of SkyView Investment Advisors, a traditional and alternative investment advisory firm. Markowitz also served on the Investment Committee of LWI Financial Inc. ("[Loring Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loring_Ward?action=edit&redlink=1 "Loring Ward")"), a San Jose, California-based investment advisor; on the advisory panel of [Robert D. Arnott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_D._Arnott "Robert D. Arnott")'s [Newport Beach, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Beach,_California "Newport Beach, California") based investment management firm, [Research Affiliates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Affiliates "Research Affiliates"); on the advisory board of Mark T. Hebner's Irvine, California and internet based wealth management and taxes firm, Index Fund Advisors; and as an advisor to the Investment Committee of [1st Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Global?action=edit&redlink=1 "1st Global"), a Dallas, Texas-based wealth management and investment advisory firm. Markowitz advised and served on the board of ProbabilityManagement.org, a 501(c)(3) non-profit that aims "to reshape the communication and calculation of uncertainty."

Markowitz was co-founder and Chief Architect of [GuidedChoice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GuidedChoice?action=edit&redlink=1 "GuidedChoice"), a 401(k) managed accounts provider and investment advisor. Markowitz's more recent work included designing the backbone software analytics for the GuidedChoice investment solution and heading the GuidedChoice Investment Committee. He was actively involved in designing the next step in the retirement process: assisting retirees with wealth distribution through GuidedSpending.

In 2018 Markowitz donated his [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") and diploma to the [Geisel Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geisel_Library "Geisel Library") of the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego") due to his "love for the campus and the joy he [got] out of teaching" at the [Rady School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rady_School_of_Management "Rady School of Management"). He died on June 22, 2023, at the age of 95. He died in a hospital in [San Diego, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego,_California "San Diego, California") due to complications from pneumonia and sepsis.

## Research

A *Markowitz-efficient portfolio* is one where [diversification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversification_(finance) "Diversification (finance)") cannot lower the portfolio's [risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk "Risk") for a given return expectation (alternately, no additional expected return can be gained without increasing the risk of the portfolio). The Markowitz [Efficient Frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_portfolio_theory "Modern portfolio theory") is the set of all portfolios that will give the highest expected return for each given level of risk. These concepts of efficiency were essential to the development of the [capital asset pricing model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_asset_pricing_model "Capital asset pricing model").

Markowitz also co-edited the textbook *The Theory and Practice of Investment Management* with [Frank J. Fabozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_J._Fabozzi "Frank J. Fabozzi") of [Yale School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_School_of_Management "Yale School of Management"). In the same line, Markowitz was part of the editorial board of AESTIMATIO, the IEB International Journal of Finance.
