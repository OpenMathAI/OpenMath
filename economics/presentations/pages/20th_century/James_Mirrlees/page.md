**Sir James Alexander Mirrlees** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh "Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh") [FBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_British_Academy "Fellow of the British Academy") (5 July 1936 – 29 August 2018) was a British economist and winner of the 1996 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences"). He was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_honours_system "British honours system") in the [1997 Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Birthday_Honours "1997 Birthday Honours").

## Early life and education

Born in [Minnigaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnigaff "Minnigaff"), [Wigtownshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigtownshire "Wigtownshire"), Mirrlees was educated at [Douglas Ewart High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Ewart_High_School "Douglas Ewart High School"), then at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh "University of Edinburgh") ([MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_(Scotland) "Master of Arts (Scotland)") in Mathematics and Natural Philosophy in 1957) and [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge") ([Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Mathematical_Tripos "Cambridge Mathematical Tripos") and PhD in 1963 with thesis title *Optimum Planning for a Dynamic Economy*, supervised by [Richard Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Stone "Richard Stone")). He was a very active student debater. A contemporary, [Quentin Skinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Skinner "Quentin Skinner"), has suggested that Mirrlees was a member of the [Cambridge Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Apostles "Cambridge Apostles") along with fellow Nobel Laureate [Amartya Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amartya_Sen "Amartya Sen") during the period.

## Economics

Between 1968 and 1976, Mirrlees was a visiting professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") three times. He was also a visiting professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") (1986) and [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") (1989). He taught at both [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University "Oxford University") (as [Edgeworth Professor of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgeworth_Professor_of_Economics "Edgeworth Professor of Economics") 1968–1995) and [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") (1963–1968 and 1995–2018).

During his time at Oxford, he published papers on [economic models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_model "Economic model") for which he would eventually be awarded his Nobel Prize. The papers centred on [asymmetric information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_information "Asymmetric information"), which determines the extent to which they should affect the optimal rate of saving in an economy. Among other results, he demonstrated the principles of "[moral hazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_hazard "Moral hazard")" and "[optimal income taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_income_taxation "Optimal income taxation")" discussed in the books of [William Vickrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Vickrey "William Vickrey"). The methodology has since become the standard in the field.

Mirrlees and Vickrey shared the 1996 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") "for their fundamental contributions to the economic theory of incentives under asymmetric information".

Mirrlees was also co-creator, with MIT Professor [Peter A. Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_A._Diamond "Peter A. Diamond"), of the Diamond–Mirrlees efficiency theorem, which was developed in 1971.

Mirrlees was emeritus [Professor of Political Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_of_Political_Economy,_Cambridge_University "Professor of Political Economy, Cambridge University") at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") and a Fellow of [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"). He spent several months a year at the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne "University of Melbourne"), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"). He was the Distinguished Professor-at-Large of the [Chinese University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_University_of_Hong_Kong "Chinese University of Hong Kong") as well as [University of Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Macau "University of Macau").

In 2009, he was appointed Founding Master of the [Morningside College of the Chinese University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morningside_College_of_the_Chinese_University_of_Hong_Kong "Morningside College of the Chinese University of Hong Kong").

Mirrlees was a member of [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland "Scotland")'s [Council of Economic Advisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Economic_Advisers_(Scotland) "Council of Economic Advisers (Scotland)"). He also led the [Mirrlees Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirrlees_Review "Mirrlees Review"), a review of the UK tax system by the [Institute for Fiscal Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Fiscal_Studies "Institute for Fiscal Studies").

His PhD students included eminent academics and policymakers like professor [Franklin Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Allen "Franklin Allen"), Sir [Partha Dasgupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partha_Dasgupta "Partha Dasgupta"), professor [Huw Dixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huw_Dixon "Huw Dixon"), professor [Hyun-Song Shin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyun-Song_Shin "Hyun-Song Shin"), [Lord Nicholas Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Stern,_Baron_Stern_of_Brentford "Nicholas Stern, Baron Stern of Brentford"), professor [Anthony Venables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Venables "Anthony Venables"), [Sir John Vickers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Vickers "John Vickers"), and professor [Zhang Weiying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Weiying "Zhang Weiying"). He died in Cambridge, England, on 29 August 2018.

## Personal life

Mirrlees became an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist").
