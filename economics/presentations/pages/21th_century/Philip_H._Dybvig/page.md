**Philip Hallen Dybvig** (born May 22, 1955) is an American economist. He is the [Boatmen's Bancshares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boatmen's_Bancshares "Boatmen's Bancshares") Professor of Banking and Finance at the [Olin Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olin_Business_School "Olin Business School") of [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis").

## Career

Dybvig attended [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University "Indiana University") and received in 1976 a BA in mathematics and physics. He then attended the economics PhD program at [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") for one year before transferring to [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"), where he received the MA (1978), MPhil (1978), and PhD (1979) degrees in economics. His thesis, titled "Recovering additive utility functions", was supervised by [Stephen A. Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_A._Ross "Stephen A. Ross").

Dybvig specializes in [asset pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_pricing "Asset pricing"), banking, investments, and [corporate governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_governance "Corporate governance"). He was formerly a professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"), and assistant professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University").

Dybvig was president of the [Western Finance Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Finance_Association "Western Finance Association") from 2002 to 2003, and was director of the Institute of Financial Studies at [Southwestern University of Finance and Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_University_of_Finance_and_Economics "Southwestern University of Finance and Economics") ([Chengdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu "Chengdu"), [PRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRC "PRC")) from 2010 to 2021. He has been editor or associate editor of multiple journals, including the *Journal of Economic Theory*, *Finance and Stochastics*, *Journal of Finance*, *Journal of Financial Intermediation*, *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis*, and *Review of Financial Studies*.

Dybvig is known for his work with [Douglas Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Diamond "Douglas Diamond") on the [Diamond–Dybvig model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond–Dybvig_model "Diamond–Dybvig model") of bank runs.

Dybvig was awarded the 2022 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences"), jointly with Diamond and [Ben Bernanke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Bernanke "Ben Bernanke"), "for research on banks and financial crises". Dybvig and Diamond wrote “Bank Runs, Deposit Insurance, and Liquidity” in 1983, in which they first introduced their mathematical model describing the vulnerability of banks during financial crises.

### Controversies

In 2022, seven former students attending [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis") alleged that Dybvig sexually harassed them. One of the alumna alleged that Dybvig pulled her onto his lap for a photo, gifted her chocolates, touched her hand while sitting on a couch together, and messaged her on Facebook telling her that he missed her smile. Another student alleged that Dybvig wrapped his hand and fingers around her waist for a photo while not doing the same to a man on his other side in the photo.
