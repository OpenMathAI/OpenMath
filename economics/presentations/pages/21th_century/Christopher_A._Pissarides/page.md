**Sir Christopher Antoniou Pissarides** [FBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_British_Academy "Fellow of the British Academy") ([/ˌpɪsəˈriːdiːz/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language"): Χριστόφορος Αντωνίου Πισσαρίδης; born 20 February 1948) is a Cypriot economist. He is [Regius Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regius_Professor "Regius Professor") of Economics at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics"), and Professor of European Studies at the [University of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cyprus "University of Cyprus"). His research focuses on [macroeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomics "Macroeconomics"), [labour economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_economics "Labour economics"), [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth "Economic growth"), and [economic policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_policy "Economic policy"). In 2010 he received the [Nobel Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") along with [Peter Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Diamond "Peter Diamond") and [Dale Mortensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_T._Mortensen "Dale T. Mortensen"), "for their analysis of markets with [theory of search frictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_theory "Search theory")."

## Early life

Pissarides was born in [Nicosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicosia "Nicosia"), Cyprus, into a [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox "Greek Orthodox") family from the village of [Agros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agros,_Cyprus "Agros, Cyprus"). He first studied at the [Pancyprian Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancyprian_Gymnasium "Pancyprian Gymnasium") in Nicosia. After [serving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_service "National service") in the [Cypriot National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypriot_National_Guard "Cypriot National Guard"), he attended the [University of Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Essex "University of Essex"), where he received undergraduate and graduate degrees in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics"). He then studied at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics"), where he received a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in economics, writing a thesis titled *Individual behaviour in markets with imperfect information* under the supervision of [Michio Morishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michio_Morishima "Michio Morishima").

## Career

Pissarides is [Regius Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regius_Professor "Regius Professor") of Economics at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics"), where he has taught since 1976. He is chairman of the [Centre for Macroeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Macroeconomics "Centre for Macroeconomics"), which deploys economists from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), the London School of Economics, the [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London"), the [Bank of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_England "Bank of England"), and the [National Institute of Economic and Social Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Economic_and_Social_Research "National Institute of Economic and Social Research").

He has held a lectureship at the [University of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southampton "University of Southampton") (1974–76), and visiting professorships at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") (1979–80) and the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") (1990–91).

He served as the chairman of the National Economy Council of the [Republic of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Cyprus "Republic of Cyprus") during the [country's financial crisis in 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012–2013_Cypriot_financial_crisis "2012–2013 Cypriot financial crisis"), and resigned to focus on his academic work at the end of 2014.

In 2018, in collaboration with [Naomi Climer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Climer "Naomi Climer") and Anna Thomas, he founded the Institute for the Future of Work, a London-based research and development institute exploring how new technologies transform work and working lives.

In February 2020, [Greek prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Greece "Prime Minister of Greece") [Kyriakos Mitsotakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyriakos_Mitsotakis "Kyriakos Mitsotakis") appointed Pissarides to the chairmanship of a committee tasked with drafting a long-term growth strategy for the country. Since September 2020 he has been chairman of the economic council of [EuroAfrica Interconnector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroAfrica_Interconnector "EuroAfrica Interconnector").

In June 2021, it was announced that he would lead a review into the future of work and wellbeing, a three-year collaboration between the Institute for the Future of Work, [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London "Imperial College London"), and [Warwick Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick_Business_School "Warwick Business School"), funded by a £1.8 million grant from the [Nuffield Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuffield_Foundation "Nuffield Foundation"). The Pissarides Review into the Future of Work and Wellbeing was launched in March 2022.

## Academic contributions

One of Pissarides' papers, "Job Creation and Job Destruction in the Theory of Unemployment" (with [Dale Mortensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Mortensen "Dale Mortensen")), was published in the *[Review of Economic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_of_Economic_Studies "Review of Economic Studies")* in 1994. He has also written the book *Equilibrium Unemployment Theory*, a study of the macroeconomics of unemployment.
