**Dale Thomas Mortensen** (February 2, 1939 – January 9, 2014) was an American economist, a professor at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University"), and a winner of the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences").

## Early life and education

Mortensen was born in Enterprise, Oregon. He received his BA in economics from [Willamette University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_University "Willamette University") in 1961 and his PhD in Economics from [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University "Carnegie Mellon University") in 1967.

## Career

[Peter Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Diamond "Peter Diamond"), Dale T. Mortensen, [Christopher A. Pissarides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_A._Pissarides "Christopher A. Pissarides"), [Konstantin Novoselov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Novoselov "Konstantin Novoselov"), [Andre Geim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Geim "Andre Geim"), [Akira Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Suzuki_(chemist) "Akira Suzuki (chemist)"), [Ei-ichi Negishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ei-ichi_Negishi "Ei-ichi Negishi"), and [Richard Heck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Heck "Richard Heck"), Nobel Prize Laureates 2010, at a press conference at the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences") in Stockholm

Mortensen had been on the faculty of [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University") since 1965 and a professor of Managerial Economics and Decision Sciences at the [Kellogg School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg_School_of_Management "Kellogg School of Management") since 1980. He was the Niels Bohr Visiting Professor at the School of Economics and Management, [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University "Aarhus University"), from 2006 to 2010.

He was awarded the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") jointly with [Christopher A. Pissarides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_A._Pissarides "Christopher A. Pissarides") from the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics") and [Peter A. Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_A._Diamond "Peter A. Diamond") from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") in 2010 "for their analysis of markets with [search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_theory "Search theory") frictions". In May 2011, Mortensen was awarded an honorary doctorate from his alma mater, Willamette University. He was married to [Beverly Mortensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Mortensen "Beverly Mortensen"), also a Northwestern Professor.

Mortensen's research focused on [labor economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_economics "Labour economics"), [macroeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomics "Macroeconomics") and [economic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_theory "Economic theory"). He is especially known for his pioneering work on the [search and matching theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_and_matching_theory "Search and matching theory") of [frictional unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_types#Frictional_unemployment "Unemployment types"). He extended the insights from this work to study labor turnover and reallocation, research and development, and personal relationships.

Mortensen was a past president of the Society of Economic Dynamics and one of the founding editors of the *[Review of Economic Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_of_Economic_Dynamics "Review of Economic Dynamics")*.

## Death

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Grave_of_Dale_Thomas_Mortensen_%281939%E2%80%932014%29_at_Memorial_Park_Cemetery%2C_Skokie%2C_IL.jpg/250px-Grave_of_Dale_Thomas_Mortensen_%281939%E2%80%932014%29_at_Memorial_Park_Cemetery%2C_Skokie%2C_IL.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Grave_of_Dale_Thomas_Mortensen_(1939–2014)_at_Memorial_Park_Cemetery,_Skokie,_IL.jpg)

Mortensen's grave at Memorial Park Cemetery

Mortensen died of stage 4 lung cancer on January 9, 2014, at the age of 74, at his home in Wilmette. He was buried at Memorial Park Cemetery in Skokie.

In 2019 Mortensen's family donated his [Nobel Prize medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") to [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University").

## The Dale T. Mortensen Building

In February 2011, Mortensen had a building named in his honor at [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University "Aarhus University"). The Dale T. Mortensen Building is the central hub for all international and PhD activities and contains the new PhD House, Dale's Café, the university's International Centre and the new IC Dormitory for international PhD students.
