**Philippe Mario Aghion** [FBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_British_Academy "Fellow of the British Academy") (French: [[filip aɡjɔ̃]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 17 August 1956) is a French [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist") who is Chair of Economics of Institutions, Innovation and Growth professor at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France"), Kurt Björklund Chaired Professor in Innovation and Growth at [INSEAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INSEAD "INSEAD"), and Visiting Professor of Economics at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics").

Aghion and [Peter Howitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Howitt_(economist) "Peter Howitt (economist)") are known for the [Aghion–Howitt model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghion–Howitt_model "Aghion–Howitt model"). For this work, they shared half of the 2025 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") "for the theory of sustained growth through [creative destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_destruction "Creative destruction")".

## Early life and education

Philippe Aghion was born in Paris in 1956, he is the son of [Gaby Aghion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaby_Aghion "Gaby Aghion"), a French fashion designer and founder of the French fashion house [Chloé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloé "Chloé"). Gaby is said to have coined the phrase *[prêt-à-porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ready-to-wear "Ready-to-wear")*. His father, Raymond Aghion, had an [art gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_gallery "Art gallery") in [Boulevard Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_Saint-Germain "Boulevard Saint-Germain"). Both his parents are from [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews_in_Greece "Jews in Greece") families from [Alexandria, Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria,_Egypt "Alexandria, Egypt"). They later moved to [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), in the [Quartier latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartier_latin "Quartier latin") before buying a house in [Neuilly-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuilly-sur-Seine "Neuilly-sur-Seine"). In an interview, Aghion recalled that he grew up surrounded by artists, including [Karl Lagerfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Lagerfeld "Karl Lagerfeld").

Aghion graduated from the mathematics section of the [École normale supérieure de Cachan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure_de_Cachan "École Normale Supérieure de Cachan") (now [ENS Paris-Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENS_Paris-Saclay "ENS Paris-Saclay"), [University of Paris-Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris-Saclay "University of Paris-Saclay")), and obtained a *[diplôme d'études approfondies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplôme_d'études_approfondies "Diplôme d'études approfondies")* and a *doctorat de troisième cycle* (third cycle doctorate) in [mathematical economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_economics "Mathematical economics") from the [Université Paris I Panthéon-Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_1_Panthéon-Sorbonne_University "Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne University"). He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in economics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") in 1987.

## Career

Aghion began his academic career in 1987 when he joined the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") as an Assistant Professor. In 1989 he returned to France and became a researcher at the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research "French National Centre for Scientific Research") (CNRS). In 1990, he was appointed Deputy Chief Economist of the [European Bank for Reconstruction and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Bank_for_Reconstruction_and_Development "European Bank for Reconstruction and Development") (EBRD), before moving to the [Nuffield College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuffield_College,_Oxford "Nuffield College, Oxford") and then [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London") in 1996. In 2002, he returned to Harvard where he became the Robert C. Waggoner Professor in Economics, a chair he held until 2015 when he was named Centennial Professor of Economics at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics") (LSE).

In 2015, as he became professor at LSE, Aghion was also appointed Chair of Economics at [College de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France"), a special academic institution in France. Statutory chair professorship at College de France is a position highly regarded among French academia.

In 2020, Aghion became The Kurt Björklund Chaired Professor in Innovation and Growth at [INSEAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INSEAD "INSEAD") and the academic director of INSEAD’s Economics of Innovation Lab. That year, he became visiting professor at LSE and continued to be an associate at LSE's Centre for Economic Performance.

### Other activities

Aghion was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 2009 and he is a member of the Executive and Supervisory Committee (ESC) of [CERGE-EI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERGE-EI "CERGE-EI"). He was president of the [European Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Association "European Economic Association") in 2017. He has been an editor of the *[Annual Review of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Review_of_Economics "Annual Review of Economics")* since 2018.

Ahead of the [2012 French presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_French_presidential_election "2012 French presidential election"), Aghion co-signed an appeal of several economists in support of candidate [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Hollande "François Hollande").

In 2016, Aghion was appointed by [United Nations Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary-General "United Nations Secretary-General") [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon "Ban Ki-moon") to an expert group advising the High-Level Commission on Health Employment and Economic Growth, which was co-chaired by presidents [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Hollande "François Hollande") of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") and [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma "Jacob Zuma") of [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa"). In 2021, he was appointed to the [World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank "World Bank")–[International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund") High-Level Advisory Group (HLAG) on Sustainable and Inclusive Recovery and Growth, co-chaired by [Mari Pangestu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari_Pangestu "Mari Pangestu"), [Ceyla Pazarbasioglu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceyla_Pazarbasioglu "Ceyla Pazarbasioglu"), and [Nicholas Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Stern "Nicholas Stern").

Additional advisory activities include:

- [Center for Economic Research and Graduate Education – Economics Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERGE-EI "CERGE-EI") (CERGE-EI), Member of the Board of Trustees
- Centre de Recerca en Economia Internacional (CREI), [Pompeu Fabra University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeu_Fabra_University "Pompeu Fabra University") (UPF), Member of the Advisory Board

## Research

At [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University "Boston University") (February 2015).

In 1992, together with [Peter Howitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Howitt_(economist) "Peter Howitt (economist)"), he proposed a foundational model of Schumpeterian [endogenous growth theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endogenous_growth_theory "Endogenous growth theory"), centered on innovation and [creative destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_destruction "Creative destruction").

In the 2000s, he explored the links between competition, institutions, and growth, notably the inverted-U relationship between competition intensity and innovation.

AOC Media has criticized Aghion for techno-optimism, an attachment to GDP growth as the headline indicator, and a limited understanding of the contemporary multi-factor environmental crisis.

### Aghion–Howitt (1992) and the paradox of creative destruction

According to [Ufuk Akcigit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ufuk_Akcigit "Ufuk Akcigit"), the framework developed by Philippe Aghion and Peter Howitt in their 1992 article revolutionized the analysis of endogenous economic growth dynamics by enabling back-and-forth between theory and firm-level data, which explains the model’s prominence in the economic literature. Their approach is characterized by the introduction of the Schumpeterian mechanism of “creative destruction,” whereby innovations render old technologies obsolete (the “business-stealing” effect) and govern firm entry and exit. Innovation effort is a response to firms’ pursuit of rents. These rents act as a carrot and creative destruction as a stick; however, when rents become too large, they allow dominant firms to erect barriers to entry. At the same time, the motivation to innovate fades, and growth can slow when the stick is gone and the carrot already secured. It is notably the incorporation of firm [heterogeneity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneity_and_heterogeneity "Homogeneity and heterogeneity") that marks the originality of this paradox, allowing the study of growth dynamics depending on whether firms are small or large, incumbents or entrants, at the [technological frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_frontier?action=edit&redlink=1 "Technology frontier") or in an imitator position.

### The "Middle-Income Trap"

Philippe Aghion has also contributed to the analysis of the "middle-income trap," referring to the difficulty some emerging economies face in crossing the threshold to [advanced-economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_economy "Advanced economy") status. In his view, growth driven by [capital accumulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_accumulation "Capital accumulation") and technological imitation first enables rapid catch-up but reaches its limits as countries approach the technological frontier.

## Aghion Report (2010)

Philippe Aghion led a working group of ten international experts whose work initially focused on an international comparison of university autonomy, and then on the implementation of campuses of academic excellence ([Initiative d'excellence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initiative_d'excellence?action=edit&redlink=1 "Initiative d'excellence")). A two-part report was submitted in January 2010 to the Minister of Higher Education [Valérie Pécresse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valérie_Pécresse "Valérie Pécresse").

This report recommended establishing "balanced governance" in universities. Drawing on the examples of Harvard, MIT, Oxford, or Cambridge, it acknowledged that there is no single governance model.

It proposed the creation of two governing bodies at the head of universities. The first, the board of directors, would be composed mostly of external members, who would appoint a president with extensive powers. The second would be embodied in an "academic senate", a genuine forum for scientific and pedagogical proposals.

## Political views

As a student in France, he was a communist sympathizer.

During the [2012 French presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_French_presidential_election "2012 French presidential election"), he signed the appeal of economists supporting candidate [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Hollande "François Hollande") because of "the relevance of the proposed options, in particular with regard to the recovery of growth and employment". In 2017, he expressed his support for [Emmanuel Macron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Macron "Emmanuel Macron").
