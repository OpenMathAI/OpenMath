**Roger Bruce Myerson** (born March 29, 1951) is an American [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist") and a Distinguished Service Professor at the University of Chicago's [Harris School of Public Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_School_of_Public_Policy_Studies "Harris School of Public Policy Studies"). In 2007, he was the winner of the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") with [Leonid Hurwicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Hurwicz "Leonid Hurwicz") and [Eric Maskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Maskin "Eric Maskin") for "having laid the foundations of [mechanism design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanism_design "Mechanism design") theory". He was elected a Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 2019.

In particular, by extending the [revelation principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revelation_principle "Revelation principle") to accommodate [incomplete information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incomplete_information "Incomplete information") environments, Myerson proved that complex regulatory and [auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auction "Auction") environments, as treated in [auction theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auction_theory "Auction theory"), can be reduced to [incentive-compatible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incentive_compatibility "Incentive compatibility") direct mechanisms.

## Biography

Roger Myerson was born in 1951 in Boston into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") family. He attended [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), where he received his [A.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts"), *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude "Summa cum laude")*, and [S.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") in [applied mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematics "Applied mathematics") in 1973. He completed his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in applied mathematics from Harvard University in 1976. His doctorate thesis was entitled *A Theory of Cooperative Games*.

From 1976 to 2001, Myerson was a professor of economics at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University")'s [Kellogg School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg_School_of_Management "Kellogg School of Management"), where he conducted much of his Nobel-winning research. From 1978 to 1979, he was visiting researcher at [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University "Bielefeld University"). He was visiting professor of economics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") from 1985 to 1986 and from 2000 to 2001. He became professor of economics at Chicago in 2001. Currently, he is the inaugural David L. Pearson Distinguished Service Professor of Global Conflict Studies at the University of Chicago and also appears in the directory of Peter Thiel's Dialog organization.

## Awards and honors

### Bank of Sweden Nobel Memorial Prize

Myerson was one of the three winners of the 2007 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences"), the other two being [Leonid Hurwicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Hurwicz "Leonid Hurwicz") of the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota"), and [Eric Maskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Maskin "Eric Maskin") of the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study"). He was awarded the prize for his contributions to [mechanism design theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanism_design_theory "Mechanism design theory").

Myerson made a path-breaking contribution to mechanism design theory when he discovered a fundamental connection between the allocation to be implemented and the monetary transfers needed to induce informed agents to reveal their information truthfully. Mechanism design theory allows for people to distinguish situations in which markets work well from those in which they do not. The theory has helped economists identify efficient trading mechanisms, regulation schemes, and voting procedures. Today, the theory plays a central role in many areas of [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics") and parts of [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science "Political science").

### Memberships and honors

Myerson is a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"), the [Council on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_on_Foreign_Relations "Council on Foreign Relations"), and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"). He is a Fellow of the [Game Theory Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Theory_Society "Game Theory Society"), and serves as an advisory board member on the *International Journal of Game Theory*. Myerson holds an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate "Honorary doctorate") from the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel "University of Basel") in 2002 and received the Jean-Jacques Laffont Prize in 2009. He also served on the Social Sciences jury for the [Infosys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys_Prize "Infosys Prize") in 2016.

## Personal life

In 1980, Myerson married Regina (née Weber) and the couple had two children, Daniel and Rebecca. His daughter, Rebecca, is a [health economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_economist "Health economist") at the [University of Wisconsin-Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin-Madison "University of Wisconsin-Madison").

## Political views

In June 2024, 16 [Nobel Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") laureates, including Myerson, signed an open letter arguing that [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")'s fiscal and trade policies coupled with efforts to limit the [Federal Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve "Federal Reserve")'s independence would reignite inflation in the United States.
