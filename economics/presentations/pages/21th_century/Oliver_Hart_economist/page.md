**Sir Oliver Simon D'Arcy Hart** (born October 9, 1948) is a British-American economist, currently the Lewis P. and Linda L. Geyser University Professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). Together with [Bengt R. Holmström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengt_R._Holmström "Bengt R. Holmström"), he received the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") in 2016.

## Biography

Oliver Hart was born in Britain to [Philip D'Arcy Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_D'Arcy_Hart "Philip D'Arcy Hart"), a medical researcher, and Ruth Meyer, a gynecologist. Both his parents were [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish"); his father was a member of the [Montagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron_Swaythling "Baron Swaythling") family; Oliver's great-grandfather was [Samuel Montagu, 1st Baron Swaythling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Montagu,_1st_Baron_Swaythling "Samuel Montagu, 1st Baron Swaythling").

Hart earned his B.A. in mathematics at [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge "King's College, Cambridge"), in 1969 (where his contemporaries included the former [Bank of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_England "Bank of England") Governor [Mervyn King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mervyn_King,_Baron_King_of_Lothbury "Mervyn King, Baron King of Lothbury")), his [M.A. in economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Economics "Master of Economics") at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick "University of Warwick") in 1972, and his Ph.D. in economics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in 1974. He completed his doctoral dissertation, titled "Essays in the economics of uncertainty", under the supervision of [Michael Rothschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Rothschild "Michael Rothschild"). He was a lecturer in economics at [University of Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Essex "University of Essex"), a fellow at [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge "Churchill College, Cambridge"), and then a professor at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics"). In 1984, he returned to the U.S., where he taught at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") and, since 1993, at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). He became the first Andrew E. Furer Professor of Economics in 1997 and was chairman of the Harvard economics department from 2000 to 2003. He is a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society "Econometric Society"), of the [American Finance Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Finance_Association "American Finance Association"), a corresponding fellow of the [British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy "British Academy"), and a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"). He has been president of the [American Law and Economics Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Law_and_Economics_Association "American Law and Economics Association") and vice president of the [American Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Association "American Economic Association") and has several honorary degrees.

In 2016, Hart won the Nobel Prize in Economics with [Bengt Holmström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengt_Holmström "Bengt Holmström") for their work on [contract theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_theory "Contract theory"), including his work on how ownership should be allocated and when contracting is beneficial over ownership.

Hart was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") as a [Knight Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in the [2023 Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Birthday_Honours "2023 Birthday Honours") for services to economic theory.

## Academics

Hart is an expert on [contract theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_theory#Economics "Contract theory"), [theory of the firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_the_firm "Theory of the firm"), [corporate finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_finance "Corporate finance"), and [law and economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_and_economics "Law and economics"). His research centers on the roles that [ownership structure and contractual arrangements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incomplete_contracts "Incomplete contracts") play in the governance and boundaries of corporations. His research has emphasized the importance of contractual incompleteness — the inability of parties to contract on every contingency. He has used his theoretical work on firms in two legal cases as a government expert (Black and Decker v. U.S.A. and WFC Holdings Corp. (Wells Fargo) v. U.S.A.) where companies claimed tax-related benefits as a result from selling some of their business. The government used Hart's research to claim that because the companies retained control of the sold assets, they could not lay claim to the tax benefits.

## Political views

In June 2024, 16 [Nobel Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") laureates, including Hart, signed an open letter arguing that [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")’s fiscal and trade policies coupled with efforts to limit the [Federal Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve "Federal Reserve")'s independence would reignite inflation in the United States.

## Personal life

Hart holds dual British and United States citizenship.

He is married to Rita B. Goldberg, a Harvard literature professor and author of the second-generation Holocaust memoir *Motherland: Growing Up With the Holocaust*. They have two sons, Daniel and Benjamin.
