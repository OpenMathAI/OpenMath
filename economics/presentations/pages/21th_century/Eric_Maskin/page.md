**Eric Stark Maskin** (born December 12, 1950) is an American economist and mathematician. He was jointly awarded the 2007 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") with [Leonid Hurwicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Hurwicz "Leonid Hurwicz") and [Roger Myerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Myerson "Roger Myerson") "for having laid the foundations of [mechanism design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanism_design "Mechanism design") theory". He is the [Adams University Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University_Professor "Harvard University Professor") and Professor of Economics and Mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University").

Until 2011, he was the [Albert O. Hirschman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_O._Hirschman "Albert O. Hirschman") Professor of Social Science at the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study"), and a visiting lecturer with the rank of professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University").

## Early life and education

Maskin was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") on December 12, 1950, into a Jewish family, and spent his youth in [Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine,_New_Jersey "Alpine, New Jersey"), New Jersey. He graduated from [Tenafly High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenafly_High_School "Tenafly High School") in [Tenafly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenafly,_New_Jersey "Tenafly, New Jersey"), New Jersey, in 1968. In 1972, he graduated with [A.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in mathematics from [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College "Harvard College"). In 1974, he earned [A.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") in applied mathematics and in 1976 earned a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in applied mathematics, both at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). In 1975–76, he was a visiting student at [Darwin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_College,_Cambridge "Darwin College, Cambridge"), Cambridge University.

## Career and topics

In 1976, after earning his doctorate, Maskin became a research fellow at [Jesus College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Cambridge "Jesus College, Cambridge"), Cambridge University. In the following year, he joined the faculty at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"). In 1985 he returned to Harvard as the Louis Berkman Professor of Economics, where he remained until 2000. In 1987, he was a visiting fellow at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge "St John's College, Cambridge"). During the 1990s he advised the [Bank of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Italy "Bank of Italy") on the operation of its [bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_(finance) "Bond (finance)") auctions. In 2000, he moved to the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study "Institute for Advanced Study") in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey "Princeton, New Jersey"), New Jersey. In addition to his position at the Princeton Institute, Maskin is the director of the Jerusalem Summer School in Economic Theory at [The Institute for Advanced Studies at The Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies_at_The_Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem "The Institute for Advanced Studies at The Hebrew University of Jerusalem"). In 2010, he was conferred an honorary doctoral degree in economics from The [University of Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambodia "University of Cambodia"). In 2011, Maskin returned to [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") as the Adams University Professor and professor of economics and mathematics.

Maskin has worked in diverse areas of [economic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_theory "Economic theory"), such as [game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_theory "Game theory"), the economics of incentives, and contract theory. He is particularly known for his papers on [mechanism design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanism_design "Mechanism design")/[implementation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implementation_theory "Implementation theory") and [dynamic games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_game "Dynamic game"). With [Jean Tirole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Tirole "Jean Tirole"), he advanced the concept of [Markov perfect equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_perfect_equilibrium "Markov perfect equilibrium"). His research projects include comparing different electoral rules, examining the causes of inequality, and studying coalition formation.

Maskin is a Fellow of the *[American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences")*, *[Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society "Econometric Society")*, and the *[European Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Association "European Economic Association")*, and a Corresponding Fellow of the *[British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy "British Academy")*. He was president of the *Econometric Society* in 2003.

In 2014, Maskin was appointed as a visiting professor at [Covenant University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_University "Covenant University"), Nigeria.

In September 2017, Maskin received the title of [HEC Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HEC_Paris "HEC Paris") [Honoris Causa Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree "Honorary degree"). He also served on the Social Sciences jury for the [Infosys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys_Prize "Infosys Prize") in 2018.

Furthermore, he is the chairman of the advisory board of the [International Economics Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Economics_Olympiad "International Economics Olympiad"). In June 2026 he became the president of the [International Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Economic_Association "International Economic Association") succeeding [Elhanan Helpman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elhanan_Helpman "Elhanan Helpman") in the role.

## Political views

Along with [Ned Foley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ned_Foley "Ned Foley"), Maskin has proposed the use of [Baldwin's voting method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanson's_method#Baldwin_method "Nanson's method"), under the name "Total Vote Runoff", as a way to fix problems with the [instant-runoff method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant-runoff_voting "Instant-runoff voting") ("Ranked Choice Voting") in U.S. jurisdictions that use it, ensuring majority support of the winner and electing more broadly-acceptable candidates.

In June 2024, 16 [Nobel Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") laureates, including Maskin, signed an open letter arguing that [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")’s fiscal and trade policies coupled with efforts to limit the [Federal Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve "Federal Reserve")'s independence would reignite inflation in the United States.

## Software patents

Maskin suggested that [software patents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_patent_debate "Software patent debate") inhibit [innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovation "Innovation") rather than stimulate progress. Software, semiconductor, and computer industries have been innovative despite historically weak patent protection, he argued. Innovation in those industries has been sequential and complementary, so competition can increase firms' future profits. In such a dynamic industry, "patent protection may reduce overall innovation and social welfare". A natural experiment occurred in the 1980s when patent protection was extended to software", wrote Maskin with co-author [James Bessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bessen "James Bessen"). "Standard arguments would predict that R&D intensity and productivity should have increased among patenting firms. Consistent with our model, however, these increases did not occur". Other evidence supporting this model includes a distinctive pattern of [cross-licensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-licensing "Cross-licensing") and a positive relationship between rates of innovation and firm entry.
