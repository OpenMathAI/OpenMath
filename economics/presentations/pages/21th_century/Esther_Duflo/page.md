**Esther Caroline Duflo**, [FBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_British_Academy "Fellow of the British Academy") (French: [[ɛstɛʁ dyflo]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 25 October 1972) is a [French-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Americans "French Americans") economist, and the Abdul Latif Jameel Professor of Poverty Alleviation and Development Economics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (MIT). In 2019, she was jointly awarded the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") alongside her husband [Abhijit Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhijit_Banerjee "Abhijit Banerjee") and [Michael Kremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kremer "Michael Kremer") "for their experimental approach to [alleviating global poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_reduction "Poverty reduction")".

In addition to her academic appointment, Duflo is the co-founder and co-director of the [Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Latif_Jameel_Poverty_Action_Lab "Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab") (J-PAL), an MIT-based research center promoting the use of randomized controlled trials in policy evaluation. As of 2020, more than 400 million people had been impacted by programs tested by [J-PAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Latif_Jameel_Poverty_Action_Lab "Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab") affiliated researchers. Since 2024, Duflo has also been the president of the [Paris School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_School_of_Economics "Paris School of Economics") alongside her appointment at MIT. In October 2025, the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich") announced that Duflo and Banerjee would be joining the faculty of the UZH School of Business, Economics, and Informatics in July 2026.

Duflo is a Research Associate of the [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Economic_Research "National Bureau of Economic Research") (NBER), a board member of the [Bureau for Research and Economic Analysis of Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_for_Research_and_Economic_Analysis_of_Development "Bureau for Research and Economic Analysis of Development") (BREAD), and the director of the [development economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_economics "Development economics") program of the [Centre for Economic Policy Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Economic_Policy_Research "Centre for Economic Policy Research"). Her research focuses on the [microeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microeconomics "Microeconomics") of development and spans topics such as household behavior, education, [financial inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_inclusion "Financial inclusion"), [political economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_economy "Political economy"), gender, and health. Prior to receiving the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences"), Duflo was awarded the [Calvó-Armengol International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvó-Armengol_International_Prize "Calvó-Armengol International Prize") (2019), the [Elaine Bennett Research Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_Bennett_Research_Prize "Elaine Bennett Research Prize") (2002) and the [John Bates Clark Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bates_Clark_Medal "John Bates Clark Medal") (2010) by the [American Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Association "American Economic Association").

Together with [Abhijit Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhijit_Banerjee "Abhijit Banerjee"), Duflo is the co-author of *[Poor Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poor_Economics "Poor Economics")* and *[Good Economics for Hard Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Economics_for_Hard_Times "Good Economics for Hard Times")*, published in April 2011 and November 2019, respectively. According to the [Open Syllabus Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Syllabus_Project "Open Syllabus Project"), Duflo is the seventh most frequently cited author on college syllabi for economics courses.

## Early life and education

Duflo was born on 25 October 1972 to Violaine and [Michel Duflo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Duflo "Michel Duflo") at the Port Royal Hospital in Paris, France. Her father was a mathematics professor, and her mother was a pediatrician. During Duflo's childhood, her mother often traveled, volunteering for a humanitarian [NGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NGO "NGO") providing support to childhood victims of war. Duflo was raised and attended schools until grade 11 in [Asnières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asnières-sur-Seine "Asnières-sur-Seine"), a western suburb of Paris. Duflo completed her secondary schooling in 1990 at the [Lycée Henri-IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Henri-IV "Lycée Henri-IV"), a magnet school in central Paris.

After secondary school, Duflo pursued an undergraduate degree at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_(Paris) "École normale supérieure (Paris)") in Paris, where she specialized in history and economics. She intended to study history prior to beginning her degree, but was recruited to study economics by [Daniel Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Cohen_(economist) "Daniel Cohen (economist)"). From 1993 to 1994, she worked as a French teaching assistant in Moscow, where she wrote her history master's dissertation. In Moscow, she worked as a research assistant at the [Central Bank of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Russia "Central Bank of Russia"), and as an assistant to [Jeffrey Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Sachs "Jeffrey Sachs"), an American economist selected to advise the [Russian Ministry of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_(Russia) "Ministry of Finance (Russia)") in the aftermath of the collapse of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"). The experience led her to conclude that "economics had potential as a lever of action in the world" and she could satisfy academic ambitions while doing "things that mattered".

She finished her degree in history and economics at [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_(Paris) "École normale supérieure (Paris)") in 1994 and received a master's degree from DELTA, now the [Paris School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_School_of_Economics "Paris School of Economics"), in 1995. While in Moscow, Duflo met [Thomas Piketty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Piketty "Thomas Piketty"), who encouraged her to apply for graduate study at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"). She gained admission to MIT's PhD program in economics, and enrolled alongside her then-boyfriend, [Emmanuel Saez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Saez "Emmanuel Saez"), in 1995 after finishing her master's degree. Duflo's first class in development economics was co-taught by [Abhijit Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhijit_Banerjee "Abhijit Banerjee") and [Michael Kremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kremer "Michael Kremer"), with whom she would later share the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences"). Her classmates at the time include several prominent development economists, including [Eliana La Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliana_La_Ferrara "Eliana La Ferrara"), [Asim Ijaz Khwaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asim_Ijaz_Khwaja "Asim Ijaz Khwaja"), and Jishnu Das.

Duflo completed her PhD in 1999, under the joint supervision of [Abhijit Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhijit_Banerjee "Abhijit Banerjee") and [Joshua Angrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Angrist "Joshua Angrist"). Her dissertation research leveraged a [natural experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_experiment "Natural experiment") —a large-scale school expansion program in Indonesia — to study the effects of education on future earnings, providing the first causal evidence that increased schooling improves earnings later in life.

## Career

After completing her PhD in 1999, Duflo became an assistant professor of economics at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT "MIT"), her alma mater. Economics professors are rarely hired from the PhD students in their own departments; however, following the departure of [Michael Kremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kremer "Michael Kremer") to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), the department made an exception to strengthen MIT's [development economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_economics "Development economics") group. From 2001 to 2002, Duflo took leave from MIT to pursue a visiting academic position at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"). Upon her return, she was promoted to Associate Professor and became among the youngest faculty members in the department's history to be offered tenure.

In 2003, Duflo was promoted to full professor after receiving competing offers from Princeton and [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"). Alongside [Abhijit Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhijit_Banerjee "Abhijit Banerjee") and [Sendhil Mullainathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendhil_Mullainathan "Sendhil Mullainathan"), Duflo secured additional funding as part of her retention offer to found a laboratory aimed at promoting the use of randomized controlled trials in policy evaluation. The [Poverty Action Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_Action_Lab "Poverty Action Lab") was initially led by [Rachel Glennerster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Glennerster "Rachel Glennerster"), a British economist and the wife of [Michael Kremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kremer "Michael Kremer"), co-winner of the 2019 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences"). In 2005, with the support of MIT President [Susan Hockfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Hockfield "Susan Hockfield"), the Poverty Action Lab was endowed by Mohammed Abdul Latif Jameel, an MIT alumnus and president of the [Abdul Latif Jameel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Latif_Jameel "Abdul Latif Jameel") corporation.

In line with Duflo and Banerjee's experience in the Indian context, [J-PAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-pal "J-pal")'s first regional office was founded in 2007 in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai "Chennai") at the [Institute for Financial Management and Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Financial_Management_and_Research "Institute for Financial Management and Research"). Additional regional offices have since been founded at [Pontifical Catholic University of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Catholic_University_of_Chile "Pontifical Catholic University of Chile"), the [Paris School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_School_of_Economics "Paris School of Economics"), the [University of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town "University of Cape Town"), [American University in Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University_in_Cairo "American University in Cairo"), and the [University of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Indonesia "University of Indonesia"). As of 2024, the J-PAL network included 900 researchers based at 97 universities around the world. Many prominent development economists are on the board of the organization, including [Marianne Bertrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianne_Bertrand "Marianne Bertrand"), [Chris Blattman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Blattman "Chris Blattman"), [Pascaline Dupas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascaline_Dupas "Pascaline Dupas"), and [Amy Finkelstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Finkelstein "Amy Finkelstein").

Alongside her work at J-PAL, Duflo is a Research Associate at the [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Economic_Research "National Bureau of Economic Research"), a board member at the [Bureau for Research and Economic Analysis of Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_for_Research_and_Economic_Analysis_of_Development "Bureau for Research and Economic Analysis of Development") (BREAD), and director of the [development economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_economics "Development economics") program of the [Centre for Economic Policy Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Economic_Policy_Research "Centre for Economic Policy Research"). Since 2023, she has held the Poverty and Public Policy Chair at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France"), and in 2024 assumed the presidency of the [Paris School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_School_of_Economics "Paris School of Economics"). She is former editor-in-chief of the *[American Economic Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Review "American Economic Review")*, was the founding editor of the *[American Economic Journal: Applied Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Journal:_Applied_Economics "American Economic Journal: Applied Economics")*, and previously was on the editorial boards of the *[Annual Review of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Review_of_Economics "Annual Review of Economics")*, [*Review of Economics and Statistics*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Review_of_Economics_and_Statistics "The Review of Economics and Statistics"), and *[Journal of Development Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Development_Economics "Journal of Development Economics")*.

Duflo has also held several advisory appointments in government. As of 2024, she was a member of the economic advisory committee of the Indian state of [Tamil Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Nadu "Tamil Nadu"). From 2012 to 2017, she was on the Global Development Council of President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama"), led by economist [Mohamed El-Erian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_El-Erian "Mohamed El-Erian").

## Research

Duflo's research focuses on a range of topics in the microeconomics of development, such as health, education, financial inclusion, political economy, gender, and household behavior. Much of her research leverages [randomized controlled trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_controlled_trial "Randomized controlled trial") to evaluate the causal effects of social interventions on development outcomes of interest.

### Education

Duflo's dissertation research examined the labor market returns to education through analysis of a unique [policy experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_experiment "Natural experiment"): a mass school construction program in Indonesia. Published in the *[American Economic Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Review "American Economic Review")*, the study showed that children exposed to the program (i.e. who were aged 2 to 6 in 1974) received between 0.12 and 0.19 more years of education and had higher wages in adulthood. The paper provided some of the first causal evidence in a developing country context that increased education does lead to increased wages.

Among Duflo's most recognized work leverages randomized impact evaluations to study interventions aimed at improving educational outcomes in the developing world. In 2007, Duflo — alongside co-authors [Abhijit Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhijit_Banerjee "Abhijit Banerjee"), Shawn Cole, and Leigh Linden — published a study in *[The Quarterly Journal of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Quarterly_Journal_of_Economics "The Quarterly Journal of Economics")* evaluating a remedial education program aimed at improving learning outcomes of those "left behind" in Indian schools. They found that the program substantially improved learning outcomes, in contrast to other interventions such as providing textbooks. Their research has encouraged the proliferation of "Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL)", an educational program aimed at improving learning outcomes by providing targeted instruction to primary school pupils behind on mathematics and reading.

### Gender

In other early work, Duflo examines the role of gender in the intra-household allocation of resources by leveraging another unique policy shock: a large increase in the value of old-age pensions in [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa") in 1991. Duflo shows that in households containing elderly females ("grandmothers"), the increase in pension was associated with an increase in the [body mass index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_mass_index "Body mass index") of young girls ("granddaughters"). In contrast, she documents no such effect if the only pension recipient is an elderly man ("grandfather"). This result suggests that girls may benefit when a larger proportion of household resources are controlled by older female family members.

### Microfinance

Among Duflo's most cited work leverages a randomized impact evaluation to test the effects of [microfinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfinance "Microfinance") on household consumption and well-being. The research was a direct response to the popularity of [microfinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfinance "Microfinance") as a tool to eliminate global poverty, and Duflo's perception that [microcredit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcredit "Microcredit") was being celebrated as a development intervention despite no systematic evidence on its efficacy. Alongside Cynthia Kinnan, [Abhijit Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhijit_Banerjee "Abhijit Banerjee"), and [Rachel Glennerster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Glennerster "Rachel Glennerster"), Duflo partnered with a microcredit firm in [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad "Hyderabad"), India to conduct a [randomized controlled trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_controlled_trial "Randomized controlled trial") on the effects of expanding access to [microfinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfinance "Microfinance") on development outcomes of interest. She found that [microfinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfinance "Microfinance") may allow some individuals to start businesses or acquire assets, but found little evidence that [microfinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfinance "Microfinance") caused an increase in overall consumption. The results were received negatively in the microfinance industry, and inspired several follow-on studies of the effects of microfinance in other geographic contexts. In 2019, Rachael Meager — a former PhD student of Duflo — published a [meta-analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-analysis "Meta-analysis") of the literature in the [*American Economic Journal: Applied Economics*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Journal "American Economic Journal"), finding little evidence to suggest that [microfinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfinance "Microfinance") raises consumption or encourages household small business creation.

## Personal life

Duflo is married to economist [Abhijit Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhijit_Banerjee "Abhijit Banerjee"), her longtime collaborator in development economics. The couple met in 1995, when Duflo was a student in Banerjee’s development economics class at MIT, and Banerjee was still married to his previous wife. They have two children, born in 2012 and 2014, and were married in 2015.

## Selected works

### Books

In April 2011, Duflo released her book *[Poor Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poor_Economics "Poor Economics")*, co-authored with Banerjee. It documents their 15 years of experience in conducting randomized control trials to alleviate poverty. The book has received critical acclaim. [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates "List of Nobel laureates") [Amartya Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amartya_Sen "Amartya Sen") called it "a marvelously insightful book by the two outstanding researchers on the real nature of poverty." In 2019, she co-authored with Banerjee their latest book, "Good Economics for Hard Times," where she discusses possible solutions to a series of current issues such as inequality, climate change, and globalization.

## Awards

### Nobel prize in Economic Sciences

Duflo was awarded the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel in 2019 along with her two co-researchers Abhijit Banerjee and Michael Kremer "for their experimental approach to alleviating global poverty". Duflo is the youngest person (at age 46) and the second woman to win this award (after [Elinor Ostrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elinor_Ostrom "Elinor Ostrom") in 2009).

The press release from the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences") noted: "Their experimental research methods now entirely dominate [development economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_economics "Development economics")." The Nobel committee commented:

> Banerjee, Duflo and their co-authors concluded that students appeared to learn nothing from additional days at school. Neither did spending on textbooks seem to boost learning, even though the schools in Kenya lacked many essential inputs. Moreover, in the Indian context Banerjee and Duflo intended to study, many children appeared to learn little: in results from field tests in the city of [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara "Vadodara") fewer than one in five third-grade students could correctly answer first-grade curriculum math test questions.

> In response to such findings, Banerjee, Duflo and co-authors argued that efforts to get more children into school must be complemented by reforms to improve school quality.

Responding by telephone to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, Duflo explained that she received the prize "at an extremely opportune and important time" and hoped that it would "inspire many, many other women to continue working and many other men to give them the respect that they deserve, like every single human being." She also revealed that she wanted to use the award as a "megaphone" in her fighting efforts to tackle poverty and to improve children's education.

French President [Emmanuel Macron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Macron "Emmanuel Macron") offered his congratulations: "Duflo's magnificent Nobel Prize is a reminder that French economists are currently among the best in the world and shows that research in that field can have concrete impact on human welfare."

Much of the discussion related to the prize shared by Duflo and her co-laureates focused on their influential use of [randomized controlled trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_controlled_trials "Randomized controlled trials") in designing their experiments. Summarizing the research approach which she had utilized along with Banerjee and Kremer, Duflo said simply, "Our goal is to make sure that the fight against poverty is based on [scientific evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_evidence "Scientific evidence")."

Duflo came under criticism from the [Bharatiya Janata Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Party "Bharatiya Janata Party"), a [Hindu nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_nationalism "Hindu nationalism") party currently in power in India, due to the party's displeasure over her husband Abhijit Banerjee achieving the Nobel Prize. Many within the party derogatorily commented that Banerjee had been preferred by the [Nobel committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_committee "Nobel committee") over other Hindu academicians, due to him marrying a white European woman (viz Duflo), which was in violation of the Hindu preference for [endogamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endogamy "Endogamy").

### Other awards

- Duflo was awarded the Elaine Bennett Research Prize by the [American Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Association "American Economic Association") in 2002, which honours a female economist under 40 who has made outstanding contributions in any field of economic research.
- In 2005, the think tank *[Cercle des économistes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercle_des_économistes "Cercle des économistes")* awarded her the Best Young French Economist prize.
- In 2008, *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist "The Economist")* listed Duflo as one of the top eight young economists in the world.
- In May 2008, the American magazine *[Foreign Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Policy "Foreign Policy")* named her as one of the top 100 public intellectuals in the world.
- In 2009, she was named a [MacArthur Foundation Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program "MacArthur Fellows Program"), otherwise known as a "[genius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genius "Genius")" grant. Also in 2009, she was selected as the first recipient of the [Calvó-Armengol International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvó-Armengol_International_Prize "Calvó-Armengol International Prize"), which awarded every two years to a top researcher in Economics or the social sciences aged 40 or younger for contributions to the theory and comprehension of the mechanisms of social interaction.
- She is a recipient of the 2010 [John Bates Clark Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bates_Clark_Medal "John Bates Clark Medal") for economists under 40 who have made the most significant contribution to economic thought and knowledge. She received her (first) honorary doctorate from the [Université catholique de Louvain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_catholique_de_Louvain "Université catholique de Louvain"), on 2 February 2010.
- In 2010, *Foreign Policy* again named her to its list of top 100 global thinkers.
- She was named one of *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine's [100 most influential people in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100") in April 2011.
- In 2012, Duflo was picked by *Foreign Policy* magazine as one of its Top 100 Global Thinkers.
- She shared the 2012 [Gerald Loeb Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Loeb_Award "Gerald Loeb Award") Honorable Mention for Business Book for *Poor Economics* with co-author [Abhijit Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhijit_Banerjee "Abhijit Banerjee").
- In 2013, Duflo was awarded the [Dan David Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_David_Prize "Dan David Prize") for her contribution to the advancement of "Preventive Medicine"
