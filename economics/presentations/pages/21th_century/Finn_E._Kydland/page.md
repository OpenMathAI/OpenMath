**Finn Erling Kydland** (born 1 December 1943) is a [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics") known for his contributions to business cycle theory. He is the Henley Professor of Economics at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara "University of California, Santa Barbara"). He also holds the Richard P. Simmons Distinguished Professorship at the [Tepper School of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepper_School_of_Business "Tepper School of Business") of [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University "Carnegie Mellon University"), where he earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD"), and a part-time position at the [Norwegian School of Economics (NHH)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_School_of_Economics "Norwegian School of Economics"). Kydland was a co-recipient of the 2004 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Sweden_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences_in_Memory_of_Alfred_Nobel "Bank of Sweden Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel"), with [Edward C. Prescott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_C._Prescott "Edward C. Prescott"), "for their contributions to dynamic [macroeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomics "Macroeconomics"): the [time consistency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_inconsistency "Dynamic inconsistency") of economic policy and the driving forces behind [business cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_cycle "Business cycle")."

## Biography

### Early years

Kydland grew up as the eldest of six siblings at the family farm at Søyland in [Gjestal Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gjestal_Municipality "Gjestal Municipality"), which is located in the [Jæren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jæren "Jæren") farming region in [Rogaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogaland "Rogaland") county, southwestern [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway"). He recalls having had a liberal upbringing, his parents not imposing many limitations on their children. Finn Kydland became interested in mathematics and economics as a young adult, after he did some [bookkeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookkeeping "Bookkeeping") at a friend's [mink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mink "Mink") farm.

With a freshly awakened interest in theoretical economics, Kydland earned a [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") from the [Norwegian School of Economics (NHH)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_School_of_Economics "Norwegian School of Economics") in 1968 and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in economics from [Carnegie Mellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon "Carnegie Mellon") in 1973, [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation "Dissertation"): *Decentralized Macroeconomic Planning*, supervised by [Edward C. Prescott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_C._Prescott "Edward C. Prescott"). After his PhD he returned to NHH as an assistant professor. In 1978 he moved back to Carnegie Mellon as an associate professor. He has been living in the United States since then.

### Scholarship

Kydland's areas of expertise are [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics") in general and [political economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_economy "Political economy"). His main areas of teaching and interest are [business cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_cycle "Business cycle"), [monetary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_policy "Monetary policy") and [fiscal policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_policy "Fiscal policy") and [labor economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_economics "Labor economics"). He joined the faculty of [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University "Carnegie Mellon University") in 1977, where he served as a [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor "Professor") of Economics until 2004, when he became a faculty member of the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara "University of California, Santa Barbara") and founded the Laboratory for Aggregate Economics and Finance (LAEF) at this same institution. He is a Research Associate for the [Federal Reserve Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Banks "Federal Reserve Banks") of [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_Dallas "Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas"), [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_Cleveland "Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland") and [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_St._Louis "Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis"), and a Fellow at the IC² Institute at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin "University of Texas at Austin"). He is also an [adjunct professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunct_professor "Adjunct professor") at the [Norwegian School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_School_of_Economics "Norwegian School of Economics"), and has held visiting scholar and professor positions at the [Hoover Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoover_Institution "Hoover Institution") and the [Universidad Torcuato di Tella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Torcuato_di_Tella "Universidad Torcuato di Tella") in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires"), [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina "Argentina").

### Personal life

Kydland married Liv Kjellevold in 1968, with whom he had four children: Jon Martin, Eirik, Camilla, and Kari. He is now married to Tonya Schooler.
