**Robert Butler "Bob" Wilson, Jr.** (born May 16, 1937) is an American [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist") who is the Adams Distinguished Professor of Management, Emeritus at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"). He was jointly awarded the 2020 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, together with his Stanford colleague and former student [Paul R. Milgrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Milgrom "Paul Milgrom"), "for improvements to auction theory and inventions of new [auction formats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auction#Types "Auction")". Two more of his students, [Alvin E. Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_E._Roth "Alvin E. Roth") and [Bengt Holmström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengt_Holmström "Bengt Holmström"), are also Nobel Laureates in their own right.

Wilson is known for his contributions to [management science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_research "Operations research") and [business economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_economics "Business economics"). His doctoral thesis introduced [sequential quadratic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequential_quadratic_programming "Sequential quadratic programming"), which became a leading [iterative method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iterative_method "Iterative method") for [nonlinear programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_programming "Nonlinear programming"). With other [mathematical economists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_economist "Mathematical economist") at Stanford, he helped to reformulate the economics of [industrial organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_organization "Industrial organization") and [organization theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_theory "Organization theory") using [non-cooperative game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-cooperative_game_theory "Non-cooperative game theory"). His research on [nonlinear pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_pricing "Nonlinear pricing") has influenced policies for large firms, particularly in the [energy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_industry "Energy industry"), especially electricity.

## Early life and academic career

Wilson was born on May 16, 1937, in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva,_Nebraska "Geneva, Nebraska"), [Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska "Nebraska"). He graduated from [Lincoln High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_High_School_(Lincoln,_Nebraska) "Lincoln High School (Lincoln, Nebraska)") in Lincoln, Nebraska and earned a full scholarship to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). He received his [B.A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree from [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College "Harvard College") in 1959. Wilson subsequently obtained an [M.B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration "Master of Business Administration") in 1961 and a [D.B.A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Business_Administration "Doctor of Business Administration"). in 1963 from [Harvard Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_School "Harvard Business School"). He worked at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles "University of California, Los Angeles"), for a very brief time and then joined the faculty at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"). He has been on the faculty of the [Stanford Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Business_School "Stanford Business School") since 1964. He was also an affiliated faculty member of [Harvard Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Law_School "Harvard Law School") from 1993 to 2001.

## Research

Wilson is known for research and teaching on market design, pricing, negotiation, and related topics concerning industrial organization and information economics. He is an expert on game theory and its applications. He has been a major contributor to auction designs and competitive bidding strategies in the oil, communication, and power industries, and to the design of innovative pricing schemes. His work on pricing of priority service for electric power has been implemented in the utility industry.

Wilson's 1968 *[Econometrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometrica "Econometrica")* paper *The Theory of the Syndicates* influenced a whole generation of economics, finance, and accounting students. The paper poses a fundamental question: Under what conditions does the expected utility representation describe the behavior of a group of individuals who choose lotteries and share risk in a [Pareto-optimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto-optimal "Pareto-optimal") way?

He has published about a hundred articles in professional journals and books since completing his education. He has been an associate editor of several journals, and delivered several public lectures.

In 1993, Wilson published a book on Nonlinear Pricing. It is an encyclopedic analysis of tariff design and related topics for public utilities, including power, communications, and transport. The book won the 1995 Leo Melamed Prize, a prize awarded biannually by the University of Chicago for "outstanding scholarship by a business professor."

Other contributions to game theory includes wage bargaining and strikes, and in legal contexts, settlement negotiations. He has authored some of the basic studies of reputational effects in [predatory pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predatory_pricing "Predatory pricing"), price wars, and other competitive battles.

## Honors

Since Wilson completed the Bachelor, Master's, and Doctoral Degrees at Harvard College and the Harvard Business School, he has published about 100 articles in professional journals and books, for which he has received many honors.

### Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded Wilson and Paul Milgrom as the co-recipients of the 2020 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics for having "improved auction theory and invented new auction formats, benefitting sellers, buyers and taxpayers around the world". [Alvin Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_E._Roth "Alvin E. Roth") (an economist who was a co-recipient of the 2012 Nobel Memorial Prize and who had Wilson as his doctoral advisor) said that Wilson and Milgrom "haven't just profoundly changed the way we understand auctions – they have changed how things are auctioned." Specifically, the Academy acknowledged Wilson's efforts in analyzing common value auctions, where bidders derive a common value from the underlying resource, and his theoretical proofs that bidders tend to bid lower than their best estimates of common value, in order to avoid the [winner's curse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winner's_curse "Winner's curse"). Some examples of common value auctions include radio frequency spectrum, and minerals.

### Memberships and awards

He is an elected member of the National Academy of Sciences, a designated 'distinguished fellow' of the American Economic Association, and a fellow, former officer and Council member of the Econometric Society. He was conferred an honorary Doctor of Economics degree in 1986 by the Norwegian School of Economics and Business Administration. In 1995, he was conferred an honorary Doctor of Laws degree by the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"). In 2014, Wilson won a [Golden Goose Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Goose_Award "Golden Goose Award") for his work involving auction design.

He has won the [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Award "BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award") (2015) in the Economics, Finance and Management category for his "pioneering contributions to the analysis of strategic interactions when economic agents have limited and different information about their environment". With colleagues [David M. Kreps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_M._Kreps "David M. Kreps") and [Paul Milgrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Milgrom "Paul Milgrom"), he was awarded the 2018 [John J. Carty Award for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_J._Carty_Award_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "John J. Carty Award for the Advancement of Science").

### Political views

In June 2024, 16 [Nobel Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") laureates, including Wilson, signed an open letter arguing that [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")’s fiscal and trade policies coupled with efforts to limit the [Federal Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve "Federal Reserve")'s independence would reignite inflation in the United States.
