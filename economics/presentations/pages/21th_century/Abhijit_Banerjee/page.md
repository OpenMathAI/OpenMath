**Abhijit Vinayak Banerjee** (Bengali pronunciation: [[obʱid͡ʒit̪ binae̯ɔk bɔnd̪opad̪ʱːae̯]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Bengali "Help:IPA/Bengali"); born 21 February 1961) is an [Indian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Americans "Indian Americans") [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist") who is currently the [Ford Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Foundation "Ford Foundation") International Professor of Economics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"). He is co-founder and co-director of the [Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab (J-PAL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Latif_Jameel_Poverty_Action_Lab "Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab"), an [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT "MIT") based global research center promoting the use of scientific evidence to inform poverty alleviation strategies. In 2019, Banerjee shared the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") with [Esther Duflo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Duflo "Esther Duflo") and [Michael Kremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kremer "Michael Kremer"), "for their experimental approach to alleviating global poverty." He and [Esther Duflo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Duflo "Esther Duflo") are married, and became the [sixth married couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_couples_awarded_the_Nobel_Prize "List of couples awarded the Nobel Prize") to jointly win a [Nobel or Nobel Memorial Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") on economic sciences.

Banerjee's academic appointments include being a fellow of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society "Econometric Society"), a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"), and a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"). In 1994, he received a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship "Sloan Research Fellowship"), awarded annually to early career researchers with the "potential to revolutionize their fields." According to [Research Papers in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Papers_in_Economics "Research Papers in Economics"), Banerjee is among the most productive development economists in the world, ranking in the top 75 researchers by total research output.

## Early life and education

Banerjee was born to a [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis "Bengalis") father and a [Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_people "Marathi people") mother in [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"). His father, [Dipak Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipak_Banerjee "Dipak Banerjee"), was a professor of economics at [Presidency College, Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_College_Calcutta "Presidency College Calcutta"), and received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") from the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics") under the supervision of [Richard Lipsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Lipsey "Richard Lipsey"). His mother, Nirmala (née Patankar) Banerjee was a professor at the [Centre for Studies in Social Sciences, Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Studies_in_Social_Sciences,_Calcutta "Centre for Studies in Social Sciences, Calcutta").

Banerjee attended secondary school at [South Point School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Point_School "South Point School") in [Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata "Kolkata"), where he was described as a "brilliant" but "very quiet" student. During high school, he was interested in literature, history, philosophy, and mathematics, choosing to pursue his undergraduate studies in the latter at the [Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Statistical_Institute,_Kolkata "Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata"). He dropped out of the program after one week, transferring to [Presidency College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_University,_Kolkata "Presidency University, Kolkata"), then an affiliate of the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta "University of Calcutta"), to study economics.

Banerjee spent three years at [Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_University,_Kolkata "Presidency University, Kolkata"), receiving a [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSc "BSc") (Honors) in Economics in 1981. He took classes with his father, [Dipak Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipak_Banerjee "Dipak Banerjee"), in addition to [Mihir Rakshit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihir_Rakshit "Mihir Rakshit"). His favorite subject was [economic history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history "Economic history"), taught by [Nabhendu Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabhendu_Sen "Nabhendu Sen").

After completing his undergraduate studies, Banerjee pursued an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") in Economics at [Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru_University "Jawaharlal Nehru University"), selecting to study there over the [Delhi School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_School_of_Economics "Delhi School of Economics") because of its political life, and the latter's reputation as a stepping stone to [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") programs in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), which Banerjee had little interest in pursuing. His teachers included [Anjan Mukherjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anjan_Mukherjee "Anjan Mukherjee") and [Krishna Bharadwaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Bharadwaj_(economist) "Krishna Bharadwaj (economist)"), the latter of whom taught a course on the history of economic thought. While studying at [JNU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru_University "Jawaharlal Nehru University"), Banerjee was arrested, imprisoned, and beaten at [Tihar Jail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tihar_Jail "Tihar Jail"), in response to a protest in which students [gheraoed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gherao "Gherao") the then vice chancellor of the university. He completed his degree in 1983, and was encouraged by his parents and teachers to apply for [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") programs in economics.

Banerjee applied to [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University"), and the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), attending the first of these despite no students from [Jawaharlal Nehru University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru_University "Jawaharlal Nehru University") having previously been admitted to the university. At [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), his classmates included [Tyler Cowen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Cowen "Tyler Cowen"), [Alan Krueger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Krueger "Alan Krueger"), [Steven Kaplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Kaplan_(economist) "Steven Kaplan (economist)"), and [Nouriel Roubini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouriel_Roubini "Nouriel Roubini"). He attended courses with [Andreu Mas-Colell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreu_Mas-Colell "Andreu Mas-Colell"), [Lawrence Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Summers "Lawrence Summers"), [Kala Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kala_Krishna "Kala Krishna"), [Oliver Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Hart_(economist) "Oliver Hart (economist)"), and [Susan Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_M._Collins_(economist) "Susan M. Collins (economist)"), and briefly served as a research assistant to [Jeffrey Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Sachs "Jeffrey Sachs"). His dissertation research, supervised by [Eric Maskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Maskin "Eric Maskin"), was primarily theoretical, and examined the economics of information.

## Academic career

Banerjee is currently the Ford Foundation International Professor of Economics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology; he has taught at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"). He has also been a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow "Guggenheim Fellow") and an [Alfred P. Sloan Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship "Sloan Fellowship").

His work focuses on [development economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_economics "Development economics"). Together with Esther Duflo he has discussed [field experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_experiments "Field experiments") as an important methodology to discover causal relationships in economics.
He was elected a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 2004. In 2009, he received the [Infosys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys_Prize "Infosys Prize") in the social sciences (economics) category. He served on the Social Sciences jury for the [Infosys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys_Prize "Infosys Prize") in 2018.
In 2012, he shared the [Gerald Loeb Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Loeb_Award "Gerald Loeb Award") Honorable Mention for Business Book with co-author Esther Duflo for their book *[Poor Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poor_Economics "Poor Economics")*.

In 2013, he was named by the United Nations Secretary-General [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon "Ban Ki-moon") to a panel of experts tasked with updating the [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goals "Millennium Development Goals") after 2015 (their expiration date).

In 2014, he received the Bernhard-Harms-Prize from the [Kiel Institute for the World Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel_Institute_for_the_World_Economy "Kiel Institute for the World Economy").

In 2019, he delivered Export-Import Bank of India's 34th Commencement Day Annual Lecture on Redesigning Social Policy. That same year, was awarded the Nobel Prize in Economics, together with Esther Duflo and [Michael Kremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kremer "Michael Kremer"), "for their experimental approach to alleviating global poverty".

In October 2025, the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich") announced that Banerjee and Duflo would be joining the faculty of the UZH Faculty of Business, Economics, and Informatics in July 2026, where they would co-direct the newly established Lemann Center for Development, Education and Public Policy. The center and Banerjee and Duflo's endowed professorships were created following a 26 million franc (US$32.5 million) donation from Swiss-Brazilian billionaire [Jorge Paulo Lemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Paulo_Lemann "Jorge Paulo Lemann")'s Lemann Foundation. The couple would maintain part-time positions at MIT.

### Research and work in India

Banerjee and his co-workers try to measure the effectiveness of actions (such as government programmes) in improving people's lives. For this, they use [randomized controlled trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_controlled_trials "Randomized controlled trials"), similar to clinical trials in medical research. For example, although [polio vaccination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polio_vaccination "Polio vaccination") is freely available in India, many mothers were not bringing their children for the vaccination drives. Banerjee and Prof. Esther Duflo, also from MIT, tried an experiment in [Rajasthan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan "Rajasthan"), where they gave a bag of pulses to mothers who vaccinated their children. Soon, the immunization rate went up in the region. In another experiment, they found that learning outcomes improved in schools that were provided with teaching assistants to help students with special needs.

Banerjee is a co-founder of [Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Latif_Jameel_Poverty_Action_Lab "Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab") (along with economists the thavam[Esther Duflo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Duflo "Esther Duflo") and [Sendhil Mullainathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendhil_Mullainathan "Sendhil Mullainathan")). In India he serves on the academic advisory board of Plaksha University, a science and technology university established in 2010.

Banerjee wrote a cookbook in 2021, *Cooking To Save Your Life*, published by Juggernaut.

## Personal life

Abhijit Banerjee has been married twice. His first marriage was to [literary scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_scholar "Literary scholar") Arundhati Tuli Banerjee. The couple met as childhood friends in [Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata "Kolkata") and both worked at MIT. They [divorced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorced "Divorced") in 2014, and their only son died by [suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide "Suicide") in 2016.

Banerjee's second marriage is to [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist") [Esther Duflo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Duflo "Esther Duflo"). The couple met in 1995 when Duflo was a student in Banerjee's development economics class at MIT. They have two children, born in 2012 and 2014, and were married in 2015.

## Awards

Abhijit Banerjee was awarded the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") in 2019 along with his two co-researchers [Esther Duflo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Duflo "Esther Duflo") and [Michael Kremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kremer "Michael Kremer") "for their experimental approach to alleviating global poverty".

The press release from the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences") noted: "Their experimental research methods now entirely dominate [development economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_economics "Development economics")."

The Nobel committee commented:

:   :   "Banerjee, Duflo and their co-authors concluded that students appeared to learn nothing from additional days at school. Neither did spending on textbooks seem to boost learning, even though the schools in [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya "Kenya") lacked many essential inputs. Moreover, in the Indian context Banerjee and Duflo intended to study, many children appeared to learn little: in results from field tests in the city of [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara "Vadodara") fewer than one in five third-grade students could correctly answer first-grade curriculum math test questions.

:   :   "In response to such findings, Banerjee, Duflo and co-authors argued that efforts to get more children into school must be complemented by reforms to improve school quality."

The Nobel Prize was a major recognition for their chosen field - Development Economics, and for the use of Randomised Controlled Trials. It evoked mixed emotions in India, where his success was celebrated with nationalistic fervour while his approach and pro-poor focus were seen as a negation of India's current government's right-wing ideology as well as broader development discourse.

Banerjee's achievement of the Nobel Prize was received with a cold shoulder by the [right-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_politics "Right-wing politics") [Bharatiya Janata Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Party "Bharatiya Janata Party"), which is in power at the Union level in India, because he was one of the economists that were consulted by [Rahul Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahul_Gandhi "Rahul Gandhi") in formulating a basic-income support scheme called [NYAY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyuntam_Aay_Yojana "Nyuntam Aay Yojana"), which was the main electoral promise of the [Indian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress") in the [2019 Indian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Indian_general_election "2019 Indian general election"). In response to his criticism of the [Union government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India "Government of India")'s handling of the [country's economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_India "Economy of India"), [Commerce & Industries Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Commerce_and_Industry_(India) "Ministry of Commerce and Industry (India)") [Piyush Goyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piyush_Goyal "Piyush Goyal"), while speaking on Banerjee's receiving of the Nobel Prize on [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter"), stated that Banerjee's economic theories are based on [a leftist viewpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_politics#Economics "Left-wing politics") and by voting for the BJP, the Indian masses have 'totally rejected' his thoughts. BJP leader [Rahul Sinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahul_Sinha "Rahul Sinha"), who had served as the [state BJP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Party,_West_Bengal "Bharatiya Janata Party, West Bengal") president in Banerjee's native state of [West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal "West Bengal"), downplayed his achievements and interpreted the Nobel Committee's decision to award Banerjee only after he had divorced his Hindu wife in order to marry a [non-Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mleccha "Mleccha") person as [Hinduphobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Hindu_sentiment "Anti-Hindu sentiment"). Banerjee remains the lone [Indian Nobel laurete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_Nobel_laureates "List of Indian Nobel laureates") not to be felicitated by the Indian government.

He was awarded the [Doctor of Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Letters "Doctor of Letters") ([Honoris Causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa "Honoris causa")) by the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta "University of Calcutta") in January 2020.

Abhijit Banerjee and [Esther Duflo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Duflo "Esther Duflo") received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") in September 2022.
