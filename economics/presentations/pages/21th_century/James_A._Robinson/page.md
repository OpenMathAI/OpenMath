**James Alan Robinson** (born 27 February 1960, [Chelmsford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelmsford "Chelmsford")) is a British-American [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist") and [political scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_scientist "Political scientist"). He is a university professor at the [Harris School of Public Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_School_of_Public_Policy_Studies "Harris School of Public Policy Studies") of the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"), where he led the Pearson Institute for the Study and Resolution of Global Conflicts. Robinson taught at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") from 2004 to 2015.

With [Daron Acemoglu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daron_Acemoglu "Daron Acemoglu"), he is the co-author of several books, including *The Narrow Corridor*, *[Why Nations Fail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_Nations_Fail "Why Nations Fail")*, and *Economic Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy*. In 2024, Robinson, Acemoglu, and [Simon Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Johnson_(economist) "Simon Johnson (economist)") were awarded the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") for their [comparative studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-cultural_studies "Cross-cultural studies") on prosperity between nations.

## Education

Robinson received a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in economics from the [London School of Economics and Political Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics_and_Political_Science "London School of Economics and Political Science") in 1982, a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") in economics from the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick "University of Warwick") in 1986, and a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in economics from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") in 1993.

## Career

Robinson's main fields of research are in [political economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_economy "Political economy") and comparative politics, as well as in economic and political development.

In 2004, Robinson was appointed [Associate Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor "Associate professor") of Government at Harvard University. He later held named chair positions at Harvard, first as the David Florence Professor of Government (2009–2014) and later as the Wilbur A. Cowett Professor of Government (2014–2015). On 1 July 2015, he was appointed as one of nine University Professors at the [Harris School of Public Policy Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_School_of_Public_Policy_Studies "Harris School of Public Policy Studies") of the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"). He previously held the title Reverend Dr. Richard L. Pearson Professor of Global Conflict Studies. On 9 May 2016, Professor Robinson was awarded an honorary doctorate by the [National University of Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Mongolia "National University of Mongolia") during his first visit to the country.

He has conducted research in countries around the world including [Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana "Botswana"), [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile "Chile"), the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo"), [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti "Haiti"), the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), [Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone_Civil_War "Sierra Leone Civil War"), [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa") and [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia "Colombia"), where he teaches every summer at the University of the Andes in [Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogotá "Bogotá").

On 17 March 2023, James Robinson met with students, scientists, leaders of social opinion, and representatives of finance, economy, and business circles in [Tashkent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashkent "Tashkent"), [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan "Uzbekistan"). In an interview, Robinson discussed the construction of [inclusive institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusive_institutions "Inclusive institutions") in [authoritarian countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism "Authoritarianism"), the challenging development of countries post-colonialism, the "mistakes" made consciously, and answered questions regarding the "King of Cotton" section on [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan "Uzbekistan") in his book *[Why Nations Fail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_Nations_Fail "Why Nations Fail")*.

He has collaborated extensively with long-time co-author [Daron Acemoglu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daron_Acemoglu "Daron Acemoglu") after meeting at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics").

## Research

### *Economic Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy*

*Economic Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy* (2006), co-authored by Robinson with Daron Acemoglu analyzes the creation and [consolidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_consolidation "Democratic consolidation") of [democratic societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy"). They argue that "democracy consolidates when elites do not have a strong incentive to overthrow it. These processes depend on (1) the strength of civil society, (2) the structure of political institutions, (3) the nature of political and economic crises, (4) the level of [economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality "Economic inequality"), (5) the structure of the economy, and (6) the form and extent of [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization")." The book's title is derived from *[Social Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Origins_of_Dictatorship_and_Democracy "Social Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy")*, a 1966 book by [Barrington Moore Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrington_Moore_Jr. "Barrington Moore Jr.")

### *Why Nations Fail*

In *[Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_Nations_Fail:_The_Origins_of_Power,_Prosperity,_and_Poverty "Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty")* (2012), Acemoglu and Robinson argue that economic growth at the forefront of technology requires [political stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_stability "Political stability"), which the [Mayan civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization "Maya civilization") (to name only one) did not have, and [creative destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_destruction "Creative destruction"). The latter cannot occur without institutional restraints on the granting of [monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly "Monopoly") and [oligopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligopoly "Oligopoly") rights. They say that the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") began in [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain "Great Britain"), because the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England "England") [Bill of Rights 1689](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_of_Rights_1689 "Bill of Rights 1689") created such restraints. For example, a [steamboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat "Steamboat") built in 1705 by [Denis Papin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Papin "Denis Papin") was demolished by a [boatmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailor "Sailor") guild in [Münden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hann._Münden "Hann. Münden"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany"). Papin went to [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London"), where several of his papers were published by the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society"). [Thomas Newcomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Newcomen "Thomas Newcomen") extended Papin's work into a [steam engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcomen_atmospheric_engine "Newcomen atmospheric engine") in 1712, and became a commercial success, while Papin died in 1713 and was buried in an unmarked [pauper's grave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauper's_funeral "Pauper's funeral").

Acemoglu and Robinson insist that "development differences across countries are exclusively due to differences in political and economic institutions, and reject other theories that attribute some of the differences to culture, weather, geography or lack of knowledge about the best policies and practices." For example, "Soviet Russia generated rapid growth as it caught up rapidly with some of the advanced technologies in the world [but] was running out of steam by the 1970s" because of a lack of creative destruction.

### *The Narrow Corridor*

In *The Narrow Corridor. States, Societies, and the Fate of Liberty* (2019), Acemoglu and Robinson argue that a free society is attained when the power of the state and of society have evolved in rough balance.

### A critique of modernization theory

[Daron Acemoglu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daron_Acemoglu "Daron Acemoglu") and James A. Robinson, in their article "Income and Democracy" (2008) show that even though there is a strong cross-country correlation between income and democracy, once one controls for country fixed effects and removes the association between income per capita and various measures of democracy, there is "no causal effect of income on democracy." In "Non-Modernization" (2022), they further argue that [modernization theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernization_theory "Modernization theory") cannot account for various paths of political development "because it posits a link between economics and politics that is not conditional on institutions and culture and that presumes a definite endpoint—for example, an '[end of history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_history "End of history")'."
