**Robert James Shiller** (born March 29, 1946) is an American [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist "Economist"), academic, and author. As of 2022, he served as a [Sterling Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_Professor "Sterling Professor") of [Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics") at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") and is a fellow at the [Yale School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_School_of_Management "Yale School of Management")'s International Center for Finance. Shiller has been a research associate of the [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Economic_Research "National Bureau of Economic Research") (NBER) since 1980, was vice president of the [American Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Association "American Economic Association") in 2005, its president for 2016, and president of the [Eastern Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Economic_Association "Eastern Economic Association") for 2006–2007. He is also the co‑founder and chief economist of the investment management firm MacroMarkets LLC.

Shiller is known for four major intellectual contributions: co-developing the [Case–Shiller housing price index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case–Shiller_index "Case–Shiller index") with [Karl Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_E._Case "Karl E. Case"), as well as the [cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclically_adjusted_price-to-earnings_ratio "Cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio"); challenging the [Efficient Market Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient-market_hypothesis "Efficient-market hypothesis"); and warning years in advance of the stock market and housing bubble that would become the [subprime mortgage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subprime_mortgage_crisis "Subprime mortgage crisis").

Shiller was ranked by the *IDEAS* [RePEc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Papers_in_Economics "Research Papers in Economics") publications monitor in 2008 as among the 100 most influential economists of the world; and was still on the list in 2019. [Eugene Fama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Fama "Eugene Fama"), [Lars Peter Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Peter_Hansen "Lars Peter Hansen") and Shiller jointly received the 2013 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences"), "for their empirical analysis of asset prices".

## Background

Shiller was born in [Detroit, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit,_Michigan "Detroit, Michigan"), the son of Ruth R. ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Née "Née") Radsville) and Benjamin Peter Shiller, an engineer-cum-entrepreneur. He is of [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanians "Lithuanians") descent. He is married to Virginia Marie (Faulstich), a psychologist, and has two children. He was raised as a [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist "Methodist").

Shiller attended [Kalamazoo College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamazoo_College "Kalamazoo College") for two years before transferring to the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan") where he graduated [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa "Phi Beta Kappa") with a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree in 1967. He received the [S.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") degree from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (MIT) in 1968, and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") from MIT in 1972 with thesis entitled *[Rational expectations and the structure of interest rates](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/13926)* under the supervision of [Franco Modigliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Modigliani "Franco Modigliani").

## Career and views

Shiller has taught at Yale since 1982, and previously held faculty positions at the [Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_School_of_the_University_of_Pennsylvania "Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania") and the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota"), also giving frequent lectures at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics"). He has written on economic topics that range from [behavioral finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioral_finance "Behavioral finance") to [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate "Real estate") to [risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_management "Risk management"), and has been co-organizer of the NBER workshops on behavioral finance with [Richard Thaler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Thaler "Richard Thaler") from 1991-2015. His book *[Macro Markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macro_Markets "Macro Markets")* won [TIAA-CREF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TIAA-CREF "TIAA-CREF")'s first annual [Paul A. Samuelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Samuelson "Paul Samuelson") Award. He publishes a syndicated column and has been a regular contributor to *[Project Syndicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Syndicate "Project Syndicate")* since 2003.

In 1981 Shiller published an article in which he challenged the [efficient-market hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient-market_hypothesis "Efficient-market hypothesis"), which was the dominant view in the economics profession at the time. Shiller argued that in a rational [stock market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market "Stock market"), investors would base stock prices on the expected receipt of future dividends, discounted to a present value. He examined the performance of the U.S. stock market since the 1920s, and considered the kinds of expectations of future dividends and discount rates that could justify the wide range of variation experienced in the stock market. Shiller concluded that the volatility of the stock market was greater than could plausibly be explained by any rational view of the future. This article was later named as one of the "top 20" articles in the 100-year history of the [American Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Association "American Economic Association").

The behavioral finance school gained new credibility following the [October 1987 stock market crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Monday_(1987) "Black Monday (1987)"). Shiller's work included survey research that asked investors and stock traders what motivated them to make trades; the results further bolstered his hypothesis that these decisions are often driven by emotion instead of rational calculation. Much of this survey data has been gathered continuously since 1989.

In 1991 he formed Case Shiller Weiss with economists [Karl Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_E._Case "Karl E. Case") and [Allan Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Weiss "Allan Weiss") who served as the CEO from inception to the sale to Fiserv. The company produced a repeat-sales index using home sales price data from across the nation, studying home pricing trends. The index was developed by Shiller and Case when Case was studying unsustainable house pricing booms in Boston and Shiller was studying the behavioral aspects of [economic bubbles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_bubble "Economic bubble"). The repeat-sales index developed by Case and Shiller was later acquired and further developed by [Fiserv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiserv "Fiserv") and [Standard & Poor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_&_Poor "Standard & Poor"), creating the [Case-Shiller index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case-Shiller_index "Case-Shiller index").

His book *[Irrational Exuberance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_Exuberance_(book) "Irrational Exuberance (book)")* (2000) – a *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times "New York Times")* bestseller – warned that the stock market had become a [bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market_bubble "Stock market bubble") in March 2000 (the very height of the market top), which could lead to a sharp decline.

On [CNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNBC "CNBC")'s "How to Profit from the Real Estate Boom" in 2005, he noted that housing price rises could not outstrip inflation in the long term because, except for land restricted sites, house prices would tend toward building costs plus normal economic profit. Co‑panelist [David Lereah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lereah "David Lereah") disagreed. In February, Lereah had put out his book *Are You Missing the Real Estate Boom?* signaling the market top for housing prices. While Shiller repeated his precise timing again for another market bubble, because the general level of nationwide residential real estate prices do not reveal themselves until after a lag of about one year, people did not believe Shiller had called another top until late 2006 and early 2007.

Shiller was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 2003.

That same year, he co-authored a [Brookings Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings_Institution "Brookings Institution") paper entitled "Is There a Bubble in the Housing Market?" Shiller subsequently refined his position in the 2nd edition of *[Irrational Exuberance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_Exuberance_(book) "Irrational Exuberance (book)")* (2005), acknowledging that "further rises in the [stock and housing] markets could lead, eventually, to even more significant declines... A long-run consequence could be a decline in consumer and business confidence, and another, possibly worldwide, recession. This extreme outcome ... is not inevitable, but it is a much more serious risk than is widely acknowledged." Writing in *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")* in August 2006, Shiller again warned that "there is significant risk of a very bad period, with slow sales, slim commissions, falling prices, rising default and foreclosures, serious trouble in financial markets, and a possible recession sooner than most of us expected." In September 2007, almost exactly one year before the collapse of [Lehman Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehman_Brothers "Lehman Brothers"), Shiller wrote an article in which he predicted an imminent collapse in the U.S. housing market, and subsequent financial panic.

Shiller was awarded the [Deutsche Bank Prize in Financial Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bank_Prize_in_Financial_Economics "Deutsche Bank Prize in Financial Economics") in 2009 for his pioneering research in the field of financial economics, relating to the dynamics of asset prices, such as fixed income, equities, and real estate, and their metrics. His work has been influential in the development of the theory as well as its implications for practice and policy making. His contributions on risk sharing, financial market volatility, bubbles and crises, have received widespread attention among academics, practitioners, and policymakers alike. In 2010, he was named by Foreign Policy magazine to its list of top global thinkers.

In 2010 Shiller supported the idea that to fix the financial and banking systems, in order to avoid future financial crisis, banks need to issue a new kind of debt, known as [contingent capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contingent_convertible_bond "Contingent convertible bond"), that automatically converts into equity if the regulators determine that there is a systemic national financial crisis, and if the bank is simultaneously in violation of capital-adequacy.

In 2011 he attained the [Bloomberg 50 most influential people in global finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50_Most_Influential_(Bloomberg_Markets_ranking) "50 Most Influential (Bloomberg Markets ranking)") ranking list. In 2012, [Thomson Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_Reuters "Thomson Reuters") named him a contender for that year's [Nobel Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences"), citing his "pioneering contributions to [financial market volatility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatility_(finance) "Volatility (finance)") and the dynamics of [asset prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_price_inflation "Asset price inflation")".

On October 14, 2013, it was announced that Shiller had received the 2013 [Nobel Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") alongside [Eugene Fama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Fama "Eugene Fama") and [Lars Peter Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Peter_Hansen "Lars Peter Hansen").

His lecture at the prize ceremony explained why markets are not efficient. He presented an argument on why Eugene Fama's [Efficient Market Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_Market_Hypothesis "Efficient Market Hypothesis") (EMH) was fallacious. [EMH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient-market_hypothesis "Efficient-market hypothesis") postulates that the present value of an asset reflects the efficient incorporation of information into prices. According to Shiller, the results of the movement of the market are extremely erratic, unlike Fama's assertion where the movement would be smoother if it would reflect the intrinsic value of the assets: the "excess volatility puzzle". The results of the graphs provided by Shiller showed a clear aberration from that of the [Efficient Market Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_Market_Hypothesis "Efficient Market Hypothesis"). For example, the dividend growth had been 2% per year on stocks. However, it contradicted the [EMH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient-market_hypothesis "Efficient-market hypothesis") since the growth did not reflect the expected dividends. It is further explained by Shiller's Linearized Present Value model, which is a result of collaboration with his colleague and former student [John Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Y._Campbell "John Y. Campbell"), that only one-half to one-third of the fluctuations in the stock market are explained by the expected dividends model. Also, in the lecture, Shiller pointed out that variables such as interest rates and building costs did not explain the movement of the housing market.

On the other hand, Shiller believes that more information regarding the asset market is crucial for its efficiency. Additionally, he alluded to [John Maynard Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Keynes "John Maynard Keynes")'s explanation of stock markets to point out the irrationality of people while making decisions. Keynes compared the stock market to a beauty contest where people instead of betting on who they find attractive, bet on the contestant who the majority of people find attractive. Therefore, he believes that people do not use complicated mathematical calculations and a sophisticated economic model while participating in the asset market. He argued that a huge set of data is required for the market to operate efficiently. Since there were very minuscule data available on the asset markets for his research, let alone for the common people, he developed the [Case-Shiller index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case-Shiller_index "Case-Shiller index") that provides information about the trends in home prices. Thus, he added that the use of modern technology can benefit economists to accrue data of broader asset classes that will make the market more information-based and the prices more efficient.

In interviews in June 2015, Shiller warned of the potential of a stock market crash. In August 2015, after a flash crash in individual stocks, he continued to see bubbly conditions in stocks, bonds and housing.

In 2015, the [Council for Economic Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_Economic_Education "Council for Economic Education") honored Shiller with its Visionary Award.

In 2017, Shiller was quoted as calling [Bitcoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin "Bitcoin") the biggest financial bubble at the time. The perceived failure of the [Cincinnati Time Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Time_Store "Cincinnati Time Store") has been used as an analogy to suggest that cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin are a "speculative bubble" waiting to burst, according to Shiller.

In 2019, Shiller published *Narrative Economics*. The book received favourable reviews and was selected among the *Best books of 2019* list published by the *[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times "Financial Times")*.

In June 2024, 16 [Nobel Prize in Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") laureates, including Shiller, signed an open letter arguing that [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")’s fiscal and trade policies coupled with efforts to limit the [Federal Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve "Federal Reserve")'s independence would reignite inflation in the United States.

### Op-eds

Shiller has written [op-eds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed "Op-ed") since at least 2007 for such publications as *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*, where he has appeared in print on at least two dozen occasions.

- In "The Transformation of the [American Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dream "American Dream")", Shiller starts his history lesson on the evolution of language in 1931 with [James Truslow Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Truslow_Adams "James Truslow Adams")'s "dream of... opportunity for each according to his ability or achievement", through a chaplain's "equal opportunity for all men" (1954) to the [Allard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Allard "Wayne Allard") and [Sessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Sessions "Jeff Sessions") ([108th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/108th_United_States_Congress "108th United States Congress")) 2003 [American Dream Downpayment Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dream_Downpayment_Assistance_Act "American Dream Downpayment Assistance Act"), which was designed for the [Secretary of Housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development "United States Secretary of Housing and Urban Development") "to assist low-income families to achieve homeownership".
