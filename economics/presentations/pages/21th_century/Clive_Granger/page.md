**Sir Clive William John Granger** ([/ˈɡreɪndʒər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); 4 September 1934 – 27 May 2009) was a British [econometrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometrics "Econometrics") known for his contributions to nonlinear [time series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series "Time series") analysis. He taught in Britain, at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham "University of Nottingham") and in the United States, at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego"). Granger was awarded the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences") in 2003 in recognition of the contributions that he and his co-winner, [Robert F. Engle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Engle "Robert F. Engle"), had made to the analysis of time series data. This work fundamentally changed the way in which economists analyse financial and macroeconomic data.

## Biography

### Early life

Clive Granger was born in 1934 in [Swansea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swansea "Swansea"), south [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales "Wales"), [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom"), to Edward John Granger and Evelyn Granger. The next year his parents moved to [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Lincolnshire "Lincoln, Lincolnshire").

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") Granger and his mother moved to [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge") because Edward joined the Royal Air Force and deployed to North Africa. Here they stayed first with Evelyn's mother, then later Edward's parents, while Clive began school. Clive would later recall a primary school teacher telling his mother that "[Clive] would never be successful".

Clive started secondary school in Cambridge, but continued in [Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham "Nottingham"), where his family moved after the war. Here two teachers encouraged Granger's interest in physics and applied mathematics. He had anticipated following the convention of completing schooling at age 16 to enter the workforce and saw himself working in a bank or insurance company. However, positive social influence from his peers and support from his father led him to enroll in [sixth-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_form "Sixth form") for two years as preparation for a university degree.

Granger enrolled in a joint degree in economics and mathematics at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham "University of Nottingham") but switched to full mathematics in his second year. After receiving his BA in 1955, he remained at the University of Nottingham for a PhD in statistics under the supervision of [Harry Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Pitt "Harry Pitt").

In 1956, aged 21, Granger was appointed a junior lecturer in statistics at the university. His interest in applied statistics and economics led him to choose as the topic of his doctoral thesis [time series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series "Time series"), a field in which he felt that relatively little work had been done at the time. In 1959 Granger completed his PhD degree with a thesis titled "Testing for [Non-stationarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_process "Stationary process")".

### Academic life

Granger spent the next academic year, 1959–60, at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") under a [Harkness Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harkness_Fellowship "Harkness Fellowship") of the Commonwealth Fund. He had been invited to Princeton by [Oskar Morgenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Morgenstern "Oskar Morgenstern") to participate in his Econometrics Research Project. Here, Granger and [Michio Hatanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michio_Hatanaka?action=edit&redlink=1 "Michio Hatanaka") as assistants to [John Tukey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tukey "John Tukey") on a project using [Fourier analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_analysis "Fourier analysis") on economic data.

In 1964, Granger and Hatanaka published the results of their research in a book on *Spectral Analysis of Economic Time Series* (Tukey had encouraged them to write this themselves, as he was not going to publish the research results.) In 1963, Granger also wrote an article on "The typical spectral shape of an economic variable", which appeared in *[Econometrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometrica "Econometrica")* in 1966. Both the book and the article proved influential in the adoption of the new methods.

Granger also became a full professor at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham "University of Nottingham").

In a 1969 paper in *Econometrica*, Granger also introduced his concept of [Granger causality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granger_causality "Granger causality").

After reading a pre-print copy of the time series book by [George Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Box "George Box") and [Gwilym Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwilym_Jenkins "Gwilym Jenkins") in 1968, Granger became interested in forecasting. For the next few years he worked on this subject with his post-doctoral student, [Paul Newbold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Newbold "Paul Newbold"); and they wrote a book which became a standard reference in time series forecasting (published in 1977). Using simulations, Granger and Newbold also wrote the famous 1974 paper on [spurious regression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spurious_relationship "Spurious relationship") which led to a re-evaluation of previous empirical work in economics and to the econometric methodology.

Granger spent 22 years at the University of Nottingham. In 2005, the building that houses the Economics and Geography Departments was renamed the *Sir Clive Granger Building* in honor of his Nobel prize award.

In 1974 Granger moved to the [University of California at San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_San_Diego "University of California at San Diego"). In 1975 he participated in a [US Bureau of Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Bureau_of_Census "US Bureau of Census") committee, chaired by [Arnold Zellner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Zellner "Arnold Zellner"), on [seasonal adjustment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_adjustment "Seasonal adjustment"). At UCSD, Granger continued his research on time series, collaborating closely with Nobel prize co-recipient [Robert Engle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Engle "Robert Engle") (whom he helped bring to UCSD), [Roselyne Joyeux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roselyne_Joyeux?action=edit&redlink=1 "Roselyne Joyeux") (on [fractional integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoregressive_fractionally_integrated_moving_average "Autoregressive fractionally integrated moving average")), [Timo Teräsvirta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timo_Teräsvirta "Timo Teräsvirta") (on [nonlinear time series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_time_series?action=edit&redlink=1 "Nonlinear time series")) and others. Working with Robert Engle, he developed the concept of [cointegration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cointegration "Cointegration"), introduced in a 1987 joint paper in *[Econometrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometrica "Econometrica")*; for which he was awarded the Nobel prize in 2003.

Granger also supervised many PhD students, including [Mark Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Watson_(economist) "Mark Watson (economist)") (co-advisor with Robert Engle).

In later years Granger also used time series methods to analyse data outside economics. He worked on a project forecasting deforestation in the [Amazon rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_rainforest "Amazon rainforest"). In 2003, Granger retired from UCSD as a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus "Professor emeritus"). He was a Visiting Eminent Scholar of the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne "University of Melbourne") and the [University of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Canterbury "University of Canterbury"). He was a supporter of the [Campaign for the Establishment of a United Nations Parliamentary Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_the_Establishment_of_a_United_Nations_Parliamentary_Assembly "Campaign for the Establishment of a United Nations Parliamentary Assembly"), an organisation which campaigns for democratic reform of the United Nations.

Granger was married to Patricia (Lady Granger) from 1960 until his death. He was survived by their son, Mark William John, and their daughter, Claire Amanda Jane.

Granger died on 27 May 2009, at Scripps Memorial Hospital in [La Jolla, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla,_California "La Jolla, California").

## Honors and awards

In 2003, Granger and his collaborator Robert Engle were jointly awarded the [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences"). He was made a [Knight Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in the New Year's Honours in 2005.

Granger was a fellow of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society "Econometric Society") since 1972 and a Corresponding Fellow of the [British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy "British Academy") since 2002. In 2004, he was voted as one of the [100 Welsh Heroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_Welsh_Heroes "100 Welsh Heroes").
