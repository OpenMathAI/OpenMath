**Andrew Michael Spence** (born November 7, 1943) is a Canadian-American economist and Nobel laureate.

Spence is the William R. Berkley Professor in Economics and Business at the [Stern School of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_Stern_School_of_Business "New York University Stern School of Business") at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University"), and the Philip H. Knight Professor of Management, Emeritus, and Dean, Emeritus, at the [Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Graduate_School_of_Business "Stanford Graduate School of Business").

Together with [George A. Akerlof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Akerlof "George Akerlof") and [Joseph E. Stiglitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_E._Stiglitz "Joseph E. Stiglitz"), Spence is a co-recipient of the 2001 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences "Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences"), "for their analyses of markets with asymmetric information."

## Career

Spence is noted for his job-market [signaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signaling_(economics) "Signaling (economics)") model, which inspired research into this branch of [contract theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_theory "Contract theory"). In this model, employees signal their respective skills to employers by acquiring a certain degree of education, which is costly to them. Employers will pay higher wages to more educated employees, because they know that the proportion of employees with high abilities is higher among the educated ones, as it is less costly for them to acquire education than it is for employees with low abilities. For the model to work, it is not even necessary for education to have any intrinsic value if it can convey information about the sender (employee) to the recipient (employer) and if the signal is costly.

Spence received his middle and high school education at the [University of Toronto Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto_Schools "University of Toronto Schools"). He later joined the [Rotman School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotman_School_of_Management "Rotman School of Management") at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto "University of Toronto") as a member of the Rotman Dean's Advisory Board.

Spence attended [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") as an undergraduate student and graduated summa cum laude with a B.A. in philosophy in 1966, completing a senior thesis titled "Freedom and Determinism". Spence then studied at [Magdalen College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford "Magdalen College, Oxford"), [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") as a [Rhodes Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Scholarship "Rhodes Scholarship"), and received a B.A./M.A. in mathematics in 1968. Spence then began graduate studies in economics at Harvard University with the support of a Danforth Graduate Fellowship in the fall of 1968. He received a Ph.D. in economics in 1972, completing a dissertation titled "Market signalling" under the supervision of [Kenneth Arrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Arrow "Kenneth Arrow") and [Thomas C. Schelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Schelling "Thomas Schelling"). Spence was awarded the David A. Wells Prize for outstanding doctoral dissertation in 1972.

He stepped down as Dean of the Stanford Graduate School of Business in 1999 and joined [Oak Hill Capital Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Hill_Capital_Partners "Oak Hill Capital Partners"). He is the Chairman of the [Commission on Growth and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_Commission "Growth Commission"), and a distinguished visiting fellow at the [Council on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_on_Foreign_Relations "Council on Foreign Relations").

Spence joined the faculty of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University")'s [Stern School of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_Stern_School_of_Business "New York University Stern School of Business") on September 1, 2010. He joined the faculty of [SDA Bocconi School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SDA_Bocconi_School_of_Management "SDA Bocconi School of Management") in Italy in July 2011.

He is a senior fellow at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University")'s [Hoover Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoover_Institution "Hoover Institution") and the Philip H. Knight Professor Emeritus of Management in the Graduate School of Business. Spence is also a Commissioner for the Global Commission on Internet Governance. Additionally, Spence is also a member of the [Berggruen Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berggruen_Institute "Berggruen Institute")'s 21st Century Council.

He is the author of three books and 50 articles, and has also been a consistent contributor to *[Project Syndicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Syndicate "Project Syndicate")*, an international newspaper syndicate, since 2008. Among his beliefs are that [high-frequency trading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-frequency_trading "High-frequency trading") should be banned.

Spence had both [Bill Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Gates "Bill Gates") and [Steve Ballmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Ballmer "Steve Ballmer") in a graduate-level economics class at Harvard. In a 1999 *Fortune* interview, however, Gates and Ballmer admitted not attending class and passing only after cramming for four days before the final.

## Honors and awards

Spence is an Honorary Fellow of [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford "Magdalen College, Oxford"), where he studied as a [Rhodes Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Scholar "Rhodes Scholar"). He was the recipient of the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 2001, as well as the [John Bates Clark Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bates_Clark_Medal "John Bates Clark Medal") from the American Economics Association in 1981.

Spence was elected as a Fellow of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society "Econometric Society") in 1976 and a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1983.
