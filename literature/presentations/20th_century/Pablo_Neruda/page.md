**Pablo Neruda** ([/nəˈruːdə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*nə-ROO-də*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); Spanish: [[ˈpaβlo neˈɾuða]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Spanish "Help:IPA/Spanish") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pablo-Neruda.ogg "File:Pablo-Neruda.ogg"); born **Ricardo Eliécer Neftalí Reyes Basoalto**; 12 July 1904 – 23 September 1973) was a Chilean [poet-diplomat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet-diplomat "Poet-diplomat") and politician who won the [1971 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1971 Nobel Prize in Literature"). Neruda became known as a poet when he was 13 years old and wrote in a variety of styles, including [surrealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealism#Surrealist_literature "Surrealism") poems, [historical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_fiction "Historical fiction") [epics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_poetry "Epic poetry"), [political manifestos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_manifesto "Political manifesto"), a prose autobiography, and passionate love poems such as the ones in his collection *[Twenty Love Poems and a Song of Despair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty_Love_Poems_and_a_Song_of_Despair "Twenty Love Poems and a Song of Despair")* (1924).

Neruda occupied many diplomatic positions in various countries during his lifetime and served a term as a senator for the [Chilean Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_Communist_Party "Chilean Communist Party"). When President [Gabriel González Videla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_González_Videla "Gabriel González Videla") [outlawed communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Permanent_Defense_of_Democracy "Law of Permanent Defense of Democracy") in Chile in 1948, a warrant was issued for Neruda's arrest. Friends hid him for months, and in 1949 he escaped through [a mountain pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilpela_Pass "Lilpela Pass") near [Maihue Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maihue_Lake "Maihue Lake") into Argentina; he would not return to Chile for more than three years. He was a close advisor to Chile's [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Chile "Socialist Party of Chile") president [Salvador Allende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Allende "Salvador Allende"); Neruda served as Chile's ambassador to France under his presidency. When he went back to Chile after accepting his Nobel Prize in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm"), Allende invited him to read at the [Estadio Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Nacional_de_Chile "Estadio Nacional de Chile") before 70,000 people.

In September 1973, Neruda was diagnosed with prostate cancer. The same month, after the [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Chilean_coup_d'état "1973 Chilean coup d'état") led by [Augusto Pinochet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Pinochet "Augusto Pinochet") overthrew Allende's government and resulted in Allende's suicide, Neruda made plans to flee to Mexico in exile. On 23 September, the day before he was to leave the country, Neruda died at the Santa María medical clinic in Santiago, where he had been receiving cancer treatment. While the official cause of death was listed as complications from prostate cancer, the cause and circumstances of his death have been a subject of intense debate, controversy, and investigation ever since.

In 2011, an investigation was opened after Manuel Araya, Neruda's former personal assistant and driver, came forward with claims that Neruda had been murdered at the direction of Pinochet. According to Araya Neruda called him from the clinic hours before he died and said that a doctor had injected him in the stomach with an unknown substance. In 2013, Judge Mario Carroza ordered Neruda's remains exhumed for forensic testing. Initial results did not find any evidence to indicate Neruda had been poisoned. However, the [Chilean government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_and_Public_Security_(Chile) "Ministry of the Interior and Public Security (Chile)") issued a statement in 2015 acknowledging that "it was clearly possible and highly likely" that Neruda was killed as a result of "the intervention of third parties". In 2017, it was reported that an international panel of 13 forensic experts had determined that there was no indication Neruda had died of cancer, and that they had identified evidence of a potentially "laboratory cultivated bacteria". In 2023, a toxicology report confirmed the presence of *[Clostridium botulinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clostridium_botulinum "Clostridium botulinum")* in Neruda's teeth, indicating that he had in fact been poisoned. A court of appeals ruling reopened the official inquiry into his death in February 2024.

Neruda is often considered the [national poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_poet "National poet") of Chile, and his works have been popular and influential worldwide. The Colombian novelist [Gabriel García Márquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_García_Márquez "Gabriel García Márquez") once called him "the greatest poet of the 20th century in any language", and the critic [Harold Bloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Bloom "Harold Bloom") included Neruda as one of the writers central to the Western tradition in his book *[The Western Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Western_Canon "The Western Canon")*.

## Early life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Pablo_Neruda_Ricardo_Reyes.jpeg/250px-Pablo_Neruda_Ricardo_Reyes.jpeg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pablo_Neruda_Ricardo_Reyes.jpeg)

Neruda as a young man

Ricardo Eliécer Neftalí Reyes Basoalto was born on 12 July 1904, in [Parral, Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parral,_Chile "Parral, Chile"), a city in [Linares Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linares_Province "Linares Province"), now part of the greater [Maule Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maule_Region "Maule Region"), some 350 km south of [Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago,_Chile "Santiago, Chile"). His father, José del Carmen Reyes Morales, was a railway employee, and his mother Rosa Neftalí Basoalto Opazo was a school teacher who died on 14 September two months after he was born. On 26 September, he was baptized in the parish of San Jose de Parral. Neruda grew up in Temuco with Rodolfo, a half-brother born after his father remarried, and a half-sister, Laura Herminia "Laurita," from one of his father's extramarital affairs (her mother was Aurelia Tolrà, a Catalan woman). He composed his first poems in the winter of 1914. Neruda was an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist").

## Literary career

Neruda's father opposed his son's interest in writing and literature, but he received encouragement from others, including the future Nobel Prize winner [Gabriela Mistral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Mistral "Gabriela Mistral"), who headed the local school. On 18 July 1917, at the age of 13, he published his first work, an essay titled "Entusiasmo y perseverancia" ("Enthusiasm and Perseverance") in the local daily newspaper *La Mañana,* and signed it Neftalí Reyes. From 1918 to mid-1920, he published numerous poems, such as "Mis ojos" ("My eyes"), and essays in local magazines as Neftalí Reyes. In 1919, he participated in the literary contest Juegos Florales del Maule and won third place for his poem "Comunión ideal" or "Nocturno ideal." By mid-1920, when he adopted the pseudonym Pablo Neruda, he was a published author of poems, prose, and journalism. He is thought to have derived his pen name from the [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs "Czechs") poet [Jan Neruda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Neruda "Jan Neruda"), though other sources say the true inspiration was Moravian violinist [Wilma Neruda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilma_Neruda "Wilma Neruda"), whose name appears in Arthur Conan Doyle's novel *[A Study in Scarlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Study_in_Scarlet "A Study in Scarlet").*

> something started in my soul,
> fever or forgotten wings,
> and I made my own way,
> deciphering
> that fire
> and wrote the first faint line,
> faint without substance, pure
> nonsense,
> pure wisdom,
> of someone who knows nothing,
> and suddenly I saw
> the heavens
> unfastened
> and open.

— From "Poetry", *Memorial de Isla Negra* (1964).
 Trans. Alastair Reid.

In 1921, at the age of 16, Neruda moved to [Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago,_Chile "Santiago, Chile") to study [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language") at the [Universidad de Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_de_Chile "Universidad de Chile") with the intention of becoming a teacher. However, he soon devoted all his time to writing poems, and with the help of well-known writer [Eduardo Barrios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Barrios "Eduardo Barrios"), he managed to meet and impress Don Carlos George Nascimento, the most important publisher in Chile at the time. In 1923, his first volume of verse, *Crepusculario* (*Book of Twilights*), was published by Editorial Nascimento, followed the next year by *[Veinte poemas de amor y una canción desesperada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veinte_poemas_de_amor_y_una_canción_desesperada "Veinte poemas de amor y una canción desesperada")* (*Twenty Love Poems and a song of despair*), a collection of love poems that was controversial for its eroticism, especially considering its author's young age. Both works were critically acclaimed and have been translated into many languages. A second edition of *Veinte poemas* appeared in 1932. In the years since its publication, millions of copies have been sold, and it became Neruda's best-known work. Almost 100 years later, Veinte Poemas is still the best-selling poetry book in the Spanish language. By the age of 20, Neruda had established an international reputation as a poet but faced poverty.

In 1926, Neruda published the collection *tentativa del hombre infinito* (*venture of the infinite man*) and the novel *El habitante y su esperanza* (*The Inhabitant and His Hope*). In 1927, out of financial desperation, he took an honorary consulship in [Rangoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangoon "Rangoon"), the capital of [British-ruled Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Burma "British rule in Burma"), then administered from [New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Delhi "New Delhi") as a [province of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidencies_and_provinces_of_British_India "Presidencies and provinces of British India"). Later, mired in isolation and loneliness, he worked in [Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo "Colombo"), [Batavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Dutch_East_Indies "Batavia, Dutch East Indies") and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_in_the_Straits_Settlements "Singapore in the Straits Settlements"). In Batavia the following year, he met and married his first wife, a Dutch bank employee known as Maruca. While he was in the diplomatic service, Neruda read large amounts of verse, experimented with many different poetic forms, and wrote the first two volumes of [*Residencia en la Tierra*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residence_on_Earth "Residence on Earth"), which include many [surrealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealism "Surrealism") poems.

## Diplomatic and political career

### Spanish Civil War

After returning to Chile, Neruda was given diplomatic posts in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires") and, later, [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona "Barcelona"), Spain. He succeeded Gabriela Mistral as consul in [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid "Madrid"), where he became the center of a lively literary circle, befriending such writers as [Rafael Alberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Alberti "Rafael Alberti"), [Federico García Lorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_García_Lorca "Federico García Lorca"), and the Peruvian poet [César Vallejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/César_Vallejo "César Vallejo"). His only offspring, his daughter **Malva Marina (Trinidad) Reyes**, was born in Madrid in 1934, the product of his first marriage to María Antonia Hagenaar Vogelzang. Reyes was plagued with severe health problems, particularly suffering from [hydrocephalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocephalus "Hydrocephalus"). She died in 1943 at the age of eight, having spent most of her short life with a foster family in the Netherlands after Neruda ignored and abandoned her, forcing her mother to work to support her care. Half of that time was during the occupation of Holland by the Nazis, who viewed birth defects as denoting genetic inferiority. Reyes was repudiated, mocked, and abandoned by her father and died in utter indigence in war-devastated and Nazi-occupied Netherlands.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Graf_van_Malva_Marina_Reyes%2C_Oude_Begraafplaats%2C_Gouda.jpg/250px-Graf_van_Malva_Marina_Reyes%2C_Oude_Begraafplaats%2C_Gouda.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Graf_van_Malva_Marina_Reyes,_Oude_Begraafplaats,_Gouda.jpg)

Grave of Malva Marina, the sole daughter of Pablo Neruda

During this period, Neruda became estranged from his wife and instead began a relationship with [Delia del Carril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delia_del_Carril?action=edit&redlink=1 "Delia del Carril"), an aristocratic Argentine artist who was 20 years his senior.
As Spain became engulfed in [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War"), Neruda became intensely politicized for the first time. His experiences during the Spanish Civil War and its aftermath moved him away from privately focused work in the direction of collective obligation. Neruda became an ardent [Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism") for the rest of his life. The radical leftist politics of his literary friends, as well as that of del Carril, were contributing factors, but the most important catalyst was the execution of García Lorca by forces loyal to the dictator [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco "Francisco Franco"). Through his speeches and writings, Neruda threw his support behind the [Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic "Second Spanish Republic"), publishing the collection *España en el corazón* (*Spain in Our Hearts*, 1938). He lost his post as consul due to his political militancy. In July 1937, he attended the Second International Writers' Congress, the purpose of which was to discuss the attitude of intellectuals toward the war in Spain, held in [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia "Valencia"), [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona "Barcelona"), and [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid "Madrid") and attended by many writers including [André Malraux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Malraux "André Malraux"), [Ernest Hemingway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Hemingway "Ernest Hemingway"), and [Stephen Spender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Spender "Stephen Spender").

Neruda's marriage to Vogelzang broke down, and he eventually obtained a divorce in Mexico in 1943. His estranged wife moved to [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo "Monte Carlo") to escape the hostilities in [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain") and then to the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands "Netherlands") with their very ill only child, and he never saw either of them again. After leaving his wife, Neruda lived with Delia del Carril in France, eventually marrying her (shortly after his divorce) in [Tetecala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetecala "Tetecala") in 1943; however, his new marriage was not recognized by Chilean authorities as his divorce from Vogelzang was deemed illegal.

Following the election of [Pedro Aguirre Cerda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Aguirre_Cerda "Pedro Aguirre Cerda") (whom Neruda supported) as President of Chile in 1938, Neruda was appointed special Consul for Spanish emigrants in Paris. There he was responsible for what he called "the noblest mission I have ever undertaken": transporting 2,000 Spanish refugees who had been housed by the French in [squalid camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_camps_in_France "Concentration camps in France") to Chile on an old ship called the *[Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_(ship) "Winnipeg (ship)")*. Neruda is sometimes charged with having selected only fellow Communists for emigration, to the exclusion of others who had fought on the side of the Republic. Many Republicans and Anarchists were killed during the German invasion and occupation. Others deny these accusations, pointing out that Neruda chose only a few hundred of the 2,000 refugees personally; the rest were selected by the Service for the Evacuation of Spanish Refugees set up by [Juan Negrín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Negrín "Juan Negrín"), President of the [Spanish Republican Government in Exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Republican_government_in_exile "Spanish Republican government in exile").

### Mexican appointment

Neruda's next diplomatic post was as Consul General in [Mexico City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_City "Mexico City") from 1940 to 1943. During his time there, he married del Carril and learned that his daughter Malva had died at the age of eight in Nazi-occupied Netherlands. In 1940, following the failed assassination attempt against [Leon Trotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky "Leon Trotsky"), Neruda arranged a Chilean visa for the Mexican painter [David Alfaro Siqueiros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Alfaro_Siqueiros "David Alfaro Siqueiros"), who had been accused of involvement in the conspiracy to assassinate Trotsky. Neruda later stated that he had done it at the request of the Mexican President, [Manuel Ávila Camacho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Ávila_Camacho "Manuel Ávila Camacho"). This allowed Siqueiros, who was then imprisoned, to leave Mexico for Chile, where he stayed at Neruda's private residence. In return for Neruda's assistance, Siqueiros spent over a year painting a mural at a school in [Chillán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chillán "Chillán"). While Neruda's relationship with Siqueiros drew criticism, he dismissed the allegation that he had intended to aid an assassin as "sensationalist politico-literary harassment".

### Return to Chile

In 1943, upon his return to Chile, Neruda embarked on a tour of [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru "Peru"), where he visited [Machu Picchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machu_Picchu "Machu Picchu"). This experience later inspired [*Alturas de Macchu Picchu*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Heights_of_Macchu_Picchu "The Heights of Macchu Picchu"), a book-length poem in 12 parts that he completed in 1945. The poem expressed his growing awareness of and interest in the ancient civilizations of the Americas. He further explored this theme in *[Canto General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canto_General "Canto General")* (1950). In *Alturas*, Neruda celebrated the achievement of *Macchu Picchu* but also condemned the slavery that had made it possible. In *Canto XII*, he called upon the dead of many centuries to be reborn and speak through him. [Martín Espada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martín_Espada "Martín Espada"), a poet and professor of creative writing at the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst "University of Massachusetts Amherst"), has hailed the work as a masterpiece, declaring that "there is no greater political poem".

### Communism

Bolstered by his experiences in the Spanish Civil War, Neruda, like many left-leaning intellectuals of his generation, came to admire the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") of [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin "Joseph Stalin"). He did so partly due to the role it played in defeating [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") and partly because of an idealistic interpretation of [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist "Marxist") doctrine. This sentiment is echoed in poems such as *Canto a Stalingrado* ("Song to Stalingrad") (1942) and *Nuevo canto de amor a Stalingrado* ("New Love Song to Stalingrad") (1943). In 1953, Neruda was awarded the [Stalin Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalin_Peace_Prize "Stalin Peace Prize"). Upon Stalin's death that same year, Neruda wrote an ode to him, as he also wrote poems in praise of [Fulgencio Batista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulgencio_Batista "Fulgencio Batista"), *Saludo a Batista* ("Salute to Batista"), and later, [Fidel Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Castro "Fidel Castro"). His fervent Stalinism eventually drove a wedge between Neruda and his long-time friend, Mexican poet [Octavio Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavio_Paz "Octavio Paz"), who commented that "Neruda became more and more Stalinist, while I became less and less enchanted with Stalin." Their differences came to a head after the Nazi-Soviet [Ribbentrop–Molotov Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbentrop–Molotov_Pact "Ribbentrop–Molotov Pact") of 1939, when they almost came to blows in an argument over Stalin. Although Paz still considered Neruda "The greatest poet of his generation", in an essay on [Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Solzhenitsyn "Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn"), he wrote that when he thinks of "Neruda and other famous Stalinist writers and poets, I feel the gooseflesh that I get from reading certain passages of the *[Inferno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inferno_(Dante) "Inferno (Dante)")*. No doubt they began in good faith ... but insensibly, commitment by commitment, they saw themselves becoming entangled in a mesh of lies, falsehoods, deceits, and perjuries, until they lost their souls." On 15 July 1945, at [Pacaembu Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacaembu_Stadium "Pacaembu Stadium") in [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/São_Paulo "São Paulo"), Brazil, Neruda read to 100,000 people in honor of the Communist revolutionary leader [Luís Carlos Prestes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luís_Carlos_Prestes "Luís Carlos Prestes").

Neruda hailed [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin "Vladimir Lenin") as the "great genius of this century". In a speech he gave on 5 June 1946, Neruda also paid tribute to the late Soviet leader [Mikhail Kalinin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Kalinin "Mikhail Kalinin"), whom Neruda regarded as a "man of noble life," "the great constructor of the future," and "a comrade in arms of Lenin and Stalin." Neruda later came to regret his fondness for the Soviet Union, explaining that "in those days, Stalin seemed to us the conqueror who had crushed [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler "Hitler")'s armies." Of a subsequent visit to China in 1957, Neruda wrote: "What has estranged me from the Chinese revolutionary process has not been [Mao Tse-tung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong "Mao Zedong") but Mao Tse-tungism." He labeled this Mao Tse-Stalinism as "the repetition of a cult of a Socialist deity". Despite his disillusionment with Stalin, Neruda never lost his fundamental faith in communist theory and remained loyal to the Communist party. Anxious not to provide ammunition to his ideological enemies, he would later refuse publicly to condemn the Soviet repression of dissident writers like [Boris Pasternak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Pasternak "Boris Pasternak") and [Joseph Brodsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Brodsky "Joseph Brodsky"), an attitude with which even some of his staunchest admirers disagreed.

[![Wikisource logo](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Wikisource-logo.svg/40px-Wikisource-logo.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wikisource-logo.svg)

English [Wikisource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikisource "Wikisource") has original text related to this article:

**[I Accuse](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/en:I%20accuse%20(Neruda) "s:en:I accuse (Neruda)")**

On 4 March 1945, Neruda was elected as a Communist [Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Chile "Senate of Chile") representing the northern provinces of [Antofagasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antofagasta_Region "Antofagasta Region") and [Tarapacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarapacá_Region "Tarapacá Region") in the [Atacama Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atacama_Desert "Atacama Desert"). He officially joined the [Communist Party of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Chile "Communist Party of Chile") four months later. In 1946, the Radical Party's presidential candidate, [Gabriel González Videla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_González_Videla "Gabriel González Videla"), asked Neruda to act as his campaign manager. González Videla was supported by a coalition of left-wing parties, and Neruda fervently campaigned on his behalf. However, once in office, González Videla turned against the Communist Party and enacted the *[Ley de Defensa Permanente de la Democracia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Permanent_Defense_of_the_Democracy "Law of Permanent Defense of the Democracy")* (Law of Permanent Defense of the Democracy). The breaking point for Senator Neruda was the violent repression of a Communist-led miners' strike in [Lota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lota,_Chile "Lota, Chile") in October 1947, when striking workers were herded into island military prisons and a [concentration camp in the town of Pisagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisagua_Prison_Camp "Pisagua Prison Camp"). Neruda's criticism of González Videla culminated in a dramatic speech in the Chilean senate on 6 January 1948, which became known as "Yo acuso" ("I accuse"), during which he read out the names of the miners and their families who were imprisoned at the concentration camp.

In 1959, Neruda was present when [Fidel Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Castro "Fidel Castro") was honored at a welcoming ceremony hosted by the [Central University of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_University_of_Venezuela "Central University of Venezuela"). There, he spoke to a massive gathering of students and read his poem *Un canto para Bolívar* ("A Song for Bolívar"). Prior to this, he shared his sentiments: "In this painful and victorious hour that the peoples of the Americas are living, my poem, with changes in location, can be understood as directed towards Fidel Castro, because in the struggles for freedom, the destiny of a man always emerges to instill confidence in the spirit of greatness in the history of our nations." During the late 1960s, Argentine writer [Jorge Luis Borges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Luis_Borges "Jorge Luis Borges") was asked for his opinion on Neruda. Borges stated, "I think of him as a very fine poet, a very fine poet. I don't admire him as a man; I think of him as a very mean man." He said that Neruda had not spoken out against Argentine President [Juan Perón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Perón "Juan Perón") because he was afraid to risk his reputation, noting, "I was an Argentine poet; he was a Chilean poet; he's on the side of the Communists; I'm against them. So I felt he was behaving very wisely in avoiding a meeting that would have been quite uncomfortable for both of us."

## Hiding and exile, 1948–1952

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-10640-0020%2C_Berlin%2C_III._Weltfestspiele%2C_Vorbereitung.jpg/250px-Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-10640-0020%2C_Berlin%2C_III._Weltfestspiele%2C_Vorbereitung.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bundesarchiv_Bild_183-10640-0020,_Berlin,_III._Weltfestspiele,_Vorbereitung.jpg)

Neruda with his wife and [Erich Honecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_Honecker "Erich Honecker") in 1951

A few weeks after his "Yo acuso" speech in 1948, finding himself threatened with arrest, Neruda went into hiding. He and his wife were smuggled from house to house, hidden by supporters and admirers for the next 13 months. While in hiding, Senator Neruda was removed from office, and in September 1948, the Communist Party was banned altogether under the *Ley de Defensa Permanente de la Democracia,* called by critics the *Ley Maldita* (Accursed Law), which eliminated over 26,000 people from the electoral registers, thus stripping them of their right to vote. Neruda later moved to [Valdivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdivia,_Chile "Valdivia, Chile") in southern Chile. From Valdivia, he moved to *Fundo Huishue,* a forestry estate in the vicinity of [Huishue Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huishue_Lake "Huishue Lake"). Neruda's life underground ended in March 1949 when he fled over the [Lilpela Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilpela_Pass "Lilpela Pass") in the [Andes Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes_Mountains "Andes Mountains") to [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina "Argentina") on horseback. He would dramatically recount his escape from Chile in his Nobel Prize lecture.

Once out of Chile, he spent the next three years in exile. In [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires"), Neruda took advantage of the slight resemblance between him and his friend, the future Nobel Prize-winning novelist and cultural attaché to the [Guatemalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala "Guatemala") embassy, [Miguel Ángel Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Ángel_Asturias "Miguel Ángel Asturias"), to travel to Europe using Asturias' passport. [Pablo Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Picasso "Pablo Picasso") arranged his entrance into [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), and Neruda made a surprise appearance there to a stunned World Congress of Peace Forces, while the Chilean government denied that the poet could have escaped the country. Neruda spent those three years traveling extensively throughout Europe as well as taking trips to India, China, [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka"), and the Soviet Union. His trip to Mexico in late 1949 was lengthened due to a serious bout of [phlebitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phlebitis "Phlebitis"). A Chilean singer named [Matilde Urrutia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilde_Urrutia "Matilde Urrutia") was hired to care for him, and they began an affair that would, years later, culminate in marriage. During his exile, Urrutia would travel from country to country, shadowing him, and they would arrange meetings whenever they could. Matilde Urrutia was the muse for *[Los versos del capitán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_versos_del_capitán "Los versos del capitán")*, a book of poetry that Neruda later published anonymously in 1952.

> from **"Full Woman, Fleshly Apple, Hot Moon"**
>
> Full woman, fleshly apple, hot moon,
> thick smell of seaweed, crushed mud and light,
> what obscure brilliance opens between your columns?
> What ancient night does a man touch with his senses?
>
> Loving is a journey with water and with stars,
> with smothered air and abrupt storms of flour:
> loving is a clash of lightning-bolts
> and two bodies defeated by a single drop of honey.

— From "Full Woman, Fleshly Apple, Hot Moon",
*Selected Poems* translated by Stephen Mitchell (1997)

While in Mexico, Neruda also published his lengthy epic poem *[Canto General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canto_General "Canto General")*, a [Whitmanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Whitman "Walt Whitman") catalog of the history, geography, and flora and fauna of South America, accompanied by Neruda's observations and experiences. Many of them dealt with his time underground in Chile, during which he composed much of the poem. In fact, he had carried the manuscript with him during his escape on horseback. A month later, a different edition of 5,000 copies was boldly published in Chile by the outlawed Communist Party, based on a manuscript Neruda had left behind. In Mexico, he was granted honorary Mexican citizenship. Neruda's 1952 stay in a villa owned by Italian historian [Edwin Cerio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Cerio "Edwin Cerio") on the island of [Capri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capri "Capri") was fictionalized in [Antonio Skarmeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Skarmeta "Antonio Skarmeta")'s 1985 novel *Ardiente Paciencia* (*Ardent Patience,* later known as *El cartero de Neruda,* or *Neruda's Postman*), which inspired the popular film *[Il Postino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Postino "Il Postino")* (1994).

## Second return to Chile

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Pablo_Neruda_%281966%29.jpg/250px-Pablo_Neruda_%281966%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pablo_Neruda_(1966).jpg)

Neruda recording his poetry at the U.S. Library of Congress in 1966

By 1952, the González Videla government was on its last legs, weakened by corruption scandals. The Chilean Socialist Party was in the process of nominating [Salvador Allende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Allende "Salvador Allende") as its candidate for the September 1952 presidential elections and was keen to have the presence of Neruda, by now Chile's most prominent left-wing literary figure, to support the campaign. Neruda returned to Chile in August of that year and rejoined Delia del Carril, who had traveled ahead of him some months earlier, but the marriage was crumbling. Del Carril eventually learned of his affair with Matilde Urrutia, and he sent her back to Chile in 1955. She convinced the Chilean officials to lift his arrest, allowing Urrutia and Neruda to go to Capri, Italy. Now united with Urrutia, Neruda would, aside from many foreign trips and a stint as Allende's ambassador to France from 1970 to 1973, spend the rest of his life in Chile.

By this time, Neruda enjoyed worldwide fame as a poet, and his books were being translated into virtually all the major languages of the world. He vigorously denounced the United States during the [Cuban Missile Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Missile_Crisis "Cuban Missile Crisis") and later in the decade repeatedly condemned the U.S. for its involvement in the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War"). But being one of the most prestigious and outspoken left-wing intellectuals alive, he also attracted opposition from ideological opponents. The [Congress for Cultural Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_for_Cultural_Freedom "Congress for Cultural Freedom"), an anti-communist organization covertly established and funded by the U.S. [Central Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency"), adopted Neruda as one of its primary targets and launched a campaign to undermine his reputation, reviving the old claim that he had been an accomplice in the attack on Leon Trotsky in Mexico City in 1940. The campaign became more intense when it became known that Neruda was a candidate for the 1964 Nobel Prize, which was eventually awarded to [Jean-Paul Sartre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Paul_Sartre "Jean-Paul Sartre"). (who rejected it).

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/La_Sebastiana_Neruda_1.jpg/250px-La_Sebastiana_Neruda_1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:La_Sebastiana_Neruda_1.jpg)

La Sebastiana, Neruda's house in [Valparaíso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valparaíso "Valparaíso")

In 1966, Neruda was invited to attend an [International PEN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_PEN "International PEN") conference in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"). Officially, he was barred from entering the U.S. because he was a communist, but the conference organizer, playwright [Arthur Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Miller "Arthur Miller"), eventually prevailed upon the [Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson "Lyndon B. Johnson") Administration to grant Neruda a visa. Neruda gave readings to packed halls and even recorded some poems for the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress"). Miller later opined that Neruda's adherence to his communist ideals of the 1930s was a result of his protracted exclusion from "bourgeois society." Due to the presence of many [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc "Eastern Bloc") writers, Mexican writer [Carlos Fuentes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Fuentes "Carlos Fuentes") later wrote that the PEN conference marked a "beginning of the end" of the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War").

Upon Neruda's return to Chile, he stopped in Peru, where he gave readings to enthusiastic crowds in [Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima "Lima") and [Arequipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arequipa "Arequipa") and was received by President [Fernando Belaúnde Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Belaúnde_Terry "Fernando Belaúnde Terry"). However, this visit also prompted an unpleasant backlash; because the Peruvian government had come out against the government of [Fidel Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Castro "Fidel Castro") in [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba "Cuba"), July 1966 saw more than 100 Cuban intellectuals retaliate against the poet by signing a letter that charged Neruda with colluding with the enemy, calling him an example of the "tepid, pro-Yankee revisionism" then prevalent in Latin America. The affair was particularly painful for Neruda because of his previous outspoken support for the Cuban revolution, and he never visited the island again, even after receiving an invitation in 1968.

After the death of [Che Guevara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che_Guevara "Che Guevara") in [Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivia "Bolivia") in 1967, Neruda wrote several articles regretting the loss of a "great hero". At the same time, he told his friend Aida Figueroa not to cry for Che but for [Luis Emilio Recabarren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Emilio_Recabarren "Luis Emilio Recabarren"), the father of the Chilean communist movement who preached a pacifist revolution over Che's violent ways.

## Last years and death

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/La_Chascona_Santiago_de_Chile.jpg/250px-La_Chascona_Santiago_de_Chile.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:La_Chascona_Santiago_de_Chile.jpg)

La Chascona, Neruda's house in [Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago,_Chile "Santiago, Chile")

In 1970, Neruda was nominated as a candidate for the Chilean presidency but ended up giving his support to [Salvador Allende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Allende "Salvador Allende"), who later won the [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Chilean_presidential_election "1970 Chilean presidential election") and was inaugurated in 1970 as Chile's first democratically elected socialist head of state. Shortly thereafter, Allende appointed Neruda the Chilean ambassador to France, lasting from 1971 to 1973; his final diplomatic posting. During his stint in Paris, Neruda helped to renegotiate the external debt of Chile, billions owed to European and American banks, but within months of his arrival in Paris, his health began to deteriorate. Neruda returned to Chile two-and-a-half years later due to his failing health.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/55/Neruda_Argentina.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Neruda_Argentina.jpg)

Buenos Aires 1971

In 1971, Neruda was awarded the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize"), a decision that did not come easily because some of the committee members had not forgotten Neruda's past praise of Stalinist dictatorship. But his Swedish translator, [Artur Lundkvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Lundkvist "Artur Lundkvist"), did his best to ensure the Chilean received the prize. "A poet," Neruda stated in his Stockholm speech of acceptance of the Nobel Prize, "is at the same time a force for solidarity and for solitude." The following year, Neruda was awarded the prestigious Golden Wreath Award at the [Struga Poetry Evenings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struga_Poetry_Evenings "Struga Poetry Evenings").

As the [coup d'état of 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_coup_of_1973 "Chilean coup of 1973") unfolded, Neruda was diagnosed with [prostate cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostate_cancer "Prostate cancer"). The military coup led by General [Augusto Pinochet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Pinochet "Augusto Pinochet") saw Neruda's hopes for Chile destroyed. Shortly thereafter, during a search of the house and grounds at Isla Negra by Chilean armed forces at which Neruda was reportedly present, the poet famously remarked: "Look around – there's only one thing of danger for you here – poetry."

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b1/F%C3%A9retro_Neruda.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Féretro_Neruda.jpg)

Neruda laid out in his coffin, 1973

It was originally reported that, on the evening of 23 September 1973, at Santiago's Santa María Clinic, Neruda had died of heart failure.

However, "(t)hat day, he was alone in the hospital where he had already spent five days. His health was declining, and he called his wife, Matilde Urrutia, so she could come immediately because they were giving him something, and he wasn't feeling good." On 12 May 2011, the Mexican magazine *Proceso* published an interview with his former driver Manuel Araya Osorio in which he states that he was present when Neruda called his wife and warned that he believed Pinochet had ordered a doctor to kill him, and that he had just been given an injection in his stomach. He died six-and-a-half hours later. Even reports from the pro-Pinochet El Mercurio newspaper, the day after Neruda's death, refer to an injection given immediately before Neruda's death. According to an official Chilean Interior Ministry report prepared in March 2015 for the court investigation into Neruda's death, "he was either given an injection or something orally" at the Santa María Clinic "which caused his death six-and-a-half hours later. The 1971 Nobel laureate was scheduled to fly to Mexico where he may have been planning to lead a government in exile that would denounce General Augusto Pinochet, who led the coup against Allende on September 11, according to his friends, researchers, and other political observers". The funeral took place amidst a massive [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carabineros_de_Chile "Carabineros de Chile") presence, and mourners took advantage of the occasion to protest against the new regime, established just a couple of weeks before. Neruda's house was broken into, and his papers and books taken or destroyed.

In 1974, his *Memoirs* appeared under the title *I Confess I Have Lived,* updated to the last days of the poet's life, and including a final segment describing the [death of Salvador Allende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Salvador_Allende "Death of Salvador Allende") during the storming of the Moneda Palace by General Pinochet and other generals – occurring only 12 days before Neruda died. [Matilde Urrutia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilde_Urrutia "Matilde Urrutia") subsequently compiled and edited for publication the memoirs and possibly his final poem "Right Comrade, It's the Hour of the Garden." These and other activities brought her into conflict with Pinochet's government, which continually sought to curtail Neruda's influence on the Chilean collective consciousness. Urrutia's own memoir, *My Life with Pablo Neruda,* was published posthumously in 1986. Manuel Araya, his Communist-Party–appointed chauffeur, published a book about Neruda's final days in 2012.

## Controversy

### Rumored murder and exhumation

In June 2013, a Chilean judge ordered an investigation to be launched following suggestions that Neruda had been killed by the Pinochet regime due to his pro-Allende stance and political views. Neruda's driver, Manuel Araya, claimed that he had seen Neruda two days before his death and that doctors had administered poison as the poet was preparing to go into exile. Araya claimed that he was driving Neruda to buy medicine when he was suddenly stopped by military personnel who arrested him, hijacked the [Fiat 125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_125 "Fiat 125") he was driving, and took him to police headquarters where they tortured him. He found out Neruda had died after Santiago Archbishop Raúl Silva Henríquez informed him. In December 2011, Chile's Communist Party asked Chilean Judge Mario Carroza to order the exhumation of the remains of the poet. Carroza had been conducting probes into hundreds of deaths allegedly connected to abuses of Pinochet's regime from 1973 to 1990. Carroza's inquiry during 2011–12 uncovered enough evidence to order the exhumation in April 2013. Eduardo Contreras, a Chilean lawyer who was leading the push for a full investigation, commented, "We have world-class labs from India, Switzerland, Germany, the US, Sweden; they have all offered to do the lab work for free." The Pablo Neruda Foundation fought the exhumation on the grounds that Araya's claims were unbelievable.

In June 2013, a court order was issued to find the man who allegedly poisoned Neruda. Police were investigating [Michael Townley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Townley "Michael Townley"), who was facing trial for the killings of General [Carlos Prats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Prats "Carlos Prats") (Buenos Aires, 1974), and ex-Chancellor, [Orlando Letelier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Letelier "Orlando Letelier") (Washington, 1976). The Chilean government suggested that the 2015 test showed it was "highly probable that a third party" was responsible for his death.

Test results were released on 8 November 2013, of the seven-month investigation by a 15-member forensic team. Patricio Bustos, the head of Chile's medical legal service, stated, "No relevant chemical substances have been found that could be linked to Mr. Neruda's death" at the time. However, Carroza said that he was waiting for the results of the last scientific tests conducted in May (2015), which found that Neruda was infected with the *[Staphylococcus aureus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staphylococcus_aureus "Staphylococcus aureus")* bacterium, which can be highly toxic and result in death if modified.

A team of 16 international experts, led by Spanish forensic specialist Aurelio Luna, from the [University of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Murcia "University of Murcia"), announced on 20 October 2017 that, "from analysis of the data, we cannot accept that the poet had been in an imminent situation of death at the moment of entering the hospital," and that death from prostate cancer was not likely at the moment when he died. The team also discovered something in Neruda's remains that could possibly be a laboratory-cultivated bacterium. The results of their continuing analysis were expected in 2018. His cause of death was, in fact, listed as a heart attack. Scientists who exhumed Neruda's body in 2013 also supported claims that he was suffering from prostate cancer when he died.

In 2023, a team from [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University "McMaster University") and the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen") confirmed the presence of the *[Clostridium botulinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clostridium_botulinum "Clostridium botulinum")* bacteria in Neruda's bloodstream, although it is not clear if this contributed to his death. McMaster researcher Debi Poinar noted that if Neruda had died of [botulism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botulism "Botulism"), he would have suffered paralysis or [sepsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepsis "Sepsis"). The bacteria was found to have been mainly concentrated in one of Neruda's [molars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molar_(tooth) "Molar (tooth)").

Some scientists involved in the testing also spoke with *[Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle "Deutsche Welle")* to deny the family's claim that the testing confirmed he was poisoned. Despite at times being used as a biological weapon, the bacteria also has a long history of being present in food products such as fruit, vegetables, seafood and canned food, and at times has even been used for medical treatment. John Austin, who leads the Botulism Reference Service for Canada, also told *Deutsche Welle* that the mere presence of *C. botulinum* is not harmful to humans, and that any harm that comes from it is due to the toxins it produces when it grows. Austin further stated that the bacteria in Neruda's mouth could have expanded after he died, as it is common for bacteria to multiply in the body among people after they die. Fabrizio Anniballi, another botulism expert who was not directly involved in the research on Neruda's remains, further noted it was too unlikely that the injection he was alleged to have been given into his belly gave him botulism, noting that it was also claimed he died a mere six hours after it happened, which is not a feasible amount of time to trigger botulism. Debi Poinar also acknowledged to *Deutsche Welle* that, while some C. botulinum was found in Neruda's bones, it had yet to be discerned whether it was from the same source as that found in the molar.

### Sexual assault passage

In November 2018, the Cultural Committee of Chile's lower house voted in favor of renaming Santiago's main airport after Neruda. The decision sparked protests from feminist groups who highlighted a passage in Neruda's memoirs describing raping a woman in 1929 in Ceylon ([Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka")). Several feminist groups, bolstered by a growing [#MeToo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me_Too_movement "Me Too movement") and [anti-femicide movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni_una_menos "Ni una menos"), stated that Neruda should not be honored by his country, describing the passage as evidence of rape. Neruda remains a controversial figure for Chileans, and especially for [Chilean feminists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism_in_Chile "Feminism in Chile").

## Legacy

Neruda owned three houses in Chile; today, they are all open to the public as museums: [La Chascona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Chascona "La Chascona") in Santiago, La Sebastiana in [Valparaíso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valparaíso "Valparaíso"), and [Casa de Isla Negra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_de_Isla_Negra "Casa de Isla Negra") in [Isla Negra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_Negra "Isla Negra"), where he and Matilde Urrutia are buried. A bust of Neruda stands on the grounds of the [Organization of American States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_American_States "Organization of American States") building in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.")

### In popular culture

#### Music

In 1998, Ortega expanded the piece into an opera, leaving Neruda's text intact.

#### Literature

- The character of The Poet in [Isabel Allende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabel_Allende "Isabel Allende")'s debut novel *[The House of the Spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_House_of_the_Spirits "The House of the Spirits")* (1982) is an allusion to Neruda.
- Neruda's 1952 stay in a villa on the island of [Capri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capri "Capri") was fictionalized in Chilean author [Antonio Skármeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Skármeta "Antonio Skármeta")'s 1985 novel *[Ardiente paciencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardiente_paciencia "Ardiente paciencia")*. The novel in turn served as the basis for the 1994 film *[Il Postino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Postino:_The_Postman "Il Postino: The Postman")* as well as the 2010 [opera of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Postino_(opera) "Il Postino (opera)") by [Daniel Catán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Catán "Daniel Catán").
- Neruda is included as a character in Chilean author [Roberto Bolaño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Bolaño "Roberto Bolaño")'s 2000 novella *[Nocturno de Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturno_de_Chile "Nocturno de Chile")*.
- In the 2007 novel *[The Reluctant Fundamentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reluctant_Fundamentalist "The Reluctant Fundamentalist")* by Pakistani author [Mohsin Hamid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohsin_Hamid "Mohsin Hamid"), a key time in the political radicalization of the protagonist – a young Pakistani intellectual – is his short stay in Chile, in the course of which he visits the preserved home of Pablo Neruda.
- In 2008, the writer [Roberto Ampuero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Ampuero "Roberto Ampuero") published a novel, *El caso Neruda*, about his private eye Cayetano Brulé where Pablo Neruda is one of the protagonists.
- *The Dreamer* (2010) is a children's fictional biography of Neruda, written by [Pam Muñoz Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pam_Muñoz_Ryan "Pam Muñoz Ryan") and illustrated by [Peter Sís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sís "Peter Sís"). The text and illustrations are printed in Neruda's signature green ink.
- Isabel Allende's 2019 novel *[A Long Petal of the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Long_Petal_of_the_Sea "A Long Petal of the Sea")* has numerous Chilean historical key figures in its narrative. Allende writes about the life of Neruda and his involvement in the transportation of numerous fugitives from the Franco regime to Chile.
- Xia Xia's 夏夏 2023 book of poems for children *How Many Questions Will the Cat Have?* (《一隻貓會有多少問題？》, Ryefield Press, Taiwan, ISBN 9786267281178) was inspired by Neruda's *Book of Questions* (in the Chinese translation by Chen Li) - she wrote new poems in Chinese following the same titles as Neruda's poems.

#### Film & Television

In [The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons") episode [Bart Sells His Soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bart_Sells_His_Soul "Bart Sells His Soul"), Lisa quotes Neruda while discussing the relationship between laughter and the soul. Annoyed, Bart responds "I am *familiar* with the works of Pablo Neruda."

The biographical dramas [*Neruda*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neruda_(film) "Neruda (film)") (2016) and *[Alborada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborada_(film) "Alborada (film)")* (2021) center on Neruda's life.

The Italian film *[Il Postino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Postino:_The_Postman "Il Postino: The Postman")* (1994) is a fictional work about a humble man who is hired to deliver mail by bicycle to just one recipient of the island [Procida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procida "Procida"), Neruda, living there in exile.

The English film *[Truly, Madly, Deeply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truly,_Madly,_Deeply_(film) "Truly, Madly, Deeply (film)")* (1990), written and directed by [Anthony Minghella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Minghella "Anthony Minghella"), uses Neruda's poem "The Dead Woman" as a pivotal device in the plot when Nina ([Juliet Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliet_Stevenson "Juliet Stevenson")) understands she must let go of her dead lover Jamie ([Alan Rickman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Rickman "Alan Rickman")).

Other films referencing Neruda's works include [Mindwalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindwalk "Mindwalk") (1990), *[Patch Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patch_Adams_(film) "Patch Adams (film)")* (1998), *[Chemical Hearts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Hearts "Chemical Hearts")* (2020) and *[Happiness for Beginners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happiness_for_Beginners "Happiness for Beginners")* (2023).
