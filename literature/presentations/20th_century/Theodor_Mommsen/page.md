**Christian Matthias Theodor Mommsen** ([/ˈmɒmzən/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); German: [[ˈteːodoːɐ̯ ˈmɔmzn̩]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Theodor_Mommsen.ogg "File:De-Theodor Mommsen.ogg"); 30 November 1817 – 1 November 1903) was a German [classical scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics "Classics"), [historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian "Historian"), [jurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurist "Jurist"), [journalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist "Journalist"), [politician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politician "Politician") and [archaeologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeologist "Archaeologist"). He is widely regarded as one of the greatest [classicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classicists "Classicists") of the 19th century. He received the [1902 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1902_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1902 Nobel Prize in Literature") for his historical writings, including *[The History of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_History_of_Rome_(Mommsen) "The History of Rome (Mommsen)")*, after having been nominated by 18 members of the [Prussian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences "Prussian Academy of Sciences"). He was also a prominent German politician, as a member of the Prussian and German parliaments. His works on [Roman law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_law "Roman law") and on the [law of obligations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_obligations "Law of obligations") had a significant impact on the [German civil code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bürgerliches_Gesetzbuch "Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch").

## Life

Mommsen was born to German parents in [Garding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garding "Garding") in the [Duchy of Schleswig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Schleswig "Duchy of Schleswig") in 1817, then part of Denmark, and grew up in [Bad Oldesloe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Oldesloe "Bad Oldesloe") in [Holstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holstein "Holstein"), where his father was a [Lutheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheranism "Lutheranism") minister. His parents were originally from [Friesland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friesland_(district) "Friesland (district)"). He had two brothers. He studied mostly at home, though he attended the [Gymnasium Christianeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_Christianeum "Gymnasium Christianeum") in [Altona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altona,_Hamburg "Altona, Hamburg") for four years. He studied [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") and received his diploma in 1837. As he could not afford to study at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen "Göttingen"), he enrolled at the [University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel "University of Kiel").

Mommsen studied [jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisprudence "Jurisprudence") at Kiel from 1838 to 1843, finishing his studies with the degree of Doctor of Roman Law. During this time he was the roommate of [Theodor Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Storm "Theodor Storm"), who was later to become a renowned poet. Together with Mommsen's brother Tycho, the three friends published a collection of poems (*Liederbuch dreier Freunde*). Based on his research into Roman law, Mommsen then devoted himself to Ancient History, which was just being established as a separate subject area at the time; thanks to a royal Danish grant, he was able to visit France and Italy to study preserved classical Roman inscriptions. During the [revolution of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_1848 "Revolution of 1848") he worked as a war correspondent in then-Danish [Rendsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendsburg "Rendsburg"), supporting the German [annexation of Schleswig-Holstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schleswig-Holstein#History "Schleswig-Holstein") and a constitutional reform. Having been forced to leave the country by the Danes, he became a professor of law in the same year at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig "University of Leipzig"). When Mommsen protested against the new constitution of [Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Saxony "Kingdom of Saxony") in 1851, he had to resign. However, the next year he obtained a professorship in Roman law at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich") and then spent a couple of years in exile. In 1854 he became a professor of law at the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau "University of Breslau") where he met [Jakob Bernays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Bernays "Jakob Bernays"). Mommsen became a research professor at the [Berlin Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences "Prussian Academy of Sciences") in 1857. He later helped to create and manage the German Archaeological Institute in Rome.

In 1858 Mommsen was appointed a member of the Academy of Sciences in Berlin, and he also became professor of Roman History at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin "University of Berlin") in 1861, where he held lectures up to 1887. Mommsen received high recognition for his academic achievements: foreign membership of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1859, the Prussian medal [Pour le Mérite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pour_le_Mérite "Pour le Mérite") in 1868, honorary citizenship of Rome, elected a member of the [American Antiquarian Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Antiquarian_Society "American Antiquarian Society") in 1870, and the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature") in 1902 for his main work *Römische Geschichte* (*Roman History*). (He is one of the very few non-fiction writers to receive the Nobel Prize in Literature.)

In 1873, he was elected as a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society").

At 2 a.m. on 7 July 1880 a fire occurred in the upper floor workroom-library of Mommsen's house at Marchstraße 6 in Berlin. After being burned while attempting to remove valuable papers, he was restrained from returning to the blazing house. Several old [manuscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuscript "Manuscript") were burnt to ashes, including *Manuscript 0.4.36*, which was on loan from the library of [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"). There is information that the Manuscript of [Jordanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanes "Jordanes") from [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University "Heidelberg University") library was burnt. Two other important manuscripts, from [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels "Brussels") and [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle,_Saxony-Anhalt "Halle, Saxony-Anhalt"), were also destroyed.

Mommsen had sixteen children with his wife Marie (daughter of the publisher and editor Karl Reimer of Leipzig). Their oldest daughter Maria married [Ulrich von Wilamowitz-Moellendorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich_von_Wilamowitz-Moellendorff "Ulrich von Wilamowitz-Moellendorff"), a Classics scholar. Their grandson [Theodor Ernst Mommsen (1905–1958)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Ernst_Mommsen_(historian) "Theodor Ernst Mommsen (historian)") became a professor of medieval history in the United States. Two of the great-grandsons, [Hans Mommsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Mommsen "Hans Mommsen") and [Wolfgang Mommsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Mommsen "Wolfgang Mommsen"), were German historians.

## Mommsen as editor and organiser

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/Theodor_Mommsen_by_Ludwig_Knaus_%281881%29.jpg/250px-Theodor_Mommsen_by_Ludwig_Knaus_%281881%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Theodor_Mommsen_by_Ludwig_Knaus_(1881).jpg)

Theodor Mommsen in 1881

While he was secretary of the Historical-Philological Class at the [Berlin Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences "Prussian Academy of Sciences") (1874–1895), Mommsen organised countless scientific projects, mostly editions of original sources.

### *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum*

At the beginning of his career, when he published the inscriptions of the [Neapolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples "Naples") Kingdom (1852), Mommsen was planning a collection of all then-known ancient Latin inscriptions. He received additional impetus and training from [Bartolomeo Borghesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolomeo_Borghesi "Bartolomeo Borghesi") of [San Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marino "San Marino"). The complete *[Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Inscriptionum_Latinarum "Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum")* would consist of seventeen volumes, the latest of which was published in 1986. Fifteen of these volumes were published in Mommsen's lifetime, and he prepared five of them himself. The basic principle of the edition (contrary to previous collections) was the method of autopsy, according to which all copies (i.e., modern transcriptions) of inscriptions were to be checked and compared to the original.

### Further editions and research projects

Mommsen published the fundamental collections in Roman law: the *[Corpus Iuris Civilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Iuris_Civilis "Corpus Iuris Civilis")* and the *[Codex Theodosianus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Theodosianus "Codex Theodosianus")*. Furthermore, he played an important role in the publication of the *[Monumenta Germaniae Historica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumenta_Germaniae_Historica "Monumenta Germaniae Historica")*, the edition of the texts of the [Church Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Fathers "Church Fathers"), the [limes romanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limes_(Roman_Empire) "Limes (Roman Empire)") (Roman frontiers) research and countless other projects.

Mommsen was a delegate to the [Prussian House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_House_of_Representatives "Prussian House of Representatives") from 1863 to 1866 and again from 1873 to 1879, and delegate to the [Reichstag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_(German_Empire) "Reichstag (German Empire)") from 1881 to 1884, at first for the liberal [German Progress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Progress_Party "German Progress Party") (*Deutsche Fortschrittspartei*), later for the [National Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberal_Party_(Germany) "National Liberal Party (Germany)"), and finally for the [Secessionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Union_(Germany) "Liberal Union (Germany)"). He was concerned with questions about academic and educational policies and held national positions. Although he had supported [German Unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Unification "German Unification"), he was disappointed with the politics of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire") and he was quite pessimistic about its future. Mommsen strongly disagreed with [Otto von Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Bismarck "Otto von Bismarck") about social policies in 1881, advising collaboration between Liberals and [Social Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Germany "Social Democratic Party of Germany") and using such strong language that he narrowly avoided prosecution.

As a [Liberal nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_and_nationalism "Liberalism and nationalism") Mommsen favored [assimilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_assimilation "Cultural assimilation") of ethnic minorities into German society, not exclusion. In 1879, his colleague [Heinrich von Treitschke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_von_Treitschke "Heinrich von Treitschke") began a political campaign against Jews (the so-called *Berliner Antisemitismusstreit*). Mommsen strongly opposed [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism") and wrote a pamphlet in which he denounced von Treitschke's views. Mommsen viewed a solution to antisemitism in voluntary cultural assimilation, suggesting that the Jews could follow the example of the people of [Schleswig-Holstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schleswig-Holstein "Schleswig-Holstein"), [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover "Hanover") and other German states, which gave up some of their special customs when integrating into [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia "Prussia"). In 1890 Mommsen became one of the co-founders of the Association for Combating Antisemitism (Abwehrverein), a humanist association created to publicly counter the growing antisemitism in Germany.

Mommsen was a vehement spokesman for [German nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nationalism "German nationalism"), maintaining a militant attitude towards the [Slavic nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_peoples "Slavic peoples"), to the point of advocating the use of violence against them. In an 1897 letter to the *[Neue Freie Presse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neue_Freie_Presse "Neue Freie Presse")* of [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna "Vienna"), Mommsen called [Czechs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs "Czechs") "apostles of barbarism" and wrote that "the Czech skull is impervious to reason, but it is susceptible to blows".

## Influence of Mommsen

Fellow Nobel Laureate (1925) [Bernard Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bernard_Shaw "George Bernard Shaw") cited Mommsen's interpretation of the last First Consul of the Republic, Julius Caesar, as one of the inspirations for his 1898 (1905 on [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre")) play, *[Caesar and Cleopatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesar_and_Cleopatra_(play) "Caesar and Cleopatra (play)")*.

Noted naval historian and theorist [Alfred Thayer Mahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Thayer_Mahan "Alfred Thayer Mahan") formulated the thesis for his magnum opus, *[The Influence of Sea Power upon History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Influence_of_Sea_Power_upon_History "The Influence of Sea Power upon History")*, while reading Mommsen's *History of Rome*.

The playwright [Heiner Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiner_Müller "Heiner Müller") wrote a 'performance text' entitled *Mommsens Block* (1993), inspired by the publication of Mommsen's fragmentary notes on the later Roman empire and by the [East German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_German "East German") government's decision to replace a statue of [Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx "Karl Marx") outside the [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin") with one of Mommsen.

There is a [Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)") (academic high school) named for Mommsen in his hometown of [Bad Oldesloe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Oldesloe "Bad Oldesloe"), Schleswig-Holstein, Germany. His birthplace [Garding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garding "Garding") in the west of Schleswig styles itself "Mommsen-Stadt Garding".

### Mark Twain

"One of the highpoints of [Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain "Mark Twain")'s European tour of 1892 was a large formal banquet at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin "University of Berlin")... . Mark Twain was an honoured guest, seated at the head table with some twenty 'particularly eminent professors'; and it was from this vantage point that he witnessed the following incident..." In Twain's own words:

> When apparently the last eminent guest had long ago taken his place, again those three bugle-blasts rang out, and once more the swords leaped from their scabbards. Who might this late comer be? Nobody was interested to inquire. Still, indolent eyes were turned toward the distant entrance, and we saw the silken gleam and the lifted sword of a guard of honor plowing through the remote crowds. Then we saw that end of the house rising to its feet; saw it rise abreast the advancing guard all along like a wave. This supreme honor had been offered to no one before. There was an excited whisper at our table—'MOMMSEN!'—and the whole house rose. Rose and shouted and stamped and clapped and banged the beer mugs. Just simply a storm!

> Then the little man with his long hair and [Emersonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Waldo_Emerson "Ralph Waldo Emerson") face edged his way past us and took his seat. I could have touched him with my hand—Mommsen!—think of it! ... I would have walked a great many miles to get a sight of him, and here he was, without trouble or tramp or cost of any kind. Here he was clothed in a titanic deceptive modesty which made him look like other men.

## Bibliography

Mommsen published over 1,500 works, and effectively established a new framework for the systematic study of [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome") history. He pioneered [epigraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigraphy "Epigraphy"), the study of [inscriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscription "Inscription") in material artefacts. Although the unfinished *[History of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Rome_(Mommsen) "History of Rome (Mommsen)")*, written early in his career, has long been widely considered as his main work, the work most relevant today is, perhaps, the *[Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Inscriptionum_Latinarum "Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum")*, a collection of Roman inscriptions he contributed to the [Berlin Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences "Prussian Academy of Sciences").

- *The History of Rome*, three volumes (1854–1856), English translation in four volumes by [William Purdie Dickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Purdie_Dickson "William Purdie Dickson") published from 1862 to 1866. Dickson's translation went through many editions on both sides of the Atlantic and there are several examples online, including this set from 1868 at the [Internet Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Archive "Internet Archive"): [Volume 1](https://archive.org/details/historyofrome0001theo), [Volume 2](https://archive.org/details/historyofrome0002theo_m7e1), [Volume 3](https://archive.org/details/historyofrome0003unse_u4s2), [Volume 4](https://archive.org/details/historyofromebyt0004will).
- *Roman Chronology to the Time of Caesar* (1858) written with his brother [August Mommsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Mommsen "August Mommsen").
- *[Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Inscriptionum_Latinarum "Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum")*, lead editor and editor (1861, et seq.)
- *Res gestae divi Augusti, ex monumentis Ancyrano et Apolloniensi*, editor (1865)
- *[Digesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digest_(Roman_law) "Digest (Roman law)")* (of [Justinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I "Justinian I")), editor (1866–1870, two volumes)
- *Roman Constitutional Law* (1871–1888). This systematic treatment of [Roman constitutional law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Law "Roman Law") in three volumes has been of importance for research on ancient history.
- *Iordanis Romana et Getica* (1882) was Mommsen's critical edition of [Jordanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanes "Jordanes")' *The Origin and Deeds of the Goths*, commonly known as *[Getica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getica "Getica")*.
- *The Provinces of the Roman Empire from Caesar to Diocletian* (1885), published as Volume 5 of his *[History of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Rome_(Mommsen) "History of Rome (Mommsen)")*, is a description of the regions under Roman control during the early [imperial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire "Roman Empire"). The English translation was again entrusted to William Purdie Dickson (two volumes, 1886): [Volume 1](https://archive.org/details/gri_33125009721354), [Volume 2](https://archive.org/details/gri_33125009721214). Mommsen never got around to writing the missing fourth volume of his history, intended to be a general account of imperial Rome. A makeshift reconstructed from lecture notes taken by his students has been published in German as *Römische Kaisergeschichte* (1992) and in English as *A History of Rome under the Emperors* (1996).
- *Roman Criminal Law* (1899)
- *[Codex Theodosianus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Theodosianus "Codex Theodosianus")*, editor (1905, posthumous)
