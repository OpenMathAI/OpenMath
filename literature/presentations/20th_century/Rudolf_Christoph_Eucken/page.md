**Rudolf Christoph Eucken** ([/ˈɔɪkən/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); German: [[ˈʁuːdɔlf ˈɔʏkn̩]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Rudolf_Eucken.ogg "File:De-Rudolf Eucken.ogg"); 5 January 1846 –  15 September 1926) was a German [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher "Philosopher"). He received the [1908 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1908 Nobel Prize in Literature") "in recognition of his earnest search for truth, his penetrating power of thought, his wide range of vision, and the warmth and strength in presentation with which in his numerous works he has vindicated and developed an idealistic philosophy of life", after he had been nominated by a member of the [Swedish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy "Swedish Academy").

## Early life

Eucken was born on 5 January 1846 in [Aurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurich "Aurich"), then in the [Kingdom of Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hanover "Kingdom of Hanover") (now [Lower Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Saxony "Lower Saxony")). His father, Ammo Becker Eucken died when he was a child, and he was brought up by his mother, Ida Maria (née Gittermann). He was educated at Aurich, where one of his teachers was the [classical philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_philologist "Classical philologist") and philosopher Ludwig Wilhelm Maximilian Reuter. He studied at [Göttingen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_University "Göttingen University") (1863–1866), where [Hermann Lotze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Lotze "Hermann Lotze") was one of his teachers, and [Berlin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin"). In the latter place, [Friedrich Adolf Trendelenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Adolf_Trendelenburg "Friedrich Adolf Trendelenburg") was a professor whose ethical tendencies and historical treatment of philosophy greatly attracted him.

## Career

Eucken received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in classical [philology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology "Philology") and [ancient history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history "Ancient history") from [Göttingen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_University "Göttingen University") in 1866 with a dissertation titled *De Aristotelis dicendi ratione*. However, the inclination of his mind was definitely towards the philosophical side of [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology "Theology"). In 1871, after five years working as a school teacher at [Husum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husum "Husum"), Berlin und [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt "Frankfurt"), he was appointed Professor of [Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy") at the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel "University of Basel"), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland"), succeeding another of his former teachers at Göttingen, [Gustav Teichmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Teichmüller "Gustav Teichmüller"), and beating Friedrich Nietzsche in competition for the position. He stayed there until 1874 when he took up a similar position at the [University of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena "University of Jena"). He stayed there until he retired in 1920. In 1912–13, Eucken spent half of the year as an exchange professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), and in 1913 he served as a Deem lecturer at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University"). During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), Eucken, like many of his academic colleagues, took a strong line in favor of the causes with which his country had associated itself. The Great War destroyed what had been an international community of scholars.

## Ethical activism

Eucken's philosophical work is partly historical and partly [constructive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_constructivism "Moral constructivism"), the former side being predominant in his earlier, the latter in his later works. Their most striking feature is the close organic relationship between the two parts. The aim of the historical works is to show the necessary connection between philosophical concepts and the age to which they belong; the same idea is at the root of his constructive speculation. All philosophy is [philosophy of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_life "Philosophy of life"), the development of a new culture, not mere [intellectualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectualism "Intellectualism"), but the application of a vital religious inspiration to the practical problems of society. This practical idealism Eucken described by the term "**ethical activism**" ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language "German language"): *Aktivismus*). In accordance with this principle, Eucken gave considerable attention to social and educational problems.

He maintained that humans have [souls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul "Soul"), and that they are therefore at the junction between nature and spirit. He believed that people should overcome their non-spiritual nature by continuous efforts to achieve a [spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirituality "Spirituality") life, another aspect of his [ethical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethical "Ethical") [activism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activism "Activism") and [meaning of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meaning_of_life "Meaning of life").

## Later life and death

Rudolf Eucken married Irene Passow in 1882 and had a daughter and two sons. His son [Walter Eucken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Eucken "Walter Eucken") became a famous founder of [ordoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordoliberalism "Ordoliberalism") thought in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberal_economics "Neoliberal economics"). His son [Arnold Eucken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Eucken "Arnold Eucken") was a chemist and physicist.

Rudolf Eucken died on 15 September 1926 in [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jena "Jena") at the age of 80.

## Major works

He was a prolific writer; his best-known works are:

- [*Die Lebensanschauungen der großen Denker*](https://archive.org/details/dielebensanschau00euckuoft) (1890; 7th ed., 1907; 1918; Eng. trans., W. Hough and Boyce Gibson, *The Problem of Human Life*, 1909) (The Problem of Human Life as Viewed by the Great Thinkers)
- *Der Kampf um einen geistigen Lebensinhalt* (1896) (The Struggle for a Spiritual Content of Life)
- [*Der Wahrheitsgehalt der Religion*](https://archive.org/details/derwahrheitsgeh00euckgoog) (1901) (The Truth of Religion)
- [*Grundlinien einer neuen Lebensanschauung*](https://archive.org/details/grundlinieneine00euckgoog) (1907) (Life's Basis and Life's Ideal: The Fundamentals of a New Philosophy of Life)
- [*Der Sinn und Wert des Lebens*](https://archive.org/stream/dersinnundwertde00euck#page/n3/mode/2up) (1908) (The Meaning and Value of Life)
- *Geistige Strömungen der Gegenwart* (1908; first appeared in 1878 as [*Die Grundbegriffe der Gegenwart*](https://archive.org/details/diegrundbegriff01euckgoog); Eng. trans. by M. Stuart Phelps, New York, 1880) (Main Currents of Modern Thought)
- *Können wir noch Christen sein?* (1911) ([Can We Still Be Christians?](https://archive.org/details/canwestillbechri00euck), 1914)
- *Present Day Ethics in their Relation to the Spiritual Life* (1913) (Deem Lectures given at New York University)
- *Der Sozialismus und seine Lebensgestaltung* (1920) ([Socialism: an Analysis](https://archive.org/details/socialismanalysi00euck) (1922))

Other notable works are:

- *Die Methode der aristotelischen Forschung* (1872) (The Aristotelian Method of Research)
- *[Geschichte der philosophische Terminologie](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb11172140?page=5)* (1879) (History of Philosophical Terminology)
- *Prolegomena zu Forschungen über die Einheit des Geisteslebens* (1885) (Prolegomena to Research on the Unity of the Spiritual Life)
- *Beiträge zur Geschichte der neueren Philosophie* (1886, 1905) (Contributions to the History of the Newer Philosophies)
- *Die Einheit des Geisteslebens* (1888) (The Unity of the Spiritual Life)
- [*Thomas von Aquino und Kant*](https://archive.org/details/thomasvonaquino00euck) (1901) (Thomas Aquinas and Kant)
- *Gesammelte Aufsätze zu Philosophische und Lebensanschauung* (1903) (Collected Essays on Views of Philosophy and Life)
- *Philosophie der Geschichte* (1907) (Philosophy of History)
- *Einführung in die Philosophie der Geisteslebens* (1908; Eng. trans., The Life of the Spirit, F. L. Pogson, 1909, Crown Theological Library) (Introduction to the Philosophy of the Life of the Spirit)
- [*Hauptprobleme der Religionsphilosophie der Gegenwart*](https://archive.org/details/hauptproblemeder00euckuoft) (1907) (Main Problems of the Current Philosophy of Religion)

Other English translations of his work include:

- *Liberty in Teaching in the German Universities* (1897)
- *Are the Germans still a Nation of Thinkers?* (1898)
- *Progress of Philosophy in the 19th Century* (1899)
- *The Finnish Question* (1899)
- *The Present Status of Religion in Germany* (1901)
- [*The Problem of Human Life as Viewed by the Great Thinkers from Plato to the Present Time*](https://archive.org/stream/problemhumanlif01euckgoog#page/n8/mode/2up), Charles Scribner's Sons, 1909.
- [*Back to Religion*](https://archive.org/details/backtoreligion00euckrich), 1912.
- [*Main Currents of Modern Thought: A Study of the Spiritual and Intellectual Movements of the Present Day*](https://archive.org/stream/maincurrentsmode00euckuoft#page/n5/mode/2up), T. Fisher Unwin, 1912.
- [*The Meaning and Value of Life*](https://archive.org/stream/themeaningandval00euckuoft#page/n5/mode/2up), A. and C. Black, 1913.
- [*Can we Still be Christians?*](https://archive.org/stream/canwestillbechri00euckrich#page/n5/mode/2up), The Macmillan Company, 1914.
- [*Collected Essays*](https://archive.org/stream/collectedessayso00euckuoft#page/n7/mode/2up), edited and translated by [Meyrick Booth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meyrick_Booth "Meyrick Booth"), [T. Fisher Unwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._Fisher_Unwin "T. Fisher Unwin"), 1914.
- [*Knowledge and Life*](https://archive.org/details/knowledgelife00euck) (translation), G.P. Putnam's Sons, 1914.

He delivered lectures in England in 1911 and spent six months lecturing at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") and elsewhere in the United States in 1912–1913.
