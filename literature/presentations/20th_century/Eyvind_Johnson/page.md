**Eyvind Johnson** (29 July 1900 – 25 August 1976) was a Swedish novelist and short story writer. Regarded as the most groundbreaking novelist in modern Swedish literature he became a member of the [Swedish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy "Swedish Academy") in 1957 and shared the [1974 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1974 Nobel Prize in Literature") with [Harry Martinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Martinson "Harry Martinson") with the citation: *for a narrative art, far-seeing in lands and ages, in the service of freedom*.

## Biography

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Eyvind_Johnson-Bagarstugan.jpg/250px-Eyvind_Johnson-Bagarstugan.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Eyvind_Johnson-Bagarstugan.jpg)

The house in Björkelund where Eyvind Johnson was born in 1900.

Johnson was born Olof Edvin Verner Jonsson 29 July 1900 in a village near the town of [Boden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boden,_Sweden "Boden, Sweden") in [Norrbotten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norrbotten_County "Norrbotten County"). The small house where he was born is preserved and marked with a commemorative plaque.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bb/Eyvindj.gif?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Eyvindj.gif)

Johnson left school at the age of thirteen and then held various jobs such as [log driving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log_driving "Log driving") and working at a [saw mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saw_mill "Saw mill") and as a ticket-seller and projectionist in a [cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_theatre "Movie theatre"). In 1919 he left his hometown and moved to [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm") where he began to publish articles in [anarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist "Anarchist") magazines like *[Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_(magazine) "Brand (magazine)")*. In Stockholm he became friends with other young [proletarian writers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletarian_writer "Proletarian writer") and started the magazine *Vår nutid*. He travelled in Germany in the 1920s and lived in [Saint-Leu-la-Foret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Leu-la-Foret "Saint-Leu-la-Foret"), near [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), France, between 1927 and 1930 with his wife [Aase Christoffersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aase_Christoffersen?action=edit&redlink=1 "Aase Christoffersen") (1900–1938). At this point he had published his first books. The first *De fyra främlingarna*, a collection of short stories, was published in 1924. Influenced by writers such as [Marcel Proust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Proust "Marcel Proust"), [André Gide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Gide "André Gide") and [James Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Joyce "James Joyce"), Johnson gradually took distance from the traditional novel and became the most important representative of [modernist literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist_literature "Modernist literature") in Sweden. Johnson's early novels were not widely read, but his 1929 novel *Kommentar till ett stjärnfall* ("Comment on a falling star"), an attack on [capitalist society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalist_society "Capitalist society"), was a critical success.

Johnson's first major success as a writer was four [autobiographical novels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autobiographical_novel "Autobiographical novel") published between 1934 and 1937, published together as *[Romanen om Olof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanen_om_Olof?action=edit&redlink=1 "Romanen om Olof")* ("The Novel about Olof"), about a young man growing up in the northern parts of Sweden. In the novels Johnson blended realism with fairy tales and typical modernist features such as [inner monologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_monologue "Inner monologue") and changing [point of view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_of_view_(literature) "Point of view (literature)"). *Romanen om Olof* became a [classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_book "Classic book") in Swedish literature and was later filmed as *[Here Is Your Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_Is_Your_Life "Here Is Your Life")*.

Increasingly upset by the rising totalitarianism in the 1930s, Johnson was strongly against [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism "Fascism") and [nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism"). During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") he was editor of the magazine *[Håndslag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Håndslag "Håndslag")* and published *[Krilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krilon "Krilon")*, a trilogy of novels that in the form of an [allegory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegory "Allegory") deals with the events during the war. In the novels Johnson condemns Nazi oppression and criticises the controversial [Swedish neutrality policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_during_World_War_II "Sweden during World War II") during the war. *Krilon* is considered to be one of Johnson's best works. In the same period he was a member of the nationalist association [Samfundet Nordens Frihet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samfundet_Nordens_Frihet "Samfundet Nordens Frihet") and among the contributors of its magazine, *[Nordens Frihet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordens_Frihet "Nordens Frihet")*.

In 1946 he published one of his most famous novels, *[Return to Ithaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_to_Ithaca_(novel) "Return to Ithaca (novel)")* (*Strändernas svall*), based on the story of [Odysseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odysseus "Odysseus") as he returns to Ithaca after the Trojan war. Johnson married translator [Cilla Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilla_Johnson?action=edit&redlink=1 "Cilla Johnson") in 1940. He lived with his family in Switzerland 1947–1949 and then a year in England. Travels to Italy and France inspired him to write several well-received [historical novels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_novels "Historical novels"). Among the best known are *[Dreams of Roses and Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_of_Roses_and_Fire "Dreams of Roses and Fire")* (*Drömmar om rosor och eld*, 1949) set in [Cardinal Richelieus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Richelieu "Cardinal Richelieu") 17th century France, and *Molnen över Metapontion* ("The Clouds above Metapontion", 1957) that typically for his later novels switches back and forth between different time levels.

Johnson's most noted works internationally include *Return to Ithaca* and *[The Days of His Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Days_of_His_Grace "The Days of His Grace")* (*Hans nådes tid*, 1960) which have been translated to many languages. For the latter novel Johnson was awarded the [Nordic Council Literature Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Council_Literature_Prize "Nordic Council Literature Prize") in 1962.

### Member of the Swedish Academy and Nobel Prize in Literature

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Authors-from-across-the-country-arrive-at-Writers-meeting-142347119180.jpg/330px-Authors-from-across-the-country-arrive-at-Writers-meeting-142347119180.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Authors-from-across-the-country-arrive-at-Writers-meeting-142347119180.jpg)

Eyvind Johnson (left) at a writers meeting in [Karlstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlstad "Karlstad") in 1958, with authors [Harry Martinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Martinson "Harry Martinson") and [Gabriel Jönsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Jönsson "Gabriel Jönsson") and Jönsson's wife.

In 1957 Johnson was elected a member of the [Swedish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy "Swedish Academy"). He was a member of the [Nobel Committee for Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Committee_for_Literature "Nobel Committee for Literature") between 1959 and 1972. Johnson nominated several authors that were subsequently awarded the prize, including the 1963 Nobel laureate [Giorgos Seferis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgos_Seferis "Giorgos Seferis") twice, in 1962 and the year Seferis won.

In 1974, Eyvind Johnson was awarded the [1974 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1974 Nobel Prize in Literature") jointly with [Harry Martinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Martinson "Harry Martinson"). Johnson was awarded "for a narrative art, farseeing in lands and ages, in the service of freedom"
