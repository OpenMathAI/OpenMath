**Peter Handke** (German: [[ˈpeːtɐ ˈhantkə]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German"); born 6 December 1942) is an Austrian novelist, playwright, translator, poet, film director, and screenwriter. He was awarded the [2019 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "2019 Nobel Prize in Literature") "for an influential work that with linguistic ingenuity has explored the periphery and the specificity of human experience." Handke is considered to be one of the most influential and original German-language writers in the second half of the 20th century.

In the late 1960s, he earned his reputation as a member of the [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde "Avant-garde") with such plays as *[Offending the Audience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offending_the_Audience "Offending the Audience")* (1966) in which actors analyze the nature of theatre and alternately insult the audience and praise its "performance", and *[Kaspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaspar_(play) "Kaspar (play)")* (1967). His novels, mostly ultra objective, [deadpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadpan "Deadpan") accounts of characters in extreme states of mind, include *[The Goalie's Anxiety at the Penalty Kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Goalie's_Anxiety_at_the_Penalty_Kick "The Goalie's Anxiety at the Penalty Kick")* (1970) and *[The Left-Handed Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Left-Handed_Woman_(novel)?action=edit&redlink=1 "The Left-Handed Woman (novel)")* (1976). Prompted by his mother's suicide in 1971, he reflected her life in the novella *[A Sorrow Beyond Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Sorrow_Beyond_Dreams "A Sorrow Beyond Dreams")* (1972).

A dominant theme of his works is the deadening effects and underlying irrationality of ordinary language, everyday reality, and rational order. Handke was a member of the *[Grazer Gruppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazer_Gruppe "Grazer Gruppe")* (an association of authors) and the [Grazer Autorenversammlung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazer_Autorenversammlung "Grazer Autorenversammlung"), and co-founded the Verlag der Autoren publishing house in Frankfurt. He collaborated with director [Wim Wenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wim_Wenders "Wim Wenders"), and wrote such screenplays as *[The Wrong Move](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wrong_Move "The Wrong Move")* and *[Wings of Desire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wings_of_Desire "Wings of Desire")*.

In 1973, he won the [Georg Büchner Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Büchner_Prize "Georg Büchner Prize"), the most important literary prize for German-language literature. In 1999, as a protest against the [NATO bombing of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_bombing_of_Yugoslavia "NATO bombing of Yugoslavia"), Handke returned the prize money to the [German Academy for Language and Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Akademie_für_Sprache_und_Dichtung "Deutsche Akademie für Sprache und Dichtung"). Handke has drawn significant controversy for his public support of [Serbian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_nationalism "Serbian nationalism") in the wake of the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars "Yugoslav Wars").

## Life

### Early life and family

Handke was born in [Griffen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffen,_Austria "Griffen, Austria"), in [Carinthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthia "Carinthia"), Austria (then an annexed part of Germany). His father, Erich Schönemann, was a bank clerk and German soldier whom Handke did not meet until adulthood. His mother Maria, a [Carinthian Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthian_Slovenes "Carinthian Slovenes"), married Bruno Handke, a tram conductor and [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht "Wehrmacht") soldier from Berlin, before Peter was born. The family lived in the Soviet-occupied [Pankow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pankow_(locality) "Pankow (locality)") district of Berlin from 1944 to 1948, where Maria Handke had two more children: Peter's half-sister and half-brother. Then the family moved to his mother's home town of Griffen. Peter experienced his stepfather as more and more violent due to [alcoholism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholism "Alcoholism").

In 1954, Handke was sent to the [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") *Marianum* boys' [boarding school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boarding_school "Boarding school") at [Tanzenberg Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzenberg_Castle "Tanzenberg Castle") in [Sankt Veit an der Glan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankt_Veit_an_der_Glan "Sankt Veit an der Glan"). There, he published his first writing in the school newspaper, *Fackel*. In 1959, he moved to [Klagenfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klagenfurt "Klagenfurt"), where he went to high school, and commenced law studies at the [University of Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Graz "University of Graz") in 1961.

Handke's mother took her own life in 1971, reflected in his novel *[Wunschloses Unglück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Sorrow_Beyond_Dreams "A Sorrow Beyond Dreams")* (*A Sorrow Beyond Dreams*).

After leaving Graz, Handke lived in [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Düsseldorf "Düsseldorf"), Berlin, [Kronberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronberg_im_Taunus "Kronberg im Taunus"), Paris, the U.S. (1978–1979) and [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg "Salzburg") (1979–1988). Since 1990, he has resided in [Chaville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaville "Chaville") near Paris. He is the subject of the documentary film *[Peter Handke: In the Woods, Might Be Late](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Handke:_In_the_Woods,_Might_Be_Late "Peter Handke: In the Woods, Might Be Late")* (2016), directed by [Corinna Belz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinna_Belz "Corinna Belz"). Since 2012, Handke has been a member of the [Serbian Academy of Sciences and Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Arts "Serbian Academy of Sciences and Arts"). He is a member of the Serbian Orthodox Church.

In November 2019, Austrian authorities investigated reports that Handke had obtained a Yugoslav passport and possibly Yugoslav nationality during the 1990s, which could have triggered automatic loss of his [Austrian citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_citizenship "Austrian citizenship"). The investigation was closed in 2021.

### Career

While studying, Handke established himself as a writer, linking up with the *[Grazer Gruppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazer_Gruppe "Grazer Gruppe")* (the [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz "Graz") Authors' Assembly), an association of young writers. The group published a magazine on literature, *[manuskripte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuskripte?action=edit&redlink=1 "Manuskripte")*, which published Handke's early works. Group members included [Wolfgang Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Bauer_(Austrian_writer) "Wolfgang Bauer (Austrian writer)") and [Barbara Frischmuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Frischmuth "Barbara Frischmuth").

Handke abandoned his studies in 1965, after the German publishing house [Suhrkamp Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhrkamp_Verlag "Suhrkamp Verlag") accepted his novel *[Die Hornissen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Hornissen?action=edit&redlink=1 "Die Hornissen") (The Hornets)* for publication. He gained international attention after an appearance at a meeting of [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde "Avant-garde") artists belonging to the [Gruppe 47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruppe_47 "Gruppe 47") in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey "Princeton, New Jersey"), [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey "New Jersey"), in 1966. The same year, his play *[Publikumsbeschimpfung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offending_the_Audience "Offending the Audience")* (*Offending the Audience*) premiered at the [Theater am Turm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_am_Turm "Theater am Turm") in Frankfurt, directed by [Claus Peymann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claus_Peymann "Claus Peymann"). Handke became one of the co-founders of the publishing house [Verlag der Autoren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verlag_der_Autoren?action=edit&redlink=1 "Verlag der Autoren") in 1969 with a new commercial concept, as it belonged to the authors. He co-founded the [Grazer Autorenversammlung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazer_Autorenversammlung "Grazer Autorenversammlung") in 1973 and was a member until 1977.

Handke's first play, *[Publikumsbeschimpfung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offending_the_Audience "Offending the Audience")* (*Offending the Audience*), which premiered in Frankfurt in 1966 and made him well known, was the first of several experimental plays without a conventional plot. In his second play, *[Kaspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaspar_(play) "Kaspar (play)")*, he treated the story of [Kaspar Hauser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaspar_Hauser "Kaspar Hauser") as "an allegory of conformist social pressures".

Handke in 1983

Handke collaborated with director [Wim Wenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wim_Wenders "Wim Wenders") on [a film version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Goalkeeper's_Fear_of_the_Penalty "The Goalkeeper's Fear of the Penalty") of *[Die Angst des Tormanns beim Elfmeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Goalie's_Anxiety_at_the_Penalty_Kick "The Goalie's Anxiety at the Penalty Kick")*, wrote the script for *[Falsche Bewegung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wrong_Move "The Wrong Move")* (*The Wrong Move*) and co-wrote the screenplay for *[Der Himmel über Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wings_of_Desire "Wings of Desire")* (*Wings of Desire*) including the poem at its opening and *[Les Beaux Jours d'Aranjuez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beautiful_Days_of_Aranjuez "The Beautiful Days of Aranjuez")* (*The Beautiful Days of Aranjuez*). He also directed films, including adaptations from his novels *[The Left-Handed Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Left-Handed_Woman "The Left-Handed Woman")* after *Die linkshändige Frau*, and *[The Absence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Absence_(1992_film) "The Absence (1992 film)")* after *Die Abwesenheit*. *The Left-Handed Woman*, was released in 1978 and was nominated for the [Golden Palm Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palme_d'Or "Palme d'Or") at the [Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival "Cannes Film Festival") in 1978 and won the Gold Award for German Arthouse Cinema in 1980. *[Leonard Maltin's Movie Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Maltin's_Movie_Guide "Leonard Maltin's Movie Guide")'s* description of the film is that a woman demands that her husband leave and he complies. "Time passes... and the audience falls asleep." Handke also won the 1975 German Film Award in Gold for his screenplay for *[Falsche Bewegung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wrong_Move "The Wrong Move")* (*The Wrong Move*). Since 1975, Handke has been a jury member of the European literary award [Petrarca-Preis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarca-Preis "Petrarca-Preis").

In 2019, Handke was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature") "for an influential work that with linguistic ingenuity has explored the periphery and the specificity of human experience."

## Literary reception

In 1977, reviewing *[A Moment of True Feeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Moment_of_True_Feeling "A Moment of True Feeling")*, [Stanley Kauffmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Kauffmann "Stanley Kauffmann") wrote that Handke "is the most important new writer on the international scene since [Samuel Beckett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Beckett "Samuel Beckett")." [John Updike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Updike "John Updike") reviewed the same novel in [The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker") and was equally impressed, noting that "there is no denying his [Handke's] willful intensity and knifelike clarity of evocation. He writes from an area beyond psychology, where feelings acquire the adamancy of randomly encountered, geologically analyzed pebbles." The *[Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Allgemeine_Zeitung "Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung")* described him as "the darling of the [West German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany "West Germany") critics." [Hugo Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Hamilton_(writer) "Hugo Hamilton (writer)") stated that, since his debut, Handke "has tested, inspired and shocked audiences." [Joshua Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Cohen_(writer) "Joshua Cohen (writer)") noted that Handke "commands one of the great German-language [prose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prose_poetry "Prose poetry") styles of the [post-war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-war "Post-war") period, a riverine rhetoric deep and swift and contrary of current," while [Gabriel Josipovici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Josipovici "Gabriel Josipovici") described him, "despite reservations about some of his recent work," as one of the most significant [German-language writers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_literature "German literature") of the post-war era. [W. G. Sebald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._G._Sebald "W. G. Sebald") was inspired by Handke's intricate prose. In an essay on *Repetition*, he wrote about "a great and, as I have since learned, lasting impression" the book made on him. "I don’t know," he lauded, "if the forced relation between hard drudgery and airy magic, particularly significant for the literary art, has ever been more beautifully documented than in the pages of *Repetition.*" [Karl Ove Knausgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Ove_Knausgård "Karl Ove Knausgård") described *A Sorrow Beyond Dreams* as one of the "most important books written in German in our time." The book and its author were also praised in Knausgård's *[My Struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Struggle_(Knausgård_novels) "My Struggle (Knausgård novels)")*.

## Controversies

In 1996, Handke's [travelogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_literature "Travel literature") *Eine winterliche Reise zu den Flüssen Donau, Save, Morawa und Drina oder Gerechtigkeit für Serbien* (published in English as *A Journey to the Rivers: Justice for Serbia*) created controversy, as Handke portrayed [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia "Serbia") as being among the victims of the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars "Yugoslav Wars"). In the same essay, Handke also criticised Western media for misrepresenting the causes and consequences of the war.



Sebastian Hammelehle wrote that Handke's view of the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars "Yugoslav Wars"), which has provoked numerous controversies, was probably romanticized, but that it represented the view of a writer, not a [war reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_correspondent "War correspondent"). The American translator Scott Abbott, who travelled with Handke through [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia "Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia") after which numerous essays were published, stated that Handke considered Yugoslavia as the "incredible, rich [multicultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism "Multiculturalism") state that lacked the kind of nationalisms that he saw in Germany and Austria". Abbott added that Handke viewed the [disintegration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia "Breakup of Yugoslavia") of country as the disappearance of [utopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopia "Utopia"). Reviewing *[The Moravian Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Moravian_Night "The Moravian Night")*, Joshua Cohen stated that Handke's Yugoslavia was not a country, but a symbol of himself, a symbol of literature or the "European Novel". [Volker Hage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volker_Hage "Volker Hage") wrote that *The Moravian Night* is "extremely cosmopolitan" and connected to the present, while also that the book represents the autobiographical summary of Handke's life as a writer. Tanjil Rashid noted that "Handke’s novels, plays and memoirs demonstrate the evil of banality".

After his play *Voyage by Dugout* was staged in 1999, Handke was condemned by other writers: [Susan Sontag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Sontag "Susan Sontag") proclaimed Handke to be "finished" in New York. [Salman Rushdie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salman_Rushdie "Salman Rushdie") declared him as a candidate for "International Moron of the Year" due to his "idiocies", while [Alain Finkielkraut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Finkielkraut "Alain Finkielkraut") said that he was an "ideological monster", and [Slavoj Žižek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavoj_Žižek "Slavoj Žižek") stated that his "glorification of the Serbs is cynicism". When Handke was awarded the [International Ibsen Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Ibsen_Award "International Ibsen Award") in 2014, the Norwegian author [Øyvind Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Øyvind_Berg_(lyric_poet) "Øyvind Berg (lyric poet)") called for the jury to resign.

However, disputing such interpretations of his work as listed above as misinterpreted by the English press, Handke has described the Srebrenica massacre as an "infernal vengeance, eternal shame for the Bosnian Serbs responsible." This concern about the imprecision and political nature of language, carries through Handke's view. In a 2006 interview, Handke commented on concerns about the stereotyped language of the media that "knew everything", endlessly recycling words like "the butcher of Belgrade".

Handke’s literary fame was overshadowed in 2006 by his politics. The writer’s public support of [Slobodan Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobodan_Milošević "Slobodan Milošević"), the former president of Yugoslavia who died that year while on trial for genocide and war crimes, caused controversy after Handke spoke at his funeral. Because of this the administrator of the theatre [Comédie-Française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comédie-Française "Comédie-Française"), [Marcel Bozonnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Bozonnet "Marcel Bozonnet"), removed Handke's play "Voyage au pays sonore ou L'art de la question" from the forthcoming 2007 schedule. This event once again drew both supportive and critical voices. [Renaud Donnedieu de Vabres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaud_Donnedieu_de_Vabres "Renaud Donnedieu de Vabres"), the French minister of culture, implicitly criticized Bozonnet's action in a letter addressed to him, and by deciding to invite Handke to the ministry. A petition against the censorship of his work was signed by [Emir Kusturica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir_Kusturica "Emir Kusturica"), [Patrick Modiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Modiano "Patrick Modiano") (winner of the [Nobel Prize for Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Literature "Nobel Prize for Literature") in 2014), [Paul Nizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Nizon "Paul Nizon"), [Bulle Ogier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulle_Ogier "Bulle Ogier"), [Luc Bondy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc_Bondy "Luc Bondy") and Handke’s compatriot [Elfriede Jelinek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elfriede_Jelinek "Elfriede Jelinek") (winner of the [Nobel Prize for Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Literature "Nobel Prize for Literature") in 2004).
Handke was subsequently selected to receive that year’s [Heinrich Heine Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Heine_Prize "Heinrich Heine Prize"), though he refused it before it was to be revoked from him.

In 2013, [Tomislav Nikolić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomislav_Nikolić "Tomislav Nikolić"), as the President of Serbia, expressed gratitude saying that some people still remember those who suffered for Christianity, implying that Handke was a victim of scorn for his views, to which Handke replied with an explanation, "I was not anyone's victim, the Serbian people is victim." This was said during the ceremony at which Handke received the *Gold Medal of Merit of the Republic of Serbia*.

In 2019, [The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept "The Intercept") published a number of articles by [Peter Maass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Maass "Peter Maass") criticizing Peter Handke's [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature") reception. In another article by Intercept, Maass went to great lengths accusing Handke of being an "exponent of white nationalism". Subsequently in an interview conducted by Maass in December 2019, asking Handke whether the 1995 [Srebrenica massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_massacre "Srebrenica massacre") had happened, Handke responded: “I prefer waste paper, an anonymous letter with waste paper inside, to your empty and ignorant questions.” Maass also claims that two Nobel prize jurors were adhering to "conspiracy theories" with regard to American involvement in the Yugoslav conflicts, and that the jurors were "misinformed" about Handke's literary achievements. Peter Handke received countless mails that included threats, or unsanitary content. Germany's [Eugen Ruge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugen_Ruge "Eugen Ruge") also protested against the scale of the criticism. In November, around 120 authors, literary scholars, translators and artists expressed their unease in an open letter. They felt that the criticism against Handke was no longer rational.

In February 2020, Handke was decorated with the [Order of Karađorđe's Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Karađorđe's_Star "Order of Karađorđe's Star") for "special merits in representing Serbia and its citizens" as he "wholeheartedly defended the Serbian truth". The current President of Serbia [Aleksandar Vučić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Vučić "Aleksandar Vučić") presented recipients on the occasion of the Serbian Statehood Day.

## Works

Handke has written novels, plays, screenplays, essays and poems, often published by [Suhrkamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhrkamp_Verlag "Suhrkamp Verlag"). Many works were translated into English. His works are held by the [German National Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_National_Library "German National Library"), including:
