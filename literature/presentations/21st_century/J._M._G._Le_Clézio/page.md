**Jean-Marie Gustave Le Clézio** (French: [[ʒɑ̃ maʁi ɡystav lə klezjo]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 13 April 1940), usually identified as **J. M. G. Le Clézio**, is a writer and professor who holds both French and Mauritian nationality. The author of over forty works, he was awarded the 1963 [Prix Renaudot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Renaudot "Prix Renaudot") for his novel *[The Interrogation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Interrogation_(novel) "The Interrogation (novel)")* and the [2008 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "2008 Nobel Prize in Literature") for his life's work, as an "author of new departures, poetic adventure and sensual ecstasy, explorer of a humanity beyond and below the reigning civilization".

## Biography

Le Clézio's mother was born in the [French Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera "French Riviera") city of [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice "Nice"), his father on the island of [British Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Mauritius "British Mauritius") (which was a British possession, but his father was ethnically Breton, in France). Both his father's and his mother's ancestors were originally from [Morbihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbihan "Morbihan"), on the south coast of [Brittany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany "Brittany").
His paternal ancestor François Alexis Le Clézio fled France in 1798 and settled with his wife and daughter on [Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_de_France_(Mauritius) "Isle de France (Mauritius)"), which was then a French colony but would soon pass into British hands. The colonists were allowed to maintain their customs and use the French language. Le Clézio has never lived in Mauritius for more than a few months at a time, but he has stated that he regards himself both as a Frenchman and a Mauritian. He has dual French and Mauritian citizenship (Mauritius gained independence in 1968) and calls Mauritius his "little fatherland".

Le Clézio was born in Nice, his mother's native city, during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") when his father was serving in the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army "British Army") in Nigeria. He was raised in [Roquebillière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquebillière "Roquebillière"), a small village near Nice until 1948 when he, his mother, and his brother boarded a ship to join his father in [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Nigeria "Colonial Nigeria"). His 1991 novel *[Onitsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onitsha_(novel) "Onitsha (novel)")* is partly autobiographical. In a 2004 [essay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_African_(essay) "The African (essay)"), he reminisced about his childhood in Nigeria and his relationship with his parents.

After studying at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol "University of Bristol") in England from 1958 to 1959, Le Clézio finished his undergraduate degree at Nice's Institut d'études littéraires. In 1964 Le Clézio earned a master's degree from the [University of Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-Marseille_University "Aix-Marseille University") with a thesis on [Henri Michaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Michaux "Henri Michaux") and the mystical experience.

After several years spent in London and Bristol, Le Clézio moved to the United States to work as a teacher. In 1967 he served as an aid worker in [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand") as part of his national service, but was quickly expelled from the country for protesting against child prostitution and sent to [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico "Mexico") to finish his national service. From 1970 to 1974, he lived with the [Embera-Wounaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embera-Wounaan "Embera-Wounaan") tribe in [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama "Panama"). He has been married since 1975 to Jémia Jean, who is Moroccan, and has three daughters (one by his first marriage with Rosalie Piquemal). Since the 1990s they have divided their residence between [Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albuquerque,_New_Mexico "Albuquerque, New Mexico"), Mauritius, and Nice.

In 1983 Le Clézio wrote a doctoral thesis on colonial Mexican history for the [University of Perpignan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Perpignan "University of Perpignan"), on the conquest of the [Purépecha people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purépecha_people "Purépecha people") who inhabit the present-day state of [Michoacán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michoacán "Michoacán"). It was serialized in a French magazine and published in Spanish in 1985.

Le Clézio has taught at a number of universities around the world. A frequent visitor to [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"), he taught French language and literature at [Ewha Womans University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewha_Womans_University "Ewha Womans University") in [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul "Seoul") during the 2007 academic year. In November 2013, Le Clézio joined Nanjing University in China as a professor.

## Literary career

Le Clézio began writing at the age of seven; his first work was a book about the sea. He achieved success at the age of 23, when his first novel, *[The Interrogation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Interrogation_(novel) "The Interrogation (novel)")* (*Le Procès-Verbal*), was the [Prix Renaudot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Renaudot "Prix Renaudot") and was shortlisted for the [Prix Goncourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Goncourt "Prix Goncourt"). Since then he has published more than thirty-six books, including short stories, novels, essays, two translations on the subject of [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Indigenous peoples of the Americas") mythology, and several children's books.

From 1963 to 1975, Le Clézio explored themes such as [insanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insanity "Insanity"), [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language "Language"), nature, and writing. He devoted himself to formal experimentation in the wake of such contemporaries as [Georges Perec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Perec "Georges Perec") or [Michel Butor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Butor "Michel Butor"). His persona was that of an [innovator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovation "Innovation") and a rebel, for which he was praised by [Michel Foucault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Foucault "Michel Foucault") and [Gilles Deleuze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilles_Deleuze "Gilles Deleuze").

During the late 1970s, Le Clézio's style changed drastically; he abandoned experimentation, and the mood of his novels became less tormented as he used themes like [childhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childhood "Childhood"), adolescence, and travelling, which attracted a broader audience. In 1980, Le Clézio was the first winner of the newly created Grand Prix [Paul Morand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Morand "Paul Morand"), awarded by the [Académie Française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_Française "Académie Française"), for his novel *[Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_(novel) "Desert (novel)")* (*Désert*). In 1994, a survey conducted by the French literary magazine *[Lire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lire_(magazine) "Lire (magazine)")* showed that 13 per cent of the readers considered him to be the greatest living French-language writer. His works have been translated to over 30 different languages.

## Nobel Prize

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Nobel2008Literature_news_conference1-1.jpg/250px-Nobel2008Literature_news_conference1-1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nobel2008Literature_news_conference1-1.jpg)

[Horace Engdahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Engdahl "Horace Engdahl") announces Le Clézio winning the Nobel Prize for Literature on 9 October 2008

The [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature") for 2008 went to Le Clézio for works characterized by the [Swedish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy "Swedish Academy") as being "poetic adventure and sensual ecstasy" and for being focused on the environment, especially the desert. The [Swedish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy "Swedish Academy"), in announcing the award, called Le Clézio an "author of new departures, poetic adventure and sensual ecstasy, explorer of a humanity beyond and below the reigning civilization." Le Clézio used his Nobel prize acceptance lecture to attack the subject of information poverty. The title of his lecture was *Dans la forêt des paradoxes* ("In the forest of paradoxes"), a title he attributed to [Stig Dagerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stig_Dagerman "Stig Dagerman").

[Gao Xingjian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gao_Xingjian "Gao Xingjian"), a Chinese émigré writing in [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese "Mandarin Chinese"), was the previous French citizen to receive the prize (for 2000); Le Clézio was the first French-language writer to receive the Nobel Prize in Literature since [Claude Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Simon "Claude Simon") for 1985, and the fourteenth since [Sully Prudhomme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sully_Prudhomme "Sully Prudhomme"), laureate of the first prize of 1901.

## Controversy

Le Clézio is a staunch defender of Mama Rosa, director of a Mexican shelter raided by the police in July 2014 when children were found eating rotten food and kept against the will of their parents. He wrote an article in *Le Monde* arguing that she is close to sanctity.
