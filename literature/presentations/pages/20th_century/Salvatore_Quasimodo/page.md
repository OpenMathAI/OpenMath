**Salvatore Quasimodo** (Italian: [[salvaˈtoːre kwaˈziːmodo]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian "Help:IPA/Italian"); 20 August 1901 – 14 June 1968) was an Italian poet and translator, awarded the [1959 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1959 Nobel Prize in Literature") "for his lyrical poetry, which with classical fire expresses the tragic experience of life in our own times". Along with [Giuseppe Ungaretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Ungaretti "Giuseppe Ungaretti") and [Eugenio Montale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Montale "Eugenio Montale"), he was one of the foremost Italian poets of the 20th century.

## Biography

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Salvatore_Quasimodo_1968.jpg/250px-Salvatore_Quasimodo_1968.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Salvatore_Quasimodo_1968.jpg)

Quasimodo in 1968

Quasimodo was born in [Modica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modica "Modica"), [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily "Sicily"), into a [Sicilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilians "Sicilians")-[Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks "Greeks") family. His paternal grandmother, Rosa (*née* Papandreou), was from [Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patras "Patras"), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece"). Quasimodo integrated both of his ancestries in his works, in which he defined himself as a "*Siculo-Greco*" (lit. 'Sicilian-Greek'). In his biography for [Elio Filippo Accrocca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elio_Filippo_Accrocca "Elio Filippo Accrocca"), he even sought to relocate his birthplace to [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily "Syracuse, Sicily"), the most notable city of [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia "Magna Graecia"), instead of Modica, where he was actually born.

Quasimodo spent his childhood in [Roccalumera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roccalumera "Roccalumera"). In 1908 his family moved to [Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messina "Messina"), as his father had been sent there to help the local population struck by a devastating earthquake. The impressions of the effects of natural forces would have a great impact on the young Quasimodo. In 1919 he graduated from the local Technical College. In Messina, he also made friends with [Giorgio La Pira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_La_Pira "Giorgio La Pira"), future mayor of [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence "Florence").

Salvatore Quasimodo was introduced to the [Scottish Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Rite "Scottish Rite") Freemasonry by his father, who was a member of the [Masonic Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonic_Lodge "Masonic Lodge") "[Arnaldo da Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnaldo_da_Brescia "Arnaldo da Brescia")". The [Grand Orient of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Orient_of_Italy "Grand Orient of Italy") has recognized Quasimodo as one of his most notable brothers.

In 1917 Quasimodo founded the short-lived *Nuovo giornale letterario* ("New Literary Journal"), in which he published his first poems. In 1919 he moved to Rome to finish his engineering studies, but poor economic conditions forced him to find work as a technical draughtsman. In the meantime, he collaborated with several reviews and studied [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language") and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language "Latin language").

In 1929, invited by [Elio Vittorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elio_Vittorini "Elio Vittorini"), who had married Quasimodo's sister, he moved to Florence. Here he met poets such as [Alessandro Bonsanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Bonsanti "Alessandro Bonsanti") and [Eugenio Montale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Montale "Eugenio Montale"). In 1930 he took a job with Italy's Civil Engineering Corps in [Reggio Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_Calabria "Reggio Calabria"). Here he met the Misefari brothers, who encouraged him to continue writing. Developing his nearness to the [hermetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermeticism_(poetry) "Hermeticism (poetry)") movement, Quasimodo published his first collection, *Acque e terre* ("Waters and Earths") in that year.

In 1931 he was transferred to [Imperia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperia "Imperia") and then to [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa "Genoa"), where he got acquainted with [Camillo Sbarbaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camillo_Sbarbaro "Camillo Sbarbaro") and other personalities of the *[Circoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circoli "Circoli")* magazine, with which Quasimodo started a fruitful collaboration. In 1932 he published with them a new collection, *Oboe sommerso*, including all his lyrics from 1930 to 1932.

In 1934 Quasimodo moved to [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan "Milan"). Starting from 1938 he devoted himself entirely to writing, working with [Cesare Zavattini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Zavattini "Cesare Zavattini") and for *Letteratura*, the official review of the [Hermetic movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermeticism_(poetry) "Hermeticism (poetry)"). In 1938 he published *Poesie*, followed by the translations of *Lirici Greci* ("Greek Poets") published by *[Corrente di Vita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrente_di_Vita "Corrente di Vita")* in 1939.

Though an outspoken anti-Fascist, during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") Quasimodo did not take part in the Italian resistance against the German occupation. In that period he devoted himself to the translation of the [Gospel of John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_John "Gospel of John"), of some of [Catullus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catullus "Catullus")'s cantos, and several episodes of the *Odyssey*. In 1945 he joined the [Italian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Communist_Party "Italian Communist Party"), but only remained a member for a short period.

In 1946 he published another collection, *Giorno dopo giorno* ("Day After Day"), which made clear the increasing moral engagement and the epic tone of the social criticism of the author. The same theme characterized his next works, *La vita non è sogno* ("Life Is Not a Dream"), *Il falso e il vero verde* ("The False and True Green") and *La terra impareggiabile* ("The Incomparable Land"). In all this period Quasimodo did not stop producing translations of classic authors and collaborating as a journalist for some of the most prestigious Italian publications (mostly with articles about the theatre).

In the 1950s Quasimodo won the following literary awards: Premio San Babila (1950), Premio Etna-Taormina (1953), [Premio Viareggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premio_Viareggio "Premio Viareggio") (1958) and, finally, the Nobel Prize for Literature (1959). In 1960 and 1967 he received *honoris causa* degrees from the Universities of [Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messina "Messina") and [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford "Oxford"), respectively.

In his last years, the poet made numerous voyages to Europe and America, giving public speeches and public lectures on his poems, which had been translated into several foreign languages.

### Death

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Salvatore_Quasimodo_grave_Milan_2015.jpg/250px-Salvatore_Quasimodo_grave_Milan_2015.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Salvatore_Quasimodo_grave_Milan_2015.jpg)

Quasimodo's grave at the [Cimitero Monumentale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimitero_Monumentale "Cimitero Monumentale") in Milan in 2015

In June 1968, when he was in [Amalfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalfi "Amalfi") for a discourse, Quasimodo was struck by a [cerebral haemorrhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_haemorrhage "Cerebral haemorrhage"). He died a few days later in the hospital in [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples "Naples"). He was interred in the [Cimitero Monumentale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimitero_Monumentale "Cimitero Monumentale") in [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan "Milan").

## Poetic language

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Salvatore_Quasimodo_1958.jpg/250px-Salvatore_Quasimodo_1958.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Salvatore_Quasimodo_1958.jpg)

Quasimodo in 1958

Traditional literary critique divides Quasimodo's work into two major periods: the [hermetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermeticism_(poetry) "Hermeticism (poetry)") period until World War II and the post-hermetic era until his death. Although these periods are distinct, they can be seen as a single poetical quest. This quest or exploration for a unique language took him through various stages and various modalities of expression.

As an intelligent and clever poet, Quasimodo used a [hermetical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermeticism_(poetry) "Hermeticism (poetry)"), "closed" language to sketch recurring motifs like Sicily, religion and death. Subsequently, the translation of authors from Roman and Greek Antiquity enabled him to extend his linguistic toolkit. The disgust and sense of absurdity of World War II also had an impact on the poet's language.
This bitterness, however, faded in his late writings and was replaced by the mature voice of an old poet reflecting upon his world.

## Global policy

He was one of the signatories of the agreement to convene a convention for drafting a [world constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_constitution "World constitution"). As a result, for the first time in human history, a [World Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Constituent_Assembly "World Constituent Assembly") convened to draft and adopt the [Constitution for the Federation of Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_for_the_Federation_of_Earth "Constitution for the Federation of Earth").
