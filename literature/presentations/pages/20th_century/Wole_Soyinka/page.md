**Akinwande Oluwole Babatunde Soyinka** ([Listen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/73/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Chikeme_Chizurum-Akinwande_Oluwole_Babatunde_Soyinka.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Chikeme_Chizurum-Akinwande_Oluwole_Babatunde_Soyinka.wav.mp3 "Play audio")[ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:LL-Q1860_(eng)-Chikeme_Chizurum-Akinwande_Oluwole_Babatunde_Soyinka.wav "File:LL-Q1860 (eng)-Chikeme Chizurum-Akinwande Oluwole Babatunde Soyinka.wav"); born 13 July 1934) is a Nigerian writer, playwright, and poet. He has written three novels, ten collections of short stories, seven poetry collections, twenty-five plays and five memoirs. He has also written two translated works and many articles and short stories for newspapers and periodicals. He was awarded the [1986 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1986 Nobel Prize in Literature") for his "wide cultural perspective and poetic overtones fashioning the drama of existence".

Born into an [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Communion "Anglican Communion") [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people "Yoruba people") family in Aké, [Abeokuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeokuta "Abeokuta"), Soyinka had a preparatory education at [Government College, Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_College,_Ibadan "Government College, Ibadan"), and proceeded to the [University College Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Ibadan "University College Ibadan"). During his education, he co-founded the [Pyrate Confraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrate_Confraternity "Pyrate Confraternity"). Soyinka left Nigeria for England to study at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds "University of Leeds"). During that period, he was the editor of the university's magazine, *The Eagle*, before becoming a full-time author in the 1950s. In the UK, he started writing short stories and making records for the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") Lecture series. He wrote many plays which were performed on radios and in theatres in Nigeria and the UK, especially the [Royal Court Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Court_Theatre "Royal Court Theatre").

Many of Soyinka's novels and plays are set in Nigeria, reflecting the country's history, culture, and political struggles. Alongside these works, he has produced a wide range of [satirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satirical "Satirical") writings that have reached a broad audience and enjoyed considerable popularity. He is also an accomplished poet, with numerous individual poems and several published collections to his name. He has achieved success with his plays, including *The Swamp Dwellers* (1958), *[The Lion and the Jewel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lion_and_the_Jewel "The Lion and the Jewel")* (1959), and *The Invention* (1957), an early play produced at the Royal Court Theatre. [Soyinka has written a number of other works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_works_by_Wole_Soyinka "List of works by Wole Soyinka"), including *[The Interpreters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Interpreters_(novel) "The Interpreters (novel)")* (1965), *[Season of Anomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Season_of_Anomy "Season of Anomy")* (1973), *[Death and the King's Horseman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_the_King's_Horseman "Death and the King's Horseman")*, *[Chronicles from the Land of the Happiest People on Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicles_from_the_Land_of_the_Happiest_People_on_Earth "Chronicles from the Land of the Happiest People on Earth")* (2021), and *[Harmattan Haze on an African Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmattan_Haze_on_an_African_Spring "Harmattan Haze on an African Spring")* (2012). In July 2024, [Bola Tinubu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bola_Tinubu "Bola Tinubu") renamed the [National Arts Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Arts_Theatre "National Arts Theatre") after Soyinka during his 90th birthday.

## Early life and education

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Abeokuta_Grammar_School_Gate%2C_Idi-aba%2CAbeokuta2.jpg/250px-Abeokuta_Grammar_School_Gate%2C_Idi-aba%2CAbeokuta2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Abeokuta_Grammar_School_Gate,_Idi-aba,Abeokuta2.jpg)

[Abeokuta Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeokuta_Grammar_School "Abeokuta Grammar School"), where Soyinka had his secondary education

Soyinka was born on 13 July 1934 in Ake, [Abeokuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeokuta "Abeokuta"), Nigeria into a [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian "Christian") family of [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people "Yoruba people") descent. He was the second out of seven children of Samuel Ayodele Soyinka, an [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican "Anglican") minister and headmaster from the [royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_family "Royal family") of [Isara-Remo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isara-Remo "Isara-Remo") and [Grace Soyinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Soyinka "Grace Soyinka") (née Jenkins-Harrison), a shopkeeper and activist from the [Ransome-Kuti family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransome-Kuti_family "Ransome-Kuti family").

His mother Grace's extended family, the Ransome-Kutis, were a powerful Nigerian dynasty that has been described as Nigeria's answer to the [Kennedy family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_family "Kennedy family") in the United States. Their founding patriarch, the Anglican priest J. J. Ransome-Kuti, was a pioneering figure in his country's music recording industry in the 1920s.

From 1940 to 1946, Soyinka attended St. Peters Primary School, where his father was the headmaster. He had his secondary education at [Abeokuta Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeokuta_Grammar_School "Abeokuta Grammar School") and his university preparatory studies at [Government College, Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_College,_Ibadan "Government College, Ibadan") from 1946 to 1951. He was admitted into the [University College Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Ibadan "University College Ibadan"), where he studied [English literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_literature "English literature"), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language"), and [Western history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_history "Western history") from 1952 to 1954. During his final years in the university, he wrote a short radio play, *Keffi's Birthday Treat*, which was broadcast in July 1954 by the [Federal Radio Corporation of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Radio_Corporation_of_Nigeria "Federal Radio Corporation of Nigeria"). Along with his classmates including Olumuyiwa Awẹ, Ralph Opara, Aig-Imoukhuede, and Pius Oleghe, he founded the [National Association of Seadogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Seadogs "National Association of Seadogs"), the first [confraternity in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confraternities_in_Nigeria "Confraternities in Nigeria").

Soyinka relocated from Nigeria to England in 1954 to continue his studies in English literature at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds "University of Leeds") under the supervision of [G. Wilson Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._Wilson_Knight "G. Wilson Knight"). The decision to study abroad at that time when master's degree study was not available in Ibadan was through Joyce M. Green, an English woman who joined the University College, Ibadan from [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge") in 1950, and who had been one of Soyinka's teachers and had provided him with a well-worded reference to Leeds. At Leeds University, he served as the editor of the university's satirical magazine, *The Eagle*, in which he wrote a column on academic life. In August 1955, he started recording for [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") Lecture and wrote many short stories. In 1957, he won the annual oratory competition run by the University.

## Career

### 1957–1967: Early career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Royal_Court_theater%2C_Sloane_Square_Chelsea_London_UK_2020.jpg/250px-Royal_Court_theater%2C_Sloane_Square_Chelsea_London_UK_2020.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Royal_Court_theater,_Sloane_Square_Chelsea_London_UK_2020.jpg)

The [Royal Court Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Court_Theatre "Royal Court Theatre") in 2020. Most of Soyinka's early plays were performed in the theatre.

Soyinka remained in [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds "Leeds") after getting his BA honours in English in 1957. In 1957, he began writing his first plays, *The Swamp Dwellers* and *[The Lion and the Jewel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lion_and_the_Jewel "The Lion and the Jewel")*. Soyinka moved to London and worked as a play reader for the Royal Court Theatre. He led the Nigerian Drama Group that performed *The Swamp Dwellers* in 1958. During that period, his two plays were performed in [Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadan "Ibadan"), Nigeria. In 1957, his play, *The Invention*, was produced at [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London")'s [Royal Court Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Court_Theatre "Royal Court Theatre"), and was his first work to be produced there. During this period, he wrote poems, including "The Immigrant" and "My Next Door Neighbour", which appeared in *[Black Orpheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Orpheus_(magazine) "Black Orpheus (magazine)")*. In 1959, Soyinka returned to Nigeria after receiving a [Rockefeller Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation "Rockefeller Foundation") for his research on African theatre. In November of the same year, he replaced [Janheinz Jahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janheinz_Jahn "Janheinz Jahn") as the co-editor of *Black Orpheus*. In 1960, he completed his radio play *Camwood on the Leaves*, and his play *[The Trials of Brother Jero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trials_of_Brother_Jero "The Trials of Brother Jero")* premiered in the Mellanby Hall residence of University College Ibadan in April 1960. He formed the 1960s Mask, a theatre group. His play *A Dance of The Forest*, became the official play for the [Nigerian Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nigeria#Independence "History of Nigeria") and on 1 October 1960, it premiered in Lagos.

Soyinka's first full-length play, *My Father's Burden*, was directed by [Olusegun Olusola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olusegun_Olusola "Olusegun Olusola") and it was featured on the [Western Nigeria Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Nigeria_Television "Western Nigeria Television") on 6 August 1960. In 1962, Soyinka wrote essays that defended Nigerian literacy during that period including "Death and the King's Horsemen" and "Towards a True Theater", which were published in *[Transition Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_Magazine "Transition Magazine")*. In the same year, he was appointed lecturer at [Obafemi Awolowo University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obafemi_Awolowo_University "Obafemi Awolowo University") in [Ifẹ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifẹ "Ifẹ"). In 1963, his first feature-length movie, *Culture in Transition*, was released. Soyinka resigned from his university post in 1964, as a protest against imposed pro-government behaviour by the authorities. He claimed the university's authorities aligned the institution with the unpopular government of [Samuel Ladoke Akintola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Ladoke_Akintola "Samuel Ladoke Akintola"). During that period, he produced *The Lion and the Jewel* in a season of plays in English and [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language "Yoruba language") as well as formed The Orisun Theatre Company, a theatre group.

In 1965, he produced the satirical play *Before the Blackout*. His play *[Kongi's Harvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongi's_Harvest "Kongi's Harvest")* premiered in August 1965 in Lagos. On 14 September 1965, his play *[The Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Road_(play) "The Road (play)")* premiered in London at the Commonwealth Arts Festival and at the [Theatre Royal Stratford East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Royal_Stratford_East "Theatre Royal Stratford East"). Soyinka was appointed as the senior lecturer at the [University of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lagos "University of Lagos"). Soon, his novel *The Interpreters* was published in London by [André Deutsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Deutsch "André Deutsch"). In the same year, he was arrested for the first time, charged with holding up a radio station at gunpoint and replacing the tape of a recorded speech by the premier of [Western Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Region,_Nigeria "Western Region, Nigeria") with a different tape containing accusations of [electoral malpractice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_malpractice "Electoral malpractice"). He was released after some months of confinement as a result of protests by the international community of writers. He also wrote *The Detainee*, a radio play for [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") in London.

### 1966–1968: Nigerian Civil War

After becoming Chair of Drama at the [University of Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ibadan "University of Ibadan"), Soyinka became politically active. Following the [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Civil_War#Military_coups "Nigerian Civil War") of January 1966, he secretly met with [Chukwuemeka Odumegwu Ojukwu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukwuemeka_Odumegwu_Ojukwu "Chukwuemeka Odumegwu Ojukwu"), the military governor of the [Southeastern Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Western_Region,_Nigeria "Mid-Western Region, Nigeria") in an effort to avert the [Nigerian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Civil_War "Nigerian Civil War"). He also went to Ẹnugu, where he met his fellow Yoruba man, Victor Banjọ, who works with the Biafran government. Soyinka, who sought the support of Western Region military leaders, delivered Banjo's message to [Lieutenant Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Colonel "Lieutenant Colonel") [Olusegun Obasanjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olusegun_Obasanjo "Olusegun Obasanjo"), who had recently been appointed as commanding officer for the [Western Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Region,_Nigeria "Western Region, Nigeria"). Four evenings after Soyinka returned to the West, Biafran forces invaded the Midwest region. Following the occupation of the Midwest, Soyinka met Obasanjo to tell him the aim of Biafrans since Obasanjo had already decided to align with the Nigerian government. However Biafra's invasion of the Midwest resulted in retaliation by the federal government forces, and the civil war began. Obasanjo disclosed his meeting with Soyinka to the Nigerian government, who declared Soyinka a traitor, hence he was arrested by federal authorities and imprisoned for 22 months. He wrote a significant body of poems and notes criticising the Nigerian government while in prison.

In early 1967, his works *Kongi’s Harvest* and *Idanre and Other Poems* were published. With [Tom Stoppard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Stoppard "Tom Stoppard"), he received the [John Whiting Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Whiting_Award "John Whiting Award") in London. He was also appointed Head of the School of Drama, University of Ibadan but unable to take up the position because of his imprisonment in August. Despite his imprisonment, his play *The Lion and The Jewel* was produced in [Accra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra "Accra"), Ghana in September 1967. In November, *The Trials of Brother Jero* and *The Strong Breed* were produced in the Greenwich Mews Theatre in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") by Off-Broadway.

In 1968, Soyinka received the [Jock Campbell-New Statesman Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Statesman "New Statesman"). The [Negro Ensemble Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negro_Ensemble_Company "Negro Ensemble Company") produced *[Kongi's Harvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongi's_Harvest "Kongi's Harvest")* at St. Mark's Theatre, New York. He translated [D. O. Fagunwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._O._Fagunwa "D. O. Fagunwa")'s *Ogboju Ode Ninu Igbo Irunmale* from Yoruba to English. It was published as *[The Forest of a Thousand Demons: A Hunter's Saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forest_of_a_Thousand_Demons:_A_Hunter's_Saga "The Forest of a Thousand Demons: A Hunter's Saga")*.

### Release and literary production

In October 1969, a few months before the civil war came to an end, amnesty was proclaimed, and Soyinka and other political prisoners were freed. For the first few months after his release, Soyinka stayed at a friend's farm in southern France, where he sought solitude. He wrote *[The Bacchae of Euripides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bacchae_of_Euripides "The Bacchae of Euripides")* (1969), a reworking of the [Pentheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentheus "Pentheus") myth. He also published in London a book of poetry, *Poems from Prison*. At the end of the year, he returned to his office as Chair of Drama at Ibadan.

In 1970, he produced the play *Kongi's Harvest*, while simultaneously adapting it as a film of the same title. In June 1970, he finished another play, called *Madmen and Specialists*. Together with the group of 15 actors of Ibadan University Theatre Art Company, he went on a trip to the United States, to the [Eugene O'Neill Memorial Theatre Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_O'Neill_Memorial_Theatre_Center "Eugene O'Neill Memorial Theatre Center") in [Waterford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterford,_Connecticut "Waterford, Connecticut"), [Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut "Connecticut"), where his latest play premiered.

In 1971, his poetry collection *[A Shuttle in the Crypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Shuttle_in_the_Crypt "A Shuttle in the Crypt")* was published. *Madmen and Specialists* was produced in Ibadan that year. In April 1971, concerned about the political situation in Nigeria, Soyinka resigned from his duties at the University in Ibadan, and began years of voluntary exile.

Soyinka travelled to [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"), France, to take the lead role of [Patrice Lumumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Lumumba "Patrice Lumumba"), the murdered first Prime Minister of the [Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo "Democratic Republic of the Congo"), in [Joan Littlewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Littlewood "Joan Littlewood")'s May 1971 production of *Murderous Angels*, [Conor Cruise O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conor_Cruise_O'Brien "Conor Cruise O'Brien")'s play about the [Congo Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Crisis "Congo Crisis"). In July in Paris, excerpts from Soyinka's well-known play *The Dance of The Forests* were performed.

In 1972, his novel *[Season of Anomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Season_of_Anomy "Season of Anomy")* and his *Collected Plays* were both published by [Oxford University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press "Oxford University Press"). His powerful autobiographical work *[The Man Died](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_Died "The Man Died")*, a collection of notes from prison, was also published that year. He was awarded an [Honoris Causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_Causa "Honoris Causa") doctorate by the University of Leeds in 1973. In the same year the [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_National_Theatre "Royal National Theatre"), London, commissioned and premiered the play *The Bacchae of Euripides*, and his plays *Camwood on the Leaves* and *Jero's Metamorphosis* were also first published. From 1973 to 1975, Soyinka spent time on scientific studies. He spent a year as a visiting fellow at [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge "Churchill College, Cambridge") (1973–74) and wrote *[Death and the King's Horseman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_the_King's_Horseman "Death and the King's Horseman")*, which had its first reading at Churchill College.

In 1974, Oxford University Press issued Soyinka's *Collected Plays, Volume II*. He compiled the [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_in_poetry "1975 in poetry") anthology *[Poems of Black Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poems_of_Black_Africa "Poems of Black Africa")*, which was published as part of the [Heinemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinemann_(book_publisher) "Heinemann (book publisher)") *[African Writers Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinemann_African_Writers_Series "Heinemann African Writers Series")*. Also in 1975, Soyinka was promoted to the position of editor for *[Transition Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_Magazine "Transition Magazine")*, which was based in the Ghanaian capital of [Accra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra "Accra"), where he moved. He used his columns in the magazine to criticise the "negrophiles" (for instance, his article "Neo-Tarzanism: The Poetics of Pseudo-Transition") and military regimes. He protested against the military junta of [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin "Idi Amin") in Uganda. After the political turnover in Nigeria and the subversion of Gowon's military regime in 1975, Soyinka returned to his homeland and resumed his position as Chair of Comparative Literature at the [University of Ife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ife "University of Ife").

### Teaching

From 1975 to 1999, Soyinka served as a Professor of [Comparative literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_literature "Comparative literature") at [Obafemi Awolowo University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obafemi_Awolowo_University "Obafemi Awolowo University"). In the US, he taught at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") as the [Goldwin Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldwin_Smith "Goldwin Smith") professor for African Studies and Theatre Arts from 1988 to 1991. At [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University "Emory University"), he was appointed [Robert W. Woodruff Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_W._Woodruff_Professor "Robert W. Woodruff Professor") of the Arts in 1996 and has been a Professor of Creative Writing at the [University of Nevada, Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nevada,_Las_Vegas "University of Nevada, Las Vegas"). Soyinka served as scholar-in-residence at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University")'s Institute of African American Affairs and at [Loyola Marymount University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyola_Marymount_University "Loyola Marymount University") in Los Angeles, California. He has also taught at the universities of [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford"), [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") and [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale "Yale"). He was a Distinguished Scholar in Residence at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University") in 2008.

In December 2017, Soyinka received the [Europe Theatre Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_Theatre_Prize "Europe Theatre Prize") in the "Special Prize" category, awarded to someone who has "contributed to the realization of cultural events that promote understanding and the exchange of knowledge between peoples". On 1 September 2022, Soyinka took up the appointment of Professor of Theatre at [New York University Abu Dhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_Abu_Dhabi "New York University Abu Dhabi").

In 1976, he published his poetry collection *Ogun Abibiman*, as well as a collection of essays entitled *Myth, Literature and the African World*. In these, Soyinka explores the genesis of mysticism in African theatre and, using examples from both European and African literature, compares and contrasts the cultures. He delivered a series of guest lectures at the [Institute of African Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_African_Studies "Institute of African Studies") at the [University of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ghana "University of Ghana") in [Legon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legon "Legon"). In October 1976, the French version of *The Dance of The Forests* was performed in [Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar "Dakar"), while in Ife, his play *Death and The King's Horseman* premiered.

In 1977, *Opera Wọnyọsi*, his adaptation of [Bertolt Brecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertolt_Brecht "Bertolt Brecht")'s *[The Threepenny Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Threepenny_Opera "The Threepenny Opera")*, was staged in Ibadan. In 1979, Soyinka both directed and acted in [Jon Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Blair "Jon Blair") and [Norman Fenton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Felton "Norman Felton")'s drama *The Biko Inquest*, a work based on the life of [Steve Biko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Biko "Steve Biko"), a South African student and human rights activist who was beaten to death by [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid") police forces. In 1981 Soyinka published his autobiographical work *[Aké: The Years of Childhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aké:_The_Years_of_Childhood "Aké: The Years of Childhood")*, which won a 1983 [Anisfield-Wolf Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisfield-Wolf_Book_Award "Anisfield-Wolf Book Award").

Soyinka went on to establish another theatrical group, the Guerrilla Unit, which sought to engage directly with local communities. Its purpose was to help them reflect on their challenges and give voice to their grievances through short dramatic performances. In 1983, his play *Requiem for a Futurologist* had its first performance at the University of Ife. In July that year, one of his musical projects, the Unlimited Liability Company, issued a long-playing record entitled *I Love My Country*, on which several prominent Nigerian musicians played songs composed by Soyinka. In 1984, he directed the film *Blues for a Prodigal*, which was screened at the University of Ife. His *A Play of Giants* was produced the same year.

During the years 1975–84, Soyinka was more politically active. At the University of Ife, his administrative duties included the security of public roads. He criticized the corruption in the government of the democratically elected President [Shehu Shagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shehu_Shagari "Shehu Shagari"). When Shagari was replaced by the army general [Muhammadu Buhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammadu_Buhari "Muhammadu Buhari"), Soyinka was often at odds with the military. In 1984, a Nigerian court banned his 1972 book *[The Man Died: Prison Notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_Died:_Prison_Notes "The Man Died: Prison Notes")*. In 1985, his play *Requiem for a Futurologist* was published in London by [Rex Collings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rex_Collings "Rex Collings").

### 1986: Nobel Prize winning

Soyinka was awarded the [Nobel Prize for Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Literature "Nobel Prize for Literature") in 1986, becoming the first African laureate. He was described as one "who in a wide cultural perspective and with poetic overtones fashions the drama of existence". Reed Way Dasenbrock writes that the award of the Nobel Prize in Literature to Soyinka is "likely to prove quite controversial and thoroughly deserved". He also notes that "it is the first Nobel Prize awarded to an African writer or to any writer from the 'new literatures' in English that have emerged in the former colonies of the British Empire." His Nobel acceptance speech, "This Past Must Address Its Present", was devoted to South African freedom-fighter [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela "Nelson Mandela"). Soyinka's speech was an outspoken criticism of [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid "Apartheid") and the politics of racial segregation imposed on the majority by the [National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_(South_Africa) "National Party (South Africa)") South African government.

### 1988–present: Later career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/WoleSoyinka2015.jpg/250px-WoleSoyinka2015.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:WoleSoyinka2015.jpg)

Soyinka in 2015

In 1988, his collection of poems *Mandela's Earth, and Other Poems* was published, while in Nigeria another collection of essays, entitled *Art, Dialogue and Outrage: Essays on Literature and Culture*, appeared. In the same year, Soyinka accepted the position of Professor of African Studies and Theatre at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"). In 1989, he published a third book, *Ìsarà: A Voyage around Essay*, inspired by his father's intellectual circle ("Essay" being the nickname of his father S. A. Soyinka). In July 1991, the [BBC African Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_World_Service#Africa "BBC World Service") broadcast Soyinka's radio play *A Scourge of Hyacinths*, and the next year (1992) in [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena "Siena") (Italy), his play *[From Zia with Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_Zia_with_Love "From Zia with Love")* had its premiere. Both works are bitter political parodies, based on events that took place in Nigeria in the 1980s. In 1993, Soyinka was awarded an honorary doctorate from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). The following year, another part of his autobiography was published: *[Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadan "Ibadan"): The Penkelemes Years (A Memoir: 1946–1965)*. In 1995, his play *The Beatification of Area Boy* was published. In October 1994, he was appointed [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") Goodwill Ambassador for the Promotion of African culture, human rights, freedom of expression, media and communication.

In November 1994, Soyinka fled from Nigeria on a motorcycle via the border with [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin "Benin"), and then went to the United States. In 1996, his book *The Open Sore of a Continent: A Personal Narrative of the Nigerian Crisis* was first published. In 1997, he was charged with treason by the government of General [Sani Abacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sani_Abacha "Sani Abacha"). The International Parliament of Writers (IPW) was established in 1993 to provide support for writers victimized by persecution. Soyinka became the organization's second president, serving in the role from 1997 to 2000. In 1999, a new volume of poems by Soyinka, entitled *Outsiders*, was released. That same year, a BBC-commissioned play called *Document of Identity* aired on [BBC Radio 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_3 "BBC Radio 3"), telling the lightly-fictionalized story of the problems his daughter's family encountered during a stopover in [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain "Great Britain") when they fled Nigeria for the US in 1996; her son, Oseoba Airewele was born in [Luton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton "Luton") and became a stateless person.

Soyinka's play *King Baabu* premièred in Lagos in 2001, a political satire on the theme of African dictatorship. In 2002, a collection of his poems entitled *Samarkand and Other Markets I Have Known* was published by [Methuen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methuen_Publishing "Methuen Publishing"). In April 2006, his memoir *You Must Set Forth at Dawn* was published by [Random House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_House_Publishing "Random House Publishing"). In 2006 he cancelled his keynote speech for the annual [S.E.A. Write Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.E.A._Write_Award "S.E.A. Write Award") Ceremony in [Bangkok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok "Bangkok") to protest the [Thai military's successful coup against the government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Thailand_coup "2006 Thailand coup").

In April 2007, Soyinka called for the cancellation of the Nigerian presidential elections held two weeks earlier, beset by widespread fraud and violence. In the wake of the [attempted bombing on a Northwest Airlines flight to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Airlines_Flight_253 "Northwest Airlines Flight 253") by a Nigerian student who had become radicalised in Britain, Soyinka questioned the [British government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_United_Kingdom "Government of the United Kingdom")'s social logic in allowing every religion to openly proselytise their faith, asserting that it was being abused by religious fundamentalists, thereby turning England into, in his view, a cesspit for the breeding of extremism. He supported the freedom of worship, but warned against the consequence of the illogic of allowing religions to preach apocalyptic violence.

In August 2014, Soyinka delivered a recording of his speech "From Chibok with Love" to the [World Humanist Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Humanist_Congress "World Humanist Congress") in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford "Oxford"), hosted by the [International Humanist and Ethical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Humanist_and_Ethical_Union "International Humanist and Ethical Union") and the [British Humanist Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Humanist_Association "British Humanist Association"). The Congress theme was *Freedom of thought and expression: Forging a 21st Century Enlightenment*. He was awarded the 2014 [International Humanist Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Humanist_Award "International Humanist Award"). He served as Arts Professor of Theatre at [NYU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYU "NYU")'s Institute of African American Affairs.

In December 2020, Soyinka described [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_in_Nigeria "2020 in Nigeria") as the most challenging year in the nation's history, saying: "With the turbulence that characterised year 2020, and as activities wind down, the mood has been repugnant and very negative. I don't want to sound pessimistic, but this is one of the most pessimistic years I have known in this nation and it wasn't just because of [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_disease_2019 "Coronavirus disease 2019"). Natural disasters had happened elsewhere, but how have you managed to take such in their strides?"

September 2021 saw the publication of *Chronicles from the Land of the Happiest People on Earth*, Soyinka's first novel in almost 50 years, described in the *[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times "Financial Times")* as "a brutally satirical look at power and corruption in Nigeria, told in the form of a whodunnit involving three university friends." Reviewing the book in *The Guardian* newspaper, [Ben Okri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Okri "Ben Okri") said: "It is Soyinka's greatest novel, his revenge against the insanities of the nation's ruling class and one of the most shocking chronicles of an African nation in the 21st century. It ought to be widely read."

The [film adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_adaptation "Film adaptation") by [Biyi Bandele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biyi_Bandele "Biyi Bandele") of Soyinka's 1975 stage play *[Death and the King's Horseman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_the_King's_Horseman "Death and the King's Horseman")*, co-produced by [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") and [Ebonylife TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebonylife_TV "Ebonylife TV"), titled *[Elesin Oba, The King's Horseman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elesin_Oba,_The_King's_Horseman "Elesin Oba, The King's Horseman")*, premiered at the [Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival "Toronto International Film Festival") (TIFF) in September 2022. It is Soyinka's first work to be made into a feature film internationally, and the first [Yoruba-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba-language "Yoruba-language") film to premiere at TIFF.

Around July 2023, Soyinka came under severe criticism after writing an open letter to the Emir of Ilorin, [Ibrahim Sulu-Gambari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Sulu-Gambari "Ibrahim Sulu-Gambari"), over the cancellation of the Isese festival proposed by an *Osun* priestess, Omolara Olatunji.

Two films have been made about Soyinka's life. *The Man Died*, directed by [Awam Amkpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awam_Amkpa "Awam Amkpa"), is a feature film based on a fictionalized form of Soyinka's 1973 prison memoirs of the same title. *[Ebrohimie Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebrohimie_Road "Ebrohimie Road")*, written and directed by [Kola Tubosun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kola_Tubosun "Kola Tubosun"), explores the Ibadan house where Soyinka lived between 1967, when he returned to direct the School of Drama, and 1972, when he left for exile following his released from prison.

## Personal life

Soyinka has been married three times and divorced twice. He has eight children from his three marriages and two other daughters. His first marriage was in 1958 to the late British writer Barbara Dixon, whom he met at the University of Leeds in the 1950s. Barbara was the mother of his first son, [Olaokun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaokun_Soyinka "Olaokun Soyinka"). Soyinka's first daughter Morenike was born in 1964. After they separated, he married Olaide Idowu, a Nigerian librarian, in 1963. The couple had three daughters: Moremi, Iyetade (1965–2013), and Peyibomi, and a son, Ilemakin. Soyinka's youngest daughter, Amani, was born in 1981. Soyinka married Folake Doherty in 1989, and the couple have three sons: Tunlewa, Bojode and Eniara.

On 15 July 2024, Oba Albert Adebose Mayungbe, the traditional monarch of [Isara-Remo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isara-Remo "Isara-Remo"), conferred three traditional titles on Soyinka's children.

In 2014, Soyinka revealed his battle with [prostate cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostate_cancer "Prostate cancer").

Soyinka has commented on his close friendships with [Toni Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Morrison "Toni Morrison") and [Henry Louis Gates Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Louis_Gates_Jr. "Henry Louis Gates Jr."), saying: "Friendship, to me, is what saves one's sanity."

Soyinka is an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist"). In 2013, he visited the [Benin Moat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_Moat "Benin Moat") as the representative of [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") in recognition of the Naija seven Wonders project. He has served as the consultant for the [Lagos Black Heritage Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Black_Heritage_Festival "Lagos Black Heritage Festival") since the festival's establishment in 2009 by the [Government of Lagos State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Lagos_State "Government of Lagos State"). He was appointed a patron of [Humanists UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanists_UK "Humanists UK") in 2020.

In a book published in 2020, [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London") academic Caroline Davis examined archival evidence of the [Central Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "Central Intelligence Agency") (CIA) funding of African authors in the post-independence period. A chapter of the book was titled "Wole Soyinka, the Transcription Centre, and the CIA", and focused on Soyinka's receipt of funding from CIA front organisations such as the Farfield Foundation and the Transcription Centre. The funding supported Soyinka's publishing and the global production of some of his theatre plays. The book states that even after the CIA's covert role in some of these initiatives was revealed in the 1960s, Soyinka had "unusually close ties to the US government even to the point of frequently meeting with US intelligence in the late 1970s". When the book was published, Soyinka denied having been a CIA agent. Nigerian academic Adekeye Adebajo has argued in the *[Johannesburg Review of Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg_Review_of_Books "Johannesburg Review of Books")* that Davis does not directly accuse Soyinka of being a CIA agent and that Soyinka's denials are therefore also misdirected.

Soyinka is a vocal critic of President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump"). In October 2025, he said that the United States had revoked his visa after comments he made characterizing Trump as "[Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin "Idi Amin") in white face". He has also been banned from entering the US.

Singer [Adekunle Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adekunle_Gold "Adekunle Gold") became the first musician to sell out the Wole Soyinka Centre for Culture and the Creative Arts in Lagos on 26 December 2025.

The government of [Ogun State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogun_State "Ogun State") postponed the unveiling of Wole Soyinka Train Station scheduled formerly for 3 January 2026.

In 2025, a viral photo which circulated on social media was claimed to be Soyinka and a young [Gbenga Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gbenga_Daniel "Gbenga Daniel") standing behind him. The image, according to netizens, depicted 'loyalty'. However, the image has been labelled false by *Dubawa*, a fact-checking website.

On 9 December 2025, Soyinka criticised the large number of armed security personnel that he saw accompanying Seyi Tinubu, the son of President Tinubu.

## Views

### Religion

Soyinka is a critic of [organized religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_religion "Organized religion"), particularly [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") and [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), viewing them as human-created systems that can corrupt and limit potential, often leading to conflict and hypocrisy, though he acknowledges spirituality and uses [Yoruba mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_mythology "Yoruba mythology") (in this case, [Oriṣa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriṣa "Oriṣa") worship) as a creative source, seeing deities as "companions" rather than deities to be worshipped, embracing his African roots while rejecting imposed faiths. He identifies as an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist") but a "spirit-sensitive" one, valuing humanity as the highest religion and finding [African traditional religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_traditional_religions "African traditional religions") more authentic and less destructive than foreign ones.

Soyinka is also a critic of [religious extremism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_extremism "Religious extremism"). In a 2012 essay titled "Religion Against Humanity", published by *[Granta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granta "Granta")*, he labelled religion as the greatest enemy of humanity. He told *[Channels Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channels_Television "Channels Television")* that he dislikes how religion is used to justify violence, flout laws, and create societal problems in Nigeria, calling it the "number one problem for Nigerians".

## Legacy

### Influence

The Wole Soyinka Annual Lecture Series was founded in 1994 by the [National Association of Seadogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Seadogs "National Association of Seadogs"), dedicated "to honouring one of Nigeria and Africa's most outstanding and enduring literary icons: Wole Soyinka". In 2011, the African Heritage Research Library and Cultural Centre built a writers' enclave in Soyinka's honour at Adeyipo in [Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadan "Ibadan"), [Oyo State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyo_State "Oyo State").

In 2014, *Crucible of the Ages: Essays in Honour of Wole Soyinka at 80*, edited by [Ivor Agyeman-Duah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivor_Agyeman-Duah "Ivor Agyeman-Duah") and Ogochwuku Promise, was published by Bookcraft in Nigeria and Ayebia Clarke Publishing in the UK, with tributes and contributions from [Nadine Gordimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadine_Gordimer "Nadine Gordimer"), [Toni Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Morrison "Toni Morrison"), [Ama Ata Aidoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ama_Ata_Aidoo "Ama Ata Aidoo"), [Ngugi wa Thiong'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngugi_wa_Thiong'o "Ngugi wa Thiong'o"), [Henry Louis Gates Jr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Louis_Gates_Jr "Henry Louis Gates Jr"), [Margaret Busby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Busby "Margaret Busby"), [Kwame Anthony Appiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Anthony_Appiah "Kwame Anthony Appiah"), [Ali Mazrui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Mazrui "Ali Mazrui"), and [Sefi Atta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefi_Atta "Sefi Atta"). In 2018, Henry Louis Gates Jr tweeted that Nigerian filmmaker and writer [Onyeka Nwelue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onyeka_Nwelue "Onyeka Nwelue") visited him in Harvard and was making a documentary film about Soyinka.

In 2018, the [University of Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ibadan "University of Ibadan") renamed its arts theatre to Wole Soyinka Theatre. In commemoration of Nigeria's 65th Independence Day Anniversary on 1 October 2025, President [Bola Tinubu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bola_Tinubu "Bola Tinubu") renamed the [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre,_Nigeria "National Theatre, Nigeria") to Wole Soyinka Centre for Culture and Creative Arts.

### Honours

Soyinka won the [John Whiting Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Whiting_Award "John Whiting Award") in 1967 for his play *The Interpreters*. In 1986, he won the [Nobel Prize for Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Literature "Nobel Prize for Literature"), as well as the Agip Prize for Literature. He also received the [Commander of the Order of the Federal Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_Federal_Republic "Commander of the Order of the Federal Republic"), the second highest honour, by the military head of state [Ibrahim Babangida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Babangida "Ibrahim Babangida"). He won the [Anisfield-Wolf Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisfield-Wolf_Book_Award "Anisfield-Wolf Book Award") for *Aké: The Years of Childhood* in 1983 and in 2013. He won the Special Jury Prize category of the [Mondello Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondello_Prize "Mondello Prize") in 1990.

Soyinka was vested with the right to use the [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people "Yoruba people") title [Oloye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oba_(ruler)#Oloye "Oba (ruler)") as a pre-nominal [honorific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorific "Honorific"). At various times, he was given the [chieftaincy titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_chiefs "Nigerian chiefs") of the "Akogun" of Isara-Remo and the "Akinlatun" of Egbaland. He was honoured with the Golden Plate Award by the [Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement"), presented to him by the Awards Council member, [Archbishop Desmond Tutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Tutu "Desmond Tutu") at [St. George's Cathedral, Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George's_Cathedral,_Cape_Town "St. George's Cathedral, Cape Town"). In 2017, he received the Special Prize category of the [Europe Theatre Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_Theatre_Prize "Europe Theatre Prize") in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome"). In August 2024, the President of Cuba, [Miguel Diaz-Canel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Diaz-Canel "Miguel Diaz-Canel"), honoured Soyinka with the [Haydée Santamaría](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haydée_Santamaría "Haydée Santamaría") medal.
