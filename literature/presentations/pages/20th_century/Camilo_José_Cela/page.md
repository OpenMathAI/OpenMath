**Camilo José Cela y Trulock, 1st Marquess of Iria Flavia** (Spanish: [[kamilo xoˈse ˈθela]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Spanish "Help:IPA/Spanish"); 11 May 1916 – 17 January 2002) was a [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaniards "Spaniards") novelist, poet, story writer and essayist associated with the [Generation of '36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_of_'36 "Generation of '36") movement.

He was awarded the [1989 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1989 Nobel Prize in Literature") "for a rich and intensive prose, which with restrained compassion forms a challenging vision of man's vulnerability".

## Childhood and early career

Camilo José Cela was born in the rural parish of [Iria Flavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iria_Flavia "Iria Flavia"), in [Padrón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padrón "Padrón"), [A Coruña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_A_Coruña "Province of A Coruña"), Spain, on 11 May 1916. He was the oldest child of nine. His father, Camilo Crisanto Cela y Fernández, was [Galician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_people "Galician people"). His mother, Camila Emanuela Trulock y Bertorini, was a Galician of [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people "English people") and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians "Italians") ancestry. The family was upper-middle-class and Cela described his childhood as being "so happy it was hard to grow up."

He lived with his family in [Vigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigo "Vigo") from 1921 to 1925, when they moved to [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid "Madrid"). There, Cela studied at a [Piarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piarist "Piarist") school. In 1931 he was diagnosed with [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis") and admitted to the [sanatorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanatorium "Sanatorium") of [Guadarrama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadarrama "Guadarrama"), where he took advantage of his free time to work on his novel *[Pabellón de reposo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rest_Home_(novel) "Rest Home (novel)")*. While recovering from the illness Cela began intensively reading works by [José Ortega y Gasset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Ortega_y_Gasset "José Ortega y Gasset") and [Antonio de Solís y Ribadeneyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_de_Solís_y_Ribadeneyra "Antonio de Solís y Ribadeneyra").

The [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War") broke out in 1936 when Cela was 20 years old and just recovering from his illness. His political leanings were conservative and he escaped to the rebel zone. He enlisted as a soldier, and was wounded and hospitalized in [Logroño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logroño "Logroño").

## Career

The civil war ended in 1939; after the war, Cela became indecisive about his university studies and ended up working in a bureau of textile industries. It was here where he began to write what would become his first novel, *La familia de Pascual Duarte* (*[The Family of Pascual Duarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Family_of_Pascual_Duarte "The Family of Pascual Duarte")*), which was published when he was 26, in 1942. Pascual Duarte has trouble finding validity in conventional morality and commits a number of crimes, including murders, for which he feels nothing. The novel is of particular importance as it played a large part in shaping the direction of the post-[World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") Spanish novel.

Cela became a censor in [Francoist Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain "Francoist Spain") in 1943. Perhaps his best-known work was produced during a period where his own writing came under scrutiny from his fellow censors, including *[La colmena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hive_(Cela_novel) "The Hive (Cela novel)")* (*The Hive*) which was published in Buenos Aires in 1951. It was banned in Spain because of the perceived immorality of its erotic themes, and his name could no longer appear in the printed media. The novel portrays more than 300 characters in a style showing the influence of both Spanish [realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_realism "Literary realism") and contemporary English- and French-language authors. Cela's signature style—a sarcastic, often grotesque, form of realism—is epitomized in *La colmena*. Cela remained loyal to Francoist Spain, even working as an informer for the Spanish secret police by reporting on the activities of dissident groups and betraying fellow intellectuals.

From the late 1960s, with the publication of *[San Camilo, 1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Camilo,_1936 "San Camilo, 1936")*, Cela's work became increasingly experimental. In 1988 he wrote *[Christ Versus Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Versus_Arizona "Christ Versus Arizona")* ([Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language "Spanish language"): *Cristo versus Arizona*), which tells the story of the [Gunfight at the O.K. Corral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunfight_at_the_O.K._Corral "Gunfight at the O.K. Corral") in a single sentence that is more than one hundred pages long.

In his later years Cela became known for his scandalous outbursts; in an interview with [Mercedes Milá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes_Milá "Mercedes Milá") for Spanish state television he boasted of his ability to absorb litres of water via his anus while offering to demonstrate. Cela had already scandalized Spanish society with his *Diccionario secreto* (Secret Dictionary, 1969–1971), a dictionary of slang and taboo words. In 1998, Cela expressed discomfort towards the presence of [homosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_male_sexuality#Homosexuality "Human male sexuality") groups at the commemoration of [Federico García Lorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_García_Lorca "Federico García Lorca")'s centenary, stating that, "For me, I would prefer a more straightforward and less anecdotal commemoration without the support of gay groups. I have nothing against gays, I just do not take it up the ass".

## Distinctions

On 26 May 1957, Cela was appointed a member of the [Royal Spanish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Academy "Royal Spanish Academy") and given Seat Q. He was appointed Royal Senator in the [Constituent Cortes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Cortes "Constituent Cortes"), where he exerted some influence in the wording of the [Spanish Constitution of 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Constitution_of_1978 "Spanish Constitution of 1978"). In 1987, he was awarded the [Prince of Asturias Award for Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Asturias_Award_for_Literature "Prince of Asturias Award for Literature").

Cela was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature") in 1989 *"for a rich and intensive prose, which with restrained compassion forms a challenging vision of man's vulnerability"*.

In 1994, he was awarded the [Premio Planeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premio_Planeta "Premio Planeta"), although some question the objectivity of the awards, and winners on occasion have refused to accept it. Two years later, in recognition of his contributions to literature, Cela was ennobled on 17 May 1996 by [King Juan Carlos I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_I_of_Spain "Juan Carlos I of Spain"), who gave Cela the hereditary title of [Marquess of Iria Flavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquess_of_Iria_Flavia "Marquess of Iria Flavia") in the [nobility of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_nobility "Spanish nobility"). On his death the title passed to his son [Camilo José Cela Conde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camilo_José_Cela_Conde "Camilo José Cela Conde").

After winning the Nobel Prize Cela described the Spanish [Cervantes Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premio_Cervantes "Premio Cervantes") for lifetime achievement as a writer as being "covered with shit". In 1995 he was offered the prize, which he accepted.

## Death

Cela died from [heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_disease "Heart disease") on 17 January 2002 at the Hospital Centro in [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid "Madrid"), aged 85. He was buried in his hometown at the parish cemetery of *Santa María de Adina*.

Cela's will was contested because he favoured his widow and second younger wife, Marina Castaño, over his son [Camilo José Cela Conde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camilo_José_Cela_Conde "Camilo José Cela Conde") from his first marriage to Rosario Conde; Conde was awarded two-thirds of his father's estate.
