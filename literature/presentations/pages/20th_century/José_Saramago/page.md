**José de Sousa Saramago** (European Portuguese: [[ʒuˈzɛ ðɨ ˈso(w)zɐ sɐɾɐˈmaɣu]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Portuguese "Help:IPA/Portuguese"); 16 November 1922 – 18 June 2010) was a [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people "Portuguese people") writer. He was the recipient of the [1998 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1998 Nobel Prize in Literature") for his "parables sustained by imagination, compassion and irony [with which he] continually enables us once again to apprehend an elusory reality." His works, some of which can be seen as [allegories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegory "Allegory"), commonly present [subversive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subversion "Subversion") perspectives on historic events, emphasizing the [theopoetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theopoetics "Theopoetics") human factor.

In 2003 [Harold Bloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Bloom "Harold Bloom") described Saramago as "the most gifted novelist alive in the world today" and in 2010 said he considers Saramago to be "a permanent part of the [Western canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_canon "Western canon")", while [James Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wood_(critic) "James Wood (critic)") praises "the distinctive tone to his fiction because he narrates his novels as if he were someone both wise and ignorant."

More than two million copies of Saramago's books have been sold in Portugal alone and his work has been translated into 25 languages. A proponent of [libertarian communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_communism "Libertarian communism"), Saramago criticized institutions such as the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church"), the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") and the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund"). An [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism "Atheism"), he defended [love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love "Love") as an instrument to improve the [human condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_condition "Human condition"). In 1992, the [Government of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Portugal "Government of Portugal") under Prime Minister [Aníbal Cavaco Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aníbal_Cavaco_Silva "Aníbal Cavaco Silva") ordered the removal of one of his works, *[The Gospel According to Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gospel_According_to_Jesus_Christ "The Gospel According to Jesus Christ")*, from the [Aristeion Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristeion_Prize "Aristeion Prize")'s shortlist, claiming the work was religiously offensive. Feeling disheartened by what he perceived as political [censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship "Censorship") of his work, Saramago went into exile on the Spanish island of [Lanzarote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzarote "Lanzarote"), where he lived alongside his Spanish wife [Pilar del Río](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilar_del_Río "Pilar del Río") until his death in 2010.

Saramago was a founding member of the National Front for the Defense of Culture in Lisbon in 1992. In 2007, Saramago founded his namesake foundation, [José Saramago Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Saramago_Foundation "José Saramago Foundation"). Its institutional principles are to defend and spread the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights "Universal Declaration of Human Rights"), the promotion of [culture in Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_culture "Portuguese culture") as well as in all countries, and particular concerns about [environmentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism "Environmentalism").

## Biography

### Early and middle life

Saramago was born in 1922 into a family of very poor landless peasants in [Azinhaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azinhaga "Azinhaga"), Portugal, a small village in [Ribatejo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribatejo_Province "Ribatejo Province"), some one hundred kilometres northeast of [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon "Lisbon"). His parents were José de Sousa and Maria da Piedade. "Saramago", the Portuguese word for *[Raphanus raphanistrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphanus_raphanistrum "Raphanus raphanistrum")* (wild radish), was the insulting nickname given to his father, and was accidentally incorporated into his name by the village clerk upon registration of his birth.

In 1924, Saramago’s family relocated to Lisbon, where his father began working as a police officer. Several months after the move, his older brother Francisco died at the age of four. He spent vacations with his grandparents in Azinhaga. When his grandfather suffered a stroke and was to be taken to Lisbon for treatment, Saramago recalled, "He went into the yard of his house, where there were a few trees, fig-trees, olive trees. And he went one by one, embracing the trees and crying, saying goodbye to them because he knew he would not return. To see this, to live this, if that doesn't mark you for the rest of your life," Saramago said, "you have no feeling." Although Saramago was a good pupil, his parents were unable to afford to keep him in grammar school, and instead moved him to a technical school at age 12.

After graduating as a [lathe operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lathe "Lathe"), he worked as a car mechanic for two years. At this time Saramago had acquired a taste for reading and started to frequent a public library in Lisbon in his free time. He married [Ilda Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilda_Reis "Ilda Reis"), a typist and later artist, in 1944 (they divorced in 1970). Their only daughter, Violante, was born in 1947. By this time he was working in the Social Welfare Service as a civil servant. Later he worked at the publishing company *Estúdios Cor* as an editor and translator, and then as a journalist. By that time, in 1968, he met and became lover of writer [Isabel da Nóbrega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabel_da_Nóbrega "Isabel da Nóbrega"), the longtime partner of author and critic [João Gaspar Simões](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/João%20Gaspar%20Simões "pt:João Gaspar Simões"). Nóbrega became Saramago's devoted literary mentor, to whom he would later dedicate *Memorial do Convento* and *O Ano da Morte de Ricardo Reis*.

After the [democratic revolution in 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution "Carnation Revolution"), on 9 April 1975, during the rule of [Vasco Gonçalves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_Gonçalves "Vasco Gonçalves"), Saramago became the assistant director of the newspaper *[Diário de Notícias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diário_de_Notícias "Diário de Notícias")*, and the editorial line became clearly pro-communist. A group of 30 journalists – half the editorial staff – handed the board a petition calling for the editorial line to be revised and for it to be published. A plenary was called and, following an angry intervention by Saramago, 24 journalists were expelled, accused of being right-wingers. After the [Coup of 25 November 1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_of_25_November_1975 "Coup of 25 November 1975") that put an end to the communist [PREC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processo_Revolucionário_Em_Curso "Processo Revolucionário Em Curso"), Saramago, in turn, was fired from the newspaper.

Saramago published his first novel, *[Land of Sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Sin "Land of Sin")*, in 1947. It remained his only published literary work until a poetry book, *Possible Poems*, was published in 1966. It was followed by another book of poems, *Probably Joy*, in 1970, three collections of newspaper articles in 1971, 1973 and 1974 respectively, and the long poem *The Year of 1993* in 1975. A collection of political writing was published in 1976 under the title *Notes*. After his dismissal from *Diário de Notícias* in 1975, Saramago embraced his writing more seriously and in following years he published a series of important works including *Manual de Pintura e Caligrafia* (1977), *Objecto Quase* (1978), *Levantado do Chão* (1980) and *Viagem a Portugal* (1981).

### Later life and international acclaim

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/1999-Saramago_a_Siena.jpg/250px-1999-Saramago_a_Siena.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1999-Saramago_a_Siena.jpg)

José Saramago in 1999.

Saramago did not achieve widespread recognition and acclaim until he was sixty, with the publication of his fourth novel, *Memorial do Convento* (1982). A [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque "Baroque") tale set during the Inquisition in 18th-century Lisbon, it tells of the love between a maimed soldier and a young clairvoyant, and of a renegade priest's heretical dream of flight. The novel's translation in 1988 as *[Baltasar and Blimunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltasar_and_Blimunda "Baltasar and Blimunda")* (by [Giovanni Pontiero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Pontiero "Giovanni Pontiero")) brought Saramago to the attention of an international readership. This novel won the Portuguese PEN Club Award.

Following acclaimed novels such as *[The Year of the Death of Ricardo Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Year_of_the_Death_of_Ricardo_Reis "The Year of the Death of Ricardo Reis")* and *[The History of the Siege of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_History_of_the_Siege_of_Lisbon "The History of the Siege of Lisbon")*, Saramago was hailed by literary critics for his complex yet elegant style, his broad range of references and his wit.

For the former novel, Saramago received the British [Independent Foreign Fiction Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Foreign_Fiction_Prize "Independent Foreign Fiction Prize"). The multilayered *The History of the Siege of Lisbon* deals with the uncertainty of historical events and includes the story of a middle-aged isolated proofreader who falls in love with his boss. Saramago acknowledged that there is a lot of himself in the protagonist of the novel, and dedicated the novel to his wife.

In 1986 Saramago met a Spanish intellectual and journalist, [Pilar del Río](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilar_del_Río "Pilar del Río"), 27 years his junior, and he promptly ended his relationship with Isabel Nóbrega, his partner since 1968. They married in 1988 and remained together until his death in June 2010. Del Río is the official translator of Saramago's books into Spanish.

Saramago joined the [Portuguese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Communist_Party "Portuguese Communist Party") in 1969 and remained a member until the end of his life. He was a self-confessed [pessimist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pessimism "Pessimism"). His views aroused considerable controversy in Portugal, especially after the publication of *[The Gospel According to Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gospel_According_to_Jesus_Christ "The Gospel According to Jesus Christ")*. Members of the country's Catholic community were outraged by Saramago's representation of [Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus "Jesus") and particularly [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God "God") as fallible, even cruel human beings. Portugal's social-democratic government, led by then-prime minister [Aníbal Cavaco Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aníbal_Cavaco_Silva "Aníbal Cavaco Silva"), did not allow Saramago's work to compete for the [Aristeion Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristeion_Prize "Aristeion Prize"), arguing that it offended the Catholic community. As a result, Saramago and his wife moved to [Lanzarote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzarote "Lanzarote"), an island in the Canaries.

In 1998 Saramago was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature") with the prize notation: "who with parables sustained by imagination, compassion and irony continually enables us once again to apprehend an elusory reality."

Saramago was expected to speak as the guest of honour at the European Writers' Parliament in 2010, which was convened in Istanbul following a proposal he had co-authored. However, Saramago died before the event took place.

### Death and funeral

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Saramago.jpg/250px-Saramago.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Saramago.jpg)

"Thank you José Saramago", [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon "Lisbon"), October 2010

Saramago suffered from [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia"). He died on 18 June 2010, aged 87, having spent the last few years of his life in [Lanzarote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzarote "Lanzarote"), Spain. His family said that he had breakfast and chatted with his wife and translator Pilar del Río on Friday morning, after which he started feeling unwell and died. *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* described him as "the finest Portuguese writer of his generation", while Fernanda Eberstadt of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* said he was "known almost as much for his unfaltering [Communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism") as for his fiction".

Saramago's English language translator, [Margaret Jull Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Jull_Costa "Margaret Jull Costa"), paid tribute to his "wonderful imagination," calling him "the greatest contemporary Portuguese writer". Saramago continued his writing until his death. His most recent publication, *Claraboia*, was published posthumously in 2011. Saramago had suffered from [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia "Pneumonia") a year before his death. Assuming a full recovery, he was set to appear at the Edinburgh International Book Festival in August 2010.

Portugal declared two days of mourning. There were tributes from senior international politicians: [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiz_Inácio_Lula_da_Silva "Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva") (Brazil), [Bernard Kouchner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Kouchner "Bernard Kouchner") (France) and [José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Luis_Rodríguez_Zapatero "José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero") (Spain), while Cuba's [Raúl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raúl_Castro "Raúl Castro") and [Fidel Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Castro "Fidel Castro") sent flowers.

Saramago's funeral was held in Lisbon on 20 June 2010, in the presence of more than 20,000 people, many of whom had travelled hundreds of kilometres, but also notably in the absence of right-wing [President of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Portugal "President of Portugal") [Aníbal Cavaco Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aníbal_Cavaco_Silva "Aníbal Cavaco Silva"), who was holidaying in the [Azores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores "Azores") as the ceremony took place. Cavaco Silva, the Prime Minister who removed Saramago's work from the shortlist of the [Aristeion Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristeion_Prize "Aristeion Prize"), said he did not attend Saramago's funeral because he "had never had the privilege to know him". In an official press release, Cavaco Silva claimed having paid homage to the literary work of Saramago. Mourners, who questioned Cavaco Silva's absence in the presence of reporters, held copies of the red carnation, symbolic of [Portugal's democratic revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution "Carnation Revolution"). Saramago's cremation took place in Lisbon, and his ashes were buried on the anniversary of his death, 18 June 2011, underneath a hundred-year-old olive tree on the square in front of the José Saramago Foundation (Casa dos Bicos).

## Lost novel

The [José Saramago Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Saramago_Foundation "José Saramago Foundation") announced in October 2011 the publication of a "lost novel" published as *[Skylight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylight_(novel) "Skylight (novel)")* (*Claraboia* in Portuguese). It was written in the 1950s and remained in the archive of a publisher to whom the manuscript had been sent. Saramago remained silent about the work up to his death. The book has been translated into several languages.

## Style and themes

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Saramago_dos.jpg/250px-Saramago_dos.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Saramago_dos.jpg)

Saramago at the [Teatro Jorge Eliécer Gaitán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Jorge_Eliécer_Gaitán?action=edit&redlink=1 "Teatro Jorge Eliécer Gaitán") in [Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogotá "Bogotá") in 2007

Saramago's experimental style often features long sentences, at times more than a page long. He used full stops sparingly, choosing instead a loose flow of clauses joined by commas. Many of his paragraphs extend for pages without pausing for dialogue (which Saramago chooses not to delimit by quotation marks); when the speaker changes, Saramago capitalizes the first letter of the new speaker's clause. His works often refer to his other works. In his novel *Blindness*, Saramago completely abandons the use of proper nouns, instead referring to characters simply by some unique characteristic, an example of his style reflecting the recurring themes of identity and meaning found throughout his work.

Saramago's novels often deal with fantastic scenarios. In his 1986 novel *[The Stone Raft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stone_Raft "The Stone Raft")*, the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula "Iberian Peninsula") breaks off from the rest of Europe and sails around the Atlantic Ocean. In his 1995 novel *[Blindness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blindness_(novel) "Blindness (novel)")*, an entire unnamed country is stricken with a mysterious plague of "white blindness". In his 1984 novel *[The Year of the Death of Ricardo Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Year_of_the_Death_of_Ricardo_Reis "The Year of the Death of Ricardo Reis")* (which won the PEN Award and the *Independent* Foreign Fiction Award), [Fernando Pessoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Pessoa "Fernando Pessoa")'s [heteronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteronym_(literature) "Heteronym (literature)") survives for a year after the poet himself dies. Additionally, his novel *[Death with Interruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_with_Interruptions "Death with Interruptions")* (also translated as *Death at Intervals*) takes place in a country in which, suddenly, nobody dies, and concerns, in part, the spiritual and political implications of the event, although the book ultimately moves from a synoptic to a more personal perspective.

Saramago addresses serious matters with empathy for the [human condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_condition "Human condition") and for the isolation of contemporary urban life. His characters struggle with their need to connect with one another, form relations and bond as a community, and also with their need for individuality, and to find meaning and dignity outside of political and economic structures.

When asked to describe his daily writing routine in 2009, Saramago responded, "I write two pages. And then I read and read and read."

## Personal life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/Saramago_by_bottelho.jpg/250px-Saramago_by_bottelho.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Saramago_by_bottelho.jpg)

Saramago by Portuguese painter Carlos Botelho

Saramago was an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism "Atheism"). The [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") criticised him on numerous occasions due to the content of some of his novels, mainly *[The Gospel According to Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gospel_According_to_Jesus_Christ "The Gospel According to Jesus Christ")* and *[Cain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cain_(novel) "Cain (novel)")*, in which he uses [satire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satire "Satire") and biblical quotations to present the figure of God in a comical way.

The Portuguese government lambasted his 1991 novel *O Evangelho Segundo Jesus Cristo* (*The Gospel according to Jesus Christ*) and struck the writer's name from nominees for the European Literature Prize, saying the atheist work offended Portuguese Catholic convictions.

The book portrays a Christ who, subject to human desires, lives with Mary Magdalene and tries to back out of the crucifixion. Following the [Swedish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy "Swedish Academy")'s decision to present Saramago with the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature"), the Vatican questioned the decision on political grounds, though gave no comment on the aesthetic or literary components of Saramago's work. Saramago responded: "The Vatican is easily scandalized, especially by people from outside. They should just focus on their prayers and leave people in peace. I respect those who believe, but I have no respect for the institution."

Saramago was a member of the [Communist Party of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Portugal "Communist Party of Portugal"), and in his late years defined himself as a proponent of [libertarian communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_communism "Libertarian communism").

He ran in the 1989 Lisbon local election as part of the "Coalition For Lisbon," and was elected [alderman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alderman "Alderman") presiding officer of the [Municipal Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembleia_Municipal "Assembleia Municipal") of Lisbon. Saramago was also a candidate of the [Democratic Unity Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Unity_Coalition "Democratic Unity Coalition") in all elections of the [European Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament "European Parliament") from 1989 to 2009, though he ran for positions of which it was thought he had no possibility of winning. He was a critic of [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union") (EU) and [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund "International Monetary Fund") (IMF) policies.

Many of his novels are acknowledged as political satire of a subtle kind. In *The Notebook,* a collection of blog entries from September 2008 to August 2009, Saramago's political convictions are made the most clear. According to *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")*, "Saramago aims to cut through the web of 'organized lies' surrounding humanity, and to convince readers by delivering his opinions in a relentless series of unadorned, knock-down prose blows." His political engagement has led to comparisons with [George Orwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Orwell "George Orwell").

When speaking to *The Observer* in 2006, Saramago said he "believe[s] that we all have some influence, not because of the fact that one is an artist, but because we are citizens. As citizens, we all have an obligation to intervene and become involved, it's the citizen who changes things. I can't imagine myself outside any kind of social or political involvement."

During the [Second Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Intifada "Second Intifada"), while visiting [Ramallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramallah "Ramallah") in March 2002, Saramago said that "what is happening in Palestine is a crime we can put on the same plane as what happened at [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz "Auschwitz") ... A sense of impunity characterises the Israeli people and its army. They have turned into rentiers of the Holocaust." In an essay he wrote expanding on his views, Saramago wrote of Jews: "educated and trained in the idea that any suffering that has been inflicted . . . on everyone else . . . will always be inferior to that which they themselves suffered in the Holocaust, the Jews endlessly scratch their own wound to keep it bleeding, to make it incurable, and they show it to the world as if it were a banner." Critics of these statements charged that they were [antisemitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism"). Six months later, Saramago clarified. "To have said that Israel's action is to be condemned, that war crimes are being perpetrated – really the Israelis are used to that. It doesn't bother them. But there are certain words they can't stand. And to say 'Auschwitz' there ... note well, I didn't say that Ramallah was the same as Auschwitz, that would be stupid. What I said was that the spirit of Auschwitz was present in Ramallah. We were eight writers. They all made condemning statements, [Wole Soyinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wole_Soyinka "Wole Soyinka"), [Breyten Breytenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breyten_Breytenbach "Breyten Breytenbach"), [Vincenzo Consolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Consolo "Vincenzo Consolo") and others. But the Israelis weren't bothered about those. It was the fact that I put my finger in the Auschwitz wound that made them jump."

During the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War "2006 Lebanon War"), Saramago joined [Tariq Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq_Ali "Tariq Ali"), [John Berger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Berger "John Berger"), [Noam Chomsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Chomsky "Noam Chomsky"), and others in condemning what they characterized as "a long-term military, economic and geographic practice whose political aim is nothing less than the liquidation of the Palestinian nation".

He was also a supporter of [Iberian Federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Federalism "Iberian Federalism"). In a 2008 press conference for the filming of *Blindness* he asked, in reference to the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession "Great Recession"), "Where was all that money poured on markets? Very tight and well kept; then suddenly it appears to save what? lives? no, banks." He added, "Marx was never so right as now", and predicted "the worst is still to come."

### Nobel Prize in Literature

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Ivo_Andri%C4%87_and_Jos%C3%A9_Saramago_2022_stamp_of_Serbia.jpg/250px-Ivo_Andri%C4%87_and_Jos%C3%A9_Saramago_2022_stamp_of_Serbia.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ivo_Andrić_and_José_Saramago_2022_stamp_of_Serbia.jpg)

José Saramago (right) and the [1961 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1961 Nobel Prize in Literature") laureate [Ivo Andrić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivo_Andrić "Ivo Andrić") pictured on a 2022 Serbian stamp.

The [Swedish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy "Swedish Academy") selected Saramago as the 1998 recipient of the [Nobel Prize for Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Literature "Nobel Prize for Literature"). The announcement came when he was about to fly out of Germany after the Frankfurt Book Fair, and caught both him and his editor by surprise. The Nobel committee praised his "parables sustained by imagination, compassion and irony", and his "modern skepticism" about official truths. The choice of Saramago was generally well received internationally, but was heavily criticized by the bourgeois press in his home country and also by the [Vatican City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_City "Vatican City") who questioned the decision on political grounds and called it "yet another ideologically slanted award."

At the award ceremony in Stockholm on 10 December 1998, [Kjell Espmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kjell_Espmark "Kjell Espmark") of the [Swedish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy "Swedish Academy") described Saramago's writing as:

> literature characterised at one and the same time by sagacious reflection and by insight into the limitations of sagacity, by the fantastic and by precise realism, by cautious empathy and by critical acuity, by warmth and by irony. This is Saramago’s unique amalgam.

In 2024, Saramago's widow Pilar del Rio and the [José Saramago Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Saramago_Foundation "José Saramago Foundation") donated a number of Saramago's belongings to the [Nobel Prize Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_Museum "Nobel Prize Museum") in Stockholm, including a pair of his glasses, a stone found in [Lanzarote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzarote "Lanzarote") he kept at his home, and a manuscript written in his youth.

## The José Saramago Foundation

The [José Saramago Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Saramago_Foundation "José Saramago Foundation") was founded by José Saramago in June 2007, with the aim to defend and spread the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights "Universal Declaration of Human Rights"), the promotion of [culture in Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_culture "Portuguese culture") just like in all the countries, and [protection of the environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism "Environmentalism"). The José Saramago Foundation is located in the historic [Casa dos Bicos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_dos_Bicos "Casa dos Bicos") in the city of [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon "Lisbon").
