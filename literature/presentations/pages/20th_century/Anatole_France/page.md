**Anatole France** (French: [[anatɔl fʁɑ̃s]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born **François-Anatole Thibault** [[frɑ̃swa anatɔl tibo]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 16 April 1844 – 12 October 1924) was a French poet, journalist, and novelist. Several of his books were best-sellers. Ironic and skeptical, he was considered in his day the ideal French [man of letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_of_letters "Man of letters"). He was a member of the [Académie Française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_Française "Académie Française"), and won the [1921 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1921_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1921 Nobel Prize in Literature") "in recognition of his brilliant literary achievements, characterized as they are by a nobility of style, a profound human [sympathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympathy "Sympathy"), grace, and a true [Gallic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people "French people") temperament".

France is also widely believed to be the model for Bergotte, the literary idol of the narrator Marcel in [Marcel Proust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Proust "Marcel Proust")'s *[In Search of Lost Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Search_of_Lost_Time "In Search of Lost Time")*.

## Early years

The son of a bookseller, France, a [bibliophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliophile "Bibliophile"), spent most of his life around books. His father's bookstore specialised in books and papers on the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution") and was frequented by many writers and scholars. France studied at the [Collège Stanislas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_Stanislas_de_Paris "Collège Stanislas de Paris"), a private Catholic school, and after graduation he helped his father by working in his bookstore. After several years, he secured the position of cataloguer at Bacheline-Deflorenne and at Lemerre. In 1876, he was appointed librarian for the [French Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_(France) "Senate (France)").

## Literary career

France began his literary career as a poet and a journalist. In 1869, *[Le Parnasse contemporain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Parnasse_contemporain "Le Parnasse contemporain")* published one of his poems, "La Part de Madeleine". In 1875, he sat on the committee in charge of the third *Parnasse contemporain* compilation. As a journalist, from 1867, he wrote many articles and notices. He became known with the novel **Le Crime de Sylvestre Bonnard** (1881). Its [protagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protagonist "Protagonist"), [sceptical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sceptical "Sceptical") old scholar Sylvester Bonnard, embodied France's own personality. The novel was praised for its elegant [prose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prose "Prose") and won him a prize from the Académie Française.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ad/Anatole_France_house%2C_Villa_Said.jpg/250px-Anatole_France_house%2C_Villa_Said.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Anatole_France_house,_Villa_Said.jpg)

France's home, 5 [villa Saïd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Saïd "Villa Saïd"), 1894–1924

In **[La Rôtisserie de la Reine Pédauque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At_the_Sign_of_the_Reine_Pédauque "At the Sign of the Reine Pédauque")** (1893) France ridiculed belief in the [occult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occult "Occult"), and in **Les Opinions de Jérôme Coignard** (1893), France captured the atmosphere of the **[fin de siècle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fin_de_siècle "Fin de siècle")**. He was elected to the Académie Française in 1896.

France took part in the [Dreyfus affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreyfus_affair "Dreyfus affair"). He signed [Émile Zola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émile_Zola "Émile Zola")'s [manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifesto "Manifesto") supporting [Alfred Dreyfus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Dreyfus "Alfred Dreyfus"), a Jewish army officer who had been falsely convicted of [espionage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espionage "Espionage"). France wrote about the affair in his 1901 novel *Monsieur Bergeret*.

France's later works include *[Penguin Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin_Island_(novel) "Penguin Island (novel)")* (**L'Île des Pingouins**, 1908) which satirises [human nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_nature "Human nature") by depicting the transformation of penguins into humans – after the birds have been baptized by mistake by the almost-blind Abbot Mael. It is a satirical [history of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_France "History of France"), starting in medieval times, going on to the author's own time with special attention to the Dreyfus affair and concluding with a [dystopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopia "Dystopia") future. *[The Gods Are Athirst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gods_Are_Athirst "The Gods Are Athirst")* (**Les dieux ont soif**, 1912) is a novel, set in Paris during the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution"), about a true-believing follower of [Maximilien Robespierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilien_Robespierre "Maximilien Robespierre") and his contribution to the bloody events of the [Reign of Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reign_of_Terror "Reign of Terror") of 1793–94. It is a warning against political and [ideological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideological "Ideological") [fanaticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanatic "Fanatic") and explores various other [philosophical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical "Philosophical") approaches to the events of the time. *[The Revolt of the Angels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Revolt_of_the_Angels "The Revolt of the Angels") (*La Révolte des Anges**, 1914) is often considered France's most profound and ironic novel. Loosely based on the Christian understanding of the [War in Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Heaven "War in Heaven"), it tells the story of Arcade, the [guardian angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian_angel "Guardian angel") of Maurice d'Esparvieu. Bored because Bishop d'Esparvieu is sinless, Arcade begins reading the bishop's books on [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology "Theology") and becomes an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist"). He moves to Paris, meets a woman, falls in love, and loses his virginity causing his wings to fall off, joins the [revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary "Revolutionary") movement of fallen angels, and meets the Devil, who realises that if he overthrew God, he would become just like God. Arcade realises that replacing God with another is meaningless unless "in ourselves and in ourselves alone we attack and destroy [Ialdabaoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaldabaoth "Yaldabaoth")." "Ialdabaoth", according to France, is God's secret name and means "the child who wanders".

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Anatole_France_1921.jpg/250px-Anatole_France_1921.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Anatole_France_1921.jpg)

France c. 1921

He was awarded the Nobel Prize in 1921. He died on 12 October 1924 and is buried in the [Neuilly-sur-Seine Old Communal Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuilly-sur-Seine_community_cemetery "Neuilly-sur-Seine community cemetery") near Paris.

On 31 May 1922, France's entire works were put on the *[Index Librorum Prohibitorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_Librorum_Prohibitorum "Index Librorum Prohibitorum")* ("List of Prohibited Books") of the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church"). He regarded this as a "distinction". This Index was abolished in 1966.

## Personal life

In 1877, France married Valérie Guérin de Sauville, a granddaughter of [Jean-Urbain Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Urbain_Guérin "Jean-Urbain Guérin"), a [miniaturist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_miniature "Portrait miniature") who painted [Louis XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XVI "Louis XVI"). Their daughter Suzanne was born in 1881 (and died in 1918).

France's relations with women were always turbulent, and in 1888 he began a relationship with [Madame Arman de Caillavet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Léontine_Lippmann "Léontine Lippmann"), who conducted a celebrated [literary salon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_salon "Literary salon") of the [Third Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Republic_of_France "Third Republic of France"). The affair lasted until shortly before her death in 1910.

After his divorce, in 1893, France had many liaisons, notably with an American, Laura Gagey, who committed suicide in 1911 after he abandoned her.

In 1920, France married for the second time, his housekeeper Emma Laprévotte.

France had [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist "Socialist") sympathies and was an outspoken supporter of the 1917 [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution "Russian Revolution"). In 1920, he gave his support to the newly founded [French Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Communist_Party "French Communist Party"). In his book *[The Red Lily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Red_Lily_(novel) "The Red Lily (novel)")*, France famously wrote, "The law, in its majestic equality, forbids rich and poor alike to sleep under bridges, to beg in the streets, and to steal loaves of bread."

## Reputation

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/AM011-15.jpg/250px-AM011-15.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:AM011-15.jpg)

Anatole France on a postage stamp of Armenia, 2015

The English writer [George Orwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Orwell "George Orwell") defended France and declared that his work remained very readable, and that "it is unquestionable that he was attacked partly from political motives".
