**Maria Wisława Anna Szymborska** (Polish: [[viˈswava ʂɨmˈbɔrska]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Polish "Help:IPA/Polish"); 2 July 1923 – 1 February 2012) was a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people "Polish people") poet, essayist, translator, and recipient of the 1996 [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature"). Born in Prowent (now part of [Kórnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kórnik "Kórnik") in west-central Poland), she resided in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków") until the end of her life. In Poland, Szymborska's books have reached sales rivaling prominent prose authors, though she wrote in a poem, "Some Like Poetry" ("Niektórzy lubią poezję"), that "perhaps" two in a thousand people like poetry.

Szymborska was awarded the [1996 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1996 Nobel Prize in Literature") "for poetry that with ironic precision allows the historical and biological context to come to light in fragments of human reality". She became better known internationally as a result. Her work has been translated into many [European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_language "European language"), as well as into [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"), [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language "Hebrew language"), [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language") and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language").

## Life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Dawna_oficyna_folwarczna_z_ok._po%C5%82._XVIII_w.%2C_ul._Prowent_4.jpg/330px-Dawna_oficyna_folwarczna_z_ok._po%C5%82._XVIII_w.%2C_ul._Prowent_4.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dawna_oficyna_folwarczna_z_ok._poł._XVIII_w.,_ul._Prowent_4.jpg)

The house where Wisława Szymborska was born, in Prowent, now part of [Kórnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kórnik "Kórnik"), Poland

Wisława Szymborska was born on 2 July 1923 in [Prowent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prowent "Prowent"), the second daughter of Wincenty Szymborski and Anna (née Rottermund) Szymborska. Her father was, at that time, the [steward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steward_(office) "Steward (office)") of Count [Władysław Zamoyski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Władysław_Zamoyski "Władysław Zamoyski"), a Polish patriot and charitable [patron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronage "Patronage"). After Zamoyski's death in 1924, her family moved to [Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toruń "Toruń"), and in 1931 to [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków"), where she lived and worked until her death in early 2012.

When [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") broke out in 1939, she continued her education in [underground classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Poland_during_World_War_II "Education in Poland during World War II"). From 1943, she worked as a railroad employee and managed to avoid being deported to Germany as a [forced labourer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labor_in_Nazi_Germany "Forced labor in Nazi Germany"). During this time, her career as an artist began, with illustrations for an English-language textbook. She also began writing stories and [occasional poems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occasional_poems "Occasional poems"). In 1945, she began studying Polish literature before switching to sociology at [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University "Jagiellonian University") in Kraków. There, she became involved in the local writing scene, and met and was influenced by [Czesław Miłosz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czesław_Miłosz "Czesław Miłosz"). In March 1945, she published her first poem, "Szukam słowa" ("Looking for words"), in the daily newspaper *[Dziennik Polski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dziennik_Polski "Dziennik Polski")*. Her poems continued to be published in various newspapers and periodicals for a number of years. In 1948, she quit her studies without a degree, due to poor financial circumstances; the same year, she married poet [Adam Włodek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Włodek "Adam Włodek"), whom she divorced in 1954. They remained close until Włodek's death in 1986. Their union was childless. Around the time of her marriage, she was working as a secretary for an educational biweekly magazine as well as an illustrator. Her first book was to be published in 1949, but did not pass censorship as it "did not meet socialist requirements".

Szymborska adhered to the [People's Republic of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Poland "People's Republic of Poland")'s (PRL) [official ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinism_in_Poland "Stalinism in Poland") early in her career. For example, during the [Polish anti-religious campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_anti-religious_campaign "Polish anti-religious campaign"), she signed an infamous 1953 political petition condemning [Polish priests accused of treason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinist_show_trial_of_the_Kraków_Curia "Stalinist show trial of the Kraków Curia") in a [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków") [show trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Show_trial "Show trial"). Her early work supported socialist themes, as seen in her debut collection *Dlatego żyjemy* (*That is what we are living for*), containing the poems "Lenin" and "Młodzieży budującej Nową Hutę" ("For the Youth who are building [Nowa Huta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowa_Huta "Nowa Huta")"), about the construction of a Stalinist industrial town near Kraków. She became a member of the ruling [Polish United Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_United_Workers'_Party "Polish United Workers' Party").

Although initially close to the official party line, as the Polish Communist Party shifted from the Stalinist communists to "national" communists, Szymborska grew estranged from socialist ideology and renounced her earlier political work. Although she did not officially leave the Communist party until 1966, she began to establish contacts with dissident intellectuals. As early as 1957, she befriended [Jerzy Giedroyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Giedroyc "Jerzy Giedroyc"), the editor of the influential Paris-based émigré journal *[Kultura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kultura "Kultura")*, to which she contributed. In 1964, she opposed a Communist-backed protest to *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")* against independent intellectuals, demanding freedom of speech instead.

In 1953, Szymborska joined the staff of the literary review magazine *Życie Literackie* (*Literary Life*), where she continued to work until 1981 and from 1968 had a book review column, *Lektury Nadobowiązkowe*. Many of her essays from this period were later published in book form. From 1981 to 1983, she was an editor of the Kraków-based monthly periodical *NaGlos* (*OutLoud*). In the 1980s, she intensified her oppositional activities, contributing to the [samizdat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samizdat "Samizdat") periodical *Arka* under the pseudonym "Stańczykówna", as well as to *Kultura*. In the early 1990s, with a poem published in *[Gazeta Wyborcza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazeta_Wyborcza "Gazeta Wyborcza")*, she supported the vote of no confidence in the first non-Communist government that brought former Communists back to power. The last collection published while Szymborska was still alive, *Dwukropek*, was chosen as the best book of 2006 by readers of Poland's *[Gazeta Wyborcza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazeta_Wyborcza "Gazeta Wyborcza")*. She also translated [French literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_literature "French literature") into Polish, in particular [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque "Baroque") poetry and the works of [Agrippa d'Aubigné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrippa_d'Aubigné "Agrippa d'Aubigné"), a [Huguenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot "Huguenot") soldier-poet during the [French Wars of Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Wars_of_Religion "French Wars of Religion"). In the [Germanosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanosphere "Germanosphere"), Szymborska is closely associated with [Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łódź "Łódź")-born [literary translator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_translator "Literary translator") [Karl Dedecius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Dedecius "Karl Dedecius"), who did much to popularize postwar [Polish literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_literature "Polish literature") there.

From 1967 she was in a relationship with [Kornel Filipowicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kornel_Filipowicz "Kornel Filipowicz").

## Death and last works

Surrounded by friends and relatives, Szymborska died peacefully of [lung cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_cancer "Lung cancer") in her sleep at home in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków") in 2012, aged 88. She was working on new poetry at the time of her death, but was unable to arrange her final poems for publication in the way she wanted. Her last poetry was published later in 2012. In 2013, the [Wisława Szymborska Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisława_Szymborska_Award "Wisława Szymborska Award") was established in honour of her legacy.

In 2024, the [Wisława Szymborska Foundation](https://www.szymborska.org.pl/) president Michał Rusinek signed an agreement with [Polskie Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polskie_Radio "Polskie Radio")'s OFF Radio Krakow for the rights to use her voice recordings for [generated speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_AI#Audio "Generative AI") to be used for an interview-like programme. The programme, broadcast on 29 October that year, was swiftly condemned by both Polish audiences and media producers.

## Themes

Szymborska frequently employed literary devices such as ironic precision, [paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradox_(literature) "Paradox (literature)"), contradiction, and understatement to illuminate philosophical themes and obsessions. Many of her poems feature war and terrorism.
She wrote from unusual points of view, such as a [cat in the newly empty apartment of its dead owner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_in_an_Empty_Apartment "Cat in an Empty Apartment"). Her reputation rests on a relatively small body of work, fewer than 350 poems. When asked why she had published so few poems, she said, "I have a trash can in my home".

## Pop culture

Szymborska's poem "Buffo" was set to music by [Barbara Maria Zakrzewska-Nikiporczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Maria_Zakrzewska-Nikiporczyk "Barbara Maria Zakrzewska-Nikiporczyk") in 1985.

Her poem "Love at First Sight" was used in the film *[Turn Left, Turn Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_Left,_Turn_Right "Turn Left, Turn Right")*, starring [Takeshi Kaneshiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeshi_Kaneshiro "Takeshi Kaneshiro") and [Gigi Leung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigi_Leung "Gigi Leung"). [Krzysztof Kieślowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzysztof_Kieślowski "Krzysztof Kieślowski")'s film *[Three Colors: Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Colors:_Red "Three Colors: Red")* was also inspired by "Love at First Sight".

In her last year, Szymborska collaborated with Polish jazz trumpeter [Tomasz Stańko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomasz_Stańko "Tomasz Stańko"), who dedicated his record *Wisława* ([ECM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECM_Records "ECM Records"), 2013) to her memory, taking inspiration from their collaboration and her poetry.

Szymborska's poem "People on the Bridge" was made into a film by [Beata Poźniak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beata_Poźniak "Beata Poźniak"). It was shown worldwide and at a New Delhi film festival. As an award, it was screened 36 more times in 18 Indian cities.

The poem *Nothing Twice* (*Nic dwa razy*) has inspired multiple musical adaptations, including [Łucja Prus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łucja_Prus "Łucja Prus")'s performance at the 1965 Sopot International Song Festival and [Maanam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maanam "Maanam")'s song *Nic dwa razy*, from their 1994 album *Róża*. In 2022, [Sanah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanah_(singer) "Sanah (singer)") adapted the poem into a song as part of her project based on Polish poetry, *Sanah śpiewa Poezyje*.
