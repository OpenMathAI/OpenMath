**Mikhail Aleksandrovich Sholokhov** (Russian: Михаил Александрович Шолохов, IPA: [[ˈʂoləxəf]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Russian "Help:IPA/Russian"); 24 May [[O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style "Old Style") 11 May] 1905 – 21 February 1984) was a Russian [novelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novelist "Novelist") and winner of the [1965 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1965 Nobel Prize in Literature"). He is known for writing about life and fate of [Don Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Cossacks "Don Cossacks") during the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution "Russian Revolution"), the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War "Russian Civil War") and the period of [collectivization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_collectivization "Soviet collectivization"), primarily in his most famous novel, *[And Quiet Flows the Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/And_Quiet_Flows_the_Don "And Quiet Flows the Don")*.

## Early life and education

Sholokhov was born in the Russian Empire, in the "land of the [Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks "Cossacks")" – the Kruzhilin hamlet, part of [stanitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanitsa "Stanitsa") [Vyoshenskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyoshenskaya "Vyoshenskaya"), in the former [Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Host_Oblast "Don Host Oblast") of the [Don Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Cossack "Don Cossack") Host. His father, a Russian, Aleksander Mikhailovich Sholokhov (1865–1925), was a member of the lower middle class, at various times a farmer, a cattle trader, and a miller. Sholokhov's mother, Anastasia Danilovna Chernikova (1871–1942), the widow of a Cossack, came from [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians "Ukrainians") [peasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peasant "Peasant") stock (her father was a peasant in the [Chernihiv oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_oblast "Chernihiv oblast")). She did not become literate until a time in her life when she wanted to correspond with her son. His family were not Don Cossacks, but *inogorodnye* ("outlanders"), the rather disparaging term used by the Don Cossacks for outsiders who settled in their territory by the banks of the [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_(river) "Don (river)"). The *inogorodnye* tended to be much poorer than the Don Cossacks and were excluded from voting for officials in the Host government (the Don Cossack Host were allowed to elect their leaders, except for the *[ataman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ataman "Ataman")* who headed the Host, who was always appointed by the Emperor).

Sholokhov attended schools in [Karginskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karginskaya?action=edit&redlink=1 "Karginskaya"), [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow"), [Boguchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boguchar "Boguchar"), and Veshenskaya until 1918, when he joined the [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks "Bolsheviks") side in the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War "Russian Civil War") at the age of 13. He spent the next few years fighting. During the Russian Civil War, the *inogorodnye* tended to support the Reds while the Don Cossacks tended to support the Whites.

## Career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/61/Sholoh_zen.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sholoh_zen.jpg)

Mikhail Sholokhov and his wife, 1924

Sholokhov began writing at 17. He completed his first literary work, the short story "The Birthmark", at 19. In 1922 Sholokhov moved to Moscow to become a journalist, but had to support himself through manual labour. He was a [stevedore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevedore "Stevedore"), a [stonemason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonemason "Stonemason"), and an [accountant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accountant "Accountant") from 1922 to 1924, but he also intermittently participated in writers' "seminars". His first published work was a satirical article, *The Test* (19 October 1923). In 1924 Sholokhov returned to Veshenskaya and devoted himself entirely to writing. In the same year he married Maria Petrovna Gromoslavskaia (1901–1992), the daughter of Pyotr Gromoslavsky, the *ataman* of [Bukanovskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukanovskaya "Bukanovskaya") village. They had two daughters and two sons.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/%D0%9F%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%82_%D0%9C._%D0%A8%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BE%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0._%D0%92.%D0%9A%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%B8%D0%BD._1938_-_1939_%D0%B3%D0%B3.jpg/250px-%D0%9F%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%82_%D0%9C._%D0%A8%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BE%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0._%D0%92.%D0%9A%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%B8%D0%BD._1938_-_1939_%D0%B3%D0%B3.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Портрет_М._Шолохова._В.Ковригин._1938_-_1939_гг.jpg)

Sholokhov in the late 1930s

Sholokhov's first book was *Tales from the Don*, a collection of stories largely based on his personal experiences in his native region during World War I and the Russian Civil War; it was published in 1926. The story "Nakhalyonok", partly based on his own childhood, was later made into a popular film.

In the same year, Sholokhov began writing *[And Quiet Flows the Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/And_Quiet_Flows_the_Don "And Quiet Flows the Don")*, which took him fourteen years to complete (1926–1940). It earned him the 1941 [State Stalin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Stalin_Prize "State Stalin Prize"). It deals with the experiences of the Cossacks before and during World War I and the Russian Civil War.

Writers Mikhail Sholokhov (in the drivers seat) and [Alexander Fadeyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Fadeyev_(writer) "Alexander Fadeyev (writer)") (in the seat of the turret gunner). Eastern Front, September 1941

Another novel, *Virgin Soil Upturned*, which earned a [Lenin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin_Prize "Lenin Prize"), took 28 years to complete. It is composed of two parts, *Seeds of Tomorrow* (1932) and *Harvest on the Don* (1960), and reflects life during collectivization in the Don area. It was heralded as a powerful example of [socialist realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_realism "Socialist realism"). *Virgin Soil Upturned* was translated and widely read in China, where it influenced China's socialist literature.

The short story "[The Fate of a Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fate_of_a_Man_(short_story) "Fate of a Man (short story)")" (1957) was made into [a popular Russian film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fate_of_a_Man_(film) "Fate of a Man (film)"). During World War II, Sholokhov wrote about the Soviet war effort for various journals. He also covered the devastation caused by [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht "Wehrmacht") troops along the Don. His mother was killed when Veshenskaya was bombed in 1942.

Sholokhov's unfinished novel *They Fought for Their Country* is about World War II (known in the Soviet Union, and now in Russia, as the [Great Patriotic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Patriotic_War "Great Patriotic War")). The book is the source for [a 1975 film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/They_Fought_for_Their_Country "They Fought for Their Country").

Sholokhov's collected works were published in eight volumes between 1956 and 1960, and he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature") in 1965.

## Authorship of texts

First rumors of Sholokhov's supposed plagiarism appeared in 1928 following the success of the first two volumes of *[And Quiet Flows the Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/And_Quiet_Flows_the_Don "And Quiet Flows the Don")*: it was speculated that the author stole the manuscript from a dead [White Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Army "White Army") officer. Sholokhov asked the *[Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravda "Pravda")* newspaper to prove his authorship, submitting his manuscripts of the first three volumes of *And Quiet Flows the Don* and the plan of the fourth one. In 1929 a special commission was formed that accepted Sholokhov's authorship. In the conclusion signed by four experts, the commission stated that there was no evidence of plagiarism on the one hand, and on the other hand the manuscripts' style was close to that of Sholokhov's previous book, *Tales from the Don*.

The allegations resurfaced in the 1960s with [Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Solzhenitsyn "Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn") as a notable proponent, possibly in retaliation for Sholokhov's scathing opinion of Solzhenitsyn's novella *[One Day in the Life of Ivan Denisovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_in_the_Life_of_Ivan_Denisovich "One Day in the Life of Ivan Denisovich")*. Several other writers have been proposed as the 'original' author, although [Fyodor Kryukov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Kryukov "Fyodor Kryukov"), a [Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack "Cossack") and [Anti-Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Communist "Anti-Communist") who had died in 1920 has emerged as the leading candidate.

In 1984 Norwegian Slavicist and mathematician [Geir Kjetsaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geir_Kjetsaa "Geir Kjetsaa"), in a monograph written with three other colleagues, provided statistical analyses of sentence lengths showing that Mikhail Sholokhov was likely the true author of *And Quiet Flows the Don*.

The debate focused on the published book, because Sholokhov's archive was destroyed in a bomb raid during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War") and no manuscript material or drafts were known. 143 pages of the manuscript of the 3rd and 4th books were later found and returned to Sholokhov; since 1975, they have been held by the Pushkin House in St Petersburg. Then, in 1987, several hundred pages of notes and drafts of the work were discovered, including chapters excluded from the final draft. The writing paper dates back to the 1920s: 605 pages are in Sholokhov's own hand, and 285 are transcribed by his wife, Maria, and sisters. Sholokhov had had his friend Vassily Kudashov look after it, and after he was killed at war his widow took possession of the manuscript, but she never disclosed it. The manuscript was finally obtained by the [Institute of World Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_World_Literature_(Moscow)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Institute of World Literature (Moscow)") of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences") in 1999 with assistance from the Russian government.

In 1999 the [Russian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Science "Russian Academy of Science") carried out an analysis of the manuscript and came to the conclusion that *And Quiet Flows the Don* had been written by Sholokhov himself. A lengthy analysis by Felix Kuznetsov of the creative process visible in the papers provides detailed support for Sholokhov's authorship.

During the 2000s a Russian-Israeli linguist [Zeev Bar-Sella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeev_Bar-Sella?action=edit&redlink=1 "Zeev Bar-Sella") once again stated that Sholokhov was not the true author of *And Quiet Flows the Don* as well as the other works attributed to him. Based on his own textual analysis of the novel he asserts that the manuscripts were written by Sholokhov not earlier than 1929 and names the writer [Viktor Sevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Sevsky?action=edit&redlink=1 "Viktor Sevsky") (real name [Veniamin Krasnushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veniamin_Krasnushkin?action=edit&redlink=1 "Veniamin Krasnushkin")) as the true author.

## Political and social activity

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Mikhail_Sholokhov_and_Nikolai_Belokhvostikov_1965.jpg/250px-Mikhail_Sholokhov_and_Nikolai_Belokhvostikov_1965.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mikhail_Sholokhov_and_Nikolai_Belokhvostikov_1965.jpg)

Sholokhov (left) with the Soviet ambassador Nikolai Belokhvostikov at the Nobel Prize ceremonies in 1965

Sholokhov met [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin "Joseph Stalin") in 1930, and subsequently was one of very few people who chose to give the leader a truthful account of what was happening in the country and nonetheless was not punished. In the 1930s, he wrote several letters to Stalin from his home in [Veshenskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veshenskaya "Veshenskaya") about the appalling conditions in the *[kolkhozes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkhoz "Kolkhoz")* and *[sovkhozes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovkhoz "Sovkhoz")* along the Don, requesting assistance for the farmers. In January 1931, he warned: "Comrade Stalin, without exaggeration, conditions are catastrophic!" On 4 April 1933, he sent a long letter in which, among many other details, he named two OGPU officers whom he accused of torturing prisoners from his district. Stalin reacted by sending a senior official, [Matvei Shkiryatov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matvei_Shkiryatov "Matvei Shkiryatov"), to investigate. The two officers were arrested and sentenced to death; their sentences were later revoked, but they were banned from working in Sholokhov's home village. Stalin also arranged for extra food to be sent to Veshenskaya and the neighbouring district.

Sholokhov joined the [CPSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPSU "CPSU") in 1932, and in 1937 he [was elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_Soviet_Union_legislative_election "1937 Soviet Union legislative election") to the [Supreme Soviet of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Soviet_of_the_Soviet_Union "Supreme Soviet of the Soviet Union"). In August 1937, his best friend, the secretary of the Veshenskaya party committee, P.K. Lugovoi, was arrested. Sholokohov was due to take part in an international writers' conference, but he refused to leave the country while Lugovoi was being held. Stalin sent another official, [Vladimir Stavsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Stavsky "Vladimir Stavsky"), to investigate, and invited Sholokhov to visit him in the Kremlin. After their meeting, on 4 November 1937, Lugovoi and two other prisoners on whose behalf Sholokhov had interceded were released, but in a subsequent letter to Stalin, he complained that the people responsible for wrongfully arresting them had not been punished.

On a visit to Moscow in 1938, Sholokhov met [Yevgenia Yezhova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevgenia_Feigenberg "Yevgenia Feigenberg"), wife of [Nikolai Yezhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Yezhov "Nikolai Yezhov"), the [People's Commissar for Internal Affairs (NKVD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD "NKVD"), and checked into a hotel room with her, unaware that the room was bugged. Yezhov heard the recording and attacked Yezhova. On 23 October 1938, Sholokhov met Stalin in the Kremlin to complain that he had been put under surveillance in Veshenskaya, but when Yezhov was summoned to explain, he claimed not to know why. They met again on 31 October: this time the officer who had been investigating Sholokhov was also summoned. He said his orders had come from Moscow, but Yezhov again denied giving the order. Sholokhov claimed that he completed the fourth and last volume of *And Quiet Flows the Don* and its sequel on 21 December 1939, the day when the USSR was celebrating what was supposedly Stalin's 60th birthday, and celebrated by opening a bottle of wine that Stalin had given him. He then wrote to Stalin to say how he had marked the special day.

In 1959 he accompanied Soviet Premier [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev "Nikita Khrushchev") on a trip to Europe and the United States. He became a member of the [CPSU Central Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPSU_Central_Committee "CPSU Central Committee") in 1961, [Academician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academician "Academician") of the [USSR Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Academy_of_Sciences "USSR Academy of Sciences") in 1939, and was a member of the [USSR Supreme Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Supreme_Soviet "USSR Supreme Soviet"). He was twice awarded the title of [Hero of Socialist Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_Socialist_Labor "Hero of Socialist Labor"), and later became vice president of the [Union of Soviet Writers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Soviet_Writers "Union of Soviet Writers").

He commented on the [Sinyavsky–Daniel trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinyavsky–Daniel_trial "Sinyavsky–Daniel trial") at the [23rd Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Congress_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union "23rd Congress of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union") by saying that the prison terms meted out to Sinyavsky and Daniel had been much too lenient compared to the "revolutionary understanding of what is right" during the 1920s, which turned part of the Soviet intelligentsia against him and resulted in two open letters by [Lydia Chukovskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Chukovskaya "Lydia Chukovskaya") and [Yuri Galanskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Galanskov "Yuri Galanskov") addressed to Sholokhov.

## Later life and death

Sholokhov almost stopped writing after 1969 and spent the late years at the [Vyoshenskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyoshenskaya "Vyoshenskaya") [stanitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanitsa "Stanitsa"). He used his [Order of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin "Order of Lenin") money to build a local school and his [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") to take the family on a road trip over Europe and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"). In 1972 he became a vocal critic of [Alexander Yakovlev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Yakovlev_(Russian_politician) "Alexander Yakovlev (Russian politician)"), then a head of the Central Committee Propaganda Department, and Yakovlev’s article, "Against Antihistoricism" which attacked Russian nationalism; this resulted in a [Politburo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politburo_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union "Politburo of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union") meeting and removal of Yakovlev from his position (he was then sent as an ambassador to [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada")).

Mikhail Sholokhov died on 21 February 1984, from [laryngeal cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laryngeal_cancer "Laryngeal cancer"). He was buried in the grounds of his house at the Vyoshenskaya stanitsa along with his wife Maria Petrovna Sholokhova ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Gromoslavskaya; 1902—1992).

## Legacy

- An [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid "Asteroid") in [main-belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main-belt "Main-belt") is named after him, [2448 Sholokhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2448_Sholokhov "2448 Sholokhov").
- [Sholokhov Moscow State University for Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sholokhov_Moscow_State_University_for_Humanities "Sholokhov Moscow State University for Humanities") bears his name.
- His house at the [Vyoshenskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyoshenskaya "Vyoshenskaya") [stanitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanitsa "Stanitsa") was turned into the [National Sholokhov Museum-Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sholokhov_Museum-Reserve "National Sholokhov Museum-Reserve").
- Monuments in Moscow and [Rostov-on-Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostov-on-Don "Rostov-on-Don").
- [Grigory and Aksinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_and_Aksinya_(sculpture_composition) "Grigory and Aksinya (sculpture composition)") and [Grigory and Aksinya in a boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_and_Aksinya_in_a_boat_(sculpture_composition) "Grigory and Aksinya in a boat (sculpture composition)") sculptures in the Vyoshenskaya stanitsa and Rostov-on-Don, respectively.
- The [Moscow National Guard Presidential Cadets School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_National_Guard_Presidential_Cadets_School "Moscow National Guard Presidential Cadets School") is named after him.
