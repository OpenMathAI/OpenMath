**Juan Ramón Jiménez Mantecón** (Spanish pronunciation: [[xwan raˈmoŋ xiˈmeneθ manteˈkon]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Spanish "Help:IPA/Spanish"); 23 December 1881 – 29 May 1958) was a Spanish [poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_poets "List of poets"), a prolific writer who received the [1956 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "1956 Nobel Prize in Literature") "for his [lyrical poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyric_poetry "Lyric poetry"), which in the Spanish language constitutes an example of high spirit and artistic purity". One of Jiménez's most important contributions to modern poetry was his advocacy of the concept of "pure poetry".

## Biography

### Early life

Juan Ramón Jiménez was born in [Moguer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moguer,_Huelva "Moguer, Huelva"), near [Huelva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huelva "Huelva"), in [Andalucia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalucia "Andalucia"), on 23 December 1881. He was educated in the Jesuit institution of San Luis Gonzaga, in [El Puerto de Santa María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Puerto_de_Santa_María "El Puerto de Santa María"), near [Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cádiz "Cádiz"). Later, he studied [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law "Law") and [painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painting "Painting") at the [University of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Seville "University of Seville"), but he soon discovered that his talents were better used for writing. He then dedicated himself to literature, under the influence of [Rubén Darío](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubén_Darío "Rubén Darío") and French [symbolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolism_(arts) "Symbolism (arts)"). He published his first two books at the age of eighteen, in 1900. The death of his father the same year devastated him, and a resulting depression led to his being sent first to France, where he had an affair with his doctor's wife, and then to a sanatorium in Madrid staffed by novice nuns, where he lived from 1901 to 1903.

In about 1904 Jiménez was hoodwinked by some Peruvians. José Gálvez, Carlos Rodríguez Hübner and a 15 year old [Maria Isabel Sanchez-Concha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Isabel_Sanchez-Concha "Maria Isabel Sanchez-Concha") created a fictional woman named Georgina Hübner and they started a correspondence with the poet. José and Carlos were hoping to get access to his writing and Sanchez-Concha did the writing. Jiménez fell in love with their creation and planned to travel to Peru to meet the young woman. The plan was only aborted by a telegram they arranged via the Spanish consul to the poet, giving him the fabricated news of Georgina's death.

### Career

He was among the contributors of the Madrid-based avant-garde magazine *[Prometeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometeo_(magazine) "Prometeo (magazine)")* between 1908 and 1912. In 1911 and 1912, he wrote many erotic poems depicting romps with numerous women in numerous locales. Some of them alluded to sex with novices who were nurses. Eventually, apparently, their mother superior discovered the activity and expelled him, although it is not known whether the sexual activity described in his poems actually occurred.

The main subjects of many of his other poems were music and color, which, at times, he compared to love or lust.

He suffered a [mental breakdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_breakdown "Mental breakdown") and [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_(mood) "Depression (mood)"), so he stayed hospitalised in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") and [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid "Madrid"). He celebrated his home region in his prose poem about a writer and his donkey called *[Platero and I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platero_and_I "Platero and I")* (1914). In 1916 he and Spanish-born writer and poet [Zenobia Camprubí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenobia_Camprubí "Zenobia Camprubí") were married in the United States. Zenobia became his indispensable companion and collaborator.

Upon the outbreak of the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War"), he and Zenobia went into exile in [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico"), where he settled in 1946. Jiménez was hospitalized for eight months due to another deep depression. He later became a Professor of Spanish Language and Literature at the [University of Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Puerto_Rico "University of Puerto Rico"). His literary influence on Puerto Rican writers strongly marks the works of [Giannina Braschi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giannina_Braschi "Giannina Braschi"), [René Marqués](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Marqués "René Marqués"), [Aurora de Albornoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_de_Albornoz "Aurora de Albornoz"), and [Manuel Ramos Otero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Ramos_Otero "Manuel Ramos Otero"). The university named a building on campus and a writing program in his honor. He was also a professor at the [University of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Miami "University of Miami") in [Coral Gables, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_Gables,_Florida "Coral Gables, Florida"). While living in Coral Gables he wrote "Romances de Coral Gables". In addition, he was a professor in the Department of Spanish and Portuguese at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland "University of Maryland"), which renamed Jimenez Hall for him in 1981.

Although he was primarily a poet, Jiménez' prose work *[Platero y yo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platero_y_yo "Platero y yo")* (1917; "Platero and I"; Platero is a donkey) sold well in Latin America and in translation won him popularity in the USA. He also collaborated with his wife in the translation of the Irish playwright [John Millington Synge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Millington_Synge "John Millington Synge")'s *[Riders to the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riders_to_the_Sea "Riders to the Sea")* (1920). His poetic output during his life was immense.

Among his better known works are:

- *Sonetos Espirituales 1914–1916* (1916; "Spiritual Sonnets, 1914–15")
- *Piedra y Cielo* (1919; "Stones and Sky")
- *Poesía en Verso*, 1917–1923 (1923; "Poetry in Verse")
- *Poesía en Prosa y verso* (1932; "Poetry in Prose and Verse")
- *Voces de mi Copla* (1945; "Voices of My Song"), and
- *Animal de Fondo* (1947; "Animal at Bottom").

Both Jiménez and Camprubí used a simplified [Spanish orthography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_orthography "Spanish orthography") different from the [RAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Academy "Royal Spanish Academy") standard.
A collection of 300 poems (1903–53) in English translation by [Eloise Roach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eloise_Roach "Eloise Roach") was published in 1962.

### Later life and death

In 1956, he received the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature"); two days later, his wife died of uterus cancer. Jiménez never recovered from the emotional devastation, and he died two years afterwards, on 29 May 1958, in the same clinic where his wife had died. Both are buried in his hometown of Moguer, Spain.

## In popular culture

- A quotation from Jiménez, "If they give you ruled paper, write the other way," is the [epigraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigraph_(literature) "Epigraph (literature)") to [Ray Bradbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Bradbury "Ray Bradbury")'s novel *[Fahrenheit 451](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit_451 "Fahrenheit 451")* (1953).
- The same quotation used in Fahrenheit 451 is used in *[The Lovely Bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lovely_Bones "The Lovely Bones")* (2002) by Alice Sebold.
- The Spanish film director Alfredo Castellón adapted Jiménez's novel *[Platero and I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platero_and_I "Platero and I")* into a movie by the same title, produced in 1965 and released in 1967.
- The [Spanglish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanglish "Spanglish") novel *[Yo-Yo Boing!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yo-Yo_Boing! "Yo-Yo Boing!")* (1998) by the Puerto Rican author [Giannina Braschi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giannina_Braschi "Giannina Braschi") features a scene in which poets and artists debate Jiménez's genius versus that of other Spanish-language poets [Francisco de Quevedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_Quevedo "Francisco de Quevedo"), [Luis de Góngora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_de_Góngora "Luis de Góngora"), [Rubén Darío](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubén_Darío "Rubén Darío"), [Pablo Neruda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Neruda "Pablo Neruda"), [Federico García Lorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_García_Lorca "Federico García Lorca"), and [Julia de Burgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_de_Burgos "Julia de Burgos").
- A rock band in Spain is named [Platero y Tú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platero_y_Tú "Platero y Tú") after Jiménez's novel.

## Streets named after Jiménez

Several streets have been named after Jiménez, including one in [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid,_Spain "Madrid, Spain"), one in [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia,_Spain "Valencia, Spain"), and one in [Quintanar de la Orden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintanar_de_la_Orden "Quintanar de la Orden"), [Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo_(province) "Toledo (province)").
