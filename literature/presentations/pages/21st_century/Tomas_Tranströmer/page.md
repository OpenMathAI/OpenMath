**Tomas Gösta Tranströmer** (Swedish: [[ˈtʊ̌mːas ˈjœ̂sːta ˈtrâːnˌstrœmːɛr]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Swedish "Help:IPA/Swedish"); 15 April 1931 – 26 March 2015) was a Swedish poet, psychologist and translator. His poems captured the long winters in [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden"), the rhythm of the seasons and the palpable, atmospheric beauty of nature. Tranströmer's work is also characterized by a sense of mystery and wonder underlying the routine of everyday life, a quality which often gives his poems a religious dimension. He has been described as a [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "Christians") poet.

Tranströmer is acclaimed as one of the most important Scandinavian writers since the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). Critics praised his poetry for its accessibility, even in translation. His poetry has been translated into over 60 languages. He received many awards for his poetry, including the [2011 Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "2011 Nobel Prize in Literature").

## Life and work

### Early life

Tranströmer was born in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm") in 1931 and raised by his mother Helmy, a schoolteacher, following her divorce from his father, Gösta Tranströmer, an editor. He received his secondary education at the [Södra Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Södra_Latin "Södra Latin") [Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)") in Stockholm, where he began writing poetry. In addition to selected journal publications, his first collection of poems, *[17 Poems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17_Poems "17 Poems")*, was published in 1954. He continued his education at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University "Stockholm University"), graduating as a psychologist in 1956 with additional studies in history, religion and literature. Between 1960 and 1966, Tranströmer split his time between working as a psychologist at the [Roxtuna centre for juvenile delinquents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anstalten_Roxtuna?action=edit&redlink=1 "Anstalten Roxtuna") and writing poetry. He worked as a psychologist at the Labor Market Institute in [Västerås](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Västerås "Västerås") from 1965 to 1990.

### Poetry

Tranströmer is considered to be one of the "most influential Scandinavian poet[s] of recent decades". Tranströmer published 15 collected works over his extensive career, which have been translated into over 60 languages. An English translation by [Robin Fulton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Fulton "Robin Fulton") of his entire body of work, *New Collected Poems*, was published in the UK in 1987 and expanded in 1997. Following the publication of *The Great Enigma*, Fulton's edition was further expanded into *The Great Enigma: New Collected Poems,* published in the US in 2006 and as an updated edition of *New Collected Poems* in the UK in 2011. He published a short autobiography, *Minnena ser mig* *(The Memories see me)*, in 1993.

By the mid-1960s, Tranströmer became close friends with poet [Robert Bly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bly "Robert Bly"). The two corresponded frequently, and Bly translated Tranströmer's poems into English. In 2001 [Bonniers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnier_Group "Bonnier Group"), Tranströmer's publisher, released *Air Mail*, a work consisting of Tranströmer's and Bly's day-to-day correspondence on personal, contemporary and literary matters c. 1965–1991 – in a style that vividly conveyed how close friends the two had soon become. Bly also helped arrange readings for his fellow poet in America. The Syrian poet [Adunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adunis "Adunis") helped spread Tranströmer's fame in the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world "Arab world"), accompanying him on reading tours.

In the 1970s, other poets accused Tranströmer of being detached from his own age, since he did not deal overtly with social and political issues in his poems and novels. His work, though, lies within and further develops the [Modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist "Modernist") and [Expressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressionist "Expressionist")/[Surrealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealist "Surrealist") language of 20th-century poetry; his clear, seemingly simple pictures from everyday life and nature, in particular, reveals a mystic insight to the universal aspects of the human mind.

Tranströmer went to [Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopal "Bhopal") immediately after the [gas tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopal_disaster "Bhopal disaster") in 1984, and alongside Indian poets such as [K. Satchidanandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Satchidanandan "K. Satchidanandan"), took part in a poetry reading session outside the plant. Late in his life, he tried his hand at some Swedish [haiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiku "Haiku").

One of his poems was read at the assassinated politician [Anna Lindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Lindh "Anna Lindh")'s memorial service in 2003. Tranströmer suffered a [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke "Stroke") in 1990 that left him partially paralyzed and unable to speak; however, he continued to write and publish poetry through the early 2000s.

### Music

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Tomas_Transtr%C3%B6mer_in_2014-2.jpg/250px-Tomas_Transtr%C3%B6mer_in_2014-2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tomas_Tranströmer_in_2014-2.jpg)

[Photographic portrait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photographic_portrait "Photographic portrait") of Tranströmer by Frankie Fouganthin, 2014

Tranströmer played the piano throughout his life; after his stroke, he taught himself to play with his left hand. He often said that the playing was a way for him to continue living after the stroke.

Tranströmer's daughter Emma is a concert [mezzo-soprano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mezzo-soprano "Mezzo-soprano"). In 2011 she released the album *Dagsmeja*, containing settings of 18 of Tranströmer's poems.

Many composers and musicians have worked with his poems. Among these are [Jan Garbarek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Garbarek "Jan Garbarek"), [Ulf Grahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulf_Grahn "Ulf Grahn"), [Madeleine Isaksson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeleine_Isaksson "Madeleine Isaksson"), [Margareta Hallin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margareta_Hallin "Margareta Hallin"), [Lars Edlund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Edlund "Lars Edlund"), [Sven-David Sandström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven-David_Sandström "Sven-David Sandström"), [Jan Sandström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Sandström_(composer) "Jan Sandström (composer)") and [Anders Eliasson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Eliasson "Anders Eliasson").

### Death

Tranströmer died in Stockholm on 26 March 2015 aged 83.
