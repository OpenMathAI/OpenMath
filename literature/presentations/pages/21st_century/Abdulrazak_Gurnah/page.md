**Abdulrazak Gurnah**  [FRSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Literature "Fellow of the Royal Society of Literature") (born 20 December 1948) is a Tanzanian  novelist and academic of Yemeni origin. He was born in the [Sultanate of Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Zanzibar "Sultanate of Zanzibar") and moved to the United Kingdom in the 1960s as a refugee during the [Zanzibar Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar_Revolution "Zanzibar Revolution"). His novels include *[Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_(Gurnah_novel) "Paradise (Gurnah novel)")* (1994), which was shortlisted for both the [Booker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Booker_Prize "Man Booker Prize") and the [Whitbread Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitbread_Prize "Whitbread Prize"); *[By the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By_the_Sea_(novel) "By the Sea (novel)")* (2001), which was longlisted for the Booker and shortlisted for the [*Los Angeles Times* Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times_Book_Prize "Los Angeles Times Book Prize"); and *[Desertion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertion_(novel) "Desertion (novel)")* (2005), shortlisted for the [Commonwealth Writers' Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Writers'_Prize "Commonwealth Writers' Prize").

In [2021, Gurnah was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "2021 Nobel Prize in Literature") "for his uncompromising and compassionate penetration of the effects of colonialism and the fates of the refugee in the gulf between cultures and continents". On 1 September 2024, Gurnah took up the appointment of the Arts Professor of Literature at [New York University Abu Dhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_Abu_Dhabi "New York University Abu Dhabi"). He is also Emeritus Professor of English and Postcolonial Literatures at the [University of Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kent "University of Kent").

## Early life and education

Abdulrazak Salim Gurnah was born on 20 December 1948 in the [Sultanate of Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Zanzibar "Sultanate of Zanzibar"). His father and uncle were businessmen who had immigrated from [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen "Yemen"). He left the island, which later became part of [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania "Tanzania"), at the age of 18, following the overthrow of the ruling Arab elite in the [Zanzibar Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar_Revolution "Zanzibar Revolution"), arriving in England in 1968 as a refugee. He is of [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab "Arab") heritage. Gurnah has been quoted as saying: "I came to England when these words, such as asylum-seeker, were not quite the same – more people are struggling and running from terror states."

He initially studied at [Christ Church College, Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church_College,_Canterbury "Christ Church College, Canterbury"), whose degrees were at the time awarded by the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London "University of London"). He then moved to the [University of Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kent "University of Kent"), where he earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") with a thesis titled *Criteria in the Criticism of West African Fiction*, in 1982.

## Career

### Academia

From 1980 to 1983, Gurnah lectured at [Bayero University Kano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayero_University_Kano "Bayero University Kano") in [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria "Nigeria"). He then became a professor of English and [postcolonial literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postcolonial_literature "Postcolonial literature") at the University of Kent, where he taught until his retirement in 2017. As of 2021 he is professor [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus "Emeritus") of English and postcolonial literatures at the university.

Since 1 September 2024, Gurnah has held the position of Arts Professor of Literature at [New York University Abu Dhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_Abu_Dhabi "New York University Abu Dhabi").

### Fiction

Alongside his work in academia, Gurnah is a creative writer and novelist. He is the author of many short stories, essays and novels. He began writing out of homesickness in his 20s, starting with writing down thoughts in his diary, which turned into longer reflections about home, and eventually grew into writing fictional stories about other people. This created a habit of using writing as a tool to understand and record his experience of being a refugee, living in another land and the feeling of being displaced. These initial stories eventually became Gurnah's first novel, *[Memory of Departure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_of_Departure "Memory of Departure")* (1987), which he wrote alongside his Ph.D. dissertation. This first book set the stage for his ongoing exploration of the themes of "the lingering trauma of colonialism, war and displacement" which feature in his subsequent novels, short stories and critical essays.

Although Gurnah's novels were received positively by critics, they were not commercially successful and, in some cases, were not published outside the United Kingdom. His readership changed dramatically after he was awarded the [Nobel Prize for Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Literature "Nobel Prize for Literature") in 2021, when publishers and booksellers struggled to meet the sudden surge in demand for his work. Only after the Nobel announcement did American publishers begin bidding for his novel. *Afterlives*, which was released by [Riverhead Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverhead_Books "Riverhead Books") in August 2022. Riverhead went on to acquire the rights to *By the Sea* and *Desertion*, two earlier works that had long been out of print.

While his first language is [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language "Swahili language"), he has used English as his literary language and integrates Swahili, Arabic and German into many of his writings despite some resistance from publishers who preferred to "italicize or [Anglicise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicisation "Anglicisation") Swahili and Arabic references and phrases in his books". Gurnah has criticised the practices in both British and American publishing that want to "make the alien seem alien" by marking "foreign" terms and phrases with italics or by putting them in a glossary. As academic [Hamid Dabashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Dabashi "Hamid Dabashi") notes, Gurnah "is integral to the manner in which Asian and African migratory and diasporic experiences have enriched and altered English language and literature. ... Calling authors like Gurnah diasporic, exilic, or any other such self-alienating term conceals the fact that English was native to him even before he set foot in England. English colonial officers had brought it home to him."

Consistent themes run through Gurnah's writing, including exile, displacement, belonging, colonialism and broken promises by the state. Most of his novels tell stories about people living in the developing world, affected by war or crisis, who may not be able to tell their own stories. Much of Gurnah's work is set on the coast of [East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa "East Africa") and many of his novels' [protagonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protagonist "Protagonist") were born in Zanzibar. Though Gurnah has not returned to live in Tanzania since he left at 18, he has said that his homeland "always asserts himself in his imagination, even when he deliberately tries to set his stories elsewhere."

Literary critic Bruce King posits that Gurnah's novels place East African protagonists in their broader international context, observing that in Gurnah's fiction "Africans have always been part of the larger, changing world". According to King, Gurnah's characters are often uprooted, alienated, unwanted and therefore are, or feel, resentful victims". Felicity Hand suggests that Gurnah's novels *[Admiring Silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiring_Silence "Admiring Silence")* (1996), *[By the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By_the_Sea_(novel) "By the Sea (novel)")* (2001) and *[Desertion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertion_(novel) "Desertion (novel)")* (2005) all concern "the alienation and loneliness that emigration can produce and the soul-searching questions it gives rise to about fragmented identities and the very meaning of 'home'." She observes that Gurnah's characters typically do not succeed abroad following their migration, using irony and humour to respond to their situation.

Novelist [Maaza Mengiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maaza_Mengiste "Maaza Mengiste") has described Gurnah's works by saying: "He has written work that is absolutely unflinching and yet at the same time completely compassionate and full of heart for people of East Africa. [...] He is writing stories that are often quiet stories of people who aren't heard, but there's an insistence there that we listen."

Aiming to build the readership for Gurnah's writing in Tanzania, the first translator of his novels into Swahili, academic Dr Ida Hadjivayanis of the [School of Oriental and African Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Oriental_and_African_Studies "School of Oriental and African Studies"), has said: "I think if his work could be read in East Africa it would have such an impact. ... We can't change our reading culture overnight, so for him to be read the first steps would be to include *Paradise* and *Afterlives* in the school curriculum."

### Other writing

Gurnah edited two volumes of *Essays on African Writing* and has published articles on a number of contemporary postcolonial writers, including [V. S. Naipaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._S._Naipaul "V. S. Naipaul"), [Salman Rushdie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salman_Rushdie "Salman Rushdie"), and [Zoë Wicomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoë_Wicomb "Zoë Wicomb"). He is the editor of *A Companion to Salman Rushdie* ([Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press "Cambridge University Press"), 2007). From 1987, Gurnah has been a contributing editor of *[Wasafiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasafiri "Wasafiri")* and as of 2021 is on the magazine's advisory board.

## Other activities

He has been a judge for literary awards, including the [Caine Prize for African Writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caine_Prize_for_African_Writing "Caine Prize for African Writing"), the [Booker Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_Prize "Booker Prize"), and the [RSL Literature Matters Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Literature "Royal Society of Literature"). He supports a boycott of Israeli cultural institutions, including publishers and literary festivals. He was an original signatory of the manifesto "Refusing Complicity in Israel's Literary Institutions".

Gurnah was featured on the [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4 "BBC Radio 4") programme *[Desert Island Discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Island_Discs "Desert Island Discs")* on 25 May 2025.

## Awards and honours

Gurnah's 1994 novel *[Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_(Gurnah_novel) "Paradise (Gurnah novel)")* was shortlisted for the [Booker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Booker_Prize "Man Booker Prize"), the [Whitbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitbread_Prize "Whitbread Prize") and the [Writers' Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers'_Guild_of_Great_Britain "Writers' Guild of Great Britain") Prizes as well as the ALOA Prize for the best Danish translation. His novel *[By the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By_the_Sea_(novel) "By the Sea (novel)")* (2001) was longlisted for the Booker and shortlisted for the [*Los Angeles Times* Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times_Book_Prize "Los Angeles Times Book Prize"), while *Desertion* (2005) was shortlisted for the 2006 [Commonwealth Writers' Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Writers'_Prize "Commonwealth Writers' Prize").

In 2006, Gurnah was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society of Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Literature "Royal Society of Literature"). In 2007, he won the [RFI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_France_Internationale "Radio France Internationale") Témoin du Monde (Witness of the World) award in France for *By the Sea*.

On 7 October 2021, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature "Nobel Prize in Literature") for 2021 "for his uncompromising and compassionate penetration of the effects of [colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonialism "Colonialism") and the fates of the [refugee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee "Refugee") in the gulf between cultures and continents". Gurnah was the first Black writer since [Toni Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Morrison "Toni Morrison") in 1993 to receive it, and the first African writer since 2007, when [Doris Lessing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doris_Lessing "Doris Lessing") was the recipient.

## Personal life

As of 2021, Gurnah lives in [Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury "Canterbury"), Kent, England, and he has British citizenship. He maintains close ties with Tanzania, where he still has family and where he says he goes when he can: "I am from there. In my mind I live there."

He is married to [Guyanese-born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyanese_people "Guyanese people") scholar of literature [Denise DeCaires Narain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denise_DeCaires_Narain?action=edit&redlink=1 "Denise DeCaires Narain").
