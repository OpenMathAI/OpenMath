**Roger Charles Louis Guillemin** (French pronunciation: [[ʁɔʒe ʃaʁl lwi ɡijmɛ̃]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); January 11, 1924 – February 21, 2024) was a French-American neuroscientist. He received the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in 1976, and the [Nobel Prize for Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1977 for his work on [neurohormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurohormone "Neurohormone"), sharing the prize that year with [Andrew Schally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Schally "Andrew Schally") and [Rosalyn Sussman Yalow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalyn_Sussman_Yalow "Rosalyn Sussman Yalow").

## Biography

Guillemin was born in [Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijon "Dijon"), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France"). After secondary studies at the Lycée Carnot in Dijon, he began his medical studies at the [University of Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dijon "University of Dijon") in 1943. He completed his medical studies at the [University of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lyon "University of Lyon") and received [MD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine "Doctor of Medicine") degree in 1949. He worked as a doctor in a small village in Burgundy, and went to [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal"), Quebec, Canada, to work with [Hans Selye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Selye "Hans Selye") at the Institute of Experimental Medicine and Surgery at the [Université de Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Montréal "Université de Montréal") where he received a Ph.D. in 1953. In 1965, he became a [naturalized citizen of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_nationality_law "United States nationality law").

In 1954, Guillemin observed that [pituitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary "Pituitary") cells do not produce hormones unless [hypothalamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamic "Hypothalamic") cells are present, which supported the theory that the hypothalamus controls the pituitary through hormones, dubbed releasing factors. Guillemin moved to [Baylor College of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baylor_College_of_Medicine "Baylor College of Medicine"), in [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston "Houston"), to develop that finding. Andrzej V. Schally, known in the US as [Andrew Schally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Schally "Andrew Schally"), joined him in 1957. Their partnership dissolved after five years for lack of progress and personal conflicts; Schally moved to the [Veterans Affairs Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_Affairs_Hospital "Veterans Affairs Hospital") in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans").

Both scientists then worked independently, processing large quantities of hypothalami: Guillemin used over two million sheep hypothalami, and Schally used pig brains—funded by the US government. The release factors are present in an extremely low amounts in the hypothalamus, and it was hard to detect them using the instrumentation available at that time. Their rivalry intensified, particularly regarding scientific credit. In 1969, as government funding was about to be cut off, [Roger Burgus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Burgus?action=edit&redlink=1 "Roger Burgus") from Guillemin's team made a breakthrough by identifying the [thyrotropin-releasing factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyrotropin-releasing_factor "Thyrotropin-releasing factor") (TRF), which controls the [thyroid gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid_gland "Thyroid gland"). The achievement secured continued funding and led to the identification of another releasing factor, FRF, which controls reproductive systems. Guillemin and Schally discovered the structures of [Thyrotropin-releasing hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyrotropin-releasing_hormone "Thyrotropin-releasing hormone") (TRH) and [Gonadotropin-releasing hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonadotropin-releasing_hormone "Gonadotropin-releasing hormone") (GnRH) in separate laboratories. They were awarded the 1977 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for this discovery.

In 1970, he joined the [Salk Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salk_Institute_for_Biological_Studies "Salk Institute for Biological Studies") in [La Jolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla "La Jolla"), where he was the head of the Laboratories for [Neuroendocrinology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroendocrinology "Neuroendocrinology") until his retirement in 1989. Here, he discovered [somatostatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatostatin "Somatostatin"), and was "among the first" to isolate [endorphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endorphins "Endorphins"). A Guillemin protégé, [Wylie Vale Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wylie_Vale_Jr. "Wylie Vale Jr."), established his own laboratory at Salk in 1977; their attempts of finding releasing factors was described as "yet another furious rivalry." Vale's lab was first to purify and sequence the CRF.

In 2007, Guillemin was an interim president of the Salk Institute.

Guillemin signed, along with other Nobel Prize winners, a petition requesting a delegation of the [Committee on the Rights of the Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_on_the_Rights_of_the_Child "Committee on the Rights of the Child") of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations "United Nations") to visit a Tibetan child who had been under house arrest in China since 1995, namely [Gedhun Choekyi Nyima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gedhun_Choekyi_Nyima "Gedhun Choekyi Nyima"), who was recognized as the 11th [Panchen Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchen_Lama "Panchen Lama") by the 14th [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalai_Lama "Dalai Lama"), [Tenzin Gyatso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Dalai_Lama "14th Dalai Lama").

Guillemin [turned 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenarian "Centenarian") on January 11, 2024, and died in San Diego, California the following month, on February 21.

He was married to Lucienne Jeanne Billard for 69 years until her death in 2021, at the age of 100. They had five daughters and a son.
