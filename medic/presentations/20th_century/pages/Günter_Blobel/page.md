**Günter Blobel** (German pronunciation: [[ˈɡʏntɐ ˈbloːbl̩]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-G%C3%BCnter_Blobel.ogg "File:De-Günter Blobel.ogg"); May 21, 1936 – February 18, 2018) was a [Silesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia "Silesia") [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans "Germans") and American [biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist "Biologist") and 1999 [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") laureate in Physiology for the discovery that proteins have intrinsic signals that govern their transport and localization in the cell.

## Education and early life

Günter Blobel was born in [Waltersdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niegosławice,_Lubusz_Voivodeship "Niegosławice, Lubusz Voivodeship") in the [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_of_Prussia "Free State of Prussia") [Province of Lower Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lower_Silesia "Province of Lower Silesia"), then located in eastern Germany. In January 1945 his family fled from native Silesia to [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden "Dresden") to escape from the advancing [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army "Red Army"). During the [bombing of Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden_in_World_War_II "Bombing of Dresden in World War II"), Blobel, then 8, stayed with his family at a relative's farm to the west of the city. After the war, Blobel grew up and attended [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)") in the [Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxony "Saxony") town of [Freiberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiberg,_Saxony "Freiberg, Saxony"). He studied medicine and graduated from the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen "University of Tübingen") in 1960. After two years service in a medical internship, he moved to Madison, Wisconsin, following an older brother, enrolling in the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison") and, joining the lab of [Van R. Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Rensselaer_Potter "Van Rensselaer Potter") for his graduate work. Blobel matriculated in 1967 with a Ph.D. He then moved to Rockefeller University as a postdoctoral fellow with George Palade, and was soon appointed as a professor.

## Career and research

Blobel was appointed to the [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute") in 1986. Blobel was the sole recipient of the 1999 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for the discovery of [signal peptides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_peptides "Signal peptides"). Signal peptides form an integral part of [protein targeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_targeting "Protein targeting"), a mechanism for cells to direct newly synthesized [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") molecules to their proper location by means of an "address tag" (i.e., a signal peptide) within the molecule.

Blobel died of cancer in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan") at New York-Presbyterian Weill Cornell Medical Center on February 18, 2018 at the age of 81. By the time of his death, Blobel was described as having "ushered cell biology into the molecular age" through his work on the fractionation and reconstitution of functional protein complexes and sub-cellular components *in vitro*.

### Philanthropy

Blobel became well known for his direct and active support for the rebuilding of [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden "Dresden") in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany"), becoming, in 1994, the founder and president of the nonprofit "Friends of Dresden, Inc." He donated all of the Nobel award money to the restoration of Dresden, in particular for the rebuilding of the [Frauenkirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden_Frauenkirche "Dresden Frauenkirche") (completed in 2005) and the building of a new [synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synagogue "Synagogue"). In Leipzig he pursued a rebuilding of the [Paulinerkirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulinerkirche,_Leipzig "Paulinerkirche, Leipzig"), the university church of the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig "University of Leipzig"), which had been blown up by the communist regime of East Germany in 1968, arguing "this is a shrine of German cultural history, connected to the most important names in German cultural history." Gunter was also a founding member of the board of directors of Research Foundation to Cure AIDS, a U.S. not-for-profit research organization.

## Personal life

Blobel lost his older sister to aerial bombing of a train she was on in 1945, shortly after the bombing of Dresden, while an older brother survived the war and became a veterinarian in the United States. Blobel worked at the [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University") in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") from 1968. He lived in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan")'s [Upper East Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_East_Side "Upper East Side") with his wife, Laura Maioglio (owner of [Barbetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbetta "Barbetta")). He was on the board of directors for [Nestlé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestlé "Nestlé") and the Board of Scientific Governors at [The Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scripps_Research_Institute "The Scripps Research Institute"). Furthermore, he was Co-Founder and Chairman of the Scientific Advisory Board for Chromocell Corporation. He sat on the Selection Committee for Life Science and Medicine which chooses winners of the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize"). Blobel had a passion for opera and architecture, in addition to his passion for experimental science.
