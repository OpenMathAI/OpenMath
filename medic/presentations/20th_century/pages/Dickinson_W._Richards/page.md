**Dickinson Woodruff Richards Jr.** (October 30, 1895 – February 23, 1973) was an American [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician "Physician") and [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiologist "Physiologist"). He was a co-recipient of the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1956 with [André Cournand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Frédéric_Cournand "André Frédéric Cournand") and [Werner Forssmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Forssmann "Werner Forssmann") for the development of [cardiac catheterization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_catheterization "Cardiac catheterization") and the characterisation
of a number of [cardiac diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_disease "Heart disease").

## Early life

Richards was born in [Orange, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange,_New_Jersey "Orange, New Jersey"). He was educated at the [Hotchkiss School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotchkiss_School "Hotchkiss School") in [Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut "Connecticut"), and entered [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") in 1913. At Yale he studied [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek"), graduating in 1917 as a member of the senior society [Scroll and Key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scroll_and_Key "Scroll and Key").

## Career

He joined the [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army") in 1917, and became an [artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery "Artillery") instructor. He served from 1918 to 1919 as an artillery officer in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France").

When he returned to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), Richards attended [Columbia University College of Physicians and Surgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University_College_of_Physicians_and_Surgeons "Columbia University College of Physicians and Surgeons"), graduating with an [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree "Master's degree") in 1922 and his [M.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine "Doctor of Medicine") degree in 1923. He was on the staff of the [Presbyterian Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York-Presbyterian_Hospital "New York-Presbyterian Hospital") in [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") until 1927, when he went to [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England "England") to work at the [National Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Medical_Research "National Institute for Medical Research") in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London"), under [Sir Henry Dale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hallett_Dale "Henry Hallett Dale"), on the control of circulation in the [liver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver "Liver").

In 1928, Richards returned to the Presbyterian Hospital and began his research on [pulmonary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_lung "Human lung") and [circulatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulatory "Circulatory") [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology"), working under [Professor Lawrence Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Joseph_Henderson "Lawrence Joseph Henderson") of [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). He began collaborations with [André Cournand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Frédéric_Cournand "André Frédéric Cournand") at [Bellevue Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue_Hospital "Bellevue Hospital"), New York, working on [pulmonary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_lung "Human lung") function. Initially their research focussed on methods to study pulmonary function in patients with pulmonary disease.

Their next area of research was the development of a technique for catheterization of the heart. Using this technique they were able to study and characterise traumatic [shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_(circulatory) "Shock (circulatory)"), the physiology of [heart failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_failure "Heart failure"). They measured the effects of cardiac drugs and described various forms of dysfunction in chronic [cardiac diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_disease "Heart disease") and pulmonary diseases and their treatment, and developed techniques for the diagnosis of [congenital heart diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congenital_heart_disease "Congenital heart disease"). For this work, Richards, Cournand, and Werner Forssmann were awarded the [Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine") for 1956.

In 1945 Richards moved his lab to [Bellevue Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue_Hospital "Bellevue Hospital"), New York. In 1947 he was made the Lambert Professor of Medicine at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), where he had taught since 1925. During his career he also served as an advisor to [Merck Sharp and Dohme Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merck_&_Co. "Merck & Co."), and edited the [Merck Manual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merck_Manual "Merck Manual"). Richards retired from his positions at Bellevue and Columbia in 1961.

## Global policy

He was one of the signatories of the agreement to convene a convention for drafting a [world constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_constitution "World constitution"). As a result, for the first time in human history, a [World Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Constituent_Assembly "World Constituent Assembly") convened to draft and adopt the [Constitution for the Federation of Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_for_the_Federation_of_Earth "Constitution for the Federation of Earth").

## Honor

Richards received many other honors, including the [John Phillips Memorial Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Phillips_Memorial_Award?action=edit&redlink=1 "John Phillips Memorial Award") of the [American College of Physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_College_of_Physicians "American College of Physicians") in 1960, the [Chevalier de la Legion d'Honneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalier_de_la_Legion_d'Honneur "Chevalier de la Legion d'Honneur") in 1963, the [Trudeau Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trudeau_Medal?action=edit&redlink=1 "Trudeau Medal") in 1968, and the [Kober Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kober_Medal "Kober Medal") of the [Association of American Physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Physicians "Association of American Physicians") in 1970.

He died in [Lakeville, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeville,_Connecticut "Lakeville, Connecticut") and his wife Constance in 1990.
