**Stanley Cohen** (November 17, 1922 – February 5, 2020) was an American biochemist who, along with [Rita Levi-Montalcini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Levi-Montalcini "Rita Levi-Montalcini"), was awarded the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in [Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1986 for the isolation of [nerve growth factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_growth_factor "Nerve growth factor") and the discovery of [epidermal growth factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidermal_growth_factor "Epidermal growth factor").

## Early life and education

Cohen was born in Brooklyn, New York, on November 17, 1922. He was the son of Fannie (née Feitel) and Louis Cohen, a tailor. His parents were [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") immigrants. Cohen received his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") in 1943 from [Brooklyn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_College "Brooklyn College"), where he had double-majored in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") and [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology "Biology"). After working as a [bacteriologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriologist "Bacteriologist") at a milk processing plant to earn money, he received his [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") in [zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology "Zoology") from [Oberlin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberlin_College "Oberlin College") in 1945. He earned a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate "Doctorate") from the department of biochemistry about the metabolism of earthworms at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan") in 1948.

## Career

His first academic employment was at the University of Colorado studying the metabolism of premature babies. In 1952 he moved to [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis"), working first in the department of radiology, learning isotope methodology, and then in the department of zoology. Working with [Rita Levi-Montalcini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Levi-Montalcini "Rita Levi-Montalcini"), he isolated nerve growth factor. He later isolated a protein that could accelerate incisor eruption and eyelid opening in newborn mice, which was renamed epidermal growth factor. He continued research on cellular growth factors after joining the faculty of [Vanderbilt University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University_School_of_Medicine "Vanderbilt University School of Medicine") in 1959.

In 1999, Cohen retired from Vanderbilt University. He died in February 2020 at the age of 97.

## Awards and legacy

Cohen received the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") together with Rita Levi-Montalcini in 1983, the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in [Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1986 for the isolation of [nerve growth factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_growth_factor "Nerve growth factor") and the discovery of [epidermal growth factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidermal_growth_factor "Epidermal growth factor") and the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in 1986.
His research on cellular growth factors has proven fundamental to understanding the development of [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer") and designing anti-cancer drugs.

His Scopus [*h*-index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-index "H-index") value was 82 as of March 2022.
