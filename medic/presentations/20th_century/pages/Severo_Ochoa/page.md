**Severo Ochoa de Albornoz** (Spanish: [[seˈβeɾo oˈtʃoa ðe alβoɾˈnoθ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Spanish "Help:IPA/Spanish"); 24 September 1905 – 1 November 1993) was a Spanish [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician "Physician") and [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"), and winner of the 1959 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") together with [Arthur Kornberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Kornberg "Arthur Kornberg") for their discovery of "the mechanisms in the [biological synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_synthesis "Biological synthesis") of [deoxyribonucleic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deoxyribonucleic_acid "Deoxyribonucleic acid") (DNA)".

## Early life and education

Ochoa was born in [Luarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luarca "Luarca") ([Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asturias "Asturias")), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain"). His father was Severo Manuel Ochoa (who he was named after), a lawyer and businessman, and his mother was Carmen de Albornoz. Ochoa was the nephew of [Álvaro de Albornoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Álvaro_de_Albornoz "Álvaro de Albornoz") (President of the Second Spanish Republic in exile and former Foreign Minister), and a cousin of the poet and critic [Aurora de Albornoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_de_Albornoz "Aurora de Albornoz"). His father died when Ochoa was seven, and he and his mother moved to [Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Málaga "Málaga"), where he attended elementary school through high school. His interest in biology was stimulated by the publications of the Spanish neurologist and Nobel laureate [Santiago Ramón y Cajal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Ramón_y_Cajal "Santiago Ramón y Cajal"). In 1923, he went to the [University of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complutense_University_of_Madrid "Complutense University of Madrid") Medical School, where he hoped to work with Ramón y Cajal, but Ramón y Cajal retired. He studied with father [Pedro Arrupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Arrupe "Pedro Arrupe"), and [Juan Negrín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Negrín "Juan Negrín") was his teacher:

> *Negrin opened wide, fascinating vistas to my imagination, not only through his lectures and laboratory teaching, but through his advice, encouragement, and stimulation to read scientific monographs and textbooks in languages other than Spanish*.

Negrín encouraged Ochoa and another student, José Valdecasas, to isolate [creatinine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creatinine "Creatinine") from urine. The two students succeeded and also developed a method to measure small levels of muscle creatinine. Ochoa spent the summer of 1927 at [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow "University of Glasgow") working with [D. Noel Paton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diarmid_Noel_Paton "Diarmid Noel Paton") on [creatine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creatine "Creatine") metabolism and improving his English skills. He also refined the assay procedure further and upon returning to Spain he and Valdecasas submitted a paper describing the work to the *[Journal of Biological Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Biological_Chemistry "Journal of Biological Chemistry")*, where it was rapidly accepted, marking the beginning of Ochoa's biochemistry career.

Ochoa completed his undergraduate medical degree in the summer of 1929 and decide to go abroad again to gain further research experience. His creatine and creatinine work led to an invitation to join [Otto Meyerhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Meyerhof "Otto Meyerhof")'s laboratory at the [Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Wilhelm_Society "Kaiser Wilhelm Society") in Berlin-Dahlem in 1929. At that time the institute was a "hot bed" of the rapidly evolving discipline of biochemistry, and thus Ochoa had the experience of meeting and interacting with scientists such as [Otto Heinrich Warburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Heinrich_Warburg "Otto Heinrich Warburg"), [Carl Neuberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Neuberg "Carl Neuberg"), [Einar Lundsgaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einar_Lundsgaard "Einar Lundsgaard"), and [Fritz Lipmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Lipmann "Fritz Lipmann") in addition to Meyerhof who had received the Nobel Prize in Physiology and Medicine less than a decade earlier.

Ochoa with wife Carmen García Cobián, in Sweden, 1959

In 1930 Ochoa returned to Madrid to complete research for his MD thesis, which he defended that year. In 1931, a newly minted MD, he married Carmen García Cobián. They did not have any children. He then began postdoctoral study at the [National Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Medical_Research "National Institute for Medical Research") in London, where he worked with [Henry Hallett Dale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hallett_Dale "Henry Hallett Dale"). His London research involved the enzyme [glyoxalase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyoxalase_system "Glyoxalase system") and was an important departure in Ochoa's career in two respects. First, the work marked the beginning of Ochoa's lifelong interest in enzymes. Second, the project was at the cutting edge of the rapidly evolving study of [intermediary metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism "Metabolism").

## Career and research

In 1933 the Ochoas returned to Madrid where he began to study glycolysis in heart muscle. Within two years, he was offered the directorship of the Physiology Section in a newly created Institute for Medical Research at the [University of Madrid Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complutense_University_of_Madrid "Complutense University of Madrid"). Unfortunately the appointment was made just as the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War "Spanish Civil War") erupted. Ochoa decided that trying to perform research in such an environment would destroy forever his "chances of becoming a scientist." Thus, "after much thought, my wife and I decided to leave Spain." In September 1936 they began what he later called the "wander years" as they traveled from Spain to Germany, to England, and ultimately to the United States within a span of four years.

Ochoa left Spain and returned to Meyerhof's Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Biology now relocated in Heidelberg, where Ochoa found a profoundly changed research focus. During his 1930 visit the laboratory work was "classical physiology," which Ochoa described as "one could see muscles twitching everywhere". By 1936 Meyerhof's laboratory had become one of the world's foremost biochemical facilities focused on processes such as [glycolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycolysis "Glycolysis") and [fermentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermentation "Fermentation"). Rather than studying muscles "twitch," the lab was now purifying and characterizing the enzymes involved in muscle action and those involved in yeast fermentation.

From then until 1938, he held many positions and worked with many people at many places. For example, [Otto Meyerhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Meyerhof "Otto Meyerhof") appointed him Guest Research Assistant at the [Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Medical_Research "Max Planck Institute for Medical Research") in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg") for one year. From 1938 until 1941 he was Demonstrator and Nuffield Research Assistant at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford").

### United States

Ochoa then went to the United States, where he again held many positions at several universities. Between 1940 and 1942, Ochoa worked for [Washington University's School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_School_of_Medicine "Washington University School of Medicine"). In 1942 he was appointed research associate in medicine at the [New York University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_School_of_Medicine "New York University School of Medicine") and there subsequently became assistant professor of biochemistry (1945), professor of pharmacology (1946), professor of biochemistry (1954), and chair of the department of biochemistry.

In 1956, he became an American citizen. He was elected to both the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1957. In 1959, Ochoa and [Arthur Kornberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Kornberg "Arthur Kornberg") were awarded the Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine "for their discovery of the mechanisms in the biological [synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_synthesis "Chemical synthesis") of [ribonucleic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA") and [deoxyribonucleic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA")". He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1961.

Ochoa continued research on [protein synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_synthesis "Protein synthesis") and replication of [RNA viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_virus "RNA virus") until 1985, when he returned to now democratic Spain where he was a science advisor. Ochoa was also a recipient of U.S. [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in 1978.

Severo Ochoa died in [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid "Madrid"), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain") on 1 November 1993. Carmen García Cobián had died in 1986.

Long after his death, Spanish actress [Sara Montiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Montiel "Sara Montiel") claimed that she and Severo Ochoa were involved in a romantic relationship in the 1950s, as stated in an interview in Spanish newspaper *[El País](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_País "El País")*: "The great love of my life was Severo Ochoa. But it was an impossible love. Clandestine. He was married, and besides, him doing research and me doing films wasn't a good match."

### Legacy

A research center that was planned in the 1970s was opened in 1975 (CBM) in the Autonomous University of Madrid (UAM). After his death, it was named the Centro de Biología Molecular Severo Ochoa.

In [Leganés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leganés "Leganés"), Madrid, a hospital bears his name, as does the [Madrid Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Metro "Madrid Metro") station serving it, [Hospital Severo Ochoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital_Severo_Ochoa_(Madrid_Metro) "Hospital Severo Ochoa (Madrid Metro)").

The [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid "Asteroid") [117435 Severochoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/117435_Severochoa "117435 Severochoa") is also named in his honor.

In 2003, the Spanish General Post Office ([Correos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correos "Correos")) issued a €0,76 postage stamp honoring Ochoa, as one of a pair featuring Spanish medical Nobel Prize winners alongside [Santiago Ramón y Cajal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Ramón_y_Cajal "Santiago Ramón y Cajal").

In June 2011, the United States Postal Service issued a stamp honoring him, as part of the *American Scientists* collection, along with [Melvin Calvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Calvin "Melvin Calvin"), [Asa Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asa_Gray "Asa Gray"), and [Maria Goeppert-Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Goeppert-Mayer "Maria Goeppert-Mayer"). This was the third volume in the series.

The main road in to the tourist resort Benidorm is named Avenida Dr. Severo Ochoa in his honor.
