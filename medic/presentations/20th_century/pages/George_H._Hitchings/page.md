**George Herbert Hitchings** (April 18, 1905 – February 27, 1998) was an American biochemist who shared the 1988 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Sir James Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_James_Black "Sir James Black") and [Gertrude Elion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_Elion "Gertrude Elion") "for their discoveries of important principles for drug treatment", Hitchings specifically for his work on [chemotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemotherapy "Chemotherapy").

## Early life and education

Hitchings was born in [Hoquiam, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoquiam,_Washington "Hoquiam, Washington"), in 1905, and grew up there, in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California "Berkeley, California"), [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego "San Diego"), [Bellingham, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellingham,_Washington "Bellingham, Washington"), and [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle"). He graduated from Seattle's [Franklin High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Public_Schools "Seattle Public Schools"), where he was [salutatorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salutatorian "Salutatorian"), in 1923, and from there went to the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington "University of Washington"), from which he graduated with a degree in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") *cum laude* in 1927, after having been elected to [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa "Phi Beta Kappa") as a junior the year before. That summer, he worked at the university's [Puget Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget_Sound "Puget Sound") Biological Station at [Friday Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_Harbor,_Washington "Friday Harbor, Washington") on [San Juan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_Island "San Juan Island") , and received a master's degree the next year for his thesis based on that work.

From the University of Washington, Hitchings went to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") as a teaching fellow, ending up at Harvard Medical School. Before getting his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in 1933, he joined [Alpha Chi Sigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Chi_Sigma "Alpha Chi Sigma") in 1929.

## Career and research

Following his PhD, he worked at Harvard and [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University "Case Western Reserve University"). In 1942, he went to work for Wellcome Research Laboratories at [Tuckahoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuckahoe_(village),_New_York "Tuckahoe (village), New York"), where he began working with [Gertrude Elion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_Elion "Gertrude Elion") in 1944. Drugs Hitchings' team worked on included [2,6-diaminopurine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2,6-diaminopurine "2,6-diaminopurine") (a compound to treat [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia")) and p-chlorophenoxy-2,4-diaminopyrimidine (a [folic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folic_acid "Folic acid") antagonist). According to his Nobel Prize autobiography,

:   The line of inquiry we had begun in the 1940s [also] yielded new drug therapies for [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria") ([pyrimethamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrimethamine "Pyrimethamine")), leukemia ([6-mercaptopurine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-mercaptopurine "6-mercaptopurine") and [thioguanine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tioguanine "Tioguanine")), [gout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gout "Gout") ([allopurinol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allopurinol "Allopurinol")), [organ transplantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_transplantation "Organ transplantation") ([azathioprine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azathioprine "Azathioprine")) and [bacterial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterium "Bacterium") [infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infection "Infection") ([co-trimoxazole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-trimoxazole "Co-trimoxazole") (trimethoprimA)). The new knowledge contributed by our studies pointed the way for investigations that led to major [antiviral drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiviral_drug "Antiviral drug") for [herpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herpes "Herpes") infections ([acyclovir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acyclovir "Acyclovir")) and [AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS "AIDS") ([zidovudine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zidovudine "Zidovudine")).

In 1967 Hitchings became vice president in Charge of Research of Burroughs-Wellcome. He became Scientist Emeritus in 1976. He also served as adjunct professor of pharmacology and of experimental medicine from 1970 to 1985 at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University").

Hitchings founded the Triangle Community Foundation in 1983. Hitchings is a member of the Medicinal Chemistry Hall of Fame.

## Personal life

His first wife, Beverly Reimer Hitchings, died in 1985. Hitchings remarried in 1989 to Joyce Carolyn Shaver-Hitchings, MD. Dr. Shaver-Hitchings died in 2009.

Hitchings died on 27 February 1998 in [Chapel Hill, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina "Chapel Hill, North Carolina").

## Awards and honors

Hitchings was awarded the Passano award by the [Passano Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passano_Foundation "Passano Foundation") in 1969, and the de Villiers award in 1970. In 1972, he was awarded the [Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Prize_for_Therapeutics_of_the_University_of_Edinburgh "Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh"). He was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1974 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1974"). In 1989, Hitchings received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").
