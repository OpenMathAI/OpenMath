**Sir Ronald Ross** [KCB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_Bath "Knight Commander of the Order of the Bath") [KCMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Commander_of_the_Order_of_St_Michael_and_St_George "Knight Commander of the Order of St Michael and St George") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") [FRCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_College_of_Surgeons "Fellow of the Royal College of Surgeons") (13 May 1857 – 16 September 1932) was a British [medical doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_doctor "Medical doctor"). He received the 1902 [Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine") "for his work on [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria"), by which he has shown how it enters the organism and thereby has laid the foundation for successful research on this disease and methods of combating it". His [discovery of the malarial parasite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_malaria "History of malaria") in the [gastrointestinal tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestion "Digestion") of a [mosquito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito "Mosquito") in 1897 proved that [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria "Malaria") was transmitted by mosquitoes, and laid the foundation for the method of [combating the disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_control "Vector control").

Ross was a [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath "Polymath"), writing a number of poems, publishing several novels, and composing songs. He was also an amateur artist and mathematician. He worked in the [Indian Medical Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Medical_Service "Indian Medical Service") for 25 years. It was during his service that he made the groundbreaking medical discovery. After resigning from his service in India, he joined the faculty of [Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_School_of_Tropical_Medicine "Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine"), and continued as Professor and Chairman of [Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Medicine "Tropical Medicine") of the institute for 10 years. In 1926, he became Director-in-Chief of the Ross Institute and Hospital for Tropical Diseases, which was established in honour of his works. He remained there until his death.

## Early life and education

Ross was born in [Almora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almora "Almora"), then in the [North-Western Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-Western_Provinces "North-Western Provinces") of [Company-ruled India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India "Company rule in India"), north west of Nepal. He was the eldest of ten children of Sir [Campbell Claye Grant Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbell_Claye_Grant_Ross "Campbell Claye Grant Ross"), a general in the [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army "British Indian Army"), and Matilda Charlotte Elderton. At age eight, he was sent to England to live with his aunt and uncle on the [Isle of Wight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Wight "Isle of Wight"). He attended Primary schools at [Ryde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryde "Ryde"), and for secondary education he was sent to a boarding school at Springhill, near [Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton "Southampton"), in 1869. From his early childhood, he developed a passion for poetry, music, literature and [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"). At fourteen years of age he won a prize for mathematics, a book titled *Orbs of Heaven* which sparked his interest in mathematics. In 1873, at sixteen, he secured first position in the [Oxford and Cambridge local examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford,_Cambridge_and_RSA_Examinations "Oxford, Cambridge and RSA Examinations") in drawing.

Although Ross wanted to become a writer, his father arranged enrollment at [St Bartholomew's Hospital Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Bartholomew's_Hospital_Medical_College "St Bartholomew's Hospital Medical College") in London, in 1874. Not fully committed, he spent most of his time composing music, and writing poems and plays. He left in 1880. In 1879 he had passed the examinations for the [Royal College of Surgeons of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Surgeons_of_England "Royal College of Surgeons of England"), and he worked as a ship's surgeon on a transatlantic steamship while studying for the [licenciate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licenciate "Licenciate") of the [Society of Apothecaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Apothecaries "Society of Apothecaries"). He qualified on second attempt in 1881, and after a four-month training at [Army Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Medical_School "Army Medical School"), was appointed a surgeon in the [Indian Medical Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Medical_Service "Indian Medical Service") on 5 April 1881, assigned to the Madras Presidency. Between June 1888 and May 1889 he took study leave to obtain the diploma in public health from the [Royal College of Physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians "Royal College of Physicians") and [Royal College of Surgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Surgeons "Royal College of Surgeons"), and took a course in [bacteriology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriology "Bacteriology") under Professor [E. E. Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Edward_Klein "Emanuel Edward Klein").

## Career

### India

Ross embarked for India on 22 September 1881 on the troopship *Jumma*. Between 1881 and 1894 he was variously posted in [Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras "Madras"), [Moulmein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawlamyine "Mawlamyine") (in Burma/[Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar")), [Baluchistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluchistan "Baluchistan"), [Andaman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Islands "Andaman Islands"), [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore "Bangalore") and [Secunderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secunderabad "Secunderabad"). In 1883, he was posted as the Acting Garrison Surgeon at Bangalore during which he noticed the possibility of controlling mosquitoes by limiting their access to water. In March 1894 he had his home leave and went to London with his family. On 10 April 1894 he met Sir [Patrick Manson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Manson "Patrick Manson") for the first time. Manson who became Ross's mentor, introduced him to the real problems in malaria research. Manson always had a firm belief that India was the best place for the study. Ross returned to India on [P&O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P&O_(company) "P&O (company)") ship *Ballaarat* on 20 March 1895 and landed in Secunderabad on 24 April. Even before his luggage was cleared in the custom office, he went straight for Bombay Civil Hospital, looking for malarial patients and started making blood films.

#### Discovery of malaria-causing mosquito

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Ross%2C_20.Aug.1897.jpg/250px-Ross%2C_20.Aug.1897.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ross,_20.Aug.1897.jpg)

The page in Ross' notebook where he recorded the "pigmented bodies" in mosquitoes that he later identified as malaria parasites

Ross made his first important step in May 1895 when he observed the early stages of malarial parasite inside a mosquito stomach. However, his enthusiasm was interrupted as he was deployed to Bangalore to investigate an outbreak of [cholera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholera "Cholera"). Bangalore had no regular cases of malaria. He confided to Manson stating, "I am thrown out of employment and have 'no work to do'." But in April he had a chance to visit Sigur Ghat near the hill station of [Ooty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ooty "Ooty"), where he noticed a mosquito on the wall in a peculiar posture, and for this he called it "dappled-winged" mosquito, not knowing the species. In May 1896, he was given a short leave that enabled him to visit a malaria-endemic region around Ooty. In spite of his daily quinine prophylaxis, he was down with severe malaria three days after his arrival. In June he was transferred to [Secunderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secunderabad "Secunderabad").

After two years of research failure, in July 1897, Ross managed to culture 20 adult "brown" mosquitoes from collected larvae. He successfully infected the mosquitoes from a patient named Husein Khan for a price of 8 [annas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_(monetary_subunit) "Anna (monetary subunit)") (one anna per blood-fed mosquito). After blood-feeding, he dissected the mosquitoes. On 20 August he confirmed the presence of the malarial parasite inside the gut of mosquito, which he originally identified as "dappled-wings" (which turned out to be a species of the genus *Anopheles*). The next day, on 21 August, he confirmed the growth of the parasite in the mosquito. This discovery was published on 27 August 1897 in the Indian Medical Gazette and subsequently in the December 1897 issue of *[British Medical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Medical_Journal "British Medical Journal")*. In the evening he composed the following poem for his discovery (originally unfinished, sent to his wife on 22 August, and completed a few days later):

Plaque from the Ronald Ross Memorial, [Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata "Kolkata")

> This day relenting God
> Hath placed within my hand
> A wondrous thing; and God
> Be praised. At His command,
> Seeking His secret deeds
> With tears and toiling breath,
> I find thy cunning seeds,

#### Discovery of malaria transmission in birds

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/Sir_R._Ross_on_steps_of_laboratory_in_Calcutta%2C_1898_Wellcome_L0011943.jpg/250px-Sir_R._Ross_on_steps_of_laboratory_in_Calcutta%2C_1898_Wellcome_L0011943.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sir_R._Ross_on_steps_of_laboratory_in_Calcutta,_1898_Wellcome_L0011943.jpg)

Ross, Mrs Ross, Mahomed Bux, and two other assistants at [Cunningham's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Douglas_Cunningham "David Douglas Cunningham") laboratory of Presidency Hospital in Calcutta

In September 1897, Ross was transferred to Bombay, from where he was subsequently sent to malaria-free [Kherwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kherwara "Kherwara") in [Rajputana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajputana "Rajputana") (now [Rajasthan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan "Rajasthan")). Frustrated by lack of work, he threatened to resign from the service as he felt that it was a death blow to his research. It was only on the representation of Patrick Manson that the government arranged for his continued service in Calcutta on "special duty". On 17 February 1898, he arrived in Calcutta (now [Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata "Kolkata")), to work in the [Presidency General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_General_Hospital "Presidency General Hospital") (now [IPGMER and SSKM Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPGMER_and_SSKM_Hospital "IPGMER and SSKM Hospital")).

Ross immediately carried out research in malaria and [Visceral leishmaniasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visceral_leishmaniasis "Visceral leishmaniasis") (also known as kala azar), for which he was assigned. He was given the use of Surgeon-Lieutenant-General Cunningham's laboratory for his research. He had no success with malarial patients because they were always immediately given medication. He built a bungalow with a laboratory at Mahanad village, where he would stay from time to time to collect mosquitoes in and around the village. He employed Mahomed (or Muhammed) Bux and Purboona (who deserted him after the first payday). As Calcutta was not a malarious place, Manson persuaded him to use birds, as being used by other scientists such as [Vasily Danilewsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Danilewsky "Vasily Danilewsky") in Russia and [William George MacCallum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_George_MacCallum "William George MacCallum") in America. Ross complied but with a complaint that he "did not need to be in India to study bird malaria". By March he began to see results on bird parasites, very closely related to the [human malarial parasites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasmodium_vivax "Plasmodium vivax").

Using more convenient model of birds (infected sparrows), by July 1898 Ross established the importance of culex mosquitoes as intermediate hosts in [avian malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avian_malaria "Avian malaria"). On 4 July he discovered that the [salivary gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salivary_gland "Salivary gland") was the storage sites of malarial parasites in the mosquito. By 8 July he was convinced that the parasites are released from the salivary gland during biting. He later demonstrated the transmission of malarial parasite from mosquitoes (in this case *[Culex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culex "Culex")* species) to healthy sparrows from an infected one, thus, establishing the complete life cycle of malarial parasite.

In September 1898 he went to southern [Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam "Assam") in ([northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India "Northeast India")) to study an epidemic of Visceral leishmaniasis. He was invited to work there by Graham Col Ville Ramsay, the second Medical Officer of the Labac Tea Estate Hospital. (His microscope and medicals tools are still preserved, and his sketches of mosquitoes are still on display at the hospital.) However, he utterly failed as he believed that the kala-azar parasite (*[Leishmania donovani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leishmania_donovani "Leishmania donovani")*, the very scientific name he later gave in 1903) was transmitted by a mosquito, which he refers to as *Anopheles rossi* (scientific name given by G.M. Giles). (It is now known that [kala azar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kala_azar "Kala azar") is transmitted by [sandflies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandfly "Sandfly").)

### England

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/Ronald_Ross_18_Cavendish_Square_blue_plaque.jpg/250px-Ronald_Ross_18_Cavendish_Square_blue_plaque.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ronald_Ross_18_Cavendish_Square_blue_plaque.jpg)

Blue plaque, 18 Cavendish Square, London

In 1899, Ross resigned from the Indian Medical Service and went to England to join the faculty of the [Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_School_of_Tropical_Medicine "Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine") as a lecturer. He continued to work on prevention of malaria in different parts of the world, including [West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa "West Africa"), the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal "Suez Canal") zone, Greece, [Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius "Mauritius"), [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus "Cyprus"), and in the areas affected by the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War"). He also established organisations for fighting malaria in India and Sri Lanka. In 1902, Ross was awarded the [Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Prize_for_Therapeutics_of_the_University_of_Edinburgh "Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh"). He was appointed as Professor and Chair of Tropical Medicine of the Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine in 1902, which he held up to 1912. In 1912 he was appointed Physician for Tropical Diseases at [King's College Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_Hospital "King's College Hospital") in London, and simultaneously hold the Chair of Tropical Sanitation in Liverpool. He remained in these posts until 1917 when he became (honorary) Consultant in [Malariology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malariology "Malariology") in [British War Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_War_Office "British War Office"). He travelled to [Thessaloniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaloniki "Thessaloniki") and Italy in November to advise and on the way, "in a landlocked bay close to the Leucadian Rock (where Sappho is supposed to have drowned herself)", his ship escaped a torpedo attack. Between 1918 and 1926 he worked as Consultant in Malaria in the [Ministry of Pensions and National Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Pensions_and_National_Insurance "Ministry of Pensions and National Insurance").

Ross developed mathematical models for the study of malaria [epidemiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiology "Epidemiology"), which he initiated in his report on Mauritius in 1908. He elaborated the concept in his book *The Prevention of Malaria* in 1910 (2nd edition in 1911) and further elaborated in a more generalised form in scientific papers published by the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") in 1915 and 1916; some of his epidemiology work was developed with mathematician [Hilda Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilda_Phoebe_Hudson "Hilda Phoebe Hudson"). These papers represented a profound mathematical interest which was not confined to epidemiology, but led him to make material contributions to both pure and applied mathematics.

Ross was one of the supporters of [Sir William Osler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_William_Osler "Sir William Osler") in the founding of the [History of Medicine Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Medicine_Society "History of Medicine Society") in 1912, and in 1913 was the history of medicine section's vice-president. Between 1913 and 1917, he received some financial support from [Sir Edwin Durning-Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Durning-Lawrence "Edwin Durning-Lawrence"), and led experiments at the Marcus Beck laboratory in the [Royal Society of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Medicine "Royal Society of Medicine") building at 1 Wimpole Street, London.

#### Ross Institute and Hospital for Tropical Diseases

The Ross Institute and Hospital for Tropical Diseases was founded in 1926 and established at Bath House, a grand house with keeper's lodge and large grounds adjacent to Tibbet's Corner at [Putney Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putney_Heath#Putney_Heath "Putney Heath"). The hospital was opened by the then [Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales "Prince of Wales"), the future King Edward VIII. Ross assumed the post of Director-in-Chief until his death. The institute was later incorporated into the [London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Hygiene_&_Tropical_Medicine "London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine") in Keppel Street. Bath House was later demolished and mansion flats built on the property. In memory of its history and owner the block was named Ross Court. Within the grounds an older dwelling, Ross Cottage, remains.

## Nobel Prize

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Ronald_Ross_4.jpg/250px-Ronald_Ross_4.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ronald_Ross_4.jpg)

Ronald Ross

Ronald Ross was awarded a Nobel Prize for his discovery of the [life cycle of the malarial parasite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasmodium "Plasmodium") in birds. He did not build his concept of malarial transmission in humans, but in birds. Ross was the first to show that malarial parasite was transmitted by the bite of infected mosquitoes, in his case the avian *[Plasmodium relictum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasmodium_relictum "Plasmodium relictum")*. In 1897, an Italian physician and zoologist [Giovanni Battista Grassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Grassi "Giovanni Battista Grassi"), along with his colleagues, had established the [developmental stages of malaria parasites in anopheline mosquitoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_cycle_of_a_malarial_parasite "Life cycle of a malarial parasite"); and they described the complete life cycles of *[P. falciparum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasmodium_falciparum "Plasmodium falciparum")*, *P. vivax* and *[P. malariae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasmodium_malariae "Plasmodium malariae")* the following year.

When the 1902 Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine was considered, the [Nobel Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Committee_for_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Committee for Physiology or Medicine") initially intended the prize to be shared between Ross and Grassi, however Ross accused Grassi of deliberate fraud. The weight of favour ultimately fell on Ross, largely due to the influences of [Robert Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Koch "Robert Koch"), the appointed neutral arbitrator in the committee; as reported, "Koch threw the full weight of his considerable authority in insisting that Grassi did not deserve the honor".

## Personal life and death

Ronald Ross was noted to be eccentric and egocentric, described as an "impulsive man" or an "impulsive genius." His professional life appeared to be in constant feud with his students, colleagues, and fellow scientists. His personal vendetta with G. B. Grassi became a legendary tale in science. He was openly envious of his mentor Patrick Manson's affluence from private practices. His *Memories of Sir Patrick Manson* (1930) was a direct attempt to belittle Manson's influences on his works on malaria. He hardly had good ties with the administration of Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine, complaining of being underpaid. He resigned twice, and was eventually discharged without any pension.

[![A horizontal gravestone, badly dilapidated and with grass growing among the cracks](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Ronald_Ross_grave_Putney_Vale_2014.jpg/250px-Ronald_Ross_grave_Putney_Vale_2014.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ronald_Ross_grave_Putney_Vale_2014.jpg)

Ross's grave at [Putney Vale Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putney_Vale_Cemetery "Putney Vale Cemetery"), London in 2014

Ross was frequently embittered by lack of government support (what he called "administrative barbarism") for scientists in medical research. In 1928 he advertised his papers for sale in the journal *Science Progress in the Twentieth Century* (1919–1933), with a statement that the money was for financial support of his wife and family. Lady Houston bought them for £2000, and offered them to the [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum "British Museum"), which turned her down for various reasons. The papers are now preserved by the [London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Hygiene_&_Tropical_Medicine "London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine") and the [Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians_and_Surgeons_of_Glasgow "Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Glasgow").

In 1889 Ross married Rosa Bessie Bloxam (d.1931). They had two daughters, Dorothy (1891–1947) and Sylvia (1893–1925), and two sons, Ronald Campbell (1895–1914) and Charles Claye (1901–1966). His wife died in 1931. Ronald and Sylvia pre-deceased him too: Ronald was killed at the [Battle of Le Cateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Le_Cateau "Battle of Le Cateau") on 26 August 1914. Ross died at the hospital of his namesake after a long illness and [asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma "Asthma") attack. He was buried at the nearby [Putney Vale Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putney_Vale_Cemetery "Putney Vale Cemetery"), next to his wife.

Sir Ronald Ross is one of 23 names to feature on the frieze of [London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Hygiene_&_Tropical_Medicine "London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine"), pioneers chosen for their contributions to public health.

[Sir Ronald Ross Institute of Parasitology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Ronald_Ross_Institute_of_Parasitology "Sir Ronald Ross Institute of Parasitology") is the building in [Begumpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begumpet "Begumpet") where Ross made the discovery that malaria was transmitted by the female [anopheles mosquito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anopheles_mosquito "Anopheles mosquito") on 20 August. 20 August later came to known as the [World Mosquito Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Mosquito_Day "World Mosquito Day"). The lab has been transformed into a small museum exhibiting photos of Ross and his family. Various charts and diagrams explain Ross' work on malaria and its transmission.

## Literary works

Ross was a prolific writer. He habitually wrote poems on most of the important events in his life. His poetic works gained him wide acclaim and they reflect his medical service, [travelogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_literature "Travel literature"), philosophical and scientific thoughts. Many of his poems are collected in his *Selected Poems* (1928) and *In Exile* (1931). Some of his notable books are *The Child of Ocean* (1899 and 1932), *The Revels of Orsera*, *The Spirit of Storm*, *Fables and Satires* (1930), *Lyra Modulatu* (1931), and five mathematical works (1929–1931). He also compiled an extensive account *The Prevention of Malaria* in 1910 and another *Studies on Malaria* in 1928. He published his autobiography *Memoirs, with a Full Account of the Great Malaria Problem and its Solution* (547 pages long) in 1923. He carefully saved virtually everything about himself: correspondence, telegrams, newspaper cuttings, drafts of published and unpublished material, and all manner of ephemera.

## Awards and recognition

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Ronald_Ross_plaque%2C_Johnston_Building%2C_Liverpool.jpg/250px-Ronald_Ross_plaque%2C_Johnston_Building%2C_Liverpool.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ronald_Ross_plaque,_Johnston_Building,_Liverpool.jpg)

Plaque at Liverpool University – on the Johnston Building, formerly the Johnston Laboratories, near Ashton Street, [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool "Liverpool")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Ross%27s_name_remembered_on_the_London_School_of_Hygiene_and_Tropical_Medicine.JPG/250px-Ross%27s_name_remembered_on_the_London_School_of_Hygiene_and_Tropical_Medicine.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ross's_name_remembered_on_the_London_School_of_Hygiene_and_Tropical_Medicine.JPG)

Ross's name remembered on the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine

Ronald Ross was awarded the Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine in 1902 "for his work on malaria, by which he has shown how it enters the organism and thereby has laid the foundation for successful research on this disease and methods of combating it".

20 August is celebrated by [London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Hygiene_&_Tropical_Medicine "London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine") as *[World Mosquito Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Mosquito_Day "World Mosquito Day")* to commemorate Ross's discovery in 1897. Additionally, Ross's name, along with 22 other pioneers of public health and tropical medicine, appears on the School's Frieze. The papers of Sir Ronald Ross are now preserved by the [London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Hygiene_&_Tropical_Medicine "London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine") and the [Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians_and_Surgeons_of_Glasgow "Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Glasgow").

He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1901](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1901 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1901") and of the [Royal College of Surgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Surgeons_of_England "Royal College of Surgeons of England") in the same year. He was appointed vice-president of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") from 1911 to 1913. In 1902 he was appointed a Companion of the Most Honourable [Order of the Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Bath "Order of the Bath") by King Edward VII. In 1911 he was promoted to the rank of [Knight Commander of the same Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Commander_of_the_Bath "Knight Commander of the Bath"). He was also decorated with the title Officer of the [Order of Leopold II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Leopold_II "Order of Leopold II") of Belgium.

Ross received honorary membership of learned societies of most countries in Europe, and elsewhere. He got an honorary M.D. degree in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm") in 1910 at the centenary celebration of the [Caroline Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Institute "Caroline Institute") and his 1923 autobiography *Memoirs* was awarded that year's [James Tait Black Memorial Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Tait_Black_Memorial_Prize "James Tait Black Memorial Prize"). While his vivacity and single-minded search for truth caused friction with some people, he enjoyed a vast circle of friends in Europe, Asia and the United States who respected him for his personality as well as for his genius.

In India, Ross is remembered with great respect as a result of his work on malaria, the deadly epidemic which used to claim thousands of lives every year. There are roads named after him in many Indian towns and cities. In [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta "Calcutta") the road linking [Presidency General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_General_Hospital "Presidency General Hospital") with [Kidderpore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidderpore "Kidderpore") Road has been renamed after him as Sir Ronald Ross Sarani. Earlier this road was known as Hospital Road. In his memory, the regional infectious disease hospital at [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad,_Telangana "Hyderabad, Telangana") was named [Sir Ronald Ross Institute of Tropical and Communicable Diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Ronald_Ross_Institute_of_Tropical_and_Communicable_Diseases "Sir Ronald Ross Institute of Tropical and Communicable Diseases"). The building where he worked and actually discovered the malarial parasite, located in Secunderabad near the [Begumpet Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begumpet_Airport "Begumpet Airport"), is a declared a heritage site and the road leading up to the building is named Sir Ronald Ross Road.

In [Ludhiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludhiana "Ludhiana"), [Christian Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Medical_College_Ludhiana "Christian Medical College Ludhiana") has named its hostel as "Ross Hostel". The young medics often refer to themselves as "Rossians".

The [University of Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Surrey "University of Surrey"), UK, has named a road after him in its Manor Park Residences.

Ronald Ross Primary School near [Wimbledon Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wimbledon_Common "Wimbledon Common") is named after him. The school's crest includes a mosquito in one quarter.

[Sir Ronald Ross Institute of Parasitology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Ronald_Ross_Institute_of_Parasitology "Sir Ronald Ross Institute of Parasitology") was established in memory of Ronald Ross in Hyderabad, under [Osmania University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmania_University "Osmania University").

In 2010 the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool "University of Liverpool") named its new biological science building "The Ronald Ross Building" in his honour. His grandson David Ross inaugurated it. The building is home to the university's facility for the Institute of Infection and Global Health.
