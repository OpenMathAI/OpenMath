**Schack August Steenberg Krogh** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") (15 November 1874 – 13 September 1949) was a [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark "Denmark") professor at the department of [zoophysiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_physiology "Animal physiology") at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen") from 1916 to 1945. He contributed a number of fundamental discoveries within several fields of [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology"), and is famous for developing [Krogh's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krogh's_principle "Krogh's principle").

In 1920 August Krogh was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for the discovery of the mechanism of regulation of the [capillaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary "Capillary") in skeletal [muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle "Muscle"). Krogh was first to describe the adaptation of blood perfusion in muscle and other organs according to demands through opening and closing the [arterioles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arteriole "Arteriole") and [capillaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary "Capillary").

Besides his contributions to medicine, Krogh was also one of the founders of what is today the [Novo Nordisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novo_Nordisk "Novo Nordisk") company.

## Life

He was born in [Grenaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenaa "Grenaa") on the peninsula of [Djursland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djursland "Djursland") in [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark "Denmark"), the son of Viggo Krogh, a shipbuilder. His mother (born Drechmann) was the daughter of a customs officer in Holstein. Through his mother’s family he claimed "a dash of [gipsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people "Romani people") blood in his veins". When he finished school in 1889 at the age of 14, he was still too young to start cathedral school. He signed up as a voluntary 'marine intern' and served time on the gunboat [HDMS Hauch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDMS_Hauch "HDMS Hauch"), traversing the Danish waters surveying the [benthic fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benthic_zone "Benthic zone") under [C.G. Joh. Petersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.G._Johannes_Petersen "C.G. Johannes Petersen"). The following year Krogh moved to [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus "Aarhus") to continue his education at the [Aarhus Katedralskole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_Katedralskole "Aarhus Katedralskole"). He attended the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen") graduating MSc in 1899 and gaining a doctorate PhD in 1903.

Krogh was a pioneer in [comparative physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_physiology "Comparative physiology"). He wrote his thesis on the respiration through the skin and lungs in [frogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frog "Frog"): *Respiratory Exchange of Animals*, 1915. Later Krogh took on studies of water and electrolyte [homeostasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeostasis "Homeostasis") of aquatic animals and he published the books: *Osmotic Regulation* (1939) and *Comparative Physiology of Respiratory Mechanisms* (1941). He contributed more than 200 research articles in international journals. He was a constructor of scientific instruments of which several had considerable practical importance, such as the [spirometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirometer "Spirometer") and the apparatus for measuring [basal metabolic rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_metabolic_rate "Basal metabolic rate").

Krogh began lecturing in the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen "University of Copenhagen") in 1908 and in 1916 was promoted to full professor, becoming the head of the first laboratory for animal [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology") (zoophysiology) at the university.

Krogh was elected an International Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1931, an International Member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1937, and an International Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1941.

In the 1930s, Krogh worked with two other Nobel prizewinners, the radiochemist [George de Hevesy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_de_Hevesy "George de Hevesy") and the physicist [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr "Niels Bohr") on the permeability of membranes to [heavy water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_water "Heavy water") and radioactive [isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope "Isotope"), and together, they managed to obtain Denmark's first [cyclotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotron "Cyclotron") for experiments on animal and plant physiology as well as in dental and medical work.

### Friendship with William Sørensen

Krogh had a lifelong friendship with [William Sørensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Sørensen_(zoologist) "William Sørensen (zoologist)"), [arachnologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachnology "Arachnology") and 'enfant terrible' of Danish zoology at the time. Sørensen was 26 years older than Krogh, but was a childhood friend of Krogh's father Viggo Krogh. Sørensen had a forming influence on the young Krogh and helped spawn his interest in the natural sciences. After Sørensen's death in 1916, Krogh stated that Sørensen was "my teacher into scientific method". Krogh was a common guest in Sørensen's home and he would visit the Sørensen family for lunch almost every Sunday. Sørensen was himself a well-esteemed arachnologist, student of professor [J.M.C.Schiödte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jørgen_Matthias_Christian_Schiødte "Jørgen Matthias Christian Schiødte"). Schiödte, however, was in fierce opposition to his colleague and the leading Danish zoologist in the latter half of the 19th century, [Japetus Steenstrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japetus_Steenstrup "Japetus Steenstrup"). Krogh and Sørensen sided with Schiödte and his other students, and Krogh helped Sørensen when he wrote his blunt and direct attacks on the opposing side, accusing them (not entirely unwarranted) of scientific plagiarism and prioritizing friendships and loyalty over scientific merit. Krogh's siding with Sørensen earned him some influential enemies among the faculty of the university, including [Eug. Warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenius_Warming "Eugenius Warming"), but this appears not to have had significant influence on his later career. Warming was thus on the committee that recommended opening a position for Krogh as lecturer in animal physiology to the Ministry of Finances in 1908.

## Foundation of Novo Nordisk

In 1922, August Krogh went on a lecture tour to North America after receiving the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. During this tour, he and his wife [Marie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Krogh "Marie Krogh"), a doctor who was herself suffering from a [type 2 diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_2_diabetes "Type 2 diabetes"), visited Toronto where the scientists [Frederick Banting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Banting "Frederick Banting"), [Charles Best](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Best_(medical_scientist) "Charles Best (medical scientist)") and [John Macleod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Macleod_(physiologist) "John Macleod (physiologist)") had just succeeded in manufacturing active insulin. Krogh received permission to manufacture insulin in the Nordic countries and joined forces with [Hans Christian Hagedorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Hagedorn "Hans Christian Hagedorn"), a physician specialising in diabetes, to start the production of insulin in Denmark. This led to the establishment of Nordisk Insulinlaboratorium company in 1923.

In 1925, brothers [Harald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Pedersen_(engineer) "Harald Pedersen (engineer)") and [Thorvald Pedersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorvald_Pedersen "Thorvald Pedersen"), who were former employees of Nordisk, formed their own company, Novo Terapeutisk Laboratorium. Novo and Nordisk competed until they merged in 1989 to become [Novo Nordisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novo_Nordisk "Novo Nordisk") A/S.

## Family

He married [Marie Krogh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Krogh "Marie Krogh") ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name "Birth name") Jørgensen, 1874–1943) in 1905. She was a renowned scientist in her own right and much of August Krogh's work was carried out in close collaboration with her.

August and Marie had four children, the youngest of whom, [Bodil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodil_Schmidt-Nielsen "Bodil Schmidt-Nielsen"), was born in 1918. She too was a physiologist, and became the first woman president of the [American Physiological Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physiological_Society "American Physiological Society") in 1975. Bodil married another eminent physiologist, [Knut Schmidt-Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knut_Schmidt-Nielsen "Knut Schmidt-Nielsen").

## Legacy

[Torkel Weis-Fogh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torkel_Weis-Fogh "Torkel Weis-Fogh"), an eminent pioneer on the study of [insect flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect_flight "Insect flight"), was a student of August Krogh's. Together they wrote a classic paper on that subject in 1951.

Krogh's name is preserved in two items now named for him:

- [Krogh length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krogh_length "Krogh length"), the distance between [capillaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillaries "Capillaries") which nutrients [diffuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffuse "Diffuse") to, based on cellular consumption of the nutrients.
- [Krogh's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krogh's_principle "Krogh's principle"), that "for... a large number of problems there will be some animal of choice, or a few such animals, on which it can be most conveniently studied."

He via his book, *The Anatomy and Physiology of Capillaries*, popularized the false belief that if you were to lay out all the blood vessels from a human body they would reach 100,000 km in length. He made false assumptions about capillary density while also using a hypothetical body that weighed 140 kg with 50 kg of pure muscle, an unrealistic physique, to get his round figure of 100,000 km. Recent finding suggest that the actual figure is somewhere between 9,000–19,000 km.
