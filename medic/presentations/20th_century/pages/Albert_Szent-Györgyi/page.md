**Albert Imre Szent-Györgyi** de [Nagyrápolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapoltu_Mare "Rapoltu Mare") ([Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language "Hungarian language"): *nagyrápolti Szent-Györgyi Albert Imre*; September 16, 1893 – October 22, 1986) was a Hungarian [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist") who won the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1937. He is credited with first isolating [vitamin C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_C "Vitamin C") and discovering many of the components and reactions of the [citric acid cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citric_acid_cycle "Citric acid cycle") and the molecular basis of [muscle contraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle_contraction "Muscle contraction").

## Early life

Szent-Györgyi was born in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest "Budapest"), [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary "Kingdom of Hungary"), on September 16, 1893. His father, Miklós Szent-Györgyi, was a landowner, born in [Marosvásárhely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Târgu_Mureş "Târgu Mureş"), [Transylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transylvania "Transylvania") (today Târgu Mureş, [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania "Romania")), a [Calvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism "Calvinism"), and could trace his ancestry back to 1608 when Sámuel, a Calvinist [predicant](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/predicant "wiktionary:predicant"), was ennobled. At the time of Szent-Györgyi's birth, being of the nobility was considered important and created opportunities that otherwise were not available. (Miklós Szent-Györgyi's parents were Imre Szent-Györgyi and Mária Csiky). His mother, Jozefina, a Roman Catholic, was a daughter of József Lenhossék and Anna Bossányi. Jozefina was a sister of [Mihály Lenhossék](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihály_Lenhossék "Mihály Lenhossék"); both of these men were Professors of [Anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy "Anatomy") at the [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University "Eötvös Loránd University"). His family included three generations of scientists. Music was important in the Lenhossék family. His mother Jozefina prepared to become an opera singer and auditioned for Gustav Mahler, then a conductor at the [Budapest Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Opera "Budapest Opera"). He advised her to marry instead, since her voice was not enough. Albert himself was good at the piano, while his brother Pál became a professional violinist.

## Education

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/71/WGBBBC_Albert_Szent-Gy%C3%B6rgyi.jpg/250px-WGBBBC_Albert_Szent-Gy%C3%B6rgyi.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:WGBBBC_Albert_Szent-Györgyi.jpg)

Szent-Györgyi in 1917 Italy

Szent-Györgyi began his studies at the [Semmelweis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semmelweis_University "Semmelweis University") in 1911, and then began research in his uncle's anatomy lab. His studies were interrupted in 1914 to serve as an army medic in World War I. In 1916, disgusted with the war, Szent-Györgyi shot himself in the arm, claimed to be wounded from enemy fire, and was sent home on medical leave. He was then able to finish his medical education and received his MD in 1917. He married Kornélia Demény, the daughter of the Hungarian Postmaster General, that same year.

After the war, Szent-Györgyi began his research career in [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava "Bratislava"), [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia "Slovakia"). He switched universities several times over the next few years, finally ending up at the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen "University of Groningen"), where his work focused on the [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") of [cellular respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_respiration "Cellular respiration"). This work landed him a position as a [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation "Rockefeller Foundation") fellow at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"). He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") in 1929 where he was a student at [Fitzwilliam House, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitzwilliam_College,_Cambridge "Fitzwilliam College, Cambridge"). His research involved isolating an [organic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_acid "Organic acid"), which he then called "hexuronic acid", from adrenal gland tissue.

## Career and research

Szent-Györgyi accepted a position at the [University of Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged "University of Szeged") in Hungary in 1930. There Szent-Györgyi and his research fellow Joseph Svirbely found that "hexuronic acid" was actually the long-sought [antiscorbutic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiscorbutic "Antiscorbutic") factor, also known as [vitamin C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_C "Vitamin C"). After [Walter Norman Haworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Norman_Haworth "Walter Norman Haworth") had determined its structure, the antiscorbutic was given the formal chemical name of [L-ascorbic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-ascorbic_acid "L-ascorbic acid"). In some experiments they used [paprika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paprika "Paprika") as the source for their vitamin C. Also during this time, Szent-Györgyi continued his work on cellular respiration, identifying [fumaric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumaric_acid "Fumaric acid") and other steps in what would become known as the [Krebs cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krebs_cycle "Krebs cycle"). In Szeged he also met [Zoltán Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoltán_Bay "Zoltán Bay"), a physicist who became his friend and research partner in bio-physics.

In 1937 he received the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") "for his discoveries in connection with the biological combustion process with special reference to vitamin C and the [catalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis "Catalysis") of [fumaric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumaric_acid "Fumaric acid")". Albert Szent-Györgyi offered all of his Nobel prize money to [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland "Finland") in 1940. ([Hungarian volunteers in the Winter War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Volunteers_in_the_Winter_War "Hungarian Volunteers in the Winter War") travelled to fight for the Finns after the [Soviet invasion of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Finland "Soviet invasion of Finland") in 1939.)

In 1938 he began work on the [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics") of [muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle "Muscle") movement. He found that muscles contain [actin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actin "Actin"), which when combined with the protein [myosin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myosin "Myosin") and the energy source [ATP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenosine_triphosphate "Adenosine triphosphate"), contract muscle fibers. In 1946, Albert received the [Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Prize_for_Therapeutics_of_the_University_of_Edinburgh "Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh").

In 1947 Szent-Györgyi established the Institute for Muscle Research at the [Marine Biological Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Biological_Laboratory "Marine Biological Laboratory") (MBL) in [Woods Hole, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woods_Hole,_Massachusetts "Woods Hole, Massachusetts") with financial support from Hungarian businessman Stephen Rath. However, he still faced funding difficulties for several years due to his foreign status and former association with the Hungarian Communist government. In 1948, he received a research position with the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health") (NIH) in [Bethesda, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethesda,_Maryland "Bethesda, Maryland") and began dividing his time between there and Woods Hole. In 1950, grants from the [Armour Meat Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armour_and_Company "Armour and Company") and the [American Heart Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Heart_Association "American Heart Association") allowed him to establish the Institute for Muscle Research at Woods Hole. Szent-Györgyi conducted research at the MBL from 1947 to 1986 year-round. There, he found that whole muscle tissue retained its contractility almost indefinitely if stored cold in a fifty percent glycerol solution, thus eliminating the need to have fresh muscle on hand.

Szent-Györgyi in 1948

During the 1950s Szent-Györgyi began using [electron microscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscope "Electron microscope") to study muscles at the subunit level. He received the [Lasker Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Lasker_Award_for_Basic_Medical_Research "Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research") in 1954. In 1955, he became a [naturalized citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen "Naturalized citizen") of the United States. He was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences "Member of the National Academy of Sciences") (NAS) in 1956.

In 1941, Szent-Györgyi developed a research interest in [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer") and developed ideas on applying the theories of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") to the biochemistry ([quantum biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_biology "Quantum biology")) of cancer. The death of Rath, who had acted as the financial administrator of the Institute for Muscle Research, left Szent-Györgyi in a financial mess. He refused to write government grant proposals minutely specifying his research methods and expected results. After Szent-Györgyi commented on his financial hardships in a 1971 newspaper interview, attorney Franklin Salisbury helped him establish a private nonprofit organization, the [National Foundation for Cancer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Foundation_for_Cancer_Research "National Foundation for Cancer Research"). Late in life, Szent-Györgyi began to pursue [free radicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_radical "Free radical") as a potential cause of cancer. He came to see cancer as being ultimately an electronic problem at the molecular level. In 1974, reflecting his interests in quantum physics, he proposed the term "[syntropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negentropy "Negentropy")" to replace "[negentropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negentropy "Negentropy")". Ralph Moss, a protégé during his cancer research years, wrote a biography, *Free Radical: Albert Szent-Gyorgyi and the Battle over Vitamin C.* Aspects of his work are an important precursor to the understanding of [redox signaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redox_signaling "Redox signaling").

### Statement on scientific discovery

Albert Szent-Györgyi, who realized that "a discovery must be, by definition, at variance with existing knowledge," divided scientists into two categories: the [Apollonians and the Dionysians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollonian_and_Dionysian "Apollonian and Dionysian"). He called Dionysians the scientific dissenters who explore "the fringes of knowledge". He wrote, "In science the Apollonian tends to develop established lines to perfection, while the Dionysian rather relies on intuition and is more likely to open new, unexpected alleys for research...The future of mankind depends on the progress of science, and the progress of science depends on the support it can find. Support mostly takes the form of grants, and the present methods of distributing grants unduly favor the Apollonian."

### Involvement in politics

As the government of [Gyula Gömbös](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyula_Gömbös "Gyula Gömbös") and the associated [Hungarian National Defence Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_National_Defence_Association "Hungarian National Defence Association") gained control of politics in Hungary, Szent-Györgyi helped his [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") friends escape from the country. During World War II, he joined the Hungarian [resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_during_World_War_II "Resistance during World War II"). Although Hungary was allied with the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers "Axis powers"), the Hungarian prime minister [Miklós Kállay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miklós_Kállay "Miklós Kállay") sent Szent-Györgyi to Istanbul in 1944 under the guise of a scientific lecture to begin secret negotiations with the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II"). The Germans learned of this plot and [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") himself issued a warrant for the arrest of Szent-Györgyi. He escaped from house arrest and spent 1944 to 1945 as a fugitive from the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo "Gestapo").

After the war, Szent-Györgyi had become well-recognized as a public figure and there was some speculation that he might become President of Hungary, should the Soviets permit it. Szent-Györgyi established a laboratory at the [University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University "Eötvös Loránd University") and became head of the biochemistry department there. He was elected a member of Parliament and helped re-establish the Academy of Sciences. Dissatisfied with the [Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist "Communist") rule of Hungary, he emigrated to the United States in 1947.

In 1967, Szent-Györgyi signed a letter declaring his intention to [refuse to pay taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_resistance "Tax resistance") as a means of protesting against the US war against Vietnam, and urging other people to take a similar stand.

He was one of the signatories of the agreement to convene a convention for drafting a [world constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_constitution "World constitution"). As a result, for the first time in human history, a [World Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Constituent_Assembly "World Constituent Assembly") convened to draft and adopt a [Constitution for the Federation of Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_for_the_Federation_of_Earth "Constitution for the Federation of Earth").

## Personal life

He married Cornelia Demény (1898–1981), daughter of the Hungarian Postmaster-General, in 1917. Their daughter, Cornelia Szent-Györgyi, was born in 1918 and died in 1969. He and Cornelia divorced in 1941.

In 1941, he wed Marta Borbiro Miskolczy. She died of cancer in 1963.

Szent-Györgyi married June Susan Wichterman, the 25-year-old daughter of [Woods Hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woods_Hole_Oceanographic_Institution "Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution") biologist Ralph Wichterman, in 1965. They were divorced in 1968.

He married his fourth wife, Marcia Houston, in 1975. They adopted a daughter, Lola von Szent-Györgyi.

### Death and legacy

Szent-Györgyi died in [Woods Hole, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woods_Hole,_Massachusetts "Woods Hole, Massachusetts"), US, on October 22, 1986. He was honored with a [Google Doodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Doodle "Google Doodle") September 16, 2011, 118 years after his birth. In 2004, nine interviews were conducted with family, colleagues, and others to create a Szent-Györgyi oral history collection.
