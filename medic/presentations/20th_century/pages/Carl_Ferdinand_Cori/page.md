**Carl Ferdinand Cori**, [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (December 5, 1896 – October 20, 1984) was an Austrian-American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist") and [pharmacologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacology "Pharmacology"). He, together with his wife [Gerty Cori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerty_Cori "Gerty Cori") and Argentine physiologist [Bernardo Houssay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Houssay "Bernardo Houssay"), received a [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in 1947 for their discovery of how the [glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose "Glucose") derivative [glycogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycogen "Glycogen") (animal starch) is broken down and resynthesized in the body for use as a store and source of energy. In 2004, both Coris were designated a [National Historic Chemical Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Chemical_Landmarks "National Historic Chemical Landmarks") in recognition of their work that elucidated [carbohydrate metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate_metabolism "Carbohydrate metabolism").

## Education and early life

Carl Ferdinand Cori was born on December 5, 1896, in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague "Prague"), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary "Austria-Hungary") (now the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic "Czech Republic")). Carl was the son of [Carl Isidor Cori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Isidor_Cori "Carl Isidor Cori"), a zoologist, and Maria Cori (née Lippich), a daughter of the Austrian physician [Ferdinand Lippich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Lippich?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ferdinand Lippich").

The Cori family came from the [Papal State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States "Papal States") (later the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic_(18th_century) "Roman Republic (18th century)"), today's [Central Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Italy "Central Italy")) to the [royal Bohemian crownland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Lands_of_the_Bohemian_Crown_(1648–1867) "History of the Lands of the Bohemian Crown (1648–1867)"), ([Monarchical Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy "Habsburg monarchy") at the end of the 17th century). Carl Ferdinand's grandfather Eduard Cori was an administrative officer and beekeeper in Brüx, and his grandmother was Rosina Trinks. Carl Ferdinand's younger sister Margarete Cori (born 1905) was a lecturer in Prague and the wife of the Bohemian geneticist [Felix Mainx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Mainx?action=edit&redlink=1 "Felix Mainx").

He grew up in [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste "Trieste"), where his father Carl Isidor was the director of the Marine Biological Station. In late 1914 the Cori family moved to [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague "Prague") and Carl entered the medical school of [Charles University in Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Ferdinands-Universität "Karl-Ferdinands-Universität"). While studying there he met his future wife [Gerty Theresa Radnitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerty_Cori "Gerty Cori"). He was drafted into the [Austro-Hungarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army "Austro-Hungarian Army") and served in the ski corps, and later was transferred to the sanitary corps, for which he set up a laboratory in Trieste. At the end of the war Carl completed his studies, graduating with Gerty in 1920. Carl and Gerty married that year and worked together in clinics in Vienna. Their only child, Tom, married Anne, a daughter of the [American constitutional lawyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_constitutional_law "American constitutional law") and anti-feminist [Phyllis Schlafly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllis_Schlafly "Phyllis Schlafly").

## Career

Carl was invited to [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz "Graz") to work with [Otto Loewi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Loewi "Otto Loewi") to study the effect of the [vagus nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve") on the [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart "Heart") (Loewi would receive the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1936 for this work). While Carl was in Graz, Gerty remained in Vienna. A year later Carl was offered a position at the State Institute for the Study of Malignant Diseases (now [Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_Park_Comprehensive_Cancer_Center "Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Center")) in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York "Buffalo, New York"), and the Coris moved to Buffalo. In 1928, they became [naturalized citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen "Naturalized citizen") of the United States.

While at the Institute the Coris' research focused on [carbohydrate metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate_metabolism "Carbohydrate metabolism"), leading to the definition of the [Cori cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cori_cycle "Cori cycle") in 1929. In 1931, Carl accepted a position at the [Washington University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_School_of_Medicine "Washington University School of Medicine") in [St. Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri "St. Louis, Missouri"). Carl joined as professor of pharmacology and in 1942 was made professor of biochemistry. In St. Louis, the Coris continued their research on glycogen and glucose and began to describe [glycogenolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycogenolysis "Glycogenolysis"), identifying and synthesizing the important enzyme [glycogen phosphorylase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycogen_phosphorylase "Glycogen phosphorylase"). For these discoveries, they received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1947, making them the [third ever married couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize#Statistics "Nobel Prize") to win the Nobel Prize.

Gerty died in 1957 and Carl married Anne Fitzgerald-Jones (1909-2006) in 1960. He stayed on at Washington University until 1966, when he retired as chair of the biochemistry department. He was appointed visiting professor of Biological Chemistry at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") while maintaining a laboratory space at the [Massachusetts General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_General_Hospital "Massachusetts General Hospital"), where he pursued research in genetics. From 1968 to 1983, he collaborated with noted geneticist [Salomé Glüecksohn-Waelsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salomé_Glüecksohn-Waelsch "Salomé Glüecksohn-Waelsch") of the [Albert Einstein College of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_College_of_Medicine "Albert Einstein College of Medicine") in New York, until the 1980s when illness prevented him from continuing. In 1976, Carl received the Laurea honoris causa in Medicine from the [University of Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Trieste "University of Trieste").
Carl shares a star with Gerty on the [St. Louis Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Walk_of_Fame "St. Louis Walk of Fame"). In 2016 the Cori's son, Thomas, donated his parents [Nobel Prize medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_medal "Nobel Prize medal") to the [University of Washington School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington_School_of_Medicine "University of Washington School of Medicine") where they are on display in the Becker Medical Library.

## Awards and honors

In addition to winning the Nobel Prize, Cori won the [Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Lasker_Award_for_Basic_Medical_Research "Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research") in 1946 and in 1959, the [Austrian Decoration for Science and Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Decoration_for_Science_and_Art "Austrian Decoration for Science and Art"). Cori was elected a member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1940, a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1947, a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), and a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1950 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1950"). The Carl Cori [Endowed Professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_endowment#Endowed_professorships "Financial endowment") at Washington University is named in his honor, currently held by [Colin Nichols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Nichols "Colin Nichols").
