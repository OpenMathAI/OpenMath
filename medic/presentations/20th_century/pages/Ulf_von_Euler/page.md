**Ulf Svante von Euler**[ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") (7 February 1905 – 9 March 1983) was a Swedish [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology") and [pharmacologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacologist "Pharmacologist"). He shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1970 for his work on [neurotransmitters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter").

## Life

Ulf Svante von Euler-Chelpin was born in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm"), the son of two noted scientists, [Hans von Euler-Chelpin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_von_Euler-Chelpin "Hans von Euler-Chelpin"), a professor of [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"), and [Astrid Cleve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrid_Cleve "Astrid Cleve"), a professor of [botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany "Botany") and [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology "Geology"). His father was German and the recipient of [Nobel Prize for Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Chemistry "Nobel Prize for Chemistry") in 1929, and his maternal grandfather was [Per Teodor Cleve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Teodor_Cleve "Per Teodor Cleve"), Professor of [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") at the [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University"), and the discoverer of the [chemical elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element "Chemical element") [thulium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thulium "Thulium") and [holmium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmium "Holmium").
Von Euler-Chelpin studied [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine "Medicine") at the [Karolinska Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karolinska_Institute "Karolinska Institute") in 1922. At Karolinska, he worked under Robin Fåhraeus in [blood sedimentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erythrocyte_sedimentation_rate "Erythrocyte sedimentation rate") and [rheology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheology "Rheology") and did research work on the [pathophysiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathophysiology "Pathophysiology") of [vasoconstriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasoconstriction "Vasoconstriction"). He presented his doctoral thesis in 1930, and was appointed as assistant professor in pharmacology in the same year, with the support of [G. Liljestrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._Liljestrand "G. Liljestrand"). From 1930 to 1931, von Euler-Chelpin got a Rochester Fellowship to do his post-doctoral studies abroad. He studied in [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England "England") with Sir [Henry Dale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hallett_Dale "Henry Hallett Dale") in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") and with I. de Burgh Daly in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham "Birmingham"), and then proceeded to the continent, studying with [Corneille Heymans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corneille_Jean_François_Heymans "Corneille Jean François Heymans") in [Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghent "Ghent"), [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium "Belgium") and with [Gustav Embden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Embden "Gustav Embden") in [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt "Frankfurt"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany"). Von Euler liked to travel, so he also worked and learned [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics") with [Archibald Vivian Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Vivian_Hill "Archibald Vivian Hill"), again in London in 1934, and [neuromuscular transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromuscular_transmission "Neuromuscular transmission") with G. L. Brown in 1938. From 1946 to 1947, he worked with [Eduardo Braun-Menéndez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Braun-Menéndez "Eduardo Braun-Menéndez") in the [Instituto de Biología y Medicina Experimental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_de_Biología_y_Medicina_Experimental "Instituto de Biología y Medicina Experimental") in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires"), which was founded by [Bernardo Houssay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Houssay "Bernardo Houssay"). His unerring instinct to work with important scientific leaders and fields was to be proved by the fact that Dale, Heymans, Hill and Houssay went to receive the Nobel prize in physiology or medicine.

In 1981, von Euler became a founding member of the [World Cultural Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cultural_Council "World Cultural Council").

From 1930 to 1957, von Euler was married to Jane Anna Margarethe Sodenstierna. They had four children: Hans Leo, scientist administrator at the US [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health"); Johan Christopher, anesthesiologist, Serafimer Hospital, Stockholm; Ursula Katarina, Ph.D., curator at The Royal Collections, The Royal Court, Stockholm, Sweden; and Marie Jane, Chemical Engineer, [Melbourne, Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne,_Australia "Melbourne, Australia"). In 1958, von Euler married countess [Dagmar Cronstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagmar_Cronstedt "Dagmar Cronstedt"), a radio broadcaster who had during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War") worked at [Radio Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Königsberg "Radio Königsberg"), broadcasting German propaganda to neutral Sweden.

## Research

His short stay as a [postdoctoral student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher "Postdoctoral researcher") in Dale's laboratory was very fruitful: in 1931 he discovered with [John H. Gaddum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_H._Gaddum "John H. Gaddum") an important autopharmacological principle, [substance P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_P "Substance P"). After returning to Stockholm, von Euler pursued further this line of research, and successively discovered four other important [endogenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endogeny "Endogeny") active substances, [prostaglandin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostaglandin "Prostaglandin"), vesiglandin (1935), [piperidine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piperidine "Piperidine") (1942) and [noradrenaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noradrenaline "Noradrenaline") (1946).

In 1939 von Euler was appointed full professor of physiology at the Karolinska Institute, where he remained until 1971. His early collaboration with Liljestrand had led to an important discovery, which was named the [Euler–Liljestrand mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Liljestrand_mechanism "Euler–Liljestrand mechanism") (a physiological arterial [shunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunt_(medical) "Shunt (medical)") in response to the decrease in local oxygenation of the [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung "Lung")).

From 1946 on, however, when [noradrenaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine") (abbreviated NA or NAd) was discovered, von Euler devoted most of his research work to this area. He and his group studied thoroughly its distribution and fate in [biological tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_tissue "Biological tissue") and in the [nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous_system "Nervous system") in physiological and pathological conditions, and found that noradrenaline was produced and stored in nerve [synaptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synapse "Synapse") terminals in [intracellular vesicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicular_monoamine_transporter "Vesicular monoamine transporter"), a key discovery which changed dramatically the course of many researches in the field. In 1970 he was distinguished with the Nobel Prize for his work, jointly with Sir [Bernard Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Katz "Bernard Katz") and [Julius Axelrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Axelrod "Julius Axelrod"). Since 1953 he was very active in the [Nobel Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Foundation "Nobel Foundation"), being a member of the [Nobel Committee for Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Committee_for_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Committee for Physiology or Medicine") and chairman of the board since 1965. He also served as vice-president of the [International Union of Physiological Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_of_Physiological_Sciences "International Union of Physiological Sciences") from 1965 to 1971. Among the many honorary titles and prizes he received in addition to the Nobel, were the [Gairdner Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gairdner_Prize "Gairdner Prize") (1961), the [Jahre Prize](https://no.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders%20Jahres%20medisinske%20pris "no:Anders Jahres medisinske pris") (1965), the Stouffer Prize (1967), the Carl Ludwig Medaille (1953), the Schmiedeberg Plaquette (1969), La Madonnina (1970), many honorary doctorates from universities around the world, and the membership to several erudite, medical and scientific societies. He was elected a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1970, a member of both the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") and the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1972, and a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1973 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1973").
