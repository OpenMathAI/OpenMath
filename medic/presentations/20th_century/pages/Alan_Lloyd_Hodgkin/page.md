**Sir Alan Lloyd Hodgkin** (5 February 1914 – 20 December 1998) was an English [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology") and [biophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics") who shared the 1963 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Andrew Huxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Huxley "Andrew Huxley") and [John Eccles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Eccles_(neurophysiologist) "John Eccles (neurophysiologist)").

## Early life and education

Hodgkin was born in [Banbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banbury "Banbury"), Oxfordshire, on 5 February 1914. He was the oldest of three sons of [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers "Quakers") George Hodgkin and Mary Wilson Hodgkin. His father was the son of [Thomas Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hodgkin_(historian) "Thomas Hodgkin (historian)") and had read for the [Natural Science Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Sciences_(Cambridge) "Natural Sciences (Cambridge)") at [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") where he had befriended electrophysiologist [Keith Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Lucas_(scientist) "Keith Lucas (scientist)").

Because of poor eyesight, he was unable to study medicine and eventually ended up working for a bank in Banbury. As members of the [Society of Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers "Quakers"), George and Mary opposed the [Military Service Act of 1916](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Service_Act_1916 "Military Service Act 1916"), which introduced conscription, and had to endure a great deal of abuse from their local community, including an attempt to throw George in one of the town canals. In 1916, George Hodgkin travelled to Armenia as part of an investigation of distress. Moved by the misery and suffering of [Armenian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide "Armenian genocide") he attempted to go back there in 1918 on a route through the Persian Gulf (as the northern route was closed because of the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution "October Revolution") in Russia). He died of dysentery in [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad "Baghdad") on 24 June 1918, just a few weeks after his youngest son, Keith, had been born.

From an early life on, Hodgkin and his brothers were encouraged to explore the country around their home, which instilled in Alan an interest in [natural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_History "Natural History"), particularly [ornithology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithology "Ornithology"). At the age of 15, he helped [Wilfred Backhouse Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfred_Backhouse_Alexander "Wilfred Backhouse Alexander") with surveys of [heronries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heronry "Heronry") and later, at [Gresham's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham's_School "Gresham's School"), he overlapped and spent a lot of time with [David Lack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lack "David Lack"). In 1930, he was the winner of a bronze medal in the Public Schools Essay Competition organised by the [Royal Society for the Protection of Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_for_the_Protection_of_Birds "Royal Society for the Protection of Birds").

### School and university

Alan started his education at [The Downs School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Downs_Malvern "The Downs Malvern") where his contemporaries included future scientists [Frederick Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Sanger "Frederick Sanger"), [Alec Bangham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Bangham "Alec Bangham"), "neither outstandingly brilliant at school" according to Hodgkin, as well as future artists [Lawrence Gowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Gowing "Lawrence Gowing") and [Kenneth Rowntree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Rowntree "Kenneth Rowntree"). After the Downs School, he went on to [Gresham's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham's_School "Gresham's School") where he overlapped with future composer [Benjamin Britten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Britten "Benjamin Britten") as well as [Maury Meiklejohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._F._M._Meiklejohn "M. F. M. Meiklejohn"). He ended up receiving a scholarship at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge") in botany, zoology and chemistry.

Between school and college, he spent May 1932 at the [Freshwater Biological Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_Biological_Association "Freshwater Biological Association") at [Wray Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wray_Castle "Wray Castle") based on a recommendation of his future Director of Studies at [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"), [Carl Pantin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Pantin "Carl Pantin"). After Wray Castle, he spent two months with a German family in [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt "Frankfurt") as "in those days it was thought highly desirable that anyone intending to read science should have a reasonable knowledge of German." After his return to England in early August 1932, his mother Mary was remarried to Lionel Smith (1880–1972), the eldest son of [A. L. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Smith_(historian) "Arthur Smith (historian)"), whose daughter Dorothy was also married to Alan's uncle [Robert Howard Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Howard_Hodgkin "Robert Howard Hodgkin").

In the autumn of 1932, Hodgkin started as a freshman scholar at Trinity College where his friends included [classicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics "Classics") [John Raven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Raven "John Raven") and [Michael Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Grant_(classicist) "Michael Grant (classicist)"), fellow-scientists [Richard Synge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Laurence_Millington_Synge "Richard Laurence Millington Synge") and [John H. Humphrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_H._Humphrey "John H. Humphrey"), as well as [Polly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polly_Hill_(economist) "Polly Hill (economist)") and [David Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Keynes_Hill "David Keynes Hill"), the children of [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "List of Nobel laureates in Physiology or Medicine") [Archibald Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.V._Hill "A.V. Hill"). He took physiology with chemistry and zoology for the first two years, including lectures by [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "List of Nobel laureates in Physiology or Medicine") [E.D. Adrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Adrian "Edgar Adrian"). For Part II of the [tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Sciences_(Cambridge) "Natural Sciences (Cambridge)") he decided to focus on physiology instead of zoology. Nevertheless, he participated in a zoological expedition to the [Atlas Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Mountains "Atlas Mountains") in Morocco led by [John Pringle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_William_Sutton_Pringle "John William Sutton Pringle") in 1934. He finished Part II of the tripos in July 1935 and stayed at [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge") as a research fellow.

During his studies, Hodgkin, who described himself as "having been brought up as a supporter of the British Labour Party" was friends with communists and actively participated in the distribution of anti-war pamphlets. At Cambridge, he knew [James Klugmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Klugmann "James Klugmann") and [John Cornford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cornford "John Cornford"), but he emphasised in his autobiography that none of his friends "made any serious effort to convert me [to [Communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism")], either then or later." From 1935 to 1937, Hodgkin was a member of the [Cambridge Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Apostles "Cambridge Apostles").

## Pre-war research

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Trinity_College_-_Great_Court_02.jpg/500px-Trinity_College_-_Great_Court_02.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Trinity_College_-_Great_Court_02.jpg)

Trinity College at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") where Hodgkin was a student from 1932 to 1935, worked as a research fellow from 1936 on and finally served as [Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_(college) "Master (college)") from 1978 to 1984

Hodgkin started conducting experiments on how electrical activity is transmitted in the [sciatic nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciatic_nerve "Sciatic nerve") of frogs in July 1934. He found that a [nerve impulse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_potential "Action potential") arriving at a cold or compression block, can decrease the electrical threshold beyond the block, suggesting that the impulse produces a spread of an [electrotonic potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_potential "Graded potential") in the nerve beyond the block. In 1936, Hodgkin was invited by [Herbert Gasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Spencer_Gasser "Herbert Spencer Gasser"), then director of the [Rockefeller Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University") in New York City, to work in his laboratory during 1937–38. There he met [Rafael Lorente de Nó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Lorente_de_Nó "Rafael Lorente de Nó") and [Kenneth Stewart Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Stewart_Cole "Kenneth Stewart Cole") with whom he ended up publishing a paper. During that year he also spent time at the [Woods Hole Marine Biological Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Biological_Laboratory "Marine Biological Laboratory") where he was introduced to the [squid giant axon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squid_giant_axon "Squid giant axon"), which ended up being the model system with which he conducted most of the research that eventually led to his Nobel Prize.
In the spring of 1938, he visited [Joseph Erlanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Erlanger "Joseph Erlanger") at [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis") who told him he would take Hodgkin's local circuit theory of nerve impulse propagation seriously if he could show that altering the resistance of the fluid outside a nerve fibre made a difference to the velocity of nerve impulse conduction. Working with single nerve fibres from [shore crabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinus_maenas "Carcinus maenas") and [squids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squids "Squids"), he showed that the conduction rate was much faster in seawater than in oil, providing strong evidence for the local circuit theory.

After his return to Cambridge he started collaborating with [Andrew Huxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Huxley "Andrew Huxley") who had entered [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge") as a freshman in 1935, three years after Hodgkin. With a £300 equipment grant from the [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation "Rockefeller Foundation"), Hodgkin managed to set up a similar physiology setup to the one he had worked with at the Rockefeller Institute. He moved all his equipment to the [Plymouth Marine Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_Marine_Laboratory "Plymouth Marine Laboratory") in July 1939. There, he and Huxley managed to insert a fine [cannula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannula "Cannula") into the giant axon of squids and record [action potentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_potentials "Action potentials") from inside the nerve fibre. They sent a short note of their success to  [*Nature*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)") just before the outbreak of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II").

## Wartime activities

Despite his [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker "Quaker") upbringing, Hodgkin was eager to join the war effort as contact with the Nazis during his stay in Germany in 1932 had removed all his [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifism "Pacifism") beliefs. His first post was at the [Royal Aircraft Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Aircraft_Establishment "Royal Aircraft Establishment") where he worked on issues in aviation medicine, such as oxygen supply for pilots at high altitudes and the [decompression sickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decompression_sickness "Decompression sickness") caused by nitrogen bubbles coming out of the blood. In February 1940 he transferred to the [Telecommunications Research Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_Research_Establishment "Telecommunications Research Establishment") (TRE) where he worked on the development of centimetric [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar"), including the design of the [village Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_Gun-Laying_Turret "Automatic Gun-Laying Turret") AGLT airborne gun-laying system. He was a member of [E.G. Bowen's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_George_Bowen "Edward George Bowen") group in [St Athan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Athan "St Athan") in South Wales and lived in a local guest house together with John Pringle and [Robert Hanbury Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hanbury_Brown "Robert Hanbury Brown"). The group moved to [Swanage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swanage "Swanage") in May 1940 where Pringle replaced Bowen as leader of the group. In March 1941, Hodgkin flew on the test flight of a [Bristol Blenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Blenheim "Bristol Blenheim") fitted with the first airborne centimetric radar system. In February and March 1944, he visited the [MIT Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Laboratory "MIT Radiation Laboratory") to help foster the interchange of information on developments in radar between Britain and America.

Providing a readable account of the little-known piece of military history that he was a part of during World War II was a main motivation for Hodgkin to write his autobiography *Chance and Design: Reminiscences of Science in Peace and War*.

## 1945–1963: Action potential theory and Nobel Prize

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Hodgkin-Huxley.svg/500px-Hodgkin-Huxley.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hodgkin-Huxley.svg)

Basic components of Hodgkin–Huxley-type models. Hodgkin–Huxley type models represent the biophysical characteristics of cell membranes. The lipid bilayer is represented as a capacitance (*C*m). Voltage-gated and leak ion channels are represented by nonlinear (*g*n) and linear (*g*L) conductances, respectively. The electrochemical gradients driving the flow of ions are represented by batteries (E), and ion pumps and exchangers are represented by current sources (*I*p).

As the [Allied Forces'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II "Allies of World War II") [invasion of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Normandy "Invasion of Normandy") and their continued [advance towards Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_advance_from_Paris_to_the_Rhine "Allied advance from Paris to the Rhine") in autumn 1944 suggested an end of the war in the foreseeable future, Hodgkin started to plan his return to a career in research at Cambridge. He renewed his collaboration with [W. A. H. Rushton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._A._H._Rushton "W. A. H. Rushton") and they published an article on how to calculate a nerve fibre's membrane resistance, membrane capacity, its axoplasm's resistance, and the resistance of the external fluid in which the fibre is placed, from experimental observations.

After being released from military service in August 1945 upon [Adrian's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Adrian "Edgar Adrian") request, Hodgkin was able to restart his experiments in collaboration with [Bernard Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Katz "Bernard Katz") and his pre-war collaborator [Andrew Huxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Huxley "Andrew Huxley"). They spent the summers of 1947, 1948, and 1949 at the [Plymouth Marine Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_Marine_Laboratory "Plymouth Marine Laboratory") where they continued to measure resting and action potentials from inside the giant axon of the squid. Together with Katz, he provided evidence that the permeability of the neuronal cell membrane for sodium increased during an action potential, thus allowing sodium ions to diffuse inward.
The data they had obtained in 1949 resulted in a series of five papers published in *[The Journal of Physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Journal_of_Physiology "The Journal of Physiology")* that described what became later known as the [Hodgkin–Huxley model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodgkin–Huxley_model "Hodgkin–Huxley model") of the [action potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_potential "Action potential") and eventually earned Hodgkin and Huxley the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine"). Building on work by [Kenneth S. Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Stewart_Cole "Kenneth Stewart Cole") they used a technique of [electrophysiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrophysiology "Electrophysiology"), known as the *[voltage clamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_clamp "Voltage clamp")* to measure ionic currents through the membranes of squid axons while holding the membrane voltage at a set level. They proposed that the characteristic shape of the [action potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_potential "Action potential") is caused by changes in the selective permeability of the membrane for different ions, specifically [sodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium "Sodium"), [potassium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium "Potassium"), and [chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloride "Chloride"). A [model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodgkin–Huxley_model "Hodgkin–Huxley model") that relies on a set of [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equation "Differential equation") and describes each component of an excitable cell as an electrical element was in good agreement with their empirical measurements.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Hodgkin-Cycle.jpg/500px-Hodgkin-Cycle.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hodgkin-Cycle.jpg)

The cell membrane depolarisation sequence where a small depolarization leads to an increase in sodium permeability, which leads to influx of sodium ions, which in turn depolarizes the membrane even more is now known as the [Hodgkin cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodgkin_cycle "Hodgkin cycle").

In addition, Hodgkin and Huxley's findings led them to hypothesize the existence of [ion channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_channel "Ion channel") on cell membranes, which were confirmed only decades later. Confirmation of ion channels came with the development of the [patch clamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patch_clamp "Patch clamp") leading to a Nobel prize in 1991 for [Erwin Neher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Neher "Erwin Neher") and [Bert Sakmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_Sakmann "Bert Sakmann"), and in 2003 for [Roderick MacKinnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roderick_MacKinnon "Roderick MacKinnon").

After establishing ion movements across a selectively permeable cell membrane as the mechanism of the action potential, Hodgkin turned his attention to how the ionic interchange that occurs during the action potential could be reversed afterwards. Together with [Richard Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Keynes "Richard Keynes") he demonstrated that in addition to the changes in permeability that lead to an action potential, there is a secretory mechanism that ejects sodium and absorbs potassium against the electrochemical gradients. A few years later, the Danish scientist [Jens Christian Skou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Christian_Skou "Jens Christian Skou") discovered the enzyme [Na+/K+-ATPase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na+/K+-ATPase "Na+/K+-ATPase") that uses [ATP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenosine_triphosphate "Adenosine triphosphate") to export three sodium ions in exchange for two potassium ions that are imported, for which he received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1997.

Hodgkin was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1953 by [Lord Adrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Adrian "Edgar Adrian"). In October 1961, he was told by Swedish journalists that he, [Huxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Huxley "Andrew Huxley"), and [Eccles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Eccles_(neurophysiologist) "John Eccles (neurophysiologist)") had been awarded the Nobel Prize. This turned out to be a false alarm, however, when shortly thereafter it was announced that the 1961 Prize was awarded to [Georg von Békésy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_von_Békésy "Georg von Békésy"). It was only two years later that Hodgkin, [Huxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Huxley "Andrew Huxley"), and [Eccles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Eccles_(neurophysiologist) "John Eccles (neurophysiologist)") were finally awarded the Prize "for their discoveries concerning the ionic mechanisms involved in excitation and inhibition in the peripheral and central portions of the nerve cell membrane". During the [Nobel Banquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Banquet "Nobel Banquet") on 10 December 1963, Hodgkin gave the traditional speech on behalf of the three neurophysiologists, thanking the [king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustaf_VI_Adolf "Gustaf VI Adolf") and the [Nobel Committee for Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Committee_for_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Committee for Physiology or Medicine") for the award. Incidentally, Hodgkin and his wife attended the Nobel Prize ceremony a second time, three years later, when Hodgkin's father-in-law, [Francis Peyton Rous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Peyton_Rous "Francis Peyton Rous"), was awarded the 1966 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

## Later career and administrative positions

From 1951 to 1969, Hodgkin was the Foulerton Professor of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") at Cambridge. In 1970 he became the John Humphrey Plummer Professor of Biophysics at Cambridge. Around this time he also ended his experiments on nerve at the Plymouth Marine Laboratory and switched his focus to visual research which he could do in Cambridge with the help of others while serving as [president of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Royal_Society "List of presidents of the Royal Society"). Together with [Denis Baylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Baylor "Denis Baylor") and Peter Detwiler he published a series of papers on turtle [photoreceptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoreceptor_cell "Photoreceptor cell").

From 1970 to 1975 Hodgkin served as the 53rd president of the Royal Society (PRS). During his tenure as PRS, he was knighted in 1972 and admitted into the [Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit "Order of Merit") in 1973.
From 1978 to 1985 he was the 34th [Master of Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Masters_of_Trinity_College,_Cambridge "List of Masters of Trinity College, Cambridge").

He served on the Royal Society Council from 1958 to 1960 and on the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(United_Kingdom) "Medical Research Council (United Kingdom)") from 1959 to 1963. He was foreign secretary of [the Physiological Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Physiological_Society "The Physiological Society") from 1961 to 1967. He also held additional administrative posts such as Chancellor, [University of Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leicester "University of Leicester"), from 1971 to 1984

## Personal life

During his stay at the Rockefeller Institute in 1937, Hodgkin got to know the American pathologist Francis Peyton Rous who was later awarded the 1966 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. When Rous invited him for dinner to his home, Hodgkin got to know Rous' daughter, [Marni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marni_Hodgkin "Marni Hodgkin"), who was then a student at [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College "Swarthmore College"). He proposed to her before going back to England in 1938, but she rejected him. When Hodgkin briefly returned to the US in 1944 (see [Wartime activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Hodgkin#Wartime_activities)), they reunited and got married on 31 March.

Their first daughter, Sarah, was born in April 1945, shortly before the Hodgkins moved back to Cambridge. They had three more children: Deborah Hodgkin (born 2 May 1947), Jonathan Hodgkin (born 24 August 1949), and Rachel Hodgkin (born June 1951).
Marni became a Children's Book Editor at [Macmillan Publishing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macmillan_Publishing_Company "Macmillan Publishing Company") and a successful writer of children's literature, including *Young Winter's Tales* and *Dead Indeed*. [Jonathan Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Hodgkin "Jonathan Hodgkin") became a molecular biologist at Cambridge University. Deborah Hodgkin is also a successful psychologist.

[Thomas Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hodgkin "Thomas Hodgkin") (1798–1866), who first described [Hodgkin's lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodgkin's_lymphoma "Hodgkin's lymphoma"), was Alan Hodgkin's great-uncle.

### Death

Hodgkin suffered from a series of medical problems that began soon after his retirement as Master of Trinity. In 1989 he had surgery to relieve pressure on the [spinal cord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_cord "Spinal cord") from one of the [intervertebral discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intervertebral_discs "Intervertebral discs") in his neck, which left him unable to walk without support, and with progressive disablement. Hodgkin died in 1998 in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge").
