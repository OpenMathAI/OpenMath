**George Emil Palade** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ForMemRS "ForMemRS") [HonFRMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HonFRMS "HonFRMS") (Romanian pronunciation: [[ˈdʒe̯ordʒe eˈmil paˈlade]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Romanian "Help:IPA/Romanian") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ro-George_Emil_Palade.ogg "File:Ro-George Emil Palade.ogg"); November 19, 1912 – October 7, 2008) was a [Romanian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian-American "Romanian-American") [cell biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_biology "Cell biology"). In 1974 he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") along with [Albert Claude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Claude "Albert Claude") and [Christian de Duve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_de_Duve "Christian de Duve"). The prize was granted for his innovations in [electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscopy "Electron microscopy") and [cell fractionation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_fractionation "Cell fractionation") which together laid the foundations of modern molecular [cell biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_biology "Cell biology"), the most notable discovery being the [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome") of the [endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum "Endoplasmic reticulum") – which he first described in 1955.

Palade also received the U.S. [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in Biological Sciences for "pioneering discoveries of a host of fundamental, highly organized structures in living cells" in 1986, and was previously elected a Member of the U.S. [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1961. In 1968 he was elected as an [Honorary Fellow of the Royal Microscopical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Fellow_of_the_Royal_Microscopical_Society "Honorary Fellow of the Royal Microscopical Society") (HonFRMS) and in 1984 he became a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1984 "List of fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1984").

## Education and early life

George Emil Palade was born on November 19, 1912, in [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iași "Iași"), Romania. His paternal family, the Palade, was a [boyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyar "Boyar") family of [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks "Greeks") descent, probably originally from [Thessaloniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaloniki "Thessaloniki"), with Palade's father being a professor of philosophy at the [University of Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza_University "Alexandru Ioan Cuza University"). His mother, Constanța Cantemir, was a high school teacher, descended on her father's side from the [Cantemir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantemirești "Cantemirești") family and on her mother's side from the family of Dimitrie Iosepide, which was likewise of Greek descent.

Religiously, Palade adhered to [Greek Orthodox Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Christianity "Greek Orthodox Christianity"). He received his [M.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine "Doctor of Medicine") in 1940 from the [Carol Davila School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Davila_University_of_Medicine_and_Pharmacy "Carol Davila University of Medicine and Pharmacy") in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest "Bucharest").

## Career and research

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/RO033-16.jpg/330px-RO033-16.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:RO033-16.jpg)

Palade on a 2016 Romanian stamp

Palade was a member of the faculty at [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest "University of Bucharest") until 1946, when he went to the United States to do [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research"). While assisting [Robert Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Chambers_(biologist) "Robert Chambers (biologist)") in the Biology Laboratory of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University"), he met Professor [Albert Claude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Claude "Albert Claude"). He later joined Claude at the [Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Institute_for_Medical_Research "Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research").

In 1952, Palade became a [naturalized citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen "Naturalized citizen") of the United States. He worked at the Rockefeller Institute (1958–1973), and was a professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") Medical School (1973–1990), and [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego") (1990–2008). At UCSD, Palade was Professor of Medicine in Residence (Emeritus) in the Department of Cellular & Molecular Medicine, as well as a Dean for Scientific Affairs (Emeritus), in the School of Medicine at [La Jolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla "La Jolla"), California.

In 1970, he was awarded the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), together with [Renato Dulbecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renato_Dulbecco "Renato Dulbecco") (winner of the 1975 [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in Physiology or Medicine) "*for discoveries concerning the functional organization of the cell that were seminal events in the development of modern cell biology*", related to his previous research carried out at the Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research. His Nobel lecture, delivered on December 12, 1974, was entitled: *"Intracellular Aspects of the Process of Protein Secretion"*, published in 1992 by the Nobel Prize Foundation, He was elected an Honorary [member of the Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_Romanian_Academy "List of members of the Romanian Academy") in 1975. He received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") in 1975. In 1981, Palade became a founding member of the [World Cultural Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cultural_Council "World Cultural Council").
In 1985, he became the founding editor of the *[Annual Review of Cell and Developmental Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Review_of_Cell_and_Developmental_Biology "Annual Review of Cell and Developmental Biology")*.
In 1988 he was also elected an Honorary Member of the American-Romanian Academy of Arts and Sciences (ARA).

Palade was the first Chairman of the Department of Cell Biology at Yale University. Presently, the Chair of Cell Biology at Yale is named the "George Palade Professorship".

Palade was known for his support and mentorship with young scientists and that colleagues of his saw him regularly engaged with graduate students at conferences and encouraged innovative experimental approaches that would be normally been seen as un-regular. Including the use of electron microscopy in the secretory pathway research.

At the Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research, Palade used [electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscope "Electron microscope") to study the internal organization of such cell structures as [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome"), [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondria "Mitochondria"), [chloroplasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroplast "Chloroplast"), the [Golgi apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golgi_apparatus "Golgi apparatus"), and others. His most important discovery was made while using an experimental strategy known as a [pulse-chase analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse-chase_analysis "Pulse-chase analysis"). In the experiment Palade and his colleagues were able to confirm an existing hypothesis that a secretory pathway exists and that the [Rough ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_ER "Rough ER") and the Golgi apparatus function together.

He focused on [Weibel-Palade bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weibel-Palade_bodies "Weibel-Palade bodies") (a storage organelle unique to the [endothelium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endothelium "Endothelium"), containing [von Willebrand factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Willebrand_factor "Von Willebrand factor") and various proteins) which he described together with the [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland") anatomist [Ewald R. Weibel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewald_R._Weibel "Ewald R. Weibel").

He was a member of the [American Association for Anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_Anatomy "American Association for Anatomy").

### Palade's coworkers and approach in the 1960s

The following is a concise excerpt from Palade's Autobiography appearing in the Nobel Award documents

> In the 1960s, I continued the work on the secretory process using in parallel or in succession two different approaches. The first relied exclusively on cell fractionation, and was developed in collaboration with [Philip Siekevitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Siekevitz "Philip Siekevitz"), [Lewis Joel Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Joel_Greene "Lewis Joel Greene"), [Colvin Redman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colvin_Redman?action=edit&redlink=1 "Colvin Redman"), [David Sabatini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_D._Sabatini "David D. Sabatini"), and [Yutaka Tashiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yutaka_Tashiro?action=edit&redlink=1 "Yutaka Tashiro"); it led to the characterization of the zymogen granules and to the discovery of the segregation of secretory products in the cisternal space of the endoplasmic reticulum. The second approach relied primarily on radioautography, and involved experiments on intact animals or pancreatic slices which were carried out in collaboration with Lucien Caro and especially James Jamieson. This series of investigations produced a good part of our current ideas on the synthesis and intracellular processing of proteins for export. A critical review of this line of research is presented in the Nobel Lecture.

One notes also that the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") was awarded in 2009 to [Venkatraman Ramakrishnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venkatraman_Ramakrishnan "Venkatraman Ramakrishnan"), [Thomas A. Steitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Steitz "Thomas A. Steitz"), and [Ada E. Yonath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_E._Yonath "Ada E. Yonath") "*for studies of the structure and function of the ribosome*", discovered by George Emil Palade.

## Personal life

He married Irina Malaxa (born in 1919, the daughter of industrialist [Nicolae Malaxa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Malaxa "Nicolae Malaxa")) on June 12, 1941. The couple had two children: Georgia (born in 1943) and Theodore (born in 1949). After his wife died in 1969, Palade married [Marilyn Farquhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilyn_Farquhar "Marilyn Farquhar"), a cell biologist at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego").
