**Sir Frederick Gowland Hopkins** [OM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_Merit "Member of the Order of Merit") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (20 June 1861 – 16 May 1947) was an English [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist") who was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1929, with [Christiaan Eijkman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiaan_Eijkman "Christiaan Eijkman"), for the discovery of [vitamins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin "Vitamin"). He also discovered the amino acid [tryptophan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryptophan "Tryptophan"), in 1901. He was [President of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Royal_Society "President of the Royal Society") from 1930 to 1935.

## Education and early life

Hopkins was born in [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne "Eastbourne"), Sussex, and initially attended the [City of London School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London_School "City of London School"). However, he soon transferred to Alexandra Park College in [Hornsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornsey "Hornsey") and completed his further study with the [University of London External Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London_External_Programme "University of London External Programme") (through evening classes at [Birkbeck College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkbeck_College "Birkbeck College")). He attended the medical school of [Guy's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy's_Hospital "Guy's Hospital"), which is now part of [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London "King's College London") School of Medicine.

## Career and research

After graduating, Hopkins then taught physiology and toxicology at [Guy's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy's_Hospital "Guy's Hospital") from 1894 to 1898. In 1898, while attending a meeting of the [Physiological Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiological_Society "Physiological Society"), he was invited by Sir Michael Foster to join the Physiological Laboratory in Cambridge to investigate the chemical aspects of physiology. [Biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") was not, at that time, recognised as a separate branch of science. He was a lecturer in chemical physiology at [Emmanuel College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_College,_Cambridge "Emmanuel College, Cambridge") in March 1900, when he received the academic rank [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_(Oxbridge_and_Dublin) "Master of Arts (Oxbridge and Dublin)") (MA) *honoris causa*. He earned a doctorate in physiology (D.Sc.) from the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London "University of London") in July 1902, and at the same time was given a readership in biochemistry at Trinity College. While at Cambridge he was initiated into [Freemasonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry "Freemasonry"). In 1910 he became a Fellow of Trinity College, and an Honorary Fellow of Emmanuel College. In 1914 he was elected to the Chair of Biochemistry at Cambridge University, thus becoming the first Professor in that discipline at Cambridge. His Cambridge students included neurochemistry pioneer [Judah Hirsch Quastel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judah_Hirsch_Quastel "Judah Hirsch Quastel") and pioneer [embryologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryologist "Embryologist") [Joseph Needham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Needham "Joseph Needham").

Hopkins had for a long time studied how cells obtain energy via a complex metabolic process of oxidation and reduction reactions. His study in 1907 with [Sir Walter Morley Fletcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Walter_Morley_Fletcher "Sir Walter Morley Fletcher") of the connection between lactic acid and muscle contraction was one of the central achievements of his work on the biochemistry of the cell. He and Fletcher showed that oxygen depletion causes an accumulation of lactic acid in the muscle. Their work paved the way for the later discovery by [Archibald Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Hill "Archibald Hill") and [Otto Fritz Meyerhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Fritz_Meyerhof "Otto Fritz Meyerhof") that a carbohydrate metabolic cycle supplies the energy used for muscle contraction.

In 1912 Hopkins published the work for which he is best known, demonstrating in a series of animal feeding experiments that diets consisting of pure proteins, carbohydrates, fats, minerals, and water fail to support animal growth. This led him to suggest the existence in normal diets of tiny quantities of as yet unidentified substances that are essential for animal growth and survival. These hypothetical substances he called "accessory food factors", later renamed vitamins. It was this work that led his being awarded (together with [Christiaan Eijkman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiaan_Eijkman "Christiaan Eijkman")) the 1929 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), Hopkins continued his work on the nutritional value of vitamins. His efforts were especially valuable in a time of food shortages and rationing. He agreed to study the nutritional value of margarine and found that it was, as suspected, inferior to butter because it lacked the vitamins A and D. As a result of his work, vitamin-enriched margarine was introduced in 1926.

Hopkins is credited with the discovery and characterisation in 1921 of [glutathione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutathione "Glutathione") extracted from various animal tissues. At the time he proposed that the compound was a dipeptide of [glutamic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutamic_acid "Glutamic acid") and [cysteine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cysteine "Cysteine"). The structure was controversial for many years but in 1929 he concluded that it was a tripeptide of [glutamic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutamic_acid "Glutamic acid"), [cysteine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cysteine "Cysteine") and [glycine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycine "Glycine"). This conclusion agreed with that from the independent work of [Edward Calvin Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Calvin_Kendall "Edward Calvin Kendall").

## Awards and honours

Hopkins was elected a [Foreign Associate of the National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Associate_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences "Foreign Associate of the National Academy of Sciences") (USA) in 1924. During his life, in addition to the Nobel Prize, Hopkins was awarded the [Royal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Medal "Royal Medal") of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") in 1918, the [Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Prize_for_Therapeutics_of_the_University_of_Edinburgh "Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh") in 1922, and the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal "Copley Medal") of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") in 1926.

Other significant honours were his election in 1905 as a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (FRS), Great Britain's most prestigious scientific organisation; his knighthood by King George V in 1925; and the award in 1935 of the [Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit "Order of Merit"), Great Britain's most exclusive civilian honour.

From 1930 to 1935, he served as president of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") and, in 1933, served as president of the [British Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "British Association for the Advancement of Science"). He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1937.

## Personal life

In 1898, he married Jessie Anne Stephens (1861–1937); they had one son and two daughters, the younger of whom, [Jacquetta Hawkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacquetta_Hawkes "Jacquetta Hawkes"), became a prominent archaeologist and married the author [J. B. Priestley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._Priestley "J. B. Priestley").

Gowland Hopkins died on 16 May 1947 in Cambridge and is buried at the [Parish of the Ascension Burial Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Parish_Burial_Ground,_Cambridge "Ascension Parish Burial Ground, Cambridge") in Cambridge, with his wife.
