**Sir Richard John Roberts** (born 6 September 1943) is a British [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist") and [molecular biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology"). He was awarded the 1993 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Phillip Allen Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Allen_Sharp "Phillip Allen Sharp") for the discovery of [introns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intron "Intron") in [eukaryotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote "Eukaryote") [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") and the mechanism of [gene-splicing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_engineering "Genetic engineering"). He currently works at [New England Biolabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Biolabs "New England Biolabs").

## Early life and education

Roberts was born in [Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby "Derby"), the son of Edna (Allsop) and John Roberts, an auto mechanic. When he was four, Roberts' family moved to [Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_Somerset "Bath, Somerset"). In Bath, he attended [City of Bath Boys' School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Bath_Boys'_School "City of Bath Boys' School"). As a child he at first wanted to be a [detective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detective "Detective") and then, when given a [chemistry set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry_set "Chemistry set"), a [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist"). In 1965 he graduated from the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield "University of Sheffield") with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") followed by a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in 1969. His thesis involved [phytochemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytochemical "Phytochemical") studies of [neoflavonoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoflavonoid "Neoflavonoid") and [isoflavonoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoflavonoid "Isoflavonoid").

## Career and research

During 1969–1972, he did [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University").
before moving to [Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Spring_Harbor_Laboratory "Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory"), where he was hired by [James Dewey Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dewey_Watson "James Dewey Watson"), a co-discoverer of the structure of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") and a fellow Nobel laureate. In this period he also visited the [MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRC_Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology") for the first time, working alongside [Fred Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Sanger "Frederick Sanger"). In 1977, he published his discovery of RNA splicing. In 1992, he moved to [New England Biolabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Biolabs "New England Biolabs"). The following year, he shared a Nobel Prize with his former colleague at Cold Spring Harbor [Phillip Allen Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Allen_Sharp "Phillip Allen Sharp").

Roberts's discovery of the [alternative splicing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_splicing "Alternative splicing") of genes, in particular, has had a profound impact on the study and applications of molecular biology. The realisation that individual genes could exist as separate, disconnected segments within longer strands of DNA first arose in his 1977 study of [adenovirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenovirus "Adenovirus"), one of the viruses responsible for causing the common cold. Robert's research in this field resulted in a fundamental shift in our understanding of [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics"), and has led to the discovery of split genes in higher organisms, including human beings.

## Awards and honours

In 1992, Roberts received an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree "Honorary degree") from the Faculty of Medicine at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University "Uppsala University"), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden "Sweden"). After becoming a Nobel laureate in 1993 he was awarded an Honorary Degree (Doctor of Science) by the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath "University of Bath") in 1994. Roberts also received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") in 1994. In 2021 he was awarded the [Lomonosov Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov_Gold_Medal "Lomonosov Gold Medal") of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences"). In 2025, he became an [honorary member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_title_(academic) "Honorary title (academic)") of the [academic staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_staff "Academic staff") of [D'Annunzio University of Chieti–Pescara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Annunzio_University_of_Chieti–Pescara "D'Annunzio University of Chieti–Pescara"), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy").

Roberts was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1995 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1995") and a [member of the European Molecular Biology Organization (EMBO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMBO_Member "EMBO Member") in the same year. In 2005, a multimillion-pound expansion to the chemistry department at the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield "University of Sheffield"), where he had been a student, was named after him. A refurbished science department at [Beechen Cliff School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechen_Cliff_School "Beechen Cliff School") (previously City of Bath Boys' School) was also named after Roberts, who had donated a substantial sum of his Nobel prize winnings to the school.

Roberts is an atheist and was one of the signers of the [Humanist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanist_Manifesto_II "Humanist Manifesto II"). He was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in the 2008 Birthday Honours.

Roberts is a member of the Advisory Board of [Patient Innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_Innovation "Patient Innovation"), a nonprofit, international, multilingual, free venue for patients and caregivers of any disease to share their innovations.

Roberts has been a [keynote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keynote "Keynote") speaker at the Congress of Future Medical Leaders (2014, 2015, 2016, 2020).

He also is the chairman of The Laureate Science Alliance, a non-profit supporting research worldwide.

In 2016, Roberts and other Nobelists composed and signed a "Laureates Letter Supporting Precision Agriculture (GMOs)" addressed to the leaders of Greenpeace, the United Nations and global governments and Sir Roberts has advocated for [Genetically Modified Organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetically_Modified_Organisms "Genetically Modified Organisms") (GMOs) in general and [Golden Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Rice "Golden Rice") in particular to advance health in developing countries, noting the high safety record of GM foods.
