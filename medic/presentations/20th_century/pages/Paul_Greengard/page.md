**Paul Greengard** (December 11, 1925 – April 13, 2019) was an American [neuroscientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscientist "Neuroscientist") best known for his work on the [molecular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule") and [cellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)") function of [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron "Neuron"). In 2000, Greengard, [Arvid Carlsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvid_Carlsson "Arvid Carlsson") and [Eric Kandel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Kandel "Eric Kandel") were awarded the [Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine") for their discoveries concerning [signal transduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_transduction "Signal transduction") in the [nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous_system "Nervous system"). He was [Vincent Astor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Astor "Vincent Astor") Professor at [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University"), and served on the Scientific Advisory Board of the Cure Alzheimer's Fund, as well as the Scientific Council of the [Brain & Behavior Research Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_&_Behavior_Research_Foundation "Brain & Behavior Research Foundation"). He was married to artist [Ursula von Rydingsvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_von_Rydingsvard "Ursula von Rydingsvard").

## Biography

Greengard was born in New York City, the son of Pearl (née Meister) and Benjamin Greengard, a vaudeville comedian. His older sister was actress [Irene Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Kane "Irene Kane"), who later became a writer by the name of [Chris Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Chase "Chris Chase"); she died in 2013, aged 89. Their mother died in childbirth and their father remarried in 1927. The Greengard siblings' parents were Jewish, but their stepmother was [Episcopalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States) "Episcopal Church (United States)"). He and his sister were "brought up in the Christian tradition".

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), he served in the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy "United States Navy") as an electronics technician at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") working on an early warning system against Japanese [kamikaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamikaze "Kamikaze") planes. After World War II, he attended [Hamilton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_College_(New_York) "Hamilton College (New York)") where he graduated in 1948 with a bachelor's degree in mathematics and physics. He decided against graduate school in physics because most post-war physics research was focusing on nuclear weapons, and instead became interested in [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics").

Greengard began his graduate studies at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University") in the lab of [Haldan Keffer Hartline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haldan_Keffer_Hartline "Haldan Keffer Hartline"). Inspired by a lecture by [Alan Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Hodgkin "Alan Hodgkin"), Greengard began work on the molecular and cellular function of neurons. He received his PhD in 1953 and began postdoctoral work at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London "University of London"), [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), and the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam "University of Amsterdam"). Greengard then became director of the Department of Biochemistry at the [Geigy Research Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novartis#History "Novartis").

After leaving Geigy in 1967, he worked briefly at [Yeshiva University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_University "Yeshiva University")'s [Albert Einstein College of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_College_of_Medicine "Albert Einstein College of Medicine") and [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University "Vanderbilt University") before taking a position as Professor in the Department of Pharmacology at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"). In 1983 he joined the faculty of [The Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rockefeller_University "The Rockefeller University"). Greengard was a member of the Board of Scientific Governors at [The Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scripps_Research_Institute "The Scripps Research Institute"). He was the acting chairman of the [Fisher Center for Alzheimer's Research Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_Center_for_Alzheimer's_Research_Foundation "Fisher Center for Alzheimer's Research Foundation") and served on the board of the Michael Stern Parkinson's Research Foundation, which later merged with [The Michael J. Fox Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Michael_J._Fox_Foundation "The Michael J. Fox Foundation"). Both internationally renowned foundations support the research conducted in the Greengard laboratory at [The Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rockefeller_University "The Rockefeller University").

He died on April 13, 2019.

## Research

Greengard's research focused on events inside the neuron caused by [neurotransmitters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter"). Specifically, Greengard and his fellow researchers studied the behavior of [second messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_messenger "Second messenger") cascades that transform the docking of a neurotransmitter with a receptor into permanent changes in the neuron. In a series of experiments, Greengard and his colleagues showed that when [dopamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine "Dopamine") interacts with a [receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_(biochemistry) "Receptor (biochemistry)") on the [cell membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane") of a neuron, it causes an increase in [cyclic AMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_AMP "Cyclic AMP") inside the cell. This increase of cyclic AMP, in turn activates a protein called [protein kinase A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_kinase_A "Protein kinase A"), which turns other proteins on or off by adding [phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate "Phosphate") groups in a reaction known as [phosphorylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorylation "Phosphorylation"). The proteins activated by phosphorylation can then perform a number of changes in the cell: transcribing [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") to make new proteins, moving more receptors to the [synapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synapse "Synapse") (and thus increasing the neuron's sensitivity), or moving [ion channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_channel "Ion channel") to the cell surface (and thus increasing the cell's excitability). He shared the 2000 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with [Arvid Carlsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvid_Carlsson "Arvid Carlsson") and [Eric Kandel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Kandel "Eric Kandel") for his work on the central regulatory protein [DARPP-32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPP-32 "DARPP-32").

## Family

Greengard had two sons from his first marriage, Claude and [Leslie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Greengard "Leslie Greengard"). Claude Greengard holds a PhD in mathematics from [UC Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley "University of California at Berkeley"), and is the Founder of Foss Hill Partners. Leslie holds an MD from the [Yale School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_School_of_Medicine "Yale School of Medicine") and a PhD in computer science from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"), and is a professor of mathematics and computer science at and former director of the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences "Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences") at [NYU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University"), a winner of the [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leroy_P._Steele_Prizes "Leroy P. Steele Prizes") for a seminal contribution to research, a recipient of both a [Packard Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_and_Lucile_Packard_Foundation "David and Lucile Packard Foundation") Fellowship and an [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation") [Presidential Young Investigator Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Young_Investigator_Award "Presidential Young Investigator Award"), and a member of both the [U.S. National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Engineering "United States National Academy of Engineering") and the [U.S. National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences").

In 1985, Greengard married sculptor [Ursula von Rydingsvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_von_Rydingsvard "Ursula von Rydingsvard").

## Discrimination complaints

In February 2018, a federal jury in the Southern District of New York found The Rockefeller University liable for discrimination based on race and national origin that occurred in 2007 in the lab of, and under the supervision of, Greengard.

## Pearl Meister Greengard Prize

Paul Greengard used his Nobel Prize honorarium to help fund the [Pearl Meister Greengard Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Meister_Greengard_Prize "Pearl Meister Greengard Prize"), an [award for women scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_prizes,_medals,_and_awards_for_women_in_science "List of prizes, medals, and awards for women in science"). The award is named after his mother, who died during childbirth. It was established in 2004 to shine a spotlight on exceptional [women in science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_science "Women in science"), since, as Greengard observed, "[women] are not yet receiving awards and honors at a level commensurate with their achievements." The annual prize is awarded to an outstanding woman conducting biomedical research.
