**Thomas Huckle Weller** (June 15, 1915 – August 23, 2008) was an American [virologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virologist "Virologist"). He, [John Franklin Enders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Franklin_Enders "John Franklin Enders") and [Frederick Chapman Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Chapman_Robbins "Frederick Chapman Robbins") were awarded a [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1954 for showing how to cultivate [poliomyelitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polio "Polio") viruses in a test tube, using a combination of human embryonic skin and muscle tissue.

## Biography

Weller was born and grew up in [Ann Arbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor "Ann Arbor"), [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan "Michigan"), where he graduated from [Ann Arbor High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_High_School_(Ann_Arbor,_Michigan) "Pioneer High School (Ann Arbor, Michigan)"). He then went to the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan "University of Michigan"), where his father [Carl Vernon Weller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Vernon_Weller?action=edit&redlink=1 "Carl Vernon Weller") was a professor in the Department of Pathology. At Michigan, he studied medical zoology and received a B.S. and an M.S., with his masters thesis on fish parasites.

In 1936, Weller entered [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School "Harvard Medical School"), and in 1939 began working under John Franklin Enders, with whom he would later (along with Frederick Chapman Robbins) share the Nobel Prize. It was Enders who got Weller involved in researching viruses and tissue-culture techniques for determining infectious disease causes. Weller received his MD in 1940, and went to work at Children's Hospital in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston").

In 1942, during World War II, he entered the Army Medical Corps and was stationed at the [Antilles Medical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilles_Medical_Laboratory?action=edit&redlink=1 "Antilles Medical Laboratory") in [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico"), earning the rank of Major and heading the facility's Departments of Bacteriology, Virology and Parasitology. After the War, he returned to Children's Hospital in Boston, and it was there in 1947, that he rejoined Enders in the newly created Research Division of Infectious Diseases. After several leading positions, in July 1954, he was appointed head of the Tropical Public Health Department at the [Harvard School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_School_of_Public_Health "Harvard School of Public Health"). Weller also served from 1953 to 1959 as director of the Commission on Parasitic Diseases of the American [Armed Forces Epidemiological Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Epidemiological_Board?action=edit&redlink=1 "Armed Forces Epidemiological Board").

In addition to his work on polio, Weller also contributed to treating [schistosomiasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schistosomiasis "Schistosomiasis"), and [Coxsackie viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxsackie_A_virus "Coxsackie A virus"). He was also the first to isolate the virus responsible for [chickenpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varicella "Varicella").

## Awards

In 1954, Weller was awarded the [George Ledlie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Ledlie "George Ledlie") prize in recognition of his research on [rubella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubella "Rubella"), [polio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polio "Polio") and [cytomegalovirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytomegalovirus "Cytomegalovirus")(CMV) viruses.

He was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") [in 1954](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "List of Nobel laureates in Physiology or Medicine") for his research on [polio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polio "Polio").

In 1996 he was awarded the [Walter Reed Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Reed_Medal "Walter Reed Medal") from the [American Society of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Tropical_Medicine_and_Hygiene "American Society of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene").

## Personal life

In 1945, Weller married Kathleen Fahey, who died in 2011 aged 95. They had two sons and two daughters.
