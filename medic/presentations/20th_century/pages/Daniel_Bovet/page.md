**Daniel Bovet** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") (French: [[danjɛl bove]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); Italian: [[ˈdaːnjɛl boˈve]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian "Help:IPA/Italian"); 23 March 1907 – 8 April 1992) was a Swiss-born Italian [pharmacologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacology "Pharmacology") who won the 1957 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for his discovery of drugs that block the actions of specific [neurotransmitters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter"). He is best known for his discovery in 1937 of [antihistamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antihistamine "Antihistamine"), which block the neurotransmitter [histamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histamine "Histamine") and are used in [allergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allergy "Allergy") medication. His other research included work on [chemotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemotherapy "Chemotherapy"), [sulfa drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfonamide_(medicine) "Sulfonamide (medicine)"), the [sympathetic nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system"), the pharmacology of [curare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curare "Curare"), and other neuropharmacological interests.

In 1965, Bovet led a study team which concluded that smoking of tobacco cigarettes increased users' intelligence. He told *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* that the object was not to "create geniuses, but only [to] put the less-endowed individual in a position to reach a satisfactory mental and intellectual development".

Bovet was born in [Fleurier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleurier "Fleurier"), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland"). He was a [native Esperanto speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Esperanto_speakers "Native Esperanto speakers"). He graduated from the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva "University of Geneva") in 1927 and received his doctorate in 1929. Between 1929 and 1947, he worked at the [Pasteur Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasteur_Institute "Pasteur Institute") in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"). He then moved to the [Istituto Superiore di Sanità](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Superiore_di_Sanità "Istituto Superiore di Sanità") (*Superior Institute of Health*) in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome") in 1947. Two years later, in 1949, Bovet was awarded the [Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Prize_for_Therapeutics_of_the_University_of_Edinburgh "Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh"). In 1964, he became a professor in at the [University of Sassari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sassari "University of Sassari") in Italy. From 1969 to 1971, he was the head of the Psychobiology and Psychopharmacology Laboratory of the [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_(Italy) "National Research Council (Italy)"), in Rome, before stepping down to become a professor at the [University of Rome La Sapienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_La_Sapienza "University of Rome La Sapienza"). He retired in 1982.
