**Jules Jean Baptiste Vincent Bordet** ([/bɔːrˈdeɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*bor-DAY*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"), French: [[ʒyl ʒɑ̃ batist vɛ̃sɑ̃ bɔʁdɛ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 13 June 1870 – 6 April 1961) was a Belgian [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist "Immunologist") and [microbiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiologist "Microbiologist"). The [bacterial genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus "Genus") *[Bordetella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordetella "Bordetella")* is named after him. The [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") was awarded to him in 1919 for his discoveries relating to immunity.

## Education and early life

Bordet was born at [Soignies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soignies "Soignies"), Belgium. He graduated as Doctor of Medicine from the [Free University of Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Brussels_(1834–1969) "Free University of Brussels (1834–1969)") in 1892 and began his work at the [Pasteur Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasteur_Institute "Pasteur Institute") in Paris in 1894, in the laboratory of [Elie Metchnikoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Metchnikoff "Elie Metchnikoff"), who had just discovered [phagocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagocytosis "Phagocytosis") of bacteria by [white blood cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_blood_cell "White blood cell"), an expression of cellular immunity.

## Career

In 1895 Bordet made his discovery that the [bacteriolytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriolytic "Bacteriolytic") effect of acquired specific [antibody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibody "Antibody") is significantly enhanced *in vivo* by the presence of innate serum components which he termed alexine (but which are now known as [complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complement_system "Complement system")). Four years later, in 1899, he described a similar destructive process involving complement, "[hemolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemolysis "Hemolysis")", in which foreign red blood cells are ruptured or "lysed" following exposure to immune serum. In 1900, he left Paris to found an institute in Brussels like Pasteur's, and continued to work extensively on the mechanisms involved in the action of complement. These studies became the basis for complement-fixation testing methods that enabled the development of serological tests for [syphilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syphilis "Syphilis") (specifically, the development of the [Wassermann test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassermann_test "Wassermann test") by [August von Wassermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Paul_von_Wassermann "August Paul von Wassermann")). The same technique is used today in serologic testing for countless other diseases.

With [Octave Gengou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave_Gengou "Octave Gengou"), he isolated *[Bordetella pertussis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordetella_pertussis "Bordetella pertussis")* in pure culture in 1906 and posited it as the cause of [whooping cough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pertussis "Pertussis"). He became Professor of [Bacteriology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriology "Bacteriology") at the [Université libre de Bruxelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_libre_de_Bruxelles "Université libre de Bruxelles") in 1907.

## Awards and honours

In March 1916, he was elected a Foreign Member of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") and in 1930, delivered their [Croonian Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croonian_Lecture "Croonian Lecture"). In this lecture, Bordet also concluded that [bacteriophages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriophages "Bacteriophages"), the bacteria-killing "invisible viruses" discovered by [Felix d'Herelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_d'Herelle "Felix d'Herelle") did not exist and that bacteria destroyed themselves using a process of autolysis. This theory collapsed in 1941 with the publication by [Ruska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Ruska "Helmut Ruska") of the first electron microscope pictures of bacteriophages. The [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") was awarded to him in 1919 for his discoveries relating to immunity.

Bordet died in 1961 and was interred in the [Ixelles Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ixelles_Cemetery "Ixelles Cemetery") in Brussels.

- **1919**: Member of the [Royal Academy of Science, Letters and Fine Arts of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Academy_of_Science,_Letters_and_Fine_Arts_of_Belgium "Royal Academy of Science, Letters and Fine Arts of Belgium").
- **1921**: Awarded the [Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Prize_for_Therapeutics_of_the_University_of_Edinburgh "Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh").

The [Bordet railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordet_railway_station "Bordet railway station") in Brussels is named after him.

The cancer hospital [Institut Jules Bordet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Jules_Bordet "Institut Jules Bordet") is also named after him.
