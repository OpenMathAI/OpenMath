**Bert Sakmann** (German pronunciation: [[ˈbɛʁt ˈzakˌman]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Bert_Sakmann.ogg "File:De-Bert Sakmann.ogg"); born 12 June 1942) is a German cell [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiologist "Physiologist"). He shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Erwin Neher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Neher "Erwin Neher") in 1991 for their work on "the function of single ion channels in cells," and the invention of the [patch clamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patch_clamp "Patch clamp"). Bert Sakmann was Professor at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University "Heidelberg University") and is an emeritus scientific member of the [Max Planck Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Medical_Research "Max Planck Institute for Medical Research") in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg"), Germany. Since 2008 he leads an emeritus research group at the [Max Planck Institute of Neurobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_of_Neurobiology "Max Planck Institute of Neurobiology").

## Life and career

Sakmann was born in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart "Stuttgart"), the son of Annemarie (née Schaefer), a physical therapist, and Bertold Sakmann, a theater director. Sakmann enrolled in Volksschule in [Lindau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindau "Lindau"), and completed the Wagenburg [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_(school) "Gymnasium (school)") in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart "Stuttgart") in 1961. He studied medicine from 1967 onwards in [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tübingen "Tübingen"), [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg "Freiburg"), [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin"), [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") and [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich"). After completing his medical exams at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich "LMU Munich"), he became a medical assistant in 1968 at LMU Munich, while also working as a scientific assistant (Wissenschaftlicher Assistent) at Munich's [Max-Planck-Institut für Psychiatrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck-Institut_für_Psychiatrie "Max-Planck-Institut für Psychiatrie"), in the [Neurophysiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurophysiology "Neurophysiology") Department under [Otto Detlev Creutzfeldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Detlev_Creutzfeldt "Otto Detlev Creutzfeldt"). In 1971, he moved to [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London"), where he worked in the Department of [Biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics") under [Bernard Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Katz "Bernard Katz"). In 1974, he completed his medical dissertation, under the title *Elektrophysiologie der neuralen Helladaptation in der Katzenretina* (*[Electrophysiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrophysiology "Electrophysiology") of Neural Light Adaption in the Cat Retina*) in the Medical Faculty of Göttingen University.

Afterwards (still in 1974), Sakmann returned to the lab of Otto Creutzfeldt, who had meanwhile moved to the [Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Biophysical_Chemistry "Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry") in Göttingen. Sakmann joined the membrane biology group in 1979.

In 1990, he accepted a position at the Faculty of Natural Science Medicine at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University "Heidelberg University"). One year later, he became a full university professor at the Faculty of Biology in Heidelberg.

On 2 June 2009, [Peter Gruss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gruss "Peter Gruss"), the president of the Max Planck Society, announced that Sakmann would serve as the scientific director of the Max Planck Florida Institute, the organization's biomedical research facility at [Florida Atlantic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_University "Florida Atlantic University") in [Jupiter, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter,_Florida "Jupiter, Florida").

Sakmann is the founder of the Bert-Sakmann-Stiftung.

## Awards and honors

In 1986, Sakmann and [Erwin Neher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Neher "Erwin Neher") were awarded the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"). In 1987, he received the [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize "Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize") of the [Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Forschungsgemeinschaft "Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft"), which is the highest honour awarded in German research. In 1991, he received the [Ralph W. Gerard Prize in Neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_W._Gerard_Prize_in_Neuroscience "Ralph W. Gerard Prize in Neuroscience"), the [Harvey Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Prize "Harvey Prize") and the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") along with Neher, with whom he had worked in Göttingen. In 1993 he became a member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina"). He was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1994 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1994").
