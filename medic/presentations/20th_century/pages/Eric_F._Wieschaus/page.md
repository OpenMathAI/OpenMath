**Eric Francis Wieschaus** (born June 8, 1947, in [South Bend, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend,_Indiana "South Bend, Indiana")) is an American [evolutionary developmental biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_developmental_biology "Evolutionary developmental biology") and 1995 Nobel Prize-winner.

## Early life

Born in [South Bend, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend,_Indiana "South Bend, Indiana"), he attended [John Carroll Catholic High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carroll_Catholic_High_School_(Birmingham,_Alabama) "John Carroll Catholic High School (Birmingham, Alabama)") in [Birmingham, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama "Birmingham, Alabama") before attending the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame "University of Notre Dame") for his undergraduate studies (B.S., biology), and [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") (Ph.D., biology) for his graduate work.

## Scientific career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Drosophila_melanogaster_Proboscis.jpg/250px-Drosophila_melanogaster_Proboscis.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Drosophila_melanogaster_Proboscis.jpg)

*Drosophila Melanogaster*, the object of Wieschaus's science

In 1978, he moved to his first independent job, at the [European Molecular Biology Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Molecular_Biology_Laboratory "European Molecular Biology Laboratory") in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") and moved from Heidelberg to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") in 1981.

Much of his research has focused on [embryogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drosophila_embryogenesis "Drosophila embryogenesis") in the fruit fly *[Drosophila melanogaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drosophila_melanogaster "Drosophila melanogaster")*, specifically in the patterning that occurs in the early *Drosophila* embryo. Most of the gene products used by the [embryo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryo "Embryo") at these stages are already present in the unfertilized egg and were produced by maternal transcription during [oogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oogenesis "Oogenesis"). A small number of gene products, however, are supplied by transcription in the embryo itself. He has focused on these ["zygotically"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygote "Zygote") active genes because he believes the temporal and spatial pattern of their transcription may provide the triggers controlling the normal sequence of embryonic development. Saturation of all the possible mutations on each [chromosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome "Chromosome") by random events to test embryonic lethality was done by Eric Wieschaus. This body of science eventually was termed the *[Heidelberg screen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_screen "Heidelberg screen")*.

In 1995, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Edward B. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_B._Lewis "Edward B. Lewis") and [Christiane Nüsslein-Volhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiane_Nüsslein-Volhard "Christiane Nüsslein-Volhard") as co-recipients, for their work revealing the genetic control of [embryonic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_embryonic_development "Animal embryonic development").

As of 2018, Wieschaus is the [Squibb Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._R._Squibb "E. R. Squibb") in [Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_Biology "Molecular Biology") at Princeton. He was formerly Adjunct Professor of Biochemistry at the [University of Medicine and Dentistry of New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Medicine_and_Dentistry_of_New_Jersey "University of Medicine and Dentistry of New Jersey") – [Robert Wood Johnson Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wood_Johnson_Medical_School "Robert Wood Johnson Medical School").

## Personal life

He has three daughters and is married to molecular biologist [Gertrud Schüpbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrud_Schüpbach "Gertrud Schüpbach"), who is also a professor of [Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_Biology "Molecular Biology") at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), working on *Drosophila* oogenesis.

Wieschaus is an atheist and is one of the 77 Nobel Laureates who signed the 2007 petition to repeal the [Louisiana Science Education Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Science_Education_Act "Louisiana Science Education Act").
