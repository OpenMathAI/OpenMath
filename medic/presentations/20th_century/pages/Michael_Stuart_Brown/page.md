**Michael Stuart Brown** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") [NAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") [AAA&S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") [APS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") (born April 13, 1941) is an American [geneticist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneticist "Geneticist") and [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate "Nobel laureate"). He was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Joseph L. Goldstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_L._Goldstein "Joseph L. Goldstein") in 1985 for describing the regulation of [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol") [metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism "Metabolism").

## Early life and education

Brown was born in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"), New York, on April 13, 1941, the son of Evelyn, a homemaker, and Harvey Brown, a textile salesman. His family is [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews"). He graduated from [Cheltenham High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheltenham_High_School "Cheltenham High School") in [Wyncote, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyncote,_Pennsylvania "Wyncote, Pennsylvania"). Brown graduated from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") in 1962 and received his M.D. from the [University of Pennsylvania School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perelman_School_of_Medicine "Perelman School of Medicine") in 1966.

## Career

Moving to the University of Texas Health Science Center in [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas "Dallas"), now the [UT Southwestern Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UT_Southwestern_Medical_Center "UT Southwestern Medical Center"), Brown and colleague [Joseph L. Goldstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_L._Goldstein "Joseph L. Goldstein") researched cholesterol metabolism and discovered that human cells have [low-density lipoprotein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-density_lipoprotein "Low-density lipoprotein") (LDL) receptors that extract cholesterol from the bloodstream. The lack of sufficient LDL receptors is implicated in [familial hypercholesterolemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Familial_hypercholesterolemia "Familial hypercholesterolemia"), which predisposes heavily for cholesterol-related diseases. In addition to explaining the underlying pathology of this disease, their work uncovered a fundamental aspect of cell biology - [receptor-mediated endocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor-mediated_endocytosis "Receptor-mediated endocytosis"). Their findings led to the development of [statin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statin "Statin") drugs, the [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol")-lowering compounds that today are used by 16 million Americans and are the most widely prescribed medications in the United States. Their discoveries are improving more lives every year, both in the US and around the world. New federal cholesterol guidelines will triple the number of Americans taking statin drugs to lower their cholesterol, reducing the risk of heart disease and stroke for countless people. Following these important advances, their team of dedicated researchers elucidated the role of lipid modification of proteins (protein [prenylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenylation "Prenylation")) in [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer"). In 1984 he was awarded the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") together with [Joseph L. Goldstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_L._Goldstein "Joseph L. Goldstein") (co-recipient of 1985 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine). In 1988, Brown received the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") for his contributions to medicine.

In 1993, their trainees [Xiaodong Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaodong_Wang_(biochemist) "Xiaodong Wang (biochemist)") and Michael Briggs purified the [sterol regulatory element binding proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterol_regulatory_element_binding_protein "Sterol regulatory element binding protein") (SREBPs). Since 1993, Brown, Goldstein, and their colleagues have described the unexpectedly complex machinery by which cells maintain the necessary levels of fats and cholesterol in the face of varying environmental circumstances.

Brown holds the W. A. (Monty) Moncrief Distinguished Chair in Cholesterol and Arteriosclerosis Research; is a Regental Professor of the University of Texas; holds the Paul J. Thomas Chair in Medicine.
