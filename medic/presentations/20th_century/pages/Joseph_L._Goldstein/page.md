**Joseph Leonard Goldstein** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") (born April 18, 1940) is an American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). He received the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1985, along with fellow [University of Texas Southwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_Southwestern_Medical_Center "University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center") researcher, [Michael Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Stuart_Brown "Michael Stuart Brown"), for their studies regarding [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol"). They discovered that human cells have [low-density lipoprotein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-density_lipoprotein "Low-density lipoprotein") (LDL) receptors that remove [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol") from the blood and that when [LDL receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LDL_receptor "LDL receptor") are not present in sufficient numbers, individuals develop [hypercholesterolemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercholesterolemia "Hypercholesterolemia") and become at risk for cholesterol related diseases, notably [coronary heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_heart_disease "Coronary heart disease"). Their studies led to the development of [statin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statin "Statin") drugs.

## Life and career

Goldstein was born in [Kingstree, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingstree,_South_Carolina "Kingstree, South Carolina"), the son of Fannie (Alpert) and Isadore E. Goldstein, who owned a clothing store. His family is [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews"). Goldstein received his BSci from [Washington and Lee University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_and_Lee_University "Washington and Lee University") in 1962, and his MD from the University of Texas [Southwestern Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_Southwestern_Medical_Center "University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center") in 1966. Upon completion of his [residency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residency_(medicine) "Residency (medicine)"), Goldstein moved to the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health") (NIH) in [Bethesda, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethesda,_Maryland "Bethesda, Maryland"), where he worked in [biochemical genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology"). In 1972, Goldstein relocated back to the Southwestern Medical Center, accepting a post as the head of the Division of Medical Genetics.

At the Southwestern Medical Center Goldstein collaborated extensively with [Michael Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Stuart_Brown "Michael Stuart Brown"), a fellow researcher at the center who had also worked at the NIH. From 1973 to 1985, Goldstein and Brown together published over one hundred major papers. They are both listed in [Thomson Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_Reuters "Thomson Reuters")’ index of highly cited authors. Frequently mentioned as a candidate for nationally prominent positions in scientific administration, Goldstein, like his colleague Michael Brown, chose to continue hands-on research.

In 1993, their postdoctoral trainees, [Wang Xiaodong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaodong_Wang_(biochemist) "Xiaodong Wang (biochemist)") and [Michael Briggs](http://www.woodlandbiosciences.com/), purified the [Sterol Regulatory Element-Binding Proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterol_regulatory_element-binding_protein "Sterol regulatory element-binding protein") (SREBPs), a family of membrane-bound transcription factors. Since 1993, Goldstein, Brown, and their colleagues have described the unexpectedly complex machinery that proteolytically releases the SREBPs from membranes, thus allowing their migration to the nucleus where they activate all the genes involved in the synthesis of cholesterol and fatty acids. The machinery for generating active SREBPs is tightly regulated by a negative feedback mechanism, which explains how cells maintain the necessary levels of fats and cholesterol in the face of varying environmental circumstances.

Goldstein is chair, Molecular Genetics at [University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_Southwestern_Medical_Center "University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center"). Together, Goldstein and Brown lead a research team that typically includes a dozen doctoral and postdoctoral trainees. They have trained over 145 graduate students and postdoctoral fellows, and six of their former postdoctoral fellows ([Thomas C. Südhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_C._Südhof "Thomas C. Südhof"), Wang Xiaodong, Helen H. Hobbs, David W. Russell, Monty Krieger, and Russell DeBose-Boyd) have been elected to the U.S. [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"). Former postdoctoral fellow [Thomas C. Südhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_C._Südhof "Thomas C. Südhof") received the 2013 Nobel Prize in Medicine or Physiology and [Helen H. Hobbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Hobbs "Helen Hobbs") received the 2015 [Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Life_Sciences "Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences").

In 1988 Goldstein received a [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in the field of molecular genetics, and in 2003 Goldstein and Brown won the [Albany Medical Center Prize in Medicine and Biomedical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany_Medical_Center_Prize_in_Medicine_and_Biomedical_Research "Albany Medical Center Prize in Medicine and Biomedical Research") in recognition for their further work in understanding cholesterol and also the discovery of an [insulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin "Insulin")-sensitive regulator, which potentially could be used to develop treatments for [diabetes mellitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_mellitus "Diabetes mellitus"). Goldstein is a member of the U.S. [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") and the [Institute of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Medicine "Institute of Medicine") and he was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1991 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1991").

Goldstein was appointed as chairman of the [Albert Lasker Medical Research Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker_Award "Lasker Award") jury in 1995, and was a recipient of the award ten years earlier. Since 2000, Goldstein has authored a series of essays on the deep relationship between art and science that appear in the annual *[Nature Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Medicine "Nature Medicine")* supplement that accompanies the Lasker Awards.

Among his professional activities, Goldstein is a member of the Board of Trustees of The [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute") and of The [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University"), where he was elected as a Life Trustee in 2015. He also serves as chairman of the Board of Scientific Counselors of the [Broad Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Institute "Broad Institute"), and is a member of the Board of Directors of [Regeneron Pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regeneron_Pharmaceuticals "Regeneron Pharmaceuticals"), Inc. He previously served on The Board of Scientific Governors of the [Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Research_Institute "Scripps Research Institute"), a nonprofit institute that conducts biomedical research.

## Essays on "The Art of Science"

Since 2000, Goldstein has authored a series of essays considering science as a creative pursuit, and explores the links between art and science. The essays have appeared in the journals Nature Medicine, Cell, and most recently, PNAS. They coincide with the annual announcement of the Lasker Awards, with which Goldstein is affiliated in the capacity of jury chairman. A collection of Goldstein's essays titled *The Art of Science* was published in 2023.
