**Tadeusz Reichstein** (20 July 1897 – 1 August 1996), also known as **Tadeus Reichstein**, was a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people "Polish people")-[Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_people "Swiss people") [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist") and a [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") laureate (1950), which was awarded for his work on the isolation of [cortisone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortisone "Cortisone").

## Early life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Tablica_tadeusza_Reichsteina_we_W%C5%82oc%C5%82awku.jpg/250px-Tablica_tadeusza_Reichsteina_we_W%C5%82oc%C5%82awku.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tablica_tadeusza_Reichsteina_we_Włocławku.jpg)

Memorial plaque in [Włocławek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Włocławek "Włocławek"), Poland

Reichstein was born on 20 July 1897 into a wealthy [Polish-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Jews "Polish Jews") family with strong Polish patriotic traditions at [Włocławek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Włocławek "Włocławek"), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire") (in the [Russian Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Partition "Russian Partition") of Poland). His parents were Gastawa (Brockmann) and Izydor Reichstein. He was named after the 18th-century Polish national hero [Tadeusz Kościuszko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Kościuszko "Tadeusz Kościuszko"). He spent his early childhood at [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev "Kiev"), where his father was an engineer. Due to the violent [pogroms occurring all over the Russian Empire in 1905](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogroms_in_the_Russian_Empire#1903–1906 "Pogroms in the Russian Empire"), his father began to explore emigration options for the family. Tadeus began his education at a boarding school in [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jena "Jena"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") and arrived in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich "Zürich"), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland") two years later (1907) at the age of 10.

## Career

Reichstein studied under [Hermann Staudinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Staudinger "Hermann Staudinger") during the latter's brief stint at the [Technical University of Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Karlsruhe "Technical University of Karlsruhe"). It was here that he met [Leopold Ruzicka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Ruzicka "Leopold Ruzicka"), also a doctoral student.

In 1933, working in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich "Zürich"), Switzerland, at the [ETHZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETHZ "ETHZ") chemical laboratories of Ruzicka, Reichstein succeeded, independently of Sir [Norman Haworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Haworth "Norman Haworth") and his collaborators in the United Kingdom, in synthesizing [vitamin C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_C "Vitamin C") (ascorbic acid) in what is now called the [Reichstein process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstein_process "Reichstein process"). In 1937, he was appointed Associate Professor at ETHZ.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Basel_2012-10-02_Mattes_%2882%29.JPG/250px-Basel_2012-10-02_Mattes_%2882%29.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Basel_2012-10-02_Mattes_(82).JPG)

Memorial plaque at Reichstein's house in [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel "Basel"), Switzerland

In 1937, Reichstein moved to the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel "University of Basel") where he became Professor of Pharmaceutical Chemistry, and then, from 1946 until his retirement in 1967, of Organic Chemistry.

Together with [Edward Calvin Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Calvin_Kendall "Edward Calvin Kendall") and [Philip Showalter Hench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Showalter_Hench "Philip Showalter Hench"), he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1950 for their work on [hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormones "Hormones") of the [adrenal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenal_cortex "Adrenal cortex") which culminated in the isolation of [cortisone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortisone "Cortisone"). In 1951, he and Kendall were jointly awarded the [Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Prize_for_Therapeutics_of_the_University_of_Edinburgh "Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh").

In later years, Reichstein became interested in the phytochemistry and [cytology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytology "Cytology") of ferns, publishing at least 80 papers on these subjects in the last three decades of his life. He had a particular interest in the use of [chromosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome "Chromosome") number and behavior in the interpretation of histories of hybridization and [polyploidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyploidy "Polyploidy"), but also continued his earlier interest in the chemical constituents of the plants.

## Retirement and death

Reichstein died at the age of 99 on 1 August 1996 in [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel "Basel"), Switzerland. The principal industrial process for the artificial synthesis of vitamin C still bears his name. Reichstein was the longest-lived Nobel laureate at the time of his death, but was surpassed in 2008 by [Rita Levi-Montalcini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Levi-Montalcini "Rita Levi-Montalcini").
