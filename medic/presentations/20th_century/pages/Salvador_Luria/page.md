**Salvador Edward Luria** ([/ˈlʊəriə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*LOOR-ee-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); born **Salvatore Luria**, Italian: [[salvaˈtoːre ˈluːrja]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian "Help:IPA/Italian"); August 13, 1912 – February 6, 1991) was an Italian [microbiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiologist "Microbiologist"), later a [naturalized U.S. citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen_of_the_United_States#Naturalization "Naturalized citizen of the United States"). He won the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1969, with [Max Delbrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Delbrück "Max Delbrück") and [Alfred Hershey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hershey "Alfred Hershey"), for their discoveries on the replication mechanism and the genetic structure of viruses. Salvador Luria also showed that bacterial resistance to viruses ([phages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phage "Phage")) is genetically inherited.

## Biography

### Early life

Luria was born Salvatore Luria in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin "Turin"), Italy to an influential Italian [Sephardi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi_Jew "Sephardi Jew") family. His parents were Davide and Ester (Sacerdote) Luria. He attended the medical school at the [University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin "University of Turin") studying with [Giuseppe Levi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Levi "Giuseppe Levi"). There, he met two other future [Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") laureates: [Rita Levi-Montalcini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Levi-Montalcini "Rita Levi-Montalcini") and [Renato Dulbecco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renato_Dulbecco "Renato Dulbecco"). He obtained his M. D. *summa cum laude* in 1935. From 1936 to 1937, Luria served his required time in the Italian army as a medical officer. He then took classes in [radiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiology "Radiology") at the [University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_La_Sapienza "University of Rome La Sapienza"). Here, he was introduced to [Max Delbrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Delbrück "Max Delbrück")'s theories on the [gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene") as a molecule and began to formulate methods for testing genetic theory with the [bacteriophages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriophages "Bacteriophages"), [viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus "Virus") that infect [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria "Bacteria").

In 1938, he received a fellowship to study in the United States, where he intended to work with Delbrück. Soon after Luria received the award, [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini "Benito Mussolini")'s [fascist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy "Fascist Italy") [banned Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_racial_laws "Italian racial laws") from academic research fellowships. Without funding sources for work in the U.S. or Italy, Luria left his home country for Paris, France in 1938. As the [Nazi German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_German "Nazi German") armies [invaded France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_France "Battle of France") in 1940, Luria fled on bicycle to [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille "Marseille") where he received an immigration [visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_(document) "Visa (document)") to the United States.

### Phage research

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/0/04/Novick_Stocker_Papazian_EMLederberg_Luria_Lindegren.jpg/250px-Novick_Stocker_Papazian_EMLederberg_Luria_Lindegren.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Novick_Stocker_Papazian_EMLederberg_Luria_Lindegren.jpg)

Salvador Luria with [Esther Lederberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Lederberg "Esther Lederberg") at the 1953 Cold Spring Harbor Symposium. In the background are [Aaron Novick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Novick "Aaron Novick"), Bruce Stocker, Haig Papazian and Geraldine Lindegren.

Luria arrived in New York City on September 12, 1940, and soon changed his first and middle names. With the help of physicist [Enrico Fermi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi "Enrico Fermi"), whom he knew from his time at the University of Rome, Luria received a [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation "Rockefeller Foundation") fellowship at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"). He soon met Delbrück and Hershey, and they collaborated on experiments at [Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Spring_Harbor_Laboratory "Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory") and in Delbrück's lab at [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University "Vanderbilt University").

His famous experiment with Delbrück in 1943, known as the [Luria–Delbrück experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luria–Delbrück_experiment "Luria–Delbrück experiment"), demonstrated statistically that inheritance in bacteria must follow [Darwinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin") rather than [Lamarckian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamarckism "Lamarckism") principles and that [mutant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutant "Mutant") bacteria occurring randomly can still bestow viral resistance without the virus being present. The idea that natural selection affects bacteria has profound consequences, for example, it explains how bacteria develop [antibiotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotic "Antibiotic") resistance.

Luria and Latarjet in 1947 published a quantitative analysis on the effect of [ultraviolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet "Ultraviolet") irradiation on [bacteriophage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriophage "Bacteriophage") multiplication during intracellular growth. During the early course of infection they found an increase in bacteriophage resistance to ultraviolet irradiation and then later a decrease. At the time this pattern, known as the Luria-Laterjet effect, was published little was known about the central role of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") in biology. Later work established that multiple specific [DNA repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_repair "DNA repair") pathways, encoded by the infecting bacteriophage, contribute to the increase in UV resistance early in infection.

From 1943 to 1950, he worked at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington "Indiana University Bloomington"). His first graduate student was [James D. Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_D._Watson "James D. Watson"), who went on to discover the structure of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") with [Francis Crick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Crick "Francis Crick"). In January 1947, Luria became a [naturalized citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen "Naturalized citizen") of the United States.

In 1950, Luria moved to the [University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana–Champaign "University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign"). In the early 1950s, Luria and [Giuseppe Bertani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Bertani "Giuseppe Bertani") discovered the phenomenon of [host-controlled restriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restriction_modification_system "Restriction modification system") and modification of a bacterial virus: a culture of *[E. coli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escherichia_coli "Escherichia coli")* can significantly reduce the production of phages grown in other strains; however, once the phage become established in that strain, they also become restricted in their ability to grow in other strains. It was later discovered by other researchers that bacteria produce [enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme "Enzyme") that cut viral DNA at particular sequences but not the bacteria's own DNA, which is protected by [methylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_methylation "DNA methylation"). These enzymes became known as [restriction enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restriction_enzyme "Restriction enzyme") and developed into one of the main molecular tools in [molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology").

Luria won the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1969, with [Max Delbrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Delbrück "Max Delbrück") and [Alfred Hershey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hershey "Alfred Hershey"), for their discoveries on the replication mechanism and the genetic structure of viruses.

### Later work

In 1959, he became chair of Microbiology at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (MIT). At MIT, he switched his research focus from phages to [cell membranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane "Cell membrane") and [bacteriocins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriocin "Bacteriocin"). While on sabbatical in 1963 to study at the [Institut Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Pasteur "Institut Pasteur") in Paris, he found that bacteriocins impair the function of cell membranes. Returning to MIT, his lab discovered that bacteriocins achieve this impairment by forming holes in the cell membrane, allowing [ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion "Ion") to flow through and destroy the [electrochemical gradient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrochemical_gradient "Electrochemical gradient") of cells. In 1972, he became chair of The [Center for Cancer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_H._Koch_Institute_for_Integrative_Cancer_Research "David H. Koch Institute for Integrative Cancer Research") at MIT. The department he established included future Nobel Prize winners [David Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Baltimore "David Baltimore"), [Susumu Tonegawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susumu_Tonegawa "Susumu Tonegawa"), [Phillip Allen Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Allen_Sharp "Phillip Allen Sharp") and [H. Robert Horvitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Robert_Horvitz "H. Robert Horvitz").

In addition to the Nobel Prize, Luria received a number of awards and recognitions. He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1959. He was named a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1960. In 1964, he was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"). From 1968 to 1969, he served as president of the [American Society for Microbiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Microbiology "American Society for Microbiology"). In 1969, he was awarded the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") together with [Max Delbrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Delbrück "Max Delbrück"), co-winner with Luria of the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1969. In the U.S. he won the 1974 [National Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Award "National Book Award") [in Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_winners_of_the_National_Book_Award#Science,_Philosophy_and_Religion "List of winners of the National Book Award") for his [popular science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_science "Popular science") book *Life: the Unfinished Experiment*
and received the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in 1991.

### Political activism

Throughout his career, Luria was an outspoken political advocate. He joined with [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling "Linus Pauling") in 1957 to protest against nuclear weapon testing. Luria was an opponent of the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") and a supporter of [organized labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_labor "Organized labor"). In the 1970s, he was involved in debates over [genetic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_engineering "Genetic engineering"), advocating a compromise position of moderate oversight and regulation rather than the extremes of a complete ban or full scientific freedom. Due to his political involvement, he was [blacklisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacklisted "Blacklisted") from receiving funding from the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health") for a short time in 1969.

[Noam Chomsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Chomsky "Noam Chomsky") describes him as a friend, and writes that Luria attempted to influence Jewish American writer [Elie Wiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Wiesel "Elie Wiesel")'s public stance on Israel.

### Death

Luria died in [Lexington, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Massachusetts "Lexington, Massachusetts") of a heart attack on 6 February 1991 at the age of 78.
