**André Michel Lwoff** (8 May 1902 – 30 September 1994) was a French [microbiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiologist "Microbiologist") and [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine").

## Education, early life and career

Lwoff was born in [Ainay-le-Château](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ainay-le-Château "Ainay-le-Château"), Allier, in [Auvergne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auvergne_(région) "Auvergne (région)"), France, into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") family of Russian-Polish origin, the son of Marie (Siminovitch), an artist, and Solomon Lwoff, a psychiatrist. He joined the [Institute Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_Pasteur "Institute Pasteur") in Paris when he was 19 years old. In 1932, he finished his PhD and, with the help of a grant from the [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation "Rockefeller Foundation"), moved with his wife and co-researcher [Marguerite Lwoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marguerite_Lwoff "Marguerite Lwoff") to the [Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Medical_Research "Max Planck Institute for Medical Research") of [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg") to [Otto Meyerhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Meyerhof "Otto Meyerhof"), where he did research on the development of [flagellates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagellate "Flagellate"). Another Rockefeller grant allowed him go to the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") in 1937. In 1938, he was appointed departmental head at the [Institut Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Pasteur "Institut Pasteur"), where he did groundbreaking research on [bacteriophages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriophage "Bacteriophage"), [microbiota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiota "Microbiota") and on the [poliovirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliovirus "Poliovirus").

## Awards and honors

He was awarded numerous prizes from the French [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences "Académie des Sciences"), the [Grand Prix Charles-Leopold Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_Charles-Leopold_Mayer "Grand Prix Charles-Leopold Mayer"), the [Leeuwenhoek Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeuwenhoek_Medal "Leeuwenhoek Medal") of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1960 and the [Keilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Keilin "David Keilin") Medal of the British [Biochemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemical_Society "Biochemical Society") in 1964. He was awarded a [Nobel Prize in Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1965 for the discovery of the mechanism that some viruses (which he named [proviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provirus "Provirus")) use to infect [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterium "Bacterium"). He was an elected member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"), the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"). Throughout his career he partnered with his wife [Marguerite Lwoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marguerite_Lwoff "Marguerite Lwoff") although he gained considerably more recognition. Lwoff was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society in 1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1958 "List of fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1958"). Lwoff was also president of the [FEMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEMS "FEMS") for a term of two years from 1974. The FEMS-Lwoff Award in microbiology is named in his honour.

## Personal life

Lwoff was married to the microbiologist and virologist Marguerite Lwoff with whom he published many works. He was also a [humanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism "Humanism") against [capital punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment "Capital punishment").
