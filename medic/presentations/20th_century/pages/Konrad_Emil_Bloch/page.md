**Konrad Emil Bloch** (German pronunciation: [[ˈkɔnʁaːt ˈblɔx]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Konrad_Bloch.ogg "File:De-Konrad Bloch.ogg"); 21 January 1912 – 15 October 2000) was a [German-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans "German Americans") [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). Bloch received the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1964 (joint with [Feodor Lynen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feodor_Lynen "Feodor Lynen")) for discoveries concerning the mechanism and [regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol_Reduction "Cholesterol Reduction") of the [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol") and [fatty acid metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatty_acid_metabolism "Fatty acid metabolism").

## Life and career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Konrad_Bloch_1963.jpg/250px-Konrad_Bloch_1963.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Konrad_Bloch_1963.jpg)

Bloch c. 1963

Bloch was born in [Neisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nysa,_Poland "Nysa, Poland") (now Nysa, [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland")), in the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire")'s [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia "Kingdom of Prussia") [Province of Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Silesia "Province of Silesia") into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") family. He was the second child of middle-class parents Hedwig (Striemer) and Frederich D. "Fritz" Bloch. He was a student at the [Gymnasium Carolinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolegium_Carolinum_Neisse "Kolegium Carolinum Neisse") in Nysa and then 1930 to 1934, he studied chemistry at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich "Technical University of Munich"). In 1934, due to the [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism") persecutions of [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew "Jew"), he fled to the *Schweizerische Forschungsinstitut* in [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos "Davos"), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland"), before moving to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") in 1936. Later he was appointed to the department of biological chemistry at [Yale Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_Medical_School "Yale Medical School").

In the United States, Bloch enrolled at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), and received a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in biochemistry in 1938. He taught at Columbia from 1939 to 1946. From there he went to the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") and then to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") as [Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Higgins "Eugene Higgins") Professor of Biochemistry in 1954, a post he held until 1982. From 1979 until 1984, he was a professor of science at their [School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_T.H._Chan_School_of_Public_Health "Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health"). After retirement at Harvard, he served as the Mack and Effie Campbell Tyner Eminent Scholar Chair in the College of Human Sciences at [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University "Florida State University").

Bloch shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1964 with his compatriat [Feodor Lynen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feodor_Lynen "Feodor Lynen"), for their discoveries concerning the mechanism and regulation of the [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol") and [fatty acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatty_acid "Fatty acid") metabolism. Their work showed that the body first makes [squalene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squalene "Squalene") from acetate over many steps and then converts the squalene to cholesterol. He traced all the carbon atoms in cholesterol back to acetate. Some of his research was conducted using radioactive acetate in bread mold: this was possible because [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi "Fungi") also produce squalene. He confirmed his results using rats. He was one of several researchers who showed that acetyl [Coenzyme A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coenzyme_A "Coenzyme A") is turned into [mevalonic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mevalonic_acid "Mevalonic acid"). Both Bloch and Lynen then showed that mevalonic acid is converted into chemically active [isoprene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoprene "Isoprene"), the precursor to squalene. Bloch also discovered that bile and a female sex hormone were made from cholesterol, which led to the discovery that all steroids were made from cholesterol. His Nobel Lecture was "The Biological Synthesis of Cholesterol."

In 1985, Bloch became a Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society"). In 1988, he was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science"). He was an elected member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"), and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society").

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Konrad_Bloch_with_family_1964.jpg/330px-Konrad_Bloch_with_family_1964.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Konrad_Bloch_with_family_1964.jpg)

Bloch with wife and children in Stockholm in 1964

Bloch and his wife Lore Teutsch first met in Munich. They married in the U.S. in 1941. They had two children, Peter Conrad Bloch and Susan Elizabeth Bloch, and two grandchildren, Benjamin Nieman Bloch and Emilie Bloch Sondel. They lived for many decades in the [mid-century modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-century_modern "Mid-century modern") enclave [Six Moon Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Moon_Hill "Six Moon Hill") in [Lexington, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Massachusetts "Lexington, Massachusetts"). He was fond of skiing, tennis, and music. Konrad died in [Burlington, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington,_Massachusetts "Burlington, Massachusetts") of congestive heart failure in 2000, aged 88. Lore Bloch died in 2010 aged 98.
