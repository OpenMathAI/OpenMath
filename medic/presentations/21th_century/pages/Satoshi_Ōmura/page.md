**Satoshi Ōmura** (大村 智, *Ōmura Satoshi*; [[oːmɯɾa saꜜtoɕi]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Japanese "Help:IPA/Japanese"); born 12 July 1935) is a Japanese [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). He is known for the discovery and development of hundreds of [pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceuticals "Pharmaceuticals") originally occurring in [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism "Microorganism"). In 2015 he shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Tu Youyou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tu_Youyou "Tu Youyou") and with [William C. Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_C._Campbell_(scientist) "William C. Campbell (scientist)") for their role in the discovery of the world's first [endectocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endectocide "Endectocide"), [ivermectin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivermectin "Ivermectin"), a safe and highly effective microfilaricide. It is believed that the large molecular size of ivermectin prevents it from crossing the blood/[aqueous humour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueous_humour "Aqueous humour") barrier, and renders the drug an important treatment of [helminthically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helminth "Helminth")-derived blindness.

## Early life and education

Satoshi Ōmura was born in [Nirasaki, Yamanashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirasaki,_Yamanashi "Nirasaki, Yamanashi"), Japan, in 1935, the second son of Ōmura family. After graduating from the [University of Yamanashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Yamanashi "University of Yamanashi") in 1958, he was appointed to science teacher at [Tokyo Metropolitan Sumida Tech High School](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京都立墨田工業高等学校 "ja:東京都立墨田工業高等学校"). In 1960, he became an auditor of [Koji Nakanishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koji_Nakanishi "Koji Nakanishi")'s course at [Tokyo University of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_University_of_Education "Tokyo University of Education"), one year later, he enrolled in the [Tokyo University of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_University_of_Science "Tokyo University of Science") (TUS) and studied sciences. Ōmura received his M.S. degree from TUS and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") in Pharmaceutical Sciences from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo "University of Tokyo") (1968, a Dissertation PhD) and a Ph.D. in [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") at TUS (1970).

## Career

Since 1965 Ōmura served at [Kitasato Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitasato_Institute "Kitasato Institute") system. From 1970 to 1990, he also became a part-time lecturer at [Tokyo University of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_University_of_Science "Tokyo University of Science").

In 1971 while he was a visiting professor at [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University "Wesleyan University"), he consulted the chairman of the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society"), [Max Tishler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Tishler "Max Tishler"), at an international conference. Together they successfully acquired research expenses from [Merck & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merck_&_Co. "Merck & Co.") Ōmura was considering continuing his research in the United States, but ultimately he decided to return to Japan.

In 1973, he became a director of the antibiotic laboratory at [Kitasato University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitasato_University "Kitasato University"), and he also started collaborative research with Merck & Co.

In 1975, he became professor of Kitasato University School of Pharmacy. Meanwhile, the Ōmura laboratory raised many researchers and produced 31 university professors and 120 doctors.

At present date, Ōmura is professor emeritus at Kitasato University and Max Tishler Professor of Chemistry at Wesleyan University.

## Research

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Avermectin_skeletal.svg/250px-Avermectin_skeletal.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Avermectin_skeletal.svg)

[Skeletal formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_formula "Skeletal formula") of [avermectin B1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abamectin "Abamectin"), one of Omura's discoveries

Satoshi Ōmura is known for the discovery and development of various pharmaceuticals originally occurring in microorganisms. He was awarded the 2015 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") jointly with [William C. Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_C._Campbell_(scientist) "William C. Campbell (scientist)") for discoveries concerning a novel therapy against infections caused by [roundworm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nematode "Nematode") [parasites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasitism "Parasitism"). More precisely, his research group isolated a strain of *[Streptomyces avermitilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streptomyces_avermitilis "Streptomyces avermitilis")* that produce the anti-parasitical compound [avermectin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avermectin "Avermectin"). Campbell later acquired these bacteria and developed the derived drug [ivermectin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivermectin "Ivermectin") that was commercialised for veterinary use in 1981, later put to human use against [Onchocerciasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onchocerciasis "Onchocerciasis") in 1987–88 with the name **Mectizan**, and is used against [river blindness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_blindness "River blindness"), [lymphatic filariasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphatic_filariasis "Lymphatic filariasis"), [scabies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scabies "Scabies"), other parasitic infections.

Since the 1970s, Ōmura has discovered more than 480 new compounds, of which 25 kinds of drugs and reagents are in use. Examples include [andrastin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrastin "Andrastin"), [herbimycin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbimycin "Herbimycin"), [neoxaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoxaline "Neoxaline") as well as:

- a specific [inhibitor of protein kinase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_kinase_inhibitor "Protein kinase inhibitor") named [staurosporine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staurosporine "Staurosporine");
- a proteasome inhibitor named [lactacystin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactacystin "Lactacystin");
- a fatty acid biosynthesis inhibitor named [cerulenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerulenin "Cerulenin");

Furthermore, compounds having a unique structure and biological activity discovered by Omura are drawing attention in [drug discovery research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_discovery_research?action=edit&redlink=1 "Drug discovery research"), and new [anticancer drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticancer_drugs "Anticancer drugs") and the like have been created.

## Social role

Ōmura served as deputy director and director at the [Kitasato Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitasato_Institute "Kitasato Institute"). He was devoted to rebuild the laboratory and promoting the establishment of the medical center that is now [Kitasato University Medical Center](https://www.kitasato-u.ac.jp/kmc-hp/). Meanwhile, he established a path to rebuilding of the corporate school juridical person, which has integrated with the School corporation Kitasato Gakuen. He succeeded in establishing a new "School corporation Kitasato Institute". In addition, he served as president of the School corporation [Joshibi University of Art and Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshibi_University_of_Art_and_Design "Joshibi University of Art and Design") twice, and served as the honorary school chief of the School corporation [Kaichi Gakuen](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/開智小学校・中学校・高等学校%20(埼玉県) "ja:開智小学校・中学校・高等学校 (埼玉県)"). In 2007, he established the [Nirasaki Omura Art Museum](http://nirasakiomura-artmuseum.com/en/) on his collection.
