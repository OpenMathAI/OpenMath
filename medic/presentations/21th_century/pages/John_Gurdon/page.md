**Sir John Bertrand Gurdon**  [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (2 October 1933 – 7 October 2025) was a British [developmental biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_biology "Developmental biology"), best known for his pioneering research in [Cellular differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_differentiation "Cellular differentiation") and [nuclear transplantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_cell_nuclear_transfer "Somatic cell nuclear transfer") and [cloning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloning "Cloning").

Awarded the [Lasker Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker_Award "Lasker Award") in 2009, in 2012, he and [Shinya Yamanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinya_Yamanaka "Shinya Yamanaka") were jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for the discovery that mature cells can be converted to [stem cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_cell "Stem cell").

## Early life and career

John Bertrand Gurdon was born on 2 October 1933 in [Dippenhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dippenhall "Dippenhall"), England, United Kingdom, and grew up in nearby [Frensham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frensham "Frensham") in [Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey "Surrey"). He attended [Frensham Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frensham_Heights_School "Frensham Heights School") Prep school before then attending [Edgeborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgeborough "Edgeborough") prep school. He then attended [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College "Eton College"), where he ranked last of the 250 boys in his year group at biology, and was in the bottom set in every other science subject. A schoolmaster wrote a report stating, "I believe he has ideas about becoming a scientist; on his present showing this is quite ridiculous." Gurdon explains it is the only document he ever framed; he also told a [reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist "Journalist"): "When you have problems like an experiment doesn't work, which often happens, it's nice to remind yourself that perhaps after all you are not so good at this job and the schoolmaster may have been right!"

Gurdon went up to [Christ Church, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford "Christ Church, Oxford"), to read [classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics "Classics") then switched to [zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology "Zoology"), graduating with an [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_(Oxford,_Cambridge_and_Dublin) "Master of Arts (Oxford, Cambridge and Dublin)") degree. For his [DPhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate "Doctorate") he studied nuclear transplantation in a frog species of the genus *[Xenopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenopus "Xenopus")*, supervised by Dr Michail Fischberg at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University "Oxford University"). After pursuing further postdoctoral work at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech "Caltech"), he returned to England where his early posts were in the Department of Zoology at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") (1962–71).

Gurdon spent much of his research career at the [MRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(United_Kingdom) "Medical Research Council (United Kingdom)") [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") and then in the Department of Zoology, where he started working in 1972. He became a professor at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University "Cambridge University") in 1983. In 1989, he became a founding member of the Wellcome/CRC Institute for Cell Biology and Cancer at Cambridge, which was renamed in his honour in 2004, serving as its chairman until 2001. He served as a [member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_council "University council") of the [Nuffield Council on Bioethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuffield_Council_on_Bioethics "Nuffield Council on Bioethics") 1991–1995, then [Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_college "Head of college") of [Magdalene College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalene_College,_Cambridge "Magdalene College, Cambridge"), from 1995 to 2002.

### Nuclear transfer

In 1958, Gurdon, then at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford"), successfully [cloned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloning "Cloning") a frog using intact [nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus") from the somatic cells of a *[Xenopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenopus "Xenopus")* tadpole. This work was an important extension of work of [Briggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Briggs_(scientist) "Robert Briggs (scientist)") and [King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Joseph_King "Thomas Joseph King") in 1952 on transplanting nuclei from embryonic [blastula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blastula "Blastula") cells and the successful induction of [polyploidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyploidy "Polyploidy") in the [stickleback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stickleback "Stickleback"), *Gasterosteus aculatus*, in 1956 by [Har Swarup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Har_Swarup "Har Swarup") reported in *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")*. At that time he could not conclusively show that the transplanted nuclei derived from a fully [differentiated cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_differentiation "Cellular differentiation"). This was finally shown in 1975 by a group working at the [Basel Institute for Immunology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Institute_for_Immunology "Basel Institute for Immunology") in Switzerland. They transplanted a nucleus from an antibody-producing lymphocyte (proof that it was fully differentiated) into an enucleated egg and obtained living tadpoles.

Gurdon's experiments captured the attention of the scientific community as it altered the notion of development and the tools and techniques he developed for nuclear transfer are still used today. The term clone (from the ancient Greek word *[κλών](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/κλών#Ancient_Greek "wikt:κλών")* (klōn, "twig")) had already been in use since the beginning of the 20th century in reference to plants. In 1963 the British biologist [J. B. S. Haldane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._S._Haldane "J. B. S. Haldane"), in describing Gurdon's results, became one of the first to use the word "clone" in reference to animals.

### Messenger RNA expression

Gurdon and colleagues also pioneered the use of *[Xenopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenopus "Xenopus")* (genus of highly aquatic frog) eggs and oocytes to translate microinjected [messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA "Messenger RNA") molecules, a technique which has been widely used to identify the proteins encoded and to study their function.

### Later career

Gurdon's later research had focused on analysing intercellular signalling factors involved in [cell differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_differentiation "Cell differentiation"), and on elucidating the mechanisms involved in [reprogramming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reprogramming "Reprogramming") the nucleus in transplantation experiments, including the role of histone variants, and [demethylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_methylation "DNA methylation") of the transplanted DNA.

## Politics and religion

Gurdon stated that he was politically "middle of the road", and religiously agnostic because "there is no scientific proof either way". During his time as [Master of Magdalene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Magdalene_College "Master of Magdalene College"), Gurdon caused some controversy by suggesting that [Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow "Fellow") might occasionally be allowed to deliver "an address on anything they would like to talk about" in [college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleges_of_the_University_of_Cambridge "Colleges of the University of Cambridge") [chapel services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_service "Church service"). In an interview with EWTN.com, Gurdon declared "I'm what you might call liberal-minded. I'm not a Roman Catholic. I'm a Christian, of the Church of England."

## Personal life and death

Gurdon married Jean Elizabeth Margaret Curtis, with whom he had two children. He believed himself to be an "anti-intellectual" and hated to read books. He was a keen skier, hiker, squash and tennis player.

Gurdon died on 7 October 2025, five days after his 92nd birthday, with professors from the University of Cambridge and the Gurdon Institute paying tribute.

## Honours and awards

Gurdon was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (FRS) [in 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1971 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1971"), and [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in the [1995 Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Birthday_Honours "1995 Birthday Honours") for "services to Developmental Biology".

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Prof._John_Gurdon_in_University_of_Yamanashi%2C_Japan%2C_11%2C_March_2016.jpg/250px-Prof._John_Gurdon_in_University_of_Yamanashi%2C_Japan%2C_11%2C_March_2016.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Prof._John_Gurdon_in_University_of_Yamanashi,_Japan,_11,_March_2016.jpg)

Prof. John Gurdon in University of Yamanashi, Japan, 11, March 2016

He was elected as member to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1978, the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1980, and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1983, since 2005 he has been an Honorary Member of the [American Association of Anatomists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_Anatomists "American Association of Anatomists"). He became a [Member of the Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Academia_Europaea "Member of the Academia Europaea") (MAE) in 1991.

In 2001, he was awarded the Edwin G. Conklin Medal from the [Society for Developmental Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Developmental_Biology "Society for Developmental Biology"). In 2004, the [Wellcome Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellcome_Trust "Wellcome Trust")/[Cancer Research UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_Research_UK "Cancer Research UK") Institute for Cell Biology and Cancer was renamed the [Gurdon Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdon_Institute "Gurdon Institute") in his honour. He was awarded the 2009 Albert Lasker Basic Medical Research Award and in 2014 delivered the [Harveian Oration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harveian_Oration "Harveian Oration") at the [Royal College of Physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians "Royal College of Physicians"). In 2017, Gurdon received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Achievement "American Academy of Achievement").

Gurdon was awarded as an Honorary Fellow of the [Cambridge Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Philosophical_Society "Cambridge Philosophical Society") in 2010, of the [Academy of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Fellow_of_the_Academy_of_Medical_Sciences "Honorary Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences") (FMedSci) in 2011, of the [Anatomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomical_Society "Anatomical Society") in 2013, the [American Association for Cancer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_Cancer_Research "American Association for Cancer Research") in 2013, the [Royal College of Physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians "Royal College of Physicians") (Hon FRCP) in 2014, and the [Royal Society of Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Biology "Royal Society of Biology") (Hon FRSB) in 2015.

He received honorary doctorates including [Hon DSc (Oxon)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degrees_of_the_University_of_Oxford "Degrees of the University of Oxford") and [Hon ScD (Cantab)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science "Doctor of Science") as well as many other [awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_academic_awards "List of academic awards") and medals.

In 1989 he was awarded the [Wolf Prize in Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Medicine "Wolf Prize in Medicine") "for his introduction of the xenopus oocyte into molecular biology and his demonstration that the nucleus of a differentiated cell and of the egg differ in expression but not in the content of genetic material".

### Nobel Prize

In 2012, Gurdon was awarded, jointly with [Shinya Yamanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinya_Yamanaka "Shinya Yamanaka"), the [Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize for Physiology or Medicine") "for the discovery that mature cells can be reprogrammed to become [pluripotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluripotent "Pluripotent")". His Nobel Lecture was called "The Egg and the Nucleus: A Battle for Supremacy".
