**Gary Bruce Ruvkun** (born March 26, 1952) is an American [molecular biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology") at [Massachusetts General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_General_Hospital "Massachusetts General Hospital") and professor of [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics") at [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School "Harvard Medical School") in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston").

Ruvkun discovered the mechanism by which *[lin-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin-4_microRNA_precursor "Lin-4 microRNA precursor")*, the first [microRNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MicroRNA "MicroRNA") (miRNA) discovered by [Victor Ambros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Ambros "Victor Ambros"), regulates the translation of target [messenger RNAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA "Messenger RNA") via imperfect base-pairing to those targets, and discovered the second miRNA, *[let-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let-7_microRNA_precursor "Let-7 microRNA precursor")*, and that it is conserved across animal phylogeny, including in humans. These miRNA discoveries revealed a new world of [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA") regulation at an unprecedented small size scale, and the mechanism of that regulation. Ruvkun also discovered many features of insulin-like signaling in the regulation of [aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageing "Ageing") and [metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism "Metabolism").

He was elected a Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 2019. Ruvkun was awarded the 2024 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for the discovery of microRNA and its role in post-transcriptional gene regulation.

## Early life and education

Ruvkun was born into a Jewish family, the son of Samuel and Dora (née Gurevich) Ruvkun, in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California "Berkeley, California").

Ruvkun received a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") (BA) with a major in [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics") from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1973. He received a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") (PhD) in biophysics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") in 1982. He conducted his doctoral studies in the laboratory of [Frederick M. Ausubel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_M._Ausubel "Frederick M. Ausubel"), where he investigated bacterial [nitrogen fixation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_fixation "Nitrogen fixation") genes. Ruvkun completed [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") with [Robert Horvitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Horvitz "Robert Horvitz") at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") (MIT) and [Walter Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Gilbert "Walter Gilbert") of Harvard.

## Research

### miRNA *lin-4*

Ruvkun's research revealed that the miRNA *lin-4*, a 22 nucleotide regulatory RNA discovered in 1992 by [Victor Ambros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Ambros "Victor Ambros")' lab, regulates its target mRNA *[lin-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin-14 "Lin-14")* by forming imperfect RNA duplexes to down-regulate translation. The first indication that the key regulatory element of the *lin-14* gene recognized by the *lin-4* gene product was in the *[lin-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin-14 "Lin-14")* 3’ untranslated region came from the analysis of *[lin-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin-14 "Lin-14")* gain-of-function mutations which showed that they are deletions of conserved elements in the *[lin-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin-14 "Lin-14")* 3’ untranslated region. Deletion of these elements relieves the normal late stage-specific repression of LIN-14 protein production, and *lin-4* is necessary for that repression by the normal *[lin-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin-14 "Lin-14")* 3' untranslated region. In a key breakthrough, the Ambros lab discovered that *lin-4* encodes a very small RNA product, defining the 22 nucleotide miRNAs. When Ambros and Ruvkun compared the sequence of the *lin-4* miRNA and the *lin-14* 3’ untranslated region, they discovered that the *lin-4* RNA base pairs with conserved bulges and loops to the 3’ untranslated region of the *lin-14* target mRNA, and that the *lin-14* gain of function mutations delete these *[lin-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin-14 "Lin-14")* complementary sites to relieve the normal repression of translation by *lin-4*. In addition, they showed that the *lin-14* 3' untranslated region could confer this *lin-4*-dependent translational repression on unrelated mRNAs by creating chimeric mRNAs that were *lin-4*-responsive. In 1993, Ruvkun reported in the journal *[Cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(journal) "Cell (journal)")* on the regulation of *lin-14* by *lin-4*. In the same issue of *Cell*, [Victor Ambros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Ambros "Victor Ambros") described the regulatory product of *lin-4* as a small RNA. These papers revealed a new world of RNA regulation at an unprecedented small size scale, and the mechanism of that regulation. Together, this research is now recognized as the first description of [microRNAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MicroRNA "MicroRNA") and the mechanism by which partially base-paired miRNA::mRNA duplexes inhibit translation.

### microRNA, *let-7*

In 2000, the Ruvkun lab reported the identification of second [C. elegans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caenorhabditis_elegans "Caenorhabditis elegans") microRNA, *let-7*, which like the first microRNA regulates translation of the target gene, in this case *lin-41*, via imperfect base pairing to the 3’ untranslated region of that mRNA. This was an indication that miRNA regulation via 3’ UTR complementarity may be a common feature, and that there were likely to be more microRNAs. The generality of microRNA regulation to other animals was established by the Ruvkun lab later in 2000, when they reported that the sequence and regulation of the *let-7* microRNA is conserved across animal phylogeny, including in humans.

### miRNAs and siRNAs

When siRNAs of the same 21-22 nucleotide size as *lin-4* and *let-7* were discovered in 1999 by Hamilton and Baulcombe in plants, the fields of RNAi and miRNAs suddenly converged. It seemed likely that the similarly sized miRNAs and siRNAs would use similar mechanisms. In a collaborative effort, the Mello and Ruvkun labs showed that the first known components of [RNA interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_interference "RNA interference") and their paralogs, Dicer and the PIWI proteins, are used by both miRNAs and siRNAs. Ruvkun's lab in 2003 identified many more miRNAs, identified miRNAs from mammalian neurons, and in 2007 discovered many new protein-cofactors for miRNA function.

### *C. elegans* metabolism and longevity

Ruvkun's laboratory has also discovered that an insulin-like signaling pathway controls *C. elegans* metabolism and longevity. Klass Johnson and [Kenyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynthia_Kenyon "Cynthia Kenyon") showed that the developmental arrest program mediated by mutations in *age-1* and *daf-2* increase *C. elegans* longevity. The Ruvkun lab established that these genes constitute an insulin like receptor and a downstream phosphatidylinositol kinase that couple to the *daf-16* gene product, a highly conserved Forkhead transcription factor. Homologues of these genes have now been implicated in regulation of human aging. These findings are also important for diabetes, since the mammalian orthologs of *daf-16* (referred to as FOXO transcription factors) are also regulated by insulin. The Ruvkun lab has used full genome RNAi libraries to discover genes that regulate aging and metabolism. Many of these genes are broadly conserved in animal phylogeny and could be targeted in diabetes drug development.

### SETG: The Search for Extraterrestrial Genomes

The Ruvkun lab in collaboration with [Maria Zuber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Zuber "Maria Zuber") at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"), Chris Carr (now at Georgia Tech), and Michael Finney (now a San Francisco biotech entrepreneur) has been developing protocols and instruments that can amplify and sequence DNA and RNA to search for life on another planet that is ancestrally related to the Tree of Life on Earth. The Search for Extraterrestrial Genomes, or SETG, project has been developing a small instrument that can determine DNA sequences on Mars (or any other planetary body), and send the information in those DNA sequence files to Earth for comparison to life on Earth.

### Innate immune surveillance

In 2012, Ruvkun made an original contribution to the field of immunology with the publication of a featured paper in the journal *[Cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(journal) "Cell (journal)")* describing an elegant mechanism for innate immune surveillance in animals that relies on the monitoring of core cellular functions in the host, which are often sabotaged by microbial toxins during the course of infection.

## Microbial life beyond the Solar System

In 2019, Ruvkun, together with Chris Carr, Mike Finney and [Maria Zuber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Zuber "Maria Zuber"), presented the argument that the appearance of sophisticated microbial life on Earth soon after it cooled, and the recent discoveries of [hot Jupiters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Jupiter "Hot Jupiter") and disruptive planetary migrations in exoplanet systems favors the spread of DNA-based microbial life across the galaxy. The SETG project is working to have [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA "NASA") send a [DNA sequencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_sequencer "DNA sequencer") to [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars "Mars") to [search for life there](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_on_Mars "Life on Mars") in the hope that [evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abiogenesis "Abiogenesis") will be uncovered that life did not arise [originally on Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earliest_known_life_forms "Earliest known life forms"), but [elsewhere in the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panspermia "Panspermia").

## Published articles and recognition

As of 2018, Ruvkun has published about 150 scientific articles. Ruvkun has received numerous awards for his contributions to medical science, for his contributions to the aging field and to the discovery of [microRNAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MicroRNAs "MicroRNAs"). He is a recipient of the [Lasker Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker_Award "Lasker Award") for Basic Medical Research, the [Gairdner Foundation International Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gairdner_Foundation_International_Award "Gairdner Foundation International Award"), and the Benjamin Franklin Medal in Life Science. Ruvkun was elected as a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") in 2008.
