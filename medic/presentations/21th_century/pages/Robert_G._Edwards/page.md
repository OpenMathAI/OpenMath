**Sir Robert Geoffrey Edwards** (27 September 1925 – 10 April 2013) was a British [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiologist "Physiologist") and pioneer in [reproductive medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_medicine "Reproductive medicine"), and [in-vitro fertilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-vitro_fertilisation "In-vitro fertilisation") (IVF) in particular. Along with obstetrician and gynaecologist [Patrick Steptoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Steptoe "Patrick Steptoe") and nurse and embryologist [Jean Purdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Purdy "Jean Purdy"), Edwards successfully pioneered conception through IVF, which led to the birth of [Louise Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Joy_Brown "Louise Joy Brown") on 25 July 1978. They founded the first IVF programme for infertile patients and trained other scientists in their techniques. Edwards was the founding editor-in-chief of *[Human Reproduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Reproduction_(journal) "Human Reproduction (journal)")* in 1986. In 2010, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") "for the development of in vitro fertilization".

## Education and early career

Edwards was born in [Batley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batley "Batley"), Yorkshire, and attended [Manchester Central High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Academy_(Moss_Side) "Manchester Academy (Moss Side)") on Whitworth Street in central Manchester, after which he served in the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army "British Army"), and then completed his undergraduate studies in biology, graduating with an [ordinary degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_degree "Ordinary degree") from [Bangor University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor_University "Bangor University"). He studied at the [Institute of Animal Genetics and Embryology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roslin_Institute "Roslin Institute") at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh "University of Edinburgh"), where he was awarded a PhD in 1955 under the supervision of R.A. Beatty and [C. H. Waddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._H._Waddington "C. H. Waddington").

## Career and research

After a year as a [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") fellow at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") he joined the scientific staff of the [National Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Medical_Research "National Institute for Medical Research") at Mill Hill. After a further year at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow "University of Glasgow"), in 1963 he moved to the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") as Ford Foundation Research Fellow at the Department of Physiology, and a member of [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge "Churchill College, Cambridge"). He was appointed [Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_(academic_rank) "Reader (academic rank)") in physiology in 1969.

### Human Fertilization

Circa 1960 Edwards started to study human [fertilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilisation "Fertilisation"), and he continued his work at Cambridge, laying the groundwork for his later success. In 1968 he was able to achieve fertilisation of a human egg in the laboratory and started to collaborate with [Patrick Steptoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Steptoe "Patrick Steptoe"), a [gynaecological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gynaecology "Gynaecology") surgeon from [Oldham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldham "Oldham"). Edwards developed human culture media to allow the fertilisation and early [embryo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryo "Embryo") culture, while Steptoe used [laparoscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laparoscopy "Laparoscopy") to recover [ovocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovocyte "Ovocyte") from patients with tubal [infertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infertility "Infertility"). Their attempts met significant hostility and opposition, including a refusal of the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(UK) "Medical Research Council (UK)") to fund their research and several lawsuits. [Roger Gosden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Gosden "Roger Gosden") was one of his first graduate students.

The birth of [Louise Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Brown "Louise Brown"), the world's first 'test-tube baby', at 11:47 pm on 25 July 1978 at the [Oldham General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldham_General_Hospital "Oldham General Hospital") made medical history: in vitro fertilisation meant a new way to help infertile couples who formerly had no possibility of having a baby. Nurse [Jean Purdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Purdy "Jean Purdy") was the first to see Brown's embryo dividing.

Bourn Hall Clinic

Refinements in technology have increased pregnancy rates and it is estimated that in 2010 about 4 million children have been born by IVF, with approximately 170,000 coming from donated [oocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oocyte "Oocyte") and embryos. Their breakthrough laid the groundwork for further innovations such as intracytoplasmatic sperm injection ([ICSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracytoplasmic_sperm_injection "Intracytoplasmic sperm injection")), embryo biopsy ([PGD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimplantation_genetic_diagnosis "Preimplantation genetic diagnosis")), and [stem cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_cell "Stem cell") research.

Edwards, Purdy, and Steptoe founded the [Bourn Hall Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourn_Hall_Clinic "Bourn Hall Clinic") as a place to advance their work and train new specialists. Purdy died in 1985 and Steptoe in 1988. Edwards continued in his career as a scientist and an editor of medical journals.

### Honours and awards

Edwards received numerous honours and awards including:

- Edwards was elected as a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1984 "List of fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1984").
- In 1988, Edwards was appointed [Commander of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire "Commander of the Order of the British Empire") (CBE) in the [1988 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_New_Year_Honours "1988 New Year Honours").
- In 1994, Doctor Honoris Causa, [University of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Valencia "University of Valencia") (Spain).
- In 2001, he was awarded the Albert Lasker Clinical Medical Research Award by the [Lasker Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker_Foundation "Lasker Foundation") "for the development of in vitro fertilization, a technological advance that has revolutionized the treatment of human infertility."
- In 2002, he was awarded Grand Hamdan International Award - Obstetrics & Gynecology by Hamdan Medical Award.
- In 2007, he was ranked 26th in *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")*'s list of 100 greatest living geniuses.
- In 2007, he was awarded an honorary doctorate from the [University of Huddersfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Huddersfield "University of Huddersfield").
- On 4 October 2010, it was announced that Edwards had been awarded the 2010 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for the development of in-vitro fertilisation. The Nobel Committee praised him for advancing the treatment of infertility and noted that babies of IVF have similar health statuses to other babies. [Göran K. Hansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göran_K._Hansson "Göran K. Hansson"), secretary of the Nobel Assembly at the Karolinska Institutet in Stockholm, announced the news. The first child of IVF [Louise Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Brown "Louise Brown") described the award as "fantastic news". A [Vatican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_City "Vatican City") official condemned the move as "completely out of order". As mentioned by [Simon Fishel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fishel "Simon Fishel"), "In December 2010, at the Nobel awards ceremony that was full of pathos in Bob's absence, these precious words were spoken, 'In the absence of this year's Nobel Laureate in Physiology or Medicine, I ask Professor Edwards' wife and long-term scientific companion, Dr [Ruth Fowler Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Fowler_Edwards "Ruth Fowler Edwards"), to come forward and receive his Prize from the hands of His Majesty the King'".”
- Edwards was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in the [2011 Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Birthday_Honours "2011 Birthday Honours") for services to human reproductive biology.
- Edwards featured in the [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4 "BBC Radio 4") series *[The New Elizabethans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Elizabethans "The New Elizabethans")* to mark the [diamond Jubilee of Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Jubilee_of_Queen_Elizabeth_II "Diamond Jubilee of Queen Elizabeth II") in 2012. A panel of seven academics, journalists, and historians named him among the group of people in the UK "whose actions during the reign of Elizabeth II have had a significant impact on lives in these islands and given the age its character".

## Politics

Edwards was a supporter of the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK) "Labour Party (UK)"), and represented [Newnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newnham,_Cambridgeshire "Newnham, Cambridgeshire") ward on [Cambridge City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_City_Council "Cambridge City Council") for two terms, from 1973 to 1978. He enjoyed the experience enough to consider at one stage standing for parliament, but nothing came of it.

## Personal life

Edwards married [Ruth Fowler Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Fowler_Edwards "Ruth Fowler Edwards") (1930–2013), also a pioneer in reproductive medicine, granddaughter of 1908 Nobel laureate physicist [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford "Ernest Rutherford") and daughter of physicist [Ralph Fowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Fowler "Ralph Fowler"), in 1956. The couple had five daughters and 12 grandchildren.

### Death

Edwards died at home near Cambridge, England on 10 April 2013. *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")* said that, as of Edwards' death, more than four million births had resulted from IVF.

A plaque was unveiled at the Bourn Hall Clinic in July 2013 by Louise Brown and Alastair MacDonald – the world's first IVF baby boy – commemorating Steptoe, Edwards and Purdy.
