**James Patrick Allison** (born August 7, 1948) is an American [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist "Immunologist") and [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates "List of Nobel laureates") who holds the position of [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor "Professor") and chair of [immunology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunology "Immunology") and executive director of [immunotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunotherapy "Immunotherapy") platform at the [MD Anderson Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MD_Anderson_Cancer_Center "MD Anderson Cancer Center") in [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston "Houston"), [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas"). Allison is Regental Professor and Founding-Director of James P. Allison Institute at the [MD Anderson Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MD_Anderson_Cancer_Center "MD Anderson Cancer Center").

His discoveries have led to [new cancer treatments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkpoint_inhibitor "Checkpoint inhibitor") for the deadliest cancers. He is also the director of the [Cancer Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_Research_Institute "Cancer Research Institute") (CRI) scientific advisory council. He has a longstanding interest in mechanisms of [T-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-cell "T-cell") development and activation, the development of novel strategies for [tumor immunotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_immunotherapy "Cancer immunotherapy"), and is recognized as one of the first people to isolate the [T-cell antigen receptor complex protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-cell_receptor "T-cell receptor").

In 2014, he was awarded the [Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Life_Sciences "Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences"); in 2018, he shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Tasuku Honjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasuku_Honjo "Tasuku Honjo").

## Early life

Allison was born on August 7, 1948, in [Alice, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice,_Texas "Alice, Texas"), the youngest of three sons of Constance Kalula (Lynn) and Albert Murphy Allison. He was inspired by his eighth-grade math teacher to pursue a career in science, spending a summer in a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation "National Science Foundation")–funded summer science-training program at the [University of Texas, Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas,_Austin "University of Texas, Austin"), and completing high-school biology by correspondence course at [Alice High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_High_School "Alice High School"). Allison earned a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree in [microbiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiology "Microbiology") from the [University of Texas, Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas,_Austin "University of Texas, Austin"), in 1969, where he was a member of [Delta Kappa Epsilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Kappa_Epsilon "Delta Kappa Epsilon") fraternity.

> "At the time, I was working on [asparaginase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asparaginase "Asparaginase"). I felt that I had to do medically related research in order to avoid going to [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") because I was at the age of being [drafted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War_draft "Vietnam War draft")." - James P. Allison

He earned his [doctor of philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_philosophy "Doctor of philosophy") degree in [biological science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_science "Biological science") in 1973, also from UT Austin, as a student of G. Barrie Kitto.

## Career

From 1974 to 1977, Allison worked as [postdoctoral fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher "Postdoctoral researcher") at [Scripps Clinic and Research Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Research_Institute "Scripps Research Institute") in [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California"). From 1977 to 1983, Allison worked as assistant biochemist and assistant professor at [M D Anderson Research Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MD_Anderson_Cancer_Center "MD Anderson Cancer Center") in [Smithville, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithville,_Texas "Smithville, Texas"). From 1983 to 1984, Allison worked as Associate biochemist and Associate professor at [M D Anderson Research Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MD_Anderson_Cancer_Center "MD Anderson Cancer Center") in [Smithville, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithville,_Texas "Smithville, Texas"). He was appointed a professor of immunology and director of the Cancer Research Laboratory at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1985 and was concurrently appointed professor at the [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco "University of California, San Francisco") from 1997.

In 2004, he moved to the [Memorial Sloan-Kettering Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Sloan-Kettering_Cancer_Center "Memorial Sloan-Kettering Cancer Center") (MSKCC) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") to become the director of the Ludwig Center for Cancer Immunotherapy and the chair of the immunology program as well as the Koch chair in immunologic studies and attending immunologist at the Memorial Sloan-Kettering Cancer Center. He was a professor of [Weill Cornell Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weill_Cornell_Medicine "Weill Cornell Medicine") and co-chair of the Department of Graduate Program in Immunology and Microbial Pathogenesis at [Weill Cornell Graduate School of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weill_Cornell_Graduate_School_of_Medical_Sciences "Weill Cornell Graduate School of Medical Sciences") from 2004 to 2012, and also a [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute") (HHMI) investigator until 2012, when he left to join the [MD Anderson Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MD_Anderson_Cancer_Center "MD Anderson Cancer Center") in 2012. Since 2012 he has been chair of immunology at [MD Anderson Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MD_Anderson_Cancer_Center "MD Anderson Cancer Center").

He is a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") and the [National Academy of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Medicine "National Academy of Medicine") (formerly Institute of Medicine), and is a fellow of the [American Academy of Microbiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Microbiology "American Academy of Microbiology") and the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science"). He is director of the [Cancer Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_Research_Institute "Cancer Research Institute") scientific advisory council. Previously, he served as president of the [American Association of Immunologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_Immunologists "American Association of Immunologists"). He is on the Research Advisory Board of Candel Therapeutics and Lytix Biopharma.

Allison serves as a commentator in the *[Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_(film) "Cancer (film)")* documentary.

## Research

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/11_Hegasy_CTLA4_PD1_Immunotherapy.png/500px-11_Hegasy_CTLA4_PD1_Immunotherapy.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:11_Hegasy_CTLA4_PD1_Immunotherapy.png)

Cancer Therapy by Inhibition of Negative Immune Regulation (CTLA4, PD1)

Allison trained at Scripps Research under tumor-immunologist Ralph Reisfeld, Ph.D., professor emeritus, researching human leukocyte antigens (HLA) and T-cells and exploring the role HLA proteins play in enabling the immune system to distinguish self from invaders. In 1977, Allison and a colleague, G. N. Callahan, reported in a letter to Nature that they had found evidence that the immune system was prevented from attacking cancer cells due to antigens’ association with additional proteins. Finding the factors that inhibited the immune attack on cancer has been key to developing checkpoint-blockade cancer immunotherapies.

In 1982, Allison first discovered the T-cell receptor. Allison's research to elucidate mechanisms of T-cell responses was conducted in the 1990s at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"). In the early 1990s, he showed that CTLA-4 acts as an inhibitory molecule to restrict T-cell responses. In 1996, Allison was the first to show that antibody blockade of a T-cell inhibitory molecule (known as [CTLA-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CTLA-4 "CTLA-4")) could lead to enhanced anti-tumor immune responses and tumor rejection.

This concept of blocking T-cell inhibitory pathways as a way of unleashing anti-tumor immune responses and eliciting clinical benefit laid the foundation for the development of other drugs that target T-cell inhibitory pathways, which have been labeled as "[immune checkpoint therapies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkpoint_inhibitor "Checkpoint inhibitor")". This work ultimately led to the clinical development of [ipilimumab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipilimumab "Ipilimumab") (Yervoy), which was approved in 2011 by the FDA for the treatment of [metastatic melanoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metastatic_melanoma "Metastatic melanoma").

Allison's research is in molecular immunology of the [T-cell antigen receptor complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-cell_receptor "T-cell receptor"), co-stimulatory receptors, and other molecules involved in T-cell activation. He is particularly interested in finding signals that lead to differentiation of [naive T-cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naive_T-cells "Naive T-cells") and also those that determine whether antigen receptor engagement will lead to functional activation or inactivation of T-cells. Once defined, the basic studies are used to develop new strategies for the treatment of [autoimmune diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoimmune_diseases "Autoimmune diseases") and [immunotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunotherapy "Immunotherapy") of [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer"). Most recently he has been interested in understanding the immune responses in cancer patients who respond to immunotherapy. He established the immunotherapy platform at MD Anderson Cancer Center to study immune responses in cancer patients.

## Honors

According to a quantitative analysis, Allison was the top-ranking recipient of the most prestigious international science awards in the period 2010–2019, having received 13 of the top 40 such awards in any field of science.

In 2011 Allison won the Jacob Heskel Gabbay Award for Biotechnology and Medicine and was awarded the [American Association of Immunologists Lifetime Achievement Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_Immunologists_Lifetime_Achievement_Award "American Association of Immunologists Lifetime Achievement Award"). In 2013 he shared the [Novartis Prize for Clinical Immunology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novartis_Prize_for_Clinical_Immunology "Novartis Prize for Clinical Immunology"). In 2014 he shared the first [Tang Prize in Biopharmaceutical Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_Prize "Tang Prize") with [Tasuku Honjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasuku_Honjo "Tasuku Honjo"), won the 9th Annual [Szent-Györgyi Prize for Progress in Cancer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szent-Györgyi_Prize_for_Progress_in_Cancer_Research "Szent-Györgyi Prize for Progress in Cancer Research") of the [National Foundation for Cancer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Foundation_for_Cancer_Research "National Foundation for Cancer Research"), received the $3 million [Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Life_Sciences "Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences"), the [Canada Gairdner International Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Gairdner_International_Award "Canada Gairdner International Award"), the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize"), the [Massry Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massry_Prize "Massry Prize") and the [Harvey Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Prize "Harvey Prize") of the [Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion "Technion") Institute of Technology in [Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa "Haifa"). In 2015, he received the [Lasker-DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker-DeBakey_Clinical_Medical_Research_Award "Lasker-DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award"). and [Paul Ehrlich and Ludwig Darmstaedter Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrlich_and_Ludwig_Darmstaedter_Prize "Paul Ehrlich and Ludwig Darmstaedter Prize").

In 2017 he received the [Wolf Prize in Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Medicine "Wolf Prize in Medicine"), the [Warren Alpert Foundation Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Alpert_Foundation_Prize "Warren Alpert Foundation Prize") and the [Balzan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balzan_Prize "Balzan Prize") for Immunological Approaches in Cancer Therapy (this prize jointly with [Robert D. Schreiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_D._Schreiber "Robert D. Schreiber")). In 2018 he received the [King Faisal International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_International_Prize "King Faisal International Prize") in Medicine, the [Jessie Stevenson Kovalenko Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Stevenson_Kovalenko_Medal "Jessie Stevenson Kovalenko Medal") and the [Albany Medical Center Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany_Medical_Center_Prize "Albany Medical Center Prize") in Medicine and Biomedical Research.

He, along with [Tasuku Honjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasuku_Honjo "Tasuku Honjo"), was jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 2018 for their discovery of cancer therapy by inhibition of negative immune regulation.

He is the subject of the 2019 documentary film "Jim Allison: Breakthrough" directed by Bill Haney. Allison received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") in 2019.

## Personal life

Allison married Malinda Bell in 1969. They have one son. They divorced in 2012. Allison met [Padmanee Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanee_Sharma "Padmanee Sharma") in 2004. Allison and Sharma became collaborators and friends and married 10 years later in 2014. Allison is stepfather to her three children.

Allison's mother died of [lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoma "Lymphoma") when he was 10. His brother died of [prostate cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostate_cancer "Prostate cancer") in 2005.

He plays the [harmonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonica "Harmonica") for a [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues") band of [immunologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunology "Immunology") and [oncologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncology "Oncology") called the Checkpoints. He also plays with a local band called the Checkmates.
