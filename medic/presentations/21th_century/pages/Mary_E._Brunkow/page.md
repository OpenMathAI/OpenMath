**Mary Elizabeth Brunkow** (born April 13, 1961) is an American [molecular biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biologist "Molecular biologist"), [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist "Immunologist") and Nobel Prize laureate. She is known for co-identifying the gene later named [FOXP3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FOXP3 "FOXP3") as the cause of the [scurfy mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scurfy_mouse "Scurfy mouse") phenotype, a finding that became foundational for modern [regulatory T cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_T_cell "Regulatory T cell") biology.

In 2025, she was jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Fred Ramsdell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Ramsdell "Fred Ramsdell") and [Shimon Sakaguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Sakaguchi "Shimon Sakaguchi") for their work in [peripheral immune tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral_tolerance "Peripheral tolerance").

## Early life and education

Brunkow was born on April 13, 1961 in [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon "Portland, Oregon"). She graduated from [St. Mary's Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary's_Academy_(Portland,_Oregon) "St. Mary's Academy (Portland, Oregon)") in Portland in 1979.

Brunkow received a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") with a major in molecular and cellular biology from the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington "University of Washington") in 1983 and a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in [molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology") from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University") in 1991. Her doctoral advisor was [Shirley M. Tilghman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_M._Tilghman "Shirley M. Tilghman"). Her doctoral dissertation was titled *Expression and function of the H19 gene in transgenic mice* (1991).

## Career

Brunkow worked in industry research in the Seattle area, at [Celltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celltech "Celltech") R&D in Bothell, Washington, which is where she and [Fred Ramsdell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Ramsdell "Fred Ramsdell") performed their Nobel Prize-winning work on FOXP3, and later she became senior program manager at the [Institute for Systems Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Systems_Biology "Institute for Systems Biology") in Seattle.

## Research

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/25-10-06-Nobel-Prize-FOXP3-Hegasy-v02.jpg/250px-25-10-06-Nobel-Prize-FOXP3-Hegasy-v02.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:25-10-06-Nobel-Prize-FOXP3-Hegasy-v02.jpg)

Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2025: Pivotal role of FoxP3+ Treg cells in peripheral immune tolerance.

Brunkow is a co-author of the 2001 *[Nature Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Genetics "Nature Genetics")* paper that identified the scurfy gene product, initially termed scurfin and later known as FOXP3, linking its disruption to a fatal [lymphoproliferative disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoproliferative_disorder "Lymphoproliferative disorder") in mice.

Brunkow's most cited work mapped the scurfy defect to FOXP3 and demonstrated that loss of this transcription factor drives uncontrolled T cell activation and lethal lymphoproliferation, positioning FOXP3 at the center of peripheral immune tolerance mediated by regulatory T cells. The genetic identification of FOXP3 provided a molecular basis for understanding how the immune system restrains self-reactivity outside the [thymus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymus "Thymus") and catalyzed extensive work on regulatory T cell development and function.

## Honors and awards

On October 6, 2025, the [Nobel Assembly at the Karolinska Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Assembly_at_the_Karolinska_Institute "Nobel Assembly at the Karolinska Institute") in [Stockholm, Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm"), announced that Brunkow, [Fred Ramsdell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Ramsdell "Fred Ramsdell"), and [Shimon Sakaguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Sakaguchi "Shimon Sakaguchi") would share the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for discoveries concerning peripheral immune tolerance. On June 24, 2026, Brunkow received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Achievement "American Academy of Achievement") presented by Awards Council members [Frances Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Arnold "Frances Arnold") and [Lotte Bjerre Knudsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_Bjerre_Knudsen "Lotte Bjerre Knudsen") at a ceremony in Washington, D.C.
