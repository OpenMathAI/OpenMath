**James Edward Rothman** (born November 3, 1950) is an American biochemist. He is the Fergus F. Wallace Professor of Biomedical Sciences at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"), the chairman of the Department of Cell Biology at [Yale School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_School_of_Medicine "Yale School of Medicine"), and the director of the Nanobiology Institute at the Yale West Campus. Rothman also concurrently serves as adjunct professor of physiology and cellular biophysics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") and a research professor at the [UCL Queen Square Institute of Neurology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCL_Queen_Square_Institute_of_Neurology "UCL Queen Square Institute of Neurology"), [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London").

Rothman was awarded the 2013 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine"), for his work on [vesicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicle_(biology_and_chemistry) "Vesicle (biology and chemistry)") trafficking (shared with [Randy Schekman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Schekman "Randy Schekman") and [Thomas C. Südhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_C._Südhof "Thomas C. Südhof")). He received many other honors including the [King Faisal International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_International_Prize "King Faisal International Prize") in 1996, the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from Columbia University and the [Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Lasker_Award_for_Basic_Medical_Research "Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research") both in 2002.

## Education

Rothman earned his high school diploma from [Pomfret School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomfret_School "Pomfret School") in 1967, then received his B.A. in physics at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") in 1971 and his Ph.D. in biological chemistry at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard "Harvard") in 1976 working with Eugene Patrick Kennedy.

## Career and research

Following his Ph.D., Rothman did [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") with [Harvey Lodish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Lodish "Harvey Lodish") at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") working on [glycosylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycosylation "Glycosylation") of [membrane proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_protein "Membrane protein"). He moved to the Department of Biochemistry at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") in 1978. He was at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University "Princeton University"), from 1988 to 1991, before coming to New York to found the Department of Cellular Biochemistry and Biophysics at [Memorial Sloan-Kettering Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Sloan-Kettering_Cancer_Center "Memorial Sloan-Kettering Cancer Center"), where he also served as vice-chairman of [Sloan-Kettering Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan-Kettering_Institute "Sloan-Kettering Institute"). In 2003, he left Sloan-Kettering to become a professor of physiology at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University")'s [College of Physicians and Surgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University_College_of_Physicians_and_Surgeons "Columbia University College of Physicians and Surgeons") and the head of Columbia's Center for Chemical Biology. He moved from Columbia to Yale in 2008, retaining a part-time appointment at Columbia. Since 2013 he is also holding a position as Distinguished Professor-in-Residence at the Shanghai Institute for Advanced Immunochemical Studies of [ShanghaiTech University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ShanghaiTech_University "ShanghaiTech University").

In 1995, Rothman joined the [Amersham plc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amersham_plc "Amersham plc") scientific advisory board. When Amersham was acquired by [GE Healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Healthcare "GE Healthcare") in 2003, Rothman was appointed as the Chief Science Advisor to GE Healthcare.

Rothman's research details how vesicles—tiny sac-like structures that transport hormones, growth factors, and other molecules within cells—know how to reach their correct destination and where and when to release their contents. This cellular trafficking underlies many critical physiological functions, including the propagation of the cell itself in division, communication between nerve cells in the brain, secretion of insulin and other hormones in the body, and nutrient uptake. Defects in this process lead to a wide variety of conditions, including diabetes and [botulism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botulism "Botulism").

His former [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral "Postdoctoral") students include [Gero Miesenböck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gero_Miesenböck "Gero Miesenböck") (postdoc) and [Suzanne Pfeffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Pfeffer "Suzanne Pfeffer").

### Awards and honors

Rothman was awarded the 2010 [Kavli Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavli_Prize "Kavli Prize") Neuroscience together with [Richard Scheller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Scheller "Richard Scheller") and [Thomas C. Südhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_C._Südhof "Thomas C. Südhof") for "discovering the molecular basis of neurotransmitters release".

Rothman was awarded the 2013 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") together with [Randy Schekman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Schekman "Randy Schekman") and [Thomas C. Südhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_C._Südhof "Thomas C. Südhof") for "their discoveries of machinery regulating vesicle traffic, a major transport system in our cells."

Rothman is a [Member of the National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences "Member of the National Academy of Sciences") and its [Institute of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Medicine "Institute of Medicine").

## Personal life

He is the son of Martin Rothman, a pediatrician, and Gloria Hartnick, both [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews").
