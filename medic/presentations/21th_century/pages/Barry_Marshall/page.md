**Barry James Marshall** (born 30 September 1951) is an Australian physician, [Nobel Laureate in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_Laureate_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize Laureate in Physiology or Medicine"), Professor of Clinical [Microbiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiology "Microbiology") and Co-Director of the Marshall Centre at the [University of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Australia "University of Western Australia"). Marshall and [Robin Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Warren "Robin Warren") showed that the bacterium *[Helicobacter pylori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicobacter_pylori "Helicobacter pylori")* (*H. pylori*) plays a major role in causing many [peptic ulcers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptic_ulcer "Peptic ulcer"), challenging decades of medical doctrine holding that ulcers were caused primarily by stress, spicy foods, and too much acid. This discovery has allowed for a breakthrough in understanding a causative link between *[Helicobacter pylori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicobacter_pylori "Helicobacter pylori")* infection and [stomach cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach_cancer "Stomach cancer").

He is a prominent example, in the tradition of [John Scott Haldane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Scott_Haldane "John Scott Haldane"), [JBS Haldane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JBS_Haldane "JBS Haldane") and, much later, [Jonas Salk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Salk "Jonas Salk"), of testing a medical hypothesis by self-administration.

## Early life and education

Marshall was born in [Kalgoorlie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalgoorlie,_Western_Australia "Kalgoorlie, Western Australia"), Western Australia and lived in Kalgoorlie and [Carnarvon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnarvon,_Western_Australia "Carnarvon, Western Australia") until moving to [Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth,_Western_Australia "Perth, Western Australia") at the age of eight. His father held various jobs, and his mother was a nurse. He is the eldest of four siblings. He attended Marist College, [Churchlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchlands "Churchlands") for his secondary education and the [University of Western Australia School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Australia_School_of_Medicine "University of Western Australia School of Medicine"), where he received a [Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Medicine,_Bachelor_of_Surgery "Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery") in 1974. He married his wife Adrienne in 1972 and has four children, a son and three daughters.

## Career and research

In 1979, Marshall was appointed Registrar in Medicine at the [Royal Perth Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Perth_Hospital "Royal Perth Hospital"). He met Dr. [Robin Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Warren "Robin Warren"), a pathologist interested in [gastritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastritis "Gastritis"), during internal medicine fellowship training at Royal Perth Hospital in 1981. Together, they both studied the presence of spiral bacteria in association with gastritis. In 1982, they performed the initial culture of *H. pylori* and developed their hypothesis on the bacterial cause of peptic ulcers and gastric cancer. It has been claimed that the *H. pylori* theory was ridiculed by established scientists and doctors, who did not believe that any bacteria could live in the acidic environment of the stomach. Marshall was quoted as saying in 1998 that "everyone was against me, but I knew I was right." On the other hand, it has also been argued that medical researchers showed a proper degree of [scientific scepticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_scepticism "Scientific scepticism") until the *H. pylori* hypothesis could be supported by evidence.

In 1982, Marshall and Warren obtained funding for one year of research. The first 30 out of 100 samples showed no support for their hypothesis. However, it was discovered that the lab technicians had been throwing out the cultures after two days. This was standard practice for throat swabs where other organisms in the mouth rendered cultures unusable after two days. Due to other hospital work, the lab technicians did not have time to immediately throw out the 31st test on the second day, and so it stayed from Thursday through to the following Monday. In that sample, they discovered the presence of *H. pylori*. They later found out that *H. pylori* grows more slowly than the conventional two days required by other mucosal bacteria, and that stomach cultures were not contaminated by other organisms.

In 1983, they submitted their findings thus far to the Gastroenterological Society of Australia, but the reviewers turned their paper down, rating it in the bottom 10% of those they received that year.

After failed attempts to infect piglets in 1984, Marshall, after having a baseline [endoscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoscopy "Endoscopy") done, drank a broth containing cultured *H. pylori*, expecting to develop, perhaps years later, an ulcer. He was surprised when, only three days later, he developed vague nausea and [halitosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halitosis "Halitosis"), due to the [achlorhydria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achlorhydria "Achlorhydria"). There was no acid to kill bacteria in the stomach and their waste products manifested as [bad breath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_breath "Bad breath"), noticed by his wife. On days 5–8, he developed achlorhydric (no acid) vomiting. On day eight, he had a repeat endoscopy, which showed massive inflammation (gastritis), and a [biopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biopsy "Biopsy") from which *H. pylori* was cultured, showing it had colonised his stomach. On the fourteenth day after ingestion, a third endoscopy was done, and Marshall began to take antibiotics. Marshall did not develop antibodies to *H. pylori*, suggesting that [innate immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innate_immunity "Innate immunity") can sometimes eradicate acute *H. pylori* infection. Marshall's illness and recovery, based on a culture of organisms extracted from a patient, fulfilled [Koch's postulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch's_postulates "Koch's postulates") for *H. pylori* and gastritis, but not for peptic ulcers. This experiment was published in 1985 in the *[Medical Journal of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Journal_of_Australia "Medical Journal of Australia")* and is among the most cited articles from the journal.

After his work at [Fremantle Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle_Hospital "Fremantle Hospital"), Marshall did research at Royal Perth Hospital (1985–86) and at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia"), USA (1986–present), before returning to Australia while remaining on the faculty of the University of Virginia. He held a Burnet Fellowship at the University of Western Australia (UWA) from 1998 to 2003. Marshall continues research related to *H. pylori* and runs the *H. pylori* Research Laboratory at UWA.

In 2007, Marshall was appointed Co-Director of The Marshall Centre for Infectious Diseases Research and Training, founded in his honour. In addition to *Helicobacter pylori* research, the Centre conducted varied research into infectious disease identification and surveillance, diagnostics and drug design, and transformative discovery. His research group expanded to embrace new technologies, including [Next-Generation Sequencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next-Generation_Sequencing "Next-Generation Sequencing") and [genomic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genomic_analysis?action=edit&redlink=1 "Genomic analysis"). Marshall also accepted a part-time appointment at the [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University "Pennsylvania State University") that same year. He established the Noisy Guts Project in 2017 – a research team dedicated to investigating new diagnostics and treatments for [irritable bowel syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irritable_bowel_syndrome "Irritable bowel syndrome"). This resulted in a spin-out company, Noisy Guts Pty Ltd, which develops [functional food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_food "Functional food") products. In August 2020, Marshall, along with Simon J. Thorpe, accepted a position at the scientific advisory board of [Brainchip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brainchip "Brainchip") INC, a computer chip company.

## Awards and honours

In 2005, the [Karolinska Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karolinska_Institutet "Karolinska Institutet") in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm") awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") to Marshall and Robin Warren, his long-time collaborator, "for their discovery of the bacterium *Helicobacter pylori* and its role in [gastritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastritis "Gastritis") and peptic ulcer disease".

Marshall also received the [Warren Alpert Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Alpert_Foundation_Prize "Warren Alpert Foundation Prize") in 1994; the [Australian Medical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Medical_Association "Australian Medical Association") Award and the [Albert Lasker Award for Clinical Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Lasker_Award_for_Clinical_Medical_Research "Albert Lasker Award for Clinical Medical Research") in 1995; the [Gairdner Foundation International Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gairdner_Foundation_International_Award "Gairdner Foundation International Award") in 1996; the [Paul Ehrlich and Ludwig Darmstaedter Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrlich_and_Ludwig_Darmstaedter_Prize "Paul Ehrlich and Ludwig Darmstaedter Prize") in 1997; the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement"), the [Dr A.H. Heineken Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_A.H._Heineken_Prize "Dr A.H. Heineken Prize") for Medicine, the [Florey Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florey_Medal "Florey Medal"), and the [Buchanan Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchanan_Medal "Buchanan Medal") of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") in 1998.

He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1999 "List of fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1999"). His certificate of election to the Royal Society reads:

> Barry Marshall, together with Robin Warren, discovered spiral bacteria in the stomachs of almost all patients with active chronic gastritis, or duodenal or gastric ulcers, and proposed that the bacteria were an important factor in the aetiology of these diseases. In 1985, Marshall showed by self-administration that this bacterium, now called Helicobacter pylori, causes acute gastritis and suggested that chronic colonisation directly leads to peptic ulceration. These results were a major challenge to the prevailing view that gastric disorders had a physiological basis, rather than being infectious diseases. Marshall showed that antibiotic and bismuth salt regimens that killed H. pylori resulted in the cure of duodenal ulcers. The view that gastric disorders are infectious diseases is now firmly established and there is increasing evidence for a role of H. pylori infection in gastric cancers. The work of Marshall has produced one of the most radical and important changes in medical perception in the last 50 years. Barry Marshall was awarded the Albert Lasker Award for Clinical Science in 1995 and the Buchanan Medal in 1998.

Marshall was awarded the [Benjamin Franklin Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Franklin_Institute_Awards "The Franklin Institute Awards") for Life Sciences in 1999; the [Keio Medical Science Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keio_Medical_Science_Prize "Keio Medical Science Prize") in 2002; and the Australian [Centenary Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenary_Medal "Centenary Medal") and [Macfarlane Burnet Medal and Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macfarlane_Burnet_Medal_and_Lecture "Macfarlane Burnet Medal and Lecture") in 2003.

Marshall was appointed a [Companion of the Order of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_of_the_Order_of_Australia "Companion of the Order of Australia") in 2007.

Marshall was elected Fellow of the [Australian Academy of Health and Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Academy_of_Health_and_Medical_Sciences "Australian Academy of Health and Medical Sciences") (FAHMS) in 2015.

Marshall was awarded the honour of [Western Australian of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian_of_the_Year_Awards "Western Australian of the Year Awards") in 2006.

Marshall was awarded the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") honorary Doctor of Science degree in 2009.

In 2015, Marshall's Alma mater, UWA, renamed the science library building into Barry J Marshall Library.

Marshall is the Ambassador for Life Sciences for Western Australia.
