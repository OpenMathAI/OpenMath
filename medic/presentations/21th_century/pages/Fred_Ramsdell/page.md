**Frederick Jay Ramsdell** (born December 4, 1960) is an American [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist "Immunologist") and Nobel Prize laureate. As of 2025, he is an advisor at Sonoma Biotherapeutics, a biotechnology company co-founded by him.

In 2025, he was jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Mary E. Brunkow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_E._Brunkow "Mary E. Brunkow") and [Shimon Sakaguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Sakaguchi "Shimon Sakaguchi") for their work in [peripheral tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral_tolerance "Peripheral tolerance").

## Early life and education

Ramsdell was born on December 4, 1960, in [Elmhurst, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmhurst,_Illinois "Elmhurst, Illinois").

After graduating from [Homestead High School (California)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_High_School_(California) "Homestead High School (California)") in 1979, Ramsdell initially attended [Foothill College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foothill_College "Foothill College") because he could not afford a four-year UC education, before transferring to [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego"). He received a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree with a major in biochemistry and cell biology from UCSD in 1983. In the same year, he enrolled at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles "University of California, Los Angeles"), as a doctoral student, and studied microbiology and immunology under the mentorship of Sidney Golub, receiving his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in 1987.

## Career

Ramsdell qualified for [food stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplemental_Nutrition_Assistance_Program "Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program") as a graduate student when he began his fellowship at the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health") for postgraduate training after earning his PhD. After that, he joined the biopharmaceutical company Immunex, where his research focus was [T cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T_cell "T cell") activation and tolerance, and gene discovery. In 1994, he joined the [Bothell, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bothell,_Washington "Bothell, Washington")-based biotechnology company Darwin Molecular (along with [Mary E. Brunkow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_E._Brunkow "Mary E. Brunkow")), where he established an immunology program. Darwin Molecular was acquired by [Chiroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiroscience "Chiroscience") in 1996. Three years later, Chiroscience merged with [Celltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celltech "Celltech") (briefly under the name Celltech Chiroscience). In 2004, Ramsdell and Brunkow left the company.

He joined [ZymoGenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZymoGenetics "ZymoGenetics") in 2004 where he led research teams focusing on novel proteins with potential regulatory activity in lymphoid cells. In 2008, he started working at [Novo Nordisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novo_Nordisk "Novo Nordisk") where he helped establish the firm's Inflammation Research Center in [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle") and became leader of the immunobiology group.

Later, he served as vice president at [aTyr Pharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATyr_Pharma "ATyr Pharma") in [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego "San Diego"), before joining the [Parker Institute for Cancer Immunotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker_Institute_for_Cancer_Immunotherapy "Parker Institute for Cancer Immunotherapy") in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), where he was chief scientific officer.

In 2019, he co-founded the San Francisco-based Sonoma Biotherapeutics with [Jeffrey Bluestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Bluestone "Jeffrey Bluestone"), Qizhi Tang and [Alexander Rudensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Rudensky "Alexander Rudensky"), where he held the role of chief scientific officer. As of 2025, he is the chair of the company's scientific advisory board.

## Research

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/25-10-06-Nobel-Prize-FOXP3-Hegasy-v02.jpg/250px-25-10-06-Nobel-Prize-FOXP3-Hegasy-v02.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:25-10-06-Nobel-Prize-FOXP3-Hegasy-v02.jpg)

Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2025: FoxP3+ Treg cells in peripheral immune tolerance

In the 1990s, while working at Celltech in Bothell, Washington, Ramsdell and Brunkow studied a strain of mice, called *scurfy*, characterized by serious [autoimmune disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoimmune_disease "Autoimmune disease"), setting out to identify the [mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation "Mutation") responsible for the phenotype. After establishing a candidate region on the [X chromosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_chromosome "X chromosome") containing approximately 20 potential genes, they identified an [insertion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insertion_(genetics) "Insertion (genetics)") of two [base pairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_pair "Base pair") in a previously unknown gene, which they named *Foxp3*. In 2001, in collaboration with [Hans D. Ochs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_D._Ochs "Hans D. Ochs"), Robert Wildin, and their teams, Ramsdell and Brunkow demonstrated that mutations in the human *[FOXP3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FOXP3 "FOXP3")* gene are found in [IPEX syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPEX_syndrome "IPEX syndrome"), a rare autoimmune disease.

## Honors and awards

In 2017, Ramsdell received, jointly with [Shimon Sakaguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Sakaguchi "Shimon Sakaguchi") and [Alexander Rudensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Rudensky "Alexander Rudensky"), the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize "Crafoord Prize") for research in [polyarthritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyarthritis "Polyarthritis"). They were cited for "their discoveries relating to [regulatory T cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_T_cell "Regulatory T cell"), which counteract harmful immune reactions in arthritis and other autoimmune diseases".

Ramsdell was jointly awarded, with Sakaguchi and [Mary E. Brunkow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_E._Brunkow "Mary E. Brunkow"), the 2025 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine"). On the day of the announcement, he was unable to be contacted to receive his prize, as he was hiking [off the grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-the-grid "Off-the-grid") in [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho"). Ramsdell later told the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") that his first response when he did learn the news from his wife was "I did not!", to which she replied that she had 200 text messages suggesting otherwise. When in Sweden to receive the award, Ramsdell donated the hiking boots he was wearing when he found out he had won the Prize to the [Nobel Prize Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_Museum "Nobel Prize Museum").
