**Harald zur Hausen** [NAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") [EASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Arts "European Academy of Sciences and Arts") [APS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") (German pronunciation: [[ˈhaʁalt tsuːɐ̯ ˈhaʊzn̩]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Harald_zur_Hausen.ogg "File:De-Harald zur Hausen.ogg"); 11 March 1936 – 29 May 2023) was a German [virologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virology "Virology"). He carried out research on [cervical cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_cancer "Cervical cancer") and discovered the role of [papilloma viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papilloma_virus "Papilloma virus") in cervical cancer, for which he received the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 2008. He was chairman of the [German Cancer Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Cancer_Research_Center "German Cancer Research Center") (*Deutsches Krebsforschungszentrum*, DKFZ) in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg").

## Early life and education

Zur Hausen was born in [Gelsenkirchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelsenkirchen "Gelsenkirchen") in a Catholic family. He completed his *[Abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur "Abitur")* at Antonianum Grammar School in [Vechta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vechta "Vechta"), then studied medicine at the universities of [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn "University of Bonn") from 1955, [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg "University of Hamburg") from 1957, and [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Düsseldorf "University of Düsseldorf") from 1958, and received a [Doctor of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine "Doctor of Medicine") degree there in 1960. He pursued internships in [Wimbern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wickede "Wickede"), [Isny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isny_im_Allgäu "Isny im Allgäu"), Gelsenkirchen, and Düsseldorf, qualifying as a physician in 1962.

## Career

He joined the Institute for Microbiology at the University of Düsseldorf as a laboratory assistant in 1962. After three and a half years there, he moved to [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia") to work at the Virus Laboratories of [Children's Hospital of Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_Hospital_of_Philadelphia "Children's Hospital of Philadelphia") together with eminent virologists [Werner and Gertrude Henle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_and_Gertrude_Henle "Werner and Gertrude Henle"), who had escaped from Nazi Germany. In 1967, he contributed to a ground-breaking study that for the first time proved a virus ([Epstein–Barr virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein–Barr_virus "Epstein–Barr virus")) can turn healthy cells (lymphocytes) into cancer cells. He became an assistant professor at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") in 1968. In 1969, he returned to Germany to become a regular teaching and researching professor at the [University of Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Würzburg "University of Würzburg")'s Institute for Virology. In 1972, he moved to the [University of Erlangen–Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen–Nuremberg "University of Erlangen–Nuremberg"). In 1977, he moved on to the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg "University of Freiburg") (Breisgau), where he headed the Department of Virology and Hygiene.

Working with [Lutz Gissmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutz_Gissmann "Lutz Gissmann"), zur Hausen first isolated human papillomavirus 6 by simple centrifugation from [genital warts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genital_wart "Genital wart"). He isolated HPV 6 DNA from genital warts, suggesting a possible new way of identifying viruses in human tumours. This discovery paid off several years later, in 1983, when zur Hausen identified HPV 16 DNA in [cervical cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_cancer "Cervical cancer") tumours by means of [Southern blot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_blot "Southern blot") hybridization. This was followed by the discovery of HPV18 a year later, thus identifying the causes of approximately 75% of human cervical cancer. The announcement of his breakthrough sparked a major scientific controversy.

From 1983 until 2003, zur Hausen served as chairman of the board and scientific advisory board member of the [German Cancer Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Cancer_Research_Center "German Cancer Research Center") (*Deutsches Krebsforschungszentrum*, DKFZ) in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg") and as professor of medicine at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University "Heidelberg University").

From 2007 to 2011, zur Hausen was a member of the scientific advisory board of Zukunftskolleg at the [University of Konstanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Konstanz "University of Konstanz"). He was editor-in-chief of the *[International Journal of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Journal_of_Cancer "International Journal of Cancer")* until the end of 2010. On 1 January 2010, zur Hausen became the vice president of [German Cancer Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Cancer_Aid "German Cancer Aid") (*Deutsche Krebshilfe*), the largest cancer charity in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe").

## Scientific merits

Zur Hausen's field of research was the study of [oncoviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncovirus "Oncovirus"). In 1976, he hypothesised that [human papillomavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_papillomavirus "Human papillomavirus") plays an important role in causing [cervical cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_cancer "Cervical cancer"). Together with his collaborators, he then identified HPV16 and HPV18 in cervical cancers in 1983–84. This research made possible the development of the [HPV vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPV_vaccine "HPV vaccine"), the first formulation of which was commercialised in 2006. He is also credited with discovery of the virus causing [genital warts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genital_warts "Genital warts") (HPV 6) and a monkey lymphotropic polyomavirus that is a close relative to a recently discovered human [Merkel cell polyomavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkel_cell_polyomavirus "Merkel cell polyomavirus"), as well as of techniques to immortalise cells with Epstein–Barr virus and to induce replication of the virus using phorbol esters. His work on papillomaviruses and cervical cancer received a great deal of scientific criticism when first published but subsequently was confirmed and was used as the basis for research on other high-risk papillomaviruses.

### Nobel Prize

Zur Hausen shared the 2008 [Nobel Prize in Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Medicine") with [Luc Montagnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc_Montagnier "Luc Montagnier") and [Françoise Barré-Sinoussi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Françoise_Barré-Sinoussi "Françoise Barré-Sinoussi"), for his discovery of [human papilloma virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_papilloma_virus "Human papilloma virus") (HPV) causing cervical cancer

The award of the 2008 Nobel Prize to zur Hausen became controversial following the revelation that Bo Angelin, a member of the Nobel Assembly that year, also sat on the board of [AstraZeneca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AstraZeneca "AstraZeneca"), a company that earns [patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent "Patent") royalties for HPV vaccines. The controversy was exacerbated by the fact that AstraZeneca had also entered into a partnership with Nobel Web and Nobel Media to sponsor documentaries and lectures to increase awareness of the prize. However, colleagues widely felt that the award was deserved, and the secretary of the Nobel Committee and Assembly issued a statement affirming that Bo Angelin was unaware of AstraZeneca's HPV vaccine patents at the time of the vote.

## Personal life

Zur Hausen had three sons from his first marriage, Jan Dirk, Axel and Gerrit. In 1993, he married [Ethel-Michele de Villiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethel-Michele_de_Villiers "Ethel-Michele de Villiers"), who at the time was a fellow researcher at the [German Cancer Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Cancer_Research_Center "German Cancer Research Center"), and who in prior years had co-authored many research journal articles with zur Hausen on papilloma virus and genital cancer, dating as far back as 1981. He acknowledged her research contributions and support in his Nobel Prize biography.

Zur Hausen died on 29 May 2023, at age 87.
