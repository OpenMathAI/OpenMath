**Mario Ramberg Capecchi** (Italian: [[ˈmaːɾjo kaˈpekki]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian "Help:IPA/Italian"); born October 6, 1937) is an Italian-born American molecular [geneticist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneticist "Geneticist") and a co-awardee of the 2007 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for discovering a method to create mice in which a specific gene is turned off, known as [knockout mice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knockout_mouse "Knockout mouse"). He shared the prize with [Martin Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Evans "Martin Evans") and [Oliver Smithies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Smithies "Oliver Smithies"). Capecchi is Distinguished Professor of [Human Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genetics "Human Genetics") and [Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology "Biology") at the [University of Utah School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah_School_of_Medicine "University of Utah School of Medicine").

## Life

Mario Capecchi was born in [Verona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verona "Verona"), Italy, as the only child of Luciano Capecchi and Lucy Ramberg, an Italian-born daughter of American-born [Impressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionist "Impressionist") painter Lucy Dodd Ramberg and German archaeologist Walter Ramberg. His parents weren't married, and due to the turmoil in Europe caused by [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), the details of his early life are unclear. In 1941 he and his mother were living near [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano "Bolzano"), about 160 miles north of his father in [Reggio Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_Emilia "Reggio Emilia") when his mother was arrested and deported for pamphleteering and belonging to an anti-[Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist "Fascist") group. Prior to her arrest she had made contingency plans by selling her belongings and giving the proceeds to a nearby peasant family to care for her child. However, it was not long before Mario ended up on the streets of Bolzano. In July 1942, a few months before his fifth birthday, Italian records suggest he was reunited with his father in Reggio Emilia, which Mario did confirm but stated that he stayed with his father for only for a few brief periods and that he mostly lived on the streets until he was placed in an orphanage towards the end of the war.

Mario almost died of malnutrition. His mother survived the war in Germany (part of the reason the details of his early life are unclear is that she would never talk about her experiences), and when it ended she began a year-long search for him. She finally found him on his ninth birthday in a hospital bed in Reggio Emilia ill with a fever and subsisting on a daily bowl of [chicory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicory "Chicory") coffee and bread crust. She took him to Rome, where he had his first bath since he had left her care and where, with money sent by his uncle, [Edward Ramberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Ramberg "Edward Ramberg"), an American physicist at [RCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Corporation "RCA Corporation"), they made arrangements to depart to the United States. He and his mother moved to [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania") to live at an "intentionally cooperative community" called [Bryn Gweled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Gweled "Bryn Gweled"), which had been co-founded by his uncle. (Capecchi's other maternal uncle, Walter Ramberg, was also a prominent American physicist). He graduated from [George School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_School "George School"), a Quaker boarding school in [Bucks County, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucks_County,_Pennsylvania "Bucks County, Pennsylvania"), in 1956.

Capecchi received his [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") and [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") in 1961 from [Antioch College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch_College "Antioch College") in [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio "Ohio"). Capecchi came to [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT "MIT") as a graduate student intending to study physics and mathematics, but during the course of his studies, he became interested in molecular biology. His change of interest was driven by the preference of working with few scientists and conducting experiments that did not require the use of big machines. He subsequently transferred to Harvard to join the lab of [James D. Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_D._Watson "James D. Watson"), co-discoverer of the structure of DNA. Capecchi received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics") in 1967 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), with his doctoral thesis completed under the tutelage of Watson. Capecchi was a Junior Fellow of the Society of Fellows at Harvard University from 1967 to 1969. In 1969 he became an assistant professor in the Department of [Biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") at [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School "Harvard Medical School"). He was promoted to associate professor in 1971. In 1973 he joined the faculty at the University of Utah. Since 1988 Capecchi has also been an investigator of the [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute"). He is a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences"). He has given a talk for [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University")'s Program in Genetics and Genomics as part of their Distinguished Lecturer Series. He was the speaker for the 2010 Racker Lectures in Biology & Medicine and Cornell Distinguished Lecture in Cell and Molecular Biology at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"). He is a member of the [Italy-USA Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy-USA_Foundation "Italy-USA Foundation"). After the Nobel committee publicly announced that Capecchi was awarded the Nobel prize, an Austrian woman named Marlene Bonelli claimed that Capecchi was her long-lost half-brother.

In May 2008, Capecchi met Bonelli, then 69, in Northern Italy, and confirmed that she was his sister.

## Knockout mice

Capecchi was awarded the Nobel prize for creating a [knockout mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knockout_mouse "Knockout mouse"). This is a mouse, created by genetic engineering and in vitro fertilization, in which a particular gene has been turned off. For this work, Capecchi was awarded the 2007 Nobel prize for medicine or physiology, along with [Martin Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Evans "Martin Evans") and [Oliver Smithies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Smithies "Oliver Smithies"), who also contributed. Capecchi has also pursued a systematic analysis of the mouse [Hox gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hox_gene "Hox gene") family. This gene family plays a key role in the control of [embryonic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryonic_development "Embryonic development") in all [multicellular animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular_animal "Multicellular animal"). They determine the placement of cellular development in the proper order along the axis of the body from head to toe.
