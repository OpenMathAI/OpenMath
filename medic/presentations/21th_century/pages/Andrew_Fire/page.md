**Andrew Zachary Fire** (born April 27, 1959) is an American [biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist "Biologist") and professor of pathology and of genetics at the [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") School of Medicine. He was awarded the [2006 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "2006 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine"), along with [Craig C. Mello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_C._Mello "Craig C. Mello"), for the discovery of [RNA interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_interference "RNA interference") (RNAi). This research was conducted at the [Carnegie Institution of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Institution_for_Science "Carnegie Institution for Science") and published in 1998.

## Biography

Andrew Z Fire was born in [Palo Alto, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto,_California "Palo Alto, California") and raised in [Sunnyvale, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnyvale,_California "Sunnyvale, California") in a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") family. He graduated from [Fremont High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_High_School_(Sunnyvale,_California) "Fremont High School (Sunnyvale, California)"). He attended the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") for his undergraduate degree, where he received a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in mathematics in 1978 at the age of 19. He then proceeded to the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology"), where he received a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in biology in 1983 under the mentorship of [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate "Nobel laureate") [geneticist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneticist "Geneticist") [Phillip Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Sharp "Phillip Sharp").

Fire moved to [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge"), England, as a [Helen Hay Whitney Postdoctoral Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Hay_Whitney_Foundation "Helen Hay Whitney Foundation"). He became a member of the [MRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(UK) "Medical Research Council (UK)") [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") group headed by Nobel laureate biologist [Sydney Brenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Brenner "Sydney Brenner").

From 1986 to 2003, Fire was a staff member of the [Carnegie Institution of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Institution_of_Washington "Carnegie Institution of Washington")’s Department of [Embryology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryology "Embryology") in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore"), [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "Maryland"). The initial work on double stranded RNA as a trigger of gene silencing was published while Fire and his group were at the Carnegie Labs. Fire became an adjunct professor in the Department of Biology at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University") in 1989 and joined the Stanford faculty in 2003. Throughout his career, Fire has been supported by research grants from the U.S. [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health").

Fire is a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"). He also serves on the Board of Scientific Counselors and the National Center for Biotechnology, National Institutes of Health.

## Nobel Prize

In 2006, Fire and Craig Mello shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for work first published in 1998 in the journal *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_(journal) "Nature (journal)")*. Fire and Mello, along with colleagues SiQun Xu, Mary Montgomery, Stephen Kostas, and Sam Driver, reported that tiny snippets of double-stranded [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA") (dsRNA) effectively shut down specific genes, driving the destruction of [messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA "Messenger RNA") (mRNA) with sequences matching the dsRNA. As a result, the mRNA cannot be translated into protein. Fire and Mello found that dsRNA was much more effective in gene silencing than the previously described method of RNA interference with single-stranded RNA. Because only small numbers of dsRNA molecules were required for the observed effect, Fire and Mello proposed that a catalytic process was involved. This hypothesis was confirmed by subsequent research.

The Nobel Prize citation, issued by Sweden's [Karolinska Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karolinska_Institute "Karolinska Institute"), said: "This year's Nobel Laureates have discovered a fundamental mechanism for controlling the flow of genetic information." The [British Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Broadcasting_Corporation "British Broadcasting Corporation") (BBC) quoted Nick Hastie, director of the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(UK) "Medical Research Council (UK)")'s Human Genetics Unit, on the scope and implications of the research:

> It is very unusual for a piece of work to completely revolutionise the whole way we think about biological processes and regulation, but this has opened up a whole new field in biology.
