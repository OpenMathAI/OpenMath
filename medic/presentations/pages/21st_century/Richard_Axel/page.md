**Richard Axel** (born July 2, 1946) is an American [molecular biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology") and [university professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_university_professors_at_Columbia_University "List of university professors at Columbia University") in the Department of Neuroscience at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") and investigator at the [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute"). His work on the [olfactory system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfactory_system "Olfactory system") won him and [Linda Buck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_B._Buck "Linda B. Buck"), a former [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") scientist in his group, the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 2004.

In February 2026, Axel resigned as co-director of Columbia University's Mortimer B. Zuckerman Mind Brain Behavior Institute, calling his past association with [Jeffrey Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein "Jeffrey Epstein") "a serious error in judgment" and apologizing for compromising trust. He also stepped down from the Howard Hughes Medical Institute.

## Education and early life

Born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") to [Polish Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Jewish "Polish Jewish") immigrants, Axel grew up in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"). He graduated from [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School "Stuyvesant High School") in 1963, (along with [Bruce Bueno de Mesquita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Bueno_de_Mesquita "Bruce Bueno de Mesquita"), [Allan Lichtman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Lichtman "Allan Lichtman"), [Ron Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Silver "Ron Silver"), and [Alexander Rosenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Rosenberg "Alexander Rosenberg")), received his B.A. in 1967 from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), and his M.D. in 1971 from [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University"). However, he was poorly suited to medicine and graduated on the promise to his department chairman that he would not practice clinically. He found his calling in research and returned to Columbia later that year, eventually becoming a full professor in 1978.

## Research and career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/Richard_Axel.jpg/250px-Richard_Axel.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Richard_Axel.jpg)

Axel c. 2008

During the late 1970s, Axel, along with [microbiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiologist "Microbiologist") Saul J. Silverstein and geneticist [Michael H. Wigler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_H._Wigler "Michael H. Wigler"), discovered a technique of [cotransformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_(genetics) "Transformation (genetics)") via [transfection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfection "Transfection"), a process which allows foreign DNA to be inserted into a host cell to produce certain proteins.
A family of [patents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent "Patent"), now colloquially referred to as the "Axel patents", covering this technique were filed for February 1980 and were issued in August 1983. As a fundamental process in recombinant DNA research as performed at [pharmaceutical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical "Pharmaceutical") and [biotech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology "Biotechnology") companies, this patent proved quite lucrative for Columbia University, earning it almost $100 million a year at one time, and a top spot on the list of top universities by licensing revenue. The Axel patents expired in August 2000.

In their landmark paper published in 1991, Buck and Axel [cloned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloning "Cloning") [olfactory receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfactory_receptor "Olfactory receptor"), showing that they belong to the family of [G protein coupled receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_protein-coupled_receptor "G protein-coupled receptor"). By analyzing rat [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA"), they estimated that there were approximately one thousand different [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene") for olfactory receptors in the [mammalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal "Mammal") [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome "Genome"). This research opened the door to the [genetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics") and [molecular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology") analysis of the mechanisms of [olfaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfaction "Olfaction"). In their later work, Buck and Axel have shown that each [olfactory receptor neuron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfactory_receptor_neuron "Olfactory receptor neuron") remarkably only expresses one kind of olfactory receptor protein and that the input from all neurons expressing the same receptor is collected by a single dedicated [glomerulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glomerulus_(olfaction) "Glomerulus (olfaction)") of the [olfactory bulb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfactory_bulb "Olfactory bulb").

Axel's primary research interest is on how the brain interprets the sense of smell, specifically mapping the parts of the brain that are sensitive to specific olfactory receptors. He holds the titles of [University Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Professor_(Columbia) "University Professor (Columbia)") at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), Professor of [Biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") and Molecular [Biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics") and of [Pathology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathology "Pathology") at Columbia University's College of Physicians and Surgeons, and Investigator of the [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute"). In addition to contributions to neurobiology, Axel has also made seminal discoveries in immunology, and his lab was one of the first to identify the link between [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV "HIV") infection and immunoreceptor [CD4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD4 "CD4").

In addition to making contributions as a scientist, Axel has also mentored many leading scientists in the field of neurobiology. Seven of his trainees have become members of the National Academy of Sciences, and currently six of his trainees are affiliated with the Howard Hughes Medical Institute's investigator and early scientist award programs.

## Awards and honors

In addition to the Nobel Prize, Axel has won numerous awards and honors. He was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") and a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1983. In 2005, Axel received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").

Axel was awarded the [Double Helix Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Helix_Medal "Double Helix Medal") in 2007, and was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") in 2014. His nomination reads:

> Richard Axel is a distinguished molecular biologist and neuroscientist. He developed gene transfer techniques that permit the introduction of virtually any gene into any cell permitting the production of a large number of clinically important proteins and leading to the isolation of a gene for CD4, the cellular receptor for the AIDS virus, [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV "HIV"). He then applied molecular biology to neuroscience revealing over a thousand genes involved in the recognition of odours, a discovery for which he shared the Nobel Prize in 2004. He currently explores how odour recognition is translated into internal representations in the brain.

## Personal life

Axel is married to fellow scientist and olfaction pioneer [Cornelia Bargmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelia_Bargmann "Cornelia Bargmann"). Previously, he had been married to Ann Axel, who is a social worker at Columbia University Medical Center. Owing to his tall stature, Axel played basketball during high school.

## Relationship with Jeffrey Epstein

Axel first met Jeffrey Epstein in the 1980s through the biotech world. In 2007, Axel said that Epstein, "He has the ability to make connections that other minds can’t make... He is extremely smart and probing."

In 2010, Axel attended a birthday party in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") for [Jeffrey Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein "Jeffrey Epstein"), who had been convicted of [sex offenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_offenses "Sex offenses") in 2008. The [Epstein files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein_files "Epstein files") show that Axel was a frequent guest at Epstein's Manhattan home. Axel served as a conduit to Columbia admissions and [philanthropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philanthropy "Philanthropy") for Epstein. Axel maintained continued contact with Epstein until three months prior to his 2019 arrest.

In February 2026, Axel resigned as co-director of the Mortimer B. Zuckerman Mind Brain Behavior Institute at Columbia University. In a statement he said his "past association with Jeffrey Epstein was a serious error in judgment" and apologized "for compromising the trust of my friends, students, and colleagues." He also resigned from his position at the [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute").
