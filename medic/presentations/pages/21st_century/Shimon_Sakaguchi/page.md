**Shimon Sakaguchi** (坂口 志文, *Sakaguchi Shimon*; born 19 January 1951) is a Japanese [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist "Immunologist"). He is a [distinguished professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Professor "Distinguished Professor") at [Osaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_University "Osaka University") and a professor emeritus at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University").

His work includes the discovery of [regulatory T cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_T_cells "Regulatory T cells") and describing their role in the [immune system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system "Immune system"). In 2025, he was jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Mary E. Brunkow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_E._Brunkow "Mary E. Brunkow") and [Fred Ramsdell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Ramsdell "Fred Ramsdell").

## Early life and education

Shimon Sakaguchi was born on 19 January 1951 in [Nagahama, Shiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagahama,_Shiga "Nagahama, Shiga"). He received a medical degree in 1976 from the [Faculty of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_School_of_Medicine_and_Faculty_of_Medicine,_Kyoto_University "Graduate School of Medicine and Faculty of Medicine, Kyoto University") at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University"). In 1982, he also received a PhD degree from Kyoto University.

## Career

Sakaguchi undertook postdoctoral research in the United States at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University") and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University") from 1983 to 1987 as a Lucille P. Markey Scholar. Later, he worked as an assistant professor in the Department of Immunology at the [Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Research "Scripps Research").

After returning to Japan in 1991, he worked at [Riken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riken "Riken") as an investigator of the [Japan Science and Technology Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Science_and_Technology_Agency "Japan Science and Technology Agency"). Later, he became the head of the Department of Immunopathology at the Tokyo Metropolitan Institute of Gerontology. Between 1998 and 2011, he worked as a professor and chairman of the Department of Experimental Pathology at the Institute for Frontier Medical Sciences of [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University"). From 2007 to 2011, he also served as the institute's director. His lab was moved to [Osaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Osaka "University of Osaka") in 2011.

## Research

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/25-10-06-Nobel-Prize-FOXP3-Hegasy-v02.jpg/250px-25-10-06-Nobel-Prize-FOXP3-Hegasy-v02.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:25-10-06-Nobel-Prize-FOXP3-Hegasy-v02.jpg)

Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2025: Pivotal role of FoxP3+ Treg cells in peripheral immune tolerance

In a 1995 study, Sakaguchi and his colleagues showed the existence of [regulatory T cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_T_cell "Regulatory T cell"), a previously unknown subset of [T cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T_cell "T cell") expressing [CD4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD4 "CD4") and [CD25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD25 "CD25") that modulate the [immune system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system "Immune system") and help maintain [immune tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_tolerance "Immune tolerance"). They injected [BALB/c](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BALB/c "BALB/c") athymic mice with a suspension of [CD4+ cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD4+_cell "CD4+ cell") previously depleted of CD25+ cells and found that the mice subsequently developed [autoimmune diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoimmune_disease "Autoimmune disease") (e.g. [thyroiditis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroiditis "Thyroiditis") and [gastritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastritis "Gastritis")). However, reconstitution of CD4+CD25+ cells shortly after the initial injection of CD4+CD25− cells prevented the development of autoimmunity. In 2003, Sakaguchi's group demonstrated the importance of [FOXP3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FOXP3 "FOXP3") in the development and function of regulatory T cells.
