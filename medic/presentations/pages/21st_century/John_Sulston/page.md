**Sir John Edward Sulston** [CH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_of_Honour "Companion of Honour") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") [MAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Academia_Europaea "Member of the Academia Europaea") (27 March 1942 – 6 March 2018) was a British biologist and academic who won the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for his work on the cell lineage and [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome "Genome") of the worm *[Caenorhabditis elegans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caenorhabditis_elegans "Caenorhabditis elegans")* in 2002 with his colleagues [Sydney Brenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Brenner "Sydney Brenner") and [Robert Horvitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Robert_Horvitz "H. Robert Horvitz") at the [MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRC_Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology"). He was a leader in human genome research and Chair of the [Institute for Science, Ethics and Innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Science,_Ethics_and_Innovation "Institute for Science, Ethics and Innovation") at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester "University of Manchester").
Sulston was in favour of science in the public interest, such as free public access of scientific information and against the patenting of genes and the privatisation of genetic technologies.

## Early life and education

Sulston was born in [Fulmer, Buckinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulmer,_Buckinghamshire "Fulmer, Buckinghamshire"), England to Arthur Edward Aubrey Sulston and Josephine Muriel Frearson, née Blocksidge. His father was an Anglican priest and administrator of the [Society for the Propagation of the Gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_the_Propagation_of_the_Gospel "Society for the Propagation of the Gospel"). His mother quit her job as an English teacher at [Watford Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watford_Grammar_School_for_Boys "Watford Grammar School for Boys"), to care for him and his sister Madeleine. and home-tutored them until he was five. At age five he entered the local preparatory school, York House School, where he soon developed an aversion to games. He developed an early interest in science, having fun with dissecting animals and sectioning plants to observe their structure and function. Sulston won a scholarship to [Merchant Taylors' School, Northwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_Taylors'_School,_Northwood "Merchant Taylors' School, Northwood") and then to [Pembroke College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembroke_College,_Cambridge "Pembroke College, Cambridge") graduating in 1963 with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree in [Natural Sciences (Chemistry)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Sciences_(Cambridge) "Natural Sciences (Cambridge)"). He joined the [Department of Chemistry, University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Chemistry,_University_of_Cambridge "Department of Chemistry, University of Cambridge"), after being interviewed by [Alexander Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_R._Todd,_Baron_Todd "Alexander R. Todd, Baron Todd") and was awarded his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in 1966 for research in [nucleotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleotide "Nucleotide") chemistry.

## Career

Between 1966 and 1969 he worked as a [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher "Postdoctoral researcher") at the [Salk Institute for Biological Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salk_Institute_for_Biological_Studies "Salk Institute for Biological Studies") in [La Jolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla "La Jolla"), California. His academic advisor Colin Reese had arranged for him to work with [Leslie Orgel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Orgel "Leslie Orgel"), who would turn his scientific career onto a different pathway. Orgel introduced him to [Francis Crick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Crick "Francis Crick") and [Sydney Brenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Brenner "Sydney Brenner"), who worked in Cambridge. He became inclined to biological research.

Although Orgel wanted Sulston to remain with him, Sydney Brenner persuaded Sulston to return to Cambridge to work on the neurobiology of *[Caenorhabditis elegans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caenorhabditis_elegans "Caenorhabditis elegans")* at the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(United_Kingdom) "Medical Research Council (United Kingdom)") (MRC) [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") (LMB). Sulston soon produced the complete map of the worm's neurons. He continued work on its DNA and subsequently the whole genome sequencing. In 1998, the whole genome sequence was published in collaboration with the Genome Institute at [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis"),
so that *C. elegans* became the first animal to have its complete genome sequenced.

Sulston played a central role in both the *C. elegans* and [human genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project "Human Genome Project") sequencing projects. He had argued successfully for the sequencing of *C. elegans* to show that large-scale genome sequencing projects were feasible. As sequencing of the worm genome proceeded, the [Human Genome Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project "Human Genome Project") began. At this point he was made director of the newly established [Sanger Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellcome_Trust_Sanger_Institute "Wellcome Trust Sanger Institute") (named after [Fred Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Sanger "Fred Sanger")), located in [Cambridgeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire "Cambridgeshire"), England.

In 2000, after the 'working draft' of the human genome sequence was completed, Sulston retired from directing the Sanger Centre. With Georgina Ferry, he narrated his research career leading to the human genome sequence in *The Common Thread: A Story of Science, Politics, Ethics, and the Human Genome* (2002).

### Awards and honours

Sulston was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1986 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1986"). His certificate of election reads:

> John Sulston is distinguished for his work on the molecular and developmental genetics of *Caenorhabditis elegans*. His initial research was in the field of chemical synthesis of oligonucleotides. Sulston began his work on C. elegans in 1974 characterising its DNA. Since then he has carried out a wide range of genetical and developmental studies on the nematode but his major research has been on the developmental lineage and [mutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation "Mutation") that affect it. In a series of studies, culminating in a paper published in 1983, Sulston has analysed and described the total cell lineage of the nematode making it the first organism for which the origin of every cell is exactly known. This work is the basis for the study of mutations affecting lineages and is the foundation on which detailed studies of development in this organism will be based. Sulston has now turned his attention to an analysis of the genome of C. elegans and was constructing a total physical map using a novel method of analysing cloned DNA fragments.

He was elected an [EMBO Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMBO_Member "EMBO Member") in 1989 and awarded the [George W. Beadle Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Beadle_Award "George W. Beadle Award") in 2000. In 2001 Sulston gave the [Royal Institution Christmas Lectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institution_Christmas_Lectures "Royal Institution Christmas Lectures") on *The Secrets of Life*. In 2002, he won the [Dan David Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_David_Prize "Dan David Prize") and the [Robert Burns Humanitarian Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Burns_Humanitarian_Award "Robert Burns Humanitarian Award"). Later, he shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Sydney Brenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Brenner "Sydney Brenner") and [Robert Horvitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Robert_Horvitz "H. Robert Horvitz"), both of whom he had collaborated with at the MRC [Laboratory of Molecular Biology (LMB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology"), for their discoveries concerning 'genetic regulation of organ development and programmed cell death'.

One of Sulston's most important contributions during his research years at the LMB was to elucidate the precise order in which cells in *C. elegans* divide. In fact, he and his team succeeded in tracing the nematode's entire embryonic cell lineage.

In 2004, Sulston received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement"). In 2006, he was awarded the George Dawson Prize in Genetics by [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin "Trinity College Dublin"). In 2013, Sulston was awarded the [Royal Society of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_New_Zealand "Royal Society of New Zealand")'s [Rutherford Memorial Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_Memorial_Lecture_(Royal_Society) "Rutherford Memorial Lecture (Royal Society)"), which he gave on the subject of population pressure.

He was appointed a [Member of the Order of the Companions of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_the_Companions_of_Honour "Member of the Order of the Companions of Honour") (CH) in the [2017 Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Birthday_Honours "2017 Birthday Honours") for services to science and society.

On 23 October 2017 he was awarded the Cambridge Chemistry Alumni Medal.

Sulston was a leading campaigner against the patenting of human genetic information.

## Personal life

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/EBI_and_Sanger_Center%2C_Genome_campus%2C_Cambridgeshire.jpg/960px-EBI_and_Sanger_Center%2C_Genome_campus%2C_Cambridgeshire.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:EBI_and_Sanger_Center,_Genome_campus,_Cambridgeshire.jpg)

The Sulston Laboratories of the [Wellcome Trust Sanger Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellcome_Trust_Sanger_Institute "Wellcome Trust Sanger Institute") are named in Sulston's honour.

John Sulston met Daphne Bate, a research assistant in Cambridge. They got married in 1966 just before they left for US for postdoctoral research. Together they had two children. Their first child, Ingrid, was born in [La Jolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla "La Jolla") in 1967, and their second, Adrian, later in England. The couple lived in [Stapleford, Cambridgeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stapleford,_Cambridgeshire "Stapleford, Cambridgeshire") where they were active members of the local community: John regularly volunteered in the local library and in working parties at [Magog Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gog_Magog_Hills#Magog_Down "Gog Magog Hills"); he was a Trustee of Cambridge Past, Present and Future.

Although brought up in a Christian family, Sulston lost his faith during his student life at Cambridge, and remained an atheist. He was a distinguished supporter of [Humanists UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanists_UK "Humanists UK"). In 2003 he was one of 22 Nobel Laureates who signed the [Humanist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism_and_Its_Aspirations "Humanism and Its Aspirations").

Sulston was in favour of free public access of scientific information. He wanted genome information freely available, and he described as "totally immoral and disgusting" the idea of profiteering from such research. He also wanted to change patent law, and argued that restrictions on drugs such as the anti-viral drug [Tamiflu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamiflu "Tamiflu") by Roche are a hindrance to patients whose lives are dependent on them.

In December 2010, Sulston backed [Julian Assange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Assange "Julian Assange") by acting as a bail surety for him, according to Assange's attorney [Mark Stephens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Stephens_(solicitor) "Mark Stephens (solicitor)"). Sulston forfeited £15,000 of the £20,000 pledged in June 2012, as Assange had entered the embassy of Ecuador to escape the jurisdiction of the English courts.

Sulston died on 6 March 2018 of stomach cancer, aged 75 years.
