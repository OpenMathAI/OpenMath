**John Michael O'Keefe** (born November 18, 1939) is an American-British [neuroscientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscientist "Neuroscientist"), [psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychologist "Psychologist") and a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor "Professor") at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London").

O'Keefe discovered [place cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_cell "Place cell") in the [hippocampus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocampus "Hippocampus"), and that they show a specific kind of [temporal coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporal_coding "Temporal coding") in the form of [theta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theta_wave "Theta wave") [phase precession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_precession "Phase precession"). He shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 2014, together with [May-Britt Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May-Britt_Moser "May-Britt Moser") and [Edvard Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Moser "Edvard Moser"); he has received several other awards.

## Early life and education

Born in New York City to Irish immigrant parents, O'Keefe attended [Regis High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regis_High_School_(New_York_City) "Regis High School (New York City)") (Manhattan) and received a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree from the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York "City College of New York") in 1963. Neither of his parents had completed elementary school in Ireland, but his father attained a high school degree in New York. He went on to study at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University "McGill University") in [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal"), [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec "Quebec"), Canada, where he obtained an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") degree in 1964, and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree in Psychology in 1967, supervised by [Ronald Melzack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Melzack "Ronald Melzack").

## Career and research

O'Keefe went to [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London") in 1967 as a US [NIMH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Mental_Health "National Institute of Mental Health") [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") fellow working with the late [Patrick Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_David_Wall "Patrick David Wall"). He has been there ever since and was promoted to Professor in 1987. At the behest of his collaborators [Edvard Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Moser "Edvard Moser") and [May-Britt Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May-Britt_Moser "May-Britt Moser") he was appointed to a part-time professorial chair at the [Norwegian University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_University_of_Science_and_Technology "Norwegian University of Science and Technology") in 2014.

### Discovery of place cells

O’Keefe and his student Jonathan Dostrovsky discovered place cells by systematically analyzing the environmental factors influencing the firing properties of individual hippocampal neurons. His many publications on place cells have been highly cited. In addition, he published an influential book with [Lynn Nadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_Nadel "Lynn Nadel"), proposing the functional role of the [hippocampus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocampus "Hippocampus") as a [cognitive map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_map "Cognitive map") for [spatial memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_memory "Spatial memory") function. In extensions of his work, place cells have been analyzed experimentally or simulated in models in hundreds of papers.

### Discovery of theta phase precession

In further research on place cells, O’Keefe found evidence for a distinctive variation of [temporal coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporal_coding "Temporal coding") of information by the timing of [action potentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_potential "Action potential") in place cells, relative to an oscillatory [EEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroencephalography "Electroencephalography") cycle known as the [theta rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theta_rhythm "Theta rhythm"), as opposed to spike timing within a single cell. In a 1993 paper, he and Michael Recce demonstrated that place cells spike at different phases relative to theta rhythm oscillations in the local field potential of the hippocampus. As a rat enters the firing field of a place cell, the spiking starts at late phases of theta rhythm, and as the rat moves through the firing field, the spikes shift to earlier phases of the theta cycle. This effect has been replicated in numerous subsequent papers, providing evidence for the coding of sensory input by the timing of spikes. Numerous models have addressed the potential physiological mechanisms of theta phase precession.

### Prediction and discovery of boundary vector cells

In a paper in 1996, O'Keefe and [Neil Burgess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Burgess_(neuroscientist) "Neil Burgess (neuroscientist)") presented data showing shifts in the position and size of place cell firing fields when the barriers defining the environment were shifted. In this and subsequent papers, they presented a model of this phenomenon predicting the existence of boundary vector cells that would respond at a specific distance from barriers in the environment. Several years later, this explicit theoretical prediction was supported by extensive experimental data demonstrating [boundary cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_cell "Boundary cell") with the predicted properties in the [subiculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subiculum "Subiculum") and the medial [entorhinal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entorhinal_cortex "Entorhinal cortex") (where they are sometimes referred to as border cells). An influential 2006 review by O'Keefe and multiple collaborators presented an extended formulation of the boundary vector cell model. O'Keefe has also coauthored research on the oscillatory interference model of grid cell firing and on the regulation of grid cell firing by environmental geometry.

### Awards and honours

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Dr._John_O%27_Keefe%2C_Nobel_laureate_in_Medicine.jpg/250px-Dr._John_O%27_Keefe%2C_Nobel_laureate_in_Medicine.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._John_O'_Keefe,_Nobel_laureate_in_Medicine.jpg)

O'Keefe giving Nobel lecture in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo "Oslo"), December 2014

O'Keefe was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1992 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1992") and a [Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Academy_of_Medical_Sciences "Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences") (FMedSci) in 1998. In addition, he received the [Feldberg Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldberg_Foundation "Feldberg Foundation") Prize in 2001 and the [Grawemeyer Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grawemeyer_Award "Grawemeyer Award") in psychology in 2006 (with Lynn Nadel). In 2007, he received the [British Neuroscience Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Neuroscience_Association "British Neuroscience Association") Award for Outstanding Contribution to British Neuroscience and in 2008 he received the [Federation of European Neuroscience Societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_European_Neuroscience_Societies "Federation of European Neuroscience Societies") [*European Journal of Neuroscience* Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Journal_of_Neuroscience "European Journal of Neuroscience"). Later in 2008, O'Keefe was awarded the [Gruber Prize in Neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruber_Prize_in_Neuroscience "Gruber Prize in Neuroscience"). He was appointed as the inaugural director of the Sainsbury Wellcome Centre for Neural Circuits and Behaviour. In 2013 he received the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") (with Edvard Moser and May-Britt Moser). In 2014, he was a co-recipient of the [Kavli Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavli_Prize "Kavli Prize") awarded by the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters "Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters") with [Brenda Milner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda_Milner "Brenda Milner") and [Marcus Raichle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Raichle "Marcus Raichle").
In 2016 he was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"). In 2019, he was admitted to the [Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy "Royal Irish Academy") as an honorary member.

O'Keefe was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") 2014, with [May-Britt Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May-Britt_Moser "May-Britt Moser") and [Edvard Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Moser "Edvard Moser").

O'Keefe received an [honorary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree "Honorary degree") [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science "Doctor of Science") degree from [University College Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Cork "University College Cork") on December 15, 2014. In May 2015, he received one from [The City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_City_College_of_New_York "The City College of New York"), and in June of the same year, he was awarded one from [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University "McGill University"), both his alma maters.

In 2014 he received the [Kavli Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavli_Prize "Kavli Prize") in Neuroscience "for the discovery of specialized brain networks for memory and cognition", together with [Brenda Milner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda_Milner "Brenda Milner") and [Marcus Raichle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Raichle "Marcus Raichle").

On March 10, 2015, O'Keefe was the guest on [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4 "BBC Radio 4")'s *[The Life Scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_Scientific "The Life Scientific")*.
