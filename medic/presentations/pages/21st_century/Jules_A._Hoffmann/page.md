**Jules Alphonse Nicolas Hoffmann** (French pronunciation: [[ʒyl ɔfman]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); born 2 August 1941) is a Luxembourgish-French biologist. During his youth, growing up in [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg "Luxembourg"), he developed a strong interest in insects under the influence of his father, Jos Hoffmann. This eventually resulted in the younger Hoffmann's dedication to the field of biology using insects as model organisms. He currently holds a faculty position at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg "University of Strasbourg"). He is a research director and member of the board of administrators of the National Center of Scientific Research ([CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS "CNRS")) in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg "Strasbourg"), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France"). He was elected to the positions of Vice-President (2005–2006) and President (2007–2008) of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences"). Hoffmann and [Bruce Beutler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Beutler "Bruce Beutler") were jointly awarded a half share of the 2011 [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in [Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for "their discoveries concerning the activation of innate immunity".

Hoffmann and [Bruno Lemaitre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Lemaitre "Bruno Lemaitre") discovered the function of the fruit fly *[Toll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_(gene) "Toll (gene)")* gene in [innate immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innate_immunity "Innate immunity"). Its mammalian homologs, the [Toll-like receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll-like_receptor "Toll-like receptor"), were discovered by Beutler. Toll-like receptors identify constituents of other organisms like fungi and bacteria, and trigger an immune response, explaining, for example, how [septic shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septic_shock "Septic shock") can be triggered by bacterial remains.

In 2012, Hoffman was appointed as an Honorary Professor in the School of Biochemistry and Immunology at [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin "Trinity College Dublin").

## Education

Jules Hoffmann went to the [Lycée de Garçons de Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_de_Garçons_de_Luxembourg "Lycée de Garçons de Luxembourg") before leaving to France. Hoffmann received undergraduate degrees in biology and chemistry at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg "University of Strasbourg"), France. In 1969, he completed his Ph.D. in biology also at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg "University of Strasbourg") under Pierre Joly in Laboratory of General Biology at the Institute of Zoology. His post-doctoral training was at the Institut für Physiologische Chemie at [Philipps-Universität](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipps-Universität_Marburg "Philipps-Universität Marburg") in [Marburg an der Lahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg_an_der_Lahn "Marburg an der Lahn"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany"), in 1973–1974.

## Studies and Research Careers

During his Ph.D. program under Pierre Joly, Hoffmann started his research in studying antimicrobial defenses in grasshoppers, inspired by the previous works done in the laboratory of Pierre Joly showing that no opportunistic infections were apparent in insects after the transplantation of certain organs from one to another. Hoffmann confirmed discovery of phagocytosis done by [Eli Metchnikoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Metchnikoff "Eli Metchnikoff"), through injection of *[Bacillus thuringiensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacillus_thuringiensis "Bacillus thuringiensis")* and observation of increase of phagocytes. In addition, he showed strong correlation between hematopoiesis and antimicrobial defenses by assessing the susceptibility of an insect to the microbial infection after X-ray treatment. Hoffmann shifts from using grasshopper model to using dipteran species in the 80s. By using *[Phormia terranovae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phormia_terranovae?action=edit&redlink=1 "Phormia terranovae")*, Hoffmann and his colleagues were able to identify 82-residues long antimicrobial polypeptide named [Diptericin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diptericin "Diptericin") which was glycine-rich, along with other polypeptides in *[Drosophila melanogaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drosophila_melanogaster "Drosophila melanogaster")* such as [Defensin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensin "Defensin"), [Cecropin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecropin "Cecropin"), and [Attacin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attacin "Attacin"). Further molecular genetic analysis revealed that the promoters for the genes encoding these antimicrobial peptides contained DNA sequences similar to the binding elements for NF-κB in mammalian DNA. *Dorsal* gene, critical in dorso-ventral patterning in the early embryo of *Drosophila melanogaster* was also identified to be in this NF-κB family. It was initially speculated by Hoffmann and colleagues that activity of Dorsal was directly linked to the expression of the *Diptericin* gene. However, it turned out that Diptericin was normally induced even in the loss-of-function *Dorsal* mutants. Further conducted research showed that Diptericin expression was dependent on the expression of imd gene. Identification of another antifungal peptide named Drosomycin and RNA blots demonstrated that two distinct pathways(Toll, Imd) exist, involving Drosomycin and Diptericin respectively. Similarities of structure and function between several members in the Drosophila embryo and members in mammals being noted, study "The Dorsoventral Regulatory Gene Cassette *spätzle/Toll/cactus* Controls the Potent Antifungal Response in Drosophila Adults" by [Lemaitre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Lemaitre "Bruno Lemaitre") and Hoffmann in 1996 illuminated the possible existing innate immunity in Drosophila in response to fungal challenge. Later works identified that Toll transmembrane receptors are present in a wide variety of phyla and are conserved through evolution along with conservation of NF-κB activating cascades.

Hoffmann was a research assistant at CNRS from 1964 to 1968, and became a research associate in 1969. Since 1974 he has been a Research Director of CNRS. Between 1978 and 2005 he was Director of the CNRS research unit "Immune Response and Development in Insects", and from 1994 to 2005 he was director of the [Institute of Molecular and Cellular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_de_biologie_moléculaire_et_cellulaire "Institut de biologie moléculaire et cellulaire") of CNRS in Strasbourg.

Hoffmann is a member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina"), the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences"), the [European Molecular Biology Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Molecular_Biology_Organization "European Molecular Biology Organization") (EMBO), the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences"), the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), the Fondation Écologie d'Avenir and the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences "Russian Academy of Sciences").

Hoffmann became a Commander of the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour "Legion of Honour") in 2012.

In 2015, Hoffmann signed the *[Mainau Declaration 2015 on Climate Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainau_Declaration "Mainau Declaration")* on the final day of the 65th [Lindau Nobel Laureate Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindau_Nobel_Laureate_Meetings "Lindau Nobel Laureate Meetings"). The declaration was signed by a total of 76 Nobel Laureates and handed to then-President of the French Republic, [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Hollande "François Hollande"), as part of the successful [COP21 climate summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Climate_Change_Conference "Paris Climate Change Conference") in Paris.

## Controversy

[Bruno Lemaitre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Lemaitre "Bruno Lemaitre"), a research associate in the Hoffmann laboratory at the time when the major work on Drosophila innate immunity was conducted (for which Hoffmann was awarded the Nobel), claims he was inadequately recognized by Hoffmann as the instigator of and main contributor to the Nobel-winning work. [Lemaitre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Lemaitre "Bruno Lemaitre") now supervises his own laboratory at the [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne "École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne") in Switzerland.
