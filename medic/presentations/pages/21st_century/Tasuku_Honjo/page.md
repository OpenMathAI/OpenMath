**Tasuku Honjo** (本庶 佑, *Honjo Tasuku*; born January 27, 1942) is a Japanese [physician-scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician-scientist "Physician-scientist") and [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist "Immunologist"). He won the 2018 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") and is best known for his identification of [programmed cell death protein 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmed_cell_death_protein_1 "Programmed cell death protein 1") (PD-1). He is also known for his molecular identification of [cytokines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytokines "Cytokines") [IL-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interleukin_4 "Interleukin 4") and [IL-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interleukin_5 "Interleukin 5"), as well as the discovery of activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID) that is essential for [class switch recombination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_switch_recombination "Class switch recombination") and [somatic hypermutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_hypermutation "Somatic hypermutation").

He was elected as an international member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") of the United States in 2001, a member of the [German National Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_National_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina "German National Academy of Sciences Leopoldina") in 2003, and as a member of the [Japan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy "Japan Academy") in 2005. In 2018, he was awarded a Nobel Prize with [James P. Allison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_P._Allison "James P. Allison"). He and Allison together had won the 2014 [Tang Prize in Biopharmaceutical Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_Prize "Tang Prize") for the same achievement.

## Life and career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Tasuku_Honjo_EM1B5529_%2846157227432%29.jpg/250px-Tasuku_Honjo_EM1B5529_%2846157227432%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tasuku_Honjo_EM1B5529_(46157227432).jpg)

At Nobel press conference in Stockholm, December 2018

Honjo was born in Kyoto in 1942. He completed his [M.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_degree "Medical degree") degree in 1966 from the Faculty of Medicine, [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University "Kyoto University"), where in 1975 he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree in medical chemistry under the supervision of [Yasutomi Nishizuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasutomi_Nishizuka "Yasutomi Nishizuka") and [Osamu Hayaishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osamu_Hayaishi "Osamu Hayaishi").

Honjo was a visiting fellow at the Department of Embryology at [Carnegie Institution of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Institution_for_Science "Carnegie Institution for Science"), from 1971 to 1973. He then moved to the U.S. [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health") (NIH) in Bethesda, Maryland, where he studied the genetic basis for the immune response at the [National Institute of Child Health and Human Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunice_Kennedy_Shriver_National_Institute_of_Child_Health_and_Human_Development "Eunice Kennedy Shriver National Institute of Child Health and Human Development") as a fellow between 1973 and 1977, followed by many years as an NIH Fogarty Scholar in Residence starting in 1992. During part of this time, Honjo also was an assistant professor at the [Faculty of Medicine, University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Medical_University "Tokyo Medical University"), between 1974 and 1979; a professor in the Department of Genetics, [Osaka University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_University "Osaka University"), between 1979 and 1984; and professor in the Department of Medical Chemistry, Kyoto University Faculty of Medicine, from 1984 to 2005. Since 2005 Honjo has been a professor in Department of Immunology and Genomic Medicine, Kyoto University Faculty of Medicine. He was the President of [Shizuoka Prefecture Public University Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Shizuoka "University of Shizuoka") from 2012 to 2017.

He is a member of the Japanese Society for Immunology and served as its president between 1999 and 2000. Honjo is also an honorary member of [American Association of Immunologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_Immunologists "American Association of Immunologists"). In 2017 he became Deputy Director-General and Distinguished Professor of Kyoto University Institute for Advanced Study (KUIAS).

### Public advocacy

Honjo has publicly criticized the Japanese government's suspension of proactive [HPV vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPV_vaccine "HPV vaccine") recommendations, describing the decision as lacking scientific justification and posing a serious public health risk. He expressed concern that misinformation and media sensationalism had undermined evidence-based policy. In support of journalist [Riko Muranaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riko_Muranaka "Riko Muranaka"), Honjo submitted a written opinion during her defamation lawsuit, emphasizing the importance of scientific reproducibility and defending her reporting on HPV vaccine safety.

### COVID-19 pandemic

During the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), a disputed claim that Honjo believed that the novel coronavirus had been "manufactured" by a laboratory in the Chinese city of Wuhan was widely disseminated on the internet in many languages. The BBC Reality Check team reported that, "In a statement published on the website of Kyoto University, he said he was 'greatly saddened' that his name had been used to spread 'false accusations and misinformation'.

## Contributions

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/11_Hegasy_CTLA4_PD1_Immunotherapy.png/250px-11_Hegasy_CTLA4_PD1_Immunotherapy.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:11_Hegasy_CTLA4_PD1_Immunotherapy.png)

Cancer therapy by inhibition of negative immune regulation (CTLA4, PD1)

Honjo has established the basic conceptual framework of [class switch recombination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_switch_recombination "Class switch recombination"). He presented a model explaining antibody gene rearrangement in class switch and, between 1980 and 1982, verified its validity by elucidating its [DNA structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_structure "DNA structure"). He succeeded in [cDNA clonings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CDNA_clone "CDNA clone") of [IL-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interleukin_4 "Interleukin 4") and [IL-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interleukin_5 "Interleukin 5") cytokines involved in class switching and [IL-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interleukin_2 "Interleukin 2") receptor alpha chain in 1986, and went on further to discover [AID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activation-induced_cytidine_deaminase "Activation-induced cytidine deaminase") in 2000, demonstrating its importance in class switch recombination and [somatic hypermutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_hypermutation "Somatic hypermutation").

In 1992, Honjo first identified [PD-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmed_cell_death_protein_1 "Programmed cell death protein 1") as an inducible gene on activated T-lymphocytes, and this discovery significantly contributed to the establishment of [cancer immunotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_immunotherapy "Cancer immunotherapy") principle by PD-1 blockade.

Honjo has received several awards and honors in his life. In 2016, he won the [Kyoto Prize in Basic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Prize "Kyoto Prize") for "Discovery of the Mechanism Responsible for the Functional Diversification of Antibodies, Immunoregulatory Molecules and Clinical Applications of PD-1". In 2018, he shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with American immunologist [James P. Allison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_P._Allison "James P. Allison"). They previously also shared the [Tang Prize in Biopharmaceutical Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_Prize "Tang Prize") in 2014.
