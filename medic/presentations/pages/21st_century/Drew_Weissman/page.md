**Drew Weissman** (born September 7, 1959) is a Nobel Prize winning American physician and [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist "Immunologist") known for his contributions to [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA") biology.
Weissman is the inaugural Roberts Family Professor in Vaccine Research, director of the Penn Institute for RNA Innovation, and professor of medicine at the [Perelman School of Medicine at the University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perelman_School_of_Medicine_at_the_University_of_Pennsylvania "Perelman School of Medicine at the University of Pennsylvania") (Penn).

Weissman's work underlies the development of [mRNA vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRNA_vaccine "MRNA vaccine"), the best known of which are [those for COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine#RNA_vaccines "COVID-19 vaccine") produced by [BioNTech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioNTech "BioNTech")/[Pfizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfizer "Pfizer") and [Moderna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderna "Moderna"). With [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist") [Katalin Karikó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katalin_Karikó "Katalin Karikó"), Weissman received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2023 "for their discoveries concerning [nucleoside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleoside "Nucleoside") base modifications that enabled the development of effective mRNA vaccines against COVID-19". Weissman has been a recipient and co-recipient of numerous awards, also including the prestigious [Lasker–DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker–DeBakey_Clinical_Medical_Research_Award "Lasker–DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award"). In 2022, he was elected to the [National Academy of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Medicine "National Academy of Medicine") and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences").

## Early life and education

Weissman was born in [Lexington, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Massachusetts "Lexington, Massachusetts"), on September 7, 1959, to Hal and Adele Weissman.
Hal is [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") and Adele is [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians "Italians"). While his mother did not convert to [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), he grew up celebrating all the Jewish holidays. He grew up in Lexington and attended [Lexington High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington_High_School_(Massachusetts) "Lexington High School (Massachusetts)"), graduating in 1977.

Weissman received his [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") and [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") degrees from [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University "Brandeis University") in 1981, where he majored in [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") and [enzymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme "Enzyme") and he worked in the lab of Gerald Fasman. He performed his graduate work in [immunology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunology "Immunology") and [microbiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiology "Microbiology") to receive his [M.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine "Doctor of Medicine") and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in 1987 at [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University "Boston University"). Afterward, Weissman did a residency at [Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Israel_Deaconess_Medical_Center "Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center"), followed by a [fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow "Fellow") at the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health") (NIH), under the supervision of [Anthony Fauci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Fauci "Anthony Fauci"), then director of the [National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Allergy_and_Infectious_Diseases "National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases").

## Career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/Drew_Weissman_and_Katalin_Karik%C3%B3_Life_Science_Medalists.jpg/250px-Drew_Weissman_and_Katalin_Karik%C3%B3_Life_Science_Medalists.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Drew_Weissman_and_Katalin_Karikó_Life_Science_Medalists.jpg)

Weissman with Katalin Karikó in 2022

In 1997, Weissman moved to the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") to start his laboratory in order to study RNA and innate immune system biology. He is now the Roberts Family Professor in Vaccine Research at the university.

At the university, Weissman, an immunologist studying vaccines, met his future colleague and collaborator [Katalin Karikó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katalin_Karikó "Katalin Karikó") at a [photocopier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photocopier "Photocopier"), where they sympathized about the lack of funding for RNA research. At the time, Karikó had been trying RNA therapy on [cerebral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain "Brain") diseases and [strokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke "Stroke"). Immunologist Weissman began collaborating with biochemist Karikó, who switched her focus to the application of RNA technology to vaccines. Weissman's support was critical in helping Karikó to continue and extend her research. Slowly they began to move the technology forward, solving problems one at a time. On the difficulty of gaining funding and recognition for their work, Weissman has commented "We had to fight the entire way."

One of the major scientific obstacles they faced was that the RNA caused unwanted immune and [inflammatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflammation "Inflammation") reactions as adverse [side effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side_effects "Side effects"). Beginning in 2005, they published several landmark studies that used [synthetic nucleosides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleic_acid_analogue "Nucleic acid analogue") to modify the RNA to prevent its degradation by the body. This breakthrough laid the groundwork for the use of RNA therapeutics, though the study received little attention at the time.

Weissman and Karikó overcame another major obstacle by developing a delivery technique to package the mRNA in [lipid nanoparticles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid_nanoparticles "Lipid nanoparticles"), a novel pharmaceutical drug delivery system for mRNA that protects the fragile molecule until it can reach the desired area of the body. They demonstrated the effectiveness of the delivery system in animals.

In 2006, Weissman and Karikó co-founded RNARx. Their objective was to develop novel RNA therapies. In 2020 their modified RNA technology became the key foundational component of the Pfizer/BioNTech and Moderna COVID-19 vaccines, which were deployed worldwide against the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic").

Weissman has been collaborating with scientists at Thailand's [Chulalongkorn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chulalongkorn_University "Chulalongkorn University"), most recently to develop and provide [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19") vaccines for the country and neighboring [low and middle income countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_and_middle_income_countries "Low and middle income countries") that may not have immediate access to the vaccine.

Weissman's laboratory continues to actively research the use of mRNA for next-generation vaccines, gene editing, and mRNA therapeutics. Projects include development of a [pan coronavirus vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_coronavirus_vaccine "Pan coronavirus vaccine"), gene editing technology to enable genes that produce missing antibodies, and treatments for acute inflammatory conditions. Weissman hopes that mRNA technology can be used to develop vaccines against influenza, herpes, and HIV.

## Recognition

For their mRNA-related work, Weissman and [Karikó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katalin_Karikó "Katalin Karikó") were awarded the 2023 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine"), the 2020 [Rosenstiel Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenstiel_Award "Rosenstiel Award"), the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize"), the [Albany Medical Center Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany_Medical_Center_Prize "Albany Medical Center Prize"), the [Lasker-DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker-DeBakey_Clinical_Medical_Research_Award "Lasker-DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award"), and the [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Awards "BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Awards") (also with [Robert S. Langer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_S._Langer "Robert S. Langer")).

Weissman obtained an honorary degree by the [Drexel University College of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drexel_University_College_of_Medicine "Drexel University College of Medicine"). In 2021, he was awarded the [Princess of Asturias Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_of_Asturias_Awards "Princess of Asturias Awards") in the category for Scientific Research. For 2022 he was awarded the [Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Life_Sciences "Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences"), the [Jessie Stevenson Kovalenko Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Stevenson_Kovalenko_Medal "Jessie Stevenson Kovalenko Medal") of the [NAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") jointly with Katalin Karikó and also the [Japan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Prize "Japan Prize") Also in 2022 he received the [Robert Koch Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Koch_Prize "Robert Koch Prize") and the [Tang Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_Prize "Tang Prize") in Biopharmaceutical Science, the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement"), and was elected to the National Academy of Medicine and American Academy of Arts and Sciences. Furthermore in 2022, Weissman and [Karikó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katalin_Karikó "Katalin Karikó") were awarded the [Novo Nordisk Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Novo_Nordisk_Prize "The Novo Nordisk Prize"). In 2023 he received the [Harvey Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Prize "Harvey Prize") of the Technion in Israel (awarded for the year 2021). In the same year, Weissman was awarded an honorary [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science "Doctor of Science") (D.Sc.) degree from [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University "Boston University").

According to a report in *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")*, Weissman gets fan mail from people all over the world, thanking him for his work that made the COVID-19 vaccine possible — one said "You've made hugs and closeness possible again" — and asking him for a picture or his autograph.

His name was included with Kariko in Time 2024 list of influential people in health.

## Patents

Weissman is the co-inventor on several patents, including US8278036B2 and US8748089B2, both with his colleague Katalin Karikó, which detail the modifications required to make RNA suitable for vaccines and other therapies. Later, these patents were licensed to Gary Dahl, founder and CEO of Cellscript, who subsequently licensed the technology to Moderna and BioNTech to ultimately use in their COVID-19 vaccines.
