**George Wald** (November 18, 1906 – April 12, 1997) was an American scientist and activist who studied [pigments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigment "Pigment") in the [retina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retina "Retina"). He won a share of the 1967 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Haldan Keffer Hartline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haldan_Keffer_Hartline "Haldan Keffer Hartline") and [Ragnar Granit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragnar_Granit "Ragnar Granit").

In 1970, Wald predicted that “civilization will end within 15 or 30 years unless immediate action is taken against problems facing mankind.”

## Biography

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Ruth_Hubbard_and_George_Wald_1967.jpg/250px-Ruth_Hubbard_and_George_Wald_1967.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ruth_Hubbard_and_George_Wald_1967.jpg)

George Wald with wife [Ruth Hubbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Hubbard "Ruth Hubbard") in 1967

George Wald was born in New York City, the son of Ernestine (Rosenmann) and Isaac Wald, Jewish immigrant parents. He was a member of the first graduating class of the [Brooklyn Technical High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Technical_High_School "Brooklyn Technical High School") in New York in 1923. He received his Bachelor of Science degree from [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University") in 1927 and his PhD in [zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology "Zoology") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in 1932. After graduating, he received a travel grant from the US National Research Council. Wald used this grant to work in Germany with [Otto Heinrich Warburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Heinrich_Warburg "Otto Heinrich Warburg") where he identified [vitamin A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_A "Vitamin A") in the retina. Wald then went on to work in Zürich, Switzerland, with the discoverer of vitamin A, [Paul Karrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Karrer "Paul Karrer"). Wald then worked briefly with [Otto Fritz Meyerhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Fritz_Meyerhof "Otto Fritz Meyerhof") in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg"), Germany, but left Europe for the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") in 1933 when [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") came to power and life in Europe became more dangerous for Jews. In 1934, Wald went to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") where he became an instructor, then a professor.

Wald was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1948. He was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") in 1950, the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1958, and in 1967 was awarded the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") for Physiology or Medicine for his discoveries in vision. In 1966 he was awarded the Frederic Ives Medal by the [OSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_Society_of_America "Optical Society of America") and in 1967 the [Paul Karrer Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Karrer_Gold_Medal "Paul Karrer Gold Medal") of the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich"). In 1992, he was elected an Honorary Member of OSA.

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Who%27s_Out_There_%281973%29.ogv/250px-seek%3D216-Who%27s_Out_There_%281973%29.ogv.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser)](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2b/Who%27s_Out_There_%281973%29.ogv?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=original)

Wald discussing the likelihood of [life on other planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterrestrial_life "Extraterrestrial life") in *[Who's Out There?](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Who's%20Out%20There%20(1973).ogv "c:File:Who's Out There (1973).ogv")* (1973)

Wald spoke out on many political and social issues and his fame as a Nobel laureate brought national and international attention to his views. He was a [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifist "Pacifist") and vocal opponent of the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War "Vietnam War") and the [nuclear arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_arms_race "Nuclear arms race"). Speaking at MIT in 1969 Wald said, "Our government has become preoccupied with death, with the business of killing and being killed." In 1980, he served as part of [Ramsey Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_Clark "Ramsey Clark")'s delegation to [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") during the [Iran hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_hostage_crisis "Iran hostage crisis").

With a small number of other Nobel laureates, he was invited in 1986 to fly to Moscow to advise [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev "Mikhail Gorbachev") on a number of [environmental questions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism "Environmentalism"). While there, he questioned Gorbachev about the arrest, detention and exile of [Yelena Bonner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelena_Bonner "Yelena Bonner") and her husband, fellow Nobel laureate [Andrei Sakharov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Sakharov "Andrei Sakharov") (Peace prize, 1975). Wald reported that Gorbachev said he knew nothing about it. Bonner and Sakharov were released shortly thereafter, in December 1986.

A member of the Circumcision Resource Center in Boston, he was one of the first scientists committed against circumcision but his article "Circumcision", rejected by *The New York Times* in 1975, was published in 2012 only by an English magazine.

Wald died in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts"). He was married twice: in 1931 to Frances Kingsley (1906–1980) and in 1958 to the biochemist [Ruth Hubbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Hubbard "Ruth Hubbard"). He had two sons with Kingsley—Michael and David; he and Hubbard had a son—musicologist and musician [Elijah Wald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elijah_Wald "Elijah Wald")—and a daughter, Deborah, a family law attorney. He was an atheist.

## Scientific career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Cone-absorbance-en.svg/250px-Cone-absorbance-en.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cone-response-en.svg)

Wald plotted the absorbance of rod pigment (black curve), then later the absorbance of cone pigments (red, green, and blue curves).

As a postdoctoral researcher, Wald discovered that [vitamin A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_A "Vitamin A") was a component of the retina. His further experiments showed that when the pigment [rhodopsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodopsin "Rhodopsin") was exposed to light, it yielded the protein [opsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opsin "Opsin") and a compound containing vitamin A. This suggested that vitamin A was essential in retinal function.

In the 1950s, Wald and his colleagues used chemical methods to extract pigments from the retina. Then, using a [spectrophotometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrophotometer "Spectrophotometer"), they were able to measure the light absorbance of the pigments. Since the absorbance of [light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light "Light") by retina pigments corresponds to the [wavelengths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength "Wavelength") that best activate [photoreceptor cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoreceptor_cell "Photoreceptor cell"), this experiment showed the wavelengths that the eye could best detect. However, since [rod cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_cell "Rod cell") make up most of the retina, what Wald and his colleagues were specifically measuring was the absorbance of rhodopsin, the main photopigment in rods. Later, with a technique called [microspectrophotometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microspectrophotometry "Microspectrophotometry"), he was able to measure the absorbance directly from cells, rather than from an extract of the pigments. This allowed Wald to determine the absorbance of pigments in the [cone cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_cell "Cone cell").
