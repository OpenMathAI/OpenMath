**John Michael Bishop** (February 22, 1936 – March 20, 2026) was an American [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist "Immunologist") and [microbiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiologist "Microbiologist") who shared the 1989 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Harold E. Varmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_E._Varmus "Harold E. Varmus"). He was a faculty member at the [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco "University of California, San Francisco") (UCSF), where he also served as chancellor from 1998 to 2009.

## Early life and education

Bishop was born in [York, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York,_Pennsylvania "York, Pennsylvania"), on February 22, 1936. He attended [Gettysburg College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_College "Gettysburg College") as an undergraduate, where he was a brother of the Theta-Pi Zeta chapter of [Lambda Chi Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambda_Chi_Alpha "Lambda Chi Alpha") fraternity. He later attended [Harvard University Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University_Medical_School "Harvard University Medical School"), where he earned an [MD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine "Doctor of Medicine") in 1962.

## Career

Bishop began his career working for the [National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Allergy_and_Infectious_Diseases "National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases"), a part of the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health"). He then spent a year working for the [Heinrich Pette Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Pette_Institute "Heinrich Pette Institute") in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg "Hamburg"), Germany, before joining the faculty of the [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco "University of California, San Francisco"), in 1968. Bishop remained on the school's faculty from 1968 on, and was [chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_(education) "Chancellor (education)") of the university from 1998 to 2009. He was director of the Bishop Lab.

He became the eighth chancellor of UCSF in 1998. He oversaw one of UCSF's major transition and growth periods, including the expanding Mission Bay development and philanthropic support recruitment. During his tenure, he unveiled the first comprehensive, campus-wide, strategic plan to promote diversity and foster a supportive work environment. During this time, UCSF also adopted a new mission: *advancing health worldwide*™.

## Research

Much of this work was conducted jointly with Harold Varmus in a notably long scientific partnership. Their best-known accomplishment was the identification of a cellular gene (c-src) that gave rise to the v-src oncogene of Rous Sarcoma Virus, a cancer-causing virus first isolated from a chicken sarcoma by [Peyton Rous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_Rous "Peyton Rous") in 1910. Their discovery was soon followed by the identification of many other cellular proto-oncogenes—progenitors of viral oncogenes and targets for mutations that drive human cancers.

## Death

Bishop died from pneumonia in San Francisco on March 20, 2026, at the age of 90.

## Awards and honors

Bishop was best known for his Nobel-winning work on [retroviral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrovirus "Retrovirus") [oncogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncogenes "Oncogenes"). Working with [Harold E. Varmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_E._Varmus "Harold E. Varmus") in the 1980s, he discovered the first human oncogene, [c-Src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Src_(gene)#c-src "Src (gene)"). Their findings allowed the understanding of how malignant [tumors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumor "Tumor") are formed from changes to the normal [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene") of a [cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)"). These changes can be produced by viruses, by radiation, or by exposure to some chemicals.

He was a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"), the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society").

Bishop was also a recipient of [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in 2003. That same year, his book *How to win the Nobel Prize: An Unexpected Life in Science* ([LCCN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LCCN_(identifier) "LCCN (identifier)") [2002-192234](https://www.loc.gov/item/2002192234)) was published. He was elected [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2008 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 2008"). In 2020, Bishop received from the [UC Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Berkeley "UC Berkeley") Academic Senate the [Clark Kerr Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Kerr_Award "Clark Kerr Award") for distinguished leadership in higher education.

## Archival collections

The University of California, San Francisco, Archives and Special Collections houses a collection of J. Michael Bishop papers, including his laboratory research notebooks, writings, photographs, and other material.
