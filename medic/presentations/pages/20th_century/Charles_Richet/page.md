**Charles Robert Richet** (French pronunciation: [[ʃaʁl ʁɔbɛʁ ʁiʃɛ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 25 August 1850 – 4 December 1935) was a French [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology") at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France") and [immunology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunology "Immunology") pioneer. In 1913, he won the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in Physiology or Medicine "in recognition of his work on [anaphylaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaphylaxis "Anaphylaxis")". Richet devoted many years to the study of [paranormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranormal "Paranormal") and [spiritualist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritualism_(beliefs) "Spiritualism (beliefs)") phenomena, coining the term "[ectoplasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectoplasm_(paranormal) "Ectoplasm (paranormal)")". He believed in the inferiority of [black people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people "Black people"), was a proponent of [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics "Eugenics"), and presided over the French Eugenics Society towards the end of his life. The Richet line of professorships of medical science continued through his son Charles and his grandson Gabriel. Gabriel Richet was also one of the pioneers of European [nephrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nephrology "Nephrology").

## Career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Charles_Richet_1922.jpg/250px-Charles_Richet_1922.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Charles_Richet_1922.jpg)

Richet in 1922

He was born on 25 August 1850 in Paris the son of Alfred Richet. He was educated at the Lycee Bonaparte in Paris then studied medicine at university in Paris.

Richet spent a period of time as an intern at the Salpêtrière hospital in Paris, where he observed [Jean-Martin Charcot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Martin_Charcot "Jean-Martin Charcot")'s work with then so called "hysterical" patients.

In 1887, Richet became professor of [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology") at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France "Collège de France") investigating a variety of subjects such as [neurochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurochemistry "Neurochemistry"), [digestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestion "Digestion"), [thermoregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoregulation "Thermoregulation") in [homeothermic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeothermy "Homeothermy") animals, and [breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breathing "Breathing"). In 1898, he became a member of the [Académie de Médecine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_de_Médecine "Académie de Médecine"). In 1914, he became a member of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences "French Academy of Sciences").

Richet discovered the analgesic drug [chloralose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloralose "Chloralose") with Maurice Hanriot.

Richet had many interests, and he wrote books about history, sociology, philosophy, psychology, as well as theatre and poetry. He was a pioneer in aviation.

He was involved in the French pacifist movement. Starting in 1902, pacifist societies began to meet at a National Peace Congress, often with several hundred attendees. Unable to unify the pacifist forces they set up a small permanent delegation of French Pacifist Societies in 1902, which Richet led, together with [Lucien Le Foyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucien_Le_Foyer "Lucien Le Foyer") as secretary-general.

## Discovery of anaphylaxis

Richet, working with [Paul Portier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Portier_(physiologist) "Paul Portier (physiologist)"), discovered the phenomenon of anaphylaxis. In 1901, they joined [Albert I, Prince of Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_I,_Prince_of_Monaco "Albert I, Prince of Monaco") on a scientific expedition around the French coast of Atlantic Ocean. On board Albert's ship, *Princesse Alice II*, they extracted a toxin (which they called a hypnotoxin) that is produced by cnidarians such as [Portuguese man o' war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_man_o'_war "Portuguese man o' war") and sea anemone (*[Actinia sulcata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actinia_sulcata "Actinia sulcata")*).

In their first experiment on the ship, they injected a dog with the toxin, expecting the dog to develop immunity (tolerance) to the toxin, but instead it suffered a severe immune reaction ([hypersensitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersensitivity "Hypersensitivity")). In 1902, they repeated the injections in their laboratory and found that dogs normally tolerated the toxin at first injection, but when given subsequent injections three weeks later, they always developed fatal shock, regardless of the dose of the toxin they were given. Thus, they discovered that the first dose, instead of inducing tolerance ([prophylaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophylaxis "Prophylaxis")) as they had expected, caused further doses to be deadly.

In 1902, Richet coined the term *aphylaxis* to describe the phenomenon; he later changed it to *anaphylaxis* because he thought it was more [euphonious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphony "Euphony"). The term is from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Greek "Classical Greek") ἀνά-, *ana-*, meaning "against", and φύλαξις, *phylaxis*, meaning "protection". On 15 February 1902, Richet and Portier jointly presented their experiments to the Societé de Biologie in Paris. Their research is regarded as the beginning of the scientific study of [allergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allergy "Allergy") (the word was coined by [Clemens von Pirquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clemens_von_Pirquet "Clemens von Pirquet") in 1906). It helped explain [hay fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hay_fever "Hay fever") and other [allergic reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allergy "Allergy") to foreign substances, [asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma "Asthma"), certain reactions to [intoxication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_intoxication "Substance intoxication"), and certain cases of [sudden cardiac death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudden_cardiac_death "Sudden cardiac death"). Richet continued to study the phenomenon of anaphylaxis, and in 1913 was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for his work.

## Parapsychology

Richet was deeply interested in the idea of [extrasensory perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrasensory_perception "Extrasensory perception"), and in hypnosis. In 1884, [Alexandr Aksakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandr_Aksakov "Alexandr Aksakov") interested him in the [medium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediumship "Mediumship") of [Eusapia Palladino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusapia_Palladino "Eusapia Palladino"). In 1891, Richet founded the *Annales des sciences psychiques*. He kept in touch with renowned [occultists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occultism "Occultism") and [spiritualists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritualism_(movement) "Spiritualism (movement)") of his time such as [Albert von Schrenck-Notzing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_von_Schrenck-Notzing "Albert von Schrenck-Notzing"), [Frederic William Henry Myers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_William_Henry_Myers "Frederic William Henry Myers") and [Gabriel Delanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Delanne "Gabriel Delanne"). In 1919, Richet became honorary chairman of the [Institut Métapsychique International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Métapsychique_International "Institut Métapsychique International") in Paris, and, in 1930, its full-time president.

Richet hoped to find a physical mechanism that would scientifically validate the existence of [paranormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranormal "Paranormal") phenomena. He wrote: "It has been shown that as regards subjective metapsychics the simplest and most rational explanation is to suppose the existence of a faculty of supernormal cognition ... setting in motion the human intelligence by certain vibrations that do not move the normal senses." In 1905, Richet was named president of the [Society for Psychical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Psychical_Research "Society for Psychical Research") in the United Kingdom.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Linda_Gazzera_medium_2.png/250px-Linda_Gazzera_medium_2.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Linda_Gazzera_medium_2.png)

[Linda Gazzera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Gazzera "Linda Gazzera") a medium Richet investigated, in Paris, 1909.

In 1894, Richet coined the term [ectoplasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectoplasm_(paranormal) "Ectoplasm (paranormal)"). Richet believed that some apparent mediumship could be explained physically as due to the external projection of a material substance (ectoplasm) from the body of the medium, but he didn't believe that this proposed substance had anything to do with spirits. He rejected the [spirit hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritualism_(movement) "Spiritualism (movement)") of mediumship as unscientific, instead supporting the sixth-sense hypothesis. According to Richet:

> It seems to me prudent not to give credence to the spiritistic hypothesis... it appears to me still (at the present time, at all events) improbable, for it contradicts (at least apparently) the most precise and definite data of physiology, whereas the hypothesis of the sixth sense is a new physiological notion which contradicts nothing that we learn from physiology. Consequently, although in certain rare cases spiritism supplies an apparently simpler explanation, I cannot bring myself to accept it. When we have fathomed the history of these unknown vibrations emanating from reality – past reality, present reality, and even future reality – we shall doubtless have given them an unwonted degree of importance. The history of the Hertzian waves shows us the ubiquity of these vibrations in the external world, imperceptible to our senses.

He hypothesized a "sixth sense", an ability to perceive hypothetical vibrations, and he discussed this idea in his 1928 book *Our Sixth Sense*. Although he believed in extrasensory perception, Richet did not believe in [life after death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_after_death "Life after death") or spirits.

He investigated and studied various mediums, such as [Eva Carrière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Carrière "Eva Carrière"), [William Eglinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Eglinton "William Eglinton"), [Pascal Forthuny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Forthuny "Pascal Forthuny"), [Stefan Ossowiecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Ossowiecki "Stefan Ossowiecki"), [Leonora Piper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonora_Piper "Leonora Piper") and [Raphael Schermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Schermann "Raphael Schermann"). From 1905 to 1910, Richet attended many séances led by the medium [Linda Gazzera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Gazzera "Linda Gazzera"), claiming that she was a genuine medium who had performed [psychokinesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychokinesis "Psychokinesis"), meaning that various objects had been moved in the séance room purely through the force of the mind. Gazzera was exposed as a fraud in 1911. Richet was also fooled into believing that [Joaquin María Argamasilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquin_María_Argamasilla "Joaquin María Argamasilla"), known as the "Spaniard with X-ray Eyes", had genuine [psychic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychic "Psychic") powers. [Harry Houdini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Houdini "Harry Houdini") exposed Argamasilla as a fraud in 1924. According to [Joseph McCabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_McCabe "Joseph McCabe"), Richet was also duped by the fraudulent mediums Eva Carrière and [Eusapia Palladino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusapia_Palladino "Eusapia Palladino").

The historian [Ruth Brandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Brandon "Ruth Brandon") criticized Richet as credulous when it came to psychical research, pointing to "his will to believe, and his disinclination to accept any unpalatably contrary indications".

## Eugenics and racial beliefs

Richet was a proponent of [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics "Eugenics"), advocating [sterilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterilization_(medicine) "Sterilization (medicine)") and marriage prohibition for those with mental disabilities. He expressed his eugenist ideas in his 1919 book *La Sélection Humaine*. From 1920 to 1926 he presided over the French Eugenics Society.

Psychologist [Gustav Jahoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Jahoda "Gustav Jahoda") has noted that Richet "was a firm believer in the inferiority of blacks", comparing [black people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people "Black people") to apes, and intellectually to [imbeciles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_disability "Intellectual disability").
