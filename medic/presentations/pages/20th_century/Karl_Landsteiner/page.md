**Karl Landsteiner** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") (German: [[kaʁl ˈlantˌʃtaɪnɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German"); 14 June 1868 – 26 June 1943) was an Austrian-American [biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist "Biologist"), physician, and [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist "Immunologist"). He emigrated with his family to New York in 1923 at the age of 55 for professional opportunities, working for the [Rockefeller Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University").

He had distinguished the main [blood groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_type "Blood type") in 1901, having developed the modern system of classification of blood groups from his identification of the presence of [agglutinins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agglutinin "Agglutinin") in the blood. In 1937, with [Alexander S. Wiener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_S._Wiener "Alexander S. Wiener"), he identified the [Rhesus factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhesus_factor "Rhesus factor"), thus enabling physicians to [transfuse blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_transfusion "Blood transfusion") without endangering the patient's life. With [Constantin Levaditi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Levaditi "Constantin Levaditi") and [Erwin Popper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Popper "Erwin Popper"), he discovered the [polio virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polio_virus "Polio virus") in 1909. He received the [Aronson Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aronson_Prize "Aronson Prize") in 1926. In 1930, he received the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine"). He was posthumously awarded the [Lasker Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker_Award "Lasker Award") in 1946, and has been described as the father of transfusion medicine.

## Early life and education

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Karl_Landsteiner_child.jpg/250px-Karl_Landsteiner_child.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Karl_Landsteiner_child.jpg)

Landsteiner as a child

He was born into a Jewish family. His father Leopold Landsteiner (1818–1875), a renowned Viennese journalist and editor-in-chief of *[Die Presse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Presse "Die Presse")*, died at age 56, when Karl was 6. The boy became very close to his mother Fanny (née Hess; 1837–1908). After graduating with the Matura exam from a Vienna secondary school, he took up the study of medicine at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna "University of Vienna"). Landsteiner wrote his doctoral thesis in 1891. While still a student he published an essay on the influence of diets on the composition of blood.

From 1891 to 1893, Landsteiner studied chemistry in [Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Würzburg "Würzburg") under [Hermann Emil Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Emil_Fischer "Hermann Emil Fischer"), in [München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/München "München"), [Eugen Bamberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugen_Bamberger "Eugen Bamberger") and in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich "Zürich") under [Arthur Rudolf Hantzsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rudolf_Hantzsch "Arthur Rudolf Hantzsch"). He had a number of publications from that period, some of them in co-operation with his professors.

## Research work in Vienna; discovery of the polio virus

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Karl_Landsteiner_young.jpg/250px-Karl_Landsteiner_young.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Karl_Landsteiner_young.jpg)

Landsteiner in the lab

After returning to Vienna he became an assistant to [Max von Gruber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_von_Gruber "Max von Gruber") at the Hygienic Institute. In his studies he concentrated on the mechanism of immunity and the nature of antibodies. From November 1897 to 1908 Landsteiner was an assistant at the pathological-anatomical institute of the University of Vienna under [Anton Weichselbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Weichselbaum "Anton Weichselbaum"), where he published 75 papers, dealing with issues in serology, bacteriology, virology and pathological anatomy. In addition he did some 3,600 autopsies in those ten years. Weichselbaum was Landsteiner's tutor for his postdoctoral lecture qualification in 1903. From 1908 to 1920 Landsteiner was prosector at the Wilhelminenspital in Vienna and in 1911 he was sworn in as an associate professor of pathological anatomy. During that time he discovered – in co-operation with [Erwin Popper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Popper "Erwin Popper") – the infectious character of [poliomyelitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliomyelitis "Poliomyelitis") and isolated the polio virus. In recognition of this groundbreaking discovery, which proved to be the basis for the fight against polio, he was posthumously inducted into the [Polio Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polio_Hall_of_Fame "Polio Hall of Fame") at [Warm Springs, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm_Springs,_Georgia "Warm Springs, Georgia"), which was dedicated in January 1958.

## Research of the blood groups

In 1900 Landsteiner found out that the blood of two people under contact [agglutinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemagglutination "Hemagglutination"), and in 1901 he found that this effect was due to contact of blood with [blood serum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_serum "Blood serum"). As a result, he succeeded in identifying [the three blood groups A, B and O, which he labelled C, of human blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABO_blood_group_system "ABO blood group system"). Landsteiner also found out that blood transfusion between persons with the same blood group did not lead to the destruction of blood cells, whereas this occurred between persons of different blood groups. Based on his findings, the first successful blood transfusion was performed by [Reuben Ottenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuben_Ottenberg "Reuben Ottenberg") at [Mount Sinai Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sinai_Hospital_(Manhattan) "Mount Sinai Hospital (Manhattan)") in New York in 1907.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d5/Karl_Landsteiner_Penning.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Karl_Landsteiner_Penning.jpg)

Karl Landsteiner depicted on a medal awarded by the [Netherlands Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Red_Cross "Netherlands Red Cross")

Today, whole blood transfusions are rare. It is now well known that persons with blood group AB can accept [red blood cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_blood_cell "Red blood cell") donations of the other blood groups, and that persons with blood group O-negative can donate red blood cells to all other groups. Individuals with blood group AB are referred to as *universal recipients* and those with blood group O-negative are known as *universal donors*. These donor-recipient relationships arise due to the fact that type O-negative blood possesses neither antigens of blood group A nor of blood group B. Therefore, the immune systems of persons with blood group A, B or AB do not refuse the donation. Further, because persons with blood group AB do not form antibodies against either the antigens of blood group A or B, they can accept red blood cells from persons with these blood groups, as well as from persons with blood group O-negative.

In 1930 Landsteiner was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in recognition of these achievements. For his pioneering work, he is recognized as the father of transfusion medicine.

## Research work in the Netherlands and the United States

After [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), Vienna and the [new republic of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Austrian_Republic "First Austrian Republic") as a whole was in a desolate economic state, a situation in which Landsteiner did not see any possibilities to carry on with his research work. He decided to move to the Netherlands and accepted a post as prosector in the small Catholic *St. Joannes de Deo* hospital (now HMC Westeinde) in [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague "The Hague") and, in order to improve his financial situation also took a job in a small factory, producing old [tuberculin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculin "Tuberculin") (*tuberculinum pristinum*). He also published a number of papers, five of them being published in Dutch by the Royal Academy of Sciences. Yet working conditions proved to be not much better than in post-war Vienna.

So Landsteiner accepted the invitation that reached him from New York, initiated by [Simon Flexner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Flexner "Simon Flexner"), who was familiar with Landsteiner's work, to work for the Rockefeller Institute. He arrived there with his family in the spring of 1923. Throughout the 1920s Landsteiner worked on the problems of immunity and allergy. In 1927 he discovered new blood groups: M, N and P, refining the work he had begun 20 years before. Shortly thereafter, Landsteiner and his collaborator, [Philip Levine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Levine_(physician) "Philip Levine (physician)"), published the work and, later that same year, the types began to be used in [paternity suits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parental_test "Parental test").

## Awards and honours

In addition to winning the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine"), Landsteiner was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1932, elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1935, and awarded the [Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Prize_for_Therapeutics_of_the_University_of_Edinburgh "Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh") in 1937. He was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1941 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1941") in 1941. In 1946, he was posthumously awarded the [Lasker-DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker-DeBakey_Clinical_Medical_Research_Award "Lasker-DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award"). Since 2005, [World Blood Donor Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Blood_Donor_Day "World Blood Donor Day") is celebrated on Landsteiner's birthday anniversary.

## Personal life

Landsteiner converted from [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism") to [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity") in 1890, and was baptised into the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church"). In 1916, he married Leopoldine Helene Wlasto, a [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox "Greek Orthodox") woman who converted to her husband's Catholic faith.

In 1937, Landsteiner unsuccessfully initiated legal action against an American publisher who had included him in the book *Who's Who in American Jewry*. Landsteiner said that "it will be detrimental to me to emphasize publicly the religion of my ancestors."
