**George Richards Minot** ([/ˈmaɪnɒt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Minot-nd.ogg "File:Minot-nd.ogg") [*MY-not*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); December 2, 1885 – February 25, 1950) was an American medical researcher who shared the 1934 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [George Hoyt Whipple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Hoyt_Whipple "George Hoyt Whipple") and [William P. Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Murphy "William P. Murphy") for their pioneering work on [pernicious anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernicious_anemia "Pernicious anemia").

## Early life

George Richards Minot was born in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston"), [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts "Massachusetts") to James Jackson Minot (1853–1938) and Elizabeth Whitney. He was namesake of his great-great-grandfather George Richards Minot (1758–1802). His father was a physician; his father's cousin was [anatomist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy "Anatomy") [Charles Sedgwick Minot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Sedgwick_Minot "Charles Sedgwick Minot") (1852–1914); one of his great-grandfathers was [James Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Jackson_(physician) "James Jackson (physician)") (1777–1867), co-founder of [Massachusetts General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_General_Hospital "Massachusetts General Hospital"). He developed interest, first, in the natural sciences, and then, in medicine.

## Education

Minot obtained his B.A. from [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College "Harvard College") in 1908, where he was elected to [The Owl Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owl_Club_(Harvard) "Owl Club (Harvard)"), and obtained his M.D. degree in 1912 from the [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"). Between 1913 and 1915, he worked in the [William Henry Howell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Howell "William Henry Howell")'s lab at [Johns Hopkins School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Medicine "Johns Hopkins School of Medicine"), studying blood thinning proteins, such as [antithrombin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antithrombin "Antithrombin"). In 1915, he secured a junior position on the medical staff of the [Massachusetts General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_General_Hospital "Massachusetts General Hospital"), where he started research on blood [anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemia "Anemia"). During the first world war, he served as a surgeon in for the US Army. As part of those duties, he worked with [Alice Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Hamilton "Alice Hamilton") to understand what was causing workers at a munitions plant in New Jersey to become ill. They eventually discovered that skin contact with TNT led to the sicknesses.

## Career

In 1917, he came to Collis P. Huntington Memorial Hospital in Boston; he became chief of medical services in 1923, and was appointed physician-in-chief in 1934. In addition, Minot became professor of medicine at the Harvard University, and was appointed director of the Thorndik Memorial Laboratory at [Boston City Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_City_Hospital "Boston City Hospital"). He also worked in the Peter Bent Brigham Hospital as a staff member. He was a member of the Pernicious Anemia Committee at Harvard and served on the Anti-Anemia Preparation Advisory Board of the [U.S. Pharmacopoeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Pharmacopeia "United States Pharmacopeia").

Minot was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") in 1926. In 1930, Minot was awarded the [Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Prize_for_Therapeutics_of_the_University_of_Edinburgh "Cameron Prize for Therapeutics of the University of Edinburgh") with [William P. Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Murphy "William P. Murphy"). Minot shared the 1934 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with [William P. Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Murphy "William P. Murphy") and [George H. Whipple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._Whipple "George H. Whipple") given for their work on the treatment of blood [anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemia "Anemia"). They all discovered an effective treatment for [pernicious anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernicious_anemia "Pernicious anemia"), which was a terminal disease at the time, with liver concentrate high in [vitamin B12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_B12 "Vitamin B12"), later identified as the critical compound in the treatment. He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1935 and a member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1937.

## Personal life and death

Minot was diagnosed with [diabetes mellitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_mellitus "Diabetes mellitus") in 1921 at the age of 35, by Dr [Elliott P. Joslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_P._Joslin "Elliott P. Joslin"), a fellow professor at Harvard Medical School and one of the leading diabetes doctors of his time. Diabetes was a fatal disease at the time. Joslin kept him alive the only way he knew, by restricting food. Minot was 6 feet one inches tall and only weighed 135 pounds. Joslin put him on a diet of only 530 calories per day. Minot, like most every diabetes patient, at the time, would probably die within a year.

However, insulin was discovered at about the same time Minot was diagnosed. Insulin became widely available about a year later Dr. [William Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bosworth_Castle "William Bosworth Castle") observed that [Frederick Banting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Banting "Frederick Banting")'s and [Charles Best](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Best_(medical_scientist) "Charles Best (medical scientist)")'s discovery of [insulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin "Insulin") in 1921, not only transformed diabetes treatment, but also, by keeping Minot alive, contributed towards the discovery of a cure for [pernicious anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernicious_anemia "Pernicious anemia"). Minot and Murphy's famous paper *Treatment of pernicious anemia by a special diet* was published in 1926.

Minot began developing complications associated with diabetes in 1940, and suffered a serious stroke in 1947, which partially paralyzed him. He died in [Brookline, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookline,_Massachusetts "Brookline, Massachusetts") on February 25, 1950. He was a [Unitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarianism "Unitarianism"). [His home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_R._Minot_House "George R. Minot House") in [Brookline, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookline,_Massachusetts "Brookline, Massachusetts"), was designated a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark "National Historic Landmark") in recognition for his work.

Minot and his wife Marian Linzee Minot (Weld) (1890–1979), whom he married in 1915, had two daughters and a son.
