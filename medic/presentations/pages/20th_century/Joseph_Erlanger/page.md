**Joseph Erlanger** (January 5, 1874 – December 5, 1965) was an American [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology") who is best known for his contributions to the field of [neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience "Neuroscience"). Together with [Herbert Spencer Gasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Spencer_Gasser "Herbert Spencer Gasser"), he identified several varieties of [nerve fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_fiber "Nerve fiber") and established the relationship between [action potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_potential "Action potential") velocity and fiber diameter. They were awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1944 for these achievements.

## Biography

Erlanger was born on January 5, 1874, in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California"). His Jewish father immigrated from [Buchau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Buchau "Bad Buchau") [Kingdom of Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Württemberg "Kingdom of Württemberg"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany") and his mother also immigrated from [Kingdom of Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Württemberg "Kingdom of Württemberg"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany"), she was also Jewish. They got to know each other in California during the [Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Gold_Rush "California Gold Rush"). Joseph was the sixth of seven children born to the couple. He completed his [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in Chemistry from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1895. He then completed his M.D. in 1899 from the [Johns Hopkins School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Medicine "Johns Hopkins School of Medicine") in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore"), [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "Maryland"), where he finished second in his class.

Upon graduating, Erlanger interned at [Johns Hopkins Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_Hospital "Johns Hopkins Hospital") under [William Osler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Osler "William Osler") and worked in a physiology laboratory. Erlanger also gave lectures at the school on digestion and metabolism. Erlanger also had an interest in [cardiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiology "Cardiology"), specifically the way that excitation transferred from the [atrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrium_(heart) "Atrium (heart)") to the [ventricle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventricle_(heart) "Ventricle (heart)") and researched with [Arthur Hirschfelder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_D._Hirschfelder "Arthur D. Hirschfelder"). Erlanger developed and patented a new type of [sphygmomanometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphygmomanometer "Sphygmomanometer") that could measure [blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_pressure "Blood pressure") from the [brachial artery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachial_artery "Brachial artery"). While working at [Johns Hopkins School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Medicine "Johns Hopkins School of Medicine") in 1901, Erlanger published a paper on the digestive systems of canines. This paper caught the attention of [William Henry Howell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Howell "William Henry Howell"), a [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology") professor at [Johns Hopkins School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Medicine "Johns Hopkins School of Medicine"). Howell recruited Erlanger as an assistant professor. Erlanger was promoted to associate professor some time before 1906.

In 1906, Erlanger accepted a position as the first chair of physiology at the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin "University of Wisconsin") in [Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin "Madison, Wisconsin"). In 1910, he left to take a position as professor at [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis"); the St. Louis position offered Erlanger more funding for his projects. Herbert Spencer Gasser, Erlanger's former student at Wisconsin, joined Erlanger's laboratory soon after the move. During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), the pair contributed to the research effort examining the effects of [shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_(circulatory) "Shock (circulatory)"). As part of this work, Erlanger was able to produce [heart block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_block "Heart block") in an animal model by clamping the [bundle of His](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundle_of_His "Bundle of His") and tightening it. Together, they managed to amplify the action potential of a [bullfrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullfrog "Bullfrog") [sciatic nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciatic_nerve "Sciatic nerve") in 1922 and published the results in the *[American Journal of Physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Physiology "American Journal of Physiology")*. It is uncertain why the pair had such a sudden shift in interest to [neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience "Neuroscience"), as Erlanger was already widely respected in the cardiology field.

Erlanger was elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1922 and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1927.

Erlanger and Gasser were able to modify a [Western Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Electric "Western Electric") [oscilloscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscilloscope "Oscilloscope") to run at low voltages. Prior to this modification, the only method available to measure neural activity was the [electroencephalograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroencephalography "Electroencephalography"), which could only show large-scale electrical activity. With this technology, they were able to observe that action potentials occurred in two phases—a spike (initial surge) followed by an after-spike (a sequence of slow changes in potential). They discovered that [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron "Neuron") were found in many forms, each with their own potential for excitability. With this research, the pair discovered that the velocity of action potentials was directly proportional to the diameter of the nerve fiber. The partnership ended in 1931, when Gasser accepted a position at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"). In 1944, they won the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in [Medicine or Physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for these discoveries.

He died of heart disease on December 5, 1965, at [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis "St. Louis"), [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri "Missouri"). The [Joseph Erlanger House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Erlanger_House "Joseph Erlanger House") in St. Louis was designated a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark "National Historic Landmark") on December 8, 1976, as a building of national significance. On January 22, 2009, the [International Astronomical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union "International Astronomical Union") named a [crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlanger_(crater) "Erlanger (crater)") on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon") after him.
