**Hamilton Othanel Smith** (August 31, 1931 – October 25, 2025) was an American [microbiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiologist "Microbiologist") and Nobel laureate.

## Early life and education

Hamilton Othanel Smith was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") on August 31, 1931 and grew up in Urbana-Champaign, Illinois.

Smith graduated from [University Laboratory High School of Urbana, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Laboratory_High_School_of_Urbana,_Illinois "University Laboratory High School of Urbana, Illinois"). He attended the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign "University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign"), but in 1950 transferred to the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"), where he earned his [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in Mathematics in 1952. He received his medical degree from [Johns Hopkins School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Medicine "Johns Hopkins School of Medicine") in 1956. Between 1956 and 1957 Smith worked for the [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis") Medical Service.

During the next few years he undertook US Navy military service as well as a medical residency at [Henry Ford Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ford_Hospital "Henry Ford Hospital"). Hamilton then went to the [University of Michigan in Ann Arbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan_in_Ann_Arbor "University of Michigan in Ann Arbor") on a US [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health") fellowship where he worked with infectious disease expert [Myron M. Levine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myron_M._Levine?action=edit&redlink=1 "Myron M. Levine").

In 1975, he was awarded a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship "Guggenheim Fellowship") he spent at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich").

## Scientific career

In 1970, Smith and Kent W. Wilcox discovered the first type II [restriction enzyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restriction_enzyme "Restriction enzyme"), which is now known as HindII. Smith went on to discover DNA [methylases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methylase "Methylase") that constitute the other half of the bacterial host restriction and modification systems, as hypothesized by [Werner Arber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Arber "Werner Arber") of Switzerland.

Smith was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1978 for discovering type II [restriction enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restriction_enzyme "Restriction enzyme") with [Werner Arber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Arber "Werner Arber") and [Daniel Nathans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Nathans "Daniel Nathans") as co-recipients.

He later became a leading figure in the nascent field of [genomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genomics "Genomics"), when in 1995 he and a team at [The Institute for Genomic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Institute_for_Genomic_Research "The Institute for Genomic Research") sequenced the first [bacterial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria "Bacteria") [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome "Genome"), that of *[Haemophilus influenzae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haemophilus_influenzae "Haemophilus influenzae")*. *H. influenza* was the same organism in which Smith had discovered restriction enzymes in the late 1960s. He subsequently played a key role in the sequencing of many of the early genomes at The Institute for Genomic Research, and in the assembly of the [human genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_genome "Human genome") at [Celera Genomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celera_Genomics "Celera Genomics"), which he joined when it was founded in 1998.

Smith later directed a team at the [J. Craig Venter Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Craig_Venter_Institute "J. Craig Venter Institute") that worked towards creating a partially [synthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_biology "Synthetic biology") bacterium, *[Mycoplasma laboratorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycoplasma_laboratorium "Mycoplasma laboratorium")*. In 2003 the same group synthetically assembled the genome of a virus, [Phi X 174](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_X_174 "Phi X 174") [bacteriophage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteriophage "Bacteriophage"). Smith was scientific director of privately held [Synthetic Genomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_Genomics "Synthetic Genomics"), which was founded in 2005 by [Craig Venter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Venter "Craig Venter") to continue this work. Synthetic Genomics is working to produce [biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel "Biofuel") on an industrial-scale using recombinant [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae "Algae") and other microorganisms.

## Personal life and death

Smith was married to Liz Smith until her death and they had five children. They also had 12 grandchildren and 15 great grandchildren. One son preceded him in death.

Smith was diagnosed with [lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoma "Lymphoma") in 2016. He died at his home in [Ellicott City, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellicott_City,_Maryland "Ellicott City, Maryland"), on October 25, 2025, at the age of 94.
