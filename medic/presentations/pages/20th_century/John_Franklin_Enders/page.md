**John Franklin Enders** (February 10, 1897 – September 8, 1985) was an American biomedical scientist and [Nobel Laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Laureate "Nobel Laureate"). Enders has been called "The Father of Modern Vaccines."

## Life and education

Enders was born in [West Hartford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Hartford,_Connecticut "West Hartford, Connecticut"), on February 10, 1897. His father, John Ostrom Enders, was CEO of the [Hartford National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harford_National_Bank "Harford National Bank") and left him a fortune of $19 million upon his death. He attended the Noah Webster School in [Hartford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Connecticut "Hartford, Connecticut"), and graduated from [St. Paul's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul's_School_(Concord,_New_Hampshire) "St. Paul's School (Concord, New Hampshire)") in [Concord, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord,_New_Hampshire "Concord, New Hampshire") in 1915. After attending [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") a short time, he joined the [United States Army Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Corps "United States Army Air Corps") in 1918 as a flight instructor and a lieutenant.

After returning from [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), he graduated from [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University"), where he was a member of [Scroll and Key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scroll_and_Key "Scroll and Key") as well as [Delta Kappa Epsilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Kappa_Epsilon "Delta Kappa Epsilon"). He went into real estate in 1922, and tried several careers before choosing the biomedical field with a focus on infectious diseases, gaining a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard "Harvard") in 1930. He later joined the faculty at [Children's Hospital Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_Hospital_Boston "Children's Hospital Boston").

Enders died at his summer home in [Waterford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterford,_Connecticut "Waterford, Connecticut"), aged 88, on 8 September 1985. His wife died in 2000.

## Biomedical career

In 1949, Enders, [Thomas Huckle Weller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Huckle_Weller "Thomas Huckle Weller"), and [Frederick Chapman Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Chapman_Robbins "Frederick Chapman Robbins") reported successful *[in vitro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro "In vitro")* culture of an [animal virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_virus "Animal virus")—[poliovirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliovirus "Poliovirus"). The three received the 1954 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") "for their discovery of the ability of [poliomyelitis viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliovirus "Poliovirus") to grow in cultures of various types of tissue".

Meanwhile, [Jonas Salk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Salk "Jonas Salk") applied the Enders-Weller-Robbins technique to produce large quantities of poliovirus, and then developed a [polio vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polio_vaccine "Polio vaccine") in 1952. Upon the 1954 polio vaccine field trial, whose success Salk announced on the radio, Salk became a public hero but failed to credit the many other researchers that his effort rode upon, and was somewhat shunned by America's scientific establishment.

In 1954, Enders and [Thomas C. Peebles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_C._Peebles "Thomas C. Peebles") isolated [measlesvirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measles_virus "Measles virus") from an 11-year-old boy, David Edmonston. Disappointed by polio vaccine's development and involvement in some cases of polio and death—what Enders attributed to Salk's technique—Enders began development of [measles vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measles_vaccine "Measles vaccine"). In October 1960, an Enders team began trials on 1,500 intellectually disabled children in New York City and on 4,000 children in [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria "Nigeria").
Refusing credit for merely himself when *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* announced the measles vaccine effective on September 17, 1961, Enders wrote to the newspaper to acknowledge the work of various colleagues and the collaborative nature of the research. In 1963, a deactivated measles vaccine and an attenuated measles vaccine were introduced by [Pfizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfizer "Pfizer") and [Merck & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merck_&_Co. "Merck & Co."), respectively.

He continued to work in virology research till the late 1970s and retired from the laboratory at the age of 80.

- 1967: Foreign Member, The [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society")
- 1970: John F. Enders Pediatric Research Laboratories dedicated

Enders also held honorary doctoral degrees from 13 universities.
