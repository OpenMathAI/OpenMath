**Phillip Allen Sharp** (born June 6, 1944) is an American [geneticist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneticist "Geneticist") and [molecular biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology") who co-discovered [RNA splicing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_splicing "RNA splicing"). He shared the 1993 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Richard J. Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_J._Roberts "Richard J. Roberts") for "the discovery that [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene") in [eukaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote "Eukaryote") are not contiguous strings but contain [introns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intron "Intron"), and that the splicing of [messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA "Messenger RNA") to delete those introns can occur in different ways, yielding different [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") from the same DNA sequence". He was awarded the 2015 [Othmer Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Othmer_Gold_Medal "Othmer Gold Medal").

Sharp's current research focuses on small RNAs and other types of [non-coding RNAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-coding_RNAs "Non-coding RNAs"). His laboratory works to identify the target [mRNAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRNAs "MRNAs") of [microRNAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MicroRNAs "MicroRNAs") (miRNAs), and has discovered a class of miRNAs that are produced from sequences adjacent to [transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_(genetics) "Transcription (genetics)") start sites. His laboratory also studies how miRNA [gene regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_of_gene_expression "Regulation of gene expression") functions in [angiogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiogenesis "Angiogenesis") and cellular stress.

## Biography

Sharp was born in [Falmouth, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falmouth,_Kentucky "Falmouth, Kentucky"), the son of Kathrin (Colvin) and Joseph Walter Sharp. He married Ann Holcombe in 1964, and they have three daughters.

Sharp studied at [Union Commonwealth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Commonwealth_University "Union Commonwealth University"), and majored in chemistry and mathematics, afterwards completing his Ph.D. in chemistry at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign "University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign") in 1969. Following his Ph.D., he did his postdoctoral training at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology") until 1971, where he studied [plasmids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasmid "Plasmid"). Later, he studied [gene expression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_expression "Gene expression") in human cells at the [Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Spring_Harbor_Laboratory "Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory") as a senior scientist under [James D. Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dewey_Watson "James Dewey Watson").

In 1974, he was offered a position at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT "MIT") by biologist [Salvador Luria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Luria "Salvador Luria"). He was director of MIT's Center for Cancer Research (now the [Koch Institute for Integrative Cancer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_Institute_for_Integrative_Cancer_Research "Koch Institute for Integrative Cancer Research")) from 1985 to 1991; head of the Biology department from 1991 to 1999; and founder and director of the [McGovern Institute for Brain Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGovern_Institute_for_Brain_Research "McGovern Institute for Brain Research") from 2000 to 2004. In 1995, the FBI confirmed that Sharp received a letter from [Ted Kaczynski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kaczynski "Ted Kaczynski"), insinuating that Sharp would become a target of the [Unabomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unabomber "Unabomber") because of his work in genetics, stating that "it would be beneficial to your health to stop your research in genetics."

He is currently MIT Institute Professor and Professor of Biology Emeritus and member of the [Koch Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_Institute_for_Integrative_Cancer_Research "Koch Institute for Integrative Cancer Research"), and was an [Institute Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_Professor "Institute Professor"), MIT's highest faculty rank, since 1999. He is also the chair of the advisory board of the [MIT Jameel Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Jameel_Clinic "MIT Jameel Clinic"). Sharp co-founded [Biogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogen "Biogen"), [Alnylam Pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alnylam_Pharmaceuticals "Alnylam Pharmaceuticals"), and Magen Biosciences, and has served on the boards of all three companies.

## Awards and honors

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/Phillip_A_Sharp.jpg/330px-Phillip_A_Sharp.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Phillip_A_Sharp.jpg)

Phillip Sharp with [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush"), at the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") awards in 2006.

In addition to the Nobel Prize, Sharp has won several notable awards, including the 2004 [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science"), the 1999 [Benjamin Franklin Medal for Distinguished Achievement in the Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin_Medal_for_Distinguished_Achievement_in_the_Sciences "Benjamin Franklin Medal for Distinguished Achievement in the Sciences") of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"), the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement") in 1981, and the 1988 [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") together with [Thomas R. Cech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_R._Cech "Thomas R. Cech").

Sharp is an elected member of several academic societies, including the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science"), the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"), and the [Institute of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Medicine "Institute of Medicine") of the National Academies. He was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2011 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 2011"). In 2012, he was elected the president of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science"). He is also a Member and Chair of the Scientific Advisory Board of Fidelity Biosciences Group; a member of the Board of Advisors of [Polaris Venture Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polaris_Partners "Polaris Partners"); chairman of the Scientific Advisory Board and member of the Board of Directors of [Alnylam Pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alnylam_Pharmaceuticals "Alnylam Pharmaceuticals"); advisor and investor at Longwood and Polaris Venture Funds; a member of the Boards of Directors at Syros Pharmaceuticals and VIR Biotechnology; and member and Chair of the Scientific Advisory Board at Dewpoint Biotechnology.

He is the subject of the 2025 documentary film *[Cracking the Code: Phil Sharp and the Biotech Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cracking_the_Code:_Phil_Sharp_and_the_Biotech_Revolution "Cracking the Code: Phil Sharp and the Biotech Revolution")*, directed by Bill Haney.

[Pendleton County, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendleton_County,_Kentucky "Pendleton County, Kentucky"), Sharp's birthplace, named its current middle school after him.

## Other activities

In October 2010, Sharp participated in the [USA Science and Engineering Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Science_and_Engineering_Festival "USA Science and Engineering Festival")'s Lunch with a Laureate program where middle and high school students got to engage in an informal conversation with a Nobel Prize-winning scientist over a brown-bag lunch. Sharp is also a member of the [USA Science and Engineering Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Science_and_Engineering_Festival "USA Science and Engineering Festival")'s Advisory Board. In 2011, he was listed at #5 on the [MIT150](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT150 "MIT150") list of the top 150 innovators and ideas from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT "MIT").

He is an editorial advisor to [Xconomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xconomy "Xconomy"), and is a member of the Board of Scientific Governors at [The Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scripps_Research_Institute "The Scripps Research Institute"). He has also served on the Faculty Advisory Board of the MIT-Harvard Research Journal and MIT Student Research Association.

In 2016, Sharp helped organize the [Laureates Letter Supporting Precision Agriculture](https://www.supportprecisionagriculture.org/nobel-laureate-gmo-letter_rjr.html), written to oppose efforts by [Greenpeace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenpeace "Greenpeace") to ban GMOs and [golden rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_rice "Golden rice") in particular.
