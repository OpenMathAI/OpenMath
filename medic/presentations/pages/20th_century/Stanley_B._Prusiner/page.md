**Stanley Ben Prusiner** (born May 28, 1942) is an American [neurologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurology "Neurology") and [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). He is the founding director of the [Institute for Neurodegenerative Diseases](https://ind.ucsf.edu/) (IND) at the [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco "University of California, San Francisco") (UCSF). Prusiner discovered [prions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prion "Prion"), a class of [infectious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infection "Infection") [self-reproducing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_reproduction "Biological reproduction") [pathogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathogen "Pathogen") primarily or solely composed of [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein"), a scientific theory considered by many as a [heretical idea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_B._Prusiner#Prion:_A_heretical_idea) when first proposed. He received the [Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Lasker_Award_for_Basic_Medical_Research "Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research") in 1994 and the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1997 for research on prion diseases developed by him and his team of experts (among them D. E. Garfin, D. P. Stites, W. J. Hadlow and C. M. Eklund) beginning in the early 1970s.

## Early life, career and research

Prusiner was born into a Jewish family in [Des Moines, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines,_Iowa "Des Moines, Iowa"), the son of Miriam (Spigel) and Lawrence Prusiner, an architect. He spent his childhood in Des Moines and [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati "Cincinnati"), Ohio, where he attended [Walnut Hills High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walnut_Hills_High_School "Walnut Hills High School"), where he was known as "the little genius" for his groundbreaking work on a repellent for [Boxelder bugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxelder_bug "Boxelder bug"). Prusiner received a Bachelor of Arts [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_degree "Academic degree") in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") and later received his [M.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine "Doctor of Medicine") from the [University of Pennsylvania School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania_School_of_Medicine "University of Pennsylvania School of Medicine"). Prusiner then completed an [internship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internship "Internship") in medicine at the University of California, San Francisco. Later Prusiner moved to the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health "National Institutes of Health"), where he studied glutaminases in *[E. coli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escherichia_coli "Escherichia coli")* in the laboratory of Earl Stadtman.

After three years at NIH, Prusiner returned to UCSF to complete a [residency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residency_(medicine) "Residency (medicine)") in [neurology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurology "Neurology"). Upon completion of the residency in 1974, Prusiner joined the faculty of the UCSF Department of Neurology. Since that time, Prusiner has held various faculty and visiting faculty positions at both UCSF and [UC Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley").

In 1999, Prusiner founded the IND at UCSF. The IND conducts research on neurodegenerative diseases that are caused by the accumulation of misfolded proteins. These include [Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creutzfeldt–Jakob_disease "Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease"), [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease "Alzheimer's disease"), [synucleinopathies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synucleinopathies "Synucleinopathies") and [tauopathies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauopathy "Tauopathy").

## Prion: a heretical idea

In his 1998 PNAS review article on prions, Prusiner wrote "The idea that scrapie prions were composed of an amyloidogenic protein was truly heretical when it was introduced" by [Tikvah Alper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikvah_Alper "Tikvah Alper"). [Transmissible spongiform encephalopathies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmissible_spongiform_encephalopathy "Transmissible spongiform encephalopathy"), such as [scrapie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrapie "Scrapie") and [bovine spongiform encephalopathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bovine_spongiform_encephalopathy "Bovine spongiform encephalopathy") ("mad cow disease"), are diseases that leave the brains of their victims full of holes. Scientists did not know what pathogen or disease-causing organism produced such a pattern. Prusiner and his co-workers suggested "one scientific theory, viewed as heretical in that it seems to challenge the role of nucleic acids as the exclusive carriers of genetic information." This theory suggested that the pathogen causing prion diseases might be a "deadly variety of a normal protein that has the ability to amplify itself in the brain. The hypothetical protein is called a prion (pronounced PREE-on)," a [portmanteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau "Portmanteau") based on the words "*pro*teinaceous" and "*in*fectious".

## Awards and honors

Stanley Prusiner was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1997 for his work in proposing an explanation for the cause of scrapie, bovine spongiform encephalopathy and human prion diseases such as Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease. He was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") in 1992 and to its governing council in 2007. He is also an elected member of [the American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "The American Academy of Arts and Sciences") (1993), a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1997 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1997"), and a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") (1998) and the [National Academy of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Medicine "National Academy of Medicine") (1992).
