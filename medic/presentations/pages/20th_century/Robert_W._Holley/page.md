**Robert William Holley** (January 28, 1922 – February 11, 1993) was an American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). He shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1968 (with [Har Gobind Khorana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Har_Gobind_Khorana "Har Gobind Khorana") and [Marshall Warren Nirenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Warren_Nirenberg "Marshall Warren Nirenberg")) for describing the structure of alanine [transfer RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRNA "TRNA"), linking [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") and [protein synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_synthesis "Protein synthesis").

Holley was born in [Urbana, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbana,_Illinois "Urbana, Illinois"), and graduated from [Urbana High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbana_High_School_(Illinois) "Urbana High School (Illinois)") in 1938. He went on to study [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign "University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign"), graduating in 1942 and commencing his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") studies in [organic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry "Organic chemistry") at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University"). During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") Holley spent two years working under Professor [Vincent du Vigneaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_du_Vigneaud "Vincent du Vigneaud") at Cornell University Medical College, where he was involved in the first chemical synthesis of [penicillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicillin "Penicillin"). Holley completed his PhD studies in 1947.

Following his graduate studies Holley remained associated with Cornell. He became an assistant professor of organic chemistry in 1948, and was appointed as professor of biochemistry in 1962. He began his research on [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA") after spending a year's sabbatical (1955–1956) studying with [James F. Bonner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_F._Bonner "James F. Bonner") at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology "California Institute of Technology").

Holley's research on [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA "RNA") focused first on isolating [transfer RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_RNA "Transfer RNA") (tRNA), and later on determining the sequence and structure of alanine [tRNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRNA "TRNA"), the molecule that incorporates the [amino acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid "Amino acid") [alanine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanine "Alanine") into [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein"). Holley's team of researchers determined the tRNA's structure by using two [ribonucleases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribonuclease "Ribonuclease") to split the tRNA molecule into pieces. Each enzyme split the molecule at location points for specific nucleotides. By a process of "puzzling out" the structure of the pieces split by the two different enzymes, then comparing the pieces from both enzyme splits, the team eventually determined the entire structure of the molecule. The group of researchers include [Elizabeth Beach Keller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Beach_Keller "Elizabeth Beach Keller"), who developed the cloverleaf model that describes transfer RNA, during the course of the research.

The structure was completed in 1964, and was a key discovery in explaining the [synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_(genetics) "Translation (genetics)") of proteins from [messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRNA "MRNA"). It was also the first nucleotide sequence of a [ribonucleic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribonucleic_acid "Ribonucleic acid") ever determined. Holley was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1968 for this discovery, and [Har Gobind Khorana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Har_Gobind_Khorana "Har Gobind Khorana") and [Marshall W. Nirenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_W._Nirenberg "Marshall W. Nirenberg") were also awarded the prize that year for contributions to the understanding of protein synthesis.

Using the Holley team's method, other scientists determined the structures of the remaining tRNA's. A few years later the method was modified to help track the sequence of nucleotides in various bacterial, plant, and human [viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus "Virus"). He died in 1993

In 1968 Holley became a resident fellow at the [Salk Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salk_Institute "Salk Institute") for Biological Studies in [La Jolla, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla,_California "La Jolla, California").

According to the *New York Times* obituary, "He was an avid outdoorsman and an amateur sculptor of bronze." His widow Ann died in 1996.
