**Ilya Ilyich Mechnikov** (15 May [[O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style "Old Style") 3 May] 1845 – 15 July 1916), [gallicised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallicised "Gallicised") and known in Western sources as **Élie Metchnikoff**, was a [zoologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoologist "Zoologist") from the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire") of [Moldavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyars_of_Moldavia_and_Wallachia "Boyars of Moldavia and Wallachia") [noble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyars_of_Moldavia_and_Wallachia "Boyars of Moldavia and Wallachia") ancestry best known for his research in [immunology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunology "Immunology") (study of immune systems) and [thanatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanatology "Thanatology") (study of death). He and [Paul Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrlich "Paul Ehrlich") were jointly awarded the 1908 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") "in recognition of their work on immunity".

Mechnikov was born in a region of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire") that is today part of modern-day [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine") to a [Moldavian noble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyars_of_Moldavia_and_Wallachia "Boyars of Moldavia and Wallachia") father and a [Ukrainian-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian-Jewish "Ukrainian-Jewish") mother. He later moved to France. Given this complex heritage, five different nations and peoples lay claim to Metchnikoff. Despite having a mother of Jewish origin, he was baptized [Russian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church "Russian Orthodox Church"), although he later became an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism "Atheism").

Honoured as the "father of [innate immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innate_immunity "Innate immunity")", Metchnikoff was the first to discover a process of immunity called [phagocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagocytosis "Phagocytosis") and the cell responsible for it, called [phagocyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagocyte "Phagocyte"), specifically [macrophage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrophage "Macrophage"), in 1882. This discovery turned out to be the major defence mechanism in innate immunity, as well as the foundation of the concept of [cell-mediated immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell-mediated_immunity "Cell-mediated immunity"), while Ehrlich established the concept of [humoral immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humoral_immunity "Humoral immunity") to complete the principles of immune system. Their works are regarded as the foundation of the science of [immunology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunology "Immunology").

Metchnikoff developed one of the earliest concepts in [ageing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageing "Ageing"), and advocated the use of lactic acid bacteria (*[Lactobacillus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactobacillus_delbrueckii_subsp._bulgaricus "Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus")*) for healthy and long life. This became the concept of [probiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probiotics "Probiotics") in medicine. Mechnikov is also credited with coining the term [gerontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerontology "Gerontology") in 1903 for the emerging study of aging and longevity. In this regard, Ilya Mechnikov is called the "father of gerontology" (although, as often happens in science, the same title is sometimes applied to multiple people who contributed to aging research).

Supporters of life extension celebrate 15 May as Metchnikoff Day and use it as a memorable date for organizing activities.

## Early life, family and education

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Leo_Tolstoy_%26_Ilya_Mechnikov.jpg/250px-Leo_Tolstoy_%26_Ilya_Mechnikov.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Leo_Tolstoy_&_Ilya_Mechnikov.jpg)

[Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy "Leo Tolstoy") (left) and Metchnikoff

Metchnikoff was born in the village of [Ivanovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivanivka,_Dvorichna_rural_hromada?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ivanivka, Dvorichna rural hromada"), [Kharkov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkov_Governorate "Kharkov Governorate"), in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire "Russian Empire"), now located in [Kupiansk Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kupiansk_Raion "Kupiansk Raion"), [Kharkiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_Oblast "Kharkiv Oblast") in Ukraine. He was the youngest of five children of Ilya Ivanovich Mechnikov, an officer of the [Imperial Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Guard_(Russia) "Imperial Guard (Russia)"). His mother, Emilia Lvovna (Nevakhovich), the daughter of the writer [Leo Nevakhovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Löb_Nevakhovich "Löb Nevakhovich"), largely influenced him on his education, especially in science. The Nevakhovich family was Jewish.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/COA_Mechnykovy.png/250px-COA_Mechnykovy.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:COA_Mechnykovy.png)

Coat of Arms of the Mechnikov family

The family name Mechnikov is a translation from Romanian, since his father was a descendant of the Chancellor [Yuri Stefanovi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Stefanovich_Spătarul?action=edit&redlink=1 "Yuri Stefanovich Spătarul"), the grandson of [Nicolae Milescu Spătarul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Spathari "Nikolai Spathari"). Yuri Stefanovich immigrated to Russia together with [Dimitrie Cantemir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrie_Cantemir "Dimitrie Cantemir") in 1711 after the unsuccessful campaign of [Peter I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great "Peter the Great") on the [Danubian Principalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danubian_Principalities "Danubian Principalities"). For two and a half centuries, the Mechnikov family lived in St. Petersburg, where it became connected by family ties with many Russian princely families. The word "mech" is a Russian translation of the Romanian "spadă" (sword), which originated with [Spătar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_Romanian_ranks_and_titles "Historical Romanian ranks and titles") (Sword-bearer). His elder brother [Lev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Metchnikoff "Lev Metchnikoff") became a prominent geographer and sociologist.

In 1856, Metchnikoff entered the Kharkov Lycée, where he developed his interest in biology. Convinced by his mother to study [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences "Natural sciences") instead of medicine, he tried in 1862 to study biology at the [University of Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Würzburg "University of Würzburg"), but the German academic session would not start by the end of the year. Metchnikoff thus enrolled at [Kharkov Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Kharkiv "National University of Kharkiv") for [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences "Natural sciences"), completing his four-year degree in two years.

In 1864, he traveled to Germany to study marine [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_(animals) "Fauna (animals)") on the small [North Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea "North Sea") island of [Heligoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heligoland "Heligoland"). He was advised by the botanist [Ferdinand Cohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Cohn "Ferdinand Cohn") to work with [Rudolf Leuckart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Leuckart "Rudolf Leuckart") at the [University of Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen "University of Giessen"). It was in Leuckart's laboratory that he made his first scientific discovery of [alternation of generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternation_of_generations "Alternation of generations") (sexual and asexual) in [nematodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nematodes "Nematodes") ([Chaetosomatida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaetosomatida "Chaetosomatida")) and then at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München"). In 1865, while at Giessen, he discovered intracellular digestion in [flatworm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trematoda "Trematoda"), and this study influenced his later works. Moving to [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples "Naples") the next year he worked on a doctoral thesis on the embryonic development of the cuttle-fish *[Sepiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepiola "Sepiola")* and the crustacean *[Nebalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebalia "Nebalia")*. A [cholera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholera "Cholera") epidemic in the autumn of 1865 made him move to the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen"), where he worked briefly with W. M. Keferstein and [Jakob Henle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Henle "Jakob Henle").

In 1867, he returned to Russia to receive his doctorate with [Alexander Kovalevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Kovalevsky "Alexander Kovalevsky") from the [University of Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University "Saint Petersburg State University"). Together they won the Karl Ernst von Baer prize for their theses on the development of [germ layers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_layer "Germ layer") in [invertebrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate "Invertebrate") embryos.

## Career and achievements

Metchnikoff was appointed [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent "Docent") at the newly established [Imperial Novorossiya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa_University "Odessa University") (now Odesa University). Only twenty-two years of age, he was younger than his students. After being involved in a conflict with a senior colleague over attending scientific meetings, he transferred to the University of Saint Petersburg in 1868, where he experienced a worse professional environment. In 1870 he returned to [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa "Odessa") to take up the appointment of Titular Professor of [Zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology "Zoology") and [Comparative Anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_Anatomy "Comparative Anatomy").

In 1882 he resigned from Odessa University due to political turmoil after the [assassination of Alexander II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Alexander_II_of_Russia "Assassination of Alexander II of Russia"). He went to [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily "Sicily") to set up his private laboratory in [Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messina "Messina"). He returned to Odessa as director of an institute set up to carry out [Louis Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Pasteur "Louis Pasteur")'s [vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccine "Vaccine") against [rabies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabies "Rabies"); due to some difficulties, he left in 1888 and went to Paris to seek Pasteur's advice. Pasteur gave him an appointment at the [Pasteur Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasteur_Institute "Pasteur Institute"), where he remained for the rest of his life.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Professeur_Metchnikoff%2C_portrait_du_scientifique_dans_un_laboratoire_de_recherche.jpg/250px-Professeur_Metchnikoff%2C_portrait_du_scientifique_dans_un_laboratoire_de_recherche.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Professeur_Metchnikoff,_portrait_du_scientifique_dans_un_laboratoire_de_recherche.jpg)

Metchnikoff in his laboratory, 1913

Metchnikoff became interested in the study of [microbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbes "Microbes"), and especially the [immune system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system "Immune system"). At Messina he discovered [phagocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagocytosis "Phagocytosis") after experimenting on the [larvae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larva "Larva") of [starfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starfish "Starfish"). In 1882 he first demonstrated the process when he inserted small [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus "Citrus") thorns into starfish larvae, then found unusual cells surrounding the thorns. He realized that in animals which have blood, the white blood cells gather at the site of inflammation, and he hypothesised that this could be the process by which bacteria were attacked and killed by the white blood cells. He discussed his hypothesis with [Carl Friedrich Wilhelm Claus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_Wilhelm_Claus "Carl Friedrich Wilhelm Claus"), Professor of Zoology at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna "University of Vienna"), who suggested to him the term "phagocyte" for a cell which can surround and kill pathogens. He delivered his findings at Odessa University in 1883.

His theory, that certain [white blood cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_blood_cell "White blood cell") could engulf and destroy harmful bodies such as bacteria, met with scepticism from leading specialists including Louis Pasteur, [Emil von Behring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_von_Behring "Emil von Behring"), and others. At the time, most bacteriologists believed that white blood cells ingested pathogens and then spread them further through the body. His major supporter was [Rudolf Virchow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Virchow "Rudolf Virchow"), who published his research in his *Archiv für pathologische Anatomie und Physiologie und für klinische Medicin* (now called the *[Virchows Archiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virchows_Archiv "Virchows Archiv")*). His discovery of these [phagocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagocytes "Phagocytes") ultimately won him the Nobel Prize in 1908. He worked with [Émile Roux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Paul_Emile_Roux "Pierre Paul Emile Roux") on [calomel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calomel "Calomel") (mercurous chloride) in ointment form in an attempt to prevent people from contracting the [sexually transmitted disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexually_transmitted_disease "Sexually transmitted disease") [syphilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syphilis "Syphilis").

In 1887, he observed that [leukocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukocytes "Leukocytes") isolated from the blood of various animals were attracted towards certain bacteria. The first studies of leukocyte killing in the presence of specific [antiserum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiserum "Antiserum") were performed by Joseph Denys and Joseph Leclef, followed by Leon Marchand and Mennes between 1895 and 1898. [Almroth E. Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almroth_Wright "Almroth Wright") was the first to quantify this phenomenon and strongly advocated its potential therapeutic importance. The so-called resolution of the humoralist and cellularist positions by showing their respective roles in the setting of enhanced killing in the presence of [opsonins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opsonin "Opsonin") was popularized by Wright after 1903, although Metchnikoff acknowledged the stimulatory capacity of immunosensitized serum on phagotic function in the case of acquired immunity.

This attraction was soon proposed to be due to soluble elements released by the bacteria (see Harris for a review of this area up to 1953). Some 85 years after this seminal observation, laboratory studies showed that these elements were low [molecular weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_weight "Molecular weight") (between 150 and 1500 [Dalton (unit)s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_(unit) "Dalton (unit)")) N-formylated oligopeptides, including the most prominent member of this group, [N-formylmethionine-leucyl-phenylalanine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-Formylmethionine-leucyl-phenylalanine "N-Formylmethionine-leucyl-phenylalanine"), that are made by a variety of replicating [gram positive bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_positive_bacteria "Gram positive bacteria") and [gram negative bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_negative_bacteria "Gram negative bacteria"). Metchnikoff's early observation, then, was the foundation for studies that defined a critical mechanism by which bacteria attract leukocytes to initiate and direct the [innate immune response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innate_immune_response "Innate immune response") of acute [inflammation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflammation "Inflammation") to sites of host invasion by [pathogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathogens "Pathogens").

Metchnikoff discovered fungal infections causing insect death in 1879 and became involved in the biological control of insect pests through his student [Isaak Krasilschik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaak_Krasilschik "Isaak Krasilschik"). They were able to make use of [green muscardine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_muscardine_disease "Green muscardine disease") for control of insects in agricultural fields.

Metchnikoff also self-experimented with cholera that initially supported the [probiotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probiotic "Probiotic") notion. During the [1892 cholera epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1881–1896_cholera_pandemic "1881–1896 cholera pandemic") in France, he was surprised by the fact that the disease affected only some people but not others when they were equally exposed to the infection. To understand the differences in susceptibility to the disease, he drank a sample of cholera but never got sick. He tested on two volunteers of which one was not affected while the other almost died. He hypothesised that the difference in cholera infection was due to differences in intestinal microbes, speculating that those who have plenty of beneficial ones would be healthier.

The issues of aging occupied a significant place in Metchnikoff's works. Metchnikoff developed a theory that [aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senescence "Senescence") is caused by toxic bacteria in the gut and that [lactic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactic_acid "Lactic acid") could prolong life. He attributed the longevity of Bulgarian peasants to their yogurt consumption that contained what was called the Bulgarian bacteria (now called [*Lactobacillus delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactobacillus_delbrueckii_subsp._bulgaricus "Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus")). To validate his theory, he drank [sour milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogurt "Yogurt") every day throughout his life. His scientific reasonings on the subject were written in his books *The Nature of Man: Studies in Optimistic Philosophy* (1903) and more expressively in *The Prolongation of Life: Optimistic Studies* (1907). He also espoused the potential life-lengthening properties of lactic acid bacteria such as *Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus*. This concept of probiotics, which he termed "orthobiosis," was influential in his lifetime, but became ignored until the mid-1990s when experimental evidence emerged.

## Awards and recognitions

Metchnikoff won the Karl Ernst von Baer prize in 1867 with Alexander Kovalevsky on the basis of their doctoral research. He shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1908 with Paul Ehrlich. He was awarded an honorary degree by the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") in Cambridge, UK, and the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal "Copley Medal") of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") in 1906. He was given honorary memberships of the Academy of Medicine in Paris and of the Academy of Sciences and Medicine in Saint Petersburg.

## Personal life and views

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/I._Mechnikov_by_Nik._Dm._Kuznetsov_%28GIM%29.jpg/250px-I._Mechnikov_by_Nik._Dm._Kuznetsov_%28GIM%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:I._Mechnikov_by_Nik._Dm._Kuznetsov_(GIM).jpg)

[Nikolai Kuznetsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Dmitriyevich_Kuznetsov_(painter) "Nikolai Dmitriyevich Kuznetsov (painter)"), *Élie Metchnikoff*, 1886

Metchnikoff married his first wife, Ludmila Feodorovitch, in 1869. She died from [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis") on 20 April 1873. Her death, combined with other problems, caused Metchnikoff to [attempt suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_attempt "Suicide attempt"), taking a large dose of [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium "Opium"). In 1875, he married his student Olga Belokopytova. In 1880 Olga suffered from severe [typhoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoid "Typhoid") and this led to his second suicide attempt. He injected himself with the [spirochete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirochete "Spirochete") of [relapsing fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relapsing_fever "Relapsing fever"). (Olga died in 1944 in Paris from typhoid.)

Despite being baptized in the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church "Russian Orthodox Church"), Metchnikoff was an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism "Atheism").

He was greatly influenced by [Charles Darwin's theory of evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwinism "Darwinism"). He first read [Fritz Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Müller "Fritz Müller")'s *Für Darwin* (*For Darwin*) in Giessen. From this he became a supporter of natural selection and [Ernst Haeckel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Haeckel "Ernst Haeckel")'s [biogenetic law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recapitulation_theory "Recapitulation theory"). His scientific works and theories were inspired by Darwinism, although he criticised Darwin for what he saw as his blind acceptance of the ideas of [Thomas Malthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Malthus "Thomas Malthus") about [overpopulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overpopulation "Overpopulation").

Metchnikoff died in 1916 in Paris from heart failure. According to his will, his body was used for [medical research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_research "Medical research") and afterwards [cremated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremated "Cremated") in [Père Lachaise Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Père_Lachaise_Cemetery "Père Lachaise Cemetery") crematorium. His cinerary urn has been placed in the [Pasteur Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasteur_Institute "Pasteur Institute") library.
