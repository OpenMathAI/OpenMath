**Feodor Felix Konrad Lynen** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") (German pronunciation: [[ˈfeːodoːɐ̯ ˈlyːnən]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Feodor_Lynen.ogg "File:De-Feodor Lynen.ogg"); 6 April 1911 – 6 August 1979) was a German [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). In 1964, he won the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") together with [Konrad Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Emil_Bloch "Konrad Emil Bloch") for their discoveries concerning the mechanism and regulation of [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol") and [fatty acid metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatty_acid_metabolism "Fatty acid metabolism") while he was director of the Max-Planck Institute for Cellular Chemistry in Munich.

## Biography

Feodor Lynen was born in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich") on 6 April 1911. His parents were Wilhelm Lynen, who taught mechanical engineering, and Frieda née Prym, whose father was an industrialist. He started his studies at the chemistry department of the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München") (LMU) in 1930 and graduated in March 1937 under [Heinrich Wieland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Otto_Wieland "Heinrich Otto Wieland") with the work: "On the Toxic Substances in Amanita". Lynen remained in Germany throughout World War II. In 1942, he became a chemistry lecturer at LMU. In 1947, he became an assistant professor and in 1953 a professor of biochemistry. From 1954 onwards, he was director of the Max-Planck Institute for Cellular Chemistry in Munich, a position which was created for him at the instigation of two senior scientists, [Otto Warburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Heinrich_Warburg "Otto Heinrich Warburg") and [Otto Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn "Otto Hahn"). In 1972, that institute was merged into the newly founded [Max-Planck Institute of Biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_of_Biochemistry "Max Planck Institute of Biochemistry") in 1972. Also in 1972, Lynen was named President of the [Gesellschaft Deutscher Chemiker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesellschaft_Deutscher_Chemiker "Gesellschaft Deutscher Chemiker") (GDCh).

In 1964, he won the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") together with [Konrad Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Emil_Bloch "Konrad Emil Bloch") for their discoveries concerning the mechanism and regulation of [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol") and [fatty acid metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatty_acid_metabolism "Fatty acid metabolism"). These discoveries took many years to work out. The Nobel Committee felt that this was important because understanding the metabolism of sterols and fatty acids could reveal how cholesterol affects heart disease and stroke. His Nobel Lecture on 11 December 1964 was 'The pathway from "activated acetic acid" to the terpenes and fatty acids'.

Working mostly separately, Lynen and Bloch both discovered the steps that created squalene and turned the squalene into cholesterol. Initially, Lynen found that acetate activated by coenzyme A was required to start the process. He discovered the chemical structure of acetyl-coenzyme A, which was required for a detailed understanding of the biochemical pathways. He also learned that [biotin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotin "Biotin"), or Vitamin B7, was required in the process.

On 14 May 1937, Lynen married Eva Wieland (1915–2002), daughter of his academic teacher. They had five children between 1938 and 1946. Feodor Lynen died in Munich, Germany, on 6 August 1979, six weeks after an operation for an aneurysm.

## Fellowship

The [Alexander von Humboldt Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation "Alexander von Humboldt Foundation") has a fellowship named in his honor.

## Honours and awards

- 1962: elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences")
- 1962: elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences")
- 1963: [Otto Warburg Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Warburg_Medal "Otto Warburg Medal") from the German Society for Biochemistry and Molecular Biology
- 1964: [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") (with [Konrad Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Bloch "Konrad Bloch")) "for their discoveries concerning the mechanism and regulation of the metabolism of cholesterol and fatty acids"
- 1965: [Grand Cross of Merit with Star and Sash of the Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Cross_of_Merit_with_Star_and_Sash_of_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany "Grand Cross of Merit with Star and Sash of the Federal Republic of Germany")
- 1966: elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society")
- 1967: Norman Medal of the German Society for Fat Research
- 1971: [Pour le Mérite for Science and Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pour_le_Mérite_for_Science_and_Art "Pour le Mérite for Science and Art")
- 1972: [Austrian Decoration for Science and Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Decoration_for_Science_and_Art "Austrian Decoration for Science and Art")
