**Fritz Albert Lipmann** (German pronunciation: [[fʁɪts ˈʔalbɛʁt ˈlɪpman]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Fritz_Albert_Lipmann.ogg "File:De-Fritz Albert Lipmann.ogg"); June 12, 1899 – July 24, 1986) was a German-American [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist") and a co-discoverer in 1945 of [coenzyme A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coenzyme_A "Coenzyme A"). For this, together with other research on coenzyme A, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1953 (shared with [Hans Adolf Krebs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Adolf_Krebs "Hans Adolf Krebs")).

## Early life and education

Lipmann was born in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg "Königsberg"), Germany, to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") family. His parents were Gertrud (Lachmanski) and Leopold Lipmann, an attorney.

Lipmann studied medicine at the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg "University of Königsberg"), the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin "Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin"), and the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München "Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München"), graduating at the Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin in 1924. He returned to the University of Königsberg to study chemistry under Professor [Hans Meerwein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Meerwein "Hans Meerwein"). In 1926, he joined [Otto Meyerhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Meyerhof "Otto Meyerhof") at the [Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Wilhelm_Institute_for_Biology "Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Biology"), [Dahlem, Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlem_(Berlin) "Dahlem (Berlin)"), for his doctoral thesis. After that he followed Meyerhof to [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg "Heidelberg") to the [Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Medical_Research "Max Planck Institute for Medical Research").

## Career

From 1939 on, Lipmann lived and worked in the United States. He was a Research Associate in the Department of Biochemistry, [Cornell University Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University_Medical_College "Cornell University Medical College"), New York from 1939 to 1941. He joined the research staff of the [Massachusetts General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_General_Hospital "Massachusetts General Hospital") in Boston in 1941, first as a Research Associate in the Department of Surgery, then heading his own group in the Biochemical Research Laboratory of the hospital. From 1949 to 1957 he was professor of [biological chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") at [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School "Harvard Medical School"). From 1957 onwards, he taught and conducted research at [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University "Rockefeller University"), [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York,_New_York "New York, New York").

In 1953, Lipmann received one half of the Nobel Prize in Physiology and Medicine "for his discovery of co-enzyme A and its importance for intermediary metabolism." The other half of the award was won by [Hans Adolf Krebs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Adolf_Krebs "Hans Adolf Krebs"). Lipmann was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in 1966. He would try to dive further into his discovery by finding a variant of co-enzyme A, now known as [Pantethine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantethine "Pantethine"). He was an elected member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences"), the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences"), and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society").

Lipmann introduced the specific squiggle designation (~) to indicate high energy-rich phosphate in energy-rich biomolecules like [ATP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenosine_triphosphate "Adenosine triphosphate") in his essay "Metabolic Generation and Utilization of Phosphate Bond Energy." Of his work, he said "that in the field of biosynthesis we have a rare example of progress leading to simplification."

## Personal life

Lipmann with Mary Soames in Stockholm in 1953

In 1931, Lipmann married Elfreda M. Hall. They had one son. Lipmann died in New York in 1986. The photo shows [Mary Soames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Soames "Mary Soames"), not Elfreda Hall Lipmann His widow died in 2008 at the age of 101.
