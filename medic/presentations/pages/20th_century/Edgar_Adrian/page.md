**Edgar Douglas Adrian, 1st Baron Adrian** [OM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_Merit "Member of the Order of Merit") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (30 November 1889 – 4 August 1977) was an English [electrophysiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrophysiology "Electrophysiology") and recipient of the 1932 [Nobel Prize for Physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine"), won jointly with [Sir Charles Sherrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Scott_Sherrington "Charles Scott Sherrington") for work on the function of [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron "Neuron"). He provided experimental evidence for the [all-or-none law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-or-none_law "All-or-none law") of nerves.

## Biography

Adrian was born in [Hampstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead "Hampstead"), London, the youngest son of [Alfred Douglas Adrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Douglas_Adrian "Alfred Douglas Adrian"), legal adviser to the [Local Government Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Board "Local Government Board"), and Flora Lavinia Barton.

He was educated at [Westminster School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_School "Westminster School") and studied Natural Sciences at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"), graduating in 1911. In 1913 he was elected to a fellowship of Trinity College on account of his research into the "all or none" law of nerves.

After completing a medical degree (MB BCh) in 1915, he undertook clinical work at [St Bartholomew's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Bartholomew's_Hospital "St Bartholomew's Hospital") London during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), treating soldiers with nerve damage and nervous disorders such as [shell shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_shock "Shell shock"). Adrian returned to Cambridge as a lecturer gaining his doctorate (MD) in 1919 and in 1925 began research on the human sensory organs by electrical methods.

### Career

Continuing earlier studies of [Keith Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Lucas_(scientist) "Keith Lucas (scientist)"), he used a [capillary electrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_electrometer "Capillary electrometer") and [cathode-ray tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode-ray_tube "Cathode-ray tube") to amplify the signals produced by the [nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous_system "Nervous system") and was able to record the electrical discharge of single nerve fibres under physical stimulus. (It seems he used frogs in his experiments) An accidental discovery by Adrian in 1928 proved the presence of electricity within nerve cells. Adrian said,

> I had arranged electrodes on the optic nerve of a toad in connection with some experiments on the retina. The room was nearly dark and I was puzzled to hear repeated noises in the loudspeaker attached to the amplifier, noises indicating that a great deal of impulse activity was going on. It was not until I compared the noises with my own movements around the room that I realised I was in the field of vision of the toad's eye and that it was signalling what I was doing.

A key result, published in 1928, stated that the excitation of the skin under constant stimulus is initially strong but gradually decreases over time, whereas the sensory impulses passing along the nerves from the point of contact are constant in strength, yet are reduced in frequency over time, and the sensation in the brain diminishes as a result.

Extending these results to the study of pain causes by the stimulus of the nervous system, he made discoveries about the reception of such signals in the brain and spatial distribution of the sensory areas of the [cerebral cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex") in different animals. These conclusions lead to the idea of a sensory map, called the [homunculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homunculus "Homunculus"), in the [somatosensory system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatosensory_system "Somatosensory system").

Later, Adrian used the [electroencephalogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroencephalogram "Electroencephalogram") to study the electrical activity of the [brain in humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_brain "Human brain"). His work on the abnormalities of the [Berger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Berger "Hans Berger") rhythm paved the way for subsequent investigation in [epilepsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epilepsy "Epilepsy") and other cerebral pathologies. He spent the last portion of his research career investigating [olfaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfaction "Olfaction").

Positions that he held during his career included Foulerton Professor 1929–1937; Professor of Physiology in the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") 1937–1951; [President of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents_of_the_Royal_Society "Presidents of the Royal Society") 1950–1955; Master of [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge"), 1951–1965; president of the [Royal Society of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Medicine "Royal Society of Medicine") 1960–1962; [Chancellor of the University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chancellors_of_the_University_of_Cambridge "List of Chancellors of the University of Cambridge") 1967–1975; Chancellor of the [University of Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leicester "University of Leicester") 1957–1971. Adrian was elected a Foreign Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1938. He was elected an International Member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "National Academy of Sciences") in 1941. In 1946 he became foreign member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences"). In 1942 he was awarded the [Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit "Order of Merit") and in the [1955 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_New_Year_Honours "1955 New Year Honours") was created [Baron Adrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron_Adrian "Baron Adrian") of Cambridge in the County of Cambridge.

## Family

On 14 June 1923 Edgar Adrian married [Hester Agnes Pinsent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hester_Adrian "Hester Adrian"), who was the daughter of [Ellen Pinsent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Pinsent "Ellen Pinsent") and sister of [David Pinsent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Pinsent "David Pinsent").
Together they had three children, first a daughter and then twins:

- Anne Pinsent Adrian, who married the physiologist [Richard Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Keynes "Richard Keynes"), who was the great-grandson of [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin"). The couple had four children.
  - Adrian Keynes (1946–1974)
  - [Randal Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randal_Keynes "Randal Keynes") (\*1948)
  - [Roger Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Keynes "Roger Keynes") (\*1951)
  - [Simon Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Keynes "Simon Keynes") (\*1952)
- [Richard Adrian, 2nd Baron Adrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Adrian,_2nd_Baron_Adrian "Richard Adrian, 2nd Baron Adrian") (1927–1995)
  - The couple had no children.
- Jennet Adrian (born 1927), who married Peter Watson Campbell
  - Further family details unknown.
