**Allan MacLeod Cormack** (February 23, 1924 – May 7, 1998) was a South African and American [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "Physicist"), academic, and [Nobel Laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Laureate "Nobel Laureate"). He was Professor of Physics at [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University "Tufts University") and won the 1979 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") (along with [Godfrey Hounsfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godfrey_Hounsfield "Godfrey Hounsfield")) for his work on [X-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray "X-ray") [computed tomography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computed_tomography "Computed tomography") (CT), a significant and unusual achievement since Cormack did not hold a doctoral degree in any scientific field.

## Early life and education

Cormack was born on February 23, 1924, in [Johannesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg "Johannesburg"), South Africa. He attended [Rondebosch Boys' High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rondebosch_Boys'_High_School "Rondebosch Boys' High School") in [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town "Cape Town"), where he was active in the debating and tennis teams. He received his B.Sc. in [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") in 1944 from the [University of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town "University of Cape Town") and his M.Sc. in [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography "Crystallography") in 1945 from the same institution. He was a doctoral student at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") from 1947 to 1949, and while at Cambridge he met his future wife, Barbara Seavey, an American physics student.

## Career

After marrying Seavey, he returned to the University of Cape Town in early 1950 to lecture. Following a sabbatical at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard "Harvard") in 1956–57, the couple agreed to move to the United States, and Cormack became a professor at [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University "Tufts University") in the fall of 1957. Cormack became a [naturalized citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen "Naturalized citizen") of the United States in 1966. Although he was mainly working on [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics "Particle physics"), Cormack's side interest in x-ray technology led him to develop the theoretical underpinnings of CT scanning. This work was initiated at the University of Cape Town and [Groote Schuur Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groote_Schuur_Hospital "Groote Schuur Hospital") in early 1956 and continued briefly in mid-1957 after returning from his sabbatical. His results were subsequently published in two papers in the Journal of Applied Physics in 1963 and 1964. These papers generated little interest until [Hounsfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godfrey_Hounsfield "Godfrey Hounsfield") and colleagues built the first CT scanner in 1971 in the UK, taking Cormack's theoretical calculations into a real application. For their independent efforts, Cormack and Hounsfield shared the 1979 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. It is notable that the two built a very similar type of device without collaboration in different parts of the world. He was member of the [International Academy of Science, Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_Science,_Munich?action=edit&redlink=1 "International Academy of Science, Munich"). In 1990, he was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science").

## Death

Cormack died of cancer in [Winchester, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester,_Massachusetts "Winchester, Massachusetts"), at age 74. He was posthumously awarded the [Order of Mapungubwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Mapungubwe "Order of Mapungubwe") on December 10, 2002, for outstanding achievements as a scientist and for co-inventing the CT scanner.
