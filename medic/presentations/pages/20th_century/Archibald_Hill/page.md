**Archibald Vivian Hill** (26 September 1886 – 3 June 1977), better known to friends and colleagues as **A. V. Hill**, was a British [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiologist "Physiologist"), one of the founders of the diverse disciplines of [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics") and [operations research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_research "Operations research"). He shared the 1922 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for his elucidation of the production of heat and [mechanical work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_work "Mechanical work") in [muscles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle "Muscle").

## Biography

### Early life

Born in [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol "Bristol"), the son of Jonathan Hill (1857–1924) and Ada Priscilla (née Rumney) (1861–1943), he was preceded on the paternal side by five generations of timber merchants at Bristol, carrying on the business which had been founded by James Hill in 1750.

He was educated at [Blundell's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blundell's_School "Blundell's School") and graduated from [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge") as third [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_(University_of_Cambridge) "Wrangler (University of Cambridge)") in the mathematics [tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripos "Tripos") before turning to [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology"). While still an undergraduate at Trinity College, he derived in 1909 what came to be known as the Langmuir equation.

This is closely related to [Michaelis–Menten kinetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michaelis–Menten_kinetics "Michaelis–Menten kinetics"). In this paper, Hill's first publication, he derived both the equilibrium form of the Langmuir equation, and also the exponential approach to equilibrium. The paper, written under the supervision of [John Newport Langley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Newport_Langley "John Newport Langley"), is a landmark in the history of [receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_(biochemistry) "Receptor (biochemistry)") theory, because the context for the derivation was the binding of nicotine and curare to the "receptive substance" at the [neuromuscular junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromuscular_junction "Neuromuscular junction").

### Family

In 1913, he married [Margaret Neville Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Hill_(social_reformer) "Margaret Hill (social reformer)") (1885-1974), daughter of the economist [John Neville Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Neville_Keynes "John Neville Keynes"), and sister of the economist [John Maynard Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Keynes "John Maynard Keynes") and the surgeon [Geoffrey Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Keynes "Geoffrey Keynes"). They had two sons and two daughters:

- [Polly Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polly_Hill_(economist) "Polly Hill (economist)") (1914–2005), economist, married K.A.C. Humphreys, registrar of the West African Examinations Council.
- [David Keynes Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Keynes_Hill "David Keynes Hill") (1915–2002), physiologist, married Stella Mary Humphrey
- [Maurice Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Hill_(geophysicist) "Maurice Hill (geophysicist)") (1919–1966), oceanographer, married Philippa Pass
- Janet Hill (1918–2000) child psychiatrist, married the immunologist [John Herbert Humphrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Herbert_Humphrey "John Herbert Humphrey").

### World War I service

While a student, he enrolled in the [Officers Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officers_Training_Corps "Officers Training Corps") as a [crack shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_shot "Crack shot").
In 1914, at the outbreak of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War "First World War"), Hill became the musketry officer of the [Cambridgeshire Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire_Regiment "Cambridgeshire Regiment").

At the end of 1915, while home on leave he was asked by [Horace Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Darwin "Horace Darwin") from the [Ministry of Munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Munitions "Ministry of Munitions") to come for a day to advise them on how to train anti-aircraft gunners. On site, Hill immediately proposed a simple two mirror method to determine airplanes' heights. Transferred to Munitions, he realized that the mirrors could measure where smoke shells burst and if he fitted this data with the [equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistics "Ballistics") describing a shell's flight they could provide accurate range tables for anti-aircraft guns. To measure and compute he assembled the [Anti-Aircraft Experimental Section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Aircraft_Experimental_Section "Anti-Aircraft Experimental Section"), a team of men too old for conscription, [Ralph H. Fowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_H._Fowler "Ralph H. Fowler") (a wounded officer), and lads too young for service including [Douglas Hartree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Hartree "Douglas Hartree"), [Arthur Milne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Milne "Arthur Milne") and [James Crowther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Crowther "James Crowther"). Someone dubbed his motley group "Hill's Brigands", which they proudly adopted. Later in the war they also worked on locating enemy planes from their sound. He sped between their working sites on his beloved motorcycle. At the end of the war, Major Hill issued certificates to more than one hundred Brigands. He was appointed an [Officer of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire "Officer of the Order of the British Empire") (OBE).

### Muscle physiology work

Hill made many exacting measurements of the heat released when skeletal muscles contract and relax. A key finding was that heat is produced during contraction, which requires investment of chemical energy, but not during relaxation, which is passive. His earliest measurements used equipment left behind by the Swedish physiologist [Magnus Blix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Blix "Magnus Blix"), Hill measured a temperature rise of only 0.003 °C. After publication he learned that German physiologists had already reported on heat and [muscle contraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle_contraction "Muscle contraction") and he went to Germany to learn more about their work. He continually improved his apparatus to make it more sensitive and to reduce the time lag between the heat released by the preparation and its recording by his [thermocouple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermocouple "Thermocouple").

Hill is regarded, along with [Hermann Helmholtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Helmholtz "Hermann Helmholtz"), as one of the founders of [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics").

Hill returned briefly to Cambridge in 1919 before taking the chair in physiology at the [Victoria University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester "Victoria University of Manchester") in 1920 in succession to [William Stirling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Stirling_(physiologist) "William Stirling (physiologist)"). Using himself as the subject — he ran every morning from 7:15 to 10:30 — he showed that running a dash relies on energy stores which afterwards are replenished by [increased oxygen consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excess_post-exercise_oxygen_consumption "Excess post-exercise oxygen consumption"). Paralleling the work of German [Otto Fritz Meyerhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Fritz_Meyerhof "Otto Fritz Meyerhof"), Hill elucidated the processes whereby mechanical work is produced in muscles. The two shared the 1922 Nobel Prize in Physiology and Medicine for this work. Hill introduced the concepts of [maximal oxygen uptake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VO2_max "VO2 max") and oxygen debt in 1922.

In 1923, he succeeded [Ernest Starling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Starling "Ernest Starling") as professor of physiology at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London "University College London"), a few years later becoming a Royal Society Research professor there, where he remained until retirement in 1951. In 1933, he became with [William Beveridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Beveridge "William Beveridge") and [Lord Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Rutherford "Lord Rutherford") a founder member and vice-president of the [Academic Assistance Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_Assisting_Refugee_Academics "Council for Assisting Refugee Academics") (which in 1936 became the Society for the Protection of Science and Learning). By the start of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War "Second World War"), the organisation had saved 900 academics (18 of whom went on to win Nobel Prizes) from [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi "Nazi") persecution. He prominently displayed in his laboratory a toy figure of Adolf Hitler with saluting arm upraised, which he explained was in gratitude for all the scientists Germany had expelled, some of whom were now working with him. Hill believed that "Laughter is the best detergent for nonsense".

### World War II service

In 1935, he served with [Patrick Blackett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Blackett "Patrick Blackett") and Sir [Henry Tizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Tizard "Henry Tizard") on the committee that gave birth to [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar "Radar"). He was also biological secretary of the Royal Society; [William Henry Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Bragg "William Henry Bragg") was president. Both had been frustrated by the delay in putting scientists to work in the previous war. The Royal Society collated a list of scientists and Hill represented the Society at the Ministry of Labour. When the war came Hill led a campaign to liberate refugee scientists who had been interned. He served as an [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_(politician) "Independent (politician)") Member of Parliament (MP) for [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_(UK_Parliament_constituency) "Cambridge University (UK Parliament constituency)") from 1940 to 1945. In 1940, he was posted to the British Embassy in Washington to promote war research in the still neutral United States. There he was authorised to swap secrets with U.S. officials and persuaded the British to show the Americans everything they were working on (except for the atomic bomb). The mobilization of Allied scientists was one of the major successes in the war.

He visited India between November 1943 and April 1944 to survey scientific and technological research. His suggestions influenced the establishment of the [Indian Institutes of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institutes_of_Technology "Indian Institutes of Technology") (IITs) in the following decade.

### Later life

After the war he rebuilt his laboratory at University College and vigorously carried on research. In 1951, his advocacy was rewarded by the establishment of a Biophysics Department under his leadership.

In 1952, he became head of the [British Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "British Association for the Advancement of Science") and Secretary General of the [International Council of Scientific Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Council_of_Scientific_Unions "International Council of Scientific Unions"). He was President of the [Marine Biological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Biological_Association_of_the_United_Kingdom "Marine Biological Association of the United Kingdom") from 1955 to 1960. In 1967, he retired to Cambridge where he gradually lost the use of his legs. He died "held in the greatest affection by more than a hundred scientific descendants all over the world".

## Blue plaque

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/A._V._HILL_1886%E2%80%931977_Physiologist_lived_here_1923_%E2%80%93_1967.jpg/250px-A._V._HILL_1886%E2%80%931977_Physiologist_lived_here_1923_%E2%80%93_1967.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:A._V._HILL_1886–1977_Physiologist_lived_here_1923_–_1967.jpg)

Blue plaque at 16 Bishopswood Road, Highgate

On 9 September 2015, an [English Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Heritage "English Heritage") [Blue plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_plaque "Blue plaque") was erected at Hill's former home, 16 Bishopswood Road, [Highgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highgate "Highgate"), where he had lived from 1923 to 1967.
Since then the house had been divided into flats and owned by [Highgate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highgate_School "Highgate School"), where Hill was a Governor from 1929 to 1960.
It has now been sold, redeveloped and renamed as Hurstbourne.
In Hill's time, according to his grandson [Nicholas Humphrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Humphrey "Nicholas Humphrey"), regular guests at the house included 18 exiled Nobel laureates, his brother-in-law, the economist [John Maynard Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Keynes "John Maynard Keynes"), and also friends as diverse and unexpected as [Sigmund Freud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmund_Freud "Sigmund Freud") and [Stephen Hawking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking "Stephen Hawking"), he met during the course of his long and extraordinary life.

Sir [Ralph Kohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Kohn "Ralph Kohn") [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") who proposed the Blue plaque, said: "The Nobel Prize winner A. V. Hill contributed vastly to our understanding of muscle physiology.
His work has resulted in wide-ranging application in sports medicine.
As an outstanding Humanitarian and Parliamentarian, he was uncompromising in his condemnation of the Nazi regime for its persecution of scientists and others.
A. V. Hill played a crucial role in assisting and rescuing many refugees to continue their work in this country".
