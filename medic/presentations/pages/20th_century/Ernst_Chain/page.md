**Sir Ernst Boris Chain** (19 June 1906 – 12 August 1979)
was a German-born British biochemist. He shared the 1945 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Alexander Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Fleming "Alexander Fleming") and [Howard Florey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Florey "Howard Florey") "for the discovery of [penicillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicillin "Penicillin") and its curative effect in various [infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease "Infectious disease")".

## Life and career

Ernst Chain in his laboratory.

Chain was born to a [German-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-Jewish "German-Jewish") family in Berlin, the son of Margarete ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Eisner) and Michael Chain, a chemist and industrialist dealing in chemical products. His father was a [Russian-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-Jewish "Russian-Jewish") émigré from [Mogilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogilev "Mogilev") (Russian Empire, now Belarus) to study chemistry abroad and his mother was from Berlin. In 1930, he received his degree in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") from [Friedrich Wilhelm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin"). He was a lifelong friend of Professor [Albert Neuberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Neuberger "Albert Neuberger"), whom he met in Berlin in the 1930s.

After the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism") came to power, Chain understood that, being Jewish, he would no longer be safe in Germany. He left Germany and moved to England, arriving on 2 April 1933 with £10 in his pocket. Geneticist and physiologist [J. B. S. Haldane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._S._Haldane "J. B. S. Haldane") helped him obtain a position at [University College Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Hospital "University College Hospital"), London.

After a couple of months he was accepted as a PhD student at [Fitzwilliam College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitzwilliam_College,_Cambridge "Fitzwilliam College, Cambridge"), where he began working on [phospholipids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phospholipid "Phospholipid") under the direction of Sir [Frederick Gowland Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Gowland_Hopkins "Frederick Gowland Hopkins"). In 1935, he accepted a job at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University "Oxford University") as a lecturer in [pathology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathology "Pathology"). During this time he worked on a range of research topics, including [snake venoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_venom "Snake venom"), [tumour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumour "Tumour") metabolism, [lysozymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysozyme "Lysozyme"), and [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") techniques. Chain was [naturalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalization "Naturalization") as a [British subject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_subject "British subject") in April 1939.

In 1939, he joined [Howard Florey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Florey "Howard Florey") to investigate natural antibacterial agents produced by [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism "Microorganism"). This led him and Florey, with crucial technical help from [Norman Heatley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Heatley "Norman Heatley"), to revisit the work of [Alexander Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Fleming "Alexander Fleming"), who had described [penicillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicillin "Penicillin") nine years earlier. Chain and Florey went on to demonstrate penicillin's therapeutic action and its chemical composition. They discovered how to isolate and concentrate the germ-killing agent in the Penicillium mould. For this research, Chain, Florey, and Fleming received the Nobel Prize in 1945. For his part, Heatley was awarded an honorary Doctorate of Medicine by Oxford University in 1990.

Along with [Edward Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Abraham "Edward Abraham") he was also involved in theorising the beta-lactam structure of penicillin in 1942, which was confirmed by [X-ray crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography "X-ray crystallography") done by [Dorothy Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Hodgkin "Dorothy Hodgkin") in 1945.
Towards the end of World War II, Chain learned his mother and sister had been killed by the Nazis. After World War II, Chain moved to Rome, to work at the [Istituto Superiore di Sanità](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Superiore_di_Sanità "Istituto Superiore di Sanità") (Superior Institute of Health). He returned to Britain in 1964 as the founder and head of the biochemistry department at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London "Imperial College London"), where he stayed until his retirement, specialising in [fermentation technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_fermentation "Industrial fermentation").

On 17 March 1948 Chain was appointed a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society").

In spite of his successful scientific career and widespread recognition from his Nobel Prize, Chain was for some time barred from entry to the United States under the [McCarran Internal Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarran_Internal_Security_Act "McCarran Internal Security Act") of 1950, being declined a visa on two occasions in 1951.

In 1948, he married [Anne Beloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Beloff-Chain "Anne Beloff-Chain"), sister of [Renee Beloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renee_Soskin "Renee Soskin"), [Max Beloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Beloff "Max Beloff"), [John Beloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Beloff "John Beloff") and [Nora Beloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nora_Beloff "Nora Beloff"), and a biochemist of significant standing herself. In his later life, his Jewish identity became increasingly important to him. Chain was an ardent Zionist and he became a member of the board of governors of the [Weizmann Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute_of_Science "Weizmann Institute of Science") at [Rehovot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehovot "Rehovot") in 1954, and later a member of the executive council. He raised his children securely within the Jewish faith, arranging much extracurricular tuition for them. His views were expressed most clearly in his speech 'Why I am a Jew' given at the World Jewish Congress Conference of Intellectuals in 1965.

Chain was appointed [Knight Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor "Knight Bachelor") in the [1969 Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Birthday_Honours "1969 Birthday Honours").

Chain died in 1979 at the [Mayo General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_University_Hospital "Mayo University Hospital") in [Castlebar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlebar "Castlebar"), Ireland. The [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London "Imperial College London") biochemistry building is named after him, as is a road in [Castlebar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlebar "Castlebar").
