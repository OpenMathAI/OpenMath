**Sir John Robert Vane** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (29 March 1927 – 19 November 2004) was a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people "British people") [pharmacologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacologist "Pharmacologist") who was instrumental in the understanding of how [aspirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspirin "Aspirin") produces pain-relief and anti-inflammatory effects and his work led to new treatments for heart and blood vessel disease and introduction of [ACE inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACE_inhibitor "ACE inhibitor"). He was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1982 along with [Sune Bergström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sune_K._Bergström "Sune K. Bergström") and [Bengt Samuelsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengt_I._Samuelsson "Bengt I. Samuelsson") for "their discoveries concerning [prostaglandins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostaglandin "Prostaglandin") and related biologically active substances".

## Education and early life

Born in [Tardebigge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tardebigge "Tardebigge"), [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire "Worcestershire"), John Vane was one of three children and grew up in suburban Birmingham. His father, Maurice Vane, was the son of [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews") Russian immigrants and his mother, Frances Vane, came from a [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire "Worcestershire") farming family. He attended a local state school from age 5, before moving on to [King Edward's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward's_School,_Birmingham "King Edward's School, Birmingham") in [Edgbaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgbaston "Edgbaston"), [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham "Birmingham"). An early interest in chemistry was to prove the inspiration for studying [the subject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") at the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham "University of Birmingham") in 1944.

During his undergraduate studies, Vane became disenchanted with chemistry but still enjoyed experimentation. When [Maurice Stacey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Stacey "Maurice Stacey"), the Professor of Chemistry at Birmingham, was asked by [Harold Burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Harold_Burn "Joshua Harold Burn") to recommend a student to go to Oxford and study pharmacology, Vane jumped at the chance and moved to Burn's department in 1946. Under Burn's guidance, Vane found motivation and enthusiasm for pharmacology, writing: *"[the] laboratory gradually became the most active and important centre for pharmacological research in the U.K. and the main school for training of young pharmacologists."* Vane completed a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") degree in [pharmacology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacology "Pharmacology") and briefly went to work at the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield "University of Sheffield"), before coming back to Oxford to complete his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree in 1953 supervised by [Geoffrey Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_S._Dawes "Geoffrey S. Dawes").

## Career and research

After completing his DPhil, Vane worked as an assistant professor the Department of Pharmacology at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University") before moving back to the United Kingdom to take up a post as a senior lecturer in the Institute of Basic Medical Sciences at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London "University of London") in 1955.

### University of London

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/John_Vane_and_Dr_Salvador_Moncada.jpg/250px-John_Vane_and_Dr_Salvador_Moncada.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:John_Vane_and_Dr_Salvador_Moncada.jpg)

John Vane and [Salvador Moncada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Moncada "Salvador Moncada") in the 1960s

Vane held a post at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London "University of London") for 18 years, progressing from senior lecturer to Professor of Experimental Pharmacology in 1966 (at the Royal College of Surgeons). During that time he developed certain [bioassay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioassay "Bioassay") techniques and focussed his research on both [angiotensin-converting enzyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiotensin-converting_enzyme "Angiotensin-converting enzyme") and the actions of aspirin, eventually leading to the publication with Priscilla Piper of the relationship between aspirin and the prostaglandins that earned him the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1982.

### Wellcome Foundation

In 1973, Vane left his academic post at the Royal College of Surgeons and took up the position as director of research at the [Wellcome Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellcome_Foundation "Wellcome Foundation"), taking a number of his colleagues with him who went on to form the Prostaglandin Research department. Under the leadership of [Salvador Moncada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Moncada "Salvador Moncada"), this group continued important research that eventually led to the discovery of [prostacyclin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostacyclin "Prostacyclin").

### Return to academia

In 1985, Vane returned to academic life and founded the [William Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Harvey "William Harvey") Research Institute at the Medical College of [St Bartholomew's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Bartholomew's_Hospital "St Bartholomew's Hospital") (now [Barts and The London School of Medicine and Dentistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barts_and_The_London_School_of_Medicine_and_Dentistry "Barts and The London School of Medicine and Dentistry"). At the William Harvey Research Institute, Vane's work focused on selective inhibitors of [COX-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COX-2 "COX-2"), and the interplay between [nitric oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitric_oxide "Nitric oxide") and [endothelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endothelin "Endothelin") in the regulation of [vascular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_vessel "Blood vessel") function.

## Awards and honours

Vane was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1974 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1974"). He was also awarded honorary doctorate degrees from [Jagiellonian University Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University_Medical_College "Jagiellonian University Medical College") (formerly Copernicus Academy of Medicine) in 1977, [Paris Descartes University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Descartes_University "Paris Descartes University") in 1978, [Mount Sinai School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sinai_School_of_Medicine "Mount Sinai School of Medicine") in 1980, the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen "University of Aberdeen") in 1983 and [Fu Jen Catholic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu_Jen_Catholic_University "Fu Jen Catholic University") in 2011. He was awarded the [Lasker Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker_Award "Lasker Award") in 1977 for the discovery of prostacyclin and was knighted in 1984 for his contributions to science. In 2000, Vane received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement "Academy of Achievement").

## Personal life

John Vane married, in 1948, (Elizabeth) Daphne Page and had 2 daughters. He died on 19 November 2004 in [Princess Royal University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Royal_University_Hospital "Princess Royal University Hospital"), [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent "Kent"), from long-term complications arising from leg and hip fractures he sustained in May of that year. Lady Vane died in 2021.
