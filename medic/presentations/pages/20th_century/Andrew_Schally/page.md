**Andrzej Viktor** "**Andrew**" **Schally** (30 November 1926 – 17 October 2024) was a Polish-American endocrinologist who was a co-recipient, with [Roger Guillemin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Guillemin "Roger Guillemin") and [Rosalyn Sussman Yalow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalyn_Sussman_Yalow "Rosalyn Sussman Yalow"), of the 1977 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine").

This award recognized his research in the discovery that the [hypothalamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") controls hormone production and release by the [pituitary gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland"), which controls the regulation of other hormones in the body.

Later in life, Schally utilized his knowledge of hypothalamic hormones to research possible methods for [birth control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_control "Birth control") and [cancer treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_treatment "Cancer treatment").

## Life and career

Andrzej Wiktor Schally was born in [Wilno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilno "Wilno") in the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic "Second Polish Republic") (now Vilnius, Lithuania) on 30 November 1926, the son of Brigadier General [Kazimierz Schally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Schally "Kazimierz Schally"), who was chief of the cabinet of President [Ignacy Mościcki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacy_Mościcki "Ignacy Mościcki") of Poland, and Maria (née Łącka). His great-grandfather, Jan Schally, was the mayor of Myślienice. He was of Polish, Austro-Hungarian, French, and Swedish descent.

In September 1939, when Poland was attacked by [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union"), Schally escaped with Poland's President [Ignacy Mościcki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacy_Mościcki "Ignacy Mościcki"), the prime minister and the whole cabinet to neutral Romania, where they were interned.

Immediately after the war, in 1945, Schally moved via Italy and France to the United Kingdom where he changed his first name to Andrew. Schally received his education in Scotland and England. In 1952, he moved to Canada. He received his doctorate in [endocrinology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrinology "Endocrinology") from [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University "McGill University") in 1957. That same year he left for a research career in the United States where he worked principally at [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University "Tulane University").

Schally conducted research in endocrinology at the Miami Veteran's Administration Medical Center in Miami, Florida. A Canadian citizen when he left Canada, Schally became a [naturalized citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_citizen "Naturalized citizen") of the United States in 1962. He was affiliated with the [Baylor College of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baylor_College_of_Medicine "Baylor College of Medicine") for some years in Houston, Texas.

By 1966 Schally had processed 100,000 pig brains to isolate 2.8 mg of [thyrotropin-releasing hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyrotropin-releasing_hormone "Thyrotropin-releasing hormone") (TRH) developing a new realm of knowledge concerning the brain's control over the body chemistry. Schally explained in his 1977 Nobel Lecture that he, alongside his researchers, further dissected 250,000 porcine [hypothalami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalami "Hypothalami") in order to isolate 5 mg more of the hormone TRH to determine its molecular structure. His work also addressed birth control methods and the effects of [growth hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_hormone "Growth hormone") on the body. Together with [Roger Guillemin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Guillemin "Roger Guillemin") he described the [neurohormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurohormone "Neurohormone") gonadotropin-releasing hormone ([GnRH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GnRH "GnRH")) that controls follicle-stimulating hormone [(FSH)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follicle-stimulating_hormone "Follicle-stimulating hormone") and luteinizing hormone ([LH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luteinizing_hormone "Luteinizing hormone")), two hormones that are integral parts of reproduction and growth and development.

Schally received an *honoris causa* doctors degree from the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University "Jagiellonian University") in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków "Kraków").

Recognized as a Fellow of the [Kosciuszko Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosciuszko_Foundation "Kosciuszko Foundation") of Eminent Scientists of Polish Origin and Ancestry.

Schally was married to Margaret Rachel White (divorced), Ana Maria de Medeiros-Comaru (deceased), Maria de Lourdes Schally (widowed). He died at his home in [Miami Beach, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Beach,_Florida "Miami Beach, Florida") , on 17 October 2024, at the age of 97.

## Cancer research

In 1981, it was demonstrated that the [gonadotropin-releasing hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonadotropin-releasing_hormone "Gonadotropin-releasing hormone") (GnRH) agonistic analogs that Schally had developed between the years of 1972 and 1978 inhibited the growth of prostate cancer in rats. Alongside Dr. George Tolis, Schally conducted the first clinical trial of GnRH for patients with advanced [prostate cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostate_cancer "Prostate cancer") in 1982. This method is now the preferred treatment for advanced prostate carcinoma. About 70% of patients with prostate cancer receive an agonist as their primary method of treatment. According to Schally, his treatment causes fewer side effects than radiation and [chemotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemotherapy "Chemotherapy"). The previous method of treatment, [orchiectomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchiectomy "Orchiectomy") or the administration of estrogens, was based on the research of [Charles Brenton Huggins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Brenton_Huggins "Charles Brenton Huggins").

In 2004, after the death of his wife due to [thyroid cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid_cancer "Thyroid cancer"), Schally found comfort in continuing his research.
