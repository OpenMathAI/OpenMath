**Erwin Neher** (German pronunciation: [[ˈɛʁviːn ˈneːɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German") [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Erwin_Neher.ogg "File:De-Erwin Neher.ogg"); [/ˈneɪər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); born 20 March 1944) is a German [biophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics "Biophysics"), specializing in the field of [cell physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_physiology "Cell physiology"). For significant contribution in the field, in 1991 he was awarded, along with [Bert Sakmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_Sakmann "Bert Sakmann"), the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") for "their discoveries concerning the function of single [ion channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_channel "Ion channel") in cells".

## Early life and education

Neher was born in [Landsberg am Lech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landsberg_am_Lech "Landsberg am Lech"), [Upper Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Bavaria "Upper Bavaria"), the son of Elisabeth (née Pfeiffer), a teacher, and Franz Xaver Neher, an executive at a dairy company. He studied physics at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich "Technical University of Munich") from 1963 to 1966.

In 1966, he was awarded a [Fulbright Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Scholarship "Fulbright Scholarship") to study in the US. He spent a year at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison"), and earned a master's degree in biophysics. While at the laboratory of the [Max-Planck-Institute for Biophysical Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck-Institute_for_Biophysical_Chemistry "Max-Planck-Institute for Biophysical Chemistry") he met fellow scientist [Eva-Maria Neher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva-Maria_Neher "Eva-Maria Neher") (born Ruhr), whom he married in 1978 and subsequently the couple had five children – [Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_A._Neher "Richard A. Neher"), Benjamin, Carola, Sigmund and Margret.

In 2003 Neher was one of 22 Nobel Laureates who signed the [Humanist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism_and_Its_Aspirations "Humanism and Its Aspirations").

## Career

In 1986, he was awarded the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") together with [Bert Sakmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_Sakmann "Bert Sakmann"). In 1987, he received the [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize "Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize") of the [Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Forschungsgemeinschaft "Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft"), which is the highest honour awarded in German research. Along with [Bert Sakmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_Sakmann "Bert Sakmann"), he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 1991 for "their discoveries concerning the function of single [ion channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_channels "Ion channels") in cells". Neher and Sakmann were the first to record the currents of single ion channels on a live cell (they were first recorded using the lipid bilayer method) through their development of the [patch-clamp technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patch-clamp_technique "Patch-clamp technique"). The methodology has been further developed with the strong encouragement of [Charles F. Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_F._Stevens "Charles F. Stevens") in his laboratory at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University").

Since 1983, he became a director at the [Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Biophysical_Chemistry "Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry") in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen "Göttingen") and led the Department for Membrane Biophysics. He turned into an emeritus director of the Institute since 2011. He is also a professor emeritus at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen "University of Göttingen") and used to be co-chair of the [Bernstein Center for Computational Neuroscience Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernstein_Network "Bernstein Network").
