**Edward Donnall** "**Don**" **Thomas** (March 15, 1920 – October 20, 2012) was an American [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician "Physician"), professor emeritus at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington "University of Washington"), and director emeritus of the clinical research division at the [Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Hutchinson_Cancer_Research_Center "Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center"). In 1990 he shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [Joseph E. Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Murray "Joseph Murray") for the development of cell and [organ transplantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_transplant "Organ transplant"). Thomas and his wife and research partner [Dottie Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dottie_Thomas "Dottie Thomas") developed [bone marrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_marrow "Bone marrow") [transplantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_cell_transplantation "Stem cell transplantation") as a treatment for [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia").

Thomas was a lead investigator in a failed series of experimental treatments for leukemia and for [Graft-versus-host disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graft-versus-host_disease "Graft-versus-host disease") at Seattle's Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center from 1981 to 1993. Participants were not informed that Thomas and other researchers had a potential financial conflict of interest in the trials, and were never properly informed of the risks. The study continued despite objections from members of the Center’s Internal Review Board. 84 of the 85 participants in the study died.

## Biography

Born in [Mart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart,_Texas "Mart, Texas"), [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas"), Thomas often shadowed his father who was a general practice doctor.
Later, he attended the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin "University of Texas at Austin") where he studied [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") and [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering "Chemical engineering"), graduating with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in 1941 and a [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree "Master's degree") in 1943. While Thomas was an undergraduate he met his wife, [Dorothy (Dottie) Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dottie_Thomas "Dottie Thomas") while she was training to be journalist. They had three children. Thomas entered [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School "Harvard Medical School") in 1943, receiving a [Doctor of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine "Doctor of Medicine") in 1946. Dottie became a lab technician during this time to support the family, and the pair worked closely thereafter. He did his residency at [Peter Bent Brigham Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bent_Brigham_Hospital "Peter Bent Brigham Hospital") before serving two years in the [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army") as an internist stationed in Germany. "In 1955, he was appointed physician in chief at the [Mary Imogene Bassett Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Imogene_Bassett_Hospital "Mary Imogene Bassett Hospital"), now Bassett Medical Center, in Cooperstown, New York, an affiliate of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University")."

At Mary Imogene Bassett, he began to study rodents that received lethal doses of radiation who were then saved by an infusion of marrow cells. At the time, patients who underwent bone marrow transplantation all died from infections or immune reactions that weren't seen in the [rodent studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_testing_on_rodents "Animal testing on rodents"). Thomas began to use dogs as a model system. In 1963, he moved his lab to the [United States Public Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Public_Health_Service "United States Public Health Service") in Seattle.

Thomas also received [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science "National Medal of Science") in 1990. In 2003 he was one of 22 Nobel laureates who signed the [Humanist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism_and_Its_Aspirations "Humanism and Its Aspirations").

He died of heart failure.
