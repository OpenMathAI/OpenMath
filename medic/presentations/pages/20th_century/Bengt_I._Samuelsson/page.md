**Bengt Ingemar Samuelsson** (21 May 1934 – 5 July 2024) was a Swedish [biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemist "Biochemist"). He shared with [Sune K. Bergström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sune_K._Bergström "Sune K. Bergström") and [John R. Vane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Vane "John R. Vane") the 1982 [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") for Physiology or Medicine for discoveries concerning [prostaglandins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostaglandin "Prostaglandin") and related substances.

## Early life and education

Samuelsson was born in [Halmstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halmstad "Halmstad") in southwest Sweden, studied at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University "Lund University"), and was a professor in medical and physiological chemistry at [Karolinska Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karolinska_Institute "Karolinska Institute") in Stockholm in 1973. He was president of [Karolinska Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karolinska_Institute "Karolinska Institute") from 1983 to 1995 and chairman of the [Nobel Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Foundation "Nobel Foundation") from 1993 to 2005.

## Research and career

Discussing the role of [prostaglandins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostaglandin "Prostaglandin") in the body, Samuelsson explained, "It's a control system for the cells that participates in many biological functions. There are endless possibilities of manipulating this system in drug development."

His research interests were originally in [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol") metabolism with importance to reaction mechanisms. Following the structural work on prostaglandins along with Sune Bergström he was interested mainly in the transformation products of [arachidonic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachidonic_acid "Arachidonic acid"). This has led to the identification of [endoperoxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoperoxide "Endoperoxide"), [thromboxanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thromboxane "Thromboxane") and the [leukotrienes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukotriene "Leukotriene"), and his group has chiefly been involved in studying the [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry"), [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") and [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology "Biology") of these compounds and their function in biological control systems. This research has implications in numerous clinical areas, especially in [thrombosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrombosis "Thrombosis"), [inflammation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflammation "Inflammation"), and [allergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allergy "Allergy").

This field has grown enormously since those days. Between 1981 and 1995, about 3,000 papers per year were published that specifically used the expression "prostaglandins," or related terms such as "prostacyclins," "leukotrienes," and "thromboxanes," in their labels and titles.

Samuelsson had served as a director on the boards of [Pharmacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacia "Pharmacia") AB, [NicOx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NicOx "NicOx") SA, and [Schering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_Schering_Pharma_AG "Bayer Schering Pharma AG") AG, and was an advisor to the venture capital fund [HealthCap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HealthCap "HealthCap").

## Death

Samuelsson died on 5 July 2024, at the age of 90.

## Awards and honours

In 1975, Samuelsson was awarded the [Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisa_Gross_Horwitz_Prize "Louisa Gross Horwitz Prize") from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") together with [Sune K. Bergström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sune_K._Bergström "Sune K. Bergström"). He was elected a [Foreign Member of the Royal Society (ForMemRS) in 1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1990 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1990").
