**David Jay Julius** (born November 4, 1955) is an American [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology") and [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") laureate known for his work on [molecular mechanisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology") of [pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain "Pain") sensation and heat, including the characterization of the [TRPV1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRPV1 "TRPV1") and [TRPM8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRPM8 "TRPM8") receptors that detect [capsaicin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsaicin "Capsaicin"), [menthol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menthol "Menthol"), and temperature. He is a professor at the [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco "University of California, San Francisco").

Julius won the 2010 [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize") in [Life Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_life_sciences "List of life sciences") and [Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine "Medicine") and the 2020 [Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Life_Sciences "Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences"). In 2020 he was awarded The [Kavli Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavli_Prize "Kavli Prize"), and in 2021 the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") jointly with [Ardem Patapoutian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardem_Patapoutian "Ardem Patapoutian").

## Early life and education

Julius was born to an [Ashkenazi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jewish "Ashkenazi Jewish") family (from Russia) in [Brighton Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_Beach "Brighton Beach"), [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"), New York City, where he attended [Abraham Lincoln High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln_High_School_(Brooklyn) "Abraham Lincoln High School (Brooklyn)"). He earned his undergraduate degree from [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") in 1977. He attained his doctorate from [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley") in 1984, under joint supervision of Jeremy Thorner and [Randy Schekman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Schekman "Randy Schekman"), where he identified [Kex2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kexin "Kexin") as the founding member of [furin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furin "Furin")-like [proprotein convertases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprotein_convertase "Proprotein convertase"). In 1989, he completed his [post-doctoral training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher "Postdoctoral researcher") with [Richard Axel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Axel "Richard Axel") at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") where he cloned and characterized the serotonin 1c receptor.

While at Berkeley and Columbia, Julius became interested in how [psilocybin mushrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psilocybin_mushroom "Psilocybin mushroom") and [lysergic acid diethylamide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysergic_acid_diethylamide "Lysergic acid diethylamide") work, which led him to look more broadly into how things from nature interact with human receptors.

## Research career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/22_Hegasy_EN_Nobel_Prize_2021_TRPV1_Piezo2.png/330px-22_Hegasy_EN_Nobel_Prize_2021_TRPV1_Piezo2.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:22_Hegasy_EN_Nobel_Prize_2021_TRPV1_Piezo2.png)

Nobel Prize work

He started his career as faculty at the [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco "University of California, San Francisco") in 1989. In 1997, Julius's lab cloned and characterized [TRPV1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRPV1 "TRPV1") which is the receptor that detects [capsaicin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsaicin "Capsaicin"), the chemical in [chili peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_pepper "Chili pepper") that makes them "hot". They found that [TRPV1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRPV1 "TRPV1") also detects noxious heat ([thermoception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoception "Thermoception")). TRPV1 is part of a large family of structurally related [TRP (transient receptor potential)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transient_receptor_potential_channel "Transient receptor potential channel") cation channels. Animals that lack TRPV1 (using genetic knockouts of the protein) lose sensitivity to noxious heat and capsaicin.

Julius's lab has also cloned and characterized [TRPM8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRPM8 "TRPM8") (CMR1) and [TRPA1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRPA1 "TRPA1"), both members of the [TRP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transient_receptor_potential_channel "Transient receptor potential channel") superfamily. They demonstrated that TRPM8 detects [menthol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menthol "Menthol") and cooler temperatures and [TRPA1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRPA1 "TRPA1") detects [mustard oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_oil "Mustard oil") (allyl isothiocyanate). These observations suggested that TRP channels detect a range of temperatures and chemicals. David Julius's lab has also made contributions to the study of [nociception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nociception "Nociception") by discovering toxins that modulate these channels, describing unique adaptations of the channels in diverse species and solving the [cryo-EM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryogenic_electron_microscopy "Cryogenic electron microscopy") structures of numerous channels.

Julius’ laboratory also made pioneering contributions to the discovery of [purinergic receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purinergic_receptor "Purinergic receptor"), both the [P2Y](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P2Y_receptor "P2Y receptor") class of [G protein-coupled receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_protein-coupled_receptor "G protein-coupled receptor"), and the P2X class of ligand-gated ion channels. This included the cloning of [P2Y12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P2Y12 "P2Y12"), the receptor for [clopidogrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clopidogrel "Clopidogrel") and related [antiplatelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiplatelet_drug "Antiplatelet drug") medications that are widely used to reduce the risk of heart disease and stroke. The group also cloned the [5HT3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-HT3_receptor "5-HT3 receptor") receptor, a [serotonin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin "Serotonin")-activated ion channel and the target for drugs such as [ondansentron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondansetron "Ondansetron") for the treatment of nausea and vomiting.

From 2007 to 2020 Julius served as the editor of the peer-reviewed journal the *[Annual Review of Physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Review_of_Physiology "Annual Review of Physiology")*.

## Awards

In 2000, Julius was awarded the inaugural [Perl-UNC Neuroscience Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perl-UNC_Prize "Perl-UNC Prize") for his work on cloning the [capsaicin receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsaicin_receptor "Capsaicin receptor"). In 2006, he was honored be The [International Prize for Translational Neuroscience](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/International%20Prize%20for%20Translational%20Neuroscience "de:International Prize for Translational Neuroscience") of the [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society "Max Planck Society"). In 2007, Julius received the [W. Alden Spencer Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._Alden_Spencer_Award "W. Alden Spencer Award") by the [College of Physicians and Surgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University_College_of_Physicians_and_Surgeons "Columbia University College of Physicians and Surgeons"), the Department of Neuroscience, and The Kavli Institute for Brain Science at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), and the Julius Axelrod Prize of the [Society for Neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Neuroscience "Society for Neuroscience"). In 2008, he held the [Jack Cooper Lecture](https://medicine.yale.edu/pharm/events/endowed-lectureships/#jack-r-cooper-lecture) at Yale School of Medicine. In 2010, he won the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize "Shaw Prize") for his work identifying the [ion channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_channel "Ion channel") involved in various aspects of [nociception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nociception "Nociception"). He received the [Passano Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passano_Foundation "Passano Foundation") in the same year. In 2014, he was honored by [Johnson & Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_&_Johnson "Johnson & Johnson") with the [Dr. Paul Janssen Award for Biomedical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Paul_Janssen_Award_for_Biomedical_Research "Dr. Paul Janssen Award for Biomedical Research") for discovering the molecular basis for pain and thermosensation. In 2017, he won the [Gairdner Foundation International Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gairdner_Foundation_International_Award "Gairdner Foundation International Award") and the [HFSP Nakasone Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HFSP_Nakasone_Award "HFSP Nakasone Award"). He has also been awarded the 2010 [Prince of Asturias Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_of_Asturias_Awards "Princess of Asturias Awards") for Technical and Scientific Research, the 2020 [Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Life_Sciences "Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences"), and the 2020 [Kavli Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavli_Prize "Kavli Prize") in Neuroscience (together with [Ardem Patapoutian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardem_Patapoutian "Ardem Patapoutian")) and the 2020 [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Awards "BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Awards").

In 2021, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") in Physiology or Medicine jointly with [Ardem Patapoutian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardem_Patapoutian "Ardem Patapoutian") for their discoveries of receptors for temperature and touch.

In 2022, Julius was awarded the UCSF Medal by the [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco "University of California, San Francisco"). In 2023, he received the John J. Bonica Award, by the American Society of Regional Anesthesia and Pain Medicine (ASRA). In 2025, Julius was awarded an Honorary Membership in the [Society of Toxicology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Toxicology "Society of Toxicology").
