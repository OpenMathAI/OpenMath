**Oliver Smithies** (23 June 1925 – 10 January 2017) was a British-American [geneticist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneticist "Geneticist") and [physical biochemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_biochemistry "Physical biochemistry"). He is known for introducing [starch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch "Starch") as a medium for [gel electrophoresis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gel_electrophoresis "Gel electrophoresis") in 1955, and for the discovery, simultaneously with [Mario Capecchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Capecchi "Mario Capecchi") and [Martin Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Evans "Martin Evans"), of the technique of [homologous recombination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homologous_recombination "Homologous recombination") of transgenic [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA") with genomic DNA, a much more reliable method of altering animal genomes than previously used, and the technique behind [gene targeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_targeting "Gene targeting") and [knockout mice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knockout_mice "Knockout mice"). He received the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 2007 for his genetics work.

## Early life and education

Smithies was born in [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_West_Yorkshire "Halifax, West Yorkshire"), [West Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire "West Yorkshire"), England, to William Smithies and his wife Doris, née Sykes. His father sold life insurance policies and his mother taught English at [Halifax Technical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calderdale_College "Calderdale College"). He had a twin brother and a younger sister. He attended a primary school in the nearby village of [Copley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley,_West_Yorkshire "Copley, West Yorkshire") and then went to [Heath Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath_Grammar_School "Heath Grammar School") in Halifax. He said that his love of science came from an early fascination with radios and [telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescopes "Telescopes").

He attended [Balliol College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford "Balliol College, Oxford") on a [Brackenbury Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brackenbury_Scholarship "Brackenbury Scholarship"), initially reading [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine "Medicine"). He studied [anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy "Anatomy") and [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology "Physiology"), winning a prize in anatomy, and graduated with a [first-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_degree_classification#First-class_honours "British degree classification") [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree in animal physiology, including [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry"), in 1946. Inspired by tutorials from [Alexander G. Ogston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_George_Ogston "Alexander George Ogston") on applying [physical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry "Physical chemistry") to biological systems, Smithies then switched away from medicine to earn a second bachelor's degree in chemistry. He published his first research paper, co-written with Ogston, in 1948. In 1951, he received a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_(Oxbridge) "Master of Arts (Oxbridge)") degree and a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") in biochemistry under Ogston's supervision; his thesis was entitled "Physico-chemical properties of solutions of proteins".

## Career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/George_W._Bush_meets_with_the_American_2007_Nobel_Award_recipients-20071126.jpg/250px-George_W._Bush_meets_with_the_American_2007_Nobel_Award_recipients-20071126.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:George_W._Bush_meets_with_the_American_2007_Nobel_Award_recipients-20071126.jpg)

Oliver Smithies (second on the left)

Smithies was awarded a [Commonwealth Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Fund "Commonwealth Fund") fellowship to take up a post-doctoral position in the United States, in the laboratory of J. W. Williams at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison")'s Department of Chemistry. A problem with acquiring a U.S. visa, due to a condition of the Commonwealth Fund fellowship, then forced him to leave the U.S. From 1953 to 1960, he worked as an associate research faculty member, under [insulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin "Insulin") researcher David A. Scott, in the [Connaught Medical Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connaught_Medical_Research_Laboratory "Connaught Medical Research Laboratory") at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto "University of Toronto") in Canada. He learned [medical genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_genetics "Medical genetics") from Norma Ford Walker at the [Hospital for Sick Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hospital_for_Sick_Children,_Toronto "The Hospital for Sick Children, Toronto") in Toronto.

In 1960, Smithies returned to the University of Wisconsin–Madison, where he worked in the Department of Genetics until 1988 as, successively, assistant, associate and Leon J. Cole and Hilldale Professor of Genetics and Medical Genetics. Subsequently, he was the Excellence Professor of Pathology and Laboratory Medicine at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill "University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill"). He continued to work in his lab there daily into his eighties. He co-authored a total of more than 350 research papers and reviews, dating from 1948 to 2016.

## Research

Smithies developed the technique of [gel electrophoresis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gel_electrophoresis "Gel electrophoresis") using a [starch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch "Starch") matrix, as a sideline of (unproductive) research into an insulin precursor molecule, at the University of Toronto. This improved the ability to resolve proteins by electrophoresis. He was assisted technically in his later electrophoresis work by Otto Hiller. He used starch electrophoresis to reveal differences between normal human [plasma proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_protein "Plasma protein"), and in collaboration with Norma Ford Walker, showed that the variation was inherited, which stimulated his interest in [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics").

While at the University of Wisconsin in the 1980s, Smithies developed [gene targeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_targeting "Gene targeting") in mice, a method of replacing single mouse genes using [homologous recombination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homologous_recombination "Homologous recombination"). [Mario Capecchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Capecchi "Mario Capecchi") also developed the technique independently. This research is the basis of methods used worldwide to investigate the role of particular genes in a wide range of human diseases including [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer"), [cystic fibrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cystic_fibrosis "Cystic fibrosis") and [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes "Diabetes"). In 2002, Smithies worked with his wife, [Nobuyo Maeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobuyo_Maeda "Nobuyo Maeda"), studying [high blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_blood_pressure "High blood pressure") using genetically altered mice.

## Awards and honors

Smithies won the 2001 [Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Lasker_Award_for_Basic_Medical_Research "Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research"), jointly with [Martin Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Evans "Martin Evans") ([Cardiff University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_University "Cardiff University")) and [Mario Capecchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Capecchi "Mario Capecchi") ([University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah "University of Utah")), for their work on homologous recombination. He received the [Wolf Prize in Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize_in_Medicine "Wolf Prize in Medicine"), with Capecchi and [Ralph L. Brinster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_L._Brinster "Ralph L. Brinster"), in 2002/3. He won the 2007 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine"), jointly with Capecchi and Evans, "for their discoveries of principles for introducing specific gene modifications in mice by the use of embryonic stem cells."

His other awards include two [Gairdner Foundation International Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gairdner_Foundation_International_Award "Gairdner Foundation International Award") (1990 and 1993), the [North Carolina Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Award "North Carolina Award") for Science (1993), the [Alfred P. Sloan, Jr. Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan,_Jr._Prize "Alfred P. Sloan, Jr. Prize") from the General Motors Foundation, jointly with Capecchi (1994), the Ciba Award from the American Heart Foundation (1996), the [Bristol Myers Squibb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Myers_Squibb "Bristol Myers Squibb") Award (1997), the [Association of American Medical Colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Medical_Colleges "Association of American Medical Colleges")' Award for Distinguished Research, jointly with Capecchi (1998), the International Okamoto Award from the Japan Vascular Disease Research Foundation (2000), the [O. Max Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._Max_Gardner "O. Max Gardner") Award, the highest award for faculty in the [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina "University of North Carolina") system (2002), the [Massry Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massry_Prize "Massry Prize") of the Meira and Shaul G. Massry Foundation (2002), shared with Capecchi, the [March of Dimes Prize in Developmental Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_Dimes_Prize_in_Developmental_Biology "March of Dimes Prize in Developmental Biology"), jointly with Capecchi (2005), and the [American Institute of Chemists Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Chemists_Gold_Medal "American Institute of Chemists Gold Medal") (2009).

Smithies was elected to the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences "United States National Academy of Sciences") (1971), the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") (1978), the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science "American Association for the Advancement of Science") (1986), the [Institute of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Medicine "Institute of Medicine") (2003), and as a foreign member of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1998 "List of Fellows of the Royal Society elected in 1998") (ForMemRS; 1998). He received honorary degrees from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago") (1991), the [University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_São_Paulo "University of São Paulo") (2008) and the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") (2011).

A [blue plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_plaque "Blue plaque") to him was erected by the [Halifax Civic Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Civic_Trust?action=edit&redlink=1 "Halifax Civic Trust").

## Personal life

Smithies married Lois Kitze, a virologist at the University of Wisconsin, in the 1950s; they separated in 1978. His second wife, [Nobuyo Maeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobuyo_Maeda "Nobuyo Maeda"), is a pathology professor at the University of North Carolina. Smithies was a naturalized American citizen, and, despite being [color-blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color-blind "Color-blind"), was a licensed private airplane pilot who enjoyed gliding. He described himself as an atheist.

Smithies died on 10 January 2017 at the age of 91.
