**Edvard Ingjald Moser** (pronounced [[ˈɛ̀dvɑɖ ˈmoːsər]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Norwegian "Help:IPA/Norwegian"); born 27 April 1962) is a Norwegian psychologist and neuroscientist, who as of May 2024 is a professor at the [Norwegian University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_University_of_Science_and_Technology "Norwegian University of Science and Technology") (NTNU) in [Trondheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trondheim "Trondheim").

He shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 2014 with long-term collaborator and then-wife [May-Britt Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May-Britt_Moser "May-Britt Moser"), and previous mentor [John O'Keefe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_O'Keefe_(neuroscientist) "John O'Keefe (neuroscientist)") for their work identifying the brain's positioning system. The two main components of the brain's GPS are grid cells and [place cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_cells "Place cells"), a specialized type of neuron that respond to specific locations in space. Together with May-Britt Moser he established the [Moser research environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moser_research_environment "Moser research environment").

In 1996 he was appointed as associate professor in [biological psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_psychology "Biological psychology") at the Department of Psychology at the [Norwegian University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_University_of_Science_and_Technology "Norwegian University of Science and Technology") (NTNU); he was promoted to professor of neuroscience in 1998. In 2002, his research group was given the status of a separate "centre of excellence". Edvard Moser has led a succession of research groups and centres, collectively known as the [Moser research environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moser_research_environment "Moser research environment").

## Early life and education

Moser was born in [Ålesund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ålesund "Ålesund") to German parents Eduard Paul Moser (1928–2013) and Ingeborg Annamarie Herholz (1931–). His parents had grown up in [Kronberg im Taunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronberg_im_Taunus "Kronberg im Taunus"), a suburb of [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt "Frankfurt"), where Moser's grandfather Eduard Moser had been Lutheran parish priest. Moser's father trained as a [pipe organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipe_organ "Pipe organ") builder and emigrated to Norway together with his friend Jakob Pieroth in 1953 when they were offered employment at a pipe organ workshop at [Haramsøy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haramsøy "Haramsøy"). They later established their own workshop and built many church pipe organs in Norway. The Moser family originally was from [Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau,_Rhineland-Palatinate "Nassau, Rhineland-Palatinate"); [Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moser_(surname) "Moser (surname)") is a South German topographic name for someone who lived near a [swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp "Swamp") or [mire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mire "Mire") (South German *Moos*). Edvard Moser grew up at [Hareid Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hareid_Municipality "Hareid Municipality") and in [Ålesund Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ålesund_Municipality "Ålesund Municipality"). He was raised in a conservative [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "Christians") family.

Edvard Moser married [May-Britt Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May-Britt_Moser "May-Britt Moser") in 1985 when they were both students. They announced that they are divorcing in 2016.

His sister is the sociologist [Ingunn Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingunn_Moser "Ingunn Moser"), known as the founding rector of [VID University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VID_Specialized_University "VID Specialized University").

Moser was awarded the cand.psychol. degree in psychology at the [Department of Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Psychology,_University_of_Oslo "Department of Psychology, University of Oslo") at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo "University of Oslo") in 1990. He was then employed as a research fellow at the Faculty of Medicine, where he obtained his *dr.philos.* doctoral research degree in the field of [neurophysiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurophysiology "Neurophysiology") in 1995. He also has studied mathematics and statistics. Early in his career, he worked under the supervision of [Per Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Andersen "Per Andersen").

Moser went on to undertake postdoctoral training with [Richard G. Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_G._Morris "Richard G. Morris") at the Centre for Neuroscience, [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh "University of Edinburgh"), from 1995 to 1997, and was a visiting postdoctoral fellow at the laboratory of [John O'Keefe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_O'Keefe_(neuroscientist) "John O'Keefe (neuroscientist)") at the [University College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_London "University College, London") for two months.

## Career

Moser returned to Norway in 1996 to be appointed associate professor in biological psychology at the Department of Psychology at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU) in Trondheim. He was promoted to full professor of neuroscience in 1998. Moser is also head of department of the NTNU Institute for Systems Neuroscience.

Moser is founding director/co-director of three [Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Council_of_Norway "Research Council of Norway")-funded centres of excellence:

- Centre for the Biology of Memory (2002-2012)
- Centre for Neural Computation (2012-2022)
- Centre for Algorithms in the Cortex (2023-2033)

In 2007 the centres became a [Kavli Institute for Systems Neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavli_Institute_for_Systems_Neuroscience "Kavli Institute for Systems Neuroscience"), with Moser as director.

as of May 2024 is a professor at the [Norwegian University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_University_of_Science_and_Technology "Norwegian University of Science and Technology") (NTNU) in [Trondheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trondheim "Trondheim").

## Research

In 2005, he and his then-wife [May-Britt Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May-Britt_Moser "May-Britt Moser") discovered [grid cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_cell "Grid cell") in the brain's medial [entorhinal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entorhinal_cortex "Entorhinal cortex"). Grid cells are specialized neurons that provide the brain with a coordinate system and a metric for space. In 2018, he discovered a neural network that expresses a person's sense of time in experiences and memories located in the brain's lateral [entorhinal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entorhinal_cortex "Entorhinal cortex").

He shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 2014 with long-term collaborator and then-wife [May-Britt Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May-Britt_Moser "May-Britt Moser"), and previous mentor [John O'Keefe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_O'Keefe_(neuroscientist) "John O'Keefe (neuroscientist)") for their work identifying the brain's positioning system. The two main components of the brain's GPS are; grid cells and [place cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_cells "Place cells"), a specialized type of neuron that respond to specific locations in space. Together with May-Britt Moser he established the [Moser research environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moser_research_environment "Moser research environment"), which they lead.

## Other activities

He is a member of the [Royal Norwegian Society of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Society_of_Sciences_and_Letters "Royal Norwegian Society of Sciences and Letters"), [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters "Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters"), the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society"), and the [Norwegian Academy of Technological Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Technological_Sciences "Norwegian Academy of Technological Sciences").

In 2015 he became an external scientific member of the [Max Planck Institute of Neurobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_of_Neurobiology "Max Planck Institute of Neurobiology"), with which he has collaborated over several years.

He is also an [honorary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_professor "Honorary professor") at the Centre for Cognitive and Neural Systems at the [University of Edinburgh Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh_Medical_School "University of Edinburgh Medical School").

Moser has been a member of the board of reviewing editors in science since 2004 and he has been reviewing editor for *[Journal of Neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Neuroscience "Journal of Neuroscience")* since 2005. He chaired the programme committee of the European Neuroscience meeting ([FENS Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_European_Neuroscience_Societies "Federation of European Neuroscience Societies")) in 2006.
