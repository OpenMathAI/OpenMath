**John Robin Warren** [AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_of_the_Order_of_Australia "Companion of the Order of Australia") (11 June 1937 – 23 July 2024) was an Australian [pathologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathologist "Pathologist"), [Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize "Nobel Prize") laureate, and researcher who is credited with the 1979 re-discovery of the [bacterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterium "Bacterium") *[Helicobacter pylori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicobacter_pylori "Helicobacter pylori")*, together with [Barry Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Marshall "Barry Marshall"). The duo proved to the medical community that the bacterium *Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori)* is the cause of most [peptic ulcers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptic_ulcers "Peptic ulcers").

## Early life and education

Warren was born 11 June 1937 in [North Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Adelaide "North Adelaide"). His father, Roger Warren, was a [winemaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winemaker "Winemaker"), and his mother, Helen Warren ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Verco), was a nurse. After completing his high school education at [St Peter's College, Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Peter's_College,_Adelaide "St Peter's College, Adelaide"), Warren received his [MBBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Medicine,_Bachelor_of_Surgery "Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery") degree from the [University of Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Adelaide "University of Adelaide").

## Career

Warren trained at the [Royal Adelaide Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Adelaide_Hospital "Royal Adelaide Hospital") and became a Registrar in Clinical Pathology at the [Institute of Medical and Veterinary Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Medical_and_Veterinary_Science "Institute of Medical and Veterinary Science") (IMVS). There, he worked in laboratory haematology, which generated his interest in pathology.

In 1963, Warren was appointed Honorary Clinical Assistant in Pathology and Honorary Registrar in Haematology at Royal Adelaide Hospital. Subsequently, he lectured in pathology at Adelaide University and then became Clinical Pathology Registrar at the Royal Melbourne Hospital. In 1967, Warren was elected to the [Royal College of Pathologists of Australasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Pathologists_of_Australasia "Royal College of Pathologists of Australasia") and became a senior pathologist at the [Royal Perth Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Perth_Hospital "Royal Perth Hospital"), where he spent the majority of his career.

### Nobel Prize work

At the [University of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Australia "University of Western Australia"), Warren along with his colleague [Barry J. Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Marshall "Barry Marshall"), proved that the bacterium is the infectious cause of [stomach ulcers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach_ulcer "Stomach ulcer"). Warren helped develop a convenient diagnostic test ([14
C-urea breath-test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urea_breath_test "Urea breath test")) for detecting *H. pylori* in ulcer patients.

In 2005, Warren and Marshall were awarded the [Nobel Prize in Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine").

An Australian documentary was made in 2006 about Warren and Marshall's road to the Nobel Prize, called "The Winner's Guide to the Nobel Prize". He was appointed a [Companion of the Order of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_of_the_Order_of_Australia "Companion of the Order of Australia") in 2007.

Asteroid [254863 Robinwarren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/254863_Robinwarren "254863 Robinwarren"), discovered by Italian amateur astronomer [Silvano Casulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvano_Casulli "Silvano Casulli") in 2005, was named in his honour. The official [naming citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meanings_of_minor-planet_names:_254001–255000#m863 "Meanings of minor-planet names: 254001–255000") was published by the [Minor Planet Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Planet_Center "Minor Planet Center") on 22 April 2016 ([M.P.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Planet_Circulars "Minor Planet Circulars") 99893).

## Personal life and death

Warren married Winifred Teresa Warren (née Williams) in the early 1960s, and together they had five children. Winifred Warren became an accomplished psychiatrist. Following her death in 1997, Warren retired from medicine.

Warren died in [Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth "Perth"), Australia, on 23 July 2024, at the age of 87.
