**Sydney Brenner** (13 January 1927 – 5 April 2019) was a South African [biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist "Biologist"). In 2002, he shared the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [H. Robert Horvitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Robert_Horvitz "H. Robert Horvitz") and Sir [John E. Sulston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_E._Sulston "John E. Sulston"). Brenner made significant contributions to work on the [genetic code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_code "Genetic code"), and other areas of molecular biology while working in the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(United_Kingdom) "Medical Research Council (United Kingdom)") (MRC) [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge"), England. He established the [roundworm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundworm "Roundworm") *[Caenorhabditis elegans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caenorhabditis_elegans "Caenorhabditis elegans")* as a [model organism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_organism "Model organism") for the investigation of [developmental biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_biology "Developmental biology"), and founded the [Molecular Sciences Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_Sciences_Institute "Molecular Sciences Institute") in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California "Berkeley, California"), United States.

## Education and early life

Brenner was born in the town of [Germiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germiston "Germiston") in the then [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_Province "Transvaal Province") (today in [Gauteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng "Gauteng")), South Africa, on 13 January 1927. His parents, Leah (née Blecher) and Morris Brenner, were Jewish immigrants. His father, a cobbler, migrated to South Africa from Lithuania in 1910, and his mother from [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga "Riga"), Latvia, in 1922. He had one sister, Phyllis and also one brother, Isaac.

He was educated at [Germiston High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germiston_High_School "Germiston High School") and the [University of the Witwatersrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Witwatersrand "University of the Witwatersrand"). Having joined the university at the age of 15, it was noted during his second year that he would be too young to qualify for the practice of medicine at the conclusion of his six-year medical course, and he was therefore allowed to complete a Bachelor of Science degree in Anatomy and Physiology. During this time he was taught physical chemistry by [Joel Mandelstam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Mandelstam "Joel Mandelstam"), microscopy by [Alfred Oettle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Oettle?action=edit&redlink=1 "Alfred Oettle") and neurology by [Harold Daitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Daitz?action=edit&redlink=1 "Harold Daitz"). He also received an introduction to anthropology and paleontology from [Raymond Dart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Dart "Raymond Dart") and [Robert Broom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Broom "Robert Broom"). The histologist [Joseph Gillman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Gillman?action=edit&redlink=1 "Joseph Gillman") and director of research in the Anatomy Department persuaded Brenner to continue towards an honours degree and beyond towards an MSc. Brenner accepted though this would mean he would not graduate from medical school and his bursary (scholarship) would be discontinued. He supported himself during this time by working as a laboratory technician. It was during this time, in 1945, that Brenner would publish his first scientific works. His masters thesis was in the field of [cytogenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytogenetics "Cytogenetics") and publications during this time in the field Brenner would later call [Cell Physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_Physiology "Cell Physiology").

In 1946 [Wilfred Le Gros Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfred_Le_Gros_Clark "Wilfred Le Gros Clark") invited Brenner to his Department of Anatomy in Oxford, during a visit to South Africa. Brenner was persuaded to finish his medical education instead. Brenner returned to medical school where he failed Medicine, nearly failed Surgery and achieved a First Class in Obstetrics and Gynecology. Six months later Brenner had finished repeating Medicine and Surgery and in 1951 received the degrees of [Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Medicine,_Bachelor_of_Surgery "Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery") (MBBCh).

Brenner received an [1851 Exhibition Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1851_Exhibition_Scholarship "1851 Exhibition Scholarship") from the [Royal Commission for the Exhibition of 1851](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_for_the_Exhibition_of_1851 "Royal Commission for the Exhibition of 1851") which enabled him to complete a [Doctor of Philosophy (DPhil)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy "Doctor of Philosophy") degree at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford") as a postgraduate student of [Exeter College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter_College,_Oxford "Exeter College, Oxford"), supervised by [Cyril Hinshelwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Norman_Hinshelwood "Cyril Norman Hinshelwood").

## Career and research

Following his DPhil, Brenner did [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research "Postdoctoral research") at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"). He spent the next 20 years at the [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") in Cambridge. There, during the 1960s, he contributed to molecular biology, then an emerging field. In 1976 he joined the [Salk Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salk_Institute "Salk Institute") in [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California").

Together with [Jack Dunitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_D._Dunitz "Jack D. Dunitz"), [Dorothy Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Hodgkin "Dorothy Hodgkin"), [Leslie Orgel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Orgel "Leslie Orgel"), and [Beryl M. Oughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryl_M._Oughton?action=edit&redlink=1 "Beryl M. Oughton"), he was one of the first people in April 1953 to see the model of the structure of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA "DNA"), constructed by [Francis Crick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Crick "Francis Crick") and [James Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_D._Watson "James D. Watson"); at the time he and the other scientists were working at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford "University of Oxford")'s Chemistry Department. All were impressed by the new DNA model, especially Brenner, who subsequently worked with Crick in the [Cavendish Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish_Laboratory "Cavendish Laboratory") at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") and the newly opened [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_(United_Kingdom) "Medical Research Council (United Kingdom)") (MRC) [Laboratory of Molecular Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_of_Molecular_Biology "Laboratory of Molecular Biology") (LMB). According to Beryl Oughton, later Rimmer, they all travelled together in two cars once [Dorothy Hodgkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Hodgkin "Dorothy Hodgkin") announced to them that they were off to Cambridge to see the model of the structure of DNA.

Brenner made several seminal contributions to the emerging field of [molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology "Molecular biology") in the 1960s (see [Phage group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phage_group "Phage group")). The first was to prove that all overlapping genetic [coding sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coding_region "Coding region") were impossible. This insight separated the coding function from structural constraints as proposed in a clever code by [George Gamow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow "George Gamow"). This led Francis Crick to propose the concept of a hypothetical molecule (later identified as [transfer RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_RNA "Transfer RNA") or tRNA) that transfers the genetic information from RNA to proteins. Brenner gave the name "[adaptor hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptor_hypothesis "Adaptor hypothesis")" in 1955. The physical separation between the [anticodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticodon "Anticodon") and the amino acid on a tRNA is the basis for the unidirectional flow of information in coded biological systems. This is commonly known as the [central dogma of molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_dogma_of_molecular_biology "Central dogma of molecular biology"), i.e. information flows from nucleic acid to protein and never from protein to nucleic acid. Following this adaptor insight, Brenner conceived of the concept of [messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA "Messenger RNA") during an April 1960 conversation with Crick and [François Jacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Jacob "François Jacob"), and together with Jacob and [Matthew Meselson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Meselson "Matthew Meselson") went on to prove its existence later that summer. Then, with Crick, [Leslie Barnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Barnett "Leslie Barnett"), and [Richard Watts-Tobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Watts-Tobin "Richard Watts-Tobin"), Brenner genetically demonstrated the triplet nature of the code of [protein translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_(biology) "Translation (biology)") through the [Crick, Brenner, Barnett, Watts-Tobin et al. experiment of 1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frameshift_mutation_experiment "Frameshift mutation experiment"), which discovered [frameshift mutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frameshift_mutation "Frameshift mutation"). Brenner collaborating with Sarabhai, Stretton and Bolle in 1964, using [amber mutants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_codon "Stop codon") defective in the [bacteriophage T4D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escherichia_virus_T4 "Escherichia virus T4") major head protein, showed that the [nucleotide sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleic_acid_sequence "Nucleic acid sequence") of the [gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene "Gene") is co-linear with the amino acid sequence of the encoded polypeptide chain.

Together with the decoding work of [Marshall Warren Nirenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Warren_Nirenberg "Marshall Warren Nirenberg") and others, the discovery of the triplet nature of the [genetic code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_code "Genetic code") was critical to deciphering the code. Barnett helped set up Sydney Brenner's laboratory in [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), many years later.

Brenner, with George Pieczenik, created the first computer matrix analysis of nucleic acids using TRAC, which Brenner continued to use. Crick, Brenner, [Klug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Klug "Aaron Klug") and Pieczenik returned to their early work on deciphering the genetic code with a pioneering paper on the origin of protein synthesis, where constraints on mRNA and tRNA co-evolved allowing for a five-base interaction with a flip of the anticodon loop, and thereby creating a triplet code translating system without requiring a [ribosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome "Ribosome"). This model requires a partially overlapping code. The published scientific paper is extremely rare in that its collaborators include three authors who independently became Nobel laureates.

Brenner then focused on establishing a free-living roundworm *[Caenorhabditis elegans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caenorhabditis_elegans "Caenorhabditis elegans")* as a [model organism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_organism "Model organism") for the investigation of animal [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_biology "Developmental biology") including [neural development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_development "Neural development"). He chose this 1-millimeter-long soil [roundworm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundworm "Roundworm") mainly because it is simple, is easy to grow in bulk populations, and turned out to be quite convenient for genetic analysis. One of the key methods for identifying important function genes was the screen for roundworms that had some functional defect, such as being *uncoordinated*, leading to the identification of new sets of proteins, such as the [UNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNC_(biology) "UNC (biology)") proteins. For this work, he shared the 2002 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine") with [H. Robert Horvitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Robert_Horvitz "H. Robert Horvitz") and [John Sulston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Sulston "John Sulston"). The title of his Nobel lecture in December 2002, "Nature's Gift to Science", is a homage to this [nematode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nematode "Nematode"); in it, he considered that having chosen the right organism turned out to be as important as having addressed the right problems to work on. In fact, the *C. elegans* community has grown rapidly in recent decades with researchers working on a wide spectrum of problems.

Brenner founded the [Molecular Sciences Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_Sciences_Institute "Molecular Sciences Institute") in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California "Berkeley, California") in 1996. As of 2015 he was associated with the [Salk Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salk_Institute "Salk Institute"), the [Institute of Molecular and Cell Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Molecular_and_Cell_Biology_(Singapore) "Institute of Molecular and Cell Biology (Singapore)"), the Singapore [Biomedical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_Research_Council "Biomedical Research Council"), the [Janelia Farm Research Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janelia_Farm_Research_Campus "Janelia Farm Research Campus"), and the [Howard Hughes Medical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Hughes_Medical_Institute "Howard Hughes Medical Institute"). In August 2005, Brenner was appointed president of the [Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Institute_of_Science_and_Technology "Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology"). He was also on the Board of Scientific Governors at [The Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scripps_Research_Institute "The Scripps Research Institute"), as well as being Professor of Genetics there. A scientific biography of Brenner was written by [Errol Friedberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errol_Friedberg "Errol Friedberg") in the US, for publication by [Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Spring_Harbor_Laboratory "Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory") Press in 2010.

Known for his penetrating scientific insight and acerbic wit, Brenner, for many years, authored a regular column ("Loose Ends") in the journal *[Current Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_Biology "Current Biology")*. This column was so popular that "Loose ends from Current Biology", a compilation, was published by Current Biology Ltd. and became a collector's item. Brenner wrote "*A Life in Science*", a paperback published by [BioMed Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioMed_Central "BioMed Central"). He is also noted for his generosity with ideas and the great number of students and colleagues his ideas have stimulated.

In 2017, Brenner co-organized a seminal lecture series in Singapore describing ten logarithmic scales of time from the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang "Big Bang") to the present, spanning the appearance of multicellular life forms, the evolution of humans, and the emergence of language, culture and technology. Prominent scientists and thinkers, including [W. Brian Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._Brian_Arthur "W. Brian Arthur"), [Svante Pääbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svante_Pääbo "Svante Pääbo"), [Helga Nowotny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helga_Nowotny "Helga Nowotny") and [Jack Szostak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_W._Szostak "Jack W. Szostak"), spoke during the lecture series. In 2018, the lectures were adapted into a popular science book titled *Sydney Brenner's 10-on-10: The Chronicles of Evolution*, published by Wildtype Books.

Brenner also gave four lectures on the history of molecular biology, its impact on neuroscience and the great scientific questions that lie ahead. The lectures were adapted into the book, *In the Spirit of Science: Lectures by Sydney Brenner on DNA, Worms and Brains*.

### American plan and European plan

The "American plan" and "European plan" were proposed by Sydney Brenner as competing models for the way brain cells determine their neural functions. According to the European plan (sometimes referred to as the British plan), the function of cells is determined by their [genetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics "Genetics") lineage. According to the American plan, a cell's function is determined by the function of its neighbours after [cell migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_migration "Cell migration"). Further research has shown that most species follow some combination of these methods, albeit in varying degrees, to transfer information to new cells.

- [Nobel prize in Physiology or Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine "Nobel prize in Physiology or Medicine") in 2002.
- [Dan David Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_David_Prize "Dan David Prize") in 2002. directed by Professor [Gad Barzilai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gad_Barzilai "Gad Barzilai")
- [March of Dimes Prize in Developmental Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_Dimes_Prize_in_Developmental_Biology "March of Dimes Prize in Developmental Biology") in 2002.
- In recognition of his pioneering role in starting what is now a global research community that work on *C. elegans*, another closely related nematode was given the scientific name *[Caenorhabditis brenneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caenorhabditis_brenneri "Caenorhabditis brenneri")*.
- The National Science and Technology Medal by the [Agency for Science, Technology and Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agency_for_Science,_Technology_and_Research "Agency for Science, Technology and Research") awarded Brenner in 2006 for his distinguished and strategic contributions to the development of Singapore's scientific capability and culture, particularly in the biomedical sciences sector.
- In 2008, the University of the Witwatersrand named the Sydney Brenner Institute for Molecular Bioscience (SBIMB) in his honour.
- [Grand Cordon of the Order of the Rising Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Rising_Sun "Order of the Rising Sun") in 2017.
- In 2019, a newly discovered species of bobtail squid, *[Euprymna brenneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euprymna_brenneri "Euprymna brenneri")*, was named in his honour.

## Personal life

Brenner was married to May Brenner ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Covitz, subsequently Balkind) from December 1952 until her death in January 2010; their children include Belinda, Carla, Stefan, and his stepson Jonathan Balkind from his wife's first marriage to Marcus Balkind. He lived in [Ely, Cambridgeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ely,_Cambridgeshire "Ely, Cambridgeshire"). He was an atheist.

Brenner died on 5 April 2019, in Singapore, at the age of 92.
